# Who brief review, round 1: Gregory of Tours and Benedict of Nursia (new drafts)

Reviewer: independent, blind to drafting. No model or API calls made. No repo file changed.

## 0. Blind pair, judged before reading anything else

My judgment, recorded first (also saved as `blind-judgment-step0.txt` beside this file):

**Passage Y is stronger.**

- **Sentences.** Each of Y's sentences carries one idea at a length you can say in one breath. X runs long. Its Chilperic sentence is about 40 words and its Salvius sentence about 30.
- **Theology.** Y says what was at stake in plain words: what Arius said, what Nicaea answered, and why it mattered ("a creature would not have joined creatures to God"). It closes on the person's own words, which carry the theology.
- **X's real strength.** X tells a scene well and gives a consequence that is sourced and attributed: "So the king desisted from his purpose" and "In his telling". On the causal link alone, X does better than Y.
- **X's weakness.** X never says why "only God, with no distinct Persons" was wrong in Gregory's eyes. The only reason it gives is an appeal to authority ("what the apostles and the Fathers had handed down"). The listener gets the conflict without the idea.

After reading: X is from the Gregory of Tours draft (its paragraph 3). Y is from the Athanasius bar brief. This is a reasoned preference for the bar, not a failure to tell them apart, so nothing needs raising to the coordinator. The weakness I found in X before I knew its source is finding G-2 below.

## GREGORY OF TOURS

### Check A: sources and quotes

**Quotes, re-verified word for word:**

- "all ruined and destroyed by the fire": Dalton l.22659-22660, with a footnote marker after "ruined". Matches.
- "Woe to our day, since the pursuit of letters has perished from among us": Brehaut l.850-851. Matches.
- "that whoso reads may doubt not that I hold the catholic faith": Dalton l.339-340. Matches.
- "This is my will, that such be thy faith, and that of the other doctors of the Church.": Dalton l.10370-10371. Matches.
- "that which Hilary and Eusebius taught": Dalton l.10374-10375, with a footnote marker after "Eusebius". Matches.
- "So the king desisted from his purpose": Dalton l.10404-10405. Matches.
- "the Nero and Herod of our time": Brehaut l.7732 and Dalton l.13278. Matches.
- "the books of the reverend man Gregory, bishop of Tours": Foulke l.5074-5075. Matches.

**Claims checked and supported:**

- Nineteenth bishop; rebuilt the cathedral larger: Dalton l.22655-22661 and Brehaut l.11144-11150.
- Martin consecrated there: Dalton l.22657-22659.
- Clermont in Auvergne: Brehaut l.85-86.
- Weary of relating the civil wars: Brehaut l.5139-5140.
- The laments, and that no one could write the deeds of the present: Brehaut l.850-853.
- His speech rude: Brehaut l.856.
- Ten books of History: Dalton l.22705.
- The envoy of Leuvigild passing through Tours and arguing the Son lesser: Dalton l.10197-10211.
- Chilperic's decree, Gregory's answer, "wiser men", Salvius: Dalton l.10360-10405.
- Leudast removed as count; his charge that Gregory would hand the city to Sigebert's son, and the adultery charge: Dalton l.10473-10490.
- The bishops at Berny, the denial and the oath: Dalton l.10684-10727.
- Chilperic's complaint "none ruleth at all, save only the bishops": Dalton l.13316-13320, and Brehaut l.7759-7761 (OCR).
- Brehaut's "often the sole authority" and "allow largely for error and prejudice": Brehaut l.66-72.

**Dates and inferences:**

- **573** is Brehaut's date (l.12231) and the margin date in the MGH edition. Gregory's own regnal count (Sigibert's twelfth year, Mir. Mart. II.1, Latin) gives it. The records hold it as Documented. Non-blocking: see G-n1.
- **"About two centuries before"** is the narrator's arithmetic from Gregory's own counts: Martin's 26 years in the see (Dalton l.22360) and Gregory's "160th year after Martin" (Brehaut l.11570ff). It is within tolerance.

### Check B: the causal link

The consequence is there, it follows from the stance, and it is sourced.

- **The chain.** Gregory refuses. The king says he will find wiser men. Salvius rejects the decree. Then comes Gregory's own "So the king desisted from his purpose" (Dalton l.10404-10405).
- **The causal claim is Gregory's own.** His connective is "So", and the brief marks it as his: "In his telling, it was the bishops' refusal that ended...". The plural "bishops'" is a fair reading: the king desisted only after both refused.
- **The honest limit is right.** "Gregory does not say the refusal cost him anything." The draft does not invent a cost, and it does not make the Leudast affair a consequence of the refusal. That matches Dalton V.44 to V.49, where Gregory draws no link.
- **Mark's complaint is answered.** He said the old brief "doesn't tell the end of that story". This draft tells the end: the king desisted. Chilperic's later complaint that only the bishops ruled carries the king-and-bishops thread forward.
- **The two stories are now one.** Mark said the old brief "read like two seperate stories". Here the stance is placed inside the History, in its opening creed and the record of the clash. The recorder story and the stance story are joined, and this is the draft's main gain.

**Result:** passes check B.

### Check C: shape and the three elements

- **Order.** Paragraphs 1 and 2 are personal: the moment, time and place, and what the world faced (civil wars, letters lost). Paragraph 3 is the stance and its consequence. Paragraph 4 goes back to personal trouble. Paragraph 5 is the impact: what he recorded and who used it. Personal, stance, personal, impact is close to Mark's order. Paragraph 4 is part of how king against bishop played out, so it sits acceptably.
- **The moment.** The ruined cathedral is a sourced scene, but it never comes back. Compare Ephrem's famine, which opens and closes that brief. Non-blocking.
- **The thread, checked in the census.**
  - Inherited: Martin of Tours (in the census) and "Hilary and Eusebius", who are not identified (G-1).
  - Passed on: Paul the Deacon (in the census), cited for the History.
  - Clovis is in the census (people). Salvius and Chilperic are not, which is fine for side figures.
  - Hilary of Poitiers is in the census, but the brief never names him that way.
- **The change, in the close.** The close says the History is the record of the church in Gaul, with Brehaut and Paul as witnesses. For a recorder, that fits Mark's ruling.

### Findings

**G-1. BLOCKING. Item 8, common names; misleading by omission.**

- **Sentence:** "He told the king to hold to what the apostles and the Fathers had handed down, 'that which Hilary and Eusebius taught.'"
- **Checked:** Dalton l.10374-10375 and Dalton's note at l.26986-26987: "Hilary of Poitiers ... and Eusebius, bishop of Vercelli".
- **Problem:** Both names are common, and neither is identified at first mention. "Eusebius" is worse than ambiguous. The census holds only Eusebius of Caesarea and Eusebius of Nicomedia. A listener who knows either would hear a bishop linked to the Arian side cited as Gregory's authority against an Arian-leaning decree. This is also the brief's one chance to name the inherited thread through a census person, Hilary of Poitiers, and it misses it.
- **Fix:** keep the quote and identify the names in the narrator's frame, from Dalton's note. For example: "...the faith that Hilary of Poitiers and Eusebius of Vercelli had taught." Or follow the quote with "meaning Hilary of Poitiers and Eusebius of Vercelli". Name Dalton as the identifier on the card.

**G-2. BLOCKING. Craft test 5 (the idea is missing) and Mark's ruling that a brief be "theological (major stanses...)".**

- **Passage:** paragraph 3. The brief says what Chilperic decreed ("only God, with no distinct Persons"). The only reason it gives for Gregory's refusal is an appeal to authority.
- **Checked:** Gregory's own reason is in the same held passage, short and plain. Dalton l.10380-10384: "It was not the Father who was made man, nor the Holy Ghost, but the Son, that He who was the Son of God might for man's redemption be held the Son of man." Also l.10385-10386: "It was not the Father who suffered, nor the Holy Spirit, but the Son."
- **Problem:** The old brief carried a version of this ("the Father is different from the Son..."). The new draft dropped it. The bar model is Athanasius: "If the Son were only a creature..., then becoming man would have left humanity just as it was". Gregory's paragraph has no sentence at that level. The blind pair turned on exactly this gap.
- **Fix:** rewrite paragraph 3 so that one sentence carries Gregory's own reason, from Dalton l.10380-10386. Only the Son became man and suffered, so that he could redeem humanity. If the Persons are erased, so is the redemption. Tell it in modern words, naming Gregory. To make room, cut the envoy sentence (G-n2).

**G-3. BLOCKING. Craft test 4 (hanging sentence) and test 2 (flow).**

- **Sentence:** "He came from Clermont in Auvergne, Ernest Brehaut says." (paragraph 1, last line)
- **Checked:** Brehaut l.85-86 supports the fact.
- **Problem:** The sentence connects to neither neighbour. It comes after the rebuilding and before the civil wars, as a backward-facing tail. It also brings in a modern editor, with no context for who he is, to carry a plain biographical fact. Compare the bar's way of placing a modern scholar: "Archibald Robertson, who edited a volume of his writings". Compare also the bar's opening moves, where the who-line sits inside the scene, as in Gregory of Nazianzus: "In 379 Gregory of Nazianzus came to Constantinople, where the Arians held the churches."
- **Fix:** rewrite paragraph 1 so that his origin is part of the arrival. For example, he came to Tours from Clermont in Auvergne and found the cathedral ruined. Move Brehaut, as the source for Clermont, to the card. Brehaut is then named once in the brief, in paragraph 5.

**NON-BLOCKING findings:**

- **G-n1. "Became bishop in 573".** The year is told flat. Gregory's own count supports it (Sigibert's twelfth year), so it is not a modern reckoning told as fact. It would be safer tied to his count, or left as "in 573" with the source on the card. The records hold it as Documented.
- **G-n2. The envoy sentence.** "An envoy of the Arian king of Spain came through Tours..." Gregory's reply and the outcome (the envoy's later conversion, Dalton l.10275-10277) are omitted, so the sentence hangs as background. Ask what the story would lose without it: little. Cut it, or give it one linked clause.
- **G-n3. "Only his own account survives."** This is an absence claim no source states. "The account is his own" claims only what the sources show.
- **G-n4. "Two centuries later Paul the Deacon..."** No context on who Paul was. The draft could say "the Lombard historian Paul the Deacon"; Foulke's header supports that.
- **G-n5. The close's stock phrase.** "Much of what we know ... comes from Gregory's own pen" leans on a stock legacy formula. It also widens Brehaut's "these events" (the end of Rome and the rise of the Frankish state) to "the church in Gaul". The widening is defensible, but the sentence is the most generic one in the brief.
- **G-n6. Brehaut named twice.** He is named twice, once with no context. G-3's fix resolves this.

### Check D: the five craft tests, Gregory, beside the bar

1. **Good storytelling: PASS.** The line in one sentence: a bishop wrote down his age because no one else would, and in that record told how he and a fellow bishop turned back a king who tried to decree the Trinity. That can be retold. Weakest point: the opening scene (the ruined cathedral) does not return. Ephrem opens and closes on the famine.
2. **Flow: FAIL in paragraph 1 (G-3).** The weakest link: "...and Gregory rebuilt it larger than it had been. He came from Clermont in Auvergne, Ernest Brehaut says." Model: Origen, "In his eighteenth year he took charge of teaching the faith in Alexandria, because everyone else had fled." Elsewhere the joins are sound and sourced: "So the bishop wrote it himself" follows the laments, and "So the king desisted" follows Salvius.
3. **No AI tells: PASS.** The most assistant-like sentence is "Much of what we know of the church in Gaul ... comes from Gregory's own pen" (G-n5). It is stock, but it is a sourced claim, not a device. Model: John Chrysostom's first paragraph. The draft's paragraph 3 reaches that level of plainness.
4. **No hanging sentences: FAIL (G-3).** The Clermont line connects to nothing. The envoy line is borderline (G-n2). The last line finishes the recorder story and is not a tag. Model close: Ephrem, "a month later, Palladius says, he died there."
5. **Everything relevant: FAIL (G-2).** The idea at the centre of the stance is missing. The envoy and the doubled Brehaut are side matter. Model: Athanasius, "If the Son were only a creature...".

## BENEDICT OF NURSIA

### Check A: sources and quotes

**Quotes, re-verified word for word:**

- "a school for the service of God, in which we hope nothing harsh nor burdensome will be ordained": Gasquet l.768-770. Matches.
- "stability, amendment of manners and obedience": Gasquet l.3595-3596. Matches.
- "that the strong may have their scope and the weak be not scared": Gasquet l.3960-3961. Matches.
- "this little Rule written for beginners": Gasquet l.4299-4300. Matches.
- "excellent for discretion and also eloquent for the style": Gardner l.5565-5566. Matches.
- "the holy man could not otherwise teach, than himself lived": Gardner l.5569-5570. Matches.
- "stability in the community": Gasquet l.399-400. Matches.

**Claims checked and supported:**

- Nursia, study at Rome, loose living, left his books and his father's house: Gardner l.3411-3420.
- Three years in the cave at Subiaco, almost forty miles from Rome, Romanus, the rope and the bell: Gardner l.3487-3511.
- Four disciples as Gregory's sources: Gardner l.3424-3434.
- The poisoned wine and the broken glass: Gardner l.3655-3685.
- Totila's test through Riggo, then Totila falling to the ground: Gardner l.4417-4471. The draft's "came to test him and fell" compresses two steps, but it is within Gregory's telling.
- Cassino, Apollo, the idol broken, the oratory of St. Martin in the temple: Gardner l.4167-4181.
- Lombards entering at night and spoiling all things: Gardner l.4657-4659.
- Gregory's purpose (miracles show how virtue is "declared and made manifest"): Gardner l.1510-1516. These lines are spoken by Peter in the dialogue and accepted by Gregory; close enough.
- The last chapter's Collations, Institutes, Lives and "Rule of our Holy Father St. Basil": Gasquet l.4283-4286.
- Gasquet's reading that the Collations are Cassian's: Gasquet l.283.
- 742, Carloman's capitulary, "ut monachi et ancillae Dei monasteriales iuxta regulam sancti Benedicti ... vivere": MGH Capitularia (pertz1881) l.2324-2325.
- 802, Lorsch annals, "legerunt regulam sancti patris Benedicti" at Aachen: l.7790-7796.
- Gasquet's "practically the only monastic Rule of the West" from the ninth to the twelfth century: Gasquet l.580-581.
- **Wonders:** every wonder is told as Gregory's ("In Gregory's telling", "Gregory says", "Gregory writes"). Mark's ruling is met.

**Dates:**

- **"About fifty years after Benedict died"** comes from two modern dates: Gardner's 593 (l.1024-1025) and the traditional 543 (Gardner's notes, Gasquet l.201-203). The file header gives about 547 as the death year, which makes it 46 years. "About fifty" holds on both counts, so the brief claims only what both sides accept. Non-blocking: see B-n1.

### Check B: the causal link

The stance is there: the Rule's "school", stability, and an abbot who tempers all things. The adoption is there: 742, 802, and from the ninth century the only Rule. The link between them is falsely denied, then handed to a 1909 editor.

**B-1. BLOCKING. A false honest limit, on the causal link itself.**

- **Sentence:** "The record shows the Franks took it up but does not say why." Then: "Gasquet judges that the Rule brought 'stability in the community' and put a written code in place of an abbot's personal will."
- **Checked:** the held file says why, in the Franks' own words.
  - The Estinnes capitulary, the year after Carloman's 742 capitulary, in the same file: "Abbates et monachi receperunt sancti patris Benedicti [regulam] ad restaurandam normam regularis vitae". In English: the abbots and monks received the Rule to restore the norm of the regular life (pertz1881 l.2449-2451).
  - Pippin's Soissons capitulary of 744: the church's rule "is to be recovered, which in the days of earlier princes had been scattered and collapsed" ("quae in diebus priorum principum dissipata corruit", l.2518-2519).
  - Boniface's letter, quoted in the editor's preface to the 742 capitulary: Carloman wanted to correct and amend church religion "trampled and scattered" for sixty or seventy years (l.2202-2205).
  - The 802 Lorsch annals add that Charlemagne ordered that anything done in monasteries against the Rule be corrected by the Rule (l.7814-7815). The draft omits this.
- **Problem:** This is the reason, and the consequence of Benedict's stance as it played out, carried by witnesses of the time. Mark's source ruling asks for exactly this. The draft instead tells the listener that no such witness exists. It then closes on the 1909 judgment of a modern Benedictine editor, Gasquet, whose leaning is not marked.
- **Fix:** rewrite paragraph 4 so the adoption carries its stated reason. The Frankish rulers and their bishops, through Boniface, Carloman and the 743/744 capitularies, said their monasteries had decayed. They took up Benedict's Rule "to restore the norm of the regular life". In 802 Charlemagne ordered that whatever went against the Rule be corrected by it. Say who held that it worked (Gasquet) in one clause or on the card, marked as a Benedictine editor's view. Remove the false sentence.

### Check C: shape and the three elements

- **Order.** Paragraphs 1 and 2 are personal (through Gregory, with the source framed plainly). Paragraph 3 is the stance, from his own words. Paragraph 4 is the impact. That is personal first, then impact, which is right.
- **Time, place and what the world faced.** This is thin. "Italy was at war" is unattributed and has no content. It rests on Gardner's introduction (l.1190-1191) and on Gregory's "in the time of the Goths" (l.4417), so it is not wrong, but it does no work.
- **The thread, checked in the census.**
  - Inherited: Lives of the Fathers (the desert-monasticism edge in the census), Basil of Caesarea (in the census), John Cassian (in the census), Martin of Tours (in the census). These are woven into paragraph 3 through the Rule's last chapter. Placing Cassian depends on an editor aside.
  - Passed on: Gregory the Great (in the census), Carloman (not a census person, and unidentified: B-3), Charlemagne (in the census). The tradition edge "early-benedictine-italian-monasticism to carolingian-reform-christianity" supports 802.
  - Paul the Deacon is in the census and would carry the Lombard sack and the restoration at Cassino under the Rule (Foulke l.7805-7813, l.12466-12479). He is not used.
- **The change, in the close.** The close is told through Gasquet, not through the held witnesses of the time (B-1).

### Findings

**B-1. BLOCKING.** Above, under check B.

**B-2. BLOCKING. Item 1: a claim no witness carries; it also cuts against the Rule's own words.**

- **Sentence:** "He did not claim to begin anything new."
- **Checked:** Gasquet l.4270-4300, l.283-287 and l.405-409. The Rule calls itself a Rule "for beginners" and points the advanced to the Fathers and Basil. Gasquet says Benedict "never intended to prohibit other customs".
- **Problem:** No source says Benedict made no claim to newness. The Prologue says the opposite in form: "We are therefore now about to institute a school for the service of God" (l.768). The sentence is the narrator's own reading, set as a short beat.
- **Fix:** rewrite paragraph 3's last three sentences so that they say what the Rule says. He called it "this little Rule written for beginners" and sent anyone seeking more to the Lives of the Fathers, Basil of Caesarea's Rule and the Collations. The thread then stands on the Rule's own words.

**B-3. BLOCKING. Item 8 (common names), and Mark's "no statements that have no context".**

- **Sentence:** "In 742 Carloman's council told monks and nuns to live by it."
- **Checked:** pertz1881 l.2199-2203. Boniface calls him "Carlomannus dux Francorum".
- **Problem:** Carloman is a common Carolingian name; Charlemagne's brother is another. The listener is not told who he was, or that he is the link between the Franks and the Rule.
- **Fix:** "Carloman, duke of the Franks," at first mention, from the file. This folds into the B-1 rewrite.

**B-4. BLOCKING. Craft test 2 (flow) and test 5 (relevance): the wonders run as a list.**

- **Passage:** paragraph 2, sentences 4 to 6, the poisoned wine, Totila, and Cassino.
- **Problem:** Three episodes stand side by side, joined only by "and" and by "Italy was at war, and". Each is told as Gregory's, which is right. But the links the sources give are left out.
  - The poisoning happens because Benedict "took order that regular life should be observed" (Gardner l.3662-3667). The same man later writes that "nothing harsh nor burdensome" will be ordained. That is the story's own turn, and it is not drawn.
  - Totila's point in Gregory, the rebuke and "from that time forward he was nothing so cruel" (l.4473-4485), is dropped. What is left is a bare prostration.
- **Weakest link:** "Italy was at war, and Gregory says that even the Gothic king Totila came to test him and fell to the ground before him." Model: Ephrem, "Ephrem feared the people would absorb them, so he wrote hymns that held the church's doctrine and used the same melodies."
- **Fix:** rewrite paragraph 2 around one or two wonders that the story uses. The strict abbot poisoned by his monks sets up the Rule's mildness. Cassino, as the place of the Rule, is the other. Cut or link Totila. This also brings the length nearer two minutes.

**NON-BLOCKING findings:**

- **B-n1. "About fifty years after".** The phrase rests on modern dates told flat. It is safe, because both datings give about fifty. To keep to the pattern's letter, put "by Edmund Gardner's date" on the card.
- **B-n2. "Gregory the Great" has no context.** The brief never says he was pope. "Pope Gregory the Great" (Gardner's title page) costs one word.
- **B-n3. "...but the Rule went on."** A bridging line with no named witness. Gregory's own text implies it (he points readers to the Rule after the sack). Paul the Deacon carries it outright: Petronax restored Cassino under the Rule, and Zacharias gave him Benedict's own copy (Foulke l.12466-12479). Name Paul, a census person, and the thread gains a link.
- **B-n4. Paragraph 3's opening thesis.** "The Rule is Benedict's own words, and in it he set down what he stood for." This opens on a thesis (item 4). Its first half is needed, under Mark's ruling that the Rule is his own text. The second half is an announcement.
- **B-n5. "He was Benedict of Nursia."** A three-word who-line, close to the template naming-sentence signal. Acceptable, as in the old brief.
- **B-n6. Gasquet's leaning.** He is a Benedictine editor and is not marked as one. The census already describes Hunter Blair that way.

### Check D: the five craft tests, Benedict, beside the bar

1. **Good storytelling: PASS.** The line in one sentence: a young man fled Rome's schools, and his life comes to us only through Gregory's book of wonders; the short Rule for beginners he left became the law the Franks laid on their monasteries. That can be retold. Model: Athanasius, from the severed hand to the exiles. The draft holds its line but sags in paragraph 2.
2. **Flow: FAIL (B-4).** The weakest link is quoted above, beside Ephrem.
3. **No AI tells: PASS, narrowly.** The weakest sentence: "The record shows the Franks took it up but does not say why." It is source-talk in status-announcement register (and false: B-1). Also "He did not claim to begin anything new" (B-2), a short punch-beat. Model: John Chrysostom's opening, which has no devices.
4. **No hanging sentences: PASS.** The close, "From the ninth to the twelfth century, he says, it was practically the only monastic Rule of the West", finishes the story and is not a tag. "He did not claim to begin anything new" is a beat; B-2 deals with it. Model: John Chrysostom's close, the body carried home.
5. **Everything relevant: FAIL (B-1 and B-4).** The why-as-idea is present: stability, and an abbot who tempers all things so the strong have scope and the weak are not scared. But the reason the Franks gave is replaced by "does not say why" and a modern editor's verdict. Paragraph 2 carries wonders that do not serve the line. The Collations aside ("which his editor Francis Gasquet takes to be John Cassian's") is source-talk kept only to place Cassian. Model: Methodius, which weighs a thin record in a few sentences and moves on, as this draft's paragraph 2 opening does well.
