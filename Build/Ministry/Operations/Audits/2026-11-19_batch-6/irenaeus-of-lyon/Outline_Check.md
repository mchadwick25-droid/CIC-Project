# Outline check: Irenaeus of Lyon

## The heart, written before reading the outline

From the census entry (`why`, `legacy`, `voices`, `sourcing`), the source sheet and what Irenaeus is known for.

**The essence.** As a boy in the province of Asia, Irenaeus heard Polycarp. By 177 he was a presbyter at Lyon in Gaul; the imprisoned martyrs sent him to Rome with their letter, and after the persecution he followed Pothinus as bishop. There he wrote the five books *Against Heresies*, the fullest answer we still have to the followers of Valentinus. Against their claim of a hidden, higher teaching he set what every church held openly: the one faith received at baptism, the churches the apostles founded with their bishops counted in order, and four Gospels, no more and no fewer. He is on the tree because that answer became the frame later writers used, and because for centuries his book was almost all anyone knew of the Valentinians, which makes it an opponent's witness.

**Also told.** The mosaic of the king rearranged into a fox (his image for how they used Scripture); his letter to Florinus and the memory of where Polycarp sat; one clause on his letter to Victor in the Easter quarrel (the Polycarp and Polycrates stories tell it); later writers drew on him (Hippolytus, Tertullian, Epiphanius, who kept the Greek of Book I, and Eusebius); his list of Rome's bishops was later argued over (Contested); his death is not known, and the martyrdom is late legend.

## Verdict: FIX

The outline's heart matches mine and the approved heart (Hearts.md, 2026-11-19): Polycarp heard as a boy, bishop of Lyon after 177, *Against Heresies* as the fullest surviving answer to the Valentinians, and the three answers: the faith received at baptism, the apostles' churches with their bishops, and four Gospels. It also carries the 177 embassy and the later witnesses. The rulings are kept: Polycarp's boyhood and the Victor letter are a clause each, the memory of where Polycarp sat stays here, no barred quote is used, and Valentinus's teaching gets one clause. The beats give the heart in the first minute and follow the life in order. I opened every quote at its lines and rendered it before comparing. Quotes 1–3 are faithful. Three claims go beyond their sources and must be fixed before writing.

## BLOCKING

**B1. Epiphanius did not copy "his first book in full."** What came 1, What came 2 and the Connection say he did. The held copy runs from the preface to I.11.1 and closes "Here end the words of Irenaeus against the Valentinians" (HOLL 29984–32949; ANF 31554 is the end of I.11.1, out of 31 chapters running to ANF 34224). The translators' note says only that Epiphanius keeps "the preface of Irenaeus, and most of the first book" (ANF-ed 29789–29793). McGiffert credits the Greek of "nearly the whole of the first book" to "numerous extracts made from it by later writers" (EUS-ed 24533–24536). Epiphanius also summarised part of Valentinus's teaching himself first, and only left "the rest" to Irenaeus (HOLL 29962–29970).
*Correction.* What came 2: "About two hundred years later Epiphanius, writing against the same teachers, gave up setting out the rest of their teaching himself and copied Irenaeus's long opening account of them, word for word. That copy is the main way the Greek of the first book survives; the whole work lives only in an old Latin translation (I2; HOLL 29966–29981, 32949; ANF-ed 29789–29793)." What came 1 and the Connection: replace "copied his first book in full / whole" with "copied his opening account of them word for word". Do not say: add "Not 'Epiphanius copied the whole first book'. He copied its opening chapters, his account of the Valentinians (HOLL 29984–32949)."

**B2. The letter to Florinus was not written against Valentinus's teaching.** Beat 7 says Florinus "was being drawn to Valentinus's teaching", and then that Irenaeus wrote that the elders "never handed him such teachings". The Connection says he "used that memory against a man ... who was being drawn to Valentinus". Eusebius keeps the two apart. The letter, *On Monarchy*, answered Florinus's apparent view "That God is not the Author of Evil. For Florinus seemed to be defending this opinion". Then, "because he was being drawn away by the error of Valentinus, Irenæus wrote his work On the Ogdoad" (EUS 30656, 30718–30721). The Polycarp memory comes from "the letter to Florinus, of which we have spoken" (EUS 30779–30781, with McGiffert's note: "The epistle On Monarchy"). A writer following the beat will say the letter was a warning against Valentinus.
*Correction.* Beat 7, after "had become an elder at Rome. He fell from that office": "He seemed, Eusebius says, to teach that God was the author of evil, and he was being drawn toward Valentinus's teaching (E8; EUS 30656, 30718–30721). Irenaeus wrote to him that the elders before them, who had known the apostles, never handed him such teachings ..." Connection, second point: "he used that memory in a letter to a man he had seen at Polycarp's side, who seemed to teach that God was the author of evil and was being drawn to Valentinus (T7, E8)". Do not say: add "Not 'he wrote to Florinus to turn him from Valentinus'. The letter answered the view that God is the author of evil; a different, lost book answered his drift to Valentinus (EUS 30656, 30718–30721, 30779–30781)."

**B3. Ending (b) gives the wrong witness for the martyrdom.** It says "only a writer four centuries later made him a martyr". McGiffert: "Jerome is the first to call him a martyr", in a commentary on Isaiah written some years after his *Lives*, about two centuries later. "Gregory of Tours first gives us a detailed account" (EUS-ed 24501–24508). That is the sheet's own E10.
*Correction.* Ending (b): "no early writer says how or when he died. About two centuries later a writer first called him a martyr, and the first full account of a martyrdom comes from Gregory of Tours, four centuries on (E10; EUS-ed 24501–24508; GT 1087–1089; in Gregory's voice, credited)."

## MINOR

**M1. The essence's "in an unbroken line"** goes beyond Irenaeus and Mark's approved heart ("counted in order"). Irenaeus writes "the succession of these men to our own times" (ANF 39770–39772). On a Contested subject (I5), "unbroken" carries a claim. *Correction:* "the churches the apostles founded, with their bishops counted one after another back to the apostles".

**M2. The Photius citation in What came 1.** Photius's "It is said" is about Irenaeus being Polycarp's pupil (PHOT 11589). Of Hippolytus he says flatly "Hippolytus, the pupil of Irenaeus" (PHOT 11632). *Correction:* replace "(I1; Photius, ninth century, 'it is said')" with "(I1; Photius, ninth century, PHOT 11632: a late report)". The narrator's "a much later writer says" stands. The same correction goes on the sheet's I1.

**M3. Quoted phrases beyond the three quotes.** What came 1 has Hippolytus's "the blessed elder Irenaeus" and Tertullian's "a very exact inquirer into all doctrines" in quotation marks, but the Quotes preamble allows only "the twelfth place from the apostles" and "without paper or ink" as short phrases. *Correction:* add to the preamble: "'The blessed elder' (Hippolytus, HIPP 10028, 'presbyter' rendered 'elder') and 'a very exact inquirer' (Tertullian, TERT 43167) may stand as short phrases." On the tree neither is used elsewhere, and the Hippolytus outline leaves "the blessed elder" to this story.

**M4. Beat 2 contradicts Leave out, and tells events out of order.** Leave out says the Lyon martyrs get "two sentences". Beat 2 has five, and it tells the ashes swept into the Rhone before the letter sent "while still in prison" (EUS 27674–27680). *Correction:* tell the prison and Pothinus's death, then the letters and Irenaeus sent to Rome, then the deaths and the ashes, in about three sentences. "Some were beheaded, others cast to the wild beasts" is Eusebius's summary of the letter (EUS 27740–27744), not the letter's own words, so credit it to Eusebius or fold it in without a quotation.

**M5. Length.** At about 500 words, the beats, the three quotes (about 170 words) and a third for What came will not fit. A plain count of the beats as written comes to well over 700. The length is in Mark's approved heart, so tighten rather than raise it. Make the "cut first" items standing cuts (beat 5's "paper or ink", Theodoret, the Easter clause down to a few words). Apply M4. Give beat 3's Marcus sentence by role in one clause (see M6). Drop Photius from What came 1. If it still runs past about 3:40, that goes to Mark with the reason, not to a faster read.

**M6. Marcus is named but not listed** under Names, and he neither acts nor links. *Correction:* beat 3, "the followers of one teacher of that school had led many women astray 'in our own district of the Rhone', he says". Keep the Do not say line that this was not said of the Valentinians in general.

**M7. Quote 3's cut point drops the chain to John.** The Connection's second point rests on "his time with John and the others who had seen the Lord". *Correction:* "If the story runs long, the writer cuts the middle ('his comings and goings ... his talks to the people'), never the last sentence."

**M8. The Gospel of Truth and 1945.** Beat 6 gives the Valentinians' "recent book" called the Gospel of Truth (ANF 41173–41178), and What came 3 has "among them a Gospel of Truth" found in 1945. Heard together, they make the two one book, and no held source says so. The finds are not held, and the Valentinus outline keeps the 1945 find and the dispute over its author. *Correction:* What came 3, "Then, in 1945, books of their own were found in Egypt" (no title). Do not say: "Not that the book he named was found in 1945."

**M9. "Known" against "seen."** The Connection says "a man he had known at Polycarp's side", but he says only "I saw thee in lower Asia with Polycarp" (EUS 30792–30805). *Correction:* "seen", as in beat 7.

**M10. Opening 1 says "swears".** Use "insists", as in the Quote 1 rendering. The Greek is διορίζοιτο καὶ λέγοι (HOLL 31910–31911): "declare and say", not an oath.

**M11. Who tells it, Eusebius "about a century and a half later".** The sheet gives about 130 years. *Correction:* "more than a century later".

**M12. The papacy line in Do not say contradicts itself.** It bars "the bishops of Rome" and then allows "the bishops of Rome, one after another". *Correction:* "Not 'the first list of popes', or anything that makes it a papacy. Say 'Rome's bishops, one after another'."

**M13. Library sweep: one held witness missing.** `acta-martyrum-selecta_gebhardt1902.txt` holds the Moscow epilogue of the *Martyrdom of Polycarp* (lines 1039–1061). It says Irenaeus taught many at Rome when Polycarp died, and heard there a voice like a trumpet saying "Polycarp has been martyred". McGiffert notes it (EUS-ed 24477–24485). The same file holds the Greek of the letter of Vienne and Lyon (from line 1708). Nothing in the outline rests on either, but a writer could use the Rome claim. *Correction:* add to Leave out: "The Moscow epilogue's claims that he was at Rome when Polycarp died and heard a voice announce it: a later epilogue, and a wonder." Add to Do not say: "Not 'he was in Rome when Polycarp died'." Record both on the sheet's sweep.
