Against: Brief-Kit `Kit.md`, `roles/reviewer.md`, the batch 6 Reviewer prompt, `Hearts.md` (Irenaeus), Decision-Log 2026-11-19 ("Batch 6: …" entries, "Change order: the stories approved before the kit…", and "Batch 6: Irenaeus's length": about 4:30 to 5:00), `Outline.md`, `Source_Sheet.md`, `Outline_Check.md`, `cic/texts/`.

## The test, from one hearing

1. **Who:** Irenaeus, a bishop at Lyon in Roman Gaul about 180, who as a boy had heard Polycarp.
2. **What happened:** after the persecution of 177 he became bishop. He then wrote five books against the followers of Valentinus, setting three open things against their secret teaching: the faith received at baptism, the bishops of the apostles' churches, counted one after another, and four Gospels.
3. **Why the connection matters:** his is the fullest answer we still have. Later writers built on it, and his account was how the Valentinians were known until their own books were found in 1945. His memory of Polycarp made the chain back to the apostles a living one.

All three lines are clear from one hearing.

## The heart

**My own essence, written before reading the heart:** Irenaeus, bishop of Lyon in Gaul in the late second century, had heard Polycarp as a boy. He wrote the fullest answer we still have to the followers of Valentinus, who claimed a secret, higher teaching. Against them he set an open faith anyone could check: the rule received at baptism, the churches the apostles founded with their bishops counted in order, and four Gospels. **Also told:** the Lyon persecution that made him bishop; the letter to Florinus and the memory of Polycarp; later writers who drew on him; that he is an opponent's witness to the Valentinians; that his Rome list is argued over.

This matches the approved heart and the outline's essence. The outline's essence has three parts: the Polycarp boyhood; bishop of Lyon after the martyrs of 177; and the book with its three answers.

- The Polycarp boyhood comes at word 196 ("…Polycarp of Smyrna.").
- "Bishop after the martyrs of 177" comes only at word 288 ("Irenaeus became bishop after Pothinus"), about two minutes in.

The essence is therefore complete at **word 288**. That is past about 150, so it is BLOCKING (finding B1).

## BLOCKING

**B1. The essence is not complete in the first minute.** The Polycarp boyhood lands at word 196. "Bishop of Lyon after the martyrs of 177" lands only at word 288. The outline's essence asks for both, and the approved heart puts them first. The essence may reach ahead (Kit Part 3), so 177 can be named in the essence and told in full at its place.

Smallest change, keeping the opening quote and everything after paragraph 4:
- Replace "That, wrote a bishop in Roman Gaul about the year 180, is what the followers of Valentinus did with Scripture." with "That, wrote Irenaeus, bishop of Lyon in Roman Gaul, about the year 180, is what the followers of Valentinus did with Scripture."
- Replace "The bishop was Irenaeus of Lyon, and his five books against them are the fullest answer we still have." with "As a boy he had heard the old bishop Polycarp of Smyrna, and he became bishop himself after a persecution in 177. His five books against these teachers are the fullest answer we still have."
- Delete paragraph 4 ("As a boy, in the Roman province of Asia, on the west coast of today's Turkey, he had heard the old bishop Polycarp of Smyrna."). It would otherwise be told twice. "Asia" is then not heard, so it needs no gloss. The story still never says he was born in or grew up in Smyrna.

With these changes, the whole essence is complete at about word 188. The rest of the overage is the outline's chosen opening: a 51-word quote and its required 21-word mosaic gloss. About 188 words (about 75 seconds) is the closest this opening allows, and I judge it acceptable. If it is not accepted, the outline's opening has to change, not the story.

The later sentence "Irenaeus became bishop after Pothinus, Eusebius says, …" stays. It is the in-order telling, with its credit, and the kit allows the essence's reach-ahead. These changes save about 12 words.

**Do not say line to add to the outline:** "Do not leave the Polycarp boyhood or 'bishop of Lyon after the martyrs of 177' until after the first minute. Name both in the essence, before the three answers are told; the persecution is then told in full at beat 2."

## MINOR

**M1. Held-back name reveal (Kit Part 1).** "wrote a bishop in Roman Gaul … The bishop was Irenaeus of Lyon" turns on a reveal. B1's first replacement fixes it. If B1 is fixed some other way, still name him in the turn: "That, wrote Irenaeus, bishop of Lyon in Roman Gaul, about the year 180, …"

**M2. "Gaul" without its plain sense.** The outline's Words to explain gives "Roman Gaul, today's France". The gloss is missing at the first hearing. It is not BLOCKING, because "Lyon" and "on the river Rhône" place it for most listeners. Fix: "bishop of Lyon in Roman Gaul, today's France, about the year 180" (+2 words). That sentence is then 27 words.

**M3. The record's force is softened.** "Others died for their faith, and their ashes were swept into the Rhône" turns "put to death, by the sword or the wild beasts … burned" (E3) into "died". Fix: replace "Others died for their faith, and their ashes were swept into the Rhône," with "Others were put to death, and their burned remains were swept into the Rhône," (+1 word; the sentence is 26 words).

**M4. The year is credited to the wrong witness.** "In the year 177, the survivors wrote, a persecution struck …" credits the year to the letter. The year comes from Eusebius's dating, with McGiffert's reckoning (S4, EUS 26706–26707). The letter gives no year. Fix: "In the year 177 a persecution struck the Christians of Lyon and Vienne, on the river Rhône. Their bishop, Pothinus, more than ninety years old, the survivors wrote, was beaten and died in prison."

**M5. Dangling "carried by Irenaeus".** In "From prison they sent letters to Rome about a quarrel that troubled the churches, carried by Irenaeus, …", heard once, "carried by" attaches to "the churches". Fix together with cut C1: "From prison they asked Irenaeus, one of the elders who led the church with its bishop, to carry their letters to Rome." This keeps the elder gloss and saves about 6 words. It keeps the Do not say lines: not that he wrote the letter, and nothing on the quarrel's side.

## Length

Mark's ruling is about 4:30 to 5:00. At 788 words and about 150 a minute, the story runs about 5:15.

Asked of each part whether the story needs it, almost everything earns its place. It serves the heart, the three Also-told answers, the record's force (Pothinus, the ashes and the taunt, his "madness and blasphemy" and "wolves"), a plain sense (Valentinus, elder, rule, mosaic, "one after another"), or the connection (Florinus and Quote 3, the later writers, 1945).

Parts it does not need, as MINOR cuts:
- **C1.** "about a quarrel that troubled the churches" (7 words). The errand needs no subject here; the outline already keeps the quarrel's side out. Folded into M5.
- **C2.** "and lost that office", after "had become an elder at Rome" (4 words). Florinus's fall does nothing in this story. His drift and his view of evil carry the scene.

With B1 (about −12), M2/M3 (+3) and C1/C2 (about −10), the story comes to about 765 words, **about 5:05**. That is the length the story needs. It holds the outline's three answers, each in its own sentence or his own words, the 177 setting of his succession, the Florinus memory that carries the connection, and the later writers and 1945 that carry what came of him. Further cuts would take a must-keep item or the record's force. The overage of about 5 seconds is within Mark's "take what it needs", and nothing should be cut to meet 5:00 exactly.
