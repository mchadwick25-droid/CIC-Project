## The test, from one hearing

1. **Who:** Polycarp, an old bishop of Smyrna in the Roman province of Asia in the second century, who, Irenaeus wrote, had known people who had seen Jesus.
2. **What happened:** hunted in a persecution and betrayed by a slave under torture, he refused to curse Christ and was burned; his church says the fire curved round him, and an executioner killed him with a dagger. His church kept the day at his bones. He and Anicetus of Rome differed over Easter but kept communion, and his letter to Philippi survives.
3. **Why the connection matters:** he links the apostles' generation to Irenaeus's. His peace with Anicetus was the precedent Irenaeus used against Victor's attempt to cut off Asia, and his letter was still read in Asia more than two centuries later.

## Findings

### B1. BLOCKING: a jump back after the death

Paragraph 7 opens "Years before, Polycarp had visited Rome", right after the story has told his death, his bones and the yearly gathering. That is the ending and then back. A listener has to re-place the Rome visit before the death on one hearing, which is where I got lost. The source check (S22) also cautioned that "Years before" claims a gap that no source gives.

**Smallest fix** (a move and two phrase changes; no other wording changes):
1. Move these four sentences out of paragraph 7 and set them as their own paragraph between paragraph 2 ("...who had seen Jesus.") and paragraph 3 ("In a time of persecution..."): "Years before, Polycarp had visited Rome, where Anicetus was bishop. The two could not agree about keeping Easter. Polycarp kept it as he had with John and the other apostles he had known. Anicetus said he must follow the customs of the elders before him. "Even so, they shared communion," Irenaeus wrote, and the two parted in peace."
2. In the moved paragraph, change "Years before," to "Earlier,". The source check said "Earlier" claims nothing about the length of the gap.
3. The Victor sentences stay where they are, after the bones paragraph, as what came of the peace. Change "Decades later Victor, another bishop of Rome," to "Decades after that visit, Victor, another bishop of Rome,". On Eusebius's own figures the interval from the visit is about 21 to 32 years before Victor even took office (source check S28). Measured from the death it is less secure, so the time phrase has to keep the visit as its point of reference.

Paragraph 3's "had shouted" can stay. Its pluperfect still points back from the stadium moment in the hook.

**Do not say** (no outline exists, so this goes to the card's source sheet for any later rework): "Do not tell the Rome visit after his death. It comes before the persecution. Victor's attempt is told after the death, as what came of the peace, and is dated from the visit ('Decades after that visit'), never from the death."

### B2. BLOCKING: "atheists" without its plain sense

The text reads: "In a time of persecution, the crowd had shouted, "Away with the atheists!" They demanded that Polycarp be found." Heard today, "atheists" means people who deny God, and the listener is not told why a crowd would call Christians that. Earlier the gloss was carried by "whom they called the destroyer of their gods". That phrase was rightly moved to the stadium (source check S8), and the move left the word bare. The settled rendering (RC Q7, cited in source check S7) asks for one gloss: people who reject the gods. Without it, a second-language listener can mishear who is being shouted at.

**Smallest fix:** change "Away with the atheists!" They demanded" to: "Away with the atheists!", meaning the Christians, who would not worship their gods. They demanded".
- Source: Mart. 3.2, the crowd's cry about "the godly race of Christians" (ANF 3830–3835); Mart. 12.2, "teaching many not to sacrifice, or to worship the gods" (ANF 4055–4058).
- The sentence becomes 21 words.

**Do not say:** "Do not use 'atheists' without its plain sense the first time: the Christians, who would not worship the gods."

### M1. MINOR: a held-back name reveal

"An old man gave that answer ... The old man was Polycarp, the bishop of the church there." The story turns on a held-back reveal, which Part 1 says is not a stock hinge.

**Rewording:**
- Change "An old man gave that answer" to "Polycarp, an old man, gave that answer". The sentence becomes 24 words.
- Change "The old man was Polycarp, the bishop of the church there." to "He was the bishop of the church there."

The source check's caution S3 still holds: "old man" stays separate from the eighty-six years, and nothing here makes the 86 his age.

### M2. MINOR: "competed" has no plain sense

"to remember those who have already competed, and to train and prepare those still to come." The image is the athletes' word (ANF note 471: literally "been athletes"). Heard once, a listener may not know who "competed", or in what.

**Smallest fix:** close the quotation after "competed," and add the plain sense in narration before the rest: "to remember those who have already competed," the martyrs before him, "and to train and prepare those still to come." Nothing inside the quotation is cut.

### M3. MINOR: the ending is on Jerome, not on Polycarp

The last sentence is Jerome's report. Part 1 asks for an ending on the person, here his own words.

**Smallest fix:** swap the last two sentences, with no change to their wording: "Polycarp's letter to the church at Philippi survives. More than two hundred years later, Jerome wrote that it was still read when the churches of Asia met. It calls its readers back from false teaching to "the word handed down to us from the beginning.""

### M4. MINOR: a name heard once

"John" ("with John and the other apostles he had known") is heard once and does not act. He has no page of his own in the census.

No change is recommended. John is the substance of Irenaeus's claim (EUS 31603–31606; ANF 54654–54657), and every listener knows the name. Listed because the role requires it.

### M5. MINOR: a cut for length (optional)

"They asked for a lion, but the beast shows were over, so they shouted for him to be burned."
- Could become "They shouted for him to be burned." This saves 13 words.
- The lion is not needed for the heart, for what is also told, or for the connection. The crowd's fury, which is the record's force, stays in "cried out against him as the destroyer of their gods".
- Keep the sentence if the scene's movement from beasts to fire is wanted.
