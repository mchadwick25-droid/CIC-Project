# Outline check: Bardaisan of Edessa

## My account of the heart, written before reading the outline

From the census entry (teaser, card), the batch job and the source sheet.

**The essence.** Bardaisan, a learned Christian teacher of Edessa writing in Syriac around 200, argued that people are free: nature rules the body and fate, the stars, rules fortune, but each person's choices are their own, and the laws of different countries, and the Christians' one law in every land, prove the stars do not compel. A pupil wrote his argument down as a dialogue, the *Book of the Laws of Countries*, and it is the one text in his voice. He is on the tree as the first Syriac Christian teacher whose thought survives, and as the man later Christians called a heretic, whom Ephrem answered in prose and in song.

**Also told.**
- How he taught: by being asked questions, in a circle of pupils; Avida's question (why did God not make us unable to sin?) and the harp-and-ship answer. Documented (OWN).
- He does not deny fate; he limits it to fortune and the body. He admits he once followed the astrologers. Documented (OWN).
- The laws of countries as evidence, told with their force (death for suspected adultery, Abgar's order to cut off hands). Documented (OWN).
- His wider work: many pupils, dialogues against Marcion's followers written in Syriac, translated into Greek by his pupils (Eusebius, LATER). *On Fate* passed into Greek (Eusebius's extract, the *Recognitions*) and was praised by Jerome. Documented as reports; the extracts Widely Accepted (EDITOR).
- Who wrote the dialogue: a pupil, soon after his death; how closely it keeps his views is a modern judgment. Widely Accepted / Dominant Modern Reconstruction.
- Called a heretic, with the witnesses disagreeing on the order: Eusebius (Valentinian first, then left, not wholly clean; yet "a powerful defender of the faith"), Epiphanius (good churchman first, then fell), Hippolytus (Valentinus's eastern school). Contested.
- Ephrem's attack in prose and verse; the songs (Bardaisan's, says Ephrem; his son Harmonius's, say Sozomen and Theodoret) whose meters Ephrem took for the church. Documented as reports; whose songs, Contested. A clause here, since `ephrem-the-syrian` tells it.
- Optional, Contested: Epiphanius's report that he refused to deny he was a Christian before a companion of "Antoninus".
- Setting: born in the 150s beside the river Daisan, died about 222 in his late sixties; Edessa, with a Christian king Abgar at whose court he stood.

Shape: Teacher or thinker.

## Verdict: FIX

The outline's heart matches mine and Mark's approved heart (`Hearts.md`, 2026-11-19) in substance: the essence names the freedom argument, the pupil's dialogue and its two proofs, and the Also told carries the dialogue's images, the admission about astrology, Eusebius's report, the disputed heresy charge and the songs as a clause. Nothing major is left out. It keeps the batch job ("later called a heretic, and answered in song by Ephrem"), the hearts ruling, and the batch's quote bars (none of its quotes is barred). Every beat, quote and impact line was opened at its cited lines in BLC, EU, EPI, EPH-P, EPH-G, SOZ, THD, JER-L, HIP, REC, CHE and BHE. Most hold as written. Two findings block.

## Findings

### BLOCKING 1. "The Seven" in Ephrem's curse is glossed with the dialogue's sense, not Ephrem's

The held English of Ephrem's line (EPH-G 11261–11263) reads: "Cursed be our trust [if it be] on the Seven; the Æons which Bardaisan confesses!" Only "[if it be]" is bracketed. "The Æons which Bardaisan confesses" is part of the line as Gwynn gives it, and his note (n. 266) glosses it "The Seven *Æons* (or Beings) of Bardesan's heresy". Mitchell's Ephrem has the same reading, restored in brackets: "Bardaisan said [seven Entities constitute] the world" (EPH-P 1549). The source sheet (I2) is wrong to call this only "Gwynn's bracketed gloss". In the dialogue, "the Seven" are stars (BLC 68354–68355). Ephrem's held words make them beings that Bardaisan "confesses", and McGiffert reports that Ephrem charged him with "teaching Polytheism, in effect if not in words", though he thinks the charge came from a misunderstanding (EU 26556–26559). So when the outline tells the writer to give Ephrem's "the Seven" the dialogue's sense, and to set the curse "beside the dialogue's own line that fate rules fortune", the story would have Ephrem cursing astrology. That softens his charge and replaces his meaning with another source's.

Correction:
- **Words to explain, "the Seven"**: replace the entry with: "**the Seven**: in the dialogue, 'those stars which are called the Seven', which astrologers said governed our lives (BLC 68354–68357). In Ephrem's curse they are, in his words, powers 'which Bardaisan confesses' (EPH-G 11261–11263). Give each sense with its own source. Never give Ephrem's line the dialogue's sense, and never say 'planets' or 'aeons'."
- **What came of him 1**: replace "Tell the curse plainly beside the dialogue's own line that fate rules fortune; the narrator adds no comment." with "Tell the curse in Ephrem's own terms: he cursed anyone who trusted in 'the Seven', powers he said Bardaisan confessed, and who said, as Bardaisan did, that rain, dew, snow and the farmer's seed come from them. Do not set it against the dialogue as though the two meant the same thing. The narrator adds no comment."
- **Do not say**: replace the line "'The Seven are the planets' in Ephrem's voice → ..." with "'The Seven are the planets' or 'the stars' in Ephrem's voice → 'the Seven, powers Ephrem says Bardaisan confessed'. The dialogue's 'seven stars' is told only where the dialogue is."

### BLOCKING 2. The Connection asserts a contrast no witness makes

The Connection field says the listener must be told "that the charges were about his teaching on the world, the soul and the body, not about the freedom he argued for." No held witness says this. It is the researcher's synthesis, and it partly runs against the record. Ephrem's curse is aimed at trust in "the Seven" and at what comes from them (EPH-G 11261–11270), and the dialogue's own account of freedom is set against "the compulsion of the Seven" (BLC 68994–68996). A narrator who follows this line would be carrying one side's view, that the freedom argument stood clear of the charges. The tradition entry calls that question open.

Correction: replace the sentence with: "that the charges the witnesses name are, each in its own witness's voice, about the first beings he said the world was made from, the soul, the body's rising, Christ's body and 'the Seven' (I1, I2). The narrator draws no contrast between these charges and the freedom argument, and does not say whether the charges touched it."

### MINOR

1. **Held-back reveal.** Opening 1 ends "The teacher was Bardaisan." That is the reveal the batch 6 ruling says is not a stock hinge, and the text names him at once ("Bardesan came and found us there", BLC 67909–67910). Correction: "...when their teacher, Bardaisan, came in, asked after their host's health, and then asked what they had been talking about, as he always did."
2. **Where the essence falls.** Openings says "Then say the essence" right after the opening. Beat 1 puts the birth, Epiphanius and Eusebius first and then says "Then say the essence." Correction: in beat 1, move "Then say the essence." to the start of the beat, so it reads "Say the essence. Then: born about 155 ...".
3. **The opening scene's details.** The text gives no place for the visit, and it does not call Avida young. It says he "has but lately joined the disciples" and that his "brethren" are "persons of my own age" (BLC 67932, 67961–67962). Correction to Opening 1: change "Some friends are visiting a friend at Edessa" to "Some pupils are visiting a friend". In Also told, change "A young doubter, Avida" to "A newcomer, Avida". Add to Do not say: "'a young man' or 'at Edessa' for the opening scene → 'a newcomer among his pupils'; the text gives no place."
4. **The echo in the ending.** Q3's job, "it brings back the opening's doubter ('the foolish will be convinced')", invites the writer to call Avida foolish. The text's own echo is Avida's "I cannot believe unless I be convinced" (BLC 67976–67977). Correction: change the job to "it echoes Avida's 'I cannot believe unless I be convinced'". Add to Do not say: "Avida as 'the foolish' → never; the dialogue does not call him so."
5. **Words to explain, the peoples.** The plain words do not line up with the terms (the Geli are not "the Persians", and Hatra is a city), and "the priests of India" says more than the text, which calls the Brahmans a "caste" with its own "teaching" (BLC 68629–68631, 68646). Correction: "**Seres**: 'a people of the far east'. **Brahmans**: 'the Brahmans of India', or 'a class in India who keep their own teaching'. **Geli**, **Hatra**: leave out. **Parthians**: 'the Parthians, whose kingdom lay to the east' (BLC 68681)." In beat 4, change "in India the priests eat no meat" to "in India the Brahmans eat no meat".
6. **"Fate" gloss.** The cited lines (BLC 68459–68463) define fate as an order "given to the Potentates and Elements by God", called "Fate and *genesis*" (nativity), and Bardaisan affirms it. Correction: "**fate**: the course God gave the heavenly powers, which shapes a person's fortune from the hour of birth (BLC 68459–68463, 68396)."
7. **Quoted phrases outside the quote list.** "the old heresy" (What came 1; Also told) and "like a most beautiful ship" (What came 1) are in quotation marks but are not among Q1–Q3. Correction: remove the quotation marks and narrate both ("never wholly washed off the stain of his old heresy"; "like a fine ship wrecked at the harbour's edge, with its passengers lost").
8. **Eusebius's "they say".** Eusebius reports the works on persecution as hearsay: "other works which they say he wrote" (EU 26641–26645). Correction to beat 5: "Eusebius reports that he was said to have written works in a time of persecution." Add to Do not say: "Do not link Epiphanius's refusal to the persecution Eusebius mentions, to Caracalla, or to any date (Hort's conjecture, EU n. 1341)."
9. **Who tells it, Epiphanius.** "Often wrong on dates" has no held source. Correction: delete it and keep "the emperor in his story is confused (Holl, EPI 26418–26425)".
10. **What he defended is not marked.** Correction: add "DEFENDED" to What came of him for human freedom against the astrologers' fate (E4–E8, Q2) and for his dialogues against Marcion's followers (E12, Eusebius's report).
11. **The *Recognitions*.** The editor reports that the direction of borrowing was argued: "Some have thought that Bardesanes or his scholar borrowed from the *Recognitions*", though "the opinion has prevailed" the other way (REC 6252–6258). "Romance" is not in the source. Correction to What came 3: "a later Christian book told under Clement's name, the *Recognitions*, has chapters taken from it in altered form, most editors judge".
12. **"The world's first elements."** Ephrem's word is "Entities" (EPH-P 1549, 2072, 2482). Correction in What came 1 and Leave out: "the first beings he said the world was made from".
13. **Jerome's superlative.** The Latin is "In quibus clarissimus ille et fortissimus liber" (JER-L 53437–53439), "among them, that most famous and strongest book". Correction to What came 3: "Jerome ... called it the most famous and strongest of them". This is close to the outline already; keep "among them".
14. **Length.** Set as written, the opening, five beats, four impact items and three quotes (Q2 in two parts) need about 560–620 words, not 440. Correction: either set Length to "about 3 minutes 45 (about 560 words)", or name the cut order: What came 4 (Theodoret's list) first, then Q2's second part, then beat 5's persecution-works clause. Nothing in the beats is filler.
15. **Sibling missed.** The tradition entry `bardaisanite-current` has a documented story, "Avida Will Not Ask, So Bardaisan Answers Anyway", that tells the same opening scene and quotes "wherever they are" from the passage Q2 draws on. Correction: add it under Sibling ("tradition documented story: same opening scene and dialogue. Here the story goes on to his life and the later quarrel. Its quoted phrase 'wherever they are' is not reused; Q2's first part, 'wherever we are', may stand, and its second part is dropped if the overlap check flags it."). Also add to Do not say: "Avida 'sitting apart' or wanting 'to seem to have found the answer himself' → not in the text; he 'was too bashful to ask' (BLC 67944–67946)." The documented story says this.
16. **Library sweep.** The sweep is real: I re-ran the name forms (about 100 files), opened the `bardaisanite-current` bucket, and spot-checked Otto, Routh, Aphrahat, Philoxenus and the Photius and Moses of Khoren files. None of them holds a primary text or major later witness the sheet lacks. One held dating witness is missing: Jerome's Latin of Eusebius's *Chronicle* ("Bardesanes alterius hereseos princeps agnoscitur", `jerome_eusebii-chronici-canones-lat_fotheringham1923.txt` 43062). It agrees with the *Church History*'s placing (D4) and changes no line. Correction: add it to the sheet's Used list under D4. The late witnesses al-Biruni and the *Chronicle of Seert* (7 and 8 hits) should be named in Set aside as late.
