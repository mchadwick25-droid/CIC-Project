## Setting

S1. **Ephesus and the province of Asia.** He was "bishop of the parish of Ephesus" (EUS 19076). "The bishops of Asia, led by Polycrates" (EUS 31310–31311). McGiffert: he "was the leader of the bishops of the province of Asia, in which province alone the Quartodeciman practice was uniformly observed. He was thus the leading opponent of Bishop Victor of Rome" (EUS-ed 31094–31098). Coxe: "When Eusebius says that the churches of 'all Asia' concurred in the Ephesine use concerning the Paschal, he evidently means Asia Minor" (ANF8-ed 72642–72645). The tree's tradition entry places the tradition in "Antioch, Asia Minor, Rome", with Asia Minor in today's Turkey (CEN `post-apostolic-house-church`, `region`, `modernCountries`). Documented (the office); "Asia" as the Roman province of Asia, Documented; its modern place, CEN.

S2. **Time.** Victor became bishop of Rome "in the tenth year of the reign of Commodus" (EUS 31002–31003, 31038; McGiffert: 189). EUS-ed 31018–31020: Lipsius "gives as his dates 189–198 or 199". Jerome's *Chronicle* puts the letters in the fourth year of Severus: "IIII Quaestione orta in Asia inter episcopos an secundum legem Moysi XIIII mensis pascha obseruandum esset, Victor Romanae urbis episcopus et Narcissus Hierosolymarum, Polycrates quoque et Hireneus et Bacchylus plurimique ecclesiarum pastores quid eis probabile uisum fuerit litteris ediderunt, quarum memoria ad nos usque perdurat" (JCHRON 43388–43397). EUS-ed 31241–31247: "The date of all of these councils is commonly given as 198 A.D., but there is no particular authority for that year. Jerome's version of the Chron. assigns the composition of the various epistles to the fourth year of Septimius Severus (196–197); but it is clear that he is giving only an approximate date. We can say only that the synods took place sometime during Victor's episcopate." Jerome: "He flourished in the reign of the emperor Severus in the same period as Narcissus of Jerusalem" (JER 40414–40416). The dates he became bishop and died "are not known to us" (EUS-ed 31099–31101). Documented: in the 190s, while Victor was bishop of Rome; any single year, Inferential-Thin. Plain words: "near the end of the second century", "in the 190s".

S3. **Tradition.** The tree's tradition is `post-apostolic-house-church`, spoken name "The Scattered Households" (CEN `informalName`). The census marks him "Named within another tradition's story" (CEN `statusWord`).

S4. **The custom.** Eusebius V.23: "A question of no small importance arose at that time. For the parishes of all Asia, as from an older tradition, held that the fourteenth day of the moon, on which day the Jews were commanded to sacrifice the lamb, should be observed as the feast of the Saviour's passover. […] It was therefore necessary to end their fast on that day, whatever day of the week it should happen to be. But it was not the custom of the churches in the rest of the world to end it at this time, as they observed the practice which, from apostolic tradition, has prevailed to the present time, of terminating the fast on no other day than on that of the resurrection of our Saviour" (EUS 31136–31140, 31216–31222). Documented (Eusebius's account). Note: Eusebius's "rest of the world" is his own claim; McGiffert notes Athanasius and Jerome speak of others keeping the fourteenth (EUS-ed 31249–31261).
  - McGiffert's gloss: the Asians kept the fourteenth of Nisan "whatever day of the week that might be, by a solemn fast, and closed the day with the communion in commemoration of the last paschal supper of Christ. The Roman church, on the other hand, … celebrated … his resurrection on the Sunday following the first full moon after the vernal equinox" (EUS-ed 31144–31152). Editor's; the plain sense (Asia ended the fast on the fourteenth whatever the weekday; the others only on Sunday) is in Eusebius's own text above.
  - Polycrates's own words for the day: "my relatives always observed the day when the people put away the leaven" (EUS 31387–31394; "the people" = "i.e. the Jews", EUS-ed 31388). Jerome's Latin: "semper Pascha celebravi, quando populus Judaeorum azyma faciebat" (PL23 55145–55147).

## Events

E1. **Other synods decide for Sunday.** "Synods and assemblies of bishops were held on this account, and all, with one consent, through mutual correspondence drew up an ecclesiastical decree, that the mystery of the resurrection of the Lord should be celebrated on no other but the Lord's day, and that we should observe the close of the paschal fast on this day only" (EUS 31224–31225, 31261–31265). Eusebius names letters from Palestine (Theophilus of Caesarea, Narcissus of Jerusalem), Rome ("which bears the name of Bishop Victor"), Pontus (Palmas), Gaul (Irenaeus), Osrhoene, and Bacchylus of Corinth (EUS 31265–31298); "that which has been given above was their unanimous decision" (EUS 31300–31301; "Namely, against the observance of the fourteenth day", EUS-ed). Documented (Eusebius saw the letters). None survives (EUS-ed 31274–31277).

E2. **Victor asked for the synods.** Polycrates: "I could mention the bishops who were present, whom I summoned at your desire" (EUS 31406–31407). McGiffert: "According to this, the Asiatic Council was summoned at the request of Victor of Rome, and in all probability this was the case with all the councils" (EUS-ed 31407–31409). Hefele, from Jerome's *Chronicle*: in 196 Victor "wrote to the most eminent bishops of every country, asking them to assemble synods … These letters—for example, those to Polycrates of Ephesus—also contained threats in case of resistance" (HEF 20318–20323). Documented that Asia's synod met at Victor's desire (Polycrates's own words). That Victor's letters held threats: Inferential, from Polycrates's "terrifying words" (L4); Hefele's view.

E3. **Asia's synod holds to the old custom.** "But the bishops of Asia, led by Polycrates, decided to hold to the old custom handed down to them. He himself, in a letter which he addressed to Victor and the church of Rome, set forth in the following words the tradition which had come down to him" (EUS 31310–31316). Jerome: he "with other bishops of Asia who in accordance with some ancient custom celebrated the passover with the Jews on the fourteenth of the month, wrote a synodical letter against Victor bishop of Rome in which he says that he follows the authority of the apostle John and of the ancients" (JER 40382–40387; Latin PL23 54715–54718, 55115–55119). Documented.

E4. **The bishops' consent.** "And they, beholding my littleness, gave their consent to the letter, knowing that I did not bear my gray hairs in vain, but had always governed my life by the Lord Jesus" (EUS 31410–31413). ANF8: "These bishops, on coming to see me, unworthy as I am, signified their united approval of the letter, knowing that I wore these grey hairs not in vain, but have always regulated my conduct in obedience to the Lord Jesus" (ANF8 72627–72632; "τὸν μικρόν", 72628–72629). How many: "whose names, should I write them, would constitute a great multitude" (EUS 31409–31410). Harnack: the participants are not known; "also wohl ein paar Dutzend" (HARN 10671–10677; his estimate). Documented (his words).

E5. **Victor tries to cut Asia off.** "Thereupon Victor, who presided over the church at Rome, immediately attempted to cut off from the common unity the parishes of all Asia, with the churches that agreed with them, as heterodox; and he wrote letters and declared all the brethren there wholly excommunicate" (EUS 31415–31419). Documented (Eusebius). Whether it took effect: see D1.

E6. **Other bishops object; Irenaeus.** "But this did not please all the bishops. And they besought him to consider the things of peace, and of neighborly unity and love. Words of theirs are extant, sharply rebuking Victor. Among them was Irenæus, who, sending letters in the name of the brethren in Gaul over whom he presided, maintained that the mystery of the resurrection of the Lord should be observed only on the Lord's day. He fittingly admonishes Victor that he should not cut off whole churches of God which observed the tradition of an ancient custom" (EUS 31431–31441). Documented. The Polycarp story tells this letter's content (Anicetus); `irenaeus-of-lyon` gives it a clause. Here: a clause.

E7. **Palestine and Alexandria.** The Palestinian bishops close their letter: "We show you indeed that also in Alexandria they keep it on the same day that we do. For letters are carried from us to them and from them to us, so that in the same manner and at the same time we keep the sacred day" (EUS 31691–31696). Documented. Optional.

E8. **How it ended: not recorded.** McGiffert: "the breach was gradually healed—just how and when we do not know; but the Roman practice gradually prevailed over the Asiatic" (EUS-ed 31185–31187). Hefele: "this debate did not bring about the uniformity which Victor desired. However, … some Churches of Asia, it appears, renounced their custom", concluded by Massuet and Valesius from Constantine's letter (HEF 20394, 20410–20416). No early source tells the outcome or Polycrates's death. Documented silence; Hefele's "some churches" is his inference.

## The letter (his own words)

L1. **The exact day.** "We observe the exact day; neither adding, nor taking away" (EUS 31324–31325). ANF8: "As for us, then, we scrupulously observe the exact day, neither adding nor taking away" (ANF8 72578–72582; Greek note "᾽Αῤῥαδιούργητον ἄγομεν τὴν ἡμέραν", 72579–72581; PL23 55043). Jerome: "Nos igitur inviolabilem celebramus diem, neque addentes aliquid, neque dementes" (PL23 55119–55121, as printed). The Greek word means "unfalsified, not tampered with". Documented.

L2. **The great lights.** "For in Asia also great lights have fallen asleep, which shall rise again on the day of the Lord's coming, when he shall come with glory from heaven, and shall seek out all the saints. Among these are Philip, one of the twelve apostles, who fell asleep in Hierapolis; and his two aged virgin daughters, and another daughter, who lived in the Holy Spirit and now rests at Ephesus; and, moreover, John, who was both a witness and a teacher, who reclined upon the bosom of the Lord, and, being a priest, wore the sacerdotal plate. He fell asleep at Ephesus. And Polycarp in Smyrna, who was a bishop and martyr; and Thraseas, bishop and martyr from Eumenia, who fell asleep in Smyrna. Why need I mention the bishop and martyr Sagaris who fell asleep in Laodicea, or the blessed Papirius, or Melito, the Eunuch who lived altogether in the Holy Spirit, and who lies in Sardis, awaiting the episcopate from heaven, when he shall rise from the dead?" (EUS 31325–31362, notes cut). "Lights" is στοιχεῖα (ANF8 72583, "luminaries"); Jerome "elementa maxima" (PL23 55121–55122), and his editor notes the other reading "luminaria" (PL23 55158–55163). ANF8 gives Melito's "awaiting the visitation from heaven" (ANF8 72608–72610; ἐπισκοπήν). Documented as his words.

L3. **All of them kept the fourteenth.** "All these observed the fourteenth day of the passover according to the Gospel, deviating in no respect, but following the rule of faith" (EUS 31364–31366). Documented.

L4. **His family.** "And I also, Polycrates, the least of you all, do according to the tradition of my relatives, some of whom I have closely followed. For seven of my relatives were bishops; and I am the eighth. And my relatives always observed the day when the people put away the leaven" (EUS 31385–31394, notes cut). ANF8: "some of whom I have succeeded" (ANF8 72614–72618). Harnack: "Auf welchen Bischofssitzen diese Verwandten in Asien zu suchen sind, ist nicht bekannt" (HARN 10670–10671). Documented (his words); which sees, not known.

L5. **Sixty-five years; not afraid.** "I, therefore, brethren, who have lived sixty-five years in the Lord, and have met with the brethren throughout the world, and have gone through every Holy Scripture, am not affrighted by terrifying words. For those greater than I have said 'We ought to obey God rather than man'" (EUS 31396–31400; Acts 5:29). ANF8: "who am sixty-five years old in the Lord, and have fallen in with the brethren in all parts of the world, and have read through all Holy Scripture, am not frightened at the things which are said to terrify us. For those who are greater than I have said, 'We ought to obey God rather than men'" (ANF8 72618–72623). Greek (PL23 55101, 55154–55156, OCR): "ἑξήκοντα πέντε ἔτη ἔχων ἐν Κυρίῳ … οὐ πτύρομαι ἐπὶ τοῖς καταπλησσομένοις. Οἱ γὰρ ἐμοῦ μείζονες εἰρήκασι, Πειθαρχεῖν δεῖ Θεῷ … ἢ ἀνθρώποις". Jerome's Latin takes the years as his age, and the threat as aimed at them: "sexaginta quinque annos aetatis meae natos in Domino … non formidabo eos, qui nobis minantur" (PL23 55147–55148, 55199–55200). Documented (his words). Whether "sixty-five years in the Lord" is his age or his years as a Christian: the Greek leaves it open; Jerome and Hefele (HEF 20361) read it as his age.

L6. **The bishops' consent and his gray hair.** See E4 (EUS 31406–31413). Documented.

## What came of him (impact, heaviest first)

I1. **His letter became the early witness to the graves of John and Philip.** Eusebius quotes it first not for Easter but for John's burial: "The time of John's death has also been given in a general way, but his burial place is indicated by an epistle of Polycrates (who was bishop of the parish of Ephesus), addressed to Victor, bishop of Rome. In this epistle he mentions him together with the apostle Philip and his daughters in the following words" (EUS 19072–19079); then L2, ending "He also sleeps at Ephesus" (EUS 19092–19168). "So much concerning their death" (EUS 19171). McGiffert: the citation "of this incidental passage from a letter upon a wholly different subject illustrates Eusebius' great diligence" (EUS-ed 19086–19090). Later users carried it on:
  - Abelard (PL178 "Saeculum XII", line 16; born 1079, line 2127), copying the Latin Eusebius: "secundum fidem Polycratis, Ephesii episcopi … Hic enim Polycrates Victori, episcopo urbis Romae, scribens et ipsius Philippi apostoli ac filiarum ejus pariter meminit" (ABEL 130335–130344; Abelard cites it as Eusebius "lib. III, cap. XXVIII"). Documented.
  - Thomas Smith, "Fellow of St. Mary Magdalen College, Oxford", *An Account of the Greek Church*, 1680 (SMITH 1–4, file header), on those who doubted John's death: "But that he died at Ephesus really and truly, and was buried there, Polycrates Bishop of that City discourses in a Letter to Victor Bishop of Rome, which is preserved by Eusebius" (SMITH 1936–1940; long s and the OCR "trucly", "Vitor", "15" read as "truly", "Victor", "is"; the date is the file's). Documented.
  - The Greek *Synaxaristes* of Nikodemos the Hagiorite (Orthodox saints' lives; this edition 1900): "That John truly died we can learn from many witnesses. For Polycrates, the bishop of Ephesus, writing to Victor of Rome, says word for word…" (SYNAX 12048–12056, my English of the modern Greek). Documented.
  - Paisios Ligarides, 17th century, from the "plate": "Polycrates as saying that St. John the Evangelist was a priest, and had been used to wear the gold πέταλον on his head" (LIG 10641–10646). Documented; optional.
  Confidence: Documented that later writers used the letter this way. Whether the Philip buried at Hierapolis was the apostle: Contested (D2).

I2. **Rome's custom prevailed; keeping the fourteenth became a mark of heresy.** What Victor tried, the later church did.
  - Hippolytus, at Rome a generation later, puts them in his *Refutation of All Heresies* (the word "(heretics)" in parentheses is the ANF translator's supplement): "certain other (heretics), contentious by nature, (and) wholly uninformed as regards knowledge, as well as in their manner more (than usually) quarrelsome, combine (in maintaining) that Easter should be kept on the fourteenth day of the first month, according to the commandment of the law, on whatever day (of the week) it should occur" (HIPP 13066–13075); yet "In other respects, however, these consent to all the traditions delivered to the Church by the Apostles" (HIPP 13092–13094). Chapter title "The Quartodecimans" (HIPP 13064–13065). Documented (his view).
  - Nicaea, 325. Constantine's letter: "it was resolved by the united judgment of all present, that this feast ought to be kept by all and in every place on one and the same day" (EUS 64606–64609); "Let us then have nothing in common with the detestable Jewish crowd" (EUS 64619–64620); the common practice is observed "in the dioceses of Asia and Pontus, and in Cilicia, with entire unity of judgment" (EUS 64680–64681). The council's own letter: "all our brethren in the East who formerly followed the custom of the Jews are henceforth to celebrate the said most sacred feast of Easter at the same time with the Romans and yourselves and all those who have observed Easter from the beginning" (COUN 6400–6406). Documented. The hostility to the Jews in Constantine's letter is part of the record and is not to be softened if told.
  - Antioch, 341, canon 1: anyone who sets aside Nicaea's decree on Easter is to be "excommunicated and cast out of the Church"; a bishop, elder or deacon "observing Easter [at the same time] with the Jews … shall thenceforth be an alien from the Church" (COUN 9438–9449). Documented.
  - Laodicea, canon 7: "Persons converted from heresies, that is, of the Novatians, Photinians, and Quartodecimans … shall not be received until they shall have anathematized every heresy" (COUN 10713–10717). Documented.
  - McGiffert: at Nicaea the Roman practice "was declared binding upon the whole Church, while the old Asiatic practice was condemned. … only scattered churches continued to cling to the practice of the earlier Asiatics, and they were branded as heretics, and called Quartodecimanians (from quarta decima), a name which we carry back and apply to all who observed the fourteenth day, even those of the second and third centuries" (EUS-ed 31186–31194). Editor's summary; the name-back-projection point is useful for Do not say.

I3. **Praised all the same.** Jerome copied the letter "to show through a small example the genius and authority of the man" (JER 40413–40414; Latin "Haec propterea posui, ut ingenium et auctoritatem viri ex parvo opusculo demonstrarem", PL23 55202–55203). McGiffert: "The fact that both Eusebius and Jerome praise Polycrates so highly, and testify to his orthodoxy, shows how completely the paschal question had been buried before their time, and how little the Quartodeciman practice was feared" (EUS-ed 31120–31123). Eusebius lists him among the bishops "the soundness of whose faith has come down to us in writing" (EUS 31124–31127). Documented (the praise); McGiffert's reading, editor's.

I4. **A later historian judges Victor.** Socrates, 5th century: "In Asia Minor most people kept the fourteenth day of the moon, disregarding the sabbath: yet they never separated from those who did otherwise, until Victor, bishop of Rome, influenced by too ardent a zeal, fulminated a sentence of excommunication against the Quartodecimans in Asia" (SOC 16224–16228, 16237; Greek SOC-gr 8959–8962). He argues the apostles "legislated" no feast day: "the feast of Easter came to be observed in each place according to the individual peculiarities of the peoples inasmuch as none of the apostles legislated on the matter" (SOC 16219–16222). Documented (his view). Optional.

## Where sources disagree or come late

D1. **Did Victor cut Asia off, or only try?** Eusebius: "attempted to cut off … and he wrote letters and declared all the brethren there wholly excommunicate" (EUS 31415–31419). McGiffert: "I cannot understand that Eusebius' words mean anything else than that he did actually cut off communion with them. The Greek reads ἀκοινωνήτους πάντας ἄρδην τοὺς ἐκεῖσε ἀνακηρύττων ἀδελφούς. This seems to me decisive" (EUS-ed 31419–31429). Socrates: he "fulminated a sentence of excommunication" (SOC 16226–16228). Hefele, after Valesius: "the Pope thought of excommunicating the Asiatics, and that he was kept from carrying out the sentence especially by S. Irenæus. Eusebius says, indeed, 'He tried to excommunicate them'" (HEF 20373–20377). Contested. Safe wording: "Victor tried to cut them off, and wrote declaring them all out of communion; whether the break took hold, historians have disagreed."

D2. **Which Philip.** Polycrates says "Philip, one of the twelve apostles". McGiffert: "Philip the apostle and Philip the evangelist are here confounded" (EUS-ed 19096); "Lightfoot … maintains that Polycrates is correct" (EUS-ed 19107–19110). Contested. Credit "Philip, whom he calls one of the twelve apostles".

D3. **John's "plate".** "Being a priest, wore the sacerdotal plate" (EUS 31332–31333). McGiffert: the Greek word is the term "for the plate or diadem of the high priest … What is meant by the word in the present connection is uncertain" (EUS-ed 19154–19156); figurative, or "as Hefele suggests, … a mythical tradition which arose after the second Jewish war" (EUS-ed 19162–19167). Vallarsi: figurative (PL23 55165–55182). Contested. Leave out.

D4. **His relatives.** Hefele: "Seven of his own relations had been bishops of Ephesus before him" (HEF 20359–20360). Polycrates says only "seven of my relatives were bishops" (EUS 31387); Harnack: which sees, not known (HARN 10670–10671). Hefele's "of Ephesus" is his addition. Do not say.

D5. **Dates.** Coxe heads the fragments "[A.D. 130–196.]" (ANF8-ed 72525–72526) and a synod "A.D. 196" (ANF8-ed 72564); JER-ed "about 196" (JER 40382); "commonly given as 198 … no particular authority" (EUS-ed 31241–31243). Inferential-Thin for any single year; "in the 190s" safe (S2).

D6. **"Rest of the world".** Eusebius says the other churches all ended the fast on Sunday (S4, E1). McGiffert: Athanasius and Jerome speak of others in Syria, Cilicia, Mesopotamia, "possible that the practice was from the beginning more widely spread than Eusebius supposed" (EUS-ed 31249–31254). Coxe: "it was the general use, save only in Asia Minor and churches which were colonies of the same" (ANF8-ed 72656–72658). Say "the other churches whose letters Eusebius saw".

D7. **Victor's character.** McGiffert: "an overbearing man, who believed that he, as Bishop of Rome, had a right to demand of all other churches conformity" (EUS-ed 31180–31182); Coxe: "the less creditable history of Victor" (ANF8-ed 72530); Hefele traces Victor's zeal to the Roman teacher Blastus (HEF 20283–20287, 20314–20317; "doubtless"). Editors' views. Do not carry any of them in the narrator's voice.

D8. **Coxe's surmises** (he knew Polycarp and Irenaeus; John "foreknew it would quietly pass away"; ANF8-ed 72540, 72562–72563): editor's; not used.
