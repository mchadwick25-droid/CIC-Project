## The three lines, from one hearing

- **Who:** Polycrates, bishop of Ephesus near the end of the second century, who led the bishops of the Roman province of Asia.
- **What happened:** Asia kept Easter on the day of the Jewish Passover, and the other churches kept it on Sunday. When Victor, bishop of Rome, pressed for one custom, Polycrates refused in a letter. It named the apostles and martyrs buried in Asia, and his seven kinsmen who were bishops, as proof that the day came from the apostles. Victor then tried to cut all of Asia off from communion.
- **Why the connection matters:** Asia's custom lost. A writer at Rome listed it among heresies, Nicaea set one day for Easter, and later councils did what Victor had tried. But the letter outlived the custom, because Eusebius copied it as a witness to where John was buried.
