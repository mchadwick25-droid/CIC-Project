# Outline check: Polycrates of Ephesus

## My heart, written before reading the outline

From the census entry (`teaser`, `card`), the batch job line and the source sheet.

**The essence.** In the 190s Polycrates, bishop of Ephesus, led the bishops of Asia in holding to their custom of keeping the Pascha on the fourteenth day of the moon, the day of the Jewish Passover, whatever the weekday, while the other churches whose letters Eusebius saw ended the fast only on Sunday (EUS 31136–31140, 31216–31222, 31310–31316). Asked by Victor of Rome to gather a synod, he wrote back for Asia naming the apostles and martyrs buried there who had kept that day, his seven kinsmen who were bishops, and "We ought to obey God rather than man" (EUS 31324–31413). Victor tried to cut Asia off from communion (EUS 31415–31419). He is on the tree as the voice of Asia's side in the first great quarrel over Easter, in the only words of his that survive.

**Also told.** What each side claimed (Asia an "older tradition", the others "apostolic tradition"); the synods held at Victor's desire; the letter's graves, family, sixty-five years and refusal; Victor's sentence and the bishops who rebuked him, Irenaeus among them (a clause); the outcome not recorded; what came of him: the letter kept by Eusebius as the early witness to John's and Philip's graves, the Sunday custom made binding at Nicaea and keeping the fourteenth later branded heresy, and Polycrates still praised by Eusebius and Jerome.
