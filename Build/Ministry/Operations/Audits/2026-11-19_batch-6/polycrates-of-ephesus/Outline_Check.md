# Outline check: Polycrates of Ephesus

## My heart, written before reading the outline

From the census entry (`teaser`, `card`), the batch job line and the source sheet.

**The essence.** In the 190s Polycrates, bishop of Ephesus, led the bishops of Asia in holding to their custom of keeping the Pascha on the fourteenth day of the moon, the day of the Jewish Passover, whatever the weekday, while the other churches whose letters Eusebius saw ended the fast only on Sunday (EUS 31136–31140, 31216–31222, 31310–31316). Asked by Victor of Rome to gather a synod, he wrote back for Asia naming the apostles and martyrs buried there who had kept that day, his seven kinsmen who were bishops, and "We ought to obey God rather than man" (EUS 31324–31413). Victor tried to cut Asia off from communion (EUS 31415–31419). He is on the tree as the voice of Asia's side in the first great quarrel over Easter, in the only words of his that survive.

**Also told.** What each side claimed (Asia an "older tradition", the others "apostolic tradition"); the synods held at Victor's desire; the letter's graves, family, sixty-five years and refusal; Victor's sentence and the bishops who rebuked him, Irenaeus among them (a clause); the outcome not recorded; what came of him: the letter kept by Eusebius as the early witness to John's and Philip's graves, the Sunday custom made binding at Nicaea and keeping the fourteenth later branded heresy, and Polycrates still praised by Eusebius and Jerome.

*Note after reading the outline: my own also-told line "the Sunday custom made binding at Nicaea" is McGiffert's summary (EUS-ed 31186–31190); the held primary letters qualify it (BLOCKING 1).*

## Findings

### BLOCKING 1. Nicaea is set up to read as overruling Polycrates's Asia, which the outline's own sources contradict

What came 2 puts Nicaea under "The custom lost", and gives the council's line about "the brothers in the East who had followed the Jews' custom". The Connection adds "Nicaea set one day for all ... so that what Victor tried, the later church did". Heard together, a listener will take Nicaea as the day Asia's custom was put down. The sources the outline holds say otherwise:
- Constantine's own letter from the council lists "the dioceses of Asia and Pontus, and in Cilicia" among the provinces that already kept the common practice (EUS 64680–64681; the sheet quotes this line at I2, but it does not reach the outline).
- The council's letter speaks of "our brethren in the East" (COUN 6400–6406), not Asia.
- Hefele reads Constantine's line to mean that some churches of Asia had already given up the custom before Nicaea. He also says the Easter question had by then taken on a new, astronomical side (HEF 20408–20426; "some Churches of Asia, it appears, renounced their custom", 20409–20412).
- Duchesne (held; see MINOR 6) says that by the fourth century, "et nommément au concile de Nicée, il ne fut plus question de cette affaire", and that the keepers of the fourteenth were by then a small sect outside the catholic churches (duchesne t1 10640–10645).
The fourth-century condemnations that do name the custom are Laodicea's canon ("Quartodecimans" among heresies) and, by its wording, Antioch's ("observing Easter with the Jews").

**Correction.**
(a) In What came 2, replace "In 325 the Council of Nicaea set one day for Easter everywhere; the council's letter said the brothers in the East who had followed the Jews' custom would now keep Easter with Rome (I2; Documented)." with: "In 325 the Council of Nicaea set one day for Easter everywhere. By then, Constantine's own letter from the council counts Asia among the provinces already keeping the common day (EUS 64680–64681; Documented). When and how Asia changed, no source says (E8)."
(b) In the Connection, replace "within a generation a writer at Rome listed it in a book against heresies, Nicaea set one day for all, and later councils cast out those who kept it, so that what Victor tried, the later church did (I2)" with: "within a generation a writer at Rome listed it in a book against heresies; by the time Nicaea set one day for all, Asia itself had come to keep the common day; and later councils cast out those who still kept the fourteenth, so that what Victor tried, the later church did (I2)."
(c) Add to Do not say: "Not 'Nicaea overruled Asia', 'Asia gave up its custom at Nicaea', or the Nicene letter's 'brethren in the East' told as Polycrates's churches. Constantine's letter counts Asia among the provinces already keeping the common day (EUS 64680–64681); how and when Asia changed, no source says (E8)."

### MINOR 1. Setting and Words to explain: "on the west coast" has no cited source

The census tradition entry gives only "Asia Minor" in today's Turkey (CEN `post-apostolic-house-church`, `modernCountries`), and no cited line says "west coast". **Correction:** in Setting (Place) and in the Asia gloss, write "the Roman province of Asia, in what is now western Turkey" only if a held line is cited for "western". Otherwise write "the Roman province of Asia, in today's Turkey".

### MINOR 2. Quote 2's rendering turns "terrifying words" into persons who threaten

McGiffert has "am not affrighted by terrifying words"; ANF8 has "the things which are said to terrify us"; the Greek τοῖς καταπλησσομένοις does not name persons. "Those who threaten us" is Jerome's Latin ("eos, qui nobis minantur"), and it states as Polycrates's own words that there were threateners. That leans on the point the outline itself marks as inferred (E2, beat 4). "Met with the brothers from all over the world" also shifts "throughout the world" (ANF8: "in all parts of the world"). **Correction:** "So, brothers, I have lived sixty-five years in the Lord. I have met the brothers all over the world, and I have gone through all of holy Scripture. I am not frightened by what is said to terrify us. For those greater than I have said, 'We must obey God rather than human beings.'" Change the note after it to: "('Us' follows ANF8 72621–72622 and Jerome's Latin; the Greek leaves it unstated.)"

### MINOR 3. Quote 3's rendering: "how small a man I am" can be heard as his height

τὸν μικρόν (McGiffert "my littleness"; ANF8 "unworthy as I am") is a word of humility. Heard once, "how small a man" can mean his size. **Correction:** "Seeing how lowly I am, they agreed to the letter. They knew I had not worn my gray hair for nothing, but had always lived my life by the Lord Jesus."

### MINOR 4. The synod's stand risks being told twice (beat 2 and beat 5)

Beat 2 ends "Asia's bishops, gathered by Polycrates, held to the old custom (E3)". Beat 5 opens with the same bishops agreeing to the letter (E4). Both describe the one synod's decision. **Correction:** end beat 2 with "Asia's bishops, gathered by Polycrates, held to the old custom, and he wrote for them all (E3); their agreement to the letter is told once, in beat 5."

### MINOR 5. Passing place names with no gloss, and "modern" for the Synaxaristes

Beat 2 lists "Pontus, Gaul, Osrhoene and Corinth". Osrhoene and Pontus are opaque to a listener hearing them once. **Correction:** add to Words to explain: "Pontus, Osrhoene: give by region if named at all: 'from Palestine and Mesopotamia to Gaul'; Osrhoene was 'a region of country in northwestern Mesopotamia' (EUS-ed, EUS 31289)." In What came 1, "a modern Greek book of saints' lives" can be heard as recent. **Correction:** "a Greek Orthodox book of saints' lives, in an edition of 1900 (SYNAX)".

### MINOR 6. The Library sweep misses Duchesne, and does not list the Liber Pontificalis

Louis Duchesne's *Histoire ancienne de l'Église* I (1907; `duchesne_histoire-ancienne-de-leglise-tome1-fra_3e-ed1907.txt` 4928–4934, 10546, 10580–10648) treats the quarrel fully. It is the most recent held historian on it, and the sweep neither uses nor sets it aside. It holds two views the outline should know: that Victor may have drawn back from his severe measures ("On peut croire que Victor revint sur ses mesures sévères", OCR "Tevint", 10638–10639), a third reading for D1, and the Nicaea point in BLOCKING 1. The *Liber Pontificalis* (Loomis, `liber-pontificalis_book-of-the-popes-v1_loomis1916.txt` 2078–2160) credits Victor with a decree that Easter be kept on the Lord's day. It is a late source that does not name Polycrates, so it belongs only in the set-aside list. **Correction:** add both to the sheet's Library sweep. Add Duchesne's view to D1 as "a historian (Duchesne, 1907) thinks Victor may have drawn back" (editor's view, card only). Neither changes a beat beyond BLOCKING 1. No held primary text is missing: Eusebius's Greek of Books I–V is confirmed not held (only `eusebius_werke-bd2-2-…`, Books VI–X). The other files that name Polycrates of Ephesus are notes, indexes or concern Polycrates of Samos, as the sheet says. Those include the Odes of Solomon notes (Harris, 14548–14579, on the "plate") and Barhebraeus (33217, an index).

## Fields confirmed

- **Words to explain:** catches every church and period word the beats use: Asia, Easter and Passover, the fourteenth day, the Lord's day, the fast, synod, communion, "fallen asleep", "great lights", leaven, heterodox and Quartodeciman. Each gloss is right for the 190s and says no more than its source. The communion gloss ("refusing to share the Lord's Supper") is borne out by Irenaeus's letter on sending the eucharist (EUS 31551–31553). Common words are left unglossed, apart from "martyr", which is borderline and harmless.
- **Length:** 2:45 fits five beats, two quotes and a third on what came of him. It is not filled, and the cut order is sensible.
- **Do not say:** apart from BLOCKING 1(c), it covers the wonders, qualifiers, numbers and outcomes a writer could get wrong (the years, the sixty-five years, "of Ephesus", which Philip, the plate, whether the break took effect, any outcome, judgments of Victor, "pope", Constantine's hostility).
- **Names, Sibling, Who tells it:** filled. Census spellings are kept (Polycarp of Smyrna, Irenaeus of Lyon, Eusebius of Caesarea, Hippolytus of Rome, Council of Nicaea, Constantine the Great, Jerome). Everyone else is given by role. The whole story rests on Eusebius, and the outline says where the story says so. There is no line about today, so none needs a source.
