## 1. The test, from one hearing

I read it once, straight through, before reading anything else.

- **Who:** Tatian, a man born in the land of the Assyrians and trained in Greek learning, who became a Christian at Rome and heard Justin.
- **What happened:** he wrote a book telling the Greeks their learning was borrowed from the "barbarians" and younger than Moses. Irenaeus says he later left the church. He went east and wove the four Gospels into one story, the Diatessaron.
- **Why the connection matters:** the Syriac-speaking churches read the Diatessaron aloud as their gospel, and Ephrem wrote a commentary on it. In the 400s two bishops replaced it with the four separate Gospels, and Theodoret gathered and put away more than two hundred copies.

### MINOR

**M1. "barbarian" first heard before its plain sense (Words to explain).** It is first heard in sentence 1 ("Do not sneer at the barbarians"). Its plain sense comes in sentence 3 ("To a Greek, a barbarian was anyone who was not Greek."). The listener is not lost, because the sense arrives within seconds and the opening's list of peoples points to it. But the rule asks for the plain sense at the first hearing.

Change "Do not sneer at the barbarians, Tatian told the Greeks, for which of your arts did not come from them?" to "Do not sneer at the barbarians, Tatian told the Greeks, meaning all who were not Greek, for which of your arts did not come from them?" Then delete "To a Greek, a barbarian was anyone who was not Greek." (net −6 words).

If the writer prefers to keep the opening as Tatian's line alone, leave it. The delay is one sentence.

**M2. "Syriac" first heard without its plain sense (Words to explain).** It is first heard in paragraph 2 ("most Syriac-speaking churches heard read"). "A form of Aramaic" comes only in paragraph 7.

Change "became the gospel most Syriac-speaking churches heard read" to "became the gospel most churches speaking Syriac, a form of Aramaic, heard read". In paragraph 7, change "In the churches that worshipped in Syriac, a form of Aramaic, it was widely read aloud" to "In those churches it was widely read aloud" (net −6 words).

**M3. A report about the record (craft).** "and his order, as quoted, gives no reason" makes the narrator speak about a document rather than tell what happened. The point it guards, that no motive is given, must stay.

Change it to "and the words we have of his order give no reason".

**M4. Moses before Homer, and one law for all, told without their weight (Also told).** The approved heart says Moses "was older than Homer". The story has only "younger than Moses", and it does not tell the proof the book sets out. That proof is the reason the book survived (I4).

After "Then comes his turn: ... my own."" add: "He then sets out to prove, from Greek, Babylonian and Egyptian writers, that Moses was older than Homer." (+19 words). Source: AG 7026–7034 and chs. 36–41 (Outline beat 3; the "Chaldean" gloss is "Babylonian").

Optional, in the same paragraph: after "mocks the philosophers as vain and greedy", add "and asks why every city keeps its own laws when one law should serve all" (+14 words; AG 6948–6950).

**M5. Aphrahat's witness not told (Also told).** The outline's harmony item begins with Aphrahat quoting it as "the beginning of the Gospel". The story gives the use through Ephrem alone.

In paragraph 7, after "and Ephrem the Syrian wrote a commentary on it", add: ". The words "In the beginning was the Word" stand in the outline's Also told and beat 5.

Optional. The use is already held by "widely read aloud as the Gospel" and Ephrem.

**M6. An unclear pronoun.** In "A later writer says Tatian went east, to the land between the Tigris and the Euphrates, and the place rests on him alone", the nearest "him" is Tatian.
