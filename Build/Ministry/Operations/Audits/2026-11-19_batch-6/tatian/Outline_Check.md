# Outline check: Tatian

Checker, batch 6, 2026-11-19. Written before reading `Outline.md`, from the census entry (not its `longDescription`), `Card.existing.json`, `Source_Sheet.md`, the batch job line as the hearts entry corrected it, and what Tatian is known for.

## My account of the heart (before reading the outline)

**The essence.** Tatian, born "in the land of the Assyrians" and schooled in Greek rhetoric, became a Christian at Rome through the Hebrew Scriptures and heard Justin. In his Address to the Greeks he turned on Greek learning: every art borrowed from "barbarians", the philosophers vain, Moses older than Homer. After Justin's death he broke with the church (Irenaeus, hostile; about 172 by Eusebius's Chronicle) and went east, and his Diatessaron, the four Gospels woven into one, became the gospel most Syriac-speaking churches heard read for about two centuries.

**Also told.**
- The Address: his search and conversion through "barbarian writings"; the arts borrowed from foreigners; one law for all; free teaching for rich and poor, women and old women; Moses older than Homer, the part later readers (Clement, Eusebius, Jerome) valued (Documented, own words).
- His charge that the philosopher Crescens tried to have Justin and himself put to death (his charge; not that Crescens had Justin killed).
- The break, as Irenaeus tells it: separated after Justin's martyrdom, "puffed up", aeons like Valentinus's followers, marriage as corruption, Adam not saved (Documented as Irenaeus's hostile account; motive credited to him). Founder of the Encratites: Contested.
- Went east to Mesopotamia (Widely Accepted, resting on Epiphanius alone, two centuries later). No held source puts him at Edessa.
- The Diatessaron: what it is, its name "fourfold"; read in Syriac churches (Doctrine of Addai, Aphrahat, Ephrem's commentary); "very widely if not universally" (Hogg), not "the only gospel". Language and whether before or after the break: Contested.
- Its removal in the 400s by Rabbula of Edessa and Theodoret of Cyrrhus, who gathered more than two hundred copies and put in the four Gospels (Documented); it survives only in later translations.
- His death: unknown.

**Shape.** Teacher or thinker. Siblings: `justin-martyr` already tells hearer, break and the Addai line; here Tatian's own book carries it. `bardaisan-of-edessa` no held tie.

## BLOCKING

**B1. The essence drops "most", the word Mark's correction added.** Outline line 5: "became for about two centuries the gospel the Syriac-speaking churches heard read". With the definite article and no qualifier, this says the one gospel, which is the wording the hearts entry corrected ("the gospel most Syriac-speaking churches heard read", Decision-Log, "Batch 6 hearts approved", 2026-11-19). Hogg holds "very widely if not universally" (DI-ed 1927–1929), and Parisot says Aphrahat sometimes used the separate Gospels (AP-P 2277–2280). The writer works from the essence first.
Correction: "became for about two centuries the gospel most Syriac-speaking churches heard read".

**B2. "He alone denied that Adam was saved" (beat 4) goes past Irenaeus.** Irenaeus says the Encratites "deny, too, the salvation of him who was first created", and that "Tatian first introduced the blasphemy"; the denial was "an opinion due entirely to himself", meaning he started it (IR 33661–33664, 33673–33675). So others held it too. Saying he was the only one contradicts the source.
Correction (beat 4): "and he was the first, Irenaeus says, to deny that Adam was saved, a view that was his own invention." Add to Do not say: "he alone denied Adam's salvation" → "he first taught it, Irenaeus says".

**B3. The Connection gives a reason for the removal that the record does not give, and leaves out the one it does.** Outline line 76: "why bishops took it away: it came from a man the Greek-speaking church called a heretic, and the church now wanted the four Gospels read separately." Rabbula's order (DI-ed 1914–1919) gives no reason at all. Theodoret gives his own reason, in a book against heresies: Tatian made it "cutting out the genealogies and whatever other passages show that the Lord was born of the seed of David according to the flesh", and the orthodox used it "not perceiving the mischief of the composition" (TH 14973–14984; DI-ed 1790–1794, 1911–1913). McGiffert reads him the same way (EU 26437–26442). Hogg says the claim about the genealogies agrees with the Ephrem source, but the wider claim "conflicts with all three texts" (DI-ed 1845–1851). Since the Connection says the listener must be told why, the reason has to be the witness's own, credited to him. "Until bishops of the 400s removed it" also overstates: Hogg says "it could not be suppressed" (DI-ed 1933).
Correction (Connection, replacing the "why" sentence and "until ... removed it"): "... until bishops of the 400s set out to remove it. Rabbula of Edessa ordered the separate Gospels read in every church, and the order as quoted gives no reason. Theodoret, writing against heresies, says Tatian's harmony left out the passages showing that Jesus was descended from David, and that faithful Christians used it without seeing the harm. A later editor finds that charge only partly borne out by the surviving texts. Its copies were still being made centuries later (Hogg)." In What came of him item 2, change "leave out, or one clause" to "one clause, credited to Theodoret, with 'a charge the surviving texts only partly bear out'". Add to Do not say: "bishops removed it because Tatian was a heretic" → "Theodoret says it cut out Jesus's descent from David; Rabbula gives no reason".

## MINOR

**M1. The opening is the held-back reveal the batch 6 kit change ruled out as a stock hinge.** "The man was Tatian, born in the land of the Assyrians" (line 40). The ruling is in the Decision-Log entry "Batch 6: era 1 first ..." (item 4), and Kit Part 1 says the same. Correction: name him in the opening's first sentence: "Tatian, born in the land of the Assyrians and once a master of Greek learning, wrote to the Greeks: do not sneer at the barbarians ..."

**M2. Beat 1 places the mysteries at Rome.** Tatian says he had "been admitted to the mysteries", had examined rites "everywhere", and then found "among the Romans" their Latiarian Jupiter (AG 6962–6966). Correction: "He had been admitted to the mysteries, and at Rome he saw, he says, a Jupiter delighting in human blood."

**M3. Beat 3 repeats the opening.** The opening already tells the borrowed arts (AG 5684–5693). Correction: beat 3 drops "the Greeks' arts are borrowed" and begins with the philosophers.

**M4. Epiphanius's timing is told as the order of events.** Beat 5 follows beat 4, so the East reads as after the break. Epiphanius dates the journey after Justin's death (EP 16000–16001). Harnack, whom McGiffert follows, has him go east before the break, return, and break at Rome (EU-ed 26224–26234; D3, Contested). Add to Do not say: "after the break he went east" as fact → "a later writer, Epiphanius, says that after Justin's death he went east".

**M5. Names are missing three people the outline has the writer name, and two fields disagree.** Beat 1 says "credit Jerome"; beat 5 names "Epiphanius", while Who tells it says "a later writer"; item 1 names "Hope Hogg". Correction: add to Names "Jerome: witness, link (`jerome`), or by role 'a writer two centuries later'"; "Epiphanius: witness, link (`epiphanius-of-salamis`), by role preferred, 'a later writer'"; "Hope Hogg: witness, by role 'a modern editor' unless named once for the 'very widely' judgment". Beat 5 should read "A later writer, two centuries on, says ...".

**M6. "as a short book" is quoted in item 2 but is not one of the three quotes.** Correction: drop the quotation marks: "Theodoret says faithful Christians used it in all innocence, simply as a handy short book".

**M7. "the orthodox" (item 2) is a judging word in the narrator's voice, like "heretic".** Correction: "those who kept to the apostles' teaching, as Theodoret puts it". Add to Do not say: "the orthodox" in the narrator's voice.

**M8. The Q3 lines are cited wrongly, and Q3 uses British spelling.** The Greek sentence is at TH 14984–14987 and the Latin at TH 14994–14996, not 14981–14984. "held in honour" should read "held in honor" (the American-spelling ruling, Release 51, 2026-11-18). The same applies to "honoured", "moralising" and "traveller" in the outline's own text.

**M9. Q1 and Q2 are cited to Otto's Greek, which the corpus map marks "SECOND WITNESS ONLY ... Not to be quoted as the original"** (`cic/corpus-map/syriac-edessa-nisibis.yaml`, the row for `tatian_oratio-ad-graecos-grc-lat_otto1851`). Both renderings also hold against Ryland. Correction: cite AG 7258–7260 and AG 7464–7471 (Ryland) as each quote's source, with AG-G as the second witness.

**M10. The DEFENDED mark is on the wrong item.** Tatian made the harmony; nothing shows him defending it. What the Address defends is Christian teaching as a "barbarian philosophy" older and truer than Greek learning and open to the poor, to women and to the old (E5). Correction: move "DEFENDED" to item 4 (the Address), or to the argument in beat 3.

**M11. "Chaldean" in beat 3 is a period word with no gloss.** Correction: "Babylonian", or add to Words to explain "Chaldean: Babylonian".

**M12. "for about two centuries" is a span the editors imply but never state.** It is in the approved heart, so it stays. Its ends are Tatian's time (the break about 172) and the removals (Rabbula d. 435; Theodoret 453). Correction: in Do not say, "for exactly two hundred years" → "from his time until bishops moved against it in the 400s".

**M13. The Library sweep misses a held primary witness for Ephrem's commentary.** Barhadbeshabba, *Cause of the Foundation of the Schools* (about 600; `barhadbeshabba_cause-de-la-fondation-des-ecoles-po4-syr-fra_scher1908.txt` 15906–15910), names "un Commentaire de l'Évangile appelé Diatessaron ... composé par le grec Tatien" among Ephrem's works. That is earlier than Isho'dad (c. 852), whom the sheet reaches only through Hogg. The Chronicle of Seert also says Ephrem "expliqua le Diatessaron" (15751). 'Abdisho is held directly in Mai, vol. 10 (`mai_scriptorum-veterum-nova-collectio-tomus10-...`). Correction: add Barhadbeshabba to I1 as the primary support for "Ephrem wrote a commentary on it", with Isho'dad (via Hogg) after it, and list both new files under the sheet's Used or Set aside.
