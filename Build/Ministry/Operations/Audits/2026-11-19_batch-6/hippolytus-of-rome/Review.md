## The three lines, from one hearing

1. **Who.** Hippolytus, a Greek-writing teacher and one of the elders of the church at Rome in the early two hundreds, who wrote the *Refutation of All Heresies*.
2. **What happened.** He fought the bishop Callistus, charging him with forgiving sinners too easily and with making Father and Son one and the same. He may have been set up as a rival bishop, which is disputed. In 235 he was exiled to Sardinia with Rome's bishop Pontianus, probably died there, and Rome kept both men on one day.
3. **Why the connection matters.** His enemy's book, lost until 1842, is nearly all we know of Callistus and of Rome's quarrel over who belongs in the church. A like question split Rome again under Novatian in 251, and Damasus later folded Hippolytus into that split, as hearsay.

None blank or wrong.

Paragraph 2, change "Hippolytus was a Greek-writing teacher, one of the elders who led the church at Rome with its bishop." to "Hippolytus was a Greek-writing teacher at Rome."
- **MINOR 2 (heresies, plain sense late).** Paragraph 2, change "In his Refutation of All Heresies he charged Callistus with" to "He wrote a book against the teachings he held false, the Refutation of All Heresies. In it he charged Callistus with". Paragraph 5 may then read "traced each of those teachings back to Greek philosophy".
- **MINOR 3 (31-word sentence; Zephyrinus heard once and does not act).** Paragraph 4, change "Nearly all we know of Callistus comes from this enemy, who says that Callistus, as adviser to bishop Zephyrinus, let the teaching spread" to "Nearly all we know of Callistus comes from this enemy. He says that Callistus, as adviser to the bishop of the day, let the teaching spread".
- **MINOR 4 (source: "former slave").** Paragraph 4, "He paints him as a former slave who made away with Christians' savings". Hippolytus says Callistus was a slave ("a domestic" of Carpophorus) when he ran the bank and made away with the deposits (HIP 13672–13709; outline beat 3, "a slave who ran a bank"). Change "a former slave who" to "a slave who".
- **MINOR 5 (cut, 7 words).** Paragraph 8, change "Historians judge that Damasus confused two different splits, and Damasus himself gave it only as hearsay." to "Historians judge that Damasus confused two different splits." The last line of the verse carries the hearsay in his own voice.
- **MINOR 6 (source: who wrote Latin).** Paragraph 7, "in a Rome that now wrote Latin". Duchesne says the clergy of Rome now spoke and wrote Latin (DUH 11728–11731; outline What came 1). Rome itself had always written Latin. Change to "His Greek books soon dropped out of sight at Rome, where the clergy now wrote Latin."
