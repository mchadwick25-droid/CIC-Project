# Outline check: Hippolytus of Rome

## My account of the heart, written before reading the outline

From the census entry (teaser, card), the source sheet and what he is known for.

**The essence.** Hippolytus was a Greek-writing teacher and elder of the church at Rome in the early two hundreds. He held a stricter church and a clear distinction between Father and Son, and in his Refutation of All Heresies, written after 222, he attacked the bishops Zephyrinus and Callistus as confusing Father and Son and Callistus as forgiving too easily. In 235 he and the bishop Pontianus were exiled together to Sardinia. He is on the tree because nearly everything known of Callistus's Rome comes from him, a hostile witness and the losing side, and because the church kept him as a martyr beside the bishop he had opposed.

**Also told.**
- His own answer to Noetus: "two persons, one power" (Against Noetus 7). Documented.
- The Refutation's plan: every heresy traced to Greek philosophy, ending with those of his own day, Noetus and Callistus. Documented.
- His charges against Callistus: the past (bank, flight, mines), the forgiveness of all sins, clergy married more than once, the ark with clean and unclean, the women of rank. His charge; Contested in what Callistus ruled; McGiffert and Duchesne say overdrawn.
- His claim to a share of the apostles' "high-priesthood"; whether he led a rival church at Rome, was bishop elsewhere, or neither. Contested, each view credited.
- Easter tables (a sixteen-year cycle) and a chronicle; Jerome says the cycle gave Eusebius the cue for his own. Documented as Jerome's report.
- Remembered as bishop in the East, a presbyter at Rome. Documented.
- The book lost, found on Mount Athos in 1842 and printed in 1851 under Origen's name; most scholars since give it to him. Contested / Widely Accepted.
- Kept on 13 August with Pontianus; how he died is not recorded; Damasus's verse makes him a follower of Novatian's schism who at the end told his people to follow the catholic faith; Prudentius's horses are legend. Documented as reported; content Contested or Inferential-Thin.
- The statue of 1551 with the Easter table and book list. Widely Accepted that it is his.
- Origen heard him preach (Jerome). Documented as Jerome's report.

The approved heart in `Hearts.md` matches this in substance.

## BLOCKING

**B1. The poet's date is wrong (What came 3).** "A Latin poet half a century after Damasus" is not in the sources. Damasus was bishop 366/7–384, and the poet's hymn is dated "in the fifth century" (McGiffert, EUS 35698–35701) or to the fifth century (Mommsen, CHR 13727: "Prudentius poeta saeculi quinti"; sheet I5, "about 400"). That is a generation after Damasus at most. Also, "his editors call the story fable" mixes up the witnesses: Harnack, who wrote "pure Fabel" (HAR 11982–11984), is a historian, not an editor. MacMahon's word is "not historic" (HIP 863–868).
Correction: replace the sentence with "A Latin poet of the next century went further and had him torn apart by wild horses, like the Hippolytus of Greek legend; historians call the story fable (I5; EUS 35698–35701, HAR 11982–11984; optional; keep in the poet's voice, never as an event)."

**B2. Callistus's sentence is left open to a false cause (beat 3, Do not say).** Beat 3's summary reads "ran a bank, lost the savings of widows, was sentenced to the mines of Sardinia". A writer will join these as "sentenced for the theft". Hippolytus says the opposite. Callistus was freed from the treadmill. He was sent to the mines because he disturbed a synagogue on the Sabbath and the prefect had him scourged (HIP 13725–13751). The Leave out list cuts that scene. Separately, "lost" softens the charge: Hippolytus says he "made away with all" the deposits (HIP 13685).
Correction: in beat 3, change the summary to "a slave who ran a bank and, his enemy says, made away with the deposits of widows and other Christians; later, after a brawl in a synagogue, sentenced to the mines of Sardinia; and in the end put in charge of the church's burial ground". Add to Do not say: "Not that Callistus was sent to the mines for the bank, theft or debt. Hippolytus says the sentence followed a disturbance in a synagogue (HIP 13725–13751). If that cause is not told, say 'later sentenced to the mines'. Not 'lost the money'. His charge is that Callistus 'made away with' it (HIP 13685)."

## MINOR

**M1. Eusebius and Jerome do list a book "Against all heresies" (What came 1).** "Eusebius and Jerome list many of his works but not the *Refutation*" holds only through McGiffert's note. Both list an *Against All the Heresies* (JER 40701; JERL 56689–56690, "adversum omnes hereses"). McGiffert says it is a shorter, earlier book that "must not be confounded with the Philosophumena" (EUS 35835–35845). A writer could easily say "no ancient list names it", and a careful listener could be misled.
Correction: replace the sentence with "Eusebius and Jerome list many of his works, among them a book against all heresies, but historians take that for a shorter, earlier book, not the *Refutation* (D1; EUS 35835–35845)." Add to Do not say: "Not 'no ancient writer mentions it'."

**M2. Overdrawn, but true at its base (beat 3).** The outline asks for "overdrawn" only. McGiffert's judgment has both halves: "it can hardly be doubted that at least the groundwork of the account is true … greatly overdrawn" (EUS 35509–35521). Duchesne is harsher: even the facts need reserve (DUH 11450–11454). If only "overdrawn" is given, the force of the record goes one way.
Correction: change the beat's last clause to "say plainly that the story is an enemy's: historians judge it true at its base but greatly overdrawn, and one warns that even its facts need caution (EUS 35509–35521; DUH 11450–11454)."

**M3. Two things are placed twice.** (a) The Roman list's "elder" appears in beat 6 ("the elder Hippolytus") and again in What came 2 ("The Roman list keeps him an elder"). (b) "Nearly all we know of Callistus comes from his enemy" is placed in beat 3 (McGiffert's point) and again in What came 1 ("which is why an enemy's portrait is nearly all we have of Callistus"), and Who tells it allows either.
Correction: delete the last sentence of What came 2. In What came 1, change the sentence to "It became almost the only record of Callistus and of Rome's quarrels in those years (I1)", and in Who tells it, say "once, in beat 3".

**M4. Opening 1 is a held-back reveal.** "The man who wrote it down … He was Hippolytus … and the bishop was Callistus" is the stock hinge that Part 1 of the kit rules out.
Correction: "In about three sentences: Hippolytus, an elder of the church at Rome, wrote that his bishop, Callistus, called Noah's ark a picture of the church, with dogs, wolves and ravens inside, the clean beside the unclean; so the church should keep its sinners too. Hippolytus set it down to show how far, in his eyes, that bishop had fallen. Then the essence."

**M5. "Confessor" in Quote 3.** The Latin "sic noster meruit confessus martyr ut esset" uses a participle: "having confessed (the faith)". It ties back to "catholicam … fidem" in the line before. "Our confessor", glossed as "one who suffered for the faith", says more than the line does.
Correction: render the line as "So, by confessing the faith, our Hippolytus earned the right to be a martyr." Delete the **confessor** entry from Words to explain.

**M6. Words to explain: two missing, one over-long.**
- Add **the cemetery of Callistus**: "the burial ground that bears Callistus's name". The beat 6 calendar line would otherwise puzzle a listener just told that Callistus was put over a burial ground (EUS 35527–35530).
- Add **heresies** (in the book's title): "teachings he held false", as beat 5 already says.
- **Portus**: cut "at the mouth of the Tiber", which no cited line holds. Say "Portus, Rome's harbour town" (HIP 13691–13697, 852–863).

**M7. The Novatian likeness needs its source (Connection).** "The same question split Rome again in 251" is the outline's own framing. The quarrel of 251 was over those who had denied the faith in persecution (the live `novatian` story). Hippolytus's charge was about grave sinners in general.
Correction: say "a like question split Rome again in 251…", and cite the held likeness: McGiffert, "We are reminded greatly of Tertullian and of Novatian in studying Hippolytus' character" (EUS 35715–35717).

**M8. A citation for "near the place of his tomb" (What came 5).** The claim holds, but its source is not cited.
Correction: add "(DUH 11649–11650 note: 'Trouvée … près de son tombeau'; LP 108988–108991)".
