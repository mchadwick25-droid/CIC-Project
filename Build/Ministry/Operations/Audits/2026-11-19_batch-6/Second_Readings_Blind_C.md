## B6-50
Original (Greek): καὶ τῶν μελλόντων ἄσκησίν τε καὶ ἑτοιμασίαν.

And to train and prepare those who are still to come.

Note: τῶν μελλόντων is "those who are about to", meaning those who will compete next (it pairs with προηθληκότων, "those who have already competed", earlier in the sentence). No misread inside the passage. Just before it, the scan reads "γενόέϑλιον" for γενέθλιον and "ὃ κύριος" for ὁ κύριος.

## B6-57
Original (Latin): afflicti suppliciis Christiani, genus hominum superstitionis novae ac maleficae.

The Christians were punished. They were a group of people given to a new and harmful superstition.

Note: maleficae can mean "wicked" or "harmful", and it can also carry a sense of sorcery. "Harmful" is the plain reading.

## B6-58
Original (Latin): Haec partim nulla reprehensione, partim etiam non mediocri laude digna in unum contuli.

I have gathered these acts together. Some deserve no blame at all, and some deserve real praise.

Note: "non mediocri laude" is literally "no small praise". The sentence goes on ("so that I might separate them from his vices and crimes"), which lies outside the 13 words.
