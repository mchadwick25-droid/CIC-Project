# Outline check: Josephus

## My own heart (written before reading Outline.md or Hearts.md)

**The essence.** Josephus was a Jewish priest from Jerusalem who led the rebels in Galilee against Rome, surrendered in 67, foretold that the Roman general Vespasian would be emperor, and lived out his life at Rome writing his people's history. In the *Antiquities* he wrote a few short lines on John the Baptist, on James "the brother of Jesus, who was called Christ", and on Jesus himself. They are the only words about these men from a Jewish writer of the first century, so Christians kept and quoted them; the passage on Jesus has been argued over for centuries, because its wording reads as a Christian's (Contested). He is on the tree as an outside witness, not a member of the church.

**Also told.**
- His life, almost all from his own pen: a priest's son, a Pharisee by choice; sent to Galilee in 66 (his two books differ on why); Jotapata; the cave, the suicide pact by lot, his survival; the prediction to Vespasian, which Suetonius confirms; the chain cut in 69; pleading at the walls of Jerusalem in 70, held a traitor by both sides; a house and pension in Rome (Documented as his own account).
- What he wrote: the *War* (first in his own language, then Greek), the *Antiquities* (about 93), the *Life*. What the three passages say: John a good man killed by Herod for fear of rebellion; James stoned by the high priest Ananus, who lost office for it; the passage on Jesus (Documented in the text; how much is his, Contested).
- How Christians used him: Origen (mid-third century) knew the John and James passages, said Josephus did not accept Jesus as Christ, and never cites the passage on Jesus; Eusebius (early fourth century) is the first known to quote it, and used it against the anti-Christian Acts of Pilate; Jerome's Latin reads "he was believed to be the Christ"; Eusebius also built the fall of Jerusalem on the *War* as God's judgment on the Jews, a reading with an anti-Jewish force that must be told plainly and credited to him.
- What the passages can and cannot tell: how short they are, that the held sources give only the 1890 and older pictures of the debate, and that his death date is unknown.

## BLOCKING

**B1. The Library sweep misses a held later witness to the passage on Jesus, and one held post-1890 editor's view.** The sweep searched only "Josephus", "Iosepho", "Josepho" and "Ἰώσηπ", so French "Josèphe" was never searched (23 files). `cic/texts/michael-the-syrian_chronique-tome1-livres-i-vii-fra_chabot1899.txt` is the twelfth-century Syriac Orthodox patriarch Michael the Syrian, in Chabot's French (1899). At 11161–11240 he quotes the passage: "En ces temps-là il y eut un homme sage nommé Jésus, s'il convient toutefois de l'appeler homme ... On pense que c'était le Messie; mais non d'après le témoignage des chefs du peuple. C'est pourquoi Pilate le livra au supplice de la croix" (11233–11235). So a Syriac line of the text says "he was thought to be the Messiah", the same reading as Jerome's "credebatur esse Christus". Chabot's note at 11208–11209 says: "Il n'est pas douteux que la mention primitive de Josèphe ait été interpolée par un chrétien. Cf. Th. Reinach, Josèphe sur Jésus ... 1897". Reinach is not held. This is the question that went to Mark, and the sheet's section 6 ("Any scholarship after 1890 ... not held") is not exact. **Correction:**

- In What came 3, after Jerome, add: "A Syriac chronicle of the twelfth century, by the patriarch Michael the Syrian, gives the passage with 'he was thought to be the Messiah' (Michael, 11233)." Optional one clause, credited, no name needed beyond "a Syriac chronicle".
- Add to Do not say: "Not 'Michael proves the original said "thought to be"'. Say only that his copy reads so."
- Rewrite section 6 of the sheet and What came 3's last sentence as: "The only held view after 1890 is a one-line note by Michael's editor, Chabot (1899), that a Christian certainly added to the original mention, citing Reinach (1897, not held). No held source gives today's balance." Keep Chabot off the audio unless Mark says otherwise. Report the correction to Mark with the outline, since his ruling rested on "nothing after 1890".
