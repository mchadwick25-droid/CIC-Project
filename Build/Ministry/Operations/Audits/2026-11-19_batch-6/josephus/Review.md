**B2. The 1890 views leave out the view that the passage is wholly genuine.** McGiffert's first view is "It is entirely genuine. That carries one side forward, against the kit's "never carries any side's view forward" and Mark's "each credited". The split this needs also fixes the sentence's length. Replace "By 1890 an editor of Eusebius wrote that the passage had been disputed since the sixteenth century, some thinking a Christian copyist had added it, others that it was partly genuine." with "By 1890 an editor of Eusebius wrote that the passage had been disputed since the sixteenth century. Some, he wrote, thought a Christian copyist had added it all, others only part, and a few still held it wholly his own." Ewald's view (a hostile passage rewritten as praise) may stay on the card. Leaving it out tilts nothing.
*Do not say line to add:* "Not the doubting views alone. If the 1890 views are told, include that a few held it wholly genuine (EH-ed 9185–9187), or Whiston's defence (I8)."
