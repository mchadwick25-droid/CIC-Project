# Source sheet: Valentinus

Census id `valentinus`. Told within **Valentinian & other Gnostic Christianities** (`valentinian-and-other-gnostic-christianities`; spoken short name "Gnostic Currents"). Census dates: "at Rome, from Hyginus to Anicetus"; place Rome. The card (`Card.existing.json`) was used as a starting list only.

## Setting

**S1. When.** Irenaeus (IR 39968–39970, *Against Heresies* III.4.3): "Valentinus came to Rome in the time of Hyginus, flourished under Pius, and remained until Anicetus." LATER (Irenaeus, about 180, some twenty to forty years on); Documented as his report. Greek of the same line, as Eusebius kept it, in Holl's apparatus (HOLL 29827–29828): "Οὐαλεντῖνος μὲν γὰρ ἦλϑεν εἰς Ῥώμην ἐπὶ Ὑγίνου, ἤχμασε δὲ ἐπὶ Πίου καὶ παρέμεινεν ἕως Ἀνιχήτου". Eusebius repeats it (EUS 22154–22155). McGiffert's dates for those bishops (EUS 22162–22164): "Hyginus held office in all probability from 137–141, and Anicetus from 154 or 155 to 166 or 167, Valentinus must have been in Rome at least thirteen years." EDITOR. Plain words: "at Rome, in the middle of the second century", "from about 140 to about 160" (Widely Accepted; the years are the editor's).

**S2. Jerome's Chronicle.** JER 42747–42750, 42768–42769 (under Antoninus's third and sixth years): "Sub Hygino Romanae urbis episcopo Valentinus heresiarches et Cerdo magister Marcionis Romam uenerunt"; "Valentinus hereticus agnoscitur et permanet usque ad Anicetum." LATER (fourth century, built on Irenaeus). Not needed in the story.

**S3. The age of the teachers.** Clement (CL 52955–52962, *Stromata* VII.17): "It was later, in the times of Adrian the king, that those who invented the heresies arose; and they extended to the age of Antoninus the elder". Tertullian (TE 21332–21335, *Prescription* 30): Marcion and Valentinus "lived not so long ago,—in the reign of Antoninus for the most part". LATER. Documented. Plain words: "under the emperors Hadrian and Antoninus".

**S4. Where from.** Epiphanius (HOLL 28941–28948, *Panarion* 31.2): "τὴν μὲν οὖν αὐτοῦ πατρίδα ἢ πόϑεν οὗτος γεγέννηται, οἱ πολλοὶ ἀγνοοῦσιν… ἐν ἀμφιλέχτῳ μέν… ἔφασαν γὰρ αὐτόν τινες γεγενῆσϑαι Φρεβωνίτην, τῆς Αἰγύπτου Παραλιώτην, ἐν ᾿Αλεξανδρείᾳ δὲ πεπαιδεῦσϑαι τὴν τῶν Ἑλλήνων παιδείαν" ("most do not know his homeland or where he was born… it is disputed… some said he was born a Phrebonite, from the coast of Egypt, and was educated in Greek learning at Alexandria"). LATER (about 375, two centuries on), a report he heard and himself calls doubtful. Inferential-Thin. The tradition entry and an ANF editor (HIP 8705) say "from Alexandria" without qualification: EDITOR. Card: "No source we hold says where he was born" (only this rumor).

**S5. His teacher, by his followers' claim.** CL 52962–52963 (*Stromata* VII.17): "Likewise they allege that Valentinus was a hearer of Theudas. And he was the pupil of Paul." His followers' claim, reported by Clement. Contested.

**S6. A Platonist, by his opponents' word.** Tertullian (TE 20321–20327, *Prescription* 7): "From this source came the Æons, and I known not what infinite forms, and the trinity of man in the system of Valentinus, who was of Plato's school." Latin (OE 523–525): "Inde aeones, et formae nescio quae infinitae, et trinitas hominis apud Valentinum; Platonicus fuerat". TE 21332–21333 (*Prescription* 30): "Valentinus … the disciple of Platonism" (OE 1724: "Valentinus, Platonicae sectator"). Hippolytus (HIP 9126–9129, *Refutation* VI.29): "from this (system), not from the Gospels, Valentinus … has collected the (materials of) heresy … and may (therefore) justly be reckoned a Pythagorean and Platonist, not a Christian." LATER, hostile. Documented as their charge.

## Events

**E1. At Rome, named in his own time.** Justin, at Rome (IR 19254–19256, *Dialogue* 35): "Some are called Marcians, and some Valentinians, and some Basilidians, and some Saturnilians, and others by other names; each called after the originator of the individual opinion". CONTEMP (Justin wrote in the 150s–160s, at Rome). Documented. The census edge (greek-apologists) already tells this; one clause at most.

**E2. The bishopric he hoped for (Tertullian only).** TE 43109–43114 (*Against the Valentinians* 4): "Valentinus had expected to become a bishop, because he was an able man both in genius and eloquence. Being indignant, however, that another obtained the dignity by reason of a claim which confessorship had given him, he broke with the church of the true faith." Latin (KR 9633–9634, 9652–9653): "sperauerat episcopatum Valentinus, quia et ingenio poterat et eloquio, sed alium ex martyrii praerogatiua loci potitum indignatus de ecclesia authenticae regulae abrupit, ut solent animi pro prioratu exciti praesumptione ultionis accendi." The last clause ("as spirits stirred up for first place are usually inflamed with the wish for revenge") is Tertullian's reading of his motive. LATER (Tertullian at Carthage; the ANF editor dates the book "not earlier than a.d. 207", TE 42916: EDITOR), hostile, one witness. Documented as Tertullian's claim; the event Contested (no other witness). **Tertullian names no city and no year.** The see is not given; "at Rome" is not in the text. "Martyrii praerogativa": the other man's claim came from having suffered for the faith (ANF renders "confessorship"; TE 43111 note "Martyrii").

**E3. Expelled more than once (Tertullian).** TE 21333–21339 (*Prescription* 30): Marcion and Valentinus "at first were believers in the doctrine of the Catholic Church, in the church of Rome under the episcopate of the blessed Eleutherus, until on account of their ever restless curiosity, with which they even infected the brethren, they were more than once expelled." LATER, hostile. Documented as his claim. The Marcion story already tells this, with Valentinus named: here one clause, not quoted. Tertullian's Eleutherus (about 174–189) does not fit Irenaeus's Hyginus to Anicetus (D1).

**E4. What he taught: paired powers (Irenaeus on Valentinus himself).** IR 31519–31541 (*Against Heresies* I.11.1): "The first of them, Valentinus, who adapted the principles of the heresy called 'Gnostic' to the peculiar character of his own school, taught as follows: He maintained that there is a certain Dyad (twofold being), who is inexpressible by any name, of whom one part should be called Arrhetus (unspeakable), and the other Sige (silence). But of this Dyad a second was produced, one part of whom he names Pater, and the other Aletheia. From this Tetrad, again, arose Logos and Zoe, Anthropos and Ecclesia. These constitute the primary Ogdoad. He next states that from Logos and Zoe ten powers were produced … But from Anthropos and Ecclesia proceeded twelve, one of which separating from the rest, and falling from its original condition, produced the rest of the universe." Then: Christ brought forth by the mother outside the fullness, "not without a kind of shadow"; the mother "brought forth another son, namely, the Demiurge". Greek (HOLL 32862–32880): "ὁ μὲν γὰρ πρῶτος ἀπὸ τῆς λεγομένης Γνωστιχῆς αἱρέσεως τὰς ἀρχὰς εἰς ἴδιον χαραχτῆρα διδασχαλείου μεϑαρμόσας Οὐαλεντῖνος οὕτως ἐξεφόρησεν, ὁρισάμενος εἶναι δυάδα (τινὰ) ἀνονόμαστον, ἧς τὸ μέν τι χαλεῖσϑαι Ἄρρητον, τὸ δὲ Σιγήν …" (OCR as printed, accents normalized in the first word). LATER, hostile. Documented as Irenaeus's account. Plain sense: first an unnamed pair, Unspeakable and Silence; from them Father and Truth; then Word and Life, Man and Church; from these, more powers; one of the last fell away, and from that fall came the world below, made by a lesser maker.

**E5. Why pairs: Hippolytus's account of the school.** HIP 9150–9175 (*Refutation* VI.29): "There is, says (Valentinus), not anything at all begotten, but the Father is alone unbegotten … (The Father,) however, was solitary, subsisting, as they say, in a state of quietude … When, however, He became productive, it seemed to Him expedient at one time to generate and lead forth the most beautiful and perfect (of those germs of existence) which He possessed within Himself, for (the Father) was not fond of solitariness. For, says he, He was all love, but love is not love except there may be some object of affection. The Father Himself, then, as He was solitary, projected and produced Nous and Aletheia, that is, a duad". LATER (Hippolytus at Rome, early third century), hostile. The name in brackets is the translator's: Hippolytus introduces the passage as the teaching of "Valentinus, therefore, and Heracleon, and Ptolemæus, and the entire school" (HIP 9129–9131), and says the school disagreed whether the Father had Silence as a spouse (HIP 9137–9149). Documented as Hippolytus's report of the school; whether the words are Valentinus's own, Contested.

**E6. Thirty powers in fifteen pairs (Epiphanius).** HOLL 28960–28965 (*Panarion* 31.5): "τριάκοντα γὰρ οὗτος … αἰῶνας βούλεται παριστᾶν, οὖς καὶ ϑεοὺς ὀνομάζει, δεχαπέντε ἄρρενας καὶ ϑηλείας τοσαύτας εἶναι λέγων. ἕκαστον δὲ αἰῶνα ἀρρενόϑηλυν καὶ ζεῦγός φησιν αὐτὸς καὶ οἱ αὐτοῦ· δεχαπέντε δὲ δυάδας φασὶν εἶναι, ἃς συζυγίας καλοῦσι." ("He would bring in thirty aeons, which he also calls gods, fifteen male and as many female. He and his followers call each aeon male-female and a pair; they say there are fifteen pairs, which they call yokings.") LATER (about 375), hostile, drawing on Irenaeus. Documented as his report.

**E7. Valentinus's own view, against his pupil's (Tertullian).** TE 43117–43122 (*Against the Valentinians* 4): "Ptolemæus afterwards entered on the same path, by distinguishing the names and the numbers of the Ænons into personal substances, which, however, he kept apart from God. Valentinus had included these in the very essence of the Deity, as senses and affections of motion." Latin (KR 9655–9660): "eam postmodum Ptolomaeus intrauit, nominibus et numeris Aeonum distinctis in personales substantias, sed extra deum determinatas, quas Valentinus in ipsa summa diuinitatis ut sensus et affectus <et> motus incluserat." LATER, hostile. Documented as Tertullian's claim. Plain sense: for Valentinus the powers were thoughts and feelings within God himself; Ptolemy made them separate beings outside God. Important: the full thirty-power scheme of Irenaeus Book I is Ptolemy's school (IR 29870–29873: "I refer especially to the disciples of Ptolemæus, whose school may be described as a bud from that of Valentinus").

**E8. His own words: the heart as an inn.** CL 34289–34303 (the image itself, 34296–34302) (*Stromata* II.20): "Valentinus too, in a letter to certain people, writes in these very words respecting the appendages: 'There is one good, by whose presence is the manifestation, which is by the Son, and by Him alone can the heart become pure, by the expulsion of every evil spirit from the heart: for the multitude of spirits dwelling in it do not suffer it to be pure; but each of them performs his own deeds, insulting it oft with unseemly lusts. And the heart seems to be treated somewhat like a caravanserai. For the latter has holes and ruts made in it, and is often filled with dung; men living filthily in it, and taking no care for the place as belonging to others. So fares it with the heart as long as there is no thought taken for it, being unclean, and the abode of many demons. But when the only good Father visits it, it is sanctified, and gleams with light. And he who possesses such a heart is so blessed, that "he shall see God."'" OWN, kept by Clement, an opponent. Documented. ANF note (CL 34290–34291): "presence" is Grabe's emendation for "boldness of speech": EDITOR. Greek not held.

**E9. His own words: the angels' terror.** CL 32722–32734 (*Stromata* II.8): "And Valentinus appears also in an epistle to have adopted such views. For he writes in these very words: 'And as terror fell on the angels at this creature, because he uttered things greater than proceeded from his formation, by reason of the being in him who had invisibly communicated a germ of the supernal essence, and who spoke with free utterance; so also among the tribes of men in the world, the works of men became terrors to those who made them,—as, for example, images and statues. … for Adam formed into the name of man inspired the dread attaching to the pre-existent man, as having his being in him; and they were terror-stricken, and speedily marred the work.'" OWN. Documented. Plain sense: the angels made Adam; but a seed from above, placed in him unseen, made him speak greater things than his makers; they took fright and spoiled their work.

**E10. His own words: a homily on death.** CL 39090–39096 (*Stromata* IV.13): "Valentinian, in a homily, writes in these words: 'Ye are originally immortal, and children of eternal life, and ye would have death distributed to you, that ye may spend and lavish it, and that death may die in you and by you; for when we dissolve the world, and are not yourselves dissolved, ye have dominion over creation and all corruption.'" ANF prints "Valentinian"; its own index files the passage under Valentinus ("Death … errors of Valentinus, concerning, 425", CL 58375–58379; the passage is on page 425, CL 39055), and Clement goes on "For he also, similarly with Basilides, supposes a class saved by nature" (CL 39096–39098). OWN. Documented. Clement's reading follows: "the origin of death is the work of the Creator of the world" (CL 39099–39100).

**E11. His own words: the common books.** CL 46642–46650 (*Stromata* VI.6): "Valentinus … in his book on *The Intercourse of Friends*, writes in these words: 'Many of the things that are written, though in common books, are found written in the church of God. For those sayings which proceed from the heart are vain. For the law written in the heart is the People of the Beloved—loved and loving Him.'" OWN. Documented. Textual note "People"/"Word" is Grabe's (CL 46644–46645): EDITOR. Hard to hear once; not chosen.

**E12. His own words on Jesus's body.** CL 36157–36161 (*Stromata* III.7, left in Latin by ANF): "Valentinus autem in Epistola ad Agathopodem: 'Cum omnia, inquit, sustinuisset, erat continens, divinitatem sibi comparavit Jesus; edebat et bibebat peculiari modo, non reddens cibos …'" (Jesus ate and drank in his own way, not passing his food; his self-control was so great that food did not decay in him). OWN. Documented. Leave out: it needs long explaining.

**E13. Saved by nature (Clement's charge).** CL 40954–40958 (*Stromata* V.1): "if one is saved by nature, as Valentinus would have it". CL 32105–32109 (II.3): "the followers of Valentinus assign faith to us, the simple, but will have it that knowledge springs up in their own selves (who are saved by nature)". LATER (Clement, about 200), hostile. Documented as Clement's reading.

**E14. A vision (Hippolytus).** HIP 10054–10059 (*Refutation* VI.42): "Valentinus likewise alleges that he had seen an infant child lately born; and questioning (this child), he proceeded to inquire who it might be. And (the child) replied, saying that he himself is the Logos, and then subjoined a sort of tragic legend; and out of this (Valentinus) wishes the heresy attempted by him to consist." LATER, hostile, one witness. A wonder; Documented as Hippolytus's report only.

**E15. How he read Scripture (Tertullian).** TE 21806–21817 (*Prescription* 38): "For although Valentinus seems to use the entire volume, he has none the less laid violent hands on the truth only with a more cunning mind and skill than Marcion. Marcion expressly and openly used the knife, not the pen … Valentinus, however, abstained from such excision, because he did not invent Scriptures to square with his own subject-matter, but adapted his matter to the Scriptures; and yet he took away more, and added more, by removing the proper meaning of every particular word". Latin (OE 2345–2353): "Neque enim si Valentinus integro instrumento uti videtur, non callidiore ingenio quam Marcion manus intulit veritati. Marcion enim exerte et palam machaera, non stilo usus est … Valentinus autem pepercit, quoniam non ad materiam scripturas, sed materiam ad scripturas excogitavit". LATER, hostile. Documented as his view.

**E16. The end of the record.** Irenaeus: he "remained until Anicetus" (S1). Epiphanius (HOLL 29802–29807, *Panarion* 31.7): "ἀλλὰ καὶ ἐν Ῥώμῃ ἀνελϑὼν κεχήρυχεν. εἰς Κύπρον δὲ ἐληλυϑώς … τῆς πίστεως ἐξέστη … ἐνομίζετο γὰρ πρὸ τούτου μέρος ἔχειν εὐσεβείας καὶ ὀρϑῆς πίστεως" ("he went up and preached at Rome too. Coming to Cyprus … he left the faith … before this he was thought to have a share in piety and right faith"). HOLL 29762–29766: he preached in Egypt too, where his seed remained in several districts. LATER, about 375. Holl's note (HOLL 29829–29833): Epiphanius's Cyprus report "steht im Widerspruch mit Irenaeus", whose "remained" implies Valentinus died at Rome: EDITOR. No source gives his death. Inferential-Thin for any end.

## Impact, heaviest first

**I1. His students carried the school and changed it.** Tertullian (TE 43117–43128; KR 9655–9666): Ptolemy, then "Heraclean and Secundus and the magician Marcus", Theotimus; "Axionicus at Antioch is the only man who at the present time does honour to the memory of Valentinus, by keeping his rules to the full." KR 9663–9664: "ita nusquam iam Valentinus, et tamen Valentiniani, qui per Valentinum" (so Valentinus is now nowhere, and yet there are Valentinians, named through Valentinus). ANF's "as yet nowhere" (TE 43124) misreads "nusquam iam"; render from the Latin. Hippolytus (HIP 9640–9663, *Refutation* VI.35): the school split into an "Italian" branch, "of whom is Heracleon and Ptolemæus", and an "Oriental" one, "of whom is Axionicus and Bardesianes". Irenaeus's Book I targets "the disciples of Ptolemæus, whose school may be described as a bud from that of Valentinus" (IR 29870–29873). Origen (OR 23166–23168, *Commentary on John* II.8) answers "Heracleon, the friend, as it is said, of Valentinus" on John's Gospel. Eusebius (EUS 26653–26657): Bardaisan "was at first a follower of Valentinus, but afterward, having rejected his teaching…". LATER, hostile. Documented.

**I2. The answer: writer after writer against his school.** Justin names the Valentinians (E1). Irenaeus wrote five books, about 180, against them (the Irenaeus sibling). Tertullian wrote *Against the Valentinians* (TE 42892–42918). Hippolytus's *Refutation* VI (HIP 8701–9815). Epiphanius, *Panarion* 31 (HOLL 28831–29807), copying Irenaeus's first book. Irenaeus says Polycarp, at Rome under Anicetus, turned many from Valentinus and Marcion (IR 39869–39873; told in the Polycarp story). Documented.

**I3. His words survive only inside his opponents' books.** Every fragment (E8–E12) is quoted by Clement, who argues against him; the teaching (E4–E7) is described only by opponents. Card `uncertain`. The tradition entry: "For eighteen centuries, these groups were known almost entirely through such opponents", until the Nag Hammadi books were found in 1945 (census tradition `longDescription`); those books are not held. Widely Accepted.

**I4. The Gospel of Truth.** Irenaeus (IR 41174–41178, III.11.9): "those who are from Valentinus … entitle their comparatively recent writing 'the Gospel of Truth,' though it agrees in nothing with the Gospels of the Apostles". Whether Valentinus wrote the book of that name found in 1945: the ANF editor says it "is not, however, considered to be from the pen of Valentinus" (HIP 8738–8741, EDITOR); the tradition entry's voices say "possibly the author" (census). Contested. Card only.

## Where sources disagree or come late

**D1. When he was at Rome.** Irenaeus: Hyginus to Anicetus (about 137 to the 160s by McGiffert's dates). Tertullian: under Eleutherus (about 174–189), a generation later. The card notes it. Irenaeus is earlier and nearer; Tertullian's dating is generally set aside, but no held source rules. Say "in the middle of the second century".

**D2. Where the bishopric was.** Tertullian names no see (E2). Do not say "bishop of Rome". The job says "nearly became bishop at Rome": the held text says only that he hoped to become a bishop and another man won the place.

**D3. What is his and what is his school's.** Irenaeus I.11.1 gives Valentinus's own scheme (E4); Book I's long account is Ptolemy's school (E7). Tertullian says Valentinus kept the powers inside God, Ptolemy made them separate beings (E7). Hippolytus mixes Valentinus with Heracleon and Ptolemy (E5), and the card notes he copies Irenaeus elsewhere (HIP 10059–10065, note 736: EDITOR). Contested.

**D4. Where he came from, and where he ended.** Only Epiphanius, two centuries on, and on hearsay: Egypt, Alexandria, Cyprus (S4, E16). Inferential-Thin. Irenaeus's "remained" is the only early word.

**D5. Why he broke.** Only Tertullian, hostile, gives a motive: thwarted ambition (E2). Irenaeus gives none. Tell it in Tertullian's voice.
