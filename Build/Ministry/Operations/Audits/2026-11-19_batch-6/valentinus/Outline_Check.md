# Outline check: Valentinus

## My heart, written before reading the outline

From the census entry ("A teacher at Rome who broke with the church and founded a school that taught a heaven of divine powers in pairs"), the batch job, Mark's corrected job line ("hoped to become a bishop", no city) and the source sheet.

**Essence.** Valentinus taught at Rome in the middle of the second century, from about the time of Hyginus to Anicetus (Irenaeus's report, Documented as his report). He founded a school that taught a hidden Father from whom heavenly powers came forth in pairs, one of which fell, so that the world below came from that fall and was made by a lesser maker (Irenaeus I.11.1; Documented as his opponents' account; how much is his own, Contested). He is on the tree because his school was the great rival the second-century writers answered, and because almost all we know of him comes from those opponents; his own words survive only in their quotations (Documented).

**Also told.** Tertullian alone tells that he hoped to become a bishop and broke away when another man, with a confessor's claim, was chosen (Tertullian's claim Documented, the event Contested; no city named). His own surviving lines, kept by Clement: the heart as an inn, the angels' fear of Adam, death dying in you (Documented, OWN). For Valentinus the powers were within God, and his pupil Ptolemy made them separate beings (Tertullian's claim). His students carried the school on and split it into Italian and Eastern branches (Documented, Hippolytus); writer after writer wrote against it: Justin, Irenaeus, Tertullian, Hippolytus, Clement (Documented). Uncertain: where he came from and how he ended (Inferential-Thin).

## Verdict: FIX

The outline's heart matches mine in substance: the teacher at Rome under Hyginus to Anicetus, the school of paired powers and a lesser maker, credited to his opponents, and his own words kept only in their books. It also matches the heart Mark approved (`Hearts.md`) and his corrected job line: "hoped to become a bishop", with no city named. It keeps every ruling that touches this story. Tertullian's account of the bishopric is quoted here. No Polycarp, Marcion or Justin quote is used. Irenaeus, Marcion and Clement each get one clause. The beats carry the heart within the first minute and follow the record once. All three quotes are verbatim against their files, and my own renderings agree with the outline's. Two structural faults and one missing gloss need fixing before writing.

## Findings

### BLOCKING

**B1. Two items are placed in more than one slot, so the writer is set up to tell them twice.**
- (a) *His words survive because opponents quoted them.* This appears in the Opening ("they survive only because an opponent, Clement of Alexandria, quoted them"), in the Essence ("his own words survive only as short quotations in their books, chiefly Clement's"), and again in What came 3 ("Everything we have of his own words was kept by opponents ... Clement above all"). Who tells it says "say once". The kit says to say a single-witness point once and never tell an event twice.
  **Correction.** The Opening keeps Clement keeping the inn passage. The Essence keeps "Almost everything known of him comes from the men who wrote against him" and drops "his own words survive only as short quotations in their books, chiefly Clement's". What came 3 becomes: "**The record and the answer are the same books.** In writing against his school, its opponents kept the only account of it; for eighteen centuries it was known almost only through them, until its own books were found in Egypt in 1945 (I3; tradition entry; one clause). Bardaisan of Edessa, Eusebius says, began as his follower (I1; optional clause)." It no longer re-says that Clement kept his words.
- (b) *Axionicus.* What came 1 ends with "only one man, Axionicus at Antioch, kept Valentinus's teaching exactly as he left it". The recommended Ending (a) tells the same line again.
  **Correction.** Delete the last sentence of What came 1 ("By Tertullian's day, he says, only one man ... (I1)."). Axionicus is told only in Ending (a).

**B2. Words to explain is missing "the true rule" in Quote 2.** The rendering ends "broke away from the church of the true rule" (KR 9652–9653, "authenticae regulae"). A listener will not know at once that "rule" means the church's rule of faith, and this is the turning point of the life.
**Correction.** Add to Words to explain: "**the true rule** (Quote 2, Tertullian's *authentica regula*): the church's rule of faith, the teaching it held as handed down. The narrator gives the plain sense alongside: 'the church that kept the true teaching'." Add to Do not say: "Not 'the true rule' heard without its plain sense."

### MINOR

**M1. The Opening is a held-back reveal.** "Then the turn: these words were written by a teacher at Rome ... Valentinus" is the hinge that Part 1 of the kit (and the batch 6 Decision-Log item 4) says is not a stock move.
**Correction.** In Openings 1, replace "Then the turn: these words were written by a teacher at Rome in the middle of the second century, Valentinus, and they survive only because ..." with "Name him in the first sentence ('Valentinus, a teacher at Rome, once wrote that the heart is like an inn ...'). End the opening on Clement keeping the words, so that it leads into the essence."

**M2. Names named in the beats are missing from the Names field.** Theudas and Paul (beat 1), Justin (beat 2) and Marcus (What came 1) are not listed.
**Correction.** Add "Justin Martyr: link, optional (beat 2); census 'Justin Martyr'". For the others, write in place: beat 1, "a teacher said to have learned from Paul" (Theudas by role); What came 1, "Ptolemy, Heracleon and others" (drop Marcus).

**M3. Two pairs could be merged by mistake.** Irenaeus's second pair is Father and Truth (IR 31523–31524). Hippolytus's first pair is Mind and Truth, brought forth by a Father who was alone (HIP 9172–9173). Hippolytus also reports that the school disagreed over whether the Father had Silence as a spouse (HIP 9137–9149).
**Correction.** Add to Do not say: "Do not merge Irenaeus's account and Hippolytus's into one sequence ('Unspeakable and Silence, then the lonely Father brought forth Mind and Truth'). They are two accounts that do not agree. Tell each under its own witness, and say plainly that they differ (D3)."

**M4. Beat 7 says Epiphanius "heard" the Cyprus report.** Epiphanius frames only the birthplace as a rumour (HOLL 28941–28947). He states the Cyprus report as fact (HOLL 29802–29807). McGiffert (EUS 22156–22159) places Cyprus *before* Rome, and Holl (HOLL 29829–29833) places it after.
**Correction.** Beat 7: "Epiphanius, two centuries later, says that he went to Cyprus and left the faith there (E16; Inferential-Thin; an account that does not fit Irenaeus's)." Add to Do not say: "Not 'in his last years he went to Cyprus' as fact."

**M5. Beat 5's "had taken on death" reads more into the text than it says.** CL 39091–39092 reads "ye would have death distributed to you."
**Correction.** "and wished to have death given out to them, so that 'death may die in you'."

**M6. The citation for E13 is short.** "not by Christ's coming" is supported, but by CL 40959–40960 ("apart from the Saviour's appearance"), not by the cited CL 40954–40958.
**Correction.** In beat 5, cite "E13; CL 40954–40960".

**M7. Overlap with the Irenaeus sibling.** Irenaeus's What came 3 also tells "known for eighteen centuries almost only through opponents" and the 1945 find.
**Correction.** None needed beyond B1(a), which keeps it to one clause here. Add to Sibling (irenaeus-of-lyon): "Both stories carry the eighteen centuries and the 1945 find in one clause; no wording or image is shared."

**M8. The Library sweep misses some passing later witnesses.** The "Set aside" list should name what a search turns up. Missing are: Cyril of Jerusalem, *Catechetical Lectures* VI (npnf207 9620ff, drawing on Irenaeus); Athanasius's passing mentions (npnf204); Harnack, *Chronologie* II (1904; Valentinian mentions, including the pseudo-Anthimus fragment *On the Three Natures*, which is not held); and Harris–Mingana, *Odes of Solomon* (1920), on the Valentinian authorship theory. None is a held primary text of Valentinus, and none changes a claim.
**Correction.** Add these four to the sheet's "Set aside" list, each with its reason ("later summary from Irenaeus"; "passing mention"; "modern scholarship, nothing the outline uses"; "modern theory, not his story").

- **Words to explain.** The glosses are right (aeon, Demiurge, Pleroma, Gnostic credited, confessor, homily, Platonist), and no common word is glossed.
- **Length.** 2:40 (about 400 words) fits a thin life with three quotes. It is not padded, and a cut order is given.
- **Library sweep.** I checked the corpus-map bucket and ran a full-text search. No held primary text or major early witness is missing, and the Greek of Clement and Hippolytus and the Nag Hammadi books are correctly listed as not held.
