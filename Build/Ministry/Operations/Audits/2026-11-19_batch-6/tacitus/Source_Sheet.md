The job (Mark, 2026-11-19): Nero's fire: the fullest outside account of Christians punished at Rome, and Christus named as executed under Pilate, told by a historian who despised them. Shape: Outside witness.

## 1. Setting

S1. **The fire, 64.** McG (EUS 13843–13844): "We learn from Tacitus, *Ann.* XV. 39, that Nero was suspected to be the author of the great Roman conflagration, which took place in 64 a.d." DUC 2362–2364: "Pendant l'été de l'année 64, un incendie terrible détruisit les principaux quartiers de Rome." SCH 24282–24283: "Als eine große Feuersbrunst Rom im Jahre 64 verheerte". MODERN. Documented (the year, by every modern witness held). The chapter held (TAC) does not give the year or tell the fire itself; it begins after the fire, with the rites that followed (TAC 68 s1–s4). Annals 15.38–43, the fire itself, is not held (TAC-H 37–43).

S2. **Who Tacitus was, as held.** His own words place him inside Rome's ruling order: Pilate is "one of our procurators" (TAC 68 s7). OWN. Documented that he writes so; that he was a senator rests only on TAC-H 5 ("Roman senator and historian"), an editor's note. MON 9287–9289 (Montaigne, 1580s, in Florio's English, reporting Tacitus): "having said, how he had exercised a certaine honourable magistracy in Rome, he goeth about to excuse himselfe". MODERN, Montaigne's report of a passage not held. WH 141166–141171: "both Josephus and Tacitus were in favour with the same Roman emperors, Vespasian, Titus, and Domitian; and since Tacitus was an eminent pleader and writer of history at Rome". MODERN, Whiston's account, no source given. Plain: "a Roman of the governing class, a lawyer and historian" (Inferential-Thin beyond his own "our").

S3. **When he wrote.** YON 756–760: Ammianus's history "began with the accession of Nerva, A.D. 96, where Tacitus and Suetonius end". MODERN. WH 138262: "About A. D.110. Tacit. Annal. lib. xv. cap. 44." MODERN, Whiston's date. MAP 2331 and TAC-H 8: "Written c. 116 CE, describing events of 64 CE." EDITOR. RB 2566: "Tacitus, the Roman historian, who lived at the close of the first century". CEN card: "Wrote after 96". Dominant Modern Reconstruction: early in the second century, about half a century after the fire. No date is in his own text.

S4. **Where.** Rome; the Christians' movement traced back to "Judaea, the first source of the evil" (TAC 68 s7). Nero's gardens and the circus (TAC 68 s11). OWN. Documented as his account.

S5. **The tradition.** CEN `tacitus`: `toldWithin` null, status "Within Another World (A5)", era 1, region Rome. No tradition's spoken name applies; he is outside the church. MAP 2330–2333: "Tacitus is a hostile outside witness ... he is not a voice of the tradition and must never be compiled as one." EDITOR.

S6. **How much is held.** TAC-H 37–43: "THIS IS ONE CHAPTER, NOT THE ANNALS. 375 words. Book 15, chapter 44, and nothing else." EDITOR. Documented (the file is one paragraph).

## 2. The chapter, in its own order

All OWN, TAC 68, in the translation standardly identified as Church and Brodribb. Documented as Tacitus's account; what it reports about the Christians rests on him alone (see D1–D4).

M1. **After the fire, the rites.** s2–s4: "The next thing was to seek means of propitiating the gods, and recourse was had to the Sibylline books, by the direction of which prayers were offered to Vulcanus, Ceres, and Proserpina. Juno, too, was entreated by the matrons ... And there were sacred banquets and nightly vigils celebrated by married women."

M2. **The rumour.** s5: "But all human efforts, all the lavish gifts of the emperor, and the propitiations of the gods, did not banish the sinister belief that the conflagration was the result of an order." Latin, SCH 24313–24314: "non ope humana, non largitionibus principis aut deum placamentis decedebat infamia, quin iussum incendium crederetur."

M3. **Nero puts the blame on the Christians.** s6: "Consequently, to get rid of the report, Nero fastened the guilt and inflicted the most exquisite tortures on a class hated for their abominations, called Christians by the populace." Latin, SCH 24314–24316: "ergo abolendo rumori Nero subdidit reos .... quos per flagitia invisos vulgus Christianos appellabat". SCH's dots mark words it leaves out; the "most exquisite tortures" clause is held only in English.

M4. **Christus under Pilate.** s7: "Christus, from whom the name had its origin, suffered the extreme penalty during the reign of Tiberius at the hands of one of our procurators, Pontius Pilatus, and a most mischievous superstition, thus checked for the moment, again broke out not only in Judaea, the first source of the evil, but even in Rome, where all things hideous and shameful from every part of the world find their centre and become popular." English only. ROL 25875–25876 (Rolfe's note): "Tacitus, Ann. 15. 44, uses the correct form, Christus, and states that He was executed in the reign of Tiberius." MODERN.

M5. **Arrests, informers, the verdict.** s8: "Accordingly, an arrest was first made of all who pleaded guilty; then, upon their information, an immense multitude was convicted, not so much of the crime of firing the city, as of hatred against mankind." Latin, SCH 24316–24318, 24348: "igitur primum correpti qui fatebantur, deinde indicio eorum multitudo ingens haud proinde in crimine incendii quam odio humani generis convicti sunt". Plain: those who admitted it were seized first; on what they told, a huge number more. Tacitus does not say what "pleaded guilty" means: to the fire, or to being Christians (D3).

M6. **The deaths as a show.** s9–s11: "Mockery of every sort was added to their deaths. Covered with the skins of beasts, they were torn by dogs and perished, or were nailed to crosses, or were doomed to the flames and burnt, to serve as a nightly illumination, when daylight had expired. Nero offered his gardens for the spectacle, and was exhibiting a show in the circus, while he mingled with the people in the dress of a charioteer or stood aloft on a car." English only.

M7. **Pity, and his own judgement.** s12: "Hence, even for criminals who deserved extreme and exemplary punishment, there arose a feeling of compassion; for it was not, as it seemed, for the public good, but to glut one man's cruelty, that they were being destroyed." English only. The contempt (s6, s7, s12) and the condemnation of Nero (s12) are both his.

## 3. What came of it (heaviest first)

I1. **The outside evidence modern historians build on, and still weigh.** McG (EUS 13847–13849): "to avert this suspicion from himself he accused the Christians of the deed, and the terrible Neronian persecution which Tacitus describes so fully was the result." SCH 24279–24281: "Wir haben darüber das wichtige Zeugnis des Tacitus, das vielfach erläutert, aber auch vielfach mißverstanden wurde." DUC 2373: "Tacite, de qui nous tenons ces détails". HAR 4052–4053, 11645–11647: Tacitus's "multitudo ingens" set beside 1 Clement 6. WH 138262–138270: Whiston lists TAC 68 s6–s7 first among "the ancient citations" bearing on Jesus. RB 2565–2632: in a public debate in England in 1876, Robert Roberts quoted Tacitus with Pliny to prove "a vast multitude throughout the Roman Empire believing in Christ" in the first century (RB 2631–2632). MODERN. Documented: the chapter is the held moderns' main evidence for Nero's punishment of the Christians.

I2. **A Christian chronicle retold it, about 400, and called them innocent.** SUL-L 4783–4795: "Interea abundante iam Christianorum multitudine accidit uti Roma incendio conflagraret, Nerone apud Antium constituto. sed opinio omnium inuidiam incendii in principem retorquebat ... ergo [OCR '^tur'] uertit inuidiam in Christianos, actaeque in inno[x]ios crudelissimae quaestiones: quin et nouae mortes excogitatae, ut ferarum tergis contecti laniatu canum interirent, multi crucibus affixi aut flamma usti, plerique in id reseruati, ut cum defecisset dies, in usum nocturni luminis urerentur. hoc initio in Christianos saeuiri coeptum." SUL-E 10659–10665: "He therefore turned the accusation against the Christians, and the most cruel tortures were accordingly inflicted upon the innocent. Nay, even new kinds of death were invented, so that, being covered in the skins of wild beasts, they perished by being devoured by dogs, while many were crucified or slain by fire, and not a few were set apart for this purpose, that, when the day came to a close, they should be consumed to serve for light during the night." LATER (about 340 years after the fire). Halm's apparatus points to Tacitus at this passage (SUL-L 4744, 4801: "Tac. Ann. 15, 37", "Tac ann. 15, 40"). EDITOR. Documented that Sulpicius tells the same deaths in the same order; that he took them from Tacitus is the editors' reading (Halm, and the closeness of the two English texts), Widely Accepted. Sulpicius goes further than Tacitus: laws and edicts later made it unlawful to be a Christian, and Paul and Peter were condemned then (SUL-E 10667–10670). Those are his, not Tacitus's.

I2a. **The phrase turned on heretics.** SUL-L 5669–5670: "infamis illa Gnosticorum haeresis ... superstitio exitiabilis arcanis occultata secretis". SUL-E 11453–11455 (Roberts's note): "'superstitio exitiabilis': the very words which Tacitus employs, when speaking of Christianity itself (*Annal*. xv. 44)." Halm's apparatus at the next sentence ("origo istius mali Oriens atque Aegyptus"): "cf. Tac. Ann. 15, 44" (SUL-L 5695). EDITOR (both). The Latin of Tacitus's phrase is not held (SCH leaves it out); TAC 68 s7 renders it "a most mischievous superstition". Documented as the editors' observation.

I3. **Tertullian answered Tacitus by name, on another passage.** TERT-L 17832–17921, *Apology* 16: Tacitus "first put this notion" that the Christians' god is an ass's head, through his account of the Jews in the fifth book of his *Histories*. "At enim idem Cornelius Tacitus, sane ille mendaciorum loquacissimus, in eadem historia refert Gneum Pompeium, cum Hierusalem cepisset proptereaque templum adisset speculandis Iudaicae religionis arcanis, nullum illic reperisse simulacrum." (TERT-L 17915–17919.) The same line in *To the Nations* 1.11, TERT-C 4918–4922: "at enim idem Cornelius Tacitus, sane ille mendaciorum lo[quacissimus], oblitus affirmationis suae in posterioribus refert Pompeium Magnum ... templum adisse et perscrutatum nihil simulacri reperisse." English, TERT-E 2583–2588 and 10093–10095 ("most loquacious in falsehood"). LATER (about 135 years after the fire, perhaps 80 to 100 after Tacitus wrote). Documented. Tertullian does not mention *Annals* 15.44. The *Histories* are not held.

I3a. **Tertullian on Nero, without naming Tacitus.** TERT-L 13007–13011, *Apology* 5: "Consulite commentarios vestros, illic reperietis primum Neronem in hanc sectam cum maxime Romae orientem Caesariano gladio ferocisse. Sed tali dedicatore damnationis nostrae etiam gloriamur. Qui enim scit illum, intellegere potest non nisi grande aliquod bonum a Nerone damnatum." TERT-E 1835–1841. Eusebius quotes it (EUS 13836–13842, *Church History* 2.25). LATER. Documented. He points the Romans to "your records"; he names no writer.

I4. **A reader of form.** ROL 375–376 (Rolfe): "Tacitus did not find a follower until the time of Ammianus Marcellinus (330-400)." MODERN, Rolfe's judgement. MON 9200–9225: Montaigne read his history straight through and judged it "not a booke to reade, but a volume to studv and to learne". MODERN. Not for this story (see Leave out).

## 4. Links to other people

- **Tertullian** (`tertullian-s-voice`): answered him by name (I3). His live story says the *Apology* dismantled "Roman slanders against Christians" (CEN); the ass's head is one of them.
- **Sulpicius Severus** (`sulpicius-severus`): retold the fire and the deaths (I2). His live story names his *Chronicle* and the trial of Priscillian (CEN).
- **Eusebius of Caesarea** (`eusebius-of-caesarea`): tells Nero as the first emperor to persecute, quoting Tertullian, not Tacitus (EUS 13836–13842). His translator McGiffert weighs Tacitus in the notes (D1).
- **Suetonius** (`suetonius`, sibling): one line on Nero's punishments; his live story says he blames Nero for the fire elsewhere "but he does not link the Christians to it" (CEN). SCH 24348 sets Suetonius's line beside Tacitus's.
- **Pliny the Younger** (`pliny-the-younger`, batch sibling): paired with Tacitus by Roberts (RB 2565–2569) and Harnack (HAR 4052–4054). That the two men were friends is not held (N2).
- **Josephus** (`josephus`, batch): Whiston thought Tacitus drew on him for the Jews (WH 139599–139602). McGiffert: "Josephus is entirely silent in regard to a persecution of his countrymen under Nero" (EUS 13856–13857).
- **Ammianus Marcellinus** (`ammianus-marcellinus`): Rolfe's "follower" (I4); began his history where Tacitus ended (S3).

## 5. Where sources disagree, and how late they are

D1. **Were the victims Christians, or the Jews?** McG (EUS 13849–13857): "Gibbon, and in recent times especially Schiller ... have maintained that Tacitus was mistaken in calling this a persecution of Christians, which was rather a persecution of the Jews as a whole. But we have no reason for impeaching Tacitus' accuracy in this case, especially since we remember that the Jews enjoyed favor with Nero through his wife Poppæa. What is very significant, Josephus is entirely silent in regard to a persecution of his countrymen under Nero." MODERN. Contested as Gibbon's and Schiller's view, which McGiffert rejects. Gibbon and Schiller are held only as McGiffert reports them.

D2. **Is the passage genuine?** RB 3162–3164 (Bradlaugh, answering Roberts, 1876): "I have not admitted the reliability of the quotation from Tacitus. I am inclined to think that the quotation from Tacitus is liable to impeachment. I think the letter of Pliny is a fairly historic document." MODERN. TAC-H 53–54: "The passage's authenticity is not seriously disputed." EDITOR. Plain: in 1876 a public opponent of the Bible doubted it; the held modern historians (McGiffert, Duchesne, Schanz, Harnack) all use it as genuine. Contested only by Bradlaugh among held witnesses; no held witness argues the case.

D3. **What were they convicted of?** SCH 24300–24304: "Die Worte wollen aber nichts anderes besagen als: die Untersuchung stellte das Christentum der Angeschuldigten fest, nicht aber die Brandstiftung. Verurteilt wurden die Christen als Brandstifter, als solche galten sie aber, weil sie Christen waren." SCH 24305–24308: "Die neronische Christenverfolgung ist eine Verfolgung der Christen, nicht aber eine Verfolgung der christlichen Religion". McG (EUS 13862–13864): "This was not a persecution of the Christians in the strict sense, that is, it was not aimed against their religion as such". DUC 2385–2386: Tacitus "laisse bien voir que personne n'attribuait l'incendie aux chrétiens". MODERN. Dominant Modern Reconstruction (held moderns agree): the verdict fixed their being Christians, not the fire.

D4. **Tacitus's contempt as itself a judgement.** DUC 2392–2394: "Ceci, c'est l'appréciation de Tacite, qui étend ici aux chrétiens l'injustice et le mépris dont il aime à accabler les juifs." DUC 7234–7236: "Pour Tacite le christianisme est une superstition exécrable; les chrétiens sont des gens odieux, criminels, dignes des derniers châtiments." SCH 25276–25277: Pliny, Tacitus and Suetonius "zeugen von der tiefen Verachtung die die gebildeten Kreise dem Christentum entgegenbrachten." MODERN. Documented as their reading; his words (s6, s7, s12) carry it plainly.

D5. **"Christians" or "Chrestians"?** DUC 2078–2082 (note): "La populace romaine désignait les chrétiens par le nom de Chrestiani ...: quos ... vulgus Chrestianos appellabat. Telle est, en effet, la véritable leçon du célèbre texte de Tacite, Ann., XV, 44 (Harnack, Die Mission, p. 297)." MODERN. TAC-H 45–51: the Medicean manuscript reads "chrestianos", corrected by a later hand. EDITOR. SCH 24316 prints "Christianos". Tertullian says Romans mispronounced the name "Chrestianus" (TERT-E 1714). Contested as a reading; held only in Duchesne's note and the Library's header. Left to the card (Suetonius's sibling story carries "Chrestus").

D6. **Where did Tacitus learn of Christus?** Not said in TAC. WH 139599–139602: "In the first citation of the famous testimony concerning our Saviour from Tacitus, almost all that was true of the Jews is directly taken by him out of Josephus". MODERN, Whiston's view, about the Jews, not Christus. Inferential-Thin. The story says only that he does not say.

D7. **Did a separate, fire-linked persecution of Christians as a named group happen in 64?** TAC-H 54–59 names a modern debate (Brent Shaw against Christopher Jones). EDITOR; neither work is held. Not for the story.

D8. **Lateness.** Tacitus wrote about half a century after the fire (S3); the Christus sentence is about eighty years after Pilate's time, by the same dating. Tertullian is a century and a half after the fire; Sulpicius more than three centuries.
