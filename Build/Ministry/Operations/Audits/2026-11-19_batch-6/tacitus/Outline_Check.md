# Outline check: Tacitus

## My own account of the heart (written before reading `Outline.md`)

From the census entry (teaser, card), the source sheet, the batch job and what Tacitus is known for.

**The essence.** Tacitus, a Roman historian writing early in the second century, about fifty years after the fire of 64, gives in one chapter of his Annals (15.44) the fullest account outside the church of the Christians punished at Rome: Nero, unable to shake the rumour that he had ordered the fire, fixed the blame on the Christians and had a huge number killed as a public spectacle. To explain the name, Tacitus says Christus was executed under Tiberius by Pontius Pilate. He despised them. (His account: Documented; the date he wrote: Dominant Modern Reconstruction.)

**Also told.**
- The chapter in its own order: rites to the gods fail; the rumour stays; Nero shifts the blame; Christus under Pilate and the "superstition" breaking out again in Judaea and Rome; arrests of those who confessed, then on their information a vast multitude, convicted less of arson than of "hatred against mankind"; the deaths as a night show in Nero's gardens; and the pity that arose even for "criminals", because they died for one man's cruelty. Documented (his own words).
- His contempt and his condemnation of Nero are both his; he is neither friend nor foe of the church (Documented as his words; Duchesne's reading).
- What the chapter cannot tell: one chapter of about 375 words; no source given for Christus; unclear what "pleaded guilty" means; modern readers hold the verdict fixed their being Christians, not the fire (Dominant Modern Reconstruction).
- What came of it: the modern historians' main outside evidence for Nero's punishment of the Christians (Documented); Sulpicius Severus, about 400, retelling the same deaths in the same order and calling the victims innocent (Documented; that he took it from Tacitus, Widely Accepted); Tertullian calling Tacitus "most loquacious in falsehood", for his account of the Jews in the Histories, not this chapter (Documented).
- Disputes: whether the victims were Christians or the Jews as a whole (Gibbon and Schiller, as McGiffert reports and rejects; Contested); a doubt about the passage raised in 1876 by Bradlaugh, with no held witness arguing it (Contested).

This matches the approved heart in `Hearts.md` (2026-11-19).

## Findings

### BLOCKING

**B1. Q2's rendering misattaches "as an example to others".** In "So pity arose, even for criminals who deserved the harshest punishment, as an example to others", the last phrase, heard once, reads as if the *pity* arose as an example. TAC 68 s12 has "criminals who deserved extreme and exemplary punishment": the punishment is the example. This is the story's last line, so the misreading is what the listener leaves with.
Correction: in Q2, replace the rendering with: **"So even for criminals who deserved to be punished harshly, as an example to all, a feeling of pity arose. For it seemed they were being destroyed not for the public good, but to feed one man's cruelty."** In Words to explain, change the "exemplary" line to: **"exemplary (Q2): carried in the rendering as 'as an example to all', attached to the punishment."**

### MINOR

**M1. Beat 5 contradicts itself, and that invites a second telling of the deaths.** It says "Tell it plainly and in full", then "add only what it left out". The opening already gives the burning, the skins and dogs, the crosses and Nero as a charioteer. Under the kit, telling them again as a new event is an event told twice.
Correction: in beat 5, replace "Tell it plainly and in full; the opening already gave the image, so add only what it left out (gardens lent, games, chariot)." with **"The opening already showed these deaths. Here the story reaches that night: say only that mockery was added, that Nero lent his gardens and held games at the racing ground, standing in a chariot. Do not list the dogs, crosses and burning again."**

**M2. Beat 7 contradicts the outline's own Do not say.** Beat 7's third bullet opens "Modern historians build on it", but Do not say bars "historians still rely on it today" because the held historians stop at 1922. Its "Historians read the verdict this way" has the same present-tense problem.
Correction: in beat 7, change these to **"Later historians built on it"** and **"Historians of a century ago read the verdict this way"**.

**M3. The 1876 doubt is told with nothing to set beside it.** Heard alone, one debater's doubt sounds like a live dispute over whether the passage is genuine. D2 holds that every modern historian in the Library (McGiffert, Duchesne, Schanz, Harnack) uses it as genuine.
Correction: at the end of beat 7's third bullet, add **"The historians who weighed it all treated it as Tacitus's own (D2)."** In Do not say, add **"Not that the passage's genuineness is widely doubted. One debater in 1876 doubted it; the historians who used it treated it as his (D2)."**

**M4. Beat 2 says more than the text.** "Rome's leaders turned to the gods" is not in TAC 68 s2–s4, which gives no subject except the matrons and married women. Also, "night-long vigils" turns "nightly" into a length of time.
Correction: in beat 2, change it to **"Rome turned to the gods, with prayers, offerings, and vigils held at night by married women."**

**M5. The essence's "Blamed for the fire" states the charge more firmly than TAC.** TAC 68 s5 reports only "the sinister belief that the conflagration was the result of an order". McGiffert has "suspected" (EUS 13843–13844), and the approved heart has "rumored".
Correction: in the essence, change "Blamed for the fire, Nero put the guilt on them" to **"Rumoured to have ordered the fire, Nero put the guilt on them."**

**M6. "Hated for their crimes" narrows "abominations", and the Do not say list leaves room to name them.** TAC 68 s6 has "a class hated for their abominations" (Latin "per flagitia invisos", SCH 24315). Tacitus does not say what the abominations were. A writer could easily add the later rumours of eating flesh or incest.
Correction: in beat 3, change "for their crimes" to **"for their shameful acts"**. In Do not say, add **"Not what the 'abominations' were (no eating of flesh, incest or the like). Tacitus does not say (M3)."**

**M7. The verdict's qualifier needs its own Do not say line.** "Not so much of ... as of" (TAC 68 s8; "haud proinde ... quam", SCH 24317) keeps a share of the arson charge. Schanz says they were condemned as arsonists, and were taken for arsonists because they were Christians (SCH 24302–24304).
Correction: add to Do not say: **"Not 'convicted not of arson but of hatred'. Keep 'less of setting the city on fire than of hatred of the human race' (M5)."**

**M8. "The pity is the crowd's" in Do not say says more than s12.** TAC 68 s12 has only "there arose a feeling of compassion", with no one named as feeling it. The same goes for Ending (a)'s "the crowd watching in the gardens".
Correction: replace that Do not say line with **"Not 'Tacitus felt sorry for the Christians' or 'came to respect them'. He reports that pity arose, and names no one who felt it; he still calls them criminals, and gives Nero's cruelty as the reason (M7, Q2)."** In Ending (a), change "the crowd watching in the gardens" to **"the night in the gardens"**.

**M9. Beat 7 drops Tertullian's own qualifier.** "People took it that the Christians, close to the Jews, did too" states as fact what Tertullian gives as his guess: "from this, I suppose, it was taken for granted" (TERT-E 2581–2582; "inde ... praesumptum opinor", TERT-L 17914).
Correction: change it to **"and, Tertullian supposed, people took it that the Christians, close to the Jews, did too."**

**M10. Q3's rendering turns "of lies" into "of liars".** "Mendaciorum" is the plural of *mendacium*, a lie. The ANF has "most loquacious in falsehood" (TERT-E 10094).
Correction: change the rendering to **"that same Cornelius Tacitus, who truly talks more lies than anyone"**.

**M11. Two glosses say more than any held source.** No held source calls the *Histories* "earlier". Tertullian gives "the fifth book of his histories", in its account of the Jews' origin (TERT-E 2571–2573), and "in posterioribus" (TERT-C 4921). No held source gives the *Annals* as a history "of the emperors".
Correction: change the Histories gloss, and beat 7's "an earlier history of his about the Jews", to **"another of his histories, where he tells where the Jews came from"**. Change the Annals gloss to **"his history of Rome, the Annals"**.

**M12. Two passing words are not glossed.** Beat 7 has "heresy" and "Eusebius's *Church History*".
Correction: add to Words to explain **"heresy (beat 7): 'a teaching the church condemned'"** and **"Church History (beat 7): 'Eusebius's history of the early church'"**.

**M13. The date he wrote is told twice.** Beat 1 says "writing about fifty years after the great fire of 64", and beat 6 says "about fifty years later" again.
Correction: in beat 6, delete "about fifty years later (S3)", so it reads **"Tacitus wrote this in his history of Rome."**

**M14. There are more quoted phrases than the kit's 2 or 3, and more names than one hearing carries well.** Beyond Q1–Q3, the beats quote "hatred against mankind" (beat 4), "a class hated for their abominations" and "a most mischievous superstition" (beat 6), "deadly superstition" (beat 7) and "so fully" (beat 7). The story also names twelve people. Gibbon, Schiller and Pompey neither link nor carry the story.
Correction: in beats 4, 6 and 7, mark these phrases **"narrator's plain words, not quoted"**: "hatred of the human race"; "people hated for their shameful acts"; "a deadly superstition, as he calls it"; and "tells the persecution most fully". In Names, make Gibbon and Schiller **"by role: 'some historians'"**, and Pompey **"by role: 'a Roman general who took Jerusalem'"**.

**M15. The Library sweep missed a held Latin witness.** `cic/texts/apologists-fragmentary_reliquiae-corpus-apologetarum-vol9-grc-lat_otto1872.txt` 12174–12191 (Maranus's prolegomena, in Otto 1872) quotes *Annals* 15 in Latin. It has "Ergo abolendo rumori ... Nero subdidit reos, et quaesitissimis poenis affecit, quos per flagitia invisos vulgus Christianos appellabat. Exitiabilis superstitio rursus erumpebat non modo per Iudaeam, originem eius mali, sed per urbem etiam, quo cuncta undique atrocia aut pudenda confluunt celebranturque."
So the Latin of the "most exquisite tortures" clause, and of "exitiabilis superstitio" and the rest of s7 after Christus, is held. The sheet's Languages paragraph, N1 and I2a say it is not. No quote changes: Q1 (the Christus clause) and Q2 (s12) are not in it.
Correction: add OTTO to the sheet's §7 Found and Used. Amend Languages, N1 and I2a to say s6 in full and s7 from "exitiabilis superstitio" on are held in Latin at OTTO 12174–12191. In the outline, cite OTTO 12189 beside the "superstition" gloss ("deadly", from *exitiabilis*).
