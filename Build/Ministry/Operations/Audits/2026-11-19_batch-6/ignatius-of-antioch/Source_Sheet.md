# Source sheet: Ignatius of Antioch

Researcher's sheet for batch 6 (2026-11-19). The job: the bishop taken under guard to Rome, and his seven letters on the road: keep to your bishop, Christ truly suffered, and do not save me from the beasts. Shape: Martyr. The writer uses the outline, the shapes and the standard only; this sheet is the evidence behind the outline.

**Files and short names used below** (all in `cic/texts/` unless marked):
- **ANF** = `anf01_apostolic-fathers-justin-irenaeus.xml`: Ante-Nicene Fathers vol. 1 (Roberts and Donaldson's translation, revised by A. Cleveland Coxe, American edition). It holds Ignatius's seven letters in English, each chapter given twice: first the **shorter** Greek form, then the **longer** (ANF 4507–4527), plus the three-letter Syriac version (from ANF 8903) and the late *Martyrdom of Ignatius* (from ANF 11423). Also Polycarp's letter to the Philippians and Irenaeus's *Against Heresies*. Coxe's own notes (ANF 4362–4472, 8912–8924, 11334–11350) and the translators' introductions (ANF 4476–4654, 8928–8947, 11355–11405) are editors' words, marked **ANF-ed**. Unless marked "longer", every Ignatius quote below is the shorter form, the one scholars accept as his (D2).
- **EUS** = `npnf201_eusebius-church-history-life-of-constantine.xml`: Eusebius, *Church History*, in Arthur McGiffert's English (1890). McGiffert's notes are marked **EUS-ed**.
- **NPNF3** = `npnf203_theodoret-jerome-gennadius-rufinus.xml`: Jerome, *Lives of Illustrious Men*, ch. 16; Theodoret, *Eranistes*. Notes marked **NPNF3-ed**.
- **NPNF4** = `npnf204_athanasius-select-works-letters.xml`: Athanasius, *On the Councils* (*De Synodis*).
- **CHR** = `npnf109_chrysostom-priesthood-ascetic-homilies-statutes.xml`: John Chrysostom, *Eulogy on Saint Ignatius*, preached at Antioch. The translator's introduction is marked **CHR-ed**.
- **HOR** = `horologion-to-mega_venice-edition-grc_koutloumousianos1851.txt`: the Great Horologion of the Eastern Church (Venice, 1851), Greek, OCR.
- **CHRON** = `jerome_eusebii-chronici-canones-lat_fotheringham1923.txt`: Eusebius's *Chronicle* in Jerome's Latin, ed. Fotheringham (1923).
- **CEN** = `cic-website/data/world-census.json` (not in `cic/texts/`): the entry `ignatius-of-antioch` (not its `longDescription`) and the tradition entry `post-apostolic-house-church`.
- **CARD** = `Card.existing.json` in this folder.

**Conventions.** Line numbers are the file's own lines. ANF, EUS, NPNF and CHR are XML: quotes give the text with tags and the translators' footnotes removed; a footnote marker sits where a note was. Words in [square brackets] are the translators' own supplements, as printed. Where a quote runs over a page break, the page tag is left out.

**Language.** Ignatius wrote in Greek. **The Greek of his letters is not held** (no Lightfoot, Zahn, Funk or Lake edition is in the Library). So every rendering of his letters is made from the ANF English of the shorter form, checked against the longer form and, where Eusebius quotes, against McGiffert's English of Eusebius. One sentence is held in Greek: the "wheat" sentence, as the Horologion prints it from Eusebius (I6); that rendering is made from the Greek.

**Witness kinds and gaps.**
- OWN = the seven letters (ANF). Written on the road, at Smyrna and at Troas. Almost all we know of him comes from them (EUS-ed 19817–19820, 19882–19887). Genuineness: Widely Accepted for the seven shorter letters, with dissent (D2). Date: Contested (D3).
- CONTEMP = **Polycarp's letter to the Philippians** ch. 9 and 13 (ANF 3484–3494, 3608–3630). Written by the bishop who met him, soon after he passed through. McGiffert: "our earliest testimony to the martyrdom of a certain Ignatius and to the existence of epistles written by him" (EUS-ed 19771–19774), and he notes the notice "has been widely attacked".
- LATER, about 70 years = **Irenaeus**, *Against Heresies* 5.28.4 (ANF 53556–53560). Quotes him without naming him. (Irenaeus's census dates: c. 177–190.)
- LATER, about 130 years = **Origen**, who named him as second bishop of Antioch and a martyr at Rome. **Not held**: known only through EUS-ed 19769, 19777–19779 and ANF-ed 4632–4636.
- LATER, about 200 years = **EUS** 3.22 and 3.36 (EUS 17006–17009, 19740–20111), and his *Chronicle* (CHRON 42198–42202, and EUS-ed 19794–19797).
- LATER, 4th–5th century = **Athanasius** (NPNF4 59204–59231), **Chrysostom** (CHR), **Jerome** (NPNF3 39787–39831), **Theodoret** (NPNF3 20250–20290).
- LATE LEGEND = the *Martyrdom of Ignatius* (ANF 11423–11726). "These belong to the fourth and subsequent centuries, are quite contradictory in their statements, and have been conclusively proved to be utterly unreliable" (EUS-ed 19761–19766). ANF's own translators note "no reference to this narrative is to be traced during the first six centuries" (ANF-ed 11366–11368). Not used except to mark what stays out.

## 1. Setting

S1. **His church: Antioch in Syria.** He calls himself "the bishop of Syria" (Rom. 2, ANF 6961–6963) and asks prayer for "the Church in Syria, which now has God for its shepherd, instead of me" (Rom. 9, ANF 7273–7274); "the Church which is at Antioch in Syria" (Philad. 10, ANF 7858; Pol. 7, ANF 8806–8807). OWN. Documented. McGiffert: these passages establish he was bishop of Antioch, granting the letters (EUS-ed 19744–19748). Plain words: "Antioch, a great city in Roman Syria". (Antioch's size is not stated in a held line; say only "Antioch in Syria".)

S2. **Bishop, and in what order.** Eusebius: "Ignatius was known as the second bishop of Antioch, Evodius having been the first" (EUS 17006–17009); "chosen bishop of Antioch, second in succession to Peter" (EUS 19740–19741). Jerome: "third bishop of the church of Antioch after Peter the apostle" (NPNF3 39787–39788). Theodoret: he "received episcopal grace by the hand of the great Peter" (NPNF3 20252–20253). LATER. McGiffert calls Eusebius's date for the start of his office (69–72) "entirely unreliable" (EUS-ed 19795–19808). Contested in detail; Widely Accepted that he was bishop of Antioch.

S3. **Century and ruler.** Second century, early. No letter gives a year. Romans is dated by day only: "on the day before the ninth of the Kalends of September (that is, on the twenty-third day of August)" (Rom. 10, ANF 7316–7319; the gloss "that is…" is the translators'). The Chronicle puts his death under Trajan: "Ignatius quoque Antiochenae ecclesiae episcopus Romam perductus bestiis traditur" (CHRON 42198–42202, 42240: "Ignatius too, bishop of the church of Antioch, was brought to Rome and handed over to the beasts"); McGiffert reads it as "the tenth year of Trajan, i.e. 107" (EUS-ed 19796–19797). Jerome: "the eleventh year of Trajan" (NPNF3 39830). Contested (D3). Plain words: "early in the second century". Census `dates`: "died under Trajan, or later" (CEN).

S4. **Tradition.** Census `toldWithin`: `post-apostolic-house-church`, spoken name "The Scattered Households" (CEN, `informalName`). Plain words: "the churches of the generation after the apostles". Do not say "post-apostolic" aloud.

S5. **The road.** Smyrna: "I write these things to you from Smyrna" (Rom. 10, ANF 7309); Eph. 21 (ANF 5581–5583), Magn. 15 (ANF 6221–6223). Troas: "The love of the brethren at Troas salutes you; whence also I write to you" (Philad. 11, ANF 7894–7895; Smyrn. 12, ANF 8466–8467). Then "I must suddenly sail from Troas to Neapolis" (Pol. 8, ANF 8854–8855). OWN. Documented. McGiffert, following Lightfoot, says his road ran through Philadelphia and Sardis, north of Ephesus, Magnesia and Tralles, so that he did not visit the three cities he wrote to from Smyrna (EUS-ed 19970–19975). Editor's reconstruction. Plain words: "Smyrna and Troas, cities of the Roman province of Asia, on the west coast of today's Turkey" (ANF 7938: "the Church which is at Smyrna, in Asia").

S6. **What the beasts were.** He expects death in the arena: "trusting through your prayers to be permitted to fight with beasts at Rome" (Eph. 1, ANF 4703–4704); "May I enjoy the wild beasts that are prepared for me" (Rom. 5, ANF 7093). Coxe: "he was already condemned, in law a dead man" (ANF-ed 4446–4447). OWN for his words; Coxe's line is the editor's. Plain words: "condemned to be killed by wild animals in the arena at Rome". The arena is not named in his letters; say "the arena", not "the Colosseum".

S7. **His other name.** "Ignatius, who is also called Theophorus" (Rom. inscription, ANF 6863–6864; Smyrn., ANF 7933–7934). The Horologion glosses it as one "carrying God dwelling in his soul" (HOR 15545–15547). The legend that the child Jesus held him (hence "carried by God") is late (ANF-ed 4362–4364, 11379–11396). Optional; plain gloss "God-bearer".

## 2. Events

E1. **Taken under guard.** "On hearing that I came bound from Syria for the common name and hope" (Eph. 1, ANF 4703); "the Church which is in Syria, whence I am led bound to Rome" (Eph. 21, ANF 5584–5585); "God has deemed me, the bishop of Syria, worthy to be sent for from the east unto the west" (Rom. 2, ANF 6961–6963). OWN. Documented. **Why or by whom he was condemned, the letters do not say.** The scene before Trajan at Antioch is only in the late *Martyrdom* (ANF 11479–11516). Eusebius: "Report says that he was sent from Syria to Rome, and became food for wild beasts on account of his testimony to Christ" (EUS 19935–19936). Of his life before the arrest, nothing is known: "The greater part of his life is buried in complete obscurity" (EUS-ed 19757–19758).

E2. **The guard.** "From Syria even unto Rome I fight with beasts, both by land and sea, both by night and day, being bound to ten leopards, I mean a band of soldiers, who, even when they receive benefits, show themselves all the worse. But I am the more instructed by their injuries [to act as a disciple of Christ]; 'yet am I not thereby justified.'" (Rom. 5, ANF 7083–7092). Eusebius quotes the same (EUS 19994–20002); the Syriac has it (ANF 9413–9416). OWN. Documented. "I fight with beasts" is reserved to this story in batch 6 (README). **"Ten leopards" and "show themselves all the worse" are already quoted in the Scattered Households story** (CEN `post-apostolic-house-church`, `longDescription`), so they are not quoted here.

E3. **Churches come out to meet him.** "The love of the Churches that have received me in the name of Jesus Christ, and not as a mere passer-by. For even those Churches which were not near to me in the way, I mean according to the flesh, have gone before me, city by city, [to meet me.]" (Rom. 9, ANF 7280–7287). At Smyrna, delegations: Ephesus, "through Onesimus, a man of inexpressible love, and your bishop in the flesh" (Eph. 1, ANF 4709–4710); Magnesia, "through Damas your most worthy bishop, and through your worthy presbyters Bassus and Apollonius, and through my fellow-servant the deacon Sotio" (Magn. 2, ANF 5675–5677); Tralles, "Polybius your bishop … who has come to Smyrna" (Trall. 1, ANF 6279–6280). Ephesus sent a deacon, Burrhus, on with him (Philad. 11, ANF 7895–7896; Smyrn. 12, ANF 8471–8472). OWN. Documented.

E4. **Smyrna and Polycarp.** He writes from Smyrna "giving thanks unto the Lord, and loving Polycarp even as I do you" (Eph. 21, ANF 5582–5583); the Ephesians at Smyrna salute the Magnesians "along with Polycarp, the bishop of the Smyrnæans" (Magn. 15, ANF 6221–6223); to Polycarp, "I have been thought worthy [to behold] thy blameless face" (Pol. 1, ANF 8551–8552). Eusebius: "when he came to Smyrna, where Polycarp was, he wrote an epistle to the church of Ephesus … and another to the church of Magnesia … and finally one to the church of Tralles" (EUS 19961–19986), and to Rome (EUS 19988–19990). OWN; Documented that they met at Smyrna.

E5. **"Keep to your bishop": what the letters teach.**
- "I exhort you to study to do all things with a divine harmony, while your bishop presides in the place of God, and your presbyters in the place of the assembly of the apostles, along with your deacons" (Magn. 6, ANF 5852–5856).
- "So neither do ye anything without the bishop and presbyters … let there be one prayer, one supplication, one mind, one hope" (Magn. 7, ANF 5897–5901).
- "Take ye heed, then, to have but one Eucharist. For there is one flesh of our Lord Jesus Christ, and one cup to [show forth] the unity of His blood; one altar; as there is one bishop, along with the presbytery and deacons" (Philad. 4, ANF 7497–7501).
- "Breaking one and the same bread, which is the medicine of immortality" (Eph. 20, ANF 5548–5549).
- At Philadelphia, as he later wrote from Troas: "when I was among you, I cried, I spoke with a loud voice: Give heed to the bishop, and to the presbytery and deacons. Now, some suspected me of having spoken thus, as knowing beforehand the division caused by some among you. But He is my witness, for whose sake I am in bonds, that I got no intelligence from any man. But the Spirit proclaimed these words: Do nothing without the bishop; keep your bodies as the temples of God; love unity; avoid divisions" (Philad. 7, ANF 7717–7730).
- "See that ye all follow the bishop, even as Jesus Christ does the Father, and the presbytery as ye would the apostles; and reverence the deacons … Let no man do anything connected with the Church without the bishop. … Wherever the bishop shall appear, there let the multitude [of the people] also be; even as, wherever Jesus Christ is, there is the Catholic Church. It is not lawful without the bishop either to baptize or to celebrate a love-feast" (Smyrn. 8, ANF 8274–8287).
OWN. Documented as his teaching. Note: he writes this to churches that each already have one bishop with elders and deacons (E3); McGiffert names "the highly developed form of church government which they reveal" as the chief reason some doubted the letters (EUS-ed 19848–19852). Plain words: "the elders" for presbytery (not "priests").

E6. **"Christ truly suffered."**
- "Jesus Christ, who was descended from David, and was also of Mary; who was truly born, and did eat and drink. He was truly persecuted under Pontius Pilate; He was truly crucified, and [truly] died" (Trall. 9, ANF 6617–6619).
- "But if, as some that are without God, that is, the unbelieving, say, that He only seemed to suffer (they themselves only seeming to exist), then why am I in bonds? Why do I long to be exposed to the wild beasts? Do I therefore die in vain?" (Trall. 10, ANF 6671–6676). The longer form has the same argument (ANF 6683–6689).
- "And He suffered truly, even as also He truly raised up Himself, not, as certain unbelievers maintain, that He only seemed to suffer" (Smyrn. 2, ANF 8006–8008).
- "But if these things were done by our Lord only in appearance, then am I also only in appearance bound. And why have I also surrendered myself to death, to fire, to the sword, to the wild beasts? But, [in fact,] he who is near to the sword is near to God" (Smyrn. 4, ANF 8102–8106).
- Of those he opposes: "They have no regard for love; no care for the widow, or the orphan, or the oppressed; of the bond, or of the free; of the hungry, or of the thirsty. They abstain from the Eucharist and from prayer, because they confess not the Eucharist to be the flesh of our Saviour Jesus Christ, which suffered for our sins" (Smyrn. 6–7, ANF 8184–8186, 8220–8225).
- He will not write their names (Smyrn. 5, ANF 8143–8147); he calls them "beasts in the shape of men" (Smyrn. 4, ANF 8097–8098).
OWN. Documented. He names no group and gives the teaching no name. Plain words: "some who said Christ only seemed to suffer".

E7. **"Do not save me from the beasts": the letter to Rome.** Written from Smyrna, on 23 August (Rom. 10, ANF 7309, 7316–7319).
- "For I am afraid of your love, lest it should do me an injury. For it is easy for you to accomplish what you please; but it is difficult for me to attain to God, if ye spare me" (Rom. 1, ANF 6917–6922). (ANF's translators think he means their prayers, ANF-ed note at 6918–6920; Eusebius reads it as "entreating them not to secure his release from martyrdom", EUS 19989–19990.)
- "For if ye are silent concerning me, I shall become God's; but if you show your love to my flesh, I shall again have to run my race" (Rom. 2, ANF 6956–6958).
- "I write to the Churches, and impress on them all, that I shall willingly die for God, unless ye hinder me. I beseech of you not to show an unseasonable good-will towards me. Suffer me to become food for the wild beasts, through whose instrumentality it will be granted me to attain to God. I am the wheat of God, and let me be ground by the teeth of the wild beasts, that I may be found the pure bread of Christ" (Rom. 4, ANF 7036–7042). In the Syriac (ANF 9389–9391). Greek in I6.
- "Entreat Christ for me, that by these instruments I may be found a sacrifice [to God]. I do not, as Peter and Paul, issue commandments unto you. They were apostles; I am but a condemned man" (Rom. 4, ANF 7046–7049).
- "Let fire and the cross; let the crowds of wild beasts; let tearings, breakings, and dislocations of bones … only let me attain to Jesus Christ" (Rom. 5, ANF 7106–7112).
- "My love has been crucified" (Rom. 7, ANF 7195–7198).
- "As to those who have gone before me from Syria to Rome for the glory of God, I believe that you are acquainted with them" (Rom. 10, ANF 7312–7314): others from Syria had gone ahead of him.
OWN. Documented. Coxe: "Ignatius has been censured for his language to the Romans, in which he seems to crave martyrdom" (ANF-ed 4445–4446): the editor's report of a criticism, not a source.

E8. **Troas: news of peace at Antioch, and the last letters.** "It is reported to me that the Church which is at Antioch in Syria possesses peace, it will become you, as a Church of God, to elect a deacon to act as the ambassador of God [for you] to [the brethren there]" (Philad. 10, ANF 7857–7860); the same request to Smyrna, "that they are [now] at peace" (Smyrn. 11, ANF 8417–8425) and to Polycarp, "to assemble a very solemn council, and to elect one whom you greatly love … that he may go into Syria" (Pol. 7, ANF 8806–8818). Then: "Inasmuch as I have not been able to write to all the Churches, because I must suddenly sail from Troas to Neapolis, as the will [of the emperor] enjoins, [I beg that] thou … wilt write to the adjacent Churches" (Pol. 8, ANF 8854–8861). OWN. Documented. "[Of the emperor]" is the translators' supplement; the letter itself says only "as the will enjoins". The ANF chapter heading "on the close of the persecution" (ANF 7853–7854) is the editors'; the letters say only "peace". Eusebius: "he commended to him, like a true and good shepherd, the flock at Antioch" (EUS 20033–20035).

E9. **The end, as report.** No held witness saw his death. Eusebius: "Report says that he was sent from Syria to Rome, and became food for wild beasts" (EUS 19935–19936). Polycarp names "the blessed Ignatius" among those whose patience the Philippians "have seen [set] before your eyes", who are "[now] in their due place in the presence of the Lord, with whom also they suffered" (ANF 3486–3492), yet asks: "Any more certain information you may have obtained respecting both Ignatius himself, and those that were with him, have the goodness to make known to us" (ANF 3619–3630). The ANF note: "Polycarp was aware of the death of Ignatius (chap. ix.), but was as yet apparently ignorant of the circumstances" (ANF-ed note at 3624–3625). McGiffert: that he died a martyr "is too well attested to be doubted", and no tradition names another city than Rome except a late one in Malalas (EUS-ed 19943–19950). Widely Accepted that he died a martyr at Rome; the manner and date: not known from any early witness. Chrysostom (late 4th c.) puts it "in the midst of the theatre, while the whole city was seated above him" (CHR 10318–10321); Jerome, that he "heard the lions roaring" (NPNF3 39826–39829); the *Martyrdom*, the amphitheatre on 20 December (ANF 11654–11686). All late.

## 3. Impact (heaviest first)

I1. **Polycarp kept and sent on his letters.** "Both you and Ignatius wrote to me, that if any one went [from this] into Syria, he should carry your letter with him … The Epistles of Ignatius written by him to us, and all the rest [of his Epistles] which we have by us, we have sent to you, as you requested. They are subjoined to this Epistle, and by them ye may be greatly profited; for they treat of faith and patience, and all things that tend to edification in our Lord" (Pol. Phil. 13, ANF 3608–3618; Eusebius quotes it, EUS 20090–20101). CONTEMP. Documented (as Polycarp's letter; see D1 on its integrity). This is how the letters were carried on: the bishop who met him gathered them and sent them to another church that asked.

I2. **Irenaeus used his words, within a lifetime, unnamed.** "As a certain man of ours said, when he was condemned to the wild beasts because of his testimony with respect to God: 'I am the wheat of Christ, and am ground by the teeth of the wild beasts, that I may be found the pure bread of God'" (AH 5.28.4, ANF 53556–53560). The note: the Latin is followed; "the Greek of Ignatius would give 'the wheat of God'" (ANF-ed 53561–53566). Eusebius gives it as "As one of our people said" (EUS 20068–20071). LATER (~70 years). Documented. Irenaeus uses it to say that suffering readies the faithful "for the royal banquet" (ANF 53553–53556). McGiffert: "Irenæus does not name Ignatius" (EUS-ed 19774).

I3. **Eusebius made him a guard of the apostles' teaching.** "As he made the journey through Asia under the strictest military surveillance, he fortified the parishes in the various cities where he stopped by oral homilies and exhortations, and warned them above all to be especially on their guard against the heresies that were then beginning to prevail, and exhorted them to hold fast to the tradition of the apostles. Moreover, he thought it necessary to attest that tradition in writing" (EUS 19952–19959). His fame "is still celebrated by a great many" (EUS 19741–19742). He lists the seven letters by name (EUS 19961–20035) and counts Ignatius with Clement among those "who have transmitted the apostolic doctrine to us in writings still extant" (EUS 20173–20186). LATER (~200 years). Documented as Eusebius's view. McGiffert: Eusebius adds nothing the letters do not hold, except that he was second bishop and died at Rome (EUS-ed 19779–19784).

I4. **His insistence on Christ's real flesh became evidence in later debates about Christ.** Athanasius, in the Nicene quarrels: "Ignatius, for instance, who was appointed Bishop in Antioch after the Apostles, and became a martyr of Christ, writes concerning the Lord thus: 'There is one physician, fleshly and spiritual, originate and unoriginate, God in man, true life in death, both from Mary and from God'" (*De Synodis* 47, NPNF4 59204–59210; the line is Eph. 7). Theodoret, in his *Eranistes*, opens his witnesses with "Testimony of Saint Ignatius, bishop of Antioch, and martyr" and quotes Smyrn. 1 on Christ "really in the time of Pontius Pilate and of Herod the tetrarch crucified for our sake in the flesh" (NPNF3 20268–20290). LATER (~250 and ~340 years). Documented.

I5. **Antioch kept his feast and, by a late tradition, his remains.** Chrysostom preached on his feast: "To-day again, this blessed and noble martyr Ignatius has succeeded to her feast" (CHR 9955–9956); "Ye sent forth a Bishop, and received a martyr" (CHR 10355); he tells of the cities carrying his remains "upon their shoulders as far as this city" (CHR 10365–10367). Jerome: "the remains of his body lie in Antioch outside the Daphnitic gate in the cemetery" (NPNF3 39830–39831). The translator: "According to a tradition which cannot be traced back earlier than the latter part of the fourth century the reliques were translated from Rome to Antioch" (CHR-ed 9814–9817). LATER (~280 years). Documented that the feast was kept; the return of the remains: tradition, Inferential-Thin.

I6. **The Eastern Church's prayer book.** The Horologion keeps him on 20 December: "μνήμη τοῦ ἁγίου Ἱερομάρτυρος Ἰγνατίου, τοῦ Θεοφόρου" (HOR 15536–15538), and quotes, citing Eusebius: "Σῖτός εἰμι τοῦ Θεοῦ, καὶ δι᾽ ὀδόντων θηρίων ἀλήθομαι, ἵνα καθαρὸς ἄρτος εὑρεθῶ" (HOR 15547–15552; OCR prints "ΒΊτος εἰμὶ τοῦ Θ εοὗ, και δὶ ὀδόντων ϑηρίων ἀλήϑομα!, ἵνα καϑαρὸς ἄρτος εὐρεϑῶ"). "I am God's wheat, and I am ground by the teeth of beasts, that I may be found pure bread." The same entry repeats the legends: disciple of John, second bishop after Evodius, given to lions (HOR 15540–15543). LATER (1851). Documented for the feast as printed in 1851. No held source says who keeps it now.

I7. **The letters became a battlefield.** "There are, in all, fifteen Epistles which bear the name of Ignatius" (ANF-ed 4490–4491); "the first eight … are spurious" (ANF-ed 4499–4505); of the seven, "we possess two Greek recensions, a shorter and a longer" (ANF-ed 4509–4511). "They contain numerous statements which bear on points of ecclesiastical order that have long divided the Christian world" (ANF-ed 4479–4481). Daillé rejected all of them in 1666; Pearson defended the shorter form in 1672 (ANF-ed 4532–4537). Cureton argued in 1845 that only three Syriac letters were genuine (ANF-ed 4586–4590). McGiffert, 1890: Zahn and Lightfoot settled it for the seven shorter Greek letters, "candid scholars almost unanimously admit" it, though "their genuineness is still stoutly denied by some" (EUS-ed 19838–19847). Editors' accounts. Contested over three centuries; Widely Accepted by 1890 (D2).

I8. **"Catholic Church."** Smyrn. 8 holds "wherever Jesus Christ is, there is the Catholic Church" (ANF 8285–8286). Coxe: "in this letter we first find the use of the phrase 'Catholic Church' in patristic writings" (ANF-ed 4469–4470). Coxe's claim, an 1885 editor's; he writes it in a paragraph about the letter to the Romans, though the phrase is in Smyrnaeans. The CARD already carries it. Not for the story (Outline, Leave out).

## 5. Where sources disagree or come late

D1. **Polycarp's two chapters.** Ch. 9 speaks of Ignatius as having suffered; ch. 13 asks for news of him and sends his letters. ANF-ed reads it as Polycarp knowing of the death but not its details (ANF-ed at 3624–3625). McGiffert: the notice "has been widely attacked", but "there is no good reason to doubt it" (EUS-ed 19771–19772).

D2. **Which letters are his.** Fifteen under his name; eight forged; seven in a longer and a shorter Greek form, and three in Syriac (I7). The shorter Greek seven: Widely Accepted (McGiffert, 1890), with dissent then and since (census `post-apostolic-house-church`, `sourcing`: "authorship and dating … remain a" matter of rival readings). The story says once that his letters were long argued over, and that most scholars came to accept seven of them.

D3. **When he died.** The Chronicle: under Trajan, read as 107 (CHRON 42198–42202; EUS-ed 19796–19797). Jerome: Trajan's eleventh year (NPNF3 39830). Zahn and Lightfoot: only "under Trajan"; Harnack: under Hadrian (EUS-ed 19813–19816, 19866–19871). ANF-ed: 107, "or, as some think … a.d. 116" (ANF-ed 4652–4654). Contested. Say "early in the second century".

D4. **Bishop number.** Second (Eusebius, Origen per EUS-ed 19777–19779), third after Peter (Jerome), made bishop by Peter (Theodoret; Apostolic Constitutions say Paul, NPNF3-ed 20254). Contested; not told.

D5. **How he died.** Theatre (Chrysostom), lions (Jerome), amphitheatre on 20 December (*Martyrdom*). All late (E9). Not told as fact.

D6. **"Disciple of John."** Tradition only: Coxe calls it "a tradition by no means inconsistent" (ANF-ed 4365–4367); the *Martyrdom* (ANF 11438–11439) and HOR (15540) state it. CHR-ed: "does not rest on any trustworthy evidence" (CHR-ed 9809–9810). Leave out.

D7. **The peace at Antioch.** The letters say only "peace" (E8). The editors' heading reads it as the end of persecution. Not settled by any held source.

**How many files.** 627 files name an "Ignatius" as a whole word; most are Ignatius of Loyola and the Jesuit records, the patriarch Ignatius of Constantinople, Ignatius Xanthopoulos, or Ignatius the Deacon. About 97 name Ignatius of Antioch or "Ignatian" letters.

**Used.** ANF (his letters, the Syriac version, Polycarp, Irenaeus, the *Martyrdom* only for what stays out, the editors' notes); EUS (3.22, 3.36 and McGiffert's notes); NPNF3 (Jerome ch. 16; Theodoret); NPNF4 (Athanasius); CHR (Chrysostom's eulogy); CHRON (the Chronicle entry); HOR (the feast and the Greek of the wheat line).

**Set aside, and why.**
- `jerome_opera-pl23-part1-with-regula-pachomii-lat_migne1883.txt` (51219–51423): Jerome's ch. 16 in Latin with a later Greek version (Sophronius's), not Ignatius's own Greek; NPNF3 carries the same in English.
- `nicephorus-callistus_historia-ecclesiastica-with-hesychast-tracts-pg147-grc-lat_migne1865.txt`, `acta-sanctorum_*`, `nikodemos-hagiorite_synaxaristes-*`: medieval and later lives built on Eusebius and the *Martyrdom*; nothing earlier.
- `npnf202_socrates-sozomen-ecclesiastical-histories.xml`: Socrates's story that Ignatius brought antiphonal singing to Antioch after a vision of angels (reported at NPNF3-ed 10473–10476; Lightfoot there doubts it). A wonder story, fifth century. Leave out.
- `john-malalas_chronographia-grc-lat_dindorf1831.txt`: the late tradition that he died at Antioch (EUS-ed 19946–19950). Leave out.
- `origen_commentarii-in-evangelium-matthaei-tomus5-lat_lommatzsch1835.txt` (928–1085): the editor's preface discussing Origen's citations, not Origen's own words.
- `gebhardt_acta-martyrum-selecta-lat-grc-deu_1902.txt`, `knopf_ausgewaehlte-maertyrerakten-lat-grc-deu_krueger1929.txt`: cite Lightfoot and Zahn's editions in bibliographies only.
- ANF's spurious letters (ANF 9509–11325) and the longer recension: later, padded; used only to check the shorter form.
