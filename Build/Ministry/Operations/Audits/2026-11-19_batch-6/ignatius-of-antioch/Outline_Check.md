# Outline check: Ignatius of Antioch

## My own heart (written before reading Outline.md)

From the census entry (`teaser`, `dates`, `region`, `toldWithin`; not its `longDescription`), the batch job and the source sheet.

**The essence.** Early in the second century Ignatius, bishop of Antioch in Syria, was taken under a guard of soldiers to Rome to be killed by wild animals. On the road, at Smyrna and Troas, he wrote seven letters to the churches that came out to meet him: keep together under your one bishop with the elders and deacons, hold to one Eucharist, and hold that Christ was truly born, truly suffered and truly died, against those who said he only seemed to; and, to Rome, do not save me from the beasts. He is on the tree because those letters, gathered by Polycarp and sent on, are among the earliest Christian writings after the apostles, the earliest to set out the church under one bishop, and were read for centuries as a martyr's own voice.

**Also told.**
- The churches of Asia sent bishops, elders and deacons to meet him at Smyrna; he met Polycarp there (Documented, OWN).
- What the letters teach: the bishop presides with elders and deacons; nothing done without the bishop; one Eucharist, one altar (Documented, OWN).
- Christ's real suffering, tied to his own coming death: if Christ only seemed to suffer, "why am I in bonds?" (Documented, OWN).
- The letter to Rome: do not hinder me; "I am the wheat of God" (Documented, OWN).
- At Troas, news that the church at Antioch had peace; he asked the churches to send envoys; then sailed on (Documented, OWN).
- His death at Rome is known only by report; Polycarp soon spoke of him among the blessed dead and asked for news (Widely Accepted; manner and date not known).
- What came of him: Polycarp collected and sent on the letters; Irenaeus quoted the wheat line unnamed; Eusebius made him a guardian of the apostles' tradition; Athanasius and Theodoret cited him on Christ's flesh; Antioch kept his feast (Chrysostom); the letters were long argued over, and seven shorter Greek letters came to be widely accepted (Widely Accepted, with dissent).

Then the outline was read.

## Verdict: FIX

Two BLOCKING findings, both small and worded below for a word-for-word fix. Everything else checked holds.

**Heart.** The outline's essence and Also told match mine in substance, and match the heart Mark approved (`Hearts.md`, 2026-11-19). No major contribution is missing. The three teachings sit in Also told, not the essence. That is the shape Mark approved, and the job line's three phrases are all carried. The opening, essence and beat 1 fall inside the first minute. The beats follow the road once, in order (Antioch, the road, Smyrna, Rome letter, Troas, sailing). The death is held back to What came, as report.

**Rulings.** These are all kept: the job line; "I fight with beasts" reserved to this story; no quote from the approved Polycarp story; "ten leopards" and "show themselves all the worse" kept out (Scattered Households); Irenaeus as one clause. Polycarp's share is a little over the slate's "here a sentence" (MINOR 5).

**Quotes.** I made my own renderings first. Quote 1 is verbatim (ANF 7083–7086; Eusebius at EUS 19994–19995 agrees). Quote 2 is verbatim (ANF 6671–6676). Quote 3's Greek is verbatim at HOR 15547–15552, read through the OCR. The outline's renderings match mine, except one point in Quote 3 (MINOR 1). None of the three is used anywhere else on the tree: I checked every census `longDescription` (except this subject's), every batch 6 `Outline.md` and Polycarp's brief.

**Sources.** I opened every cited line in ANF, EUS, NPNF3, NPNF4, CHR and HOR. Beats 1, 3, 4 and 6, What came 1, 2, 4 and 5, the Setting and Who tells it all hold, with the right confidence and witness kind, except where noted below.

**Words to explain.** The list is good on elders, Eucharist, Asia, feast, Troas and the wheat image. It has one missing gloss that is BLOCKING (Philadelphia) and two small ones (MINOR 8).

**Length.** About 3 minutes fits. The beats are dense but none is filler, and the cut-first list is sensible.

## BLOCKING

**B1. Philadelphia needs a gloss.** Beat 5 sets a scene at Philadelphia, and he wrote one of his letters to that church. A listener hears the American city. This is the same reason the outline gives for glossing "Asia". Add to Words to explain:
> - **Philadelphia**: "Philadelphia, another city of the Roman province of Asia" (ANF 7335: "the Church … which is at Philadelphia, in Asia"). Never left bare: the modern city misleads.

**B2. Connection: the claim about what would have happened without Polycarp is unsupported.** "Without Polycarp's gathering there would be no Ignatius to tell (E1, I1)" goes beyond any held source. Irenaeus and Eusebius knew the letters, and no source says what would have happened without Polycarp. Replace "First, the letters are almost all we know of him; without Polycarp's gathering there would be no Ignatius to tell (E1, I1)." with:
> First, the letters are almost all we know of him, and the earliest word of how they were carried on is Polycarp's: he sent those he held to Philippi when that church asked (E1, EUS-ed 19817–19820; I1).

Also add to Do not say:
> - Not "without Polycarp we would not have his letters" or "Polycarp saved the letters". Say that Polycarp sent on the ones he held, when Philippi asked (I1).

## MINOR

1. **Quote 3 drops "of Christ".** The Horologion's Greek is Eusebius quoting Irenaeus (EUS 20068–20071). The ANF note says that this form leaves the end of the line short (ANF-ed 53561–53566). The letter itself, in the held ANF shorter form, ends "the pure bread of Christ" (ANF 7041–7042). Beat 4 presents the line as his letter to Rome. So render it as: "I am God's wheat, and I am ground by the teeth of wild beasts, so that I may be found the pure bread of Christ." Source it as "Greek (HOR 15547–15552) for 'God's wheat … found pure bread'; 'of Christ' from the letter, ANF 7041–7042." Keep the Do not say line against "the wheat of Christ".
2. **Beat 2 says more than its source.** "Bishops came there from three other cities, with their elders and deacons" is true only of Magnesia (ANF 5675–5677). Tralles sent only its bishop (ANF 6279–6280), and Ephesus sent its bishop and a deacon. Also, the deacon who went on with him was sent by Ephesus and Smyrna together (ANF 7895–7896, 8466–8472). Replace with: "Bishops came there from three other cities: from Ephesus its bishop, Onesimus, and from Magnesia and Tralles theirs; Magnesia's came with two elders and a deacon (E3). Ephesus and Smyrna sent a deacon on with him (E3)."
3. **What came 5 has two overstatements.** "Padded" is the researcher's word, not a source's: say "a longer form and a shorter one". "Fought over them for three centuries" goes past the held record, which runs from Ussher in 1644 and Daillé in 1666 to McGiffert in 1890 (ANF-ed 4517–4537; EUS-ed 19838–19847). Say "scholars argued over them from the 1600s to the 1800s".
4. **What came 3: Athanasius.** In *De Synodis* 47 he cites Ignatius on Christ as "originate on account of the flesh … yet unoriginate" (NPNF4 59204–59225). That is Christ as both flesh and God, not Christ's real flesh alone. Write: "Athanasius quoted his words on Christ as both flesh and God in the fourth-century arguments over who Christ was; in the fifth, Theodoret quoted his words on Christ crucified in the flesh (I4)."
5. **Polycarp's share is more than the slate set.** The slate (README) says Polycarp gets "here a sentence". The outline gives him beat 2, three sentences in What came 1, and Ending (b). The Polycarp story does not tell the letters, so the jobs do not overlap, but the measure is over. Make What came 1's Polycarp part one sentence: "Polycarp, writing soon after to Philippi, counted 'the blessed Ignatius' among those who had suffered with the Lord, asked for surer news of him, and sent on the letters he held, as Philippi had asked (E9, D1, I1)." Drop Ending (b), which also ends on Polycarp rather than on the person.
6. **What he defended is not marked** (the kit's Part 3 asks for it). Add under What came of him: "Defended: that Christ was truly born, truly suffered and truly rose, against those who said he only seemed to (E6); and one church gathered at one Eucharist with its bishop, elders and deacons (E5)."
7. **Do not say: two easy errors are missing.** Add:
   - Not "the Roman Christians meant to buy or plead his release" or "the Christians bribed the guards". He does not say how they might spare him. His translators think he means their prayers, and Eusebius reads it as his release (E7, ANF-ed at 6918–6920). The note on gifts to the soldiers is the translators' guess (ANF note at 7087). Say "he begged them not to work to spare him".
   - Not "Ephesus alone sent the deacon" (see MINOR 2).
8. **Words to explain: two small points.** Philippi is heard once, in What came 1. Give it as "the church at Philippi, which Paul had once written to" (Pol. Phil. 3, ANF 3311). The gloss on "martyr" is not needed, because a grown listener knows the word; drop it.
9. **Library sweep: one claim is wrong.** The sheet says "The Greek of his letters is not held." Mirbt, *Quellen zur Geschichte des Papsttums* (1911; `mirbt_quellen-zur-geschichte-des-papsttums-3e-aufl-deu-lat_1911.txt` 2635–2690) prints in Greek, in OCR that is badly garbled but partly legible, the Romans greeting and Rom. 3–4 (including "not as Peter and Paul I command you"), Smyrn. 8 and Magn. 6. No quote in the outline rests on these passages, and the legible parts agree with the beats' paraphrase. In the sheet's Language note and its Wanted list, record that excerpts are held in Mirbt in garbled OCR. The rest of the sweep holds. I spot-checked the `post-apostolic-house-church`, Antiochene, apocrypha and `UNATTRIBUTED` buckets. I also searched `cic/texts/` for "Ignatius of Antioch", "Ignatius, bishop of Antioch", "Theophorus", the wheat line in Greek and Latin, and Ignatius in the Origen files. The Origen PG17 hit (74337) is an editor's note, not Origen. No other primary text or major early witness is missing.
10. **The connection's "the pattern of a martyr who would not be rescued"** has no supporting passage for "pattern". Tie it to what is held: "and his plea to Rome, which Irenaeus used within a lifetime to say that suffering readies the faithful 'for the royal banquet' (I2, ANF 53553–53556)."
11. **Beats 3 and 5 both return to the bishop and to Christ's real suffering.** These are different letters, so this is not one event told twice. But to keep each teaching told once with its weight, beat 5 should carry only what is new: the Philadelphia scene (his loud cry, and his claim that the Spirit, not any report, told him), and his charge that those teachers neglected the widow and the hungry. Give the doctrine itself no more than a clause there.
