- **What happened:** he tried people accused of being Christians. He had those who would not deny it led away. ## Findings

### MINOR

**M1. Roman citizens sent to Rome are left out.** The outline's Also told ("How he tried them") includes "citizens were sent to Rome" (E3, ML 2779–2780). Words to explain says to tell why citizenship mattered as the record does. Without it, a listener hears that everyone who held firm was simply led away.
*Change.* After "Those who held firm, I ordered to be led away. … deserved punishment."", and before "He does not say what became of them", add: **"Roman citizens he sent to Rome for trial."** (8 words; E3, and the Words to explain gloss.)

**M2. Only one side of the contested reading is credited.** The story says "Historians have argued since whether the reply protected Christians", but names only McGiffert, who reads it as making the faith a crime. The "relief" side, which the outline's Connection field says Christians themselves argued ("whether it was a relief or a trap"), has no witness. What came 1 holds Tertullian's partial relief (TE 1859–1861), and What came 2 holds Eusebius's "to a certain degree checked" (EU 19543–19545). Neither is told.
*Change.* The smallest fix is in Eusebius's sentence. Replace "took the story from Tertullian." with **"took the story from Tertullian, and wrote that the ruling checked the persecution to a degree."** (+11 words; I2, EU 19541–19545). Do not say is respected: this is credited to Eusebius, not stated as fact.
