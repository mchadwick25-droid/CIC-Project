# Outline check: Pliny the Younger

Checker, batch 6, 2026-11-19. Written before reading `Outline.md`.

## My own heart (from the census entry, the card, the source sheet and what Pliny is known for)

**The essence.** About the year 112, Pliny, the Roman governor of Bithynia on the Black Sea, tried people accused of being Christians, had those who kept confessing led away to punishment, and, unsure of the law, wrote to the emperor Trajan. His letter and Trajan's short reply are the earliest outside account of Christians on trial and of their worship, written by the judge himself; Trajan's rule (do not hunt them out, punish them if accused and they will not deny it) set the terms later Christians argued against.

**Also told.**
- He had never attended such trials and asked whether the name alone was a crime (Documented, ML 2771–2775).
- His method: asked three times with threats, led away those who held firm, sent citizens to Rome, released those who invoked the gods, offered incense and wine to Trajan's image and cursed Christ (Documented).
- What former Christians told him: a fixed day before dawn, a hymn to Christ as to a god sung in turn, an oath not to steal, rob, commit adultery, break faith or deny a deposit, and a later ordinary, harmless meal given up after his ban on clubs (Documented as what they said).
- Two slave women called *ministrae*, tortured; he found only a "depraved and immoderate superstition" (Documented).
- His claim that the faith had spread into villages and countryside and that the temples were filling again (his claim).
- Trajan's five-sentence reply: they are not to be sought out; punish the accused who are convicted; pardon for those who deny and sacrifice; no anonymous accusations (Documented).
- Tertullian's answer about a century later, "forbids them to be sought after as innocent ... punished as guilty" (Documented); Eusebius and the chronicles carry it on, and the number of dead grows with each retelling; Sulpicius softens it (Documented).
- Whether the rule protected or condemned Christians (Contested). That those led away died is the later writers' reading (Widely Accepted, not Pliny's word).

This matches the heart Mark approved in `Hearts.md` (2026-11-19).

## Findings

### BLOCKING

**B1. Beat 6, "He did not answer Pliny's question about the name."** This is misleading. Trajan's rule punishes anyone reported and proved to be a Christian who will not deny it (ML 2811–2818). Schanz–Hosius read it that way: being a Christian was enough to convict, with no other crime needed (SH 24494–24497). McGiffert reads it the same way (EU-ed 19570–19576). Both are editors' views. Trajan gave no answer, in words or in effect, on whether age mattered, and he did answer the question on pardon. The listener would come away thinking the central legal question was left open, and that is the reverse of how the held historians read the reply. (The sheet's E11 note, "Trajan answers none of Pliny's questions about age or the name alone", has the same error.)
*Correction.* In beat 6, replace the sentence with: "He said nothing about age. He did not say in so many words whether the name alone was a crime, but his rule punished anyone proved a Christian who would not deny it (ML 2811–2818; that this made the name itself punishable is the editors' reading, SH 24494–24497, EU-ed 19570–19576)." Add to Do not say: "Not 'Trajan left the question of the name open' or 'did not answer it'. Say he said nothing about age, and that his rule punished those proved to be Christians who would not deny it."

### MINOR

**M1. Opening: the name is held back, and the setting is stacked.** "The governor was Pliny, in Bithynia, around 112" is the held-back reveal that Part 1 says is not a stock hinge. It also puts the name, place and date in one sentence.
*Correction.* Name him in the first sentence ("Pliny, governing a Roman province on the Black Sea, wrote to the emperor Trajan that he had never once attended a trial of Christians..."), and let the year come in a later clause.

**M2. Length.** 2:50 (about 420 words) will not carry what the outline asks for: the opening, the essence, six beats full of scenes, and a third of the story for the impact. A rough count of the beats as written comes to about 550 to 600 words.
*Correction.* Set the length to about 3:30 (about 520 words). Keep the named cuts (What came 3; the man who left twenty years before) as the first things to drop if it runs over.

**M3. "Whom the Christians called ministers"** (Words to explain, beat 4, Do not say). The Latin says only *quae ministrae dicebantur*, "who were called ministers" (ML 2796; McGiffert, "who were called", EU-ed 19504). It does not say who called them that.
*Correction.* Change all three places to "two slave women who were called ministers". The Do not say entry becomes: "Not 'deaconesses' as Pliny's word, and not 'who held office'. Say 'who were called ministers' (E8)."

**M4. Lines about today with no source.** The Connection field says "historians still do", and Ending 1 says the letter "is still read". The held historians are McGiffert (1890), Harnack (1906) and Schanz–Hosius (1922). Mark ruled for Josephus in this batch that no current verdict is to be claimed.
*Correction.* Connection: "and historians have argued it since: McGiffert, Eusebius's translator, read the reply as making the faith itself a crime (EU-ed 19570–19576)." Ending 1: drop "is still read for what it says of them", and end on the governor and his question.

**M5. A fourth quoted phrase.** What came 3 quotes McGiffert's "a decided mistake", but the quotes line allows only three short phrases besides Quotes 1–3.
*Correction.* Either list "a decided mistake" (EU-ed 19570) as an allowed phrase in place of one of the three, or tell McGiffert's view in paraphrase.

**M6. What came 1, Tertullian's "oath".** Tertullian does not say oath. He writes *ad confoederandam disciplinam ... prohibentes* (TL 11724–11726), "to bind themselves together in a way of life that forbids murder, adultery, fraud". The *Apology*'s address, "to Rome's governors", is right in sense (*Romani imperii antistites*, TL 11034, the empire's magistrates sitting in judgement), but the sheet does not cite it.
*Correction.* Write "and a shared rule of life forbidding murder, adultery and fraud (TL 11724–11726)", and cite TL 11034 for the address.

**M7. "Sent by the emperor to govern"** (Words to explain, beat 1). No held line says Trajan sent him. McGiffert says only that he "became governor there" (EU-ed 19458). The letter shows he acted "according to thy demands" (*secundum mandata tua*, ML 2795).
*Correction.* Write "governing the province for the emperor" (ML 2795), or just "governor of Bithynia".

**M8. The ban on clubs is told twice.** It is in beat 1 (S4) and again in beat 3, where it ends the meal.
*Correction.* Drop it from beat 1. Tell it once, in beat 3.

**M9. Beat 1, "whatever they believed".** This paraphrase widens the Latin *qualecumque esset quod faterentur*, "whatever it was they confessed". Quote 1's own rendering is right.
*Correction.* Write "whatever it was they confessed".

**M10. Beat 6, "whatever the past".** The Latin *quamvis suspectus in praeteritum* means "though suspected for the past" (ML 2817).
*Correction.* Write "even if suspected for the past".

**M11. Do not say is missing one item a writer could easily get wrong.** A writer might say Trajan's reply became law for the whole empire.
*Correction.* Add: "Not 'Trajan's rule became the law of the empire'. He wrote to Pliny and said no general rule could be laid down (ML 2810–2811). Schanz–Hosius say it spread once the letters were published (SH 24507–24511), an editors' view."

**M12. The "(defended)" mark sits on Tertullian.** It marks what Tertullian defended, not Pliny. What Pliny himself argued for was pardon for those who turned back (E10).
*Correction.* Mark What came 1 "(the Christians' defence, Tertullian's)". Add to the beat 5 line: "Defended, Pliny's own: room for repentance and pardon (E10)."

**M13. "Syriac", a passing word with no gloss** (What came 2).
*Correction.* Add to Words to explain: "Syriac: 'in Syriac, the language of many churches of the Middle East'". Or give it by role: "a chronicler of the eastern churches in the thirteenth century".

**M14. "About a century later" for Tertullian.** The *Apology* is about 85 years after the letter (CEN c. 197; the sheet says 85 to 100).
*Correction.* Write "less than a century later".
