## 1. Setting

S1. **Who he was.** ROL 149–160: "Gaius Suetonius Tranquillus is one of the numerous Roman writers who give us little information about themselves. He only tells us that he was the son of Suetonius Laetus, a Roman knight, who took part in the battle of Betriacum as tribune of the thirteenth legion ... the date of his birth, which Mommsen assigns to the year 77 a.d., Macé with somewhat greater probability to 69. The rest of our information is derived from the Letters of the Younger Pliny and from a single allusion in Spartianus". MODERN (Rolfe). Birth year: Contested (two modern dates). THF 47–51: "born towards the close of the reign of Vespasian, who died in the year 79". MODERN.

S2. **His life, as held.** ROL 191–197: "From Pliny we learn that he practised at the bar, although it was apparently only for a short time ... He took no part in political life, and although he secured a military tribuneship through Pliny's good offices, he soon had it transferred to a relative." ROL 229–246: from Spartianus "he held the position of secretary to Hadrian ... Spartianus tells us that both Suetonius and Septicius were discharged by Hadrian ... From this time on we lose sight of Suetonius, and it seems probable that he lived in retirement and devoted himself to literary work." THF 51–57: "under whose administration he filled the office of secretary; until, with several others, he was dismissed for presuming on familiarities with the empress Sabina ... which appears to have befallen him in the year 121". MODERN, each resting on Pliny's letters and one line of a later biography (Spartianus), neither held. Widely Accepted. Plain words: "a scholar who worked for a time as the emperor Hadrian's secretary, until Hadrian dismissed him, about 121".

S3. **The friend of Pliny.** ROL 205–216: "In his letter to Trajan Pliny refers to Suetonius as contubernalis, which indicates an intimate friendship and an approximately equal age ... The letters of Pliny which refer to Suetonius cover approximately the period from 96 to 112." THF 62–68: "Several of the younger Pliny's letters are addressed to Suetonius, with whom he lived in the closest friendship ... he speaks of him as 'a most excellent, honourable, and learned man, whom he had the pleasure of entertaining under his own roof'". MODERN, reporting letters not held. Widely Accepted. (The Pliny sheet records the same tie at its RO 191–216 and chose not to tell it.)

S4. **The book and its date.** ROL 320–326: "Of all these the only work which has come down to us entire, or nearly so, is the Lives of the Caesars, published in 120. It includes the biographies of twelve 'Caesars,' from Julius to Domitian". MODERN, Rolfe's date. Dominant Modern Reconstruction (the year). ROL 262–266 and following list his other, lost works (on famous men of letters, on Rome's customs, on the games of the Greeks, and others). ROL 427–432 (Rolfe, "General Characteristics"): great events "are dismissed in a brief chapter, or with a casual allusion ... The acts of the senate and people, and the edicts of the emperors, receive fuller attention, but are wholly subordinate to the personal element." MODERN.

S5. **When the events were, and how far he wrote from them.** Claudius ruled 41–54 (MIR 2500: "Vorgehen des Kaisers Klaudius gegen die Juden in Rom (41 — 54)"; SCH 24275: "Schon unter Claudius (41—54)"). Nero ruled 54–68 (SCH 24275: "Nero (54 — 68)"). MODERN. Documented. No held source gives the year of the expulsion; Suetonius gives none. The great fire was in 64 (THF 20772–20773, note 614; Tacitus sheet S1). Written in or near 120 (S4), the Claudius line is more than sixty years after the event, the Nero line about fifty or more. Plain words: "decades later".

S6. **Where.** Rome (both lines; CEN `placeLabel` "Rome"). OWN.

S7. **The tradition.** CEN `suetonius`: `toldWithin` null, status "Within Another World (A5)", era 1. He stands outside the church. MAP 2290–2296: "A pagan Roman biographer writing from outside the church, so context, not tradition." EDITOR.

## 2. The two lines, in their place

E1. **The Claudius line.** Latin, ROL 25841–25845 (*Claudius* 25.4): "... Seleuco regi amicitiam et societatem ita demum pollicentis, si consanguineos suos Ilienses ab omni onere immunes praestitisset. Iudaeos impulsore Chresto assidue tumultuantis Roma expulit. Germanorum legatis in orchestra sedere permisit". The same Latin, MIR 2505–2506 ("Iudaeos impulsore C[h]resto assidue tu[m]ultuantes Roma expulit"); SCH 24311–24312. English, ROL 25907–25909: "Since the Jews constantly made disturbances at the instigation of Chrestus, he expelled them from Rome." THF 10639–10640: "He banished from Rome all the Jews, who were continually making disturbances at the instigation of one Chrestus." OWN. Documented as his line. The Latin has no "since", no "all" and no "one": those are the translators'. It is one sentence of seven words.

E2. **Its place in the chapter.** ROL 25901–25927 (English): the line comes after Claudius frees the people of Ilium (old Troy) from tribute, "on the ground that they were the founders of the Roman race", and before he lets the German envoys sit in the orchestra; then the Druids abolished, the Eleusinian rites, the temple of Venus Erycina, treaties struck with a pig's sacrifice. The chapter ends (ROL 25927–25931): "almost the whole conduct of his reign, were dictated not so much by his own judgment as that of his wives and freedmen". OWN. Documented.

E3. **The Nero line.** Latin, ROL 28452–28463 (*Nero* 16.2): "Multa sub eo et animadversa severe et coercita nec minus instituta: adhibitus sumptibus modus; publicae cenae ad sportulas redactae; interdictum ne quid in popinis cocti praeter legumina aut holera veniret ...; afflicti suppliciis Christiani, genus hominum superstitionis novae ac maleficae; vetiti quadrigariorum lusus ...; pantomimorum factiones cum ipsis simul relegatae." The same Latin, MIR 2567–2572; SCH 24348–24349 ("aff[l]icti suppliciis Christiani, genus hominum superstitionis novae ac maleficae"). English, ROL 28516–28530: "During his reign many abuses were severely punished and put down, and no fewer new laws were made: a limit was set to expenditures; the public banquets were confined to a distribution of food; the sale of any kind of cooked viands in the taverns was forbidden, with the exception of pulse and vegetables ... Punishment was inflicted on the Christians, a class of men given to a new and mischievous superstition. He put an end to the diversions of the chariot drivers ... The pantomimic actors and their partisans were banished from the city." THF 11612–11614: "He likewise inflicted punishments on the Christians, a sort of people who held a new and impious superstition." OWN. Documented. It is one clause of nine Latin words in a list of public-order measures. It gives no number, no year, no charge and no kind of death.

E4. **The word "malefica".** THF 20568–20569 (note 577): "'Superstitionis novae et maleficae,' are the words of Suetonius; the latter conveying the idea of witchcraft or enchantment." MODERN, editors' gloss. Rolfe translates "mischievous", Thomson–Forester "impious". Plain: "harmful" or "wicked"; the magic sense is the editors' reading (Inferential-Thin as Suetonius's meaning).

E5. **Where Suetonius files it.** ROL 28653–28656 (Latin, *Nero* 19.3): "Haec partim nulla reprehensione, partim etiam non mediocri laude digna in unum contuli, ut secernerem a probris ac sceleribus eius, de quibus dehinc dicam." ROL 28700–28704: "I have brought together these acts of his, some of which are beyond criticism, while others are even deserving of no slight praise, to separate them from his shameful and criminal deeds, of which I shall proceed now to give an account." THF 11656–11659: "These transactions, in part unexceptionable, and in part highly commendable, I have brought into one view". OWN. Documented. The punishment of the Christians stands among Nero's acts that Suetonius calls blameless or praiseworthy. MAL 5182–5185 (1910) reads it so: "Suetonius thinks, Nero did right in punishing the Christians". MODERN.

E6. **The fire, told apart.** ROL 30502–30504 (Latin, *Nero* 38): "incendit urbem tam palam, ut plerique consulares cubicularios eius cum stuppa taedaque in praediis suis deprehensos non attigerint". ROL 30559–30563: "under cover of displeasure at the ugliness of the old buildings and the narrow, crooked streets, he set fire to the city so openly that several ex-consuls did not venture to lay hands on his chamberlains although they caught them on their estates with tow and firebrands". ROL 30636–30639: watching from the tower of Maecenas, "exulting ... in 'the beauty of the flames,' he sang the whole of the 'Sack of Ilium'". OWN. Documented as his account. In chapter 38 he says Nero set the fire; he says nothing there of the Christians, and in chapter 16 nothing of the fire. ROL 30619–30623 (Rolfe's note): the accounts of Tacitus and Dio "differ from that of Suetonius". MODERN. THF 20772–20774 (note 614): the fire "was imputed to the Christians, and drew on them the persecutions mentioned in c. xvi." MODERN: the link is the 1909 editors', not Suetonius's (MAP 2309–2313 says the same). The card agrees (CARD `uncertain` 5).

## 3. What came of them (heaviest first)

I1. **The outside evidence modern historians gather, and weigh.** MIR 141–151 and 2500–2572: Mirbt's source book on the papacy opens with Suetonius's Claudius line as document 1 under the heading "the emperor Claudius's action against the Jews in Rome", then Dio as document 2, Tacitus as 3, and Suetonius's Nero line as 4. SCH 24311–24312 and 24348–24349: Schanz prints both under "Zeugnisse" ("testimonies"). BELL 1877–1878: Claudius's "expulsion of them from Rome (Suetonius, 25; Acts, xviii, 2; cf. Dio, lx, 6, 6)". THF 19531–19538 (note 335): "How little even the well-informed Suetonius knew of the real facts, we shall find in the only direct notice of the Christians contained in his works (CLAUDIUS c. xxv., NERO, c. xvi.)". ROL 25871–25876 (note on Chrestus, see D1). MODERN. Documented: modern historians held set both lines among their outside evidence; the 1909 editors stress how little they say.

I2. **Read beside Acts.** NT-G 384 (Acts 18:2): "διὰ τὸ διατεταχέναι Κλαύδιον χωρίζεσθαι πάντας τοὺς Ἰουδαίους ἀπὸ τῆς Ῥώμης". NT-E 88522–88524: Paul "found a certain Jew named Aquila, a man of Pontus by race, who had recently come from Italy with his wife Priscilla, because Claudius had commanded all the Jews to depart from Rome." SCRIPTURE (Acts, first century). EUS 12757–12763 (Eusebius, *Church History* 2.18.9): "Claudius drove the Jews out of Rome; and Aquila and Priscilla, leaving Rome with the other Jews, came to Asia ... The sacred book of the Acts informs us also of these things." LATER (fourth century), from Acts; he does not mention Chrestus. THF 20376–20377 (note 528): "St. Luke, Acts xviii. 2, mentions the expulsion of the Jews from Rome by the emperor Claudius". MIR 2554 (footnote to the Claudius line) prints Acts 18:2. SCH 24312: "Vgl. Apostelgesch. 18, 2." MODERN. Documented that historians read the two together; the reading that it is the same event is Widely Accepted.

I3. **Christian writers protest the name "Chrestus".** TERT-L 12452–12455 (Tertullian, *Apology* 3): "Christianus vero, quantum interpretatio est, de unctione deducitur. Sed et cum perperam Chrestianus pronuntiatur a vobis (nam nec nominis certa est notitia penes vos), de suavitate vel benignitate compositum est." TERT-E 1712–1716: "But Christian, so far as the meaning of the word is concerned, is derived from anointing. Yes, and even when it is wrongly pronounced by you 'Chrestianus' (for you do not even know accurately the name you hate), it comes from sweetness and benignity." The same point in *To the Nations* 1.3, TERT-L 31640–31643, TERT-E 9205–9208. LAC-L 16813–16814 (Lactantius, *Divine Institutes* 4.7.5): "ratio est propter ignorantium errorem, qui eum inmutata littera Chrestum solent dicere." LAC-E 3163: "the meaning of this name must be set forth, on account of the error of the ignorant, who by the change of a letter are accustomed to call Him Chrestus." LATER (about 200; early fourth century). Documented as their words. Neither names Suetonius. The tie to Suetonius is the editors': TERT-L 12507–12514 (Oehler's note on "Chrestianus" cites Lactantius and "Sueton. Claud. cap. 25"); ROL 25871 (Rolfe's note: "Another form of Christus; see Tert. Apol. 3 (at the end)"); LAC-E 3165 (ANF note: "Suetonius speaks of Christ as Chrestus. The Christians also were called Chrestians, as Tertullian shows in his Apology"); JUS 14040–14048 (ANF note on Justin's play on "Christos" and "chrestos": "the popular blunder of which Suetonius ... gives us an example, 'impulsore Chresto'"). MODERN. Widely Accepted as the editors' reading.

I4. **A Christian scholar took his book as a model.** JER-L 46924–46933 (Jerome, preface to *Lives of Illustrious Men*): "Hortaris me, Dexter, ut Tranquillum sequens, ecclesiasticos scriptores in ordinem digeram, et quod ille in enumerandis gentilium litterarum viris fecit illustribus, ego in nostris faciam". JER-E 38773–38779: "You have urged me, Dexter, to follow the example of Tranquillus in giving a systematic account of ecclesiastical writers, and to do for our writers what he did for the illustrious men of letters among the Gentiles". JER-L 46997–47000 (Jerome, letter to Desiderius, in Migne's note): "Scripsi librum De Illustribus Viris, ab apostolis usque ad nostram aetatem, imitatus Tranquillum, Graecumque Apollonium". LATER (the preface dates it to the fourteenth year of the emperor Theodosius, JER-E 38785; its note's "a.d. 492" is a misprint for 392, the fourteenth year of Theodosius). Documented. It is Suetonius's lost *On Illustrious Men* (ROL 266) that Jerome follows, not the *Lives of the Caesars*. JER-E 38444–38445 (editor): his models "were first of all Suetonius". MODERN.

I5. **His influence on Christian biography, by the editors.** ROL 377–392: "His influence extended to the Christian writers and is seen in the form of the Life of Ambrosius by his secretary Paulinus". ROL 399–400: "Tertullian in his De Spectaculis made use of Suetonius's work of a similar title". TERT-E 6790–6791 (Tertullian, *On the Shows* 5): "ample information is to be found in the pages of Suetonius Tranquillus". PAU 2759–2760 (Kaniecka): the Life of Ambrose "closely resembles the literary form of the Alexandrian biography, which is best seen in the Lives of Suetonius". ROL 365–368: "The historical writers, such as Eutropius, Aurelius Victor, and Orosius drew on him freely". MODERN, except Tertullian's own naming (LATER, Documented). These are about his books in general, not the two lines.

## 5. Where sources disagree, or come late

D1. **Who was "Chrestus"?** ROL 25871–25874 (Rolfe's note): "Another form of Christus; see Tert. Apol. 3 (at the end). It is uncertain whether Suetonius is guilty of an error in chronology or is referring to some Jew of that name. The former seems probable because of the absence of quodam." THF 20362–20375 (note 528): the passage confirms that "the Christians were obscurely alluded to ... The antagonism between Christianity and Judaism appears to have given rise to the tumults ... we find Suetonius ... transplanting Christ from Jerusalem to Rome, and placing him in the time of Claudius, although the crucifixion took place during the reign of Tiberius." THF 20380–20382: "It was very natural for Suetonius to write Chrestus instead of Christus, as the former was a name in use among the Greeks and Romans. Among others, Cicero mentions a person of that name". SCH 24275–24278: "Schon unter Claudius (41—54) führten Unruhen in der Judenschaft Roms, die mit dem eindringenden Christentum zusammenhingen, zur Einmischung des Staats. Juden und Christen sind dabei aber nicht unterschieden worden." (Already under Claudius, unrest among Rome's Jews, connected with the Christianity coming in, led the state to step in; Jews and Christians were not told apart.) MODERN. Contested: every modern held reads Christ, with Rolfe leaving open a Jew of that name; none is Suetonius's own. Suetonius does not say who Chrestus was, or that he was in Rome.

D2. **Expelled, or only forbidden to meet?** THF 20377–20378 (note 528): "Dio, however, says that he did not expel them, but only forbad their religious assemblies." MIR 2512–2516 prints Dio 60.6 in Greek, OCR badly damaged ("oux i^rikaae |jl£V, xw oe oyj Ttaxpcto vojjiw ßtw xpwfxevous sxeXeuas [jly] (j\)voc%'poiL,ead-ai"); this researcher's reading of the damaged words is "he did not drive them out, but ordered them, keeping their ancestral way of life, not to gather", which matches THF's note. MODERN report; Dio's Greek is not held in a clean text. BELL 1878 cites Dio beside Suetonius and Acts. Contested: Suetonius and Acts say expelled; Dio says not. Whether Dio and Suetonius describe the same moment no held source settles.

D3. **"All" the Jews?** Acts says "all the Jews" (NT-E 88523; NT-G 384 "πάντας"); THF's English adds "all" to Suetonius; Suetonius's Latin says only "Iudaeos" (E1). Documented.

D4. **The fire and the Christians.** Suetonius does not join them (E6). The 1909 editors do (THF 20772–20774). CARD `uncertain` 5. Documented that the link is not his.

D5. **His birth year.** 69 or 77 (S1). Contested.

D6. **Not held.** Pliny's letters about Suetonius; Spartianus's *Life of Hadrian*; Dio's Greek in a clean text; Orosius's *History*, which Rolfe says drew on Suetonius (ROL 366) and which is the ancient Christian writer usually cited for the Claudius line: no held text of it. No held source gives the year of the expulsion.
