# Outline check: Suetonius

## The checker's own heart (written before reading `Outline.md`)

From the census card, the source sheet and what Suetonius is known for.

**The essence.** Suetonius, a Roman scholar who served for a time as Hadrian's secretary, published his Lives of the Caesars around 120 (Rolfe's date, Dominant Modern Reconstruction). In it the Christians get two short lines, each set in a list of an emperor's measures: Claudius drove from Rome the Jews who kept rioting "at the urging of Chrestus", and under Nero the Christians, "a class of men given to a new and harmful superstition", were punished (Documented, his own words). They are among the very few notices of the first Christians at Rome by a writer outside the church, and they are still read because they are so early and so plain, though they say very little. Who "Chrestus" was is Contested.

**Also told.**
- Who he was: son of a Roman knight, a friend of Pliny the Younger, at the bar briefly, Hadrian's secretary until dismissed about 121 (Widely Accepted, from Rolfe and the 1909 preface, resting on Pliny's letters and Spartianus, not held). Birth 69 or 77 (Contested).
- How little the lines say: seven Latin words on Claudius, nine on Nero; no year, number, charge or kind of death; the Claudius line set between Ilium's freedom from tribute and the German envoys' seats; the Nero line among food-shop rules, chariot drivers and actors (Documented).
- He files the Nero line among acts he calls blameless or praiseworthy (Documented, *Nero* 19.3), and he never ties it to the fire, which he blames on Nero in a separate chapter (Documented). The tie to the fire is the 1909 editors'.
- Who "Chrestus" was: the modern editors held read Christ, the name misheard or the date wrong; Rolfe leaves open a Jew of that name (Contested). Suetonius does not say who he was or that he was in Rome.
- Read beside Acts 18:2, where Aquila and Priscilla come from Italy because Claudius ordered "all the Jews" out of Rome (that it is the same event, Widely Accepted); Dio says Claudius only forbade their meetings (Contested, Dio reported, not held clean).
- Christian writers, Tertullian about 200 and Lactantius in the early 300s, complained that pagans said "Chrestianus" and "Chrestus" for "Christ" (Documented); neither names Suetonius; the tie is the editors'.
- Jerome followed "Tranquillus" as his model for Lives of Illustrious Men (Documented), but that was Suetonius's lost book on men of letters, not the Lives of the Caesars.
- Modern source books (Mirbt 1911, Schanz 1922) set both lines among their testimonies (Documented).

This agrees in substance with the heart Mark approved in `Hearts.md` (2026-11-19).

## Findings

### BLOCKING

**B1. The sweep missed Bede, and the outline wrongly says no held source gives the year.**
- **The text it missed:** Bede's greater chronicle (to 725), in `cic/texts/bede_chronica-maiora-in-chronica-minora-3-mgh-aa-13-lat_mommsen1898.txt` 23216–23217, §287. It reads: "Nono regni sui anno Iudaeos tumultuantes Roma expulit, quod et Lucas refert" ("In the ninth year of his reign he drove the rioting Jews from Rome, which Luke also reports"). Its margin cites Orosius 7.6 and Acts 18:2.
- **Why it matters:** this is a held Christian witness. It carries the Claudius line, from Orosius, without "Chrestus", sets it beside Acts, and gives it a year. Bede is on the tree as `bede-of-jarrow`. The outline's Setting says "the year of the expulsion is not in any held source", sheet D6 says "No held source gives the year of the expulsion", and the Do not say entry on "in 49" gives the same reason. All three are wrong about the Library.
- **Correction:**
  - Setting, Time, should read: "Suetonius gives no year. A later Christian chronicle, Bede's (to 725), following Orosius, sets the expulsion in Claudius's ninth year and ties it to Acts (Bede 23216–23217). The date is Bede's and Orosius's, not Suetonius's or Acts'."
  - Do not say: replace the "in 49" entry with: "Not 'in 49' or any year in the narrator's voice. Suetonius and Acts give none; the 'ninth year' is Bede's, from Orosius (not held). Say 'in the reign of Claudius'."
  - Who tells it: add Bede, about 725, with that date credited to him.
  - Source sheet: add Bede to I2 and the sweep's Found and Used lists, and correct D6 to match.
  - Telling Bede in the story stays optional, as one clause under What came 1, after Acts. If it is not told, list it under Leave out with the reason: a late chronicle, and its date rests on an unheld Orosius.

**B2. The outline glosses "Tranquillus" wrongly as Suetonius's family name.**
- **Where:** What came 3 has "'following the example of Tranquillus', Suetonius's family name". In Gaius Suetonius Tranquillus, the family name is Suetonius. Tranquillus is his own added name: his father was Suetonius Laetus (ROL 149–152; JER-E 38776, "C. Suetonius Tranquillus"). No source supports the gloss, and it is wrong for this time and place.
- **The quoted phrase is also off.** The ANF English is "to follow the example of Tranquillus", and those words are Dexter's request (JER-E 38773–38774). Jerome's Latin, "Tranquillum sequens", means "following Tranquillus".
- **Correction:** What came 3 should read: "Near the end of the fourth century, Jerome wrote short lives of Christian writers. Asked to 'follow the example of Tranquillus', another of Suetonius's names, he did for Christian writers what Suetonius had done for Rome's men of letters (JER-E 38773–38779; I4: Documented)." Make the same change in the short-phrases list. Under Names, "Tranquillus" stays glossed only as "another of his names".

### MINOR

**M1. Q1's comma.** "He drove from Rome the Jews, who were constantly rioting…" With the comma, the clause reads as if every Jew was rioting, and so every Jew expelled. The Latin leaves this open (D3).
- **Correction:** drop the comma: "He drove from Rome the Jews who were constantly rioting at the urging of Chrestus." This also keeps the line clear of "all".

**M2. The Q3 Latin is not letter for letter.** Oehler prints "conpositum" (TERT-L 12455), with the same spelling habit as "inpudicum" two lines above. The outline and the sheet both print "compositum".
- **Correction:** "…de suavitate vel benignitate conpositum est." The English rendering does not change.

**M3. Q2's second citation.** Q2 cites "MIR 2570" for the same Latin, but Mirbt prints "adflicti" (Roth's text), not "afflicti".
- **Correction:** "(MIR 2570, as 'adflicti')".

**M4. The Chrestus gloss says "a common Greek name".** The source says "a name in use among the Greeks and Romans" (THF 20380–20382).
- **Correction:** in Words to explain and beat 3, "a name in use among Greeks and Romans, close in sound to Christus, the Latin for Christ".

**M5. The superstition gloss says more than this story's source.** "A Roman word of contempt for a foreign or wrong religion": "foreign" is not in Suetonius, whose word is "new".
- **Correction:** "superstition, his word of contempt for their religion: a new and harmful one, he says". The gloss then stays with Q2.

**M6. Tacitus, in beat 4, risks joining Suetonius's line to the fire.** After "no kind of death", the line "Another Roman, Tacitus, tells the deaths in full" lets a listener take Suetonius's punishments to be Tacitus's deaths after the fire, which Suetonius never says (E6, D4). Mirbt groups the two under 64; that pairing is the historians'.
- **Correction:** beat 4 should read: "Another Roman, Tacitus, tells at length of Christians put to death under Nero after the fire. Historians read the two side by side; Suetonius himself joins nothing to the fire." Add to Do not say: "Not that Suetonius's punishments were Tacitus's deaths, in the narrator's voice. Historians pair them (MIR 141–151); he does not."

**M7. "Open their collections" overstates the source.** In Also told, "modern historians open their collections of outside evidence with the two lines" stretches one source book. Mirbt opens with the Claudius line; Schanz prints both among his "testimonies".
- **Correction:** Also told should read: "a German source book of 1911 opens with the Claudius line, and historians print both lines among their testimonies, reading the first beside Acts (I1, I2)."

**M8. "A Jewish couple".** Acts names Aquila as "a certain Jew" who came "with his wife Priscilla" (NT-E 88522–88524). It also puts the meeting at Corinth in 18:1 (NT-E 88520), which neither the sheet nor the outline cites.
- **Correction:** "a Jew named Aquila and his wife", and cite Acts 18:1 (NT-E 88520) for Corinth.

**M9. The length is short for the beats.** The opening, the essence, four beats, three quotes, What came (a third) and the ending come to about 350–375 words, which is about 2:20–2:30 at 150 a minute. Held to 275 words, beat 3 (Acts, Dio, three views of Chrestus) would be crammed past one hearing.
- **Correction:** Length should read: "about 2 minutes 20 (about 350 words); cut Jerome, then Dio, first if it runs long". The length is set by the beats, not filled.
