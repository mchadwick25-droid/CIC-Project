## My own heart, written before reading `Outline.md`

From the census entry (`teaser`, `dates`, `region`), the existing card, the source sheet and what Hermas is known for.

**The essence.** Hermas was a Christian at Rome in the first half of the second century who wrote the Shepherd, a book of visions in which the Church, as an old woman, and then an angel dressed as a shepherd call the Christians of Rome to repent before a fixed time runs out. Its teaching that a baptized Christian who sins may repent once more, and only once, made it one of the most read books of the early church: some quoted it as Scripture, others shut it out of the canon (Documented as the book's words and as its readers' verdicts; the visions rest on his word alone).

**Also told.**
- The book's opening: the man who raised him sold him to a woman named Rhoda in Rome; the first vision accuses him of a sinful desire for her, and of neglecting a household that had sinned (Documented as the book's words; the opening varies by manuscript; that he was later freed is an editor's inference, Inferential-Thin).
- The tower built on water from fitted stones, the Church; stones set aside that can still be built in if they repent while it is building; the rich as round stones that must be cut down, Hermas's own case (Documented).
- The Church growing younger as people repent (Documented).
- The Shepherd, the angel of repentance; the rule of Commandment 4: "there is but one repentance to the servants of God" (Documented).
- Who he was: brother of Pius, bishop of Rome, in the Muratorian fragment and the Liberian Catalogue; the date and identity are Contested.
- What came of it: Irenaeus quoting it as Scripture; Clement and Origen using it, Clement carrying its second-repentance teaching on; the Muratorian fragment's "read, but not in church"; Tertullian's scorn ("the only one which favours adulterers"); Eusebius setting it among the disputed and then rejected books, though read in churches; Athanasius allowing it for those newly joining; copied at the end of Codex Sinaiticus; Jerome finding it almost unknown among the Latins (Documented; the Sinaiticus point rests on editors, Widely Accepted).

## BLOCKING

**B1. "read in churches for some three centuries" (essence, line 5).** No held line gives three centuries. The span runs from the Muratorian fragment, which says it must *not* be read out in church (MUR-L 22080–22083), to Jerome, about 392, who says it is read publicly "in some churches of Greece" (JER 39625–39629). That is about two and a half centuries, and the reading in church was never universal. The number sits in the heart, where a writer will speak it. Correction: "His book, the Shepherd, was read for centuries: some quoted it as Scripture, others shut it out (I1, I3, I6, I7; Documented)." Add to Do not say: "Not 'read in churches for three centuries'. Say 'read for centuries'; some churches read it aloud and others would not (I3, I7)."

**B2. "though one Latin copy reads it differently" (essence, line 5; also Source_Sheet E1 and D2).** Crombie's note (HE 649–652) gives the Vatican Latin reading ("He who had brought me up, sold a certain young woman at Rome") and then "So Lips.", which is the Leipzig manuscript from Athos (HE 602–605). That manuscript is Greek: Simonides' Athos text (EUS-McG 14822–14826). So two copies, one Latin and one Greek, make the young woman the one sold. The outline's qualifier halves the doubt on the fact it leads with. Correction to the essence: "His book opens, in the text most editions follow, with the words that he was once sold to a woman in Rome (E1; Documented as that text's words; two other old copies, one Latin and one Greek, say instead that the man who raised him sold a young woman; that he was later freed is never said, D2)." Add to Do not say: "Not 'one copy differs'. If the doubt is told, say 'other old copies say it was the young woman who was sold' (HE 649–652, 602–605)." The opening and the Rhoda lines may stand, credited as "he writes".

**B3. Words to explain, "repent / repentance": the gloss "turning back from sin, and being forgiven" (line 18).** The book keeps the two apart: "those who have now believed, and those who are to believe, have not repentance for their sins; but they have remission of their previous sins" (HE 1999–2002). Repentance is the sinner's own turning: "he repents, and no longer acts wickedly, but does good" (HE 1975–1976). The outline's own Do not say (line 110) relies on that difference. A gloss that puts the forgiveness inside the word lets a listener hear "one repentance" as "one forgiveness". Correction: "**repent / repentance**: 'to turn away from sin, sorry for it, and live rightly again' (HE 1975–1976). The forgiveness that follows is said separately. The book's whole subject; gloss it once, early." In the Connection (line 77), change "some teachers said there was no forgiveness after baptism at all" to "some teachers said there was no repentance after baptism, only the forgiveness given at baptism itself (Q1)".

## MINOR

**M1. Beat 1 reaches into beat 2.** "His children had sinned and 'betrayed their parents', his wife could not hold her tongue, and he had been ruined by his business dealings" comes from the little book's writing revealed in the second vision (HE 886–900, 956–963), which is beat 2's event. The first vision says only that his house had sinned and he had not warned them, and that "you have been destroyed by the affairs of this world" because of their sins (HE 769–780). Correction: in beat 1, end with "...because he had not corrected his household, whose sins had brought him to ruin in his worldly affairs (E2)". Move the children, the wife and "engaged in your wicked transactions" into beat 2, as what the book's writing said when its meaning came.

**M2. Beat 1: "God was angry less for this than because..."** The text says "God is not angry with you on account of this, but that you may convert your house" (HE 769–770). Correction: "God was not angry with him for this, but because he had let his household go wrong (E2)."

**M3. Essence and Connection: "fallen into serious sin".** Commandment 4 says "if any one is tempted by the devil, and sins" (HE 2013–2014); it names no grade of sin. Correction: "a baptized Christian who sinned" (essence, Connection, Shape).

**M4. Quoted phrases beyond the stated cap.** The Quotes note allows two short phrases aloud, but the outline also marks "betrayed their parents" (beat 1), "with difficulty will he live" (beat 4) and "very recently, in our times" (What came 2) as quotations. Correction: add these three to the note as allowed short phrases, or mark them for paraphrase. Each is verbatim (HE 889, 2022; MUR-E 60178–60179).

**M5. Do not say: keep the qualifier on repeated falling.** The text says the repentance of one who sins often "will be of no avail; for with difficulty will he live" (HE 2020–2022). Duchesne notes that Hermas is "pas très catégorique contre les récidifs" (DUCH 8414–8416). Add: "Not 'those who fell again were damned' or 'could never be saved'. Keep 'with difficulty will he live' (E10)."

**M6. Do not say: Irenaeus's standing.** McGiffert: Irenaeus quotes it as Scripture "although he omits it in his discussion of Scripture testimonies ... which shows that he considered it not quite on a level with regular Scripture" (EUS-McG 14755–14760). Add: "Not 'Irenaeus counted it among the books of the New Testament'. Say he quoted it with the words 'the Scripture declared' (I1)."

**M7. Sinaiticus: partial copy, and the date.** The codex holds "only the Visions and a small part of the Mandates" (EUS-McG 14826–14828). Its fourth-century date is held at EUS-McG 67083–67085, which the outline does not cite. Add to What came 5: "part of the book, the visions and some of the commandments" and the citation. Add to Do not say: "Not 'the whole Shepherd was copied in the Bible'."

**M8. Quote 3's narrator gloss.** "'The prophets' means the Old Testament" says more than the fragment does. The fragment gives the reason it is set aside: it was written "very recently, in our times" (MUR-L 22077–22080), so it can stand neither with the prophets, "whose number is complete", nor with the apostles. Correction: "The narrator gives the sense: it was too recent to stand with the prophets' books, which were closed, or with the apostles' writings."

**M9. Who tells it: "all we know of him comes from his own book".** The old list and the catalogue of 354 add that he was Pius's brother (L1), and What came 2 tells it. Correction: "all he tells of himself comes from his own book, and all of it from his visions; an old list adds that he was the brother of Pius, bishop of Rome."

**M10. Ending: which angel.** At HE 5196–5199 and 5270–5273 Hermas speaks to "that messenger who had delivered me to the Shepherd" (HE 5165–5166), and it is this messenger who rises and takes the Shepherd away. "The angel" will be heard as the Shepherd, the angel of repentance. Correction: "the messenger who had handed him over to the Shepherd" in both ending options. Also beat 2: "send it to other cities" should be "send it abroad" ("to foreign countries", HE 1012–1013), and "a little book" should be "a book" (HE 867).

**M11. Words to explain: two missing passing words; beat count.** "penance" (What came 3, "done penance") and "council" (Tertullian's "every council of the churches") are not listed. Add: "**done penance**: say 'had shown their repentance' (TERT 7186–7188)"; "**council**: 'a meeting of the churches' leaders'". Separately, the outline has four story beats, below the kit's five to seven. The record is thin and the opening carries a fifth event, so this is noted rather than blocked.
