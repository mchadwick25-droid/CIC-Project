- Paragraph 2, first sentence: "The man was Hermas, a Christian at Rome in the second century, and all he tells of himself comes from his visions." → "He wrote in the second century, and all he tells of himself comes from his visions."

**M2. MINOR: sentence past 30 words (paragraph 2, 32 words).** "In them the Church, and then an angel dressed as a shepherd, called the Christians of Rome to repent, to turn from sin and live rightly again, while there was still time." Split it, together with M3's fix:
"In them the Church, the whole Christian people seen as a woman, and then an angel dressed as a shepherd, called Rome's Christians to repent while there was still time. To repent was to turn from sin and live rightly again."

**M3. MINOR: Words to explain, "the Church" glossed late.** It is first heard in paragraph 2, but its plain sense ("the whole Christian people seen as an old woman") comes only in paragraph 3. **M4. MINOR: "Churches read it aloud." (paragraph 5).** Said without a qualifier, it clashes with the list's "it cannot be read out to the people in church" three sentences later. Change it to: "Some churches read it aloud."

**M5. MINOR: who told him of the fixed day (paragraph 3).** In the book, the fixed day is in the writing of the book the old woman gave him, and its meaning came to him after he fasted (HE 905–911). With M3, the sentence reads: "He writes that the Church, as an old woman, gave him a book that said a day had been fixed."

**M6. MINOR: sentence past 30 words (ending, 33 words).** Split: "Near the end of his book, Hermas says he makes God's great works known to everyone. He hopes that all who have sinned before may hear these words, repent, and receive life again."

**M7. MINOR, a source note on the ending.** HE 5196–5199 reads "all those who love them, and have sinned before". The story, like the outline, drops "who love them". This is a fair paraphrase and does not mislead. No change is needed. It is recorded only so that the card's wording stays exact.
