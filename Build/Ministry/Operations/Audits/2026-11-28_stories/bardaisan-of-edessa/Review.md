## BLOCKING findings

**B1. s26: Eusebius made to translate, and his book misdescribed.** The brief says: "In another book, written against the astrologers, Eusebius himself copied long stretches of this same conversation into Greek, under Bardaisan's name." The source, PE (Gifford) 17927–17933: "my proofs on this subject I shall present to you from one who is by birth a Syrian ... in his Dialogues with his companions it is recorded that he spake as follows". Eusebius quotes a Greek text he already has; Eusebius himself says the pupils did the translating (EU 26637–26639). "Copied ... into Greek" tells a listener that Eusebius translated it, which the outline's Do not say bars. And the *Preparation* was not "written against the astrologers". This part of it argues against fate (PE 17900–17906, "the argument in favour of free-will ... convicting of falsehood the opinions concerning fate").
**Change to:** "In another work, arguing against belief in fate, Eusebius himself quoted long stretches of this same conversation in Greek, under Bardaisan's name."

**B2. s28: Eusebius's blame told in the narrator's voice.** The brief says: "Bardaisan left them and refuted most of their tales, but he never wholly washed off the stain of that old heresy." The source, EU 26653–26656: "having rejected his teaching ... he fancied that he had come over to the more correct opinion. Nevertheless he did not entirely wash off the filth of the old heresy." This is Eusebius's judgment. In a new sentence with no witness, the "stain" and "heresy" are heard as the narrator's own. That is the first of the four faults, and the outline's Do not say bars "heretic" in the narrator's voice.
**Change to:** "Bardaisan left them, he says, and refuted most of their tales, but he never wholly washed off the stain of that old heresy."

**B3. s35: Jerome's praise told without his charge.** The brief says: "Jerome ... called Bardaisan's book on fate the most famous and strongest of all his works." The source, THD 40139–40146: "He was at first a follower of Valentinus and afterwards his opponent and himself founded a new heresy ... Among these a most remarkable and strong work is the one ... On fate". JER-L 53437 agrees. Jerome's praise and his condemnation sit in the same entry. Telling only the praise carries one side forward. It also leaves s6 ("the same writers named Bardaisan among the teachers the church condemned") shown for Eusebius alone. The outline's own Leave out says Jerome's charge "is told", but Cut first item 2 cut it, against Part 2's rule never to cut the record's force to meet a length.
**Change to:** "Jerome, writing in Latin about twenty years later, called Bardaisan's book on fate the most famous and strongest of all his works, though he too says Bardaisan began with Valentinus and founded a new heresy."

**Length after the changes.** B1 changes the count by 0 words, B2 adds 2 and B3 adds 13. To hold about 790, cut s22 ("Avida's was not the only question Bardaisan answered.", 8 words; see M2) and "of Edessa" from s18 ("King Abgar of Edessa" becomes "King Abgar", 2 words), since Edessa is already set. With M1 the brief comes to about 790.
