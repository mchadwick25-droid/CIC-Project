# Card edits: Bardaisan of Edessa

New card: `bardaisan-of-edessa.card.json`. Starting card: the `card` of `bardaisan-of-edessa` in `cic-website/data/world-census.json`. The entry exists (`major: false`, atlasId I.74), so no `.entry.json` is written. File and line numbers are lines of the files under `cic/texts/` as the census validator counts them; BLC, EU, EPI, EPH-P, EPH-G, SOZ, THD, JER-L, HIP, CHE, BHE, MS, GN, THF, PE, ANF1 are the short names of `Source_Sheet.md`. Quotation marks are placed only in the three quotation entries.

## New

- **quotationOriginals Q1 and Q2.** Kept as they stand: Q1 modern wording equals brief s13, printed BLC 68441-68443; Q2 equals brief s20, printed BLC 68968-68969. No `secondReading` (six words or more; release sets it).
- **quotationOriginals Q2, added `uncertain`.** "Eusebius's Greek has only that they are called by one name; the words of Christ stand in the Syriac ..." Evidence: PE Eng. 18338-18339; Nau Latin PS 53785-53786 (Outline, Quotes).
- **quotationOriginals Q3.** Old modern wording "When that new world is set up, all evil turmoil will cease, and every rebellion will end. The foolish will be convinced, and all that is lacking will be filled." becomes the brief's s37, "When that new world is set up, all evil turmoil will cease, and every rebellion will end." Printed becomes "at the establishment of that new world all evil commotions shall cease, and all rebellions terminate", BLC 69026-69028 (old lines 69026-69029). The brief no longer quotes the foolish clause.
- **briefSources (new).** Eleven old sources the brief draws on, each with its file: BLC; EU; PE Greek and Gifford's English (brief s26); EPI; JER-L and THD (Jerome, s35); EPH-P (s31); EPH-G (s29 to s30); SOZ (s30); ANF1 (the glosses of Marcion s24 and Valentinus s27, Outline X1, X2).
- **timeline (new).** Twelve periods from the 150s to the 1200s, every line with its reference. Evidence by line: birth, Source_Sheet S1, CHE 49, EU 26584-26589, BLC 67892-67894, EPH-G 11186-11188, BHE 4221-4225, EPI 26326-26327; teaching and scenes, E1 to E10 (BLC 67908-69031); Epiphanius on Greek, Syriac and the ruler, EPI 26331-26334, 26369-26372; Eusebius, EU 26633-26643, 26652-26656; the Preparation, PE Eng. 17927-17933, 18323-18326, 18338-18339, Greek 28446; death, BHE 4220-4221, EPI 26426-26427, EU 26586-26593; Hippolytus, HIP 9656-9664, 12054-12060; Ephrem, EPH-G 11200-11207, 11241-11247, 11261-11274, EPH-P 1352, 1937, 2090; Gregory, GN 20491; Epiphanius, EPI 26327-26331, 26413-26416, 26417, 26486-26501; Jerome, JER-L 53431-53442, THD 40139-40150; Sozomen SOZ 32079-32103; Theodoret THD 15057-15066, 35058-35063, THF 29764-29770; Michael MS 14521-14544, 14602-14607; Barhebraeus BHE 4208-4214, 4250-4264. Dates follow the Kit's rule: the 150s and early 220s are Widely Accepted (D4); "around 200", "about 375" and "about 392" are the Outline's Setting and witness dates; Hippolytus is "the 220s or 230s" because the sheet's "about 225 to 235" rests on editors; the dialogue's own lines are "date unknown" (the text gives none, Outline Setting).
- **sources.about, added.** Eusebius's *Preparation* VI.10 (PE Greek 28434-28997, Gifford's English 17920-18364); Ephrem's hymns in Gwynn's English as their own entry (author Ephrem); Theodoret *Church History* 4.26 and *Letter* 146 (THD 15057-15066, 35058-35063); Theodoret *Compendium* I.22 (THF 29764-29770); Hippolytus (HIP 9656-9664, 12054-12060); Gregory of Nyssa (GN 20491); Justin and Irenaeus (ANF1 16131-16135, 29919-29932, 33572-33582); Michael the Syrian (MS 14521-14544).
- **keyWorks, added third.** *On Fate*: named by Eusebius as his most able dialogue (EU 26641-26642) and by Jerome as the most famous and strongest of his works (JER-L 53436-53438). Whether it is the Book of the Laws of Countries goes to `uncertain`.
- **otherNames, added.** Bardasanes (SOZ 32080); Bar Daisan, glossed "Syriac for son of Daisan, a river at Edessa" (BLC 67892-67894, THD 15060).
- **legacy, added.** Theodoret on the songs still used at the festivals of the martyrs (THD 15065-15066); Eusebius on the pupils' Greek and the long quotation (EU 26637-26639, PE 17927-17933); Theodoret on reading the books on fate and against Marcion (THF 29768-29770); Jerome's question on the force in Syriac (JER-L 53440-53442; Outline, Cut first 7); his name in later lists (THD 35058-35063, GN 20491).

## Clearly wrong or standard (old words, new words, evidence)

- **knownFor.** Old: "A Syriac teacher of Edessa whose pupil's dialogue, the Book of the Laws of Countries, argues that people are free and not ruled by the stars." New: "A Christian teacher of Edessa around 200 who taught that people are free and not ruled by the stars; his argument comes down in the Book of the Laws of Countries, a conversation as one of his pupils, Philip, tells it." Why: "pupil's dialogue" states the editors' judgment on authorship as the card's own (Part 1, Rigor; Source_Sheet E11, E15); the new words are the brief's s1 and s5 (BLC 68096, 68099-68100).
- **shortDescription.** New, two sentences: the brief's s1, s4, s5 and s6.
- **datesNote.** Old: "About 155 to about 222, by later Syriac chronicles". New: born in the 150s, died in the early 220s, each with its record's own reckoning (CHE 49; BHE 4220-4221; EPI 26426-26427; EU 26586-26589). Why: Part 1's date rule; the old "about 222" stood for Holl's 221 or 222 alone and left out Hort's 223.
- **placeNote.** Unchanged.
- **role.** Unchanged.
- **keyWorks 1.** Old: "written by a disciple". New: "told by one of his pupils, Philip". Evidence: BLC 68096, 68099-68100 (the dialogue's own narrator); authorship is the editors' judgment (EU 26622-26629; BLC 67855-67857), moved to `uncertain`.
- **keyWorks 2.** Added the gloss of Marcion's followers: Justin, ANF1 16131-16135; Irenaeus, ANF1 33572-33582; brief s24.
- **legacy.remembered 2.** Old: "Ephrem attacked Bardaisan as one of the worst of heretics in his Prose Refutations." New: "Ephrem wrote a prose discourse against his teaching that the body would turn to dust even without Adam's sin, and so would not rise." Why: "one of the worst of heretics" is McGiffert's summing up of Ephrem (EU 26538-26541), not Ephrem's words, so it is a modern view stated as the card's own; the new words are Ephrem's own, EPH-P 1936-1940, 2090, and the brief's s31.
- **uncertain 1.** Old: "McGiffert thinks Eusebius's order may well be right." New: "calls it not improbable that Eusebius's order is right". Evidence: EU 26562 ("It is not improbable that he may, as Eusebius says, have been a Valentinian for a time"). Also added: Epiphanius's and Barhebraeus's orders, and the glosses of Valentinus and Marcion.
- **uncertain 2.** Old: "The dialogue was written by a disciple, not by Bardaisan, says Arthur McGiffert, who translated Eusebius. In it Bardaisan speaks to 'Philip and Baryama.'" New: same, with Pratten, and the words of Philip and Baryama without quotation marks, and McGiffert's "soon after his death" (EU 26622-26629; BLC 67855-67857, 68337-68338).
- **uncertain 3, 4.** Kept; "Domnus" explained as a discourse (EPH-P 1352); Burkitt added (EPH-P 2612).
- **uncertain 5 (old).** "In the dialogue he admits that he once followed the astrologers himself." Removed from the open questions: it is documented (BLC 68337-68342) and the brief tells it (s11); it is now in the timeline.
- **uncertain (dates).** Old text kept; added Michael's 164 and Elias's 134 (MS 14521; BHE 4254-4264). Not otherwise changed.
- **sources.ownWords.** Old title "(his argument, written down by a disciple)". New "(his argument, as one of his pupils, Philip, tells it)". Same evidence as keyWorks 1.
- **sources.about, removed.** "Introductory notice and notes to the Recognitions of Clement", Thomas Smith (modern; moved word for word to the entry's `modernWorks`); "Introduction to Ephrem, with lines of his Nisibene Hymns and Hymns against Heresies in English", John Gwynn, 1898 (modern; moved word for word to `modernWorks`; the same file is now listed as Ephrem's own hymns).
- **briefReferences, changed.** Old 0 to 5, 7, 8: kept, except 4 (the Persians no longer in the brief, removed) and 7 (added "a most skillful debater in Syriac" and "his most able dialogue On Fate"). Old 6 and 9: kept. Old 10 (the *Recognitions*): removed, the brief makes no such claim. Old 11: replaced by Jerome's praise and charge (brief s35). Old 12: "the soul, the body and the first beings he said the world was made from" cut to the body charge the brief gives (s31). Old 13: "the curse on anyone who says ... the Seven" cut, the brief does not tell it (Outline, Leave out); kept the school's hymns. Old 15: "Valentinus and Marcion of Pontus: what each taught" replaced by the Justin and Irenaeus line (the Outline's X1 and X2 give the glosses from them); "World census, Edessa" kept as its own line.
- **Connections to other entries:** none added, as asked.
