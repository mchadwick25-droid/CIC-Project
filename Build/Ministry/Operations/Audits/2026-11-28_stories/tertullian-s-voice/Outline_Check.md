# Outline check: Tertullian (`tertullian-s-voice`)

Review 1 of 3, 2026-11-28. Checker did not write the outline.

## My account of the heart (written before reading `Outline.md`)

From the census entry (`why`, `relationsSummary`, `legacy`), the source sheet and what Tertullian is known for:

- **The essence.** Tertullian of Carthage, a convert writing in Roman Africa around the year 200, became the foremost early writer of the faith in Latin. He argued the Christians' case to Rome's governors, claimed the Scriptures for the churches the apostles founded, and set out God as one substance in three persons in Latin words later Latin writers used. Then he joined the strict New Prophecy and turned on the bishops; later writers kept reading him and judged him for the turn.
- **Also told.** What the books say: the Apology (Christians hated as a name and condemned unheard; "the blood of Christians is seed"); the Prescription (a lawyer's plea: the heretics have no title to the Scriptures, which belong to the apostles' churches; "I am the heir of the apostles"); Against Praxeas (against a teacher who, by his account, made the Father himself born and suffering: one substance, distinct persons; the words trinitas, persona, substantia are in his text). The turn: the New Prophecy's Paraclete, stricter rules, the split from the "unspiritual"; Jerome and Augustine differ on why. On Modesty: forgiveness belongs to the church of the Spirit, "not the church which consists of a number of bishops".
- **What came of him** (heaviest first): (1) read by the next Latin writers, Cyprian above all ("Give me the master", Jerome's hearsay at two removes), with Novatian's On the Trinity called an epitome of his by Jerome (doubted), and Lactantius on the Apology; (2) his Latin words for God, held only as words in his text (the claim that they became the Latin church's words rests on the census and editors, so it stays out unless a held later witness carries it); (3) his followers, the Tertullianists, lasting at Carthage to Augustine's day; (4) the later verdict, Augustine's heresy listing and Vincent's praise-then-blame. **Defended:** the apostolic churches' title to the Scriptures and the faith; the Christians' innocence before Rome; God's unity with the persons distinct; later, the Paraclete's stricter discipline against the bishops.

The ruling "foremost", never "the first", applies throughout.

## Verdict: FIX

One BLOCKING finding (the turn told twice, out of order). The rest are MINOR. The heart matches mine in substance: the outline's essence and "Also told" cover the same four books and the turn. It leaves "gave Latin its words" out of the heart, which my account also held back. The sources support that choice: the words stand in his text (KRO 11801–11802, 12231, 12558–12562), and no held old or later witness says they passed from him. All three quotes, the beats' cited lines, and the Latin readings were checked at their lines. Each holds, apart from what is listed below.

### My renderings of the quotes, against the outline's

Mine: "let the truth be allowed to reach your ears at least by the hidden way of silent letters" and "One thing she longs for meanwhile: not to be condemned unknown." The outline's (a) and (b) match in sense. "For now" for *interdum* follows Thelwall's "meanwhile" (ANF3 1464). Holds.
- **Quote 2** (OEH2 2283–2289; ANF3 21742–21751). Mine: "By what right, Marcion, do you cut down my wood? Its Latin "heres" is a normalization of the scan's "haeres" (OEH2 2289). Holds.
- **Quote 3** (KRO 12221–12229). Mine: "Root and shoot are two things, but joined; spring and river are two forms, but undivided; sun and ray are two forms, but cohering … For the Spirit is third from God and the Son, as the fruit from the shoot is third from the root." The outline's matches ("tree" for *frutex* follows Holmes, ANF3 51561). Holds.

### BLOCKING

1. **The turn is told twice, and the second telling goes back in time.** Beat 4 tells the turn in his own words. Then "What came 3", placed after Cyprian (about 250) and Novatian, returns to it: it gives Jerome's and Augustine's reasons *why he turned* (JER 40506–40509; AUG 2868–2873). It also tells a new event of his life there, Augustine's "later broke from them too and gathered meetings of his own". This breaks Part 1 ("tell it once, in order, never the same event twice") and Part 2 ("no event is in two beats"). It also parts the break from its reason, though Part 1 (Explain it) wants the reason with the act where the old sources give it. **Correction:** put Jerome's motive and Augustine's cause into beat 4, credited side by side, with "Contested, do not settle" kept. Delete "What came 3". Move Augustine's "later broke from them too and gathered meetings of his own" to the head of the followers item (now "What came 3"), so it leads straight into the Tertullianists. Add Augustine's verdict there: he counts the Tertullianists among the heresies and says Tertullian became a heretic by going over to the Phrygians (AUG 2827–2835, 2868–2873). Update the Length count, the "Who tells it" line that names this scene ("What came 3"), and the story's question line. The total word count does not change.

### MINOR

2. **Length arithmetic does not add up.** The listed parts sum to 705, but the stated total is "about 755". The Cut first items sum to 92 (80 without the conditional item 5). That leaves either 613 or 663–675, not "about 640". My own recount of what the beats must carry (quotes 112 words, the glosses, the five credited witnesses) is about 715. Less the full Cut first list, that comes to about 623, so the outline stays inside Mark's "Write to 4:30" whichever count is used. That is why this is not blocking. **Correction:** restate the per-part counts so that they sum to the stated total. Then make item 5 unconditional, or set the target explicitly at the count the list actually reaches.
3. **"Three persons" is cited to Holmes's supplement.** Beat 5 has the narrator say "Father, Son and Spirit are three persons" as Tertullian's own words, and cites T4 (KRO 12558–12562). But T4's Latin has only "in tribus cohaerentibus"; "(Persons)" is Holmes's addition, as the sheet itself warns. The Latin that holds it is Against Praxeas 11: "ex tertia persona de patre et filio" (KRO 12465) and "unamquamque personam in sua proprietate constituunt" (KRO 12497–12498). **Correction:** add KRO 12465 and 12497–12498 to beat 5 and to T5 as the support for "three persons".
4. **The rival teachers have no act of their own.** Beat 3 says only that they "built new teachings on the Scriptures", and Quote 2 then names Marcion and Valentinus with nothing said of either. The Prescription itself says what each did: "One man perverts the Scriptures with his hand, another their meaning by his exposition … Marcion expressly and openly used the knife … Valentinus … removing the proper meaning of every particular word" (ANF3 21805–21818, ch. 38). **Correction:** add to beat 3, as the Explain-it line for "rival teachers", one clause from ch. 38: "one cut the Scriptures down to suit himself, another kept them whole and bent their meaning". Count it at about 15 words, inside the existing Prescription budget, or add it ahead of Cut first item 4.
5. **The Connection field states a contested point as fact.** It says the listener must understand that he "ended outside the church whose case he made". No source tells how he ended (E9). From whom he withdrew is read differently (D6), and the outline's own Do not say bars "he left the church at Carthage" as fact. **Correction:** replace that line with: "the same man the next Latin writers learned from wrote, Jerome says, directly against the church, and Augustine counted him a heretic and his followers among the heresies, yet said his books were still read".
6. **Cyprian's chain drops a link.** The suggested story clause "Jerome heard it, through Cyprian's old secretary" leaves out Paul, the old man in Italy, which hides that the report is two removes from Cyprian (JERL 55617–55624). **Correction:** use "Jerome heard it from an old man in Italy who had met Cyprian's secretary".
7. **Words to explain.** (a) "Heretic" and "heresies" appear as Augustine's words with no plain sense given. Part 1 names "heresy" as a label to explain. **Correction:** add to Words to explain: "heretic: in Augustine's book on heresies, one he counted outside the true faith for a false teaching (AUG 2827, 2868–2873)". (b) The gloss for "Phrygia, in today's Turkey" cites only E5 and the sibling story, and E5 does not name Phrygia. **Correction:** cite Augustine's "Cataphrygas", the Phrygians (AUG 2869), or Eusebius: they "crawled over Asia and Phrygia, boasting that Montanus was the Paraclete" (EUS 29267, Church History V.14).
8. **Do not say: additions.** Add each wrong form with its right wording:
   - "Cyprian's teacher", "taught Cyprian", "Cyprian studied under him". They never met in any held source. Say "Cyprian read him every day" (Jerome's report).
   - "Novatian summarized" or "copied Tertullian's book" as fact. Say "which Jerome called a kind of summary of Tertullian's work".
   - "Augustine won them back" or "reconciled them". Say "while Augustine was at Carthage" (AUG 2830–2831, "me autem ibi posito").
   - "the bishop of Rome" or "the pope" for On Modesty's bishop. Say "a bishop" (the issuer is disputed: ANF4-ed 7178–7184; E8).
   - "Augustine listed his books among the heresies". He lists his followers. Say "the Tertullianists", and say that his books are still read (AUG 2827–2828).
9. **The story's question line is ambiguous.** It says "his books were still read by the bishop who listed them as heretics", and "them" can be read as the books. **Correction:** use "by the bishop who listed his followers among the heresies".
10. **The ending repeats What came 4.** "What came 4" and Ending (a) both tell the handing over of the building. **Correction:** mark "What came 4" (after finding 1, the new "What came 3") as the ending itself, told once.
11. **Links.** "Vincent of Lérins" has no entry on the tree. **Correction:** mark him "card, no entry" and keep only Hilary of Poitiers as a link.
