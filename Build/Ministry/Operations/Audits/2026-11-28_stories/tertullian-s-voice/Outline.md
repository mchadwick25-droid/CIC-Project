# Outline: Tertullian

Sheet items (S, E, T, I, D) are in `Source_Sheet.md`, which gives the file and line for each. ANF3 and ANF4 are the Ante-Nicene Fathers English; OEH1, OEH2, KRO and CSEL20 the held Latin; JER and JERL Jerome in English and Latin; EUS Eusebius; LACT and LACT-E Lactantius; AUG Augustine, *On Heresies* 86 (Latin only); VIN Vincent of Lérins; MON Monceaux (modern).

## The heart

- **The essence:** Tertullian of Carthage was the foremost early writer of the faith in Latin, around the year 200 (Mark, 2026-11-20). He wrote to defend the Christians before Rome's governors, to claim the Scriptures for the churches the apostles founded, and to say in Latin how God is one and yet three, and later Latin writers read him closely; but in his later years he joined the strict New Prophecy and wrote against the bishops (S1, S2, E1, E4, T4, T6, E5, E8, E8a, I3; Documented for the books and the turn; "foremost" Widely Accepted as rank, held by the old witnesses together: Jerome's "chief of the Latin writers after Victor and Apollonius", JER 40495, Vincent's "as Origen holds by far the first place among the Greeks, so does Tertullian among the Latins", VIN 13555–13557, Augustine's "most eloquently written", AUG 2827–2828; "read him closely" Documented through Cyprian, I3).
- **Also told:**
  - **The Apology, about 197:** the governors condemn the name alone without asking what Christians do; the crowd blames them for every disaster; the more they are cut down, the more they grow (E1, E2; Documented; the year Widely Accepted).
  - **The Prescription:** a lawyer's plea that throws a case out before it is argued: rival teachers may not argue from Scripture at all, because the Scriptures belong to the churches that received them from the apostles (E4, T2, S6; Documented as his argument).
  - **The turn:** the New Prophecy's prophets said the Holy Spirit spoke anew through them and asked more of Christians; he took it up and set his side apart from "the unspiritual" (E5; Documented).
  - **Against Praxeas, written after the turn:** Father, Son and Spirit distinct but not divided, as root, tree and fruit; one substance, three persons; the Latin words *trinitas*, *persona*, *substantia* are in his text (E7, T3, T4, T5, T6; Documented as his book and words).
  - **On Modesty:** the church of the Spirit forgives sins, not the church that is a number of bishops; Jerome lists it among the books he wrote against the church (E8, E8a; Documented).
- **What came of them:** see below; heaviest first, Cyprian.
- **Not in the heart, by the kit's modern-work rule:** "he gave Latin its words for the Trinity" (the batch 7 heart, Widely Accepted from the census and editors only, T5, I1). No held old or later witness says the words passed from him. The story says what the record holds: the words are in his book (T5, T6), and Jerome judged Novatian's *On the Trinity* a kind of summary of his work (I2).

## The story's question

Who holds the faith, and who may speak for it? He argued, as a lawyer would, that the churches the apostles founded hold the title to the Scriptures and rival teachers have none (beat 3). Later he held that the church of the Spirit, not the church of bishops, holds the power to forgive (beat 6). The record resolves it at the end: his own followers' church, at Carthage, was handed over to the Catholic church, and his books were still read by the bishop who listed his followers among the heresies (What came 3, the Ending). The writer lets the question run through the acts; the narrator never states it.

## Setting

- **Time:** around the year 200. The Apology about 197 (Widely Accepted: EUS-ed 10294–10303; MON 17149–17150); Jerome puts his best years under the emperors Severus and Caracalla (S2, JER 40498–40499). No other year is told. Plain words: "around the year 200"; "about 197" for the Apology only.
- **Place:** Carthage, the great city of Roman Africa, today in Tunisia (S1; JER 40496; CEN `modernCountries`). Plain words: "Carthage, in Roman Africa".
- **Tradition:** the census home is `latin-apologists`, "The Case Made in Latin" (S3): the Christians who made the faith's case in Latin. Do not say "the Latin apologists" bare.
- **Language:** he wrote in Latin (a few works in Greek are lost, EUS-ed 10260–10262).

## Words to explain

- **Apology:** "a defense" (E1). "his Apology, a defense of the Christians".
- **the governors:** the Latin *antistites* are those presiding as judges (OEH1 11034–11035; ANF3 1448–1452): "the governors who judged cases for Rome". Not "emperors".
- **prescription:** "a lawyer's plea that throws a case out before it is argued" (S6; ANF3-ed 20037–20038). Work the sense in; the bare word may be left out.
- **teachers he held false:** for "heretics" (T2 context); never "heretics" in the narrator's voice except as Augustine's word, credited.
- **Marcion, Valentinus:** named only inside Quote 2; introduce them as "rival teachers" first.
- **the New Prophecy:** "a movement from Phrygia, in today's Turkey, whose prophets said the Holy Spirit was speaking anew through them" (E5; Phrygia: Augustine's "Cataphrygas", the Phrygians, AUG 2869; Eusebius, they "crawled over Asia and Phrygia, boasting that Montanus was the Paraclete", EUS 29267). Say "the Holy Spirit, the Helper Jesus promised" for the Paraclete; do not use the word "Paraclete".
- **the unspiritual:** his scornful name for the church members who did not accept the new prophets (E5; ANF3 51070–51076; KRO 11745–11746 *psychicis*).
- **second marriage:** "marrying again after a husband or wife had died" (E5; ANF4 5731–5732).
- **substance and person:** substance is what God is, one and undivided; person is who is distinct, Father, Son and Spirit (T4; KRO 12558–12562). The narrator gives it in these words beside Quote 3. Do not explain *persona* as "mask".
- **Trinity:** a grown listener knows the word; say once that its Latin form, *trinitas*, stands in this book (T6, KRO 12231).
- **heretic:** in Augustine's book on heresies, one he counted outside the true faith for a false teaching (AUG 2827, 2868–2873). Used only as Augustine's word, credited.
- **Catholic:** Augustine's word for the main church: "the Catholic church, the main body" (AUG 2833–2835).

## Shape

Teacher or thinker. The question he answered: in Latin, before Rome's courts and against rival teachers, who holds the faith and how is it to be said? What he taught: the Christians' innocence before the law; the apostles' churches as owners of the Scriptures; God one substance in three persons; later, a church of the Spirit against the bishops. Who learned it: Cyprian above all; Novatian, as Jerome judged. The trap is a list of books: each book is told through one act or line and tied to the question, and Against Marcion, Against the Valentinians and the rest stay out.

## Openings

1. **Chosen.** His own first lines in the Apology (Quote 1; E1, T1). At Carthage in Roman Africa, about 197, Tertullian wrote to the governors who judged Christians for Rome. If they would not look into the charges openly, he said, let the truth reach them in writing (Quote 1a), and he asked one thing (Quote 1b). Then the essence. The name, the place and the year are all in the first sentence.
2. Not chosen: Cyprian, half a century later, asking his secretary for "the master" (I3). It reaches ahead to the end.

## Story beats

The opening and the essence are the first minute. The beats then run once, in time order. Jerome and Augustine are credited inside beat 4 for why he turned, as witnesses, not as events of their own time; what they and others did comes in What came of him, in their own order. No beat tells his old age or death.

1. **The essence** (as above). Eusebius's word on the law may come here, in one clause, credited: "he knew Roman law well, Eusebius says" (S5; EUS 10241–10242, 10272–10280; Documented as Eusebius's; that he practised as an advocate, Contested, not said). It readies beat 3.
2. **The Apology, about 197.** The governors condemned the very name of Christian, he argued, without learning what Christians did (E1; ANF3 1472–1478; Documented as his argument). If the Tiber flooded or the rain failed, the crowd's cry was for the Christians to be thrown to the lion (E2; ANF3 3997–4003; Documented; "lion" singular, ANF3-ed 4000–4001). He ended the book defiant: the more often Rome cut them down, the more they grew, for the blood of Christians is seed (E2; ANF3 4653–4661; OEH1 30954–30955; Documented; told as his words in indirect speech, not a set quote). Explain it: the Apology was a defense written for the accused, addressed to the judges; why: Christians were being condemned in the courts (E1, ANF3 1448–1456).
3. **The Prescription.** Rival teachers built new teachings on the Scriptures and argued from them (E4; ANF3 20765–20768). Explain it, as the Prescription itself does: one cut the Scriptures down to suit himself, another kept them whole and bent their meaning (ANF3 21805–21818, ch. 38: "One man perverts the Scriptures with his hand, another their meaning by his exposition … Marcion expressly and openly used the knife … Valentinus … removing the proper meaning of every particular word"; Documented as his account). About 15 words. Tertullian answered with a plea from the law courts, one that throws a case out before it is argued (S6). Before any argument from Scripture, he said, it must be settled whose the Scriptures are; and they belong to the churches the apostles founded, such as Ephesus and Rome, which anyone could visit and which still kept what the apostles taught (E4; ANF3 20860–20866, 20937–20942, 21657–21677; Documented as his argument). Then Quote 2: he spoke as an owner speaks to trespassers on his land. Why it mattered to him: the rival teachers used the same Scriptures for teachings the churches rejected, and he denied them the right to use them at all (ANF3 20765–20768). No year is told (the date is Monceaux's).
4. **The turn.** He came to believe the New Prophecy (explained above): its prophets asked more of Christians than the church did, with stricter fasts, no second marriage, and no flight from persecution (E5; ANF4 5731–5732; Documented). He wrote that his acceptance and defense of the Spirit's new voice had set his side apart from those he called the unspiritual (E5; ANF3 51074–51076; KRO 11745–11746; Documented). From whom he withdrew is read differently (D6): say only "set his side apart from the others", never "left the church of Carthage". No year is told. Why he turned, the later writers of his life differ, credited side by side in this beat (D1; Contested; do not settle): Jerome says the envy and insults of the clergy of Rome drove him to the New Prophecy (E6; JER 40506–40509; JERL 55626–55629); Augustine says he went over to the prophets he had once argued against, and condemned second marriage as if it were sexual sin (E6; AUG 2868–2873, "quos ante destruxerat", "tanquam stupra damnare"; Documented as Augustine's). The reasons go with the act, where Part 1 (Explain it) wants them.
5. **Against Praxeas, written after the turn.** Praxeas, by Tertullian's account, taught at Rome that the Father himself was born and suffered, so that Father and Son were one and the same (E7; ANF3 51014–51016; a hostile witness: "by Tertullian's account"). Why it mattered to him: if Father and Son were one and the same, the Son was not distinct (T4). Tertullian answered that Father, Son and Spirit are distinct but not divided, and he showed it with things from nature (T6). Then Quote 3. Then, in the narrator's words: God, he wrote, is one substance, and Father, Son and Spirit are three persons (substance is what God is, person who is distinct), and the Latin word for Trinity, *trinitas*, stands in this book (T4, T5, T6; one substance and "person, not substance", KRO 12558–12562; the Spirit as "tertia persona", the third person, KRO 12465, and "unamquamque personam in sua proprietate", each person in its own nature, KRO 12497–12498, Against Praxeas 11; *trinitas*, KRO 12231; Documented). "(Persons)" in Holmes's English of ch. 12 is his supplement and is not the support. That he wrote it after the turn: he cites the Spirit's new teaching in it (E7; ANF3 51097–51100; T6, KRO 12194–12195; Documented). Make plain that every "he" in this beat is Tertullian, by naming him where Praxeas has just been named.
6. **On Modesty.** When a bishop announced he would forgive adultery once the sinner had repented (ANF4 7187–7188; the bishop is not named), Tertullian answered that the church of the Spirit would forgive sins, through a spiritual man, and not the church that is a number of bishops (E8; ANF4 9542–9545; CSEL20 15312–15314; Documented; told in indirect speech). Jerome lists it among the books he wrote directly against the church (E8a; JER 40511–40513; JERL 55630–55633). Why: he held that the power to forgive belonged to God's Spirit, "not the servant's" (ANF4 9545–9546; CSEL20 15314–15315). Do not call it his last book.

## What came of him

Heaviest first, where weight is what later witnesses carried on and used; told in time order.

1. **Cyprian.** A generation later, Cyprian, bishop of Carthage, never let a day pass without reading him, and would ask his secretary for "the master", meaning Tertullian (I3; JERL 55618–55626; Documented as Jerome's report). Jerome heard it from an old man in Italy who as a youth had met Cyprian's secretary at Rome (Jerome's Latin: Paul had seen the secretary; Richardson's English wrongly makes Paul the secretary, D2). Tell the chain in one clause: "Jerome heard it from an old man in Italy who had met Cyprian's secretary". This is the strongest sign of what he passed on: the next great Latin writer of Carthage read him daily.
2. **Novatian.** At Rome, Novatian wrote a large book On the Trinity, which Jerome called a kind of summary of Tertullian's work (I2; JER 40895–40897; Documented as Jerome's judgment). Credit Jerome; give no modern doubt.
3. **Augustine's verdict and his followers (the ending, told once).** Augustine, about two centuries after Tertullian, counted the Tertullianists among the heresies: Tertullian, he wrote, became a heretic by going over to the Phrygians, and later broke from them too and gathered meetings of his own (E6, I5; AUG 2827–2835, 2868–2873, "ab ipsis divisus, sua conventicula propagavit"; Documented as Augustine's). A small group at Carthage bore his name down to Augustine's day, dwindling; while Augustine was there, the very few left crossed into the Catholic church and handed over their church building (I5; AUG 2829–2835; Documented as Augustine's report). In the same entry he says that many of Tertullian's works, most eloquently written, are read (I8; AUG 2827–2828). This item is the Ending; it is told once.

Defended: the Christians' innocence before Rome's courts (Apology); the apostles' churches' title to the Scriptures (Prescription); Father, Son and Spirit as one God, distinct and not divided, against Praxeas; later, the New Prophecy and a church of the Spirit against the bishops.

## Connection to carry into the story

What passed from Tertullian was a Latin way of saying the faith, read and used by the Latin writers after him: the case for the Christians before the courts, the claim that the Scriptures belong to the apostles' churches, and the account of God as one substance and three persons (T2, T4, T6). How it passed, through acts: Cyprian read him every day and asked for "the master" (I3); Novatian wrote On the Trinity, which Jerome took for a summary of his work (I2). Why it passed with a mark against it: he wrote against the bishops in his later years, and Augustine counted him a heretic and his followers among the heresies, yet said his books were still read (E6, I5, I8; AUG 2827–2835, 2868–2873). The listener must understand that the same man the next Latin writers learned from wrote, Jerome says, directly against the church, and Augustine counted him a heretic and his followers among the heresies, yet said his books were still read; his books outlived his followers. Tell it through what people did; no "this matters because" sentence.

## Ending

Close on the subject; this is What came 3, told once, not again after it. (a), recommended: the last followers hand over their church building at Carthage, and Augustine, in the same entry, writes that many of Tertullian's works, written with great eloquence, are still read (I5, I8; AUG 2827–2835). It ends on his books, which are the reason he is on the tree, and closes the opening's plea to be known. (b) The handing over of the building alone. Never end on his old age or his death, which no source records.

## Do not say

- "The first Christian to write in Latin", "the first" in any form. Say "the foremost early writer of the faith in Latin" (Mark, 2026-11-20; D4).
- "He gave Latin its words for the Trinity", "the West still uses his words", "the Latin church took its words from this book", or "he coined" any word. Say the Latin words stand in his book (T5, T6), and give Novatian through Jerome.
- "He invented the word Trinity" or "the first to call God a Trinity": the Greek word is older (T5).
- "The Council of Nicaea used his words": no held source says so.
- "His last book", "his last surviving book", "probably his last": say "On Modesty", placed by Jerome among the books against the church (E8a).
- Any modern historian or editor, named or unnamed, and any modern doubt ("a modern historian doubts it").
- A year for the Prescription, the turn, Against Praxeas or On Modesty (all Monceaux's). Only the Apology gets "about 197".
- "He was a lawyer" or "an advocate in Rome" as fact. Say "he knew Roman law well, Eusebius says" (S5, D3).
- "The blood of the martyrs is the seed of the church." His words: the blood of Christians is seed (E2).
- "The Christians to the lions": the Latin is singular, "the lion" (ANF3-ed 4000–4001).
- "He left the church at Carthage", "he founded his own church" or "he was excommunicated" as fact. Say he set his side apart from those he called the unspiritual, and give Augustine's account credited (D6, E6, I5).
- "Never made a saint" or "the church refused to make him a saint": no held source says it (Mark, 2026-11-20).
- Why he turned, as fact: credit Jerome and Augustine side by side (D1).
- "Paul of Concordia was Cyprian's secretary": he had met the secretary (D2). Say "through Cyprian's old secretary".
- "Montanists" in the narrator's voice: say "the New Prophecy".
- "Praxeas taught that the Father died on the cross" as the narrator's fact: by Tertullian's account, the Father himself was born and suffered (E7). Do not call Praxeas a bishop or name the bishop of Rome.
- "Persona means mask."
- "Priest" or "presbyter": his church office rests on Jerome alone; leave it out.
- How he felt: bitter, proud, lonely, angry. None is held.
- "He ended" or "he closed" for Quote 1b: it is in chapter 1. The Apology ends with the seed (ch. 50).
- In beat 5, a "he" that could be Praxeas: name Tertullian at the turn of the argument.
- "No one records when or how he died", "we know little of his life", "most of his life rests on his own books": a sentence that only says what is missing, or a how-we-know sentence.
- A gloss or restatement after a quote: the plain sense of substance and person comes after Quote 3 as new matter (his own two words), never as a retelling of the root and the tree.
- "Cyprian's teacher", "taught Cyprian", "Cyprian studied under him": they never met in any held source. Say "Cyprian read him every day" (Jerome's report).
- "Novatian summarized" or "copied Tertullian's book" as fact. Say "which Jerome called a kind of summary of Tertullian's work".
- "Augustine won them back" or "reconciled them". Say "while Augustine was at Carthage" (AUG 2830–2831, "me autem ibi posito").
- "the bishop of Rome" or "the pope" for On Modesty's bishop. Say "a bishop" (the issuer is disputed: ANF4-ed 7178–7184; E8).
- "Augustine listed his books among the heresies". He lists his followers. Say "the Tertullianists", and say that his books are still read (AUG 2827–2828).
- "The word the Spirit's prophets used", "his teacher", "a later writer": name the thing or the person.

## Cut first

In order, to bring about 755 planned words to about 640:

1. The crowd's cry in beat 2 (the Tiber, the lion): card. About 30.
2. Novatian, What came 2: card. About 25 (names go to eight).
3. The three demands of the New Prophecy in beat 4 cut to "a stricter life" with one example, no second marriage: card for fasts and flight. About 15.
4. "such as Ephesus and Rome, which anyone could visit" in beat 3 cut to "such as Rome": card. About 10.
5. Eusebius's clause on the law in beat 1: Leave out (beat 3 explains the plea on its own). About 12.

Never cut: the explanation of the prescription, the New Prophecy, the unspiritual, substance and person; Jerome and Augustine side by side; Augustine's verdict and the followers' end.

## For the card, not the story

- Beat 1: Jerome, from Carthage, his father a centurion on the governor's staff (S4); "men are made, not born, Christians" (S7).
- "kill us, torture us, condemn us, grind us to dust; your injustice is the proof that we are innocent" (ANF3 4653–4654).
- Quote 2's opening questions and Apelles sentence (ANF3 21742–21749).
- The apostolic churches in full: Corinth, Philippi, Ephesus, Rome (ANF3 21657–21677).
- Praxeas "from Asia", the bishop of Rome and the new prophets, "put to flight the Paraclete, and crucified the Father" (ANF3 51037–51057; KRO 11738–11739).
- Quote 3's cut comparisons: the stream from the river, the tip of the ray (ANF3 51569–51571).
- Lactantius: the Apology pleaded the cause in full; his style obscure (I4).
- Vincent of Lérins and Hilary (I6); the "Gelasian" decree, its date and author disputed (I6).
- Jerome: he is said to have lived to a great old age (E9).
- The *Octavius* of Minucius Felix may be older; editors differ on its date (D4).
- Modern works, by what they cover: Monceaux (life and chronology), Harnack (chronology, Novatian), McGiffert's notes (Eusebius), Coxe and Holmes's notes (ANF).

## Leave out

- Against Marcion and Against the Valentinians beyond the two names in Quote 2: the siblings tell them.
- "What has Athens to do with Jerusalem?": about philosophy; off this story's question.
- The soldier's crown and the letter to Scapula: the census's documented stories.
- The Passion of Perpetua and Felicitas: an editor's guess that he edited it.
- His church office: Jerome alone.
- The jurist Tertullian of the Roman law books: modern hypothesis.
- Which bishop issued the pardon: disputed; the Hermas story's ground.
- The Apology in Greek and Eusebius's use of it: the Pliny story.
- His views on the soul and God as body (AUG 2836–2868): off the question.
