## BLOCKING

**B1. SOFTENED OR CHANGED: "Yet in his later years he joined a strict movement, the New Prophecy, and wrote against the bishops."** (paragraph 2)
- Jerome puts the turn after *middle* life: "Hic cum usque ad mediam aetatem presbyter Ecclesiae permansisset, invidia postea et contumeliis clericorum Romanae Ecclesiae, ad Montani dogma delapsus" (JERL 55626–55629); "He was presbyter of the church until middle life, afterwards driven…" (JER 40506–40508). He then "is said to have lived to a decrepit old age" (JER 40513–40514).
- "In his later years" sounds like old age to a listener. Jerome gives the turn as the middle of a long life.
- **Change:** "Yet later in life he joined a strict movement, the New Prophecy, and wrote against the bishops."
- **Do not say (add to the outline):** "'In his later years', 'in old age' or 'in his last years' for the turn: Jerome puts it after middle life (JERL 55626–55628, 'usque ad mediam aetatem … postea'). Say 'later in life'." (The outline's essence and Connection both carry "in his later years", and Fixes.md "Worded by me" 4 introduced it.)

**B2. ASSUMPTION ADDED: "Why he turned, the later writers of his life differ."** (paragraph 5, with the Augustine sentence after it)
- Jerome gives a motive for the turn: the envy and insults of the clergy of Rome (JERL 55627–55628). That part is HELD.
- Augustine gives no motive for the turn. His "quia" answers a different question: why Tertullian *became a heretic*. It answers the soul doctrine he has just set aside: "Non ergo ideo est Tertullianus factus haereticus: sed quia transiens ad Cataphrygas, quos ante destruxerat, coepit etiam secundas nuptias … tanquam stupra damnare" (AUG 2868–2871). In plain English: "Not for that did Tertullian become a heretic, but because, going over to the Cataphrygians whom he had earlier refuted, he *also began* to condemn second marriages as fornication."
- So the two writers are not giving rival answers to "why he turned". Framing them that way gives Augustine a claim he does not make. The source sheet's E6 and D1 carry the same frame, but the line itself does not hold it.
- Jerome's motive is still credited, as a later witness's account, and nothing is settled that the sources leave open.
- **Change:** replace "Why he turned, the later writers of his life differ. Jerome says the envy and insults of the clergy of Rome drove him to it." with "Jerome, who wrote his life, says the envy and insults of the clergy of Rome drove him to it." Keep the next sentence as it stands: "Augustine says he went over to the prophets he had once argued against, and began to condemn second marriage as if it were sexual sin."
- **Do not say (add to the outline):** "'The later writers differ on why he turned', or Augustine's line given as a reason for the turn. His 'quia' (AUG 2868–2871) says why he counted Tertullian a heretic, not why he turned; credit Jerome alone for a motive."

**B3. Event told twice: "Tertullian, he wrote, became a heretic by going over to the Phrygian prophets, then broke from them too and gathered meetings of his own."** (paragraph 9)
- Paragraph 5 has already told Augustine's going-over to the prophets. Paragraph 9 tells it again, and that jump back is exactly what the outline check's BLOCKING 1 (Fixes.md, Applied 1) set out to remove. The outline's What came 3 still scripts the repeat ("became a heretic by going over to the Phrygians").
- **Change:** "For that teaching, he wrote, Tertullian became a heretic; later he broke from the prophets too and gathered meetings of his own." "That teaching" is the condemnation of second marriage, which is Augustine's stated cause (AUG 2868–2873). "Ab ipsis divisus, sua conventicula propagavit" holds the rest.
- The sentences on either side stand: the gloss of "heresies" before it, and the followers' end after it.
- **Do not say (add to the outline):** "In What came 3, 'became a heretic by going over to the Phrygians': beat 4 has told the going-over. Give Augustine's verdict as resting on the teaching already told ('for that teaching')."

## MINOR

**M1. A failing sentence: "Jerome heard it from an old man in Italy who had met Cyprian's secretary, and Cyprian, the secretary said, would often ask for him as the master."**
- "Him" falls nearest to the secretary or the old man, so a listener can take it that Cyprian called the secretary "the master". The story's best evidence of being read closely is then lost.
- The chain itself holds: "referreque sibi solitum … ac sibi crebro dicere, Da magistrum: Tertullianum videlicet significans" (JERL 55621–55626).
- **Change:** "…and Cyprian, the secretary said, would often ask, 'Give me the master,' meaning Tertullian."

**M2. A failing sentence: "In the same book he put it in two Latin words."**
- The listener never hears the Latin words, so "two Latin words" is left hanging before the English "substance" and "persons".
- **Change:** "In the same book he put it in two words."

**M3. A failing sentence: "In another book Tertullian answered them with a plea from the law courts…"**
- The account of Marcion and Valentinus just before it comes from the Prescription itself (ANF3 21805–21818, chapter 38). Beside "as Tertullian tells it", "another book" suggests that the account and the answer come from different books.
- **Change:** "Marcion, as Tertullian tells it in another book, cut the Scriptures down to suit himself, and Valentinus kept them whole but bent their meaning. There he answered them with a plea from the law courts, one that throws a case out before it is argued."

**M4. Narrator clause: "And he ended: 'I am the heir of the apostles.'"**
- The speech goes on past that line: "Ego sum haeres apostolorum. Sicut caverunt testamento suo … ita teneo" (OEH2 2289–2290; ANF3 21751–21753).
- **Change:** "And he claimed:"

**M5. "He defended the Christians before Rome's courts"** (paragraph 2)
- Beside the plea from the law courts in paragraph 4, this edges toward "he was an advocate", which the outline's Do not say list forbids. The outline's essence says "before Rome's governors".
- The opening makes it plain that he wrote, so this one is not counted BLOCKING.
- **Change:** "He defended the Christians to Rome's governors."
