# What it was about: scan results (2026-11-23)

Ruling: Decision-Log, "The live stories reopened for what each thing was about: scan everything, then a targeted pass" (Mark, 2026-11-23, "a, scan all three"). The test is the kit's: every charge, teaching, book, quarrel or decision a text names says in plain words what it was, and why it mattered to the people in it.

Method: 24 blind readers (Opus), one chunk each, with `Reader_Prompt.md`. They read the live census and world-page text as a first-time listener, and checked no sources. Coverage was checked against the exported items: all 625 stories with a `longDescription`, all 736 connection notes and all 12 built-world pages, none missing. Each chunk's table, with every gap quoted, is in `S01.md`–`S14.md`, `E01.md`–`E04.md` and `W01.md`–`W06.md`. `Ranked.md` lists every item, worst first.

## What the counts say

- **The kit helped, and didn't close the gap.** Stories written on the kit: 48 percent clear, 4 percent many. Stories written before it: 38 percent clear, 17 percent many.
- **The built-world pages are the worst.** Ten of twelve have many gaps. They are long, they name many disputes, and none of them has been through the kit.
- **Connection notes are half clear.** Their gaps are the one-line "argued against", "condemned" or "broke with" notes that never say over what.
- **A few great disputes cause much of it.** Counting gap lines that name each dispute: the Arian or Homoian quarrel 48, Chalcedon 45, Origen 23, Easter dating 18, Pelagius 16, Nestorius 14, Marcion 9, the Donatists 8, the one-nature (miaphysite) side 7, the Three Chapters 6, Jansenism 6. A story names the dispute and leaves its content to some other page that doesn't explain it either.

The readers judged one hearing each, without sources, so a count is a reader's judgment, not a verified finding. The fix pass checks each gap before changing anything.
