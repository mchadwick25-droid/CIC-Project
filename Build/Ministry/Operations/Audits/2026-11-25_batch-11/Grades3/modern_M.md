## alexandria-catechetical
Heart: In Alexandria, Christians learned their faith step by step in a school of teachers who read Scripture for a deeper sense, from Clement and Origen to Dionysius, who won over Nepos's followers by open debate.
Findings: 0

## antiochene-church-third-century
Heart: The church of Antioch, bound by letters to churches across the empire, judged and put out its own bishop, Paul of Samosata, and its line of teachers ran on through Lucian to the Arian quarrel.
Findings: 0

## roman-church-third-century
Heart: Rome's large, mixed church argued in public over God, forgiveness and marriage, split in 251, and still chose to heal the fallen through repentance.
Findings: 0

## greek-apologists-second-century
Heart: Second-century Christians writing in Greek answered charges of atheism, cannibalism and incest by putting written defenses before emperors and pagans and asking to be judged by how they lived.
Findings: 0

## latin-apologists
Heart: Latin-speaking Christians of Rome and Africa defended their faith to the educated who despised it, answering the charges out of the pagans' own books.
Findings: 0

## post-apostolic-house-church
Heart: In the century after the apostles, household churches taught a way of life before baptism, gave thanks over bread and cup on the Lord's day, and were held together by elders, bishops and the letters between them.
Findings: 0

## syriac-edessa-nisibis
Heart: The Syriac-speaking Christians of Edessa and Nisibis, on the frontier of two empires, sang their faith in hymns, kept vowed lives for God, and came through Persian persecution to the synod of 410.
Findings: 0

## manichaeism
Heart: Mani's followers, Elect and Hearers, held that Light and Darkness were mixed in the world and spread from Mesopotamia to Africa and China while kings and emperors hunted them.
Findings: 0

## marcion-marcionism
Heart: The Marcionite churches, known only through their enemies, held to a good Father above the world's maker and kept their own Scripture, clergy and martyrs for some three hundred years.
