## alexandria-catechetical
Heart: From about 180, Alexandria's Christians learned their faith in a school where teachers like Clement and Origen led pupils step by step and read Scripture for a deeper sense beneath the plain story. That reading was tested when Dionysius publicly examined Nepos's literal thousand-year teaching and won its leader over.

## roman-church-third-century
Heart: Rome's large, mixed church of the early 200s argued in public over Father and Son, over taking back grave sinners and over whom a woman of rank might marry, until Novatian's stricter party broke away in 251 and the church resolved to heal the fallen through repentance.

## post-apostolic-house-church
Heart: In the century after the apostles, Christians met household by household, taught newcomers a way of life before baptism, gave thanks over bread and cup on the Lord's day under elders, bishops and deacons, and were held together by letters and messengers between churches.

## manichaeism
Heart: The followers of Mani held that Light and Darkness were two powers now mixed in the world, with Elect living in strict purity and Hearers serving them; hunted by Persian kings and Roman emperors, they spread from Mesopotamia to Roman Africa and to China.

## marcion-marcionism
Heart: For about three hundred years the Marcionite churches, cast out by Rome, held that Jesus came from a good Father above the God who made the world, kept their own cut-down Scripture, presbyters and martyrs, and lasted even after imperial bans until bishops like Theodoret won their villages over.
