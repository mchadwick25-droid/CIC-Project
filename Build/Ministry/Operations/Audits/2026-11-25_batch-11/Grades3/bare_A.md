## alexandria-catechetical
Heart: From about 180, Alexandria's Christians learned their faith from teachers in a school, led step by step and taught to read Scripture for a deeper sense beneath the plain story, from Clement and Origen to Heraclas and Dionysius, who won over Nepos's followers by open debate.

## antiochene-church-third-century
Heart: In the later third century Antioch's Christians were one church under a bishop, bound by letters to churches across the empire; when their bishop Paul was charged with refusing to confess that the Son came down from heaven, the bishops of the East met again and again until they put him out.

## roman-church-third-century
Heart: In the first half of the 200s, Rome's Christians were a large, mixed church under a bishop the people chose, arguing in public over how to speak of Father and Son, whether grave sinners could be taken back, and whom a woman of rank might marry, until a stricter party broke away in 251.

## greek-apologists-second-century
Heart: In the second century, Greek-writing Christians put written defenses of their faith before emperors and pagans, answering charges of godlessness, cannibalism and incest by saying they worshipped the one Maker, prayed for the emperors, and asked to be judged by how they lived.

## post-apostolic-house-church
Heart: In the hundred years after the apostles, Christians met household by household, taught newcomers a way of life before baptism, gave thanks over bread and cup on the Lord's day under elders, bishops and deacons, and were held together by letters and messengers between churches.

## syriac-edessa-nisibis
Heart: From the flood of 201 to 410, Syriac-speaking Christians in the frontier cities of Edessa and Nisibis, between Rome and Persia, sang their teaching in hymns and counted among them men and women vowed to God for life.

## manichaeism
Heart: The followers of Mani held that Light and Darkness were two powers without beginning, now mixed in the world and one day to be parted; their Elect lived in strict purity served by the Hearers, and though hunted by Persian kings and Roman emperors they spread from Roman Africa to China.

## marcion-marcionism
Heart: For about three hundred years after Rome cast Marcion out, the Marcionite churches gathered with their own clergy and martyrs, holding a cut-down Gospel and ten letters of Paul, and believing that Jesus came from a good Father above the God who made the world and abolished that God's law.
