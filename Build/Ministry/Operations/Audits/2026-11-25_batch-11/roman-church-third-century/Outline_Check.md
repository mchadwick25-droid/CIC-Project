# Outline check: The Roman Church in the Third Century

## My account of the heart (written before reading Outline.md)

From the census entry, the slate, the card and the source sheet only.

- **Essence:** The church of Rome from about 200 to 251: a large, mixed congregation of rich and poor, slaves and freedmen, women of rank and members of Caesar's household, which argued in public over who could be forgiven, whose unions counted as marriage, and how to speak of Father and Son as one God. Its fullest witness for the early decades is Hippolytus, Callistus's enemy and the losing side.
- **Who they were:** "the church of Rome" / "the Christians of Rome", under bishops Zephyrinus, Callistus (a former slave once sent to the mines), Fabian and Cornelius; with its clergy, its poor and widows, its cemetery on the Appian Way.
- **When and where:** Rome, about 200 to 251 (the story ends at 251, with the confessors' return).
- **What they faced:** inner quarrel (Noetus's and Sabellius's teaching on Father and Son; Hippolytus's opposition and rival claim); deportation of Pontianus and Hippolytus (235); Decius's persecution, Fabian's death, more than a year without a bishop, the fallen; the split of 251 over the fallen and the one bishop.
- **What they believed:** one God, spoken of in Zephyrinus's and Callistus's words as Hippolytus reports them, against those who said Father and Son were the same (Sabellius cut off) and against "two gods"; that the church holds sinners, the tares with the wheat (Callistus, as reported); in 250-251, the fallen not abandoned but called to repent; one bishop in a catholic church (Cornelius, and the confessors' own words).
- **How they practiced:** penance in public before bishop, clergy and people (Natalius); a burial ground under a church officer; bishops chosen by the vote of the brethren and people (Fabian, Cornelius); care of widows, the bedridden, prisoners and the homeless, and burial of martyrs (the clergy's letter of 250); a large ordered clergy and more than 1,500 widows and distressed fed (Cornelius).
- **What held them together:** a church that kept its sinners and its poor inside it, and the one bishop chosen with the people. Both were tested and kept: Callistus's open door (as his enemy tells it) and Rome's answer of 251 that the fallen could be healed by repentance, with the confessors coming home to one bishop.
- **What came of them:** heaviest first: Rome's answer of 251 on the fallen and the strict church that broke away (Novatianism); the one-bishop principle; (after 251, card or a clause) Dionysius of Rome's letter against both Sabellius and three Gods, quoted by Athanasius; Rome's giving to other churches. Hippolytus's book, written against Callistus, is what keeps Callistus's and Zephyrinus's words.

Confidence: the Hippolytus decades are Documented as an enemy's report, with Callistus's meaning Contested; the 250-251 material is Documented in the Romans' own letters.

## Findings

### B1. BLOCKING: Cut first item 6 drops why Fabian was set on the seat (beat 5)

Eusebius tells the people's cry as the result of the dove: "But they relate that suddenly a dove … lighted on his head … Thereupon all the people, as if moved by one Divine Spirit … cried out that he was worthy" (E 36516-36526; "chosen … through a most wonderful manifestation", 36511-36512). Item 6 cuts the dove but keeps "a newcomer … whom no one had in mind" and the cry. Told that way, the act loses its only given cause, and the listener is misled into hearing an ordinary vote.

Correction. Replace Cut first item 6 with:
"6. The dove, Fabian as a newcomer from the country whom no one had in mind, and the people's cry (beat 5), keeping only that, Eusebius says, all the brethren met to choose the bishop by vote and set Fabian on the bishop's seat, about 40 words: **for the card**."
Add to Do not say: | The people cried out for Fabian, or "no one had him in mind", with the dove cut | Either the dove with the cry, both in Eusebius's voice ("they say"), or neither. Cut, it reads: "all the brethren met to choose a bishop by vote, and set Fabian on the bishop's seat" (P3) |

### B2. BLOCKING: Beat 6 names Decius's persecution without the reason the old source gives

Part 1 requires the reason for an act of conflict where the old sources give one. Eusebius gives it: "On account of his hatred of Philip, he commenced a persecution of the churches" (E 37413-37414), and of Philip, "It is reported that he, being a Christian, desired …" (E 36995-36996).

Correction. In beat 6, replace "Under the emperor Decius persecution came, and Christians were forced to sacrifice (D1, D3);" with:
"Under the emperor Decius persecution came, begun, Eusebius says, from hatred of the emperor before him, Philip, who was reported to be a Christian, and Christians were forced to sacrifice (D1, E 37413-37414, E 36995-36996; D3);"
Add to the beat's Confidence: "Decius's motive Documented as Eusebius's report (later)." Add to Source_Sheet row D1 the two spans quoted above. Length: B1 saves about 15 words more than item 6 did, and this adds about 17, so the plan stays near 625.
