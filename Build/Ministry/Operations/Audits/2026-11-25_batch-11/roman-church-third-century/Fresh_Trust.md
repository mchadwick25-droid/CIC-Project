## BLOCKING

**B1. The heart has lost the fifteen hundred fed.** The outline's heart: "It held slaves and women of rank, dozens of priests, and more than fifteen hundred widows and poor whom it fed, under a bishop the people chose." The brief: "It held slaves and women of rank, and the poor it fed, under a bishop the people chose." The priests are carried later, in Cornelius's count ("forty-six elders"). The fifteen hundred are carried nowhere. Outline beat 7 and Cut first 32 take the widows out of Cornelius's count *because* "they are told once, in the essence". The brief no longer tells them there, so the number is gone from the story altogether. That is essential material: it is the one count the record gives of the church's poor. The slate makes this story the one that tells "the congregation, its clergy and its poor". It is also the hard evidence behind "large". Source: Cornelius to Fabius, E 38565-38615 ("over fifteen hundred widows and persons in distress, all of whom the grace and kindness of the Master nourish"); E-Gk 6919-6923 ("χήρας σὺν θλιβομένοις ὑπὲρ τὰς χιλίας πεντακοσίας, οὓς πάντας ... διατρέφει"). Documented, own words. The essence's wording, "widows and poor", is a fair rendering of χήρας σὺν θλιβομένοις.

Exact change (+4 words):
- "It held slaves and women of rank, and the poor it fed, under a bishop the people chose." → "It held slaves and women of rank, and fed more than fifteen hundred widows and poor, under a bishop the people chose."

Cuts to keep the brief at 675 or under (−3 words, nothing in "Never cut" touched):
- "Rome's clergy wrote of them to the clergy of Carthage:" → "Rome's clergy wrote of them to Carthage's clergy:" (−2)
- "rolling at the feet of clergy and people alike." → "rolling at the feet of clergy and people." (−1; the feet, clergy and people stay)

The result is 673 + 4 − 3 = 674 words.

## Noted (MINOR, not blocking)

- N1. "People troubled in conscience came to his church, along with people other groups had refused, and some whom Hippolytus's side had thrown out." H 13882-13888: "many persons were gratified with his regulation, as being stricken in conscience, and at the same time having been rejected by numerous sects". In ANF these may be the same people described two ways, and the brief makes them two groups. Also, Hippolytus calls it Callistus's "school" (13888), not his church. Callistus was Rome's bishop, so "his church" is fair in the narrator's voice. Marked SUMMARY HOLDS; no change required.
