## New lines

- **C1a (H 13722-13751).** Every span is verbatim. Callistus "created a disturbance" in the synagogue on the Sabbath. The Jews' complaint was "alleging that he is a Christian". Fuscianus, "swayed by these Jews", sent him "to a mine in Sardinia". Beat 1's clause "Hippolytus says, was sent to the mines for disturbing a synagogue" says no more than this. It is credited to Hippolytus as a hostile contemporary, Documented as his account, and no quotation is used.
- **C6a (H 23728-23732; H 13838-13839, 13845-13846, 13853-13855).** Every span is verbatim. "Hippolytus spoke of one God but two persons" rests on "I shall not indeed speak of two Gods, but of one; of two Persons however". It is marked as Hippolytus's own words. "Callistus called that two Gods" rests on "publicly saying in the way of reproach to us, 'Ye are Ditheists'". It is marked as an enemy's report and narrated, with the barred words kept out.
- **B2 (E 35447-35449).** "Hippolytus, who presided over another church, has left writings": "a writer of that time" is supported.
- **B3 (CyL 1127-1129).** The Latin reads "quidam … ruerunt" and "ecclesia stat fortiter in fide", so "some gave way" is right.
- **B4 (CyL 1053, 1235).** The letter opens with no salutation naming its writers, and Cyprian calls it "epistulae clericae". "Rome's clergy wrote" is supported.
- **Cut 31 (E 38565-38615; CyL 3392).** 7 + 42 + 52 = 101, so "more than a hundred lesser clergy" holds. Lucianus's "praesente de clero et exorcista et lectore" counts exorcists and readers as clergy, and the existing gloss covers the doorkeepers.
- **B1 (H 13872, 13884-13886; E 32244-32246).** Callistus's "school in antagonism to the Church", the "numerous sects" and Natalius's rival bishopric all support "a large, mixed church" over "one".
