## BLOCKING findings

**5. "In the first half of the 200s, the Christians of Rome were one large, mixed church."** ASSUMPTION ADDED ("one"). The only witness for these decades says the opposite. Callistus "established a school *of theology* in antagonism to the Church" (H 13872). He took in people "rejected by numerous sects" and others "by us forcibly ejected from the Church" (H 13884-13886). His people only "attempt to call themselves a Catholic Church" (H 13952). Natalius's own story has a rival group making its own bishop (E 32244-32246). "One" settles what the record leaves open, which is a trap the kind kit names. **Change:** "In the first half of the 200s, the Christians of Rome were a large, mixed church." *Do not say* for the outline: "one church" before 251 → "a large, mixed church" (H 13872).

**8. "…comes from Hippolytus, a learned teacher who withstood two of Rome's bishops and lost."** NOT FOUND ("learned teacher"). The rest holds. "Withstood": "reproved and withstood him" (Zephyrinus, H 13627-13629), then Callistus (H 13816-13822). "Lost": Callistus's "school continues" (H 13957-13958). I searched E, H and LibCat for how the old sources describe Hippolytus. Eusebius says he "presided over another church, has left writings" (E 35447-35449). The Liberian list says "Yppolitus presbyter" (LibCat 66806). "One of the most learned men" is only the NPNF editor's note (E 35651-35652), and no old source calls him a teacher. **Change:** "…comes from Hippolytus, a writer of that time who withstood two of Rome's bishops and lost." *Do not say*: "learned teacher" → "a writer of that time" (E 35447-35449).

**22. "Many gave way from terror and the fear of men, and became known as the fallen."** SOFTENED OR CHANGED. The wording comes from the Roman clergy's letter. That letter says *some* fell and that the church stood firm: "ecclesia stat fortiter in fide, licet quidam terrore ipso conpulsi, siue quod essent insignes personae siue adprehensi timore hominum, ruerunt" (CyL 1127-1129). ANF has "notwithstanding that some have been driven to fall by very terror" (H 28854). A later clergy letter speaks of what was "committed by many… diffused among so many" (H 31723-31726). That passage looks out over "almost the whole world devastated", not Rome alone. A third reader may weigh it, but it does not carry "many" for Rome on Ep. 8's words. **Change:** "Some gave way from terror or the fear of men, and became known as the fallen."

**23. "Rome's elders and deacons wrote of them to the clergy of Carthage:"** ASSUMPTION ADDED ("elders and deacons"). Ep. 8 names no writers in the held Latin (CyL 1053: the bare heading "Epistula VIII"). Its close has "the presbyters" sending greetings as a third party: "salutant uos fratres qui sunt in uinculis et presbyteri et tota ecclesia" (CyL 1177-1178). Cyprian himself wrote back that it was a letter "in quibus nec qui scripserint nec ad quos scriptum sit significanter expressum est" ("in which neither who wrote it nor to whom was clearly set out"), and asked the Roman presbyters and deacons whether it was theirs (CyL 1196, 1229-1240). "Elders and deacons" rests only on ANF's editorial title (H 28783-28784). **Change:** "Rome's clergy wrote of them to the clergy of Carthage:" Cyprian calls it "epistulae clericae", a clergy letter (CyL 1235). Q2 itself is HELD.
