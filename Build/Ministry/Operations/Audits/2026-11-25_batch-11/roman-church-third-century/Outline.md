# Outline: The Roman Church in the Third Century

Census id `roman-church-third-century`. Kind: tradition (`kinds/tradition.md`). Source codes (Z1, C5, N8 ...) are rows in `Source_Sheet.md`, which gives each file and line. Job (slate, 2026-11-25): a large, mixed congregation at Rome in public argument with itself over forgiveness, marriage, and how to speak of Father and Son, from Zephyrinus and the former slave Callistus to the split of 251; it says once, early, that its fullest witness is Hippolytus, the losing side. It tells the congregation, its clergy and its poor, and Callistus's side as far as the record gives it, not Hippolytus's charges again.

**Does the record carry Rome past 251?** Yes. The Library holds Stephen's quarrel with Cyprian (Cyprian's letters; Dionysius of Alexandria in Eusebius VII.5), Xystus's death in 258 (Cyprian Ep. 81, on the card), and Dionysius of Rome in his own words: his letter against both the Sabellians and those who preach three Gods, quoted by Athanasius in Greek and English (R1, R2), and his ransom of captives from Caesarea in Cappadocia (Basil, R4). **Recommended:** the story stops at 251, as the slate set it. The forgiveness question it opens on (a penitent at a bishop's feet under Zephyrinus) closes there, on the people taking the confessors back, and the length is already near the top of the range. Dionysius goes to the card and What came of it. The alternative, for Mark on the hearts page: a closing beat on Dionysius (Ending 2), which carries the Father-Son question to Rome's own answer in the 260s and lets Aurelian's ruling in as a clause.

## The heart

- **The essence:** In the first half of the 200s the Christians of Rome were one large, mixed church. It held slaves and women of rank, dozens of priests, and more than fifteen hundred widows and poor whom it fed, under a bishop the people chose. They argued in public over whether the church could take back people who had sinned gravely, whom a Christian woman of rank might marry, and how to speak of Father and Son, until a stricter party broke away in 251. (S4-S6, N1, N3, P3, C7-C12, Z8, N4.) Said once, early, inside a sentence about him: for the first decades almost all of it comes from Hippolytus, a learned teacher who opposed two bishops and lost (main-witness note; Z9, C3-C4).
- **Who they were:** "the church" at Rome; Callistus's side "call themselves a Catholic Church" (C14); Hippolytus calls them a "school", "Callistians" (C14). Spoken name: The Roman Church in the Third Century (census `name`).
- **When and where:** Rome, from Zephyrinus, bishop from about 200 (S1, S2), to the split of 251 (N4, N11).
- **What they faced:** a teaching from Smyrna that Father and Son were the same (Z6); their own quarrel over forgiving grave sin and over marriage (C7-C13); then Decius's persecution, a church without a bishop, and many who gave way (D1-D7); then a second bishop (N4).
- **What they believed:** one God, and Father and Son not two Gods, in Zephyrinus's and Callistus's words (Z8, C5); that the church holds sinners as a field holds tares with the wheat, in Callistus's reading (C10); against that, Hippolytus's charge that this was laxity and heresy (C6, C14). In 250 the clergy: do not abandon the fallen, urge them to repent (D3). The returned confessors: "one God ... one Christ ... one Holy Spirit ... one bishop" (N9).
- **How they practiced:** public penance before clergy and people (Z4); a church burial ground with an officer over it (S7, C2); the whole assembly choosing its bishop (P3, N1); deacons over the city's regions (P4, the list's); feeding over fifteen hundred widows and people in distress (N3); serving widows, the bedridden and prisoners, and burying martyrs (D4); taking back the penitent with the people pleading or rejoicing (N5, N8).
- **What held them together:** one bishop with clergy and people round him, who chose him and before whom sinners asked to be received back (Z4, P3, N1, N6, N8); and the open question of how far the church could forgive, argued from Zephyrinus to 251 (Z4, C7-C10, D3, N4, N11).
- **What came of them:** Rome's answer of 251 on the fallen, and the strict church that broke from it (R0); Dionysius of Rome's letter against both Sabellius and three Gods, quoted by Athanasius (R1, R2); Rome's giving to other churches, which Eusebius says lasted to his own day (R3, R4).

Zephyrinus's death and Callistus's accession: say "when Zephyrinus died" or "around 217" (S2, S3, X8). Exile of Rome's bishop and Hippolytus: 235 (P1). Fabian died under Decius, 250 (D1). - **Place:** Rome; the church's burial ground on the Appian Way (S7; the road is an editor's note, so say "the church's cemetery"); Sardinia's mines (C1, P1); Smyrna, where Noetus came from (Z6, H 13280).
- **Tradition:** the one church of the city with its bishop, priests, deacons and lesser clergy, its rich and its poor (S4, N3).

## Openings

1. **Natalius at Zephyrinus's feet (recommended).** A confessor, one who had stood trial and not given way, let the followers of a teacher Victor had cut off make him their bishop, for a monthly salary. A writer of that day says he was warned in visions and then scourged all night by holy angels (the wonder stays in his voice). In the morning he put on sackcloth and ashes and threw himself down before Bishop Zephyrinus, rolling at the feet of clergy and people alike, showing his welts; he was scarcely taken back. Then the essence. (Z1-Z4.) It opens the story's question, how far the church could forgive, with the whole congregation in the room.
2. **Zephyrinus before the people.** The bishop stood up in public and said he knew one God, Jesus Christ, and no other who was born and suffered; and again, that the Father did not die, but the Son (Z8). Weaker: it opens only one of the three quarrels, and in Hippolytus's report.

## Story beats

1. **Zephyrinus, Callistus and the quarrel over Father and Son (around 200 to 217).** A teaching from Smyrna, that Father and Son were one and the same, reached Rome (Z6). Zephyrinus told the people that he knew one God, Jesus Christ, and none other who was born and suffered, and that the Father did not die, but the Son (Z8; Hippolytus's report). His right hand was Callistus, who had been a slave and had once been sent to the mines, and whom Zephyrinus had set over the church's cemetery (C1, C2, S6; one clause). Hippolytus and his side withstood the bishop; Callistus, he says, accused them of worshipping two gods (Z9, narrated). Explain it: the teaching, in plain words (Z6); Zephyrinus's words as his own claim. Hippolytus's charge that Zephyrinus was corrupt (Z7) stays in his voice, or out. Confidence: Documented as Hippolytus's account.
2. **Callistus becomes bishop and cuts off Sabellius.** When Zephyrinus died, Callistus was made bishop and put Sabellius out of the church's fellowship (C3). Hippolytus says he did it from fear of him, to clear himself (C4; an enemy's motive, credited). Then Callistus's own formula: Q1. Hippolytus answers that Callistus still made the Father suffer with the Son (C6; his charge, in its content). Explain it: why he cut off Sabellius: Hippolytus's reason only; Sabellius's teaching, that Father and Son were the same (Z6, R1). Confidence: Documented as reported; what Callistus meant, Contested (X3).
3. **Callistus's church: who came, and the rule on sin.** Callistus let people off for sins of the body, saying he forgave them all (C7). People troubled in conscience came, and people other groups had refused, and some Hippolytus's own side had thrown out (C8). He held that a bishop should not be removed even for the gravest sin, and clergy married twice or three times kept their places (C9). His reasons, as reported: "Who are you to judge another's servant?", and the tares left to grow with the wheat, so that sinners stay in the church (C10). His people called themselves the Catholic Church; Hippolytus called them a school, Callistians (C14). Explain it: the rule, the "school", the Catholic Church (the church of the whole), each in the sheet's words. Confidence: Documented as Hippolytus's charge; the reasons Documented as reported.
4. **The marriage rule.** Callistus let a woman of rank, who would lose that rank by a lawful marriage, take a companion, slave or free, and count him her husband (C12). Hippolytus charged that such women then used drugs to avoid or end a pregnancy, not wanting a child by a slave, and called it teaching adultery and murder (C13; told in its content, credited). Explain it: the rule and the reason the record gives, "their own dignity" (C12). Confidence: Documented as the charge.
5. **After Callistus: exile, and a bishop chosen by the people.** In 235 Rome's bishop and Hippolytus were deported to Sardinia (P1; a clause, by role or with Pontianus). When the brethren met to choose a bishop, Eusebius says, a dove came down on Fabian, a newcomer from the country whom no one had in mind, and the whole people cried out that he was worthy (P3; "they say", in Eusebius's voice). A list of the bishops says Fabian divided the city's regions among the deacons and built in the cemeteries (P4; credited to the list). Explain it: the election as the record gives it, by vote of all the brethren. Confidence: the election Documented as Eusebius's report; the dove a wonder; the regions Documented as the list says.
6. **250: Decius, and a church without a bishop.** Under the emperor Decius persecution came, and Christians were forced to sacrifice (D1, D3); Fabian died a martyr (D1, D2), and Rome went without a bishop (D7). Two elders and two deacons sat in prison as confessors (D6). The clergy wrote that they had called back people on their way up to sacrifice, and of those who gave way: Q2 (D3). Every widow, bedridden person and prisoner must have someone to serve them, and the martyrs must be buried (D4). Explain it: the fallen; why they sacrificed: "terror", "fear of men" (D3); the dying penitent given communion (D4). Confidence: Documented, the clergy's own words.
7. **251: Cornelius, the count, and the split.** Cornelius, who had risen through every office, was made bishop by almost all the clergy and the vote of the people present (N1; Cyprian, his ally, credited). In a letter that year he counted his church: forty-six elders, seven deacons, seven subdeacons, forty-two attendants, fifty-two exorcists, readers and doorkeepers together, and more than fifteen hundred widows and people in distress, all fed (N3; Cornelius's words). A stricter party, holding that the fallen had no more hope, set up Novatian as a second bishop, with confessors on his side (N4). Sixty bishops met at Rome, declared Novatian's party outsiders, and resolved to heal the fallen through repentance (N11, narrated). One of the bishops who had made Novatian bishop came back, and was received as a layman, the whole people pleading for him (N5). Join these with "that year", never "then" or "after", since the old sources do not fix their order against the Ending. Explain it: the second bishop (N4, N6); the decision on the fallen (N11). Confidence: Documented.

## What came of it

1. **Rome's answer on the fallen, and the split.** The sixty bishops' decision to heal the fallen through repentance, and Novatian's church that refused it and lived on (R0, N11; census `novatianism`). Told in beat 7; the later life of Novatian's church is its own story.
2. **Dionysius of Rome's letter.** In the 260s, after brethren from Alexandria accused their own bishop before him, Dionysius of Rome wrote against both Sabellius and those who preach three Gods; Athanasius quoted it a century later against the Arians (R1, R2). Defended: the faith in one God against both errors. For the card, or for Ending 2.
3. **Rome's giving.** Eusebius says the Roman custom of sending gifts to churches everywhere lasted "down to the persecution in our own days" (R3); Basil says Dionysius of Rome sent men to ransom captives of Caesarea (R4). For the card.
4. Who keeps this today: no held source says. Leave it out.

## Connection to carry into the story

What passed from this church to Novatianism was a party of its own: elders and confessors of Rome who held that the fallen had no more hope (N4), and who set up a second bishop against the one the clergy and people had chosen (N1, N6). The listener needs to understand that the split was over forgiveness, the same question the church had argued since Zephyrinus took back a penitent confessor (Z4), and that Rome's answer was to heal the fallen through repentance (N11) and to take back those who returned (N5, N8). The link must come through what people did: the clergy writing in 250, the party choosing Novatian, the bishops deciding, the people pleading and embracing. The story must not say that Callistus's rule caused the split: no old source joins them. From the House Church: the same line of bishops, Victor to Zephyrinus (S1, Z1), carried in the opening's clause.

## Ending

1. **Recommended: the confessors come home (251).** The confessors who had sided with Novatian came to the elders and five bishops and asked that what they had done be forgotten (N7). The people were told, so they could see in the church the men they had mourned as wanderers (N7). Then Q3 (N8). The confessors said they knew one God, one Christ, one Holy Spirit, and that there should be one bishop in the catholic church (N9); their leader took his place again, and the rest were received with the people's loud approval (N10). It ends on the congregation, and sees the opening again: a penitent before clergy and people. (CyL 6785-6869; H 32989-33037.)
2. **Alternative, if Mark carries the story to 268: Dionysius of Rome.** In the 260s, when believers from Alexandria accused their own bishop at Rome, Dionysius of Rome wrote against both errors (R2), and Athanasius kept his words: "They know well that divine Scripture preaches a Trinity, but that neither the Old Testament nor the New preaches three Gods." (Rendered from the Greek, Ath-Gk 78317-78320, Migne 1857, OCR flaws confirmed by Ath's English 28570-28571.) It closes the Father-Son question on Rome's own answer, and admits Antioch's letter to Dionysius and Aurelian's ruling as a clause (R6). It would take Q2's or Q1's place, add about 90 words, and end on a later writer's record of a letter, not on the congregation.
