## The BLOCKING finding

**S22, paragraph 7.** "Many gave way from terror and the fear of men, and became known as the fallen."
- SOFTENED OR CHANGED. The Roman clergy say *some* fell, and they say it inside a sentence about the church standing firm. CyL 1127-1129 (Hartel Ep. 8.2): "ecclesia stat fortiter in fide, licet quidam terrore ipso conpulsi, siue quod essent insignes personae siue adprehensi timore hominum, ruerunt". ANF's English (H, Ep. 2.2) agrees: "The Church stands in faith, notwithstanding that some have been driven to fall by very terror". "Quidam" means "some", not "many". The outline's beat 6 does not say "many" either. No held Roman line gives the number.
- **Exact change:** "Many gave way from terror and the fear of men, and became known as the fallen." → "Some gave way from terror and the fear of men, and became known as the fallen."
- **Do not say line to add to the outline:** | "Many gave way" (at Rome, 250) | "Some gave way" (D3, "quidam ... ruerunt"; the same sentence says "ecclesia stat fortiter in fide") |

## SUMMARY HOLDS, with evidence

- **S1** "Natalius was a confessor, one who had stood trial for the faith and not given way." E 32216-32217: "a certain confessor, Natalius"; E 32252-32254: "a witness of his own sufferings". The gloss is the outline's own (Words to explain).
- **S5** "In the first half of the 200s, the Christians of Rome were one large, mixed church." The S1/S3 dates (E 32209-32211, E 35480-35485); Cornelius's count and "rich ... together with the very many, even innumerable people" (E 38565-38621).
- **S6** "It held slaves and women of rank, and the poor it fed, under a bishop the people chose." Callistus a slave of "a man of the faith" (H 13672-13674); women who would not "overturn their own dignity" (H 13933-13935); "over fifteen hundred widows and persons in distress, all of whom the grace and kindness of the Master nourish" (E 38612-38615); "all the brethren had assembled to select by vote" (E 36513-36515) and "the suffrage of the people who were then present" (CyL 7673-7679). Note, not blocking: the popular choice is shown only for Fabian and Cornelius. Nothing says how Zephyrinus or Callistus was chosen. The sentence describes the church, not either man, so it holds.
- **S7** "They argued in public over whether the church could take back grave sinners, whom a Christian woman of rank might marry, and how to speak of Father and Son, until a stricter party broke away in 251." Zephyrinus "publicly to avow" (H 13619-13620); "if even a sin unto death" (H 13890); the marriage rule (H 13923-13938); Novatus and "those who ... call themselves Cathari" (E 38265-38270). "Marry" compresses "might consider such a companion as a husband". Paragraph 5 then tells it exactly.
- **S8** "Almost all that is told of its first decades comes from Hippolytus, a learned teacher who withstood two of Rome's bishops and lost." "reproved and withstood him" (H 13627-13632); Eusebius counts him among "many learned men in the Church at that time" (E 35434-35448); "after the death of Zephyrinus ... he excommunicated Sabellius" and "whose school continues" (H 13816-13819, 13957-13958) show that Callistus held the see.
- **S9** "Zephyrinus's right hand was Callistus, who had been a slave and had once been sent to the mines." "he had Callistus as his adviser" (H 13285-13296); "a fellow-worker in the management of his clergy" (H 13802-13804); "to be sent to a mine in Sardinia" (H 13750-13751).
- **S11** "Hippolytus says Callistus did it because he feared Hippolytus, and to clear himself of the charge that he shared Sabellius's view." H 13819-13822: "He acted thus from apprehension of me, and imagining that he could in this manner obliterate the charge against him among the churches, as if he did not entertain strange opinions." Hippolytus places those opinions with Sabellius's: Callistus "alleges that he entertains similar opinions to Cleomenes" (H 13612-13616), whose system Sabellius held, and Sabellius accused him (H 13837-13840).
- **S14** "People troubled in conscience came to his church, along with people other groups had refused, and some whom Hippolytus's side had thrown out." H 13882-13888: "stricken in conscience ... rejected by numerous sects; while also some of them ... had been by us forcibly ejected from the Church". Hippolytus says "school". Leaving that word out follows the ruling (Cut first 17).
- **S17** "Hippolytus charged that such women then took drugs to avoid or end a pregnancy, not wanting a child by a slave, and he called the rule a teaching of adultery and murder." H 13938-13951: "drugs for producing sterility ... to expel what was being conceived ... not wishing to have a child either by a slave or by any paltry fellow ... inculcating adultery and murder". It leaves out "or by any paltry fellow, for the sake of their family and excessive wealth". The charge keeps its force.
- **S18** "Some years after Callistus, as a historian of the next century tells it, all the brethren met to choose a bishop by vote, and set Fabian on the bishop's seat." E 36513-36526: "when all the brethren had assembled to select by vote ... they took him and placed him upon the episcopal seat". Note, not blocking: Eusebius says Fabian "was in the mind of none" until the dove and the people's cry. This is the wording the outline's Do not say gives for the cut, and it does not say that the vote chose him.
- **S19** "In 250 the emperor Decius forced Christians to sacrifice." "those who were ascending to do that to which they were constrained" (CyL 1126-1127; H Ep. 2.2); "unlawful sacrifices" and "edicts or laws put forth against the Gospel" (H 31650-31658); "On account of his hatred of Philip, he commenced a persecution" (E 37413-37414); 250 from the Liberian list's consulship (LibCat 66820, Decio II et Grato).
- **S27** "Novatian, an elder of Rome, held that the fallen had no more hope, and with a stricter party and confessors at his side he was made a second bishop, set up against the first." "Novatus, a presbyter of the church at Rome ... as if there was no longer for them a hope of salvation ... leader of the heresy of those who ... call themselves Cathari" (E 38265-38270); "those confessors who had adhered to him" (E 38383-38387); the confessors "suffered hands to be imposed on him as if upon a bishop" (H, Ep. 45, before 32989).

## The heart

It is the right heart. The sources show one city church: rich and poor, many clergy, and the fed widows (E 38565-38621). It argued over forgiveness from Natalius (E 32267-32274) through Callistus (H 13873-13909) to the fallen of 250 and 251 (CyL 1129-1133; E 38265-38299), and over Father and Son (H 13619-13625, 13855-13857).
