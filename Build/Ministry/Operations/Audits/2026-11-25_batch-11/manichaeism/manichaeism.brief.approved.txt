Mani was born in Mesopotamia in 216 and raised in a sect of baptizers. He broke with it after what he described as revelations from his heavenly twin. He taught that reality is the battleground of two principles, Light and Darkness, which have both always existed. Particles of light are trapped in the material world, and a strict discipline of self-denial sets them free. Almost alone among the founders of his era, he wrote his own scriptures and had them translated on purpose. He meant his faith to absorb and replace Christianity, Zoroastrianism and Buddhism at once. For a time, it worked. The faith spread from Persia across the Roman Empire, and east along the Silk Road into Central Asia and China. His followers fell into two groups: the Elect, who kept the full discipline, and the Hearers, who supported them and lived ordinary lives. Augustine of Hippo was a Hearer for roughly nine years before his conversion. He spent much of his later career arguing against the teachers he had once sat under. Mani himself died in custody under the Persian king Bahram I in the 270s.
