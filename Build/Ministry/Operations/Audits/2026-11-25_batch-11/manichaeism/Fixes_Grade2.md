## What the sources hold

### Fix 1: why the temples were closed in 843

French quotes below are given with accents restored; the OCR at the cited lines is damaged in spelling only (for example "ddfaits", "religieux mani-/cheens").

The sheet's 843 row (`Source_Sheet.md` 106) says "The text gives the edict, not its reason". That is true of the *Fo tsou t'ong ki* (B26, CHI 14089-14095), the 13th-century chronicle. But the same held file, `cic/texts/manichaean-texts_traite-manicheen-retrouve-en-chine-pt2-in-journal-asiatique-11-1-fra_chavannes-pelliot1913.txt`, also prints two contemporary court texts that the sheet did not use. They give the reason in the court's own words.

- **The emperor's letter to the Uyghur khagan (Texte XXX).** CHI 13695-13702: "Pour ce qui est de la religion manichéenne, avant la période t'ien-pao elle était interdite dans le royaume du Milieu. Depuis plusieurs règnes, parce que les Ouigours avaient pour elle respect et foi, on lui a, alors seulement, permis de se développer librement." CHI 13732-13734: since news came of "la ruine des Ouigours", its believers are discouraged and its foreign religious "semblent n'avoir autant dire pas d'appui". CHI 13776-13778: the temples of the Kiang and Houai are closed "provisoirement" until the Uyghur land is calm. Witness kind: contemporary, the Chinese court's own words (the answer written in the emperor's name; the editors identify it as kept in Li Te-yu's collected works, CHI 13686-13687, 13707). Confidence: Documented, as the letter gives it. The editors date it to late 842 (CHI 13684-13686). That date is their reckoning, so the brief gives no year for the letter.
- **The order of the day of 843 (Texte XXXIII).** CHI 13950-13958: the order was issued "quand il reçut la nouvelle des succès de Lieou Mien", in the second month of 843 (the date is from the *Kieou t'ang chou*, CHI 14000-14001: "c'est lui seul qui nous donne la date"). Its own words, CHI 13960, 13979: "Maintenant que les Ouigours ont été défaits, on doit les extirper complètement." In the same order, CHI 13992-13996: the property "des Ouigours et des temples manichéens" is to be inventoried, and a report made on the temples' religious (CHI 14031-14034). Witness kind: contemporary, the order itself (Chinese, in French translation, 1913; the Chinese characters are lost in the OCR). Confidence: Documented, as the order gives it.
- **Who the Uyghurs were.** `Source_Sheet.md` 107 ("a Turkish people"; KHU title, CHI passim). B23 (CHI 8671-8673, 8750-8751) and B25 (CHI 12381-12387: "Les Ouigours ont confiance en eux et les honorent") show their favor to the faith.
- **Stays out.** The editors' own account of the motive ("la persécution commençait", CHI 13787-13788; the Taoist influence and the war on the Uyghur religion, CHI 14141-14147). It is modern. The 842 closing of the Kiang and Houai temples is held (CHI 13776-13778), but the brief keeps to 843.

### Fix 2: how the Elect's stomachs make gods, and why that frees anyone

Both halves are Augustine's, in the *Confessions*, mocking (CONF; `cic/texts/npnf101_augustine-confessions-letters.xml`, Pilkington 1886; `cic/texts/augustine_confessiones-lat_knoll-csel33.txt`, Knoll 1896). Witness kind: contemporary, an enemy who had been a Hearer, his own words. Confidence: Documented, as Augustine gives it.

- **How:** the food holds bits of God, freed when an Elect eats it. CONF III.10, NPNF 6163-6174: "had some 'saint' eaten and mingled with his entrails, he should breathe out of it angels; yea ... particles of God, which particles of the most high and true God should have remained bound in that fig unless they had been set free by the teeth and belly of some 'elect saint'!" Latin 5452-5455, 5482-5484: "miscēret uisceribus et anhelaret de illa angelos, inmo uero particulas dei ... quae particulae summi et ueri dei ligatae fuissent in illo pomo, nisi electi sancti dente ac uentre soluerentur." Augustine's example is a fig; the general point, food brought to the Elect, is his own in IV.1.
- **Why it frees the Hearers:** CONF IV.1, NPNF 6343-6346: "seeking to be purged from these our corruptions by carrying food to those who were called 'elect' and 'holy,' out of which, in the laboratory of their stomachs, they should make for us angels and gods, by whom we might be delivered." Latin 5730-5731: "in officina aqualiculi sui fabricarent angelos et deos, per quos liberaremur." This is already the brief's source (`Source_Sheet.md` 94; `Outline.md` 69).

Nothing new is claimed. The new wording adds III.10's freeing of God's particles, which the sheet's alms row already cites as CONF III.10 (NPNF 6163-6172). It stays in Augustine's voice ("he says").
