## Fix 1: the 843 reason

- **Letter (Texte XXX), CHI 13695-13702.** The words are there: "parce que les Ouigours avaient pour elle respect et foi, on lui a, alors seulement, permis de se développer librement". "China let the faith grow because the Uyghurs ... revered it" says no more than this. The emperor's letter, written by Li Te-yu, CHI 13684-13687. Contemporary, the court's own words. Documented as the letter gives it. "A later emperor's letter says" keeps it the letter's claim, and gives no year. Holds.
- **Order of the day (Texte XXXIII), CHI 13950-13960, 13979, 13992-13996, 14000-14001.** Issued in the second month of 843 "quand il reçut la nouvelle des succès de Lieou Mien". It opens "Maintenant que les Ouigours ont été défaits". In the same order, the property "des Ouigours et des temples manichéens" is inventoried, and a report is ordered on the temples' religious. "In 843, with the Uyghurs defeated" is the order's own wording. Holds.
- **Whose causal link.** The court texts make the link themselves. The letter ties the believers' loss of support to "la ruine des Ouigours" (CHI 13732-13734). It closes the Kiang and Houai temples until the Uyghur land is calm (CHI 13776-13778). The 843 order deals with the Uyghurs and the Manichaean temples in a single measure. The brief puts the two court statements side by side and adds no motive. The editors' explicit causes stay out, as the fix says: "la persécution commençait" (13787-13788), "Dès lors, il n'y avait plus de ménagements à garder" (13801-13802), and "parce que l'empereur poursuivait en lui la religion des Ouigours", with the Taoists (14141-14147). The order that closed the temples empire-wide still rests on the *Fo tsou t'ong ki* (14089-14095), as it did before. The editors say the 843 summaries come from the order of the day (13950-13952). So "an edict" is fair. Holds.
- **MINOR (sheet only).** The editors' note on the letter, CHI 13713-13716 and 13750-13753, finds "une double inexactitude" in its history. The tolerance came in 732, not in t'ien-pao, and the Uyghurs were not yet Manichaean in 732 or 743-755. The brief uses no part of the letter that this note touches ("let the faith grow" refers to "plusieurs règnes", not the time before t'ien-pao). But the sheet's new B26a row quotes "forbidden before t'ien-pao" with no flag. See the replacement below.
- "Revered" for "respect et foi": fair. "The faith's temples" fixes "their temples" so they no longer read as the Uyghurs'. The gloss "a Turkish people" stands on sheet row 107. Both new sentences are clear on one hearing (18 and 12 words).
