## 1. The test, for a tradition

- **Who these people were:** filled. The followers of Mani, with a few Elect who lived in strict purity and many Hearers who fed and served them.
- **What happened to them:** filled. They spread from Mesopotamia to Roman Africa and China. Persian kings killed Mani and his followers, Roman law fined and dispossessed them in 372, Faustus was exiled, and China banned them in 732 and closed their temples in 843.
- **What their faith was about:** filled. Light and Darkness were mixed in the world and would be parted again, they harmed nothing living, the Light was in their food, and Mani was God's last messenger.
- **Why the connection matters:** filled, but thin. The listener can say that Augustine had been one of them, debated them and answered their bishop's book. A listener with no church background does not hear plainly that Augustine left them for the church and became the voice that argued against them. Nor do they hear why their view of Christ ("never born in real flesh") set them against the church. MINOR: let the Faustus paragraph say in a clause what Augustine had become, if the outline holds it.

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing (all MINOR; none loses the thread)

1. "Augustine answered that he had been only a Hearer, never one of the Elect, and that at their prayer he had seen nothing shameful, only that they prayed facing the sun." Lost: what "Hearer" and "Elect" mean at this point, and why shame is in question.
2. "He wrote a book for King Shapur of Persia, and Biruni, a Muslim scholar of about 1000, quotes how it begins." Lost: "of about 1000" sounds like a number of people or an age, not a year. Say "who wrote about the year 1000".
3. "Augustine says the Hearers carried food to the Elect, and mocks it: their stomachs, he says, were meant to make 'angels and gods' out of it, who would then set the Hearers free." Lost: whose stomachs, what "it" is, and who sets the Hearers free. This is the reason for the central practice, and it does not land. Make the Elect the subject: the Elect, by eating, were meant to free the Light in it.
4. "Biruni says that King Bahram had Mani sought, and gives the king's reason: ..." Lost: who Bahram is. Shapur was the only king so far. "Had Mani sought" is hard for a second-language ear. Say "King Bahram, a later king of Persia, had Mani hunted down".
5. "A writer in Egypt about 300 heard instead that Mani died under Shapur, after offending him in some way." Lost: "about 300" again sounds like a count of people, and two kings now compete for one death. Say "about the year 300".
6. "Augustine says that when he asked why, they told him the day to keep was the death of one who had truly suffered, since Christ, they held, was never born in real flesh and only seemed to suffer." (38 words.) Lost: what "why" asks. A listener with no church background does not know that Christians keep Easter for Christ's death, so the contrast is gone. Name what the feast was kept instead of, if the outline holds it, and split after "suffered".
7. "Faustus of Milevis, a bishop of theirs, wrote a book of answers, and Augustine, a Hearer for nine years, kept his words, setting out each part of the book before he answered it." Lost: answers to whom, and what "kept his words" means. Say "quoted it part by part in his reply". (See also 4.)
8. "Augustine calls this the lightest of penalties, given because those same Christians pleaded for him." Lost: why the people who brought him to court then pleaded for him. It sounds like a contradiction. Keep it only if the outline gives a reason.
9. "A Chinese chronicle written four centuries later says that some seventy of their women religious at the capital all died." Lost: "women religious" sounds like a grammar slip to a second-language ear, and "all died" leaves the listener asking how. Say "women who had given their lives to the faith" or similar, and give how they died if the source does.

## 4. Bare statements (BLOCKING)

1. "He said he was the Paraclete, the Helper Jesus had promised, and he wrote his own scriptures." The scriptures are named but not explained: the listener does not learn what they held or why it mattered that Mani wrote them himself. Change: say in a clause what the scriptures were and why they mattered, from the outline, or cut "and he wrote his own scriptures".
2. "Faustus of Milevis, a bishop of theirs, wrote a book of answers, ..." The book is named without saying what it answered or why it mattered. The Faustus quote then shows its tone, but not its subject. Change: say what the book answered (for example, the Christians' questions about the gospel), as the outline gives it, or cut the book and keep Faustus by role.

The exile of Faustus is not bare: the listener connects "found to be a Manichaean" to the law of 372. The edict of 843 is told as an act with no reason; that is allowed if the sources give none.
