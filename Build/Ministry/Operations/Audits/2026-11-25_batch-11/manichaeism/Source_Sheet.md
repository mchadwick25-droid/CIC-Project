# Source sheet: Manichaeism

Census id `manichaeism` (census name Manichaeism; spoken name "the followers of Mani", `informalName` "The Followers of Mani"). Kind: tradition. Every line number below is the line number in the held file itself (not the host OCR numbering some headers give). In the verbatim column, OCR misreadings of letters are put right (for example "Manichseans", "Mam" for Mani, "Bahrani" for Bahram, lost French accents, "gemessen" for *geniessen*); no word is otherwise changed, and "..." marks a cut. Witness kinds: **own** (the community's own words), **contemporary**, **later** (with a rough gap), **late legend**. Confidence uses the project's five levels.

## E. Impact, heaviest first (what later witnesses carried on and used)

1. **Their own books and prayers outlived them, east and west.** Mani's Shabuhragan quoted by Biruni about 1000 (B4, A1); the Hearers' confession copied in Turkish at Dunhuang (B30, C9); the Chinese compendium (C13). Weight: these are the texts later witnesses used to tell what Mani taught.
2. **In the East the faith lasted for centuries:** Uyghur royal patronage (B23-B25), temples in Chinese cities (B24), the Samarkand community protected by a Turkish ruler (B27), and Biruni's report that most eastern Turks and many in China and Tibet still held it about 1000 (B29).
3. **Augustine carried their words into his books.** He was a Hearer nine years (B14), debated Fortunatus (B17-B19), and set out Faustus's book "as if stated by himself" before answering (CFa English 4312: "I will give the opinions of Faustus as if stated by himself, and mine as if in reply to him"). So the fullest Latin statement of their case survives inside his reply (link: `augustine-of-hippo`; `latin-pastoral-congregational-christianity` argued against).
4. **The Syriac church answered them in their own language:** Ephrem's prose against Mani (sibling `syriac-edessa-nisibis`, its E5; census edge "argued against").
5. **The name became a charge:** later writers called other dissenters Manichaeans (Bernard Gui's "Manichaeans of modern times" for the Cathars, on `cathars-albigensians`; Julian of Eclanum calling Augustine's party Manichaeans, on `pelagianism`). Defended: nothing; this is what was charged. Card only.
6. **Leo of Rome's exposure of the Manichaeans at Rome in 443** (Prosper, on `leo-of-rome`): the hunt carried on in the West. Card only; told on Leo's page.

Who keeps it today: no held source names a living community; the census `livingEvidence` group is "ended". Leave out.

## G. Where sources disagree or come late

- **Shapur and Mani:** the Fihrist (B7) has Shapur honor him and protect his disciples; Biruni's informant (B8) has Shapur banish him under Zoroaster's law. Credit each.
- **Mani's death:** flayed and stuffed at the gate (BIR 29125-29127; ARC); died in chains in prison (BIR 29132-29138, a pupil's book; FIH 5126-5127 "others"); hung in two halves at two gates (FIH 5112-5117). Alexander puts it under Sapor, not Bahram (ALX 24496-24498).
- **Revelation at 12 or 13:** the Fihrist "when he had completed his twelfth year" (FIH 4391); Biruni "in his 13th year" (BIR 29098-29099). The same age; say "at twelve".
- **At-Taum:** the Fihrist glosses the name "companion" (FIH 4395-4396); Flügel's commentary reads it "twin" (FIH 7083), a modern reading of the word. Say "the Companion"; "twin" (the card's "heavenly twin") rests on the editor.
- **Late witnesses:** the Fihrist and Biruni write about 700 years after Mani, drawing on Manichaean books and tradition. The Fo tsou t'ong ki is 13th century for 768 and 843. The Acts of Archelaus is a polemical tale (its story of Mani bought as a slave boy by a widow is late legend: ARC, NPI 833-836) and stays out.
- **The Augustine debate clause** (B19): the NPNF English and the Latin differ on one clause. The rendering follows the Latin.
- **The 382 law** (CTh 86429-86440) orders the death penalty for "Encratites ... Saccofori ... Hydroparastatae"; the 381 law (codex-theodosianus_latinlibrary 7062) says some Manichaeans take those names. Whether this reached Manichaeans as such is an inference; stays out.

**Set aside, and why:**
- *Acts of Archelaus* (anf06): a staged debate of the 300s; its life of Mani is polemical legend. Used only for the variant of Mani's death (B10).
- Cyril of Jerusalem, Catechetical Lecture VI (npnf207), and Socrates I.22 (npnf202): retell the Acts of Archelaus.
- Epiphanius, Panarion 66 (Holl, Oehler, Migne Greek prints): a long hostile account resting largely on the Acts; nothing the story needs that an earlier or own-words source does not give.
- Ephrem, Prose Refutations (Mitchell): the Syriac sibling tells Ephrem's answer; not repeated here.
- Chrysostom's Homily Against Marcionists and Manichaeans (npnf109): doctrinal polemic, no events.
- Augustine, On Two Souls, On the Nature of Good, On the Profit of Believing: doctrine stated to refute it; no scene or practice the story lacks.
- Chavannes-Pelliot part 1 (the Chinese treatise in translation): rich doctrine, read for C13's sibling passage in part 2; the treatise's cosmology is beyond the story.
- Le Coq, Türkische Manichaica aus Chotscho (1911, German): fragments; the Khuastuanift in English covers the Turkish Hearers' voice.
- Photius, Contra Manichaeos, and Peter of Sicily: on the Paulicians of the 800s, whom they call Manichaeans; another movement.
- The many later files (Port-Royal, Lucaris, William of Tyre, Ibn Khaldun and others): use "Manichaean" as a label for others; nothing on the community itself.
- BeDuhn (2010), on the card: modern; not held; Modern works list only.
