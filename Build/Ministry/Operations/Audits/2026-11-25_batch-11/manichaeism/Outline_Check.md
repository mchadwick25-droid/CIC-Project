# Outline check: Manichaeism

## My account of the heart (written before reading Outline.md)

From the census entry, the slate job, the source sheet and what the subject is known for.

- **The essence:** The followers of Mani, a prophet of third-century Babylonia who said he was the last of God's messengers after Buddha, Zoroaster and Jesus, and the Helper Jesus had promised. They were one church with its own scriptures, written by Mani himself, split into a few Elect who gave up meat, wine, marriage and property, and many Hearers who lived in the world and fed and served them. It spread from Mesopotamia west across the Roman Empire and east along the Silk Road to China, hunted by Persian kings and Roman emperors.
- **Who they were:** "the followers of Mani" (Romans called them Manichaeans).
- **When and where:** from Mani's birth (216, Babylonia, under the new Persian kings) through Roman Africa in the 300s-400s, to Central Asia, the Uyghurs and China (700s-800s), and remnants about 1000.
- **What they faced:** Mani's break with the baptizers he grew up among; Shapur's favor (or banishment: sources differ); Bahram's killing of Mani and the hunt after it; Roman laws (372, 399) confiscating meeting houses and hunting them out; banishment of Faustus; in China the 732 restriction and the 843 suppression; flight again in the 900s.
- **What they believed:** two eternal roots, Light and Darkness, mixed in this world and to be parted; Light trapped in the world (in food, plants) to be freed; Mani the Paraclete and seal of the prophets; no harm to animals, fire, water, plants.
- **How they practiced:** Elect and Hearers; the Hearers' alms of food carrying Light to be freed through the Elect; prayers facing the sun; fasting; the Hearers' daily confession ("My sin remit!"); the Bema feast on the day of Mani's death; the three seals; Mani's books written and translated on purpose.
- **What held them together:** the bond of Elect and Hearers around the freeing of the Light, and Mani's own books as one scripture for every land.
- **What came of them:** their own books and prayers survived and were found (Turkish confession at Dunhuang, Chinese texts), the eastern church lasted centuries (Uyghur khagans, Chinese temples, Samarkand); in the West Augustine, a Hearer nine years, preserved their arguments (Faustus) inside his replies; the name became a charge against others (card).

Ordinary believers to carry: the Hearers (Augustine at prayer; the Turkish confession).
