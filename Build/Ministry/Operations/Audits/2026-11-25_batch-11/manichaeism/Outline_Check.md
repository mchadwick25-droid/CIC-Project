# Outline check: Manichaeism

## My account of the heart (written before reading Outline.md)

From the census entry, the slate job, the source sheet and what the subject is known for.

- **The essence:** The followers of Mani, a prophet of third-century Babylonia who said he was the last of God's messengers after Buddha, Zoroaster and Jesus, and the Helper Jesus had promised. They were one church with its own scriptures, written by Mani himself, split into a few Elect who gave up meat, wine, marriage and property, and many Hearers who lived in the world and fed and served them. It spread from Mesopotamia west across the Roman Empire and east along the Silk Road to China, hunted by Persian kings and Roman emperors.
- **Who they were:** "the followers of Mani" (Romans called them Manichaeans).
- **When and where:** from Mani's birth (216, Babylonia, under the new Persian kings) through Roman Africa in the 300s-400s, to Central Asia, the Uyghurs and China (700s-800s), and remnants about 1000.
- **What they faced:** Mani's break with the baptizers he grew up among; Shapur's favor (or banishment: sources differ); Bahram's killing of Mani and the hunt after it; Roman laws (372, 399) confiscating meeting houses and hunting them out; banishment of Faustus; in China the 732 restriction and the 843 suppression; flight again in the 900s.
- **What they believed:** two eternal roots, Light and Darkness, mixed in this world and to be parted; Light trapped in the world (in food, plants) to be freed; Mani the Paraclete and seal of the prophets; no harm to animals, fire, water, plants.
- **How they practiced:** Elect and Hearers; the Hearers' alms of food carrying Light to be freed through the Elect; prayers facing the sun; fasting; the Hearers' daily confession ("My sin remit!"); the Bema feast on the day of Mani's death; the three seals; Mani's books written and translated on purpose.
- **What held them together:** the bond of Elect and Hearers around the freeing of the Light, and Mani's own books as one scripture for every land.
- **What came of them:** their own books and prayers survived and were found (Turkish confession at Dunhuang, Chinese texts), the eastern church lasted centuries (Uyghur khagans, Chinese temples, Samarkand); in the West Augustine, a Hearer nine years, preserved their arguments (Faustus) inside his replies; the name became a charge against others (card).

Ordinary believers to carry: the Hearers (Augustine at prayer; the Turkish confession).

**Comparison with the outline's heart.** The two agree in substance: the same community, two ranks, Light and Darkness, the hunt in Persia, Rome and China, the spread east, and what came of it. Nothing major is left out. One field rests on weaker ground than it shows: "the Light in food set free through the Elect" ("what they believed" and "what held them together") stands only on Augustine's mockery and on Le Coq's modern note, not on the confession lines it cites (finding 1). The approved heart stands, with that field credited as finding 1 says.

**Beats.** The opening (Hippo, 392) and the essence come within the first minute. After that, beat 3 reaches ahead to Augustine's nine years as a Hearer (370s-380s), and beat 4 then goes back to Mani's death (finding 4). Otherwise the beats follow the story once, in order, and the ending (Dunhuang) is not told early.

## Findings

### BLOCKING

1. **The alms and the Light: the own-side claim rests on a modern note (beat 3; Words to explain, "the alms of food"; heart, "what they believed" and "what held them together").** KHU 16990-17011 (article 11) is about "seven kinds of alms" offered "for the sake of the pure Faith". It speaks of angels gathering "the light of the Five-God", which "going to God is (there) to be purified". It says nothing about food or the Elect. The reading that this light is "the light contained in these offerings of food", freed when the Elect eat it, is Le Coq's own note, and he hedges it with "perhaps" (KHU 18038-18050). That is modern. The confession does tie the light to food in article 15: the light "that, every day, we have absorbed in our food" goes "to the Bad Place" because of their sin (KHU 17364-17371, 17436-17440). Only Augustine, an enemy, tells the Elect's part (NPNF 6343-6346).
   - Beat 3: replace "The Hearers brought the Elect their food; in their own prayers, the Light in it went to God to be made pure (D alms row, KHU 16996-17011); Augustine, a Hearer nine years before he turned against them (B14), mocked it: the Elect's stomachs would make them "angels and gods" (D alms row)." with: "In their own confession the Hearers held that the Light was in the food they ate, and that their sins sent it 'to the Bad Place' (KHU 17364-17371, 17436-17440). Augustine, who had been a Hearer, says the Hearers carried food to the Elect, and mocks it: the Elect's stomachs, he says, were meant to make 'angels and gods' from it, by whom the Hearers would be set free (NPNF 6343-6346; Latin 5730-5733). Confidence: Documented, each as its witness gives it; the Elect's part is Augustine's report."
   - Words to explain, "the alms of food", their side: replace "the Light in the food went with it to God to be made pure" (D alms row, KHU) with "the Light was in their food, and sin sent it 'to the Bad Place'" (KHU 17364-17371, 17436-17440).
   - Heart, "What held them together": replace "the Hearers' food carrying the Light the Elect set free (C5, D alms row)" with "the Hearers serving and feeding the Elect (C5; Augustine, NPNF 6343-6346), with the Light, in their own confession, bound up in the food they ate (KHU 17364-17371)".
   - Source_Sheet D, alms row: mark the phrase "the Hearers' alms carry it to God to be purified" as resting on Le Coq's note (modern, KHU 18038-18050).

2. **Which king killed Mani is told as fact, though the sources differ (beat 4; Setting; Do not say).** Alexander of Lycopolis, a contemporary outsider writing about 300, says Mani "served under Sapor ... and having offended him in some way, was put to death" (ALX 24496-24498). Biruni and the Fihrist, about 700 years later, give Bahram. The sheet rates the king Contested (B10; G), but beat 4 says plainly "King Bahram had him sought".
   - Beat 4: open with "Biruni, writing about 1000, says King Bahram had him sought, and gives the king's reason: "This man has come forward calling people to destroy the world" (B9). A writer in Egypt about 300 heard instead that Mani died under Shapur, after offending him in some way (ALX 24496-24498)."
   - Setting: replace "then King Bahram, under whom he died (B9, B10...)" with "then, by Biruni's and the Fihrist's account, King Bahram, under whom he died; Alexander, writing about 300, puts the death under Shapur (B10, ALX 24496-24498)".
   - Do not say: add the row: Wrong: "King Bahram killed Mani" as fact. Right: "Biruni and the Fihrist name Bahram; a writer in Egypt about 300 names Shapur" (B10).

3. **Explain it: three acts are named without the reason their sources give.**
   - (a) **Mani's imprisonment (beat 4).** The version "chained in prison till he died" comes from a book by one of Mani's own pupils, as Biruni reports it. That book gives the reason: Mani was imprisoned after he promised to cure a relative of the king, thought to be possessed by the devil, and could not (BIR 29132-29137). Replace "or chained in prison till he died (B10)" with "or, in a book by one of his own pupils that Biruni reports, imprisoned after he failed to cure a relative of the king, and kept in chains there till he died (BIR 29132-29138)".
   - (b) **Faustus's exile (beat 5).** The cause is missing, and the beat leaves out that the Christians who brought him to court pleaded for him. Augustine gives both: "convictus vel confessus quod manicheus esset ... eis ipsis christianis a quibus perducti sunt intercedentibus, levissima poena ... in insulam relegatus est ... non multo post inde omnes ... dimissi sunt" (Latin 16830-16840; English 4615-4616). Replace "Christians brought Faustus before the governor, and he and others were exiled to an island, then freed by a later decree (B20)" with "Christians brought Faustus and some others before the governor's court. Found to be a Manichaean, by proof or by his own word, he was exiled to an island. Augustine calls this the lightest of penalties, given because those same Christians pleaded for them. Not long after, a public decree freed them all together (B20; CFa Latin 16830-16840)."
   - (c) **The Bema (beat 4; Words to explain).** The gloss says what the feast was, but not why it mattered to them, and the held source gives that. When Augustine asked why, he was told that the day to keep was the suffering of the one who had truly suffered, since Christ was never born in real flesh and only seemed to suffer (CEF Latin 12879-12883; English 3733). Add to beat 4 after the Bema: "When Augustine asked why, they told him the day to keep was the death of one who had truly suffered, since Christ, they held, was never born in real flesh and only seemed to suffer (CEF Latin 12879-12883)." Gloss: "the Bema, the feast of the day Mani was killed, which they kept as the death of one who truly suffered".

4. **An event told out of order (beat 3).** "Augustine, a Hearer nine years before he turned against them (B14)" tells an event of the 370s-380s before beat 4's death of Mani in the 270s. That is a jump back. Beat 3's credit should be "Augustine, who had been a Hearer" (finding 1 does this). Move the nine years into beat 5: "Faustus of Milevis, a bishop of theirs whom Augustine, a Hearer for nine years, had met at Carthage (B14, B15)".

5. **Explain it: "the charges" in the opening.** Fortunatus asks about the "falsis criminibus" over their way of life ("De conversatione hic agitur", CFo Latin 7658-7660). The outline names "the charges" without saying what they were about. Replace "whether the charges made against them were true" with "whether the charges against their way of life were true (CFo Latin 7658-7663)".

6. **The length runs past 675 after Cut first, and the list was not extended.** The approved section says the outline "adds to its Cut first list". It has not: the list still has seven items, and its header still says "from about 650 to about 470 words, if a three-minute length is approved". My recount agrees with the independent one (687 after Cut first). Findings 2, 3 and 5 add about 75 words, so the cuts must find about 90.
   - Replace the Cut first header with: "Each cut is marked for the card or Leave out. Every item goes, per Mark's ruling of 2026-11-25; together they bring the story to 675 words or under."
   - Append these items, all marked **card**, none of which drops what a named thing was about or why it happened:
     - 8. "At twenty-four the angel told him to come out and preach" (beat 1), about 12 words.
     - 9. The Fihrist's rule for whoever loved the faith but could not tame desire (C6, beat 3), about 17 words. The Hearers gloss already carries it.
     - 10. The Uyghur inscription's "initiated the Uyghurs into the true religion" (B23, beat 6), about 15 words. Keep the khagan bringing four teachers home.
     - 11. Beat 3's "turned toward the sun" (about 5 words). The opening already tells it; keep "four or seven times a day" (see finding M2).
     - 12. Beat 4's flight across the river of Balkh: shorten to "they fled east into the lands of the Turks", which saves about 8 words.
     - 13. Beat 2's "He said he was the Paraclete, the Helper Jesus had promised": keep it, and cut "the Fihrist says" there, since the Fihrist is already credited in beat 1 (about 3 words).
   - Then recount quotes, glosses, credits and facts with all fixes in, and state the total. If it is still over 675, the next item to send to the card is the 732 edict's allowance for the Hu (B22), about 25 words, together with its Hu gloss.
