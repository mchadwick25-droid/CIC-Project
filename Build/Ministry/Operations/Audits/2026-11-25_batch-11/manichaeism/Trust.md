## BLOCKING

**3. SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** Brief: "Augustine answered that he had been only a Hearer, never one of the Elect, and that at their prayer he had seen nothing shameful, only that they prayed facing the sun."
Source, CFo Latin (`augustine_arian-sermon-collatio-maximinum-pl42-lat_migne1886.txt` 7684-7692, 7710): "Nostis autem me non Electum vestrum, sed Auditorem fuisse ... nihil turpe fieri vidi: sed solum contra fidem animadverti, quam postea didici et probavi, quod contra solem facitis orationem." What he noticed was one thing *against the faith* he later learned. The brief keeps "only" but drops "against the faith", so his answer reads as a clean bill. The outline's Do not say row (B19) already gives the right wording.
Change: "...and that at their prayer he had seen nothing shameful. He had noticed only one thing against the faith he later learned: they prayed facing the sun."

**6. ASSUMPTION ADDED.** Brief: "A few, the Elect, lived in strict purity and poverty, while the many, the Hearers, lived in the world and fed and served them."
Source: BIR 29070-29085 gives the two sets of laws, for the Siddiks ("the saints and ascetics among the Manichaeans") and for the Samma' ("their followers and adherents who have to do with worldly affairs"); FIH 4881-4898 gives who may enter and who instead guards the religion. Neither these nor CFo, CONF or CEF say the Elect were few and the Hearers many. Searched: BIR, FIH, npnf104 and CONF for "few", "many", "multitude", "small number" near Elect/Hearer/Auditor. Only Newman's modern introduction speaks of hearers in bulk (npnf104 1243), and it does not say this. The proportion is an inference.
Change: "The Elect lived in strict purity and poverty, while the Hearers lived in the world and fed and served them." (The outline's essence holds the same words; strike "A few" and "the many" there too, not only in the story.)

## SUMMARY HOLDS (with the line each rests on)

- **4.** "They were the followers of Mani, a prophet of Babylonia." BIR 29060-29061: "Mani, the messenger of the God of truth to Babylonia"; 29149 "their prophet Mani"; birth in Babylonia, BIR 29094-29097.
- **5.** "From the 200s they held that Light and Darkness were two powers without beginning, now mixed in the world, and that in the end the two would be parted again." BIR 29069-29070: "light and darkness are without beginning and end"; KHU 16655-16671: "how (thereby) Light and Darkness had intermingled ... and how (thereby) Light and Darkness will (again) be parted". "The 200s": first revelation "in the 2nd year of Ardashir" (BIR 29099-29100).
- **7.** "Hunted by Persian kings and Roman emperors, they spread from Mesopotamia west to Roman Africa and east to the Turks and China." BIR 29119-29128 (Bahram; "Hurmuz also killed a number of the Manichaeans"); FIH 5387-5393; CTh XVI.5.3 (Valentinian and Valens); CFo 7606-7607 (Hippo); CHI 6875-6886.
- **8.** "Mani was born in 216 in Babylonia, in Mesopotamia, which Persia then ruled." BIR 29094-29098: born "in the year 527 of the era of the Babylonian astronomers ... in the 4th year of the king Adharban" (216, Widely Accepted). Note, not blocking: Adharban is the last Arsacid (Ashkanian) king, so "Persia" here means the Parthian realm, before Shapur's house. Biruni counts the Ashkanians among the kings of Persia, so it holds; a reviser who wants it exact can say "which the Parthian kings of Persia then ruled".
- **14.** "The Elect kept from marriage, owned only a day's food and a year's clothing, and went about preaching." BIR 29076-29079: "food for one day and dress for one year; he further forbade sexual intercourse, and ordered them continually to wander about in the world, preaching"; FIH 4885 "den ehelichen Beischlaf lassen".
- **15.** "The Hearers gave a tenth of what they had, fasted, married one wife and served the Elect." BIR 29081-29085: "to give as alms the tithe of their property, to fast during the seventh part of life-time, to live in monogamy, to befriend the Siddiks (saints), and to remove everything that troubles and pains them."
- **16.** "The Hearers' own confession says that the Light was in the food they ate, and that their sins sent it 'to the Bad Place.'" KHU 17362-17371, 17443: "Because we ourselves have walked (lived) ... in the love of the insatiable, shameless, envious Demon, (therefore) does (that part of) the light of the Five-God, that, every day, we have absorbed in our food, ... go to the Bad Place."
- **17.** "Augustine says the Hearers carried food to the Elect, and mocks it: their stomachs, he says, were meant to make 'angels and gods' out of it, who would then set the Hearers free." CONF Latin 5726-5727 (*in officina aqualiculi sui fabricarent angelos et deos, per quos liberaremur*); NPNF `npnf101` 6342-6346. Augustine says "we" (he and his friends); generalising to the Hearers is fair.
- **19.** "A writer in Egypt about 300 heard instead that Mani died under Shapur, after offending him in some way." ALX (`anf06` 24494-24497): "They say ... that he served under Sapor, the king of the Persians, and having offended him in some way, was put to death. Some such report ... has come to me from those who were intimately acquainted with him." Egypt: Photius's "bishop of the city of Lycus", quoted at `anf06` 24313. "About 300" rests on the editor's "a.d. 301" (24293) and Alexander's own nearness to Mani's first disciples (24494-24496); a Widely Accepted date, told roughly, as the kit allows.
- **21.** "Some say he was flayed and his skin hung at the city gate." BIR 29125-29127: "he killed Mani, stripped off his skin, filled it with grass, and hung it up at the gate of Gundisapur". Biruni says "It is well known"; "Some say" fits the next sentence's differing account.
- **22.** "A book by one of his own pupils, which Biruni reports, says that he failed to cure a relative of the king and was chained in prison until he died." BIR 29130-29136: "one of Mani's pupils composed a book ... he was put in prison on account of a relative of the king ... Mani had promised to cure him, but when he could not effect it, he was chained hand and foot, and died in prison." Biruni has it through a Christian writer, Jibra'il b. Nuh (29130); "which Biruni reports" is still true.
- **24.** "Augustine says that when he asked why, they told him the day to keep was the death of one who had truly suffered, since Christ, they held, was never born in real flesh and only seemed to suffer." CEF Latin 12877-12891: "hoc ergo cum quaererem, respondebatur ejus diem passionis celebrandum esse qui vere passus esset; Christum autem qui natus non esset, neque veram, sed simulatam carnem ... non pertulisse, sed finxisse passionem." His question was why Easter was barely kept while the Bema was honored; "asked why" carries it.
- **25.** "After Mani's death, says the Fihrist, a Baghdad scholar's book of the 900s, the king killed his followers wherever he found them, and they fled east into the lands of the Turks." FIH 5386-5395: "begann er die Anhänger Mäni's wo immer er sie finden mochte hinzurichten. Sie flohen ... bis sie den Fluss von Balch überschritten hatten und in das Gebiet des Chan gelangten ... die Fürsten der Türken"; Baghdad, FIH 5428.
- **27.** "Faustus of Milevis, a bishop of theirs, wrote a book of answers, and Augustine, a Hearer for nine years, kept his words, setting out each part of the book before he answered it." Milevis: CFa Latin 15450 "civitate Milevitanus"; bishop: CONF Latin 7333-7336 "manichaeorum episcopus, Faustus nomine"; "brief and pointed replies" (npnf104 4314); nine years, CONF Latin 5683-5684; a Hearer, CFo 7684; "I will give the opinions of Faustus as if stated by himself, and mine as if in reply to him" (npnf104 4312).
- **30.** "Augustine calls this the lightest of penalties, given because those same Christians pleaded for him." CFa Latin 16832-16836: "eis ipsis christianis a quibus perducti sunt intercedentibus, levissima poena, si tamen illa poena dicenda est, in insulam relegatus est"; NPNF 4615 "on the intercession of the Christians themselves".
- **36.** "Each article ends the same way: my God, cleansing ourselves of sin, we pray, forgive my sin." KHU 17443-17447: "Because of this, my God, cleaning ourselves from sin! do we pray: Manastar hirza!"; gloss 15729 "(My sin remit!)"; the refrain closes the articles throughout (16091, 16121, 16242, 16888, 16995, 17104, 17146, 17236, 17346, 17605).

## The heart

Right for the subject: the two ranks bound by the food the Hearers brought, the Light and Darkness that food turned on, Mani's books as the last revelation, and the hunting by Persian king, Roman emperor and Chinese edict. Each of these is told from the sources above, and the ending gives the ordinary believers their own voice. On time: the essence runs from word 73 to word 164, and its core (the two powers, the Elect and the Hearers) is told by about word 135, inside the first minute.
