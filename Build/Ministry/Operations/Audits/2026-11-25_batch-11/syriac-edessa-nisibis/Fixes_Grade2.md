## Fix 1. Missing why: the king's change of course

**Finding:** "a later king ordered the churches his fathers had torn down to be rebuilt" | missing: why | asks: "Why did the king change course?" | bare statement.

### What the sources hold

Two held witnesses give an answer. Neither gives the king's own reason in his own words.

1. **The acts of the synod of 410** (contemporary, the synod's own acts, kept in a ninth-century collection; Chabot's French, 1902), `cic/texts/synodicon-orientale_recueil-de-synodes-nestoriens-syr-fra_chabot1902.txt`. Right after the grant (16155-16166), the acts say how it came about. 16168: "Ces choses advinrent du temps et lors de lélection à la primauté de l'excellent et grand homme de Dieu notre Père Mar Isaac". Then 16227-16231: "par la diligence et par les soins de l'apôtre, messager de paix, que Dieu dans ses miséricordes envoya en Orient, le Père sage, le chef honorable, Mar Marouta, évèque, qui fut le médiateur de la paix et de la concorde entre l'Orient et l'Occident, qui s'appliqua à consolider les églises du Seigneur Christ". And 16236: "et aussi par le soin des chefs et des Pères évêques de la contrée des Romains". In plain words: these things came about in Isaac's time, through the work of Marutha, a bishop who made peace between East and West, and of the Roman bishops. **Witness kind:** contemporary. **Confidence:** Documented, as the synod's own account.
2. **Socrates, *Church History* VII.8** (later, c. 440; gap about 30 years), `cic/texts/npnf202_socrates-sozomen-ecclesiastical-histories.xml`. 19133-19138: "Necessity brought it about at that time that the Roman emperor thought proper to send Maruthas bishop of Mesopotamia ... on a mission to the king of the Persians." 19139-19141: "The king discovering great piety in the man treated him with great honor". 19174-19176: "he permitted Maruthas to erect churches wherever he wished; and from that time the Christian religion was diffused among the Persians." 19186-19187: "For the Romans as a nation he had much regard". **Witness kind:** later, about 30 years. **Confidence:** Documented as Socrates' account for the envoy's mission and the king's favour. The wonders that carry it (the headache cured by prayer, 19151-19153; the magi's hidden voice under the hearth, 19154-19173; the demon cast out of the king's son, 19188-19191) are a wonder-tale in Socrates' voice: Contested; Leave out.

The two agree on the one point the story needs: a bishop came to Persia as the Roman envoy, and through his work the churches were rebuilt. The brief says only that, in the narrator's words. It does not name him (Outline 87 keeps him "the Roman envoy (Marutha)", by role) and adds no motive of the king's own.
