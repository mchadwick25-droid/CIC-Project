## tile

- **Old (158 words):** "In Edessa and Nisibis, from 200 to 410 CE, Christians taught their faith in hymn. They read Scripture as a world of types, each one answering another. They also read the four Gospels as one continuous story, not four separate books. A harmony woven a generation earlier combined the four accounts. Preachers used it for as long as this Christian tradition lasted. At the heart of this community's ascetic life stood the covenant. Men and women vowed themselves to celibacy for life, serving their own congregation instead of leaving for the desert. Whether authority rested more in that vow or in a bishop's office was never settled in their own record. On the Persian side of the frontier, the king's persecution killed the bishop Simeon bar Sabbae and his clergy, and bishops after him were killed in turn. After the last of them, the bishopric of Seleucia-Ctesiphon stood empty for twenty years. Taking the post meant near-certain death."
- **New (176 words; grade 9.2, ease 72.8, pass):** "In Edessa and Nisibis, two frontier cities between the Roman and Persian empires, Syriac-speaking Christians lived from the flood of 201 to the year 410. In Nisibis, Ephrem taught in hymns, each with a refrain that came back after every verse. In his hymn for a Persian siege, he sets the river that threatens the city beside Noah's flood. Some men and women vowed, before baptism, to live unmarried and holy for God: the sons and daughters of the covenant. In Persia, the teacher Aphrahat wrote letters, one of them to these vowed men and women. There, Sozomen says, the king believed a charge that their bishop was a friend of the Roman emperor, taxed the Christians heavily and had their priests killed. In 363 Rome handed Nisibis to Persia, and Ephrem moved to Edessa. He answered three rival teachers by name, whose followers, he wrote, fasted as his people did but did not share their faith. In 410, forty bishops of the Persian church met to set its rules, the bishop of Nisibis among them."
- **Reasons:**
  - Dates: "200 to 410 CE" becomes "from the flood of 201 to the year 410", the brief's scope (paragraphs 1-2).
  - "Read Scripture as a world of types" is cut: the brief holds no such line. In its place, the one image the brief tells: Ephrem's siege hymn setting the river beside Noah's flood (paragraph 5).
  - The four Gospels read as one story "for as long as this Christian tradition lasted" is cut: outline Do not say bars it (only Addai 65, a late legend, has it). The tile has no room to tell it inside its witness, as the brief does.
  - The covenant: "celibacy ... serving their own congregation instead of leaving for the desert" is barred by Do not say. The new line is the brief's (paragraph 3): the vow before baptism, "unmarried and holy for God", sons and daughters of the covenant.
  - "Whether authority rested ..." is cut: the authority question is in the outline's Leave out (Contested, thin), and the brief does not hold it.
  - Simeon and the twenty-year vacancy are cut: the continued-as entry's own story (batch header). The persecution is told as the brief tells it, with its reason in Sozomen's voice (paragraph 6).
  - Added from the brief: Aphrahat's letters (3), 363 and Ephrem's move (7), the rivals answered, given with what their followers shared and did not, so they are not named bare (7, Q3), and the synod of 410 with the bishop of Nisibis (9), glossed as a meeting to set rules.

## summary

- **Old (73 words):** "Between about 200 and 410, Syriac-speaking Christians lived on the border of the Roman and Persian empires, at Edessa and Nisibis. They taught the faith through hymns and symbols. Men and women of the covenant promised to stay unmarried for life. They lived in town with their families. Ephrem, a deacon, answered rival teachers by name. In the 340s, King Shapur II turned on the Christians of Persia. Bishop after bishop was killed."
- **New (93 words; grade 9.0, ease 71.0, pass):** "From the flood of 201 to 410, Syriac-speaking Christians lived at Edessa and Nisibis, between the Roman and Persian empires. They sang their teaching in hymns, and some men and women vowed to live unmarried and holy for God. Ephrem answered rival teachers whose followers fasted as his people did but held another faith. In Persia, Sozomen says, the king believed a charge that their bishop was a friend of the Roman emperor, and had their priests killed. In 410, forty bishops of the Persian church met to set its rules."
- **Reasons:**
  - Dates: "about 200" becomes "the flood of 201", as the brief.
  - "Through hymns and symbols" becomes the brief's "sang their teaching in hymns"; the brief does not say "symbols".
  - The covenant line takes the brief's words. "They lived in town with their families" is cut: outline Do not say (not held).
  - "a deacon" is cut: the brief no longer holds it (Cut first 22 sent it to the card).
  - The rivals get the explanation Part 1 asks, from Q3 (paragraph 7).
  - "In the 340s, King Shapur II ... Bishop after bishop was killed" becomes the brief's account: the king's belief in the charge, in Sozomen's voice, and the priests killed (paragraph 6). The brief names no king and no bishops killed; "the 340s" rests on Aphrahat's 344, which the summary does not need.
  - Added: the synod of 410, where the brief ends (paragraph 9).
