## BLOCKING findings

**1. Sentence 3: MODERN ONLY.** "...and its line is the first sure sign of a church there."
- `chronicle-of-edessa_cowper.txt` 53 says only "they destroyed the temple of the church of the Christians." No old source says this is the first sure sign. That ranking weighs the evidence and sets aside an older witness: Eusebius tells of a church at Edessa from the apostles' time, in the Abgar story. The weighing is the modern editor's (Cowper's note, line 368). In the chronicle itself, the entries before this one (lines 41-51) name no church in the city.
- Exact change: "...and its line is the chronicle's first word of a church in the city."

**2. Sentence 5: ASSUMPTION ADDED.** "From the flood to the year 410, they sang their teaching in hymns, heard the Gospel as one story that Tatian had woven out of the four, and counted among them men and women vowed to God for life."
- (a) The one-story Gospel is told as the narrator's fact, but the Library's only old witness that these churches read it is the late founding legend: `addai_doctrine-of-addai.txt` 65, "to the reading of the Old and New Testament, of the Diatessaron". Even that word is an editor's reading of the manuscript (Phillips's note, line 219: Cureton's text has "Ditornon"). Eusebius (`npnf201...xml` 26424-26433) says Tatian made it, "still in the hands of some", but gives no place. Gwynn (`npnf213...xml` 13666) and Phillips (221) say these churches used it, but both are modern. The Addai line was cut for length (Cut first 2), and nothing in the brief now marks this as the legend's picture.
- (b) "From the flood" stretches each practice back to 201. The held evidence for hymns and the covenant begins with Aphrahat (337, 344: `npnf213...xml` 28556-28561) and Ephrem.
- Exact change: change sentence 4's close to "...in two frontier cities between the Roman and Persian empires, from the flood to the year 410." Then sentence 5 becomes: "In the 300s they sang their teaching in hymns and counted among them men and women vowed to God for life. Edessa's later story of its own founding pictures them hearing the Gospel read as one story, which Tatian had woven out of the four."

**3. Sentence 7: MODERN ONLY.** "...to live unmarried and holy for God while serving in the church."
- Aphrahat gives the vow and the choice before baptism (`aphrahat_demonstrations-2-7_hallock1932.txt` 57; Parisot 21939-21942; NPNF VI.4, `npnf213...xml` 25835-25836, "bound by monastic vow"). He never says they served in the church. Only Chabot's modern note on the synod acts (`synodicon-orientale_recueil...chabot1902.txt` 16174-16180) suggests church service or clergy.
- Exact change: cut "while serving in the church". The sentence becomes: "Some men and women took a vow, before baptism, to live unmarried and holy for God."

**4. Sentence 15: ASSUMPTION ADDED.** "...a single voice sang the verses, and the people sang a line back."
- Hymn I gives a stanza, then a line the print marks "*R*." (`npnf213...xml` 13725-13734). No old source in the Library says who sang it, or that one voice sang and the people answered. Sozomen (`npnf202...xml` 32085-32103) says only that Harmonius gave his verses "to the choirs" and that "the Syrians sang the odes of Ephraim." The choir story in Gwynn (11253) is modern, and the outline's Do not say bars it.
- Exact change: "In Nisibis, Ephrem taught in hymns, each with a refrain, a line that came round again and again."

**5. Sentence 18: ASSUMPTION ADDED.** "So the people sang:" brings the same unheld detail into the line that introduces Q1.
- Exact change: "So the refrain runs:" (Q1 itself holds: 13733-13734, "Stretch forth Thy bow against the flood, for lo! it has lifted up its waves against our walls!").

**6. Sentence 20: SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** "...the king believed a charge that their bishop was a friend of Rome's Christian emperor."
- Sozomen, `npnf202...xml` 28887-28889: "charged him with being a friend of the Cæsar of the Romans ... Sapor believed these accusations." Sozomen does not give "Christian". That tie to the faith is the martyr acts' own reason ("Knechten des Kaisers ..., der ihr Glaubensgenosse ist", Braun 1576-1577), and the brief has it in Sozomen's mouth.
- Exact change: "...a friend of the Roman emperor."

**7. Sentence 25: ASSUMPTION ADDED.** "Rival teachers preached in these cities, and in his prose writings Ephrem answered 'the three of them' by name."
- `ephraim_prose-refutations_mitchell1912-1921.txt` 584 holds "the three of them (i.e., Marcion, Mani, and Bardaisan)". But no held line puts Marcion or Mani preaching in Edessa or Nisibis. Ephrem answers their followers as living parties: "let the party of Bardaisan be asked" (608), and "he who prays with the Manichaeans" (1205). Put right after 363, the sentence also implies a date for the refutations that no old source gives.
- Exact change: "In his day their rivals' teachings still had followers, and in his prose writings Ephrem answered 'the three of them' by name."

**8. Sentence 31: ASSUMPTION ADDED.** "...go about freely and without fear, the same vowed order Aphrahat had written for."
- The grant holds (Chabot 16165-16166: "que les prêtres, les chefs et toute la sainte alliance circulassent en toute liberté et sans crainte"). But the acts do not say this is Aphrahat's order. The editor's note on the word, in the same file (16174-16176), reads it otherwise: "Le mot qyama paraît désigner ici tout le clergé y compris les moines." So "the same vowed order" asserts a link the source does not make, and its own editor reads the word more widely.
- Exact change: cut ", the same vowed order Aphrahat had written for". The quoted word "covenant" already carries the echo. (If the echo must be spoken, say "the covenant, the same word Aphrahat used". Parisot 18150-18151 and Chabot 16165 both render the word as covenant.)

## SUMMARY HOLDS (not blocking)

- **S4**, "Those Christians spoke Syriac ... between the Roman and Persian empires": Sozomen 32053-32054 ("the language of the Syrians"); Socrates 11697 (Nisibis given up); Theodoret S2. In 201 the eastern power was Parthian (chronicle line 51), but that does not mislead across the whole span.
- **S8**, "sons and daughters of the covenant": Hallock 62, "sons of a covenant"; Parisot 18150, "foederis filios". "Daughters" is not in Aphrahat's held text. It comes from the synod usage (Chabot 16176-16178) and John of Ephesus 14152.
- **S9**, the night watch, psalms, the poor, living apart, "so that lust will not draw them back": `npnf213` 25803-25808 and 25835-25843 ("lest he turn back to his former condition"; "not be made wanton by lust"). A description recast as a request, which is fair for an exhortation.
- **S16**, "a siege by a Persian army": Hymn I itself names no enemy (13746-13751: "mounds and weapons and waves ... the river threatens me"). Nisibis's besiegers were Persian (Theodoret 11124-11127), and the hymns speak of elephants (Hymn II). No year and no join to Theodoret's siege, as Do not say requires.
- **S19**, grown in number, Magi angered: Sozomen 28873-28877.
- **S22**, "he ordered their priests killed and their churches pulled down": Sozomen 28895-28897 has the order to kill. The churches "were demolished", in the passive. The order is carried by the 410 acts, "temples destroyed by his fathers" (Chabot 16162).
- **S26**, the three teachings: 584 and 1605-1609 (Stranger, Maker); 431 and 640 (two Roots, Light and Darkness); 606-608 and 917 ("five Entities"). Line 1549 has "[seven Entities constitute] the world", but that is the editor's restoration in brackets. Five, from 917, stands.
- **S29**, died at Edessa in 373, hymns sung on: Chronicle 30 (line 103, year 684 = 373, Widely Accepted); Barhadbeshabba 20437-20438 (at Edessa for the rest of his life); Sozomen 32101-32102, "From that period the Syrians sang the odes of Ephraim."

## The heart

It is the right heart. The sources show a Syriac-speaking church at Edessa and Nisibis, held by the covenant life and by hymns, pressed by rivals and by Persia, and handed on to the Persian church in 410. Who, where and when, and the hymns and the covenant, are all told by about word 135, inside the first minute. Finding 2 keeps the one-story Gospel in the essence, now marked as the legend's picture, so the heart is not cut.
