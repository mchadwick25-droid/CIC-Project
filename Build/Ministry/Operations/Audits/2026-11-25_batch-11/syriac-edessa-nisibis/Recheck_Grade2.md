**Verdict: FIX** (two minor items, both on one-hearing clarity; no claim reaches past its source).

Checked on a scratch copy outside the repository. Each of the six old strings in `Fixes_Grade2.md` occurs exactly once in `syriac-edessa-nisibis.brief.txt`. Applied, the brief is 654 words by `wc -w`. With the replacements below applied on top, it is still 654.

## What the new sentences claim, against the sources

**"A bishop came to Persia as a Roman envoy."**
- *Who sent him.* Socrates VII.8, `npnf202_socrates-sozomen-ecclesiastical-histories.xml` 19135-19141: "the Roman emperor thought proper to send Maruthas bishop of Mesopotamia ... on a mission to the king of the Persians." This is a later witness, about 30 years after, and Documented as his account. "Roman envoy" rests on this line. The acts of 410 do not say Rome sent him. They say God sent him into the East (16227-16228, "que Dieu dans ses miséricordes envoya en Orient"), as a "médiateur de la paix et de la concorde entre l'Orient et l'Occident" (16229-16230). The acts fit Socrates and do not contradict him. "A bishop" is held in both ("évèque", 16229; "bishop of Mesopotamia", 19136). The claim says no more than its sources.
- No wonder comes in. The headache, the voice under the hearth and the demon (19151-19191) stay out, as the fix says.

**"Through his work, a later king ordered the churches his fathers had torn down to be rebuilt."**
- *What "through his work" claims.* The acts (contemporary, Documented as the synod's account) say that "ces choses", meaning the grant just told (16155-16166), came about in Isaac's time and at his election (16168-16169). They also came about "par la diligence et par les soins de ... Mar Marouta" (16227-16229), and "par le soin des chefs et des Pères évêques de la contrée des Romains" (16236-16237). So Marutha is one of three agents the acts credit. "Through his work" names his share without claiming it was the only cause. It does not reach past the acts. Socrates agrees that the bishop's standing with the king led to the grant: the king "permitted Maruthas to erect churches wherever he wished" (19174-19175), and "for the Romans as a nation he had much regard" (19186). No motive of the king's own is added. That matches the sheet's line that no held line gives his reason in his own words.
- The verb and object ("ordered ... temples destroyed by his fathers ... rebuilt", 16159-16161) are unchanged from the acts.

**Naming the bishop.** Keep him by role. Kit Part 1 (line 15) allows a name when the person acts, but does not require one. The brief already names Aphrahat, Ephrem, Tatian, Noah, Marcion, Mani, Bardaisan and Sozomen, which is past the kit's "about six" check. The outline's Names field (Outline 87) already sets "the Roman envoy (Marutha)" by role. "Marutha" would be a ninth name in the last paragraph. It would add a word and link to no page. The listener's question was why the king changed course, and the role answers it.

**Heart, force, Never-cut, Part 1.** Nothing is lost. The grant, the holy covenant freed, the forty bishops, the bishop of Nisibis, the synod's gloss and the ending all stand. The five wording cuts drop no claim. "For the church" after Aphrahat's letters is not a Never-cut item, and the next lines name the letter's readers.

**Sheet and outline edits.** The quotes added to `Source_Sheet.md` match the files word for word at their lines. One small point: "de la contrée des Romains" runs over 16236-16237, not 16236 alone. The `Outline.md` edits at lines 61, 186 and the new item 31 are accurate. They rate the claim Documented as the acts' account and as Socrates' account, each with its witness kind.

## Findings

**MINOR 1. "a later king" after the envoy sentence is unclear on one hearing.** "A bishop came to Persia as a Roman envoy. Through his work, a later king ordered..." Heard aloud, it is not clear which king the envoy came to. "A later king" can sound as if the bishop worked under one king and a later king acted. Socrates says he was sent to the king who made the grant (19141; named "Isdigerdes", 19160). Putting the king in the first sentence fixes this. It also sets Persia, and "his" in the next sentence still points to the bishop.

**MINOR 2. Cutting "that" from "hoping that want would" makes the sentence hard to follow aloud.** "Hoping want would make them..." makes the listener hear "want" as a verb first. Sozomen's own word is "want" (28894, "by the want of necessaries"), so the noun should stay, and "that" should stay too. The two words go elsewhere, with no claim lost. "So lust will not draw them back" reads cleanly. The chronicle's "the waters broke down the western wall" (`chronicle-of-edessa_cowper.txt` 53) is still told by "broke the city's western wall".
