## 1. The test

- **Who these people were:** filled. They were Syriac-speaking Christians of Edessa and Nisibis, with Aphrahat's covenant members across the border.
- **What happened to them:** filled. The listener hears the flood of 201, the siege, the persecution in Persia, the hand-over of Nisibis in 363, Ephrem's move west and the synod of 410.
- **What their faith was about:** filled, but thinly. The listener hears that they vowed themselves to Christ as a bridegroom, trusted God's promise in the rainbow, sang their teaching and held apart from three rival teachers. What they held about God comes mostly by contrast with what the rivals taught, and the "Their works are like our works" quote gives the stakes. This is enough to answer the line.
- **Why the connection matters:** filled. The Persian church gathers, Nisibis's bishop sits among the forty, and the covenant goes about freely. The listener can tell that this community carries on inside Persia.

## 2. The opening

They do not yet know where Edessa is or who these Christians were. Sentence 4 fills both at once ("spoke Syriac … two frontier cities between the Roman and Persian empires"), so the listener is never lost. Not blocking.

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing (MINOR)

1. "Edessa's later founding story pictures its people hearing the Diatessaron read: the one story Tatian wove from the four Gospels." *Meaning lost:* the founding of what, the city or its church? "Pictures" also makes it unclear whether this happened or is only told. The gloss of the Diatessaron does land. *Change:* say which founding, as the outline gives it (for example "the story Edessa later told of how its church began"). Keep the rest.
2. "In Persia, the Christians had grown in number, and this angered the Magi, the priests who guarded the Persian religion." *Meaning lost:* when. The paragraph before is Ephrem's siege hymn, and no date comes until "writing in 344". So the listener cannot tell whether this comes before or after the siege. *Change:* open the paragraph with the time marker the outline holds (a year or a king's reign), so the order is heard.
3. "Marcion taught a Stranger God, apart from the Maker of the world; Mani taught two first Roots, Light and Darkness; and Bardaisan said the world was made from five first beings." *Meaning lost:* three names and three teachings in one breath. The listener keeps "three rival teachers with strange beliefs about how the world began" but not which teacher taught what. The quote that follows rescues why it mattered, so the thread holds. *Change:* none required. Noted for the reviser. If the outline holds that Bardaisan was a teacher of Edessa itself, a clause saying so would show why Ephrem answered him there.

The long sentences the checker lists (s10, 39 words; s33, 41 words) are understood on one hearing. s10 is a clear list of four practices and does not count.

## 4. Bare statements

1. **BLOCKING.** "…and agreed that a city would no longer have two or three bishops, only one." The story names a ruling and says what it was, but not why it mattered to the bishops who made it. The listener is left asking why a city had two or three bishops, and why that was a problem. *Change:* give the reason the synod's own record gives, if the outline holds it. If not, cut the clause: "In 410, at the king's order, forty bishops of the Persian church met in a synod, a meeting of bishops to set rules for the church." This also brings the 41-word sentence down.

The checker's s5 ("In the 300s they sang their teaching in hymns and counted among them men and women vowed to God for life") is not bare. It belongs to the essence, and paragraphs 3 to 5 then show both the vows and the hymns in action.
