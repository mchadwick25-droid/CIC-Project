## Blocking findings

**1. MODERN ONLY.** "The Chronicle of Edessa, put together some 340 years later, says the city's scribes recorded the flood at the time, and its line is the first sure sign of a church there."
- The credit holds. `chronicle-of-edessa_cowper.txt` 59: "the scribes of Edessa, recorded this event". The last entries fall in the year 850 (lines 251-253), which is AD 539/540.
- "The first sure sign of a church there" is a historian's judgment that sets this line against all the other evidence. No old line says it. What the source does show is that entry 8 (line 53) is the chronicle's first mention of a church in the city. Entries 1-7 (lines 39-51) have none there: entry 5, "Marcion forsook the Catholic Church", is not about Edessa.
- **Change:** "and its line is the first sure sign of a church there." → "and its line is the chronicle's first word of a church in the city."
- **Do not say (add):** "the first sure sign of a church there" → "the chronicle's first word of a church in the city" (this replaces the outline's present right wording, which rests on a modern judgment).

**2. MODERN ONLY (the heart).** "From the flood to the year 410, they sang their teaching in hymns, heard the Gospel as one story that Tatian had woven out of the four, and counted among them men and women vowed to God for life."
- The hymns hold (Sozomen, `npnf202_...xml` 32101-32103: "From that period the Syrians sang the odes of Ephraim"), and so does the covenant (Aphrahat; synod 16166).
- The harmony clause does not. The only old line that ties the Diatessaron to these churches is the late legend, `addai_doctrine-of-addai.txt` 65: "came to the prayer of the service, and to the reading of the Old and New Testament, of the Diatessaron". Nothing old places it at Nisibis, or across the span. That wider claim rests on modern editors: Phillips's note, line 221 ("seems to have been publicly read at Edessa up to the fourth century"), and Gwynn, `npnf213` 13666 (Aphrahat "seems sometimes to follow the Diatessaron"). The brief cut the Addai picture (Cut first 2) but kept the claim, which left it with no witness.
- **Change:** keep the clause in the essence, told inside its witness: "From the flood to the year 410, they sang their teaching in hymns and counted among them men and women vowed to God for life, and the story Edessa later told of its own founding pictures them hearing the Gospel read as one story, which Tatian had woven out of the four."
- **Do not say (add):** "they heard the Gospel as one story" in the narrator's own voice → "Edessa's later founding story pictures them hearing it read as one story" (Addai 65 is the only old witness).

**3. MODERN ONLY.** "Some men and women took a vow, before baptism, to live unmarried and holy for God while serving in the church."
- The vow before baptism holds. Hallock, `aphrahat_demonstrations-2-7_hallock1932.txt` 57: "warn all (who are in) the covenant of God before baptism, and to those who choose for themselves virginity and holiness, young men and virgins". Parisot's Latin agrees (21939-21942).
- "While serving in the church" appears in no line of Aphrahat. It comes from Chabot's modern note on later synod acts, `synodicon-orientale_...chabot1902.txt` 16183-16185 ("les femmes qui étaient astreintes par des engagements au service de l'Eglise"). That note is about a different text and a later use of the word. Parisot's own gloss (3292-3293) says only "qui se Deo ad servandam continentiam pacto devoverunt".
- **Change:** "to live unmarried and holy for God while serving in the church." → "to live unmarried and holy for God."
- **Do not say (add):** "serving in the church" for Aphrahat's covenant → leave out (Chabot's note on later synods only).

**4. MODERN ONLY.** "In Nisibis, Ephrem taught in hymns: a single voice sang the verses, and the people sang a line back."
- The print shows a stanza and then a line marked "R." (`npnf213` 13725-13734). No held old line says who sang which part. Sozomen says only that the Syrians sang Ephrem's odes (32101-32103), and that Harmonius "delivered [his verses] to the choirs" (32088). The idea of one singer and a people answering rests on modern description. The only performance story in the file is Gwynn's (11249-11254), and the outline bars it.
- **Change:** "In Nisibis, Ephrem taught in hymns: a single voice sang the verses, and the people sang a line back." → "In Nisibis, Ephrem taught in hymns, with a refrain between the verses."
- "So the people sang:" can stand on Sozomen 32101-32103 once this is changed.
- **Do not say (add):** "a single voice sang the verses, and the people sang a line back" → "with a refrain between the verses".

**5. SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** "Sozomen, a Greek church historian of the next century, says the king believed a charge that their bishop was a friend of Rome's Christian emperor."
- Source, `npnf202_...xml` 28889-28891: "charged him with being a friend of the Cæsar of the Romans, and with communicating the affairs of the Persians to him. Sapor believed these accusations".
- The brief adds "Christian" to Sozomen's report. That reason, the emperor's shared faith, belongs to the martyr acts (Braun 1571-1575), not to Sozomen. The brief also drops half of the charge: that he passed on Persia's affairs.
- **Change:** "a friend of Rome's Christian emperor." → "a friend of the Roman emperor and passed Persia's affairs to him."
- **Do not say (add):** "Rome's Christian emperor" in Sozomen's report → "the Roman emperor" (Sozomen 28889-28891).

**6. ASSUMPTION ADDED.** "Rival teachers preached in these cities, and in his prose writings Ephrem answered 'the three of them' by name."
- The answer holds. `ephraim_prose-refutations_mitchell1912-1921.txt` 584: "one more refutation against the three of them (i.e., Marcion, Mani, and Bardaisan)".
- That the teachers preached in Edessa and Nisibis is in no held line. Ephrem answers their teachings and their followers (1205); he does not place Marcion or Mani in these cities. Sozomen's hymn contest is with Bardaisan's son only (32085-32097).
- **Change:** "Rival teachers preached in these cities, and in his prose writings Ephrem answered" → "In his prose writings Ephrem answered rival teachers, 'the three of them,' by name."
- **Do not say (add):** "Rival teachers preached in these cities" → leave out.

**7. ASSUMPTION ADDED.** "He let 'the priests, the leaders and all the holy covenant' go about freely and without fear, the same vowed order Aphrahat had written for."
- The grant holds. Chabot 16164-16166: "que les prêtres, les chefs et toute la sainte alliance circulassent en toute liberté et sans crainte."
- "The same vowed order" is an identification the source does not make. The editor's note on this very line, 16176-16177, reads it more widely: "Le mot qyama « fœdus » paraît désigner ici tout le clergé y compris les moines." What does hold is that the word is the same: qyama here (16177), and Aphrahat's "filii foederis" (Parisot 3292).
- **Change:** "the same vowed order Aphrahat had written for." → "the same word, covenant, that Aphrahat had used for the vowed."
- **Do not say (add):** "the same vowed order Aphrahat had written for" → "the same word, covenant, that Aphrahat had used" (Chabot reads it here as the whole clergy).

## SUMMARY HOLDS (with evidence)

- "Those Christians spoke Syriac, and with the churches of Nisibis they made up ... two frontier cities between the Roman and Persian empires." Theodoret, `npnf203` 11065-11067: Nisibis "lies on the confines of the realms of Persia and of Rome". Sozomen 32053-32054: "the learning and language of the Syrians". No held line calls Edessa itself a frontier city. Its tower "called that of the Persians" (Chronicle 53) is the nearest. Not blocking.
- "Across the border in Persia, a teacher named Aphrahat wrote letters for the church." `npnf213` 28517-28518: "written for our brethren and beloved, the children of the Church of God". He dates by "the thirty-fifth year of the Persian King" (28561).
- "They were called the sons and daughters of the covenant." Parisot 18151: "foederis filios"; Hallock 62: "sons of a covenant". Aphrahat counts "young men and virgins" in the covenant (Hallock 57). The feminine name is found only later (John of Ephesus 14152), but it fairly fills out his term. Not blocking.
- "One of his letters asks them to keep watch at night, to sing psalms and pray, to give their goods to the poor, and not to live with the other sex, so that lust will not draw them back." `npnf213` 25803-25808: "wakeful and vigilant and sing psalms and pray ... give them to the poor ... they dwell alone". 25835-25843: "not be made wanton by lust". Hallock 62: the adversary would "lead us back to the nature from which ... we have separated ourselves".
- "In his hymn for a siege by a Persian army, the river threatens the city, and Ephrem sets that flood beside Noah's." `npnf213` 13710 ("The Siege of Nisibis"), 13751 ("the river threatens me"), 13725-13732 (Noah). Hymns I-III never name the enemy (searched 13700-14700). That the besiegers were Persian stands on Theodoret 11124-11127 ("besieged by the Persian Army") and S2, without joining his siege to this hymn.
- "In Persia, the Christians had grown in number, and this angered the Magi, the priests who guarded the Persian religion." Sozomen 28873-28877. The brief leaves out "began to form churches, and appointed priests" (outline Cut first 28).
- "The king taxed the Christians heavily, hoping that want would make them give up their faith." Sozomen 28891-28896: "excessive taxes ... hoping that, by the want of necessaries ... they might be compelled to abjure their religion".
- "Marcion taught a Stranger God ...; Mani taught two first Roots, Light and Darkness; and Bardaisan said the world was made from five first beings." Mitchell 584, 1605 ("He is not our Creator"); 431 ("two Roots"), 640, 787; 606-608, 917 ("five Entities"), 636 ("creatures are made from Entities").
- "Ephrem died at Edessa in 373, and the Syrians, Sozomen says, went on singing his hymns." Chronicle 103 (year 684, June). Barhadbeshabba 20435-20438: "se retira à Édesse, où il passa le reste de sa vie". Sozomen 32101-32103.
