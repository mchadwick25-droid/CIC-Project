# Outline check: Syriac Christianity (`syriac-edessa-nisibis`)

## My own account of the heart (written before reading `Outline.md`)

From the census entry (`Card.existing.json`), the source sheet, the built world's six gravities and what the subject is known for:

- **Essence:** the Syriac-speaking Christians of Edessa and Nisibis, on the frontier between Rome and Persia, from about 200 to 410: a church that taught its faith in hymns the congregation sang, and that kept, inside the ordinary congregation, a covenant of men and women vowed to God for life.
- **Who they were:** "Syriac Christianity"; the congregations of the two cities, their sons and daughters of the covenant, the poet-deacon Ephrem, the Persian writer Aphrahat.
- **When and where:** about 200 (the flood of 201 is the first hard record) to the synod of 410; Edessa on the Roman side, Nisibis on the border, handed to Persia in 363.
- **What they faced:** rival teachers in the same language and cities (Bardaisan, Marcion, Mani); Persian sieges of Nisibis; Shapur II's persecution of the Christians inside Persia; the loss of Nisibis to Persia.
- **What they believed:** the church's faith, taught through symbol and hymn (Noah's bow over the besieged walls); the Gospel heard as one woven story (Tatian's Diatessaron); a faith defined against its rivals ("their fast is like our fast, but their faith is not like our Faith").
- **How they practiced:** hymns with refrains the people sang back; fasting, vigils, psalms, charity; a vow, often at baptism, to live unmarried and holy while serving in the church; reading the one-story Gospel.
- **What held them together:** the sung teaching (symbol and hymn) and the covenant life (the two Primary gravities).
- **What came of them:** Ephrem's hymns sung on in Syriac churches (heaviest); Nisibis and its church carried into the Persian church, second see at the synod of 410; the covenant named in 410 and still in the sixth century; Edessa keeping the record of Persia's martyrs.
