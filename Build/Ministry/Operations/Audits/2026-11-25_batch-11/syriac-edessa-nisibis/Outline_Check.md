# Outline check: Syriac Christianity (`syriac-edessa-nisibis`)

## My own account of the heart (written before reading `Outline.md`)

From the census entry (`Card.existing.json`), the source sheet, the built world's six gravities and what the subject is known for:

- **Essence:** the Syriac-speaking Christians of Edessa and Nisibis, on the frontier between Rome and Persia, from about 200 to 410: a church that taught its faith in hymns the congregation sang, and that kept, inside the ordinary congregation, a covenant of men and women vowed to God for life.
- **Who they were:** "Syriac Christianity"; the congregations of the two cities, their sons and daughters of the covenant, the poet-deacon Ephrem, the Persian writer Aphrahat.
- **When and where:** about 200 (the flood of 201 is the first hard record) to the synod of 410; Edessa on the Roman side, Nisibis on the border, handed to Persia in 363.
- **What they faced:** rival teachers in the same language and cities (Bardaisan, Marcion, Mani); Persian sieges of Nisibis; Shapur II's persecution of the Christians inside Persia; the loss of Nisibis to Persia.
- **What they believed:** the church's faith, taught through symbol and hymn (Noah's bow over the besieged walls); the Gospel heard as one woven story (Tatian's Diatessaron); a faith defined against its rivals ("their fast is like our fast, but their faith is not like our Faith").
- **How they practiced:** hymns with refrains the people sang back; fasting, vigils, psalms, charity; a vow, often at baptism, to live unmarried and holy while serving in the church; reading the one-story Gospel.
- **What held them together:** the sung teaching (symbol and hymn) and the covenant life (the two Primary gravities).
- **What came of them:** Ephrem's hymns sung on in Syriac churches (heaviest); Nisibis and its church carried into the Persian church, second see at the synod of 410; the covenant named in 410 and still in the sixth century; Edessa keeping the record of Persia's martyrs.

**Against the outline.** The outline's heart matches this account in substance: the same essence, the same two Primary gravities, the same pressures and the same hand-off. It leaves out no major contribution. (The sixth gravity, unsettled authority, is left out with a reason under Leave out, and the 410 rule of one bishop to a city still touches it.) No heart finding. But the approved heart's "Ephrem's hymns were sung on" does not survive the Cut first list (B2).

## BLOCKING

**B1. Beat 3 makes Theodoret's siege and Ephrem's "third peril" one event.** Theodoret's siege is the one where the dammed river breaks the walls. In his account Jacob is alive, praying in the church and then cursing the army from a tower (`npnf203...xml` 11142-11177). Ephrem's hymns tell it differently. The city went through "three dark seasons of siege", and in a later one its first priest was already "dead and buried within her" (Hymn XIII, `npnf213...xml` 14695-14704, 14774-14787). The Chronicle dates Jacob's death to 649 of the Greeks, 337/338 (`chronicle-of-edessa_cowper.txt` 77). Only the modern editor joins the dam to the third siege (Gwynn, 11229-11231, Dominant Modern Reconstruction). Cut first 1 removes the disagreement, so the beat would then tell that join as the record. That moves a detail onto the wrong event (fault 3).
Correction: replace beat 3's Theodoret sentence ("When a Persian army besieged the city, Theodoret ... built them up again (E4, 11151-11160; Documented as his account).") with: "When a Persian army besieged the city in what Ephrem counts its third peril (13839-13840), his hymn has the city cry that 'mounds and weapons and waves encircle me ... the river threatens me' (13746-13751; own words, Documented), and sets that flood beside Noah's:" Then Q1 follows as now. Under Who tells it, change Theodoret's line to: "**Theodoret** (old; about a century after): card only (the dammed river, Jacob's prayer, the gnats)." Add to Do not say: "Ephrem's hymn was written for the siege Theodoret tells (the dammed river, Jacob's prayer)" → never join them, and give the siege no year.

**B2. The heaviest impact leaves the story.** The approved heart's "Ephrem's hymns were sung on" (What came of it 1) is carried only by Sozomen's sentence in beat 5. Cut first 5 removes that sentence. That drops part of the approved heart.
Correction: replace Cut first 5 with: "5. Beat 5's Bardaisan's son and the meters: **Leave out** (the Ephrem page tells it). Keep one clause from Sozomen 32101-32103: from then on the Syrians sang Ephrem's hymns. Saves about 15."

**B3. The Connection rests on what Cut first 6 removes, and its rank is wrong.** In the list of sees, Nisibis comes after Seleucia-Ctesiphon and after Beit Lapat, so it is third, not second (`synodicon...chabot1902.txt` 17716-17726). Hosea of Nisibis is the "second metropolitan" (17746) and the third to sign (17861).
Correction: replace the Connection field's text after "**Continued as the early Persian Church of the East (Documented).**" with: "What passed: Nisibis, Ephrem's city, went over to Persia in 363 (Socrates 11697; beat 5). The Persian Christians Aphrahat wrote for, with their covenant, came through the persecution into the church that met in 410. That year the king freed 'the priests, the leaders and all the holy covenant' (16159-16166), and forty bishops agreed on one bishop to a city (16521-16530). The listener needs to hear that the vowed order Aphrahat wrote for is the same one the 410 grant names. The writer shows this through what the king ordered and what the bishops agreed, not through a 'this mattered because' line. Card: Nisibis listed after Seleucia-Ctesiphon and Beit Lapat, its bishop Hosea the second metropolitan and the third to sign (17716-17746, 17861)." Make the same rank fix in What came of it 2, in beat 6 ("listed the bishop of Nisibis second among their metropolitans"), in Cut first 6 and in the heart's "What came of them" ("set Nisibis among its metropolitan sees"). Add to Do not say: "Nisibis was the Persian church's second see" → "the second of its metropolitans, after Beit Lapat".
