# Second Readings, Blind (C): B11-27 to B11-38

## B11-27
Source: `augustine_arian-sermon-collatio-maximinum-pl42-lat_migne1886.txt`, lines 16422-16436, Latin.

Read: "si perroganti utrum accipiam Evangelium dixero, Omnia mea dimisi, patrem, matrem, uxorem, filios, aurum, argentum, manducare, bibere, delicias, voluptates; hoc tibi ad interrogata satis responsum puta, et beatum te futurum, si in me non fueris scandalizatus."

Rendering: If you keep asking me whether I accept the Gospel, and I answer, "I have left everything that was mine: father, mother, wife, children, gold, silver, eating, drinking, delights, pleasures," then take that as answer enough to your question. And know that you will be blessed if you do not stumble over me.

Note: the words are split across lines by the scan ("Evan- gelium", "bi- bere") and a stray marginal "A." stands between them; the text itself is clear. "Si in me non fueris scandalizatus" echoes Matthew 11:6 ("take offence at me", "stumble because of me").

## B11-28
Source: `manichaean-texts_dr-steins-turkish-khuastuanift-in-jras-1911_le-coq1911.txt`, lines 17347-17357, English (Le Coq's translation).

Read: "Every da>-, how many evil thoughts do we think! how many miserable words, that ought not to be spoken, do we speak! how many deeds, that ought not to be done, do we do!"

Rendering: Every day, how many evil thoughts we think! How many wretched words we speak that should never be spoken! How many deeds we do that should never be done!

Note: "da>-" is a scan error for "day".

## B11-29
Source: `npnf201_eusebius-church-history-life-of-constantine.xml`, lines 29119-29123, English (NPNF translation).

Read: "he said that it was not at all necessary to examine one's doctrine, but that each one should continue to hold what he believed. For he asserted that those who trusted in the Crucified would be saved, if only they were found doing good works."

Rendering: He said there was no need at all to examine anyone's teaching. Each person should keep holding what he believed. He claimed that those who trusted in the Crucified One would be saved, as long as they were found doing good works.

Note: the translator's footnote gives the Greek for "doctrine" as τὸν λόγον ("the account", "the reasoning"), so "examine one's doctrine" could also be read as "examine the argument".

## B11-30
Source: `tertullian_opera-pars3-lat_kroymann-csel47.txt`, lines 16287-16289, Latin.

Read: "quid non et in persecutionibus statim oblata acerra animam negatione lucraris? absit, inquis, absit!"

Rendering: Then why not, in times of persecution too, save your life by denying the faith the moment the incense box is held out to you? "Never," you say, "never!"

Note: the scan reads "statira", a clear misreading of "statim" ("at once"). The "acerra" is the box of incense offered for sacrifice to the gods.

## B11-31
Source: `npnf203_theodoret-jerome-gennadius-rufinus.xml`, lines 31511-31512, English (NPNF translation).

Read: "I led eight villages of Marcionists with their neighbourhood into the way of truth;"

Rendering: I led eight villages of Marcionites, and the land around them, into the way of truth.

## B11-32
Source: `mirbt_quellen-zur-geschichte-des-papsttums-3e-aufl-deu-lat_1911.txt`, lines 2790-2791, Latin.

Read: "quod essent soliti stato die ante lucem convenire carmenque Christo quasi deo dicere secum invicem"

Rendering: that they had been in the habit of meeting on a fixed day before dawn and singing a hymn to Christ as to a god, taking turns among themselves.

Note: the scan reads "sohti" for "soliti" ("accustomed"). "Carmen ... dicere secum invicem" means to recite or sing a song in alternation, one side answering the other. The clause depends on what comes before ("they declared that the whole of their fault or error was ...").

## B11-33
Source: `anf07_lactantius-apostolic-constitutions-didache-liturgies.xml`, lines 12700-12706, English (ANF translation of the Didache).

Read: "There are two ways, one of life and one of death; but a great difference between the two ways. The way of life, then, is this: First, thou shalt love God who made thee; second, thy neighbour as thyself;"

Rendering: There are two ways, one of life and one of death, and there is a great difference between the two. This is the way of life. First, you shall love God, who made you. Second, you shall love your neighbour as yourself.

Note: the passage is broken up by the translator's footnotes in the file; the running text is clear. The word count (40) ends here, before "and all things whatsoever thou wouldst ...".

## B11-34
Source: `justin-martyr_first-apology-grc_rauschen1911.txt`, lines 1759-1762, Greek.

Read: "Καὶ τῇ τοῦ ἡλίου λεγομένῃ ἡμέρᾳ πάντων κατὰ πόλεις ἢ ἀγροὺς μενόντων ἐπὶ τὸ αὐτὸ συνέλευσις γίνεται, καὶ τὰ ἀπομνημονεύματα τῶν ἀποστόλων ἢ τὰ συγγράμματα τῶν προφητῶν ἀναγινώσκεται, μέχρις ἐγχωρεῖ."

Rendering: And on the day called Sunday, all who live in the cities or in the countryside gather together in one place. The memoirs of the apostles or the writings of the prophets are read, for as long as time allows.

Note: "the day called the day of the sun" is the literal sense of τῇ τοῦ ἡλίου λεγομένῃ ἡμέρᾳ.

## B11-35
Source: `npnf213_gregory-great-ephraim-syrus-aphrahat.xml`, lines 25950-25953, English (NPNF translation of Aphrahat).

Read: "To a royal husband am I betrothed, and Him do I serve; and if I leave His service and serve thee, my betrothed will be wroth with me, and will write me a letter of divorce, and will send me away from His house;"

Rendering: I am promised in marriage to a royal husband, and I serve him. If I leave his service and serve you, the one I am promised to will be angry with me. He will write me a letter of divorce and send me away from his house.

Note: the words are spoken by a consecrated virgin to a monk who proposes to live with her.

## B11-36
Source: `npnf213_gregory-great-ephraim-syrus-aphrahat.xml`, lines 13733-13734, English (NPNF translation of Ephrem).

Read: "Stretch forth Thy bow against the flood, for lo! it has lifted up its waves against our walls!"

Rendering: Stretch out your bow against the flood, for look! It has raised its waves against our walls!

Note: this is the response line ("R.") of the hymn.

## B11-37
Source: `ephraim_prose-refutations_mitchell1912-1921.txt`, line 1205, English (Mitchell's translation).

Read: "For their works are like our works as their fast is like our fast, but their faith is not like our Faith."

Rendering: Their works are like our works, just as their fasting is like our fasting. But their faith is not like our faith.

Note: the surrounding line has gaps marked "..." where the manuscript is damaged, but this sentence is complete.

## B11-38
Source: `synodicon-orientale_recueil-de-synodes-nestoriens-syr-fra_chabot1902.txt`, lines 16165-16166, French (Chabot's translation from Syriac).

Read: "que les prêtres, les chefs et toute la sainte alliance circulassent en toute liberté et sans crainte."

Rendering: that the priests, the leaders and the whole holy covenant should move about in full freedom and without fear.

Note: the scan has a footnote marker ("alliance®)") after "alliance"; the footnote itself was not read. "La sainte alliance" is Chabot's rendering of the Syriac name for the consecrated "covenant" (the sons and daughters of the covenant), not an alliance in the political sense. The stated count (10 words) ends at "alliance"; I rendered the whole clause so it has its verb.
