## alexandria-catechetical
Heart: In Alexandria, teachers led learners step by step, from plain story to deeper sense, and Dionysius showed that teaching by examining a rival book openly with the village teachers until its leader changed his mind.

## antiochene-church-third-century
Heart: Antioch's church, bound to the churches of the empire by letters, judged and expelled its own bishop Paul for his teaching and conduct, and its line ran on through Lucian to the quarrel over Arius.
Findings: 0

## roman-church-third-century
Heart: Rome's large, mixed church argued over God, forgiveness and marriage, and when persecution made many fall, it chose, against Novatian's stricter party, to take the fallen back through repentance.
Findings: 0

## apocryphal-and-pseudepigraphal-literature
Heart: Christians read gospels and acts carrying the apostles' names, and bishops and teachers judged them against what had been handed down, even as some, like Thecla's story, went on being read.
Findings: 0

## ebionite-nazoraean-current
Heart: Jews who believed Jesus was the Christ and kept the Law are known only through church writers who argued against them, and those writers judged them, in Jerome's words, neither Jews nor Christians.
Findings: 0

## greek-apologists-second-century
Heart: Greek-writing Christians of the second century answered charges of atheism, cannibalism and incest by pleading with emperors and pagans to judge them by how they lived and what they worshipped.
Findings: 0

## latin-apologists
Heart: Latin-writing Christians of Rome and Africa answered educated pagans out of the pagans' own books, as in Minucius Felix's Octavius, where Caecilius yields to Octavius's argument.
Findings: 0

## marcion-marcionism
Heart: The Marcionites were a lasting rival church, known only through their enemies, who held to a good Father above the world's maker and kept their faith through persecution and imperial ban until bishops won their villages over.
Findings: 0

## post-apostolic-house-church
Heart: In the century after the apostles, Christians met household by household, taught a way of life before baptism, gave thanks over bread and cup under elders, bishops and deacons, and were held together by letters.
Findings: 0

## syriac-edessa-nisibis
Heart: The Syriac-speaking churches of Edessa and Nisibis sang their faith in hymns, kept vowed members of the covenant, and endured persecution on the Roman-Persian frontier until the Persian church met in synod in 410.
Findings: 0

## latin-pastoral-congregational-christianity
Heart: The Latin churches of Roman Africa lived as congregations around one bishop, and through persecution, plague, schism and invasion the record keeps asking what bishop and people owed each other.
Findings: 0

## manichaeism
Heart: Mani's followers held that Light and Darkness were two powers now mixed in the world, lived as Elect and Hearers, and spread from Mesopotamia to Africa and China though hunted by kings and emperors.
