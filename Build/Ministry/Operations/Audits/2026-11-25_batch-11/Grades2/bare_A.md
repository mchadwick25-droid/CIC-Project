## alexandria-catechetical
Heart: In Alexandria, from about 180 into the 200s, Christians learned their faith from teachers in a school, led step by step and taught to read Scripture for a deeper sense beneath the plain story, as Clement and Origen taught and Origen's pupils carried on. Their heir Dionysius met Nepos's literal teaching of a thousand-year earthly reign with a three-day public examination, and Coracion gave it up.

## antiochene-church-third-century
Heart: In the later third century the church of Antioch was one church under a bishop, bound by letters to churches across the empire. When its bishop Paul of Samosata would not confess that the Son of God came down from heaven, the bishops of the East met again and again until they put him out, and a line was later drawn from Antioch through Lucian to Arius.

## roman-church-third-century
Heart: In the first half of the 200s the Christians of Rome were a large, mixed church under a bishop the people chose, and they argued in public over how to speak of Father and Son, whether to take back grave sinners, and whom a woman of rank might marry, until a stricter party broke away in 251.

## apocryphal-and-pseudepigraphal-literature
Heart: In the second to fourth centuries Christians wrote and read gospels and tales of the apostles under the apostles' own names, books that never entered the New Testament. Congregations read them aloud and argued over them, and bishops and teachers judged them against what had been handed down from the apostles.

## ebionite-nazoraean-current
Heart: The Ebionites and Nazoraeans were Jews who believed Jesus was the Christ and went on keeping the Law of Moses. Their own books are lost, so we meet them only through church writers who mostly argued against them and disagreed about what they believed of Jesus, until Jerome judged them neither Jews nor Christians.

## greek-apologists-second-century
Heart: In the second century, Christians who wrote in Greek put written defenses of their faith before emperors and pagans. Accused of godlessness, cannibalism and incest, they answered that they worshipped the one God, prayed for the emperors, and asked to be judged by how they lived.

## marcion-marcionism
Heart: The Marcionites were churches that followed Marcion after Rome cast him out, holding that Jesus came from a good Father above the God who made the world, with a Scripture of a cut-down Luke and ten letters of Paul. For about three hundred years they kept their own presbyters and martyrs, and everything known of them comes from their enemies.

## post-apostolic-house-church
Heart: In the hundred years after the apostles, Christians in Antioch, Asia Minor and Rome met household by household. They taught newcomers a way of life before baptism, gave thanks over bread and cup on the Lord's day, were led by elders, bishops and deacons, and were held together by letters and messengers between churches.

## syriac-edessa-nisibis
Heart: The Syriac-speaking Christians of Edessa and Nisibis, two frontier cities between Rome and Persia, sang their teaching in hymns and counted among them men and women vowed to God for life, from the flood of 201 to the synod of 410.

## latin-pastoral-congregational-christianity
Heart: From the 250s to 430, the Latin-speaking Christians of Roman Africa lived as congregations around one bishop who taught them, fed their poor and decided with them whom to take back after sin. Persecution, plague and a rival church pressed on them, and the record keeps asking what a bishop and his people owed each other in danger.

## manichaeism
Heart: The Manichaeans followed Mani, a prophet of Babylonia, holding that Light and Darkness were two powers now mixed in the world and to be parted in the end. Their Elect lived in strict purity while Hearers served them, and hunted by Persian kings and Roman emperors they spread from Roman Africa to China.
