## 1. The test (tradition)

- **Who they were:** filled. "Christians of Rome and Roman Africa who, from around 200 to the reign of the emperor Diocletian, defended their faith in Latin to the educated who despised it."
- **What happened to them:** filled. They faced rumours of murdered infants and incest, and torture to make them deny their faith. Arnobius had to win the bishop over. The church at Nicomedia was torn down, and "an order laid every Christian, whatever his rank, open to torture."
- **What their faith was about:** filled. "keep one marriage, eat sober meals, and love one another as brothers"; "No temple holds God, who is unseen and everywhere, and the offering he wants is a good mind"; prayer for the emperors, and burying the poor and keeping orphans.
- **Why the connection matters:** filled, but thin. The listener can say that Lactantius built on Tertullian ("teaching the whole faith") and that Jerome later named all four to defend using pagan learning. The link to the wider church rests on that one Jerome paragraph. No change is needed.

## 2. The opening

The first three sentences give who (three friends, one a Christian, then Minucius Felix by name), where (the shore at Ostia, near Rome) and why there (a holiday walk to the sea, then an argument over a god's image). **When** is missing: "a court holiday at the grape harvest" gives no era. The era arrives in the next paragraph ("from around 200"). The listener is not lost about who or where. Not blocking.

"three friends ... their friend Minucius Felix": on one hearing, "their friend" made the listener wonder for a moment whether Minucius was a fourth man. It cleared up with "the three sat on the breakwater". MINOR, noted.

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing

1. "They defended the worst criminals, but tortured Christians who confessed, to make them deny it." The meaning lost: the listener heard that the lawyers themselves tortured people, and missed how the two halves of the contrast connect. MINOR. Change: "As lawyers they would defend the worst criminals, yet they had Christians who confessed tortured, to make them deny it." (The trust reader should confirm that "had ... tortured" matches the source's sense.)
2. "He said the torturer's task was turned around, to make Christians deny what they were." The meaning lost: a newcomer does not know what the torturer's usual task was, so "turned around" says nothing. It also sounds like a repeat of Octavius's point just before. MINOR. Change: "He said torture, used on others to make them confess, was turned around on Christians, to make them deny what they were." (This is for the trust reader to check against the Apology.)

Long but understood, so not counted: the 37-word closing sentence, and the Arnobius "hostages ... treaty" quote. That quote's plain sense follows it at once: "His seven books were his pledge, and he was let in."
