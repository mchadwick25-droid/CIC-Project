## 1. The test (tradition)

- **Who these people were:** filled. "Christians of Rome and Roman Africa who, from around 200 to Diocletian's persecution, defended their faith in Latin to the educated who despised it."
- **What happened to them:** filled. Torture into denial, the rumours, "Christian writings were burned and meeting places torn down", "the church was thrown down".
- **What their faith was about:** filled. "keep one marriage, eat sober meals, and love one another as brothers"; "No temple holds God, who is unseen and everywhere, and the offering he wants is a good mind."
- **Why the connection matters:** thin, but I can fill it. They answered the charges in Latin out of the pagans' own books, and Lactantius took the work on: "answering accusers was one thing and teaching the whole faith another, and he took on the second." I can say what they handed on. I cannot say where it went after Lactantius. Not counted as unfilled.

## 2. The opening

After three sentences I have who (Caecilius, Octavius the Christian, Minucius Felix), where (the shore at Ostia, near Rome) and why there (a court holiday walk). **When is missing.** "At the grape harvest" gives a season, not an era, and the century first comes in s5 ("from around 200"). I am not lost about who or where, so this does not block.

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing (MINOR)

1. s3: "Octavius, a Christian, rounded on their friend Minucius Felix for letting him bow to anointed stones, and the three sat on the breakwater to have it out." I have just heard two names. "Him" could be Minucius or Caecilius, and "rounded on" is an idiom a second-language listener will not know. Lost: Octavius scolds Minucius for letting Caecilius worship.
2. s12: "As lawyers, he and Minucius had believed them too, defending the worst criminals but torturing Christians into denial." On one hearing, lawyers do the torturing. Lost: how advocates took part in torture, and why the goal was denial.
3. s19: "He said torture was meant to force denial, not confession." I don't know that torture normally forced a confession, so the point of the inversion is lost. (It is also tell 2 below.)
4. s32: "Finding few pupils, he turned to writing, and he says he was teaching there when the church was thrown down." "The church" sounds like the Church as a whole. Lost: that a church building was destroyed, and by whom.

## 4. Bare statements (BLOCKING)

1. **"Diocletian's persecution"** (s5), then "In Diocletian's reign" (s22), with the burned writings (s27) and "the church was thrown down" (s32). The story names a persecution and its acts but never says who Diocletian was or what he ordered. The burnings and the torn-down meeting places are not tied to him either. **Change:** in s27 or s32, say in a clause that the emperor's persecution was what burned the writings and tore down the meeting places, as far as the outline holds it. If the outline does not hold it, cut "to Diocletian's persecution" from s5 and give the end date as the old sources allow.

## 6. Part 1 tells (BLOCKING)

1. **s18: "Minucius wrote it after Octavius died, at a date no one knows."** The clause only says what is missing, and the gap is not the story. **Change:** "Minucius wrote it after Octavius died."
2. **s19: "He said torture was meant to force denial, not confession."** This is a "not X but Y" turn. **Change:** tell the inversion plainly, which also fixes failing sentence 3: "He said other accused men were tortured to make them confess, but Christians were tortured to make them deny." Keep only the wording the outline's source line holds.

### Lines that sound machine-made (notes, not blocking)

- **Stacked appositive glosses:** "a pleader, or court lawyer", "rhetoric, the art of public speaking" (twice in effect), "Nicomedia, a Greek city in Bithynia", "An apology was a defense speech for the accused". Each gloss is fine alone. Four in the same pattern sound like a template filling in definitions. Work one or two into what happens.
- **s5–s6, the essence paragraph:** "He was one of the Latin Apologists, Christians of Rome and Roman Africa who…" The voice drops out of the scene into a glossary entry. Keep the content, but let the narrator say it as a step on from the scene.
- **s16: "Octavius has conquered him, and he has conquered his error."** The neat mirror sounds crafted. If it is the dialogue's own turn, it can stay. If it is the narrator's, plain it out.
- **s29: "His seven books were his pledge, and he was let in."** A tidy echo that closes off the quote. It is acceptable as the quote's plain sense, but it sits on the edge of a summing-up beat.
