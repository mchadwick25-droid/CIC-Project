## Worded by me

- **Fix 1, Serapis gloss:** "a god whose image Caecilius saw as the three walked along the shore toward the sea (MinLat 220-223; ANF4 17252-17256)". MinLat 220: "cum diluculo ad mare inambulando litore pergeremus"; ANF4 17252-17253: "when in the early morning we were going towards the sea along the shore". Outline, Words to explain.
- **Fix 7, settled from the sources:** no old source dates the Octavius. The two old witnesses who place Minucius order him differently: Lactantius names him before Tertullian (ANF7 4470, then 4565-4569), and Jerome's Lives put him after (NPNF2-3 40644, chapter LVIII, after Tertullian at 40491). The dates on offer, "196?" (NPNF2-3 40646) and "circa A.D. 210" (ANF4 18998-19001), are editors' notes and stay out. So the story carries no date clause. Beat 2 now ends "Minucius wrote it after Octavius had died (ANF4 17203)." ANF4 17203: "that remarkable and holy man, when he departed". The Length line drops "the uncertainty of the Octavius's date (about 15)". Outline, Story beats 2; Length.
- **Fix 9, what they left the church:** What came of it 2 moves from Card to Story, after beat 5 and before the Ending: "Later, asked why he quoted pagan books, Jerome named all four together. Their books, he said, were full of the pagans' own learning." NPNF2-6 16477-16480 (Magnus asks why he quotes secular literature), 16704-16721 (Tertullian's Apology "contains all the wisdom of the world"; Minucius "ransacked all heathen literature"; Arnobius's and Lactantius's books hold "an epitome of Cicero's dialogues"). Witness: later, about 90 years after Lactantius wrote. Documented that Jerome says it. It is narration, not a quote, so no Jerome quote is used. Outline, What came of it 2; Length.
- **Fix 10, Diocletian's persecution:** a new Words to explain entry: "Diocletian was the emperor. Lactantius says his officers burned the Scriptures and levelled the church at Nicomedia, and next day an order made every Christian open to torture." ANF7 10079 (Diocletian chose three to share the empire with him), 10161 ("The books of the Holy Scriptures were found, and they were committed to the flames … levelled that very lofty edifice with the ground"), 10174 ("Next day an edict was published … they should be subjected to tortures"). Lactantius, On the Deaths of the Persecutors 7, 12-13: own words, contemporary, at Nicomedia. Documented. Eusebius confirms the edicts ran everywhere, ordering "that the churches be leveled to the ground and the Scriptures be destroyed by fire" (NPNF2-1 43486-43491; contemporary; Documented). The tie to Arnobius's burned writings and torn-down meeting places (ANF6 48676-48678) is by time only: it rests on Jerome's dating (NPNF2-3 41022-41025), which his Chronicle contradicts (JerChron 45066). As a cause it is Inferential-Thin, so the entry says never to claim the edicts caused them. It also stays apart from the ANF7 4487 sentence, which fix 8 keeps free of a cause. Names now give Diocletian as "the emperor; one line". The Leave out line on the edicts now ends "beyond the one line under Words to explain". The Length line counts the new gloss. Outline, Words to explain; Names; Leave out; Length.
