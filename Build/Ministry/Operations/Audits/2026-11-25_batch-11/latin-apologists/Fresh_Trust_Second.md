## BLOCKING

**10. "As lawyers, he and Minucius had believed them too."** ASSUMPTION ADDED.
- Source: Octavius speaks in the first person plural and never names Minucius. ANF4 18825-18837: "Believe us ourselves when penitent, for we also were the same as you … We, however, when we undertook to defend and protect some sacrilegious and incestuous persons, and even parricides, did not think that these (Christians) were to be heard at all." The Latin is the same, MinLat 3180-3182: "nos tamen cum sacrilegos aliquos et incestos, parricidas etiam defendendos et tuendos suscipiebamus."
- The source gives a "we" who once believed the charges and who took on the defence of criminals. Naming Minucius as one of that "we" is a connection the text does not make. That Minucius was a pleader is true (ANF7 4470; NPNF2-3 40646-40647), and he calls Octavius "my companion in my mistakes" (ANF4 17213-17214). But the Octavius never says that Minucius is in this "we", or that he believed these charges, or that he tortured Christians.
- Exact change: "He and others like him, Octavius says, had once believed them too, and as lawyers had defended the worst criminals but tortured Christians who confessed, to make them deny it." This joins sentences 10 and 11 (about 4 words added). If length must stay level, use: "Octavius says that he and his fellows, as lawyers, had once believed them too."

## The heart

It is the right heart. The sources show a group of Latin writers who were not one school: a Roman pleader, a Carthaginian presbyter, and two African teachers of rhetoric. They answered the same charges of secret crimes (ANF4 17645-17669; ANF3 1947-1950) for learned readers who scorned Christian plain speech (ANF7 4456). They used the pagans' own books (ANF4 19480; NPNF2-6 16704-16719). They are tied together by the later ones reading the earlier (ANF7 4470, 4569) and by Jerome's teacher and pupil (NPNF2-3 41033). The essence is told in full by about word 140, inside the first minute. The opening scene runs 74 words, and the essence paragraph ends at word 142. The heart is on time.
