# Outline: The Latin Apologists (`latin-apologists`, tradition)

Lines are file lines in `cic/texts/`; file abbreviations as in `Source_Sheet.md` (ANF3, ANF4, ANF6, ANF7, NPNF2-1, NPNF2-3, NPNF2-6, MinLat, ArnLat, JerChron, Monc3, LactLat, Mansi 8).

## The heart

- **The essence:** Christians of Roman Africa and Rome, among them a pleader in the Roman courts and teachers of public speaking, who from around 200 to the persecution under Diocletian argued their faith in Latin to the educated people who despised it. They answered the charges against Christians from the pagans' own books, for readers who, Lactantius says, scorned the Scriptures for their plain speech. (Documented: ANF4 19475-19483; ANF7 4456, 4470; NPNF2-3 40493-40499, 41022-41025)
- **Who they were:** "the Latin Apologists" (census name; informal "The Case Made in Latin"); gloss *apology* as a defense speech. Minucius Felix, a pleader at Rome (ANF7 4470; NPNF2-3 40646-40649); Tertullian at Carthage (NPNF2-3 40493-40499); Arnobius, teacher of rhetoric at Sicca (NPNF2-3 41022-41025); Lactantius, teacher of rhetoric called to Nicomedia (NPNF2-3 41033-41038). Not one school: the later ones read the earlier (ANF7 890, 4470, 4565-4569) and Jerome makes Lactantius Arnobius's pupil (NPNF2-6 16713-16716). Documented.
- **When and where:** Carthage around 200 (Jerome: Tertullian flourished under Severus and Caracalla, NPNF2-3 40498-40499; Widely Accepted); Rome and Ostia, undated (the Octavius; Contested); Sicca in Africa and Nicomedia under Diocletian (NPNF2-3 41022-41038; Documented).
- **What they faced:** charges of secret crimes (ANF4 17582-17670; ANF3 1946-1950); torture to force denial (ANF3 1956-1958; ANF4 18839-18845); the claim that the world went to ruin since Christians came (ANF6 39480-39515); scorn for Christian writing as plain and rough (ANF7 4456); writings burned and meeting-places torn down (ANF6 48676-48678; ANF7 4487). Documented. What ended or changed them: the record held here gives no end; the last of them wrote while the persecution ran.
- **What they believed:** one God, unseen and present everywhere, whom no temple holds; the true offering a good mind (ANF4 19058-19076, 19115-19121); Christ to be worshipped as God (ANF6 40681-40686); rising again (ANF4 17724-17742, as the charge gives it). Documented.
- **How they practiced:** prayer together, for emperors, peace, even enemies (ANF3 3872-3877; ANF6 48676-48684); a monthly free gift for burying the poor and keeping orphans, the old, the shipwrecked and prisoners (ANF3 3895-3907); a sober common meal with hymns (ANF3 3953-3975; ANF4 19018-19026); calling one another brothers (ANF4 19040-19044). Documented.
- **What held them together:** answering the charges in public, and arguing with the philosophers' own weapons (ANF4 19480-19481; MinLat 4396-4397): the pagans' own books and reason, set before the learned in their own Latin (ANF7 4456; NPNF2-6 16704-16720). Documented.
- **What came of them:** Lactantius named the earlier ones, granted what each had done, and judged it had not been enough, setting out to teach the whole faith (ANF7 890, 4456-4470, 4565-4571); Jerome later named them together to defend a Christian's use of pagan learning (NPNF2-6 16477-16480, 16704-16720). Documented.
- **On the slate's "a language with no words for it yet":** the old sources hold that the learned scorned Christian books for their plain speech and that truth lacked fit heralds (ANF7 4456), and that Latin believers mouthed the Greek word *monarchia* (ANF3 51176-51186). That these writers coined the church's Latin words is a modern claim and stays out. Augustine's later remark on "three persons" names no writer (card only).

## Setting

- **Time:** around 200 (Tertullian, NPNF2-3 40498-40499); "in Diocletian's reign" for Arnobius and Lactantius (NPNF2-3 41022-41025, 41033-41035); the destruction of churches (ANF7 4487). No year for the Apology (ANF3 1443-1446) or the Octavius.
- **Places:** Ostia, the seaside town Minucius and his friends reached from Rome, on a court holiday at vintage, early morning (ANF4 17247-17255). Carthage, "in the province of Africa" (NPNF2-3 40496). Sicca "in Africa" (NPNF2-3 41022-41023). Nicomedia, "a Greek city" (NPNF2-3 41037-41038), in Bithynia (ANF7 4487).
- **Tradition:** Latin-speaking Christians of the West; spoken word "the Latin Apologists".

## Words to explain

- **apology / Apologist:** a defense speech for the accused, addressed to judges (ANF3 1448-1453).
- **Serapis:** a god whose image stood by the road to the sea; Octavius calls such images carved stones, "anointed and crowned" (ANF4 17256-17271).
- **pleader:** a lawyer who argues cases in court (ANF7 4470: "pleaders"; LactLat 22702 "causidicos").
- **rhetoric:** the art of public speaking, taught to young men (JerChron 45066-45068; NPNF2-3 41022-41023).
- **the New Prophecy:** a movement whose followers held that the Paraclete, the Holy Spirit, was now giving a stricter rule; writing for it, Tertullian judged a second marriage unlawful and fasted more often than others (ANF4 5769-5773, 6895-6905; own words; Documented).
- **Marcion:** a teacher who brought in two Gods, the Creator and a higher one of his own (ANF3 22609-22612).
- **Nicomedia:** a Greek city in Bithynia, where Lactantius was called to teach in Diocletian's reign (NPNF2-3 41033-41038; ANF7 4487).

## Shape

A story in four places, in time order where the record allows: Ostia (undated), Carthage (around 200), Sicca and Nicomedia (Diocletian's reign).

## Openings

1. **Ostia (recommended).** On an autumn holiday from the courts, three friends walk the shore at Ostia in the early morning. One, Caecilius, sees an image of the god Serapis, lifts his hand to his mouth and kisses it. Octavius rounds on their friend Minucius, for letting a friend bow to stones, and the three sit down on the breakwater to have it out. (ANF4 17247-17273, 17321-17331; MinLat 220-224, 235-238)
2. **The oiled stone.** A teacher of speaking at Sicca later remembered how, whenever he saw a stone slick with olive oil, he fawned on it and begged it for favours. That man, Arnobius, became a Christian and wrote seven books against the gods. (ANF6 40657-40670; NPNF2-6 16713-16715) This reaches ahead; use only if Ostia is cut.

## Story beats

1. **The pagan's case on the breakwater.** Caecilius argues that Christians are gathered from the dregs, the unlearned and credulous women; that they meet at night; that a novice is made to kill an infant hidden in meal; that their feasts end in incest when a dog tied to the lamp puts out the light; that they worship a donkey's head and a crucified criminal; that they have no temples, altars or images. Minucius, who wrote it, gives the pagan the full case. *Reason:* he holds the old religion is ancient and useful (ANF4 17558-17563). *Explain:* Caecilius gives them as common report and cites the orator Fronto's speech for the feast (ANF4 17628-17632, 17653-17659); Octavius calls them fables never inquired into or proved (ANF4 18829-18832). ANF4 17582-17690; MinLat 822-828, 894, 914, 947. Documented (as a charge). Q1.
2. **Octavius answers, and Caecilius yields.** They had once believed the same things: as lawyers they defended the worst criminals but would not hear Christians, and tortured them to make them deny. Christians, he says, keep one marriage, eat sober meals, and love one another because they do not know how to hate. No temple can hold God; the offering he wants is a good mind. He beats the objectors with the philosophers' own weapons. Caecilius says Octavius has conquered him, and he has conquered his error; the rest can wait till morning, for the sun is setting. They part glad. Minucius wrote it after Octavius had died, and no one knows whether before Tertullian or after. ANF4 17195-17221, 18824-18848, 19018-19044, 19058-19076, 19475-19526; MinLat 4396-4397, 4410-4425, 4464-4466. Documented; date Contested.
3. **Tertullian at Carthage (short turn).** Around 200 he wrote his Apology to the governors who judged Christians. He met the same charges, and said the torturer was made to force denial, not confession. He set out what Christians did: prayed for the emperors, gave a free monthly gift to bury the poor and keep orphans, the old, the shipwrecked and prisoners; and that very love, he says, marked them out in pagan eyes: 'See, they say, how they love one another' (ANF3 3907-3913). Later, driven, Jerome says, by the envy and insults of the Roman clergy, he went over to the New Prophecy (NPNF2-3 40506-40508; motive Contested, in Jerome's voice), and he wrote against Marcion. ANF3 1946-1958, 3868-3913; NPNF2-3 40493-40513; ANF3 22609-22612. Documented; motive for his turn Contested (Montanism's job).
4. **The convert at Sicca.** In Diocletian's reign Arnobius taught rhetoric at Sicca. Jerome, writing about eighty years later, says dreams drove him, still a pagan, toward belief, and the bishop would not take him in. Q2. His books answer the claim that the world had gone to ruin since Christians came. He owns his own past: Q3. He asks why the Christians' writings are burned and their meeting-places torn down, where prayer is made for magistrates, armies, kings, friends and enemies. JerChron 45066-45076 (Monc3 21713-21718); NPNF2-3 41022-41025; ANF6 39480-39515, 40657-40670, 48676-48705. Documented that Jerome says it; story rests on Jerome alone; date Contested (Chronicle vs Lives).
5. **Lactantius at Nicomedia (short turn).** Arnobius's pupil, Jerome says, was called to teach rhetoric in Nicomedia, a Greek city, found few pupils and turned to writing. He was teaching there when, he says, the church was thrown down. In his seven books he looked back on those before him: the learned would not believe the Scriptures because the prophets spoke plainly, and truth, he wrote, lacked fit heralds; Tertullian's Apology, he granted, had pleaded the same cause in full; but answering accusers was one thing, and teaching the whole faith another, and that second task was the one he took on. NPNF2-3 41033-41038; ANF7 4456, 4487, 4565-4571; LactLat 22697-22699, 23225ff. Documented.

## What came of it (heaviest first)

1. Lactantius named Minucius and Tertullian, cited the Octavius by title (correcting it), and judged that the learned defenders before him had not been enough; Tertullian had answered accusers, and he set out to teach the whole faith (ANF7 890, 4456-4470, 4565-4571). *Defended:* the faith taught whole to the learned in their own terms.
2. Jerome, in a letter to Magnus (dated 397 by its editor), named Tertullian, Minucius, Arnobius and Lactantius together to defend a Christian's use of pagan books (NPNF2-6 16477-16480, 16704-16720). *Defended:* a Christian's right to use pagan learning. Card.
3. Jerome's verdicts on their style (NPNF2-6 14107-14116). Card.
4. Augustine on "three persons" for want of a fitter word (NPNF1-3 8211-8227); names no writer. Card, Inferential-Thin as a link.
5. A later Latin list of books not to be received, under Gelasius's name, sets the works of Tertullian, Lactantius, Commodian and Arnobius among the 'apocrypha' (Mansi 8, 17220-17233, 17301). Documented that the list says it; attribution Contested. Card.
6. Eusebius, writing in Greek in the early 300s, quotes Tertullian's Apology from a Greek translation and calls him well versed in Roman law (NPNF2-1 10242-10330; later, c. 120 years; Documented). Card.

## Connection to carry into the story

What passed was the case itself, and a judgment on how it had been made. Lactantius read the Octavius and cites it by name, though to correct it (ANF7 890). He says Tertullian's Apology had pleaded the cause in full, but only as an answer to accusers, and that he himself would teach the whole of the faith (ANF7 4565-4571). Jerome makes him Arnobius's pupil (NPNF2-3 41033-41035). The listener needs to hear that these men were not one school or church: they were linked by the later ones reading the earlier ones' books, and, by Jerome's account, by one teacher and his pupil. It comes through Lactantius looking back and naming them, not through a summing-up.

## Ending

1. **Lactantius on Minucius (recommended).** Of those known to him, Lactantius wrote, Minucius Felix stood well among pleaders, and his Octavius showed how fit a champion of truth he might have been, had he given himself wholly to it. Circles back to the breakwater at Ostia. ANF7 4470; LactLat 22701-22705.
2. **Lactantius's own line:** truth lacked fit heralds, and the learned who came over were not enough to defend it (ANF7 4456; LactLat 22697-22699). If used, cut the same line from beat 5.

## Do not say

- "Caecilius kissed the statue" → he raised his hand to his mouth and kissed it, toward the image (MinLat 223-224).
- Any year for the Octavius, or that it came before or after Tertullian → its date is unknown.
- "Minucius was a magistrate of Cirta", "written at Cirta", "Caecilius came from Cirta" → editors' guesses; leave out.
- "Tertullian coined/invented trinitas, persona, Latin words for God" → modern claim; leave out.
- "Tertullian was the first Latin Christian writer" / "founded the tradition" → Jerome puts him after Victor and Apollonius; say only what he wrote.
- "in 197" (or 198, 204) for the Apology → "around 200".
- "Arnobius wrote to convince his bishop" as the narrator's fact → Jerome says it, eighty years on; keep it in Jerome's voice.
- "visions" or "a dream of Christ" → Jerome says only "dreams" drove him toward belief.
- "the bishop demanded the books" / "he presented the books to the bishop" → Jerome says only that he could not win the faith, wrote the books, and at last was let in "as if with hostages".
- Arnobius "in 326/327" → "in Diocletian's reign".
- "Lactantius was Arnobius's pupil" as fact → "Jerome says".
- "Caecilius was baptized" → he yields and puts the rest off till the next day.
- 'see how they love one another' as plain praise, or as a sneer → as Tertullian gives it: it 'put a brand upon us' in some eyes, said by people who hated one another.
- "Lactantius tutored Constantine's son" → stays out (Inferential-Thin; the lactantius story).
- Any barred quote ("the blood of Christians is seed", "not to be condemned unknown", "Religion must be defended…", any quote in tertullian-s-voice, lactantius, cyprian-of-carthage, jerome).
- "seven books" for Arnobius is Jerome's count; keep it, do not add a word count.
- "Lactantius praised them as his models" / "built on them" → he named them, granted what each had done, and judged it had not been enough (ANF7 4456-4470, 4569-4571).
- No quotation marks beyond Q1-Q3.
- "Lactantius studied under Arnobius at Sicca" → Jerome names no place (NPNF2-3 41033-41034).
- "Tertullian the lawyer" → only "well versed in the laws of the Romans, Eusebius says".
