# Outline check: The Latin Apologists (`latin-apologists`)

## My own account of the heart (written before reading `Outline.md`)

From the census entry (`Card.existing.json`), the source sheet and what the subject is known for, with the slate's ruling that the job drops "a language with no words for it yet".

- **The essence:** Latin-speaking converts in Roman Africa and Rome, from Tertullian at Carthage around 200 to Lactantius at Nicomedia in 303, who had been trained as advocates and teachers of rhetoric and turned that training to answering the charges against a despised people, arguing for the faith to the educated in their own terms.
- **Who they were:** "the Latin apologists": Tertullian, Minucius Felix, Arnobius of Sicca, Lactantius (Commodian at the margin). Converts, nearly all from the courts and the schools of rhetoric. Not one community but a line of writers, each knowing the earlier ones (Lactantius names Minucius and Tertullian; Jerome sets them as a set).
- **When and where:** Carthage under Severus and Caracalla; Rome and the beach at Ostia (undated, Contested); Sicca in Africa under Diocletian; Nicomedia when the church was torn down.
- **What they faced:** the rumours (a child killed and eaten, incest feasts with a dog and the lamp, an ass's head), the charge of having no altars, temples or images, the charge that the world went to ruin since Christians came, judges who tortured to force denial, and at the end Diocletian's persecution (meeting-places torn down, Scriptures burned). Also the scorn of the learned for Scripture's plain speech.
- **What they believed:** one unseen God whom no temple holds, who sees all ("we live not only in His eyes but in His bosom"); the true offering a good mind; images anointed stones; Christ to be held God.
- **How they practiced:** meetings to pray, for emperors and enemies, living and dead; Scripture read; a monthly voluntary gift for the poor, orphans, old, shipwrecked and prisoners; a sober love feast with hymns; calling one another brothers; one marriage or none.
- **What held them together:** each had once believed and done what he now answered ("we also were the same as you"), and each answered with the very weapons of the pagans' own learning: the faith needing "suitable heralds" for the learned.
- **What came of them:** Lactantius takes the earlier ones as forerunners and builds on them (Documented); Jerome carries them as the model of Christians who used pagan learning (Letter 70; Documented). Defended: the right to argue the faith in the learned's own terms, and to use pagan books. Influence on the imperial church stays out (Inferential-Thin).

**Against the outline's heart.** The two accounts agree in substance: the same people, time, places, charges, practice, the "weapons of the philosophers" as what held them together, and Lactantius and Jerome as what came of them. Mine puts weight on their being converts who once believed the charges. The outline's beats carry that (beat 2 "we were once the same", Q3), so it is no finding. The heart holds. Beats tell it in the first minute (Ostia opening, then the essence) and follow the story once, in order, with the ending (Lactantius on Minucius) not told early. One exception is under MINOR 8.

## BLOCKING

**B1. Lactantius's verdict on his forerunners is softened (Connection, What came of it 1, beat 5).** ANF7 4456-4470 says the learned who came over "have not been sufficient for its defence", and says what Minucius "might have been". ANF7 4569-4571 goes on from "Although Tertullian fully pleaded the same cause … the Apology" with "yet, inasmuch as it is one thing to answer accusers, which consists in defence or denial only, and another thing to instruct, which we do … I have not shrunk from this labour, that I might complete the subject". ANF7 890 cites the Octavius only to correct it ("These things, indeed, have some resemblance to the truth, but are not true"). "Took the earlier ones as forerunners … and built on them" leaves out the record's force. It also leaves "his seven books" in beat 5 without the explanation Part 1 requires (Explain it). Exact corrections:
- Beat 5, replace "Tertullian's Apology had pleaded the same cause in full." with: "Tertullian's Apology, he granted, had pleaded the same cause in full; but answering accusers was one thing, and teaching the whole faith another, and that second task was the one he took on. ANF7 4565-4571; LactLat 23225ff. Documented."
- What came of it 1, replace with: "Lactantius named Minucius and Tertullian, cited the Octavius by title (correcting it), and judged that the learned defenders before him had not been enough; Tertullian had answered accusers, and he set out to teach the whole faith (ANF7 890, 4456-4470, 4565-4571). *Defended:* the faith taught whole to the learned in their own terms."
- Connection, replace the first sentence with: "What passed was the case itself, and a judgment on how it had been made. Lactantius read the Octavius and cites it by name, though to correct it (ANF7 890). He says Tertullian's Apology had pleaded the cause in full, but only as an answer to accusers, and that he himself would teach the whole of the faith (ANF7 4565-4571). Jerome makes him Arnobius's pupil (NPNF2-3 41033-41035)."
- Do not say, add: "Lactantius praised them as his models" / "built on them" → he named them, granted what each had done, and judged it had not been enough (ANF7 4456-4470, 4569-4571).

**B2. "The New Prophecy" has no sourced explanation, and the turn has no reason (Words to explain, beat 3).** NPNF2-3 40506-40513 does not say it was "a movement of prophets that asked for a stricter life", and "against second marriage" is inferred from a title. Tertullian's own words hold it: ANF4 5769-5773 (the Paraclete as "the institutor of a novel discipline … which they find most harsh") and ANF4 6895-6905 ("Disciples of the New Prophecy"; "we judge second marriage, as being unlawful"; "we fast oftener"). Jerome gives a reason for the turn (40506-40508), and Part 1 requires a break to carry its reason where the old sources give one. Exact corrections:
- Words to explain, replace the New Prophecy line with: "**the New Prophecy:** a movement whose followers held that the Paraclete, the Holy Spirit, was now giving a stricter rule; writing for it, Tertullian judged a second marriage unlawful and fasted more often than others (ANF4 5769-5773, 6895-6905; own words; Documented)."
- Beat 3, replace "Later he joined the New Prophecy and wrote against Marcion." with: "Later, driven, Jerome says, by the envy and insults of the Roman clergy, he went over to the New Prophecy (NPNF2-3 40506-40508; motive Contested, in Jerome's voice), and he wrote against Marcion." (The Marcion clause stays Cut first 2.)

Exact correction: in Source_Sheet Library sweep, replace "Searches for a Decretum Gelasianum text listing Arnobius or Lactantius found none held." with "The Decretum Gelasianum is held in Mansi 8 (17220, 17222, 17233, 17301, OCR damaged): the works of Tertullian, Lactantius, Commodian and Arnobius are listed as 'apocrypha', books not to be received. Card." Add Eusebius to Who tells it (old, later).
