# Outline check: The Latin Apologists (`latin-apologists`)

## My own account of the heart (written before reading `Outline.md`)

From the census entry (`Card.existing.json`), the source sheet and what the subject is known for, with the slate's ruling that the job drops "a language with no words for it yet".

- **The essence:** Latin-speaking converts in Roman Africa and Rome, from Tertullian at Carthage around 200 to Lactantius at Nicomedia in 303, who had been trained as advocates and teachers of rhetoric and turned that training to answering the charges against a despised people, arguing for the faith to the educated in their own terms.
- **Who they were:** "the Latin apologists": Tertullian, Minucius Felix, Arnobius of Sicca, Lactantius (Commodian at the margin). Converts, nearly all from the courts and the schools of rhetoric. Not one community but a line of writers, each knowing the earlier ones (Lactantius names Minucius and Tertullian; Jerome sets them as a set).
- **When and where:** Carthage under Severus and Caracalla; Rome and the beach at Ostia (undated, Contested); Sicca in Africa under Diocletian; Nicomedia when the church was torn down.
- **What they faced:** the rumours (a child killed and eaten, incest feasts with a dog and the lamp, an ass's head), the charge of having no altars, temples or images, the charge that the world went to ruin since Christians came, judges who tortured to force denial, and at the end Diocletian's persecution (meeting-places torn down, Scriptures burned). Also the scorn of the learned for Scripture's plain speech.
- **What they believed:** one unseen God whom no temple holds, who sees all ("we live not only in His eyes but in His bosom"); the true offering a good mind; images anointed stones; Christ to be held God.
- **How they practiced:** meetings to pray, for emperors and enemies, living and dead; Scripture read; a monthly voluntary gift for the poor, orphans, old, shipwrecked and prisoners; a sober love feast with hymns; calling one another brothers; one marriage or none.
- **What held them together:** each had once believed and done what he now answered ("we also were the same as you"), and each answered with the very weapons of the pagans' own learning: the faith needing "suitable heralds" for the learned.
- **What came of them:** Lactantius takes the earlier ones as forerunners and builds on them (Documented); Jerome carries them as the model of Christians who used pagan learning (Letter 70; Documented). Defended: the right to argue the faith in the learned's own terms, and to use pagan books. Influence on the imperial church stays out (Inferential-Thin).
