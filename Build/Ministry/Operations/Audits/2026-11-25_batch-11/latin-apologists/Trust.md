## Findings (BLOCKING)

### 1. "By the road to the sea, Caecilius saw an image of the god Serapis…" — SOFTENED OR CHANGED

- Source: "itaque cum diluculo ad mare inambulando litore pergeremus … Caecilius simulacro Serapidis denotato" (MinLat 220-222). In English: "when in the early morning we were going towards the sea along the shore … Cæcilius, observing an image of Serapis" (ANF4 17251-17254).
- Minucius names no road. They saw the image on their way to the sea, walking along the shore. "By the road" adds a place detail.
- **Change:** "On the way to the sea, Caecilius saw an image of the god Serapis and raised a hand to his mouth, kissing it toward the god."

### 2. "…and gave each month to bury the poor and keep orphans, a love that marked them out to people who hated one another." — SOFTENED OR CHANGED

- Source (Latin): "Sed eiusmodi vel maxime dilectionis operatio notam nobis inurit penes quosdam. Vide, inquiunt, ut invicem se diligant; ipsi enim invicem oderunt" (TertLat1 26761-26763). In English: "it is mainly the deeds of a love so noble that lead many to put a brand upon us. See, they say, how they love one another, for themselves are animated by mutual hatred" (ANF3 3907-3911).
- *Notam inurere* means to brand with disgrace. "Marked them out" can be heard as praise, so the stigma is softened. The outline's Do not say list forbids exactly this ("as Tertullian gives it: it 'put a brand upon us' in some eyes").
- **Change:** "…and gave each month to bury the poor and keep orphans, a love that, he says, branded them in the eyes of people who hated one another."

### 3. "His turning rests on Jerome alone, who wrote about eighty years later that dreams drove him toward belief, and the bishop would not take him in." — SOFTENED OR CHANGED

- Source: Arnobius tells of his own turning: "nunc doctore tanto in uias ueritatis inductus" (ArnLat 1137-1138; ANF6 I.39, "now, having been led into the paths of truth by so great a teacher"). The brief quotes this passage itself two sentences later (Q3, "In them he owns his past").
- Only the story of *how* he turned rests on Jerome: "adhuc ethnicus ad credulitatem somniis compelleretur, neque ab episcopo impetraret fidem" (JerChron 45066-45068; Monc3 21713-21714). The outline says the same ("Documented that Jerome says it; story rests on Jerome alone"). The sentence widens Jerome's sole witness to the turning itself, and Arnobius's own words contradict that.
- **Change:** "The story of how he turned rests on Jerome alone, who wrote about eighty years later that dreams drove him toward belief, and the bishop would not take him in."

The source has "Rulers of the Roman Empire, … seated for the administration of justice … occupying there all but the highest position" (ANF3 1448-1451), with Carthage and Severus/Caracalla from Jerome (NPNF2-3 40496-40499).

## The heart

The heart is the right one. The sources show Latin-speaking defenders who answered the pagan charges in public, with the pagans' own learning, for educated readers who scorned Christian writing. Their link is Lactantius reading, naming and judging the earlier writers, and Jerome's teacher-and-pupil tie. The brief tells this through Ostia, Carthage, Sicca and Nicomedia, and it closes on Lactantius on Minucius, as the outline recommends.

The essence comes in paragraph 2: "He was one of the Latin Apologists … defended their faith in Latin to the educated who despised it", glossing *apology* and "the pagans' own books". It ends at about word 125, inside the first minute (about 150 words). It is not late.
