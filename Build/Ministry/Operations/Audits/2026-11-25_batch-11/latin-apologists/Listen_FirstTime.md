## 1. The test

- **Who these people were.** Filled. "Christians of Rome and Roman Africa who, from around 200 to Diocletian's persecution, defended their faith in Latin to the educated who despised it."
- **What happened to them.** Filled, through the charges, the torture, and the burned writings and torn-down meeting places. The persecution that frames it is never explained (see 4).
- **What their faith was about.** Filled. One unseen God who is everywhere, no temple, "the offering he wants is a good mind", one marriage, prayer for the emperors, care for the poor and orphans.
- **Why the connection matters. UNFILLED, BLOCKING.** I can follow the chain inside the story: Tertullian's Apology, Arnobius teaching Lactantius, Lactantius building on Tertullian. After one hearing, though, I cannot say what these writers left the church, or why it matters that they did this in Latin. The story ends on Lactantius's verdict on Minucius, and that tells me nothing about what came of them. **Change:** take one plain sentence from the outline's "what came of them" and put it where Lactantius takes up "the second" task (teaching the whole faith), so the listener hears what was handed on. If the outline does not hold such a sentence, the reviser asks the researcher. Do not invent one.

## 2. The opening (sentences 1 to 3)

- **Who:** three friends, Caecilius, Octavius (a Christian) and Minucius Felix. Clear.
- **Where:** the shore at Ostia, near Rome. Clear.
- **When: missing.** "On a court holiday at the grape harvest" gives the season but no century. The first date ("around 200") comes in sentence 5. A light clause would fix this. MINOR.
- **Why there:** clear enough: they are walking on a holiday, and a gesture to a god starts a quarrel. But who these people are as a group (Christians defending their faith) waits for paragraph 2. That is allowed, since the essence follows at once.
- Sentence 3 is the hardest sentence in the opening (see 3a).

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing (all MINOR)

a. "Octavius, a Christian, rounded on their friend Minucius Felix for letting him bow to anointed stones, and the three sat on the breakwater to have it out." Lost: "rounded on" and "have it out" are idioms, "anointed" is unknown at this point, and "him" could be Caecilius or Octavius. **Change:** "Octavius, a Christian, blamed their friend Minucius Felix for letting Caecilius bow to stones smeared with oil, and the three sat on the breakwater to argue it out."

b. "Common report, he says, makes newcomers kill an infant hidden in meal, and their feasts end in incest when a dog tied to the lamp puts out the light." Lost: "meal" sounds like food, not flour, and "makes" sounds like the report forces them. I also cannot picture how the dog puts out the light. **Change:** "People say, he claims, that newcomers are made to kill a baby hidden in flour, and that their feasts end in incest once a dog tied to the lamp pulls it over and the room goes dark." (The trust read confirms "pulls it over" against the source, or keeps "puts out the light".)

c. "As lawyers, he and Minucius had believed them too, defending the worst criminals but torturing Christians into denial." Lost: lawyers do not torture, and "denial" of what? **Change:** "As lawyers, he and Minucius had believed them too. They defended the worst criminals, yet had Christians tortured until they gave up their faith." (The trust read confirms the sense of the source.)

d. "He said torture was meant to force denial, not confession." Lost: the point. For a listener it is only a puzzle. **Change:** see 6.

e. "...a love that marked them out to people who hated one another." Lost: who noticed, and how. **Change:** "...and this love made them stand out among people who hated one another."

f. "Finding few pupils, he turned to writing, and he says he was teaching there when the church was thrown down." Lost: a building, or the whole church crushed? Also, the order steps back from writing to teaching. **Change:** see 4c.

g. "Looking back on those before him, Lactantius wrote that truth had lacked fit heralds." Lost: "fit heralds" is old-fashioned. **Change:** "Looking back on those before him, Lactantius wrote that the truth had never had worthy spokesmen."

## 4. Bare statements (BLOCKING)

a. **"Diocletian's persecution"** (paragraph 2; also "In Diocletian's reign", paragraph 6). It is named as an end point and never said to be what it was. I do not know who Diocletian was or what was done. **Change:** "...from around 200 to the great persecution under the emperor Diocletian, when Christian books were burned and churches pulled down..." Use only what the outline holds. The burning and pulling-down already appear in the Arnobius paragraph.

b. **"torturing Christians into denial"** (paragraph 4). An act of punishment, with no word on what was denied or why. **Change:** as 3c. "until they gave up their faith". The reason (the charges, believed without checking) is already in the sentence before.

c. **"when the church was thrown down"** (paragraph 7). An act of persecution with no plain sense and no link to Diocletian. **Change:** "...and he says he was teaching there when the church at Nicomedia was torn down." The trust read confirms the literal sense. If the source gives the reason (Diocletian's order), add it in a clause. If not, tell it as the source does.

## 5. Citations read aloud

Neither line the craft check flags counts. "Jerome says" at s 20 carries a judgment of blame from a writer long after ("driven by the Roman clergy's envy and insults"). Part 1 calls for naming the witness there. "Jerome says" at s 31 places a fact on the same late witness, and Part 1 allows naming a witness who wrote long after. Both may stand. One gap: at s 20 the listener has not yet been told who Jerome was. The gloss ("who wrote about eighty years later") comes only at s 24. **Change (MINOR):** move a short gloss to the first mention: "...Jerome, a writer of the next century, says..."

## 6. Tells (BLOCKING: 1)

- **"not X but Y" turn:** "He said torture was meant to force denial, not confession." It has the form Part 1 names, and as heard it is a riddle. **Change:** tell the argument as the narrator: "Other accused men, he said, were tortured to make them confess, but Christians were tortured to make them deny their faith." The trust read confirms that this is Tertullian's own point.
- No dash, no question answered by the narrator, no stock words, no moral close.
- Watch item, not counted: "Minucius wrote it after Octavius died, at a date no one knows." The second half only says what is missing. It is attached to a real fact, so it is not counted. Cutting "at a date no one knows" loses nothing for the listener.

## 8. Attention

- **Pull:** yes. A beach, a man kissing his hand to a god, a friend angry about it. I want to hear the argument.
- **First drift:** "At Carthage around 200, Tertullian met the same charges in his Apology to the governors who judged Christians." **Why, for the reviser:** the scene I was inside has just ended, and this paragraph answers "the same charges" again. It becomes a list of claims (torture, prayer, money for the poor, a sect, a marriage rule) with no moment to stand in. Even one concrete image from the outline would hold me, if it has one.
- **Back in:** at Arnobius. The refused convert, the books written by lamplight, and the confession about the oiled stone echo the opening.
- **To the end:** yes. The close is soft, though. It ends on one writer's opinion of another ("how fit a champion of truth he might have been"), and I leave without knowing what came of these people (see 1).
