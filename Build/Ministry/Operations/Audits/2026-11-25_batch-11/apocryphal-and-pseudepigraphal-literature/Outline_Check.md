# Outline check: Apocryphal and Pseudepigraphal Literature

## My own account of the heart (written before reading Outline.md)

From the census entry (`Card.existing.json`, the approved brief), the source sheet and what the subject is known for, under the shape ruling (told through the people who wrote, read, copied and judged the books):

- **Essence:** From the second century on, Christians wrote, read and copied books under apostles' names, and about Mary and the apostles, that never entered the New Testament. They were read in churches and at shrines, and bishops judged them by one test: whether the book had been handed down from the apostles.
- **Who they were:** not a community; the writers (the presbyter in Asia), readers (the people of Rhossus, pilgrims such as Egeria), copiers and judges (Serapion, Eusebius, Jerome) of these books.
- **When and where:** second to fourth centuries; Syria (Antioch, Rhossus), Asia, Carthage, Seleucia; later books only as what came of it.
- **What they faced:** books read as the apostles' that the apostles had not written; a congregation divided over a gospel; a writer found out; teachings bishops held false (Docetae) carried in them; women's teaching and baptizing claimed from Thecla's example.
- **What they believed:** the writers wrote to honour (the presbyter "for love of Paul"; the Book of James read to keep Mary's virginity honoured); the judges received the apostles "as Christ" and refused what was "not handed down".
- **How they practiced:** books read aloud in congregations (Rhossus) and in full at a saint's shrine (Thecla at Seleucia); a bishop reading a book through before ruling on it.
- **What held them together:** the church read these books for centuries and never made them scripture; the test of what was handed down.
- **What came of them, heaviest first:** the test of what was handed down (Serapion kept by Eusebius, then Eusebius's own sorting, Athanasius); Thecla's book read at her shrine and Thecla honoured; Mary's childhood (Joachim and Anna, the Temple, Joseph) carried by a book of James into later tradition; Jerome's warning to read for gold amid dirt; the Gospel of Peter found at Akhmim in 1886.

The census's Jewish-pseudepigrapha claim ("survive only because Christian scribes kept copying them") rests on modern editors in the held Library (Source_Sheet Library sweep), and the slate's job leaves it out; its absence is not a missing major contribution under this kit's source rule.
