# Outline check: Apocryphal and Pseudepigraphal Literature

## My own account of the heart (written before reading Outline.md)

From the census entry (`Card.existing.json`, the approved brief), the source sheet and what the subject is known for, under the shape ruling (told through the people who wrote, read, copied and judged the books):

- **Essence:** From the second century on, Christians wrote, read and copied books under apostles' names, and about Mary and the apostles, that never entered the New Testament. They were read in churches and at shrines, and bishops judged them by one test: whether the book had been handed down from the apostles.
- **Who they were:** not a community; the writers (the presbyter in Asia), readers (the people of Rhossus, pilgrims such as Egeria), copiers and judges (Serapion, Eusebius, Jerome) of these books.
- **When and where:** second to fourth centuries; Syria (Antioch, Rhossus), Asia, Carthage, Seleucia; later books only as what came of it.
- **What they faced:** books read as the apostles' that the apostles had not written; a congregation divided over a gospel; a writer found out; teachings bishops held false (Docetae) carried in them; women's teaching and baptizing claimed from Thecla's example.
- **What they believed:** the writers wrote to honour (the presbyter "for love of Paul"; the Book of James read to keep Mary's virginity honoured); the judges received the apostles "as Christ" and refused what was "not handed down".
- **How they practiced:** books read aloud in congregations (Rhossus) and in full at a saint's shrine (Thecla at Seleucia); a bishop reading a book through before ruling on it.
- **What held them together:** the church read these books for centuries and never made them scripture; the test of what was handed down.
- **What came of them, heaviest first:** the test of what was handed down (Serapion kept by Eusebius, then Eusebius's own sorting, Athanasius); Thecla's book read at her shrine and Thecla honoured; Mary's childhood (Joachim and Anna, the Temple, Joseph) carried by a book of James into later tradition; Jerome's warning to read for gold amid dirt; the Gospel of Peter found at Akhmim in 1886.

The census's Jewish-pseudepigrapha claim ("survive only because Christian scribes kept copying them") rests on modern editors in the held Library (Source_Sheet Library sweep), and the slate's job leaves it out; its absence is not a missing major contribution under this kit's source rule.

## BLOCKING

**B1. Beat 3 puts the surviving Book of James inside the window on a Dominant Modern Reconstruction.** The beat tells the surviving book's contents (Joachim and Anna, the temple at three, the angel's food, Joseph at twelve) and then has Origen know "a Book of James". That says, or lets a listener hear, that Origen's book is this book and that its story was read in the window. The sheet rates that identity Dominant Modern Reconstruction (B4b; ANF8 35968–35970: "by no means clear"). The only date for the surviving text is its eleventh-century manuscript (B4g). The kit's rule keeps this out of the story. Correction: replace Beat 3 with:
> 3. **A book of James and Mary's childhood.** Origen, the great teacher of Alexandria, reported that some, drawing on the Gospel according to Peter or a "Book of James", held that Jesus's brothers were Joseph's sons by an earlier wife; those who said so meant to keep Mary's virginity honoured to the end. A book under James's name survives in later copies, the printed one from an eleventh-century manuscript. It tells what the Gospels do not: Mary born to a childless couple, Joachim and Anna; her years in the temple from the age of three, fed, it says, by an angel's hand; and her betrothal to Joseph, an old man with children. (B4a–g; Documented as what each says. Narrate Origen; his words are barred. Do not tie the two books together.)

Add to Do not say: | "the book Origen knew told of Mary's temple years" / "Origen's Book of James" made one with the surviving book | Tell them separately: Origen reports what some drew from a Book of James; a book under James's name survives in later copies (B4a–b, B4g). | Move "What came of it" 3 to read: "A book under James's name, kept in many later copies (B4g), with later Latin retellings drawn from it (C3)." If the researcher has an old witness inside the window that shows the surviving book's own story was read, re-rate B4b with it instead (see MINOR 7, Clement), and say so in Fixes.md.

**B2. Thecla's end is told as one thing where the forms differ, and her "tomb" is added.** Beat 5 says Seleucia is "where Thecla's book says she ended her days". Only Gebhardt's form says she fell asleep there (10558–10559). The ANF form has her live seventy-two years in a cave at Seleucia, go under ground, travel to Rome, and be "buried about two or three stadia from the tomb of her master Paul" (ANF8 48109–48122). Ending 1 ("read in full at her tomb") and the Connection ("a reading at her tomb") add a tomb Egeria does not name: she says "memorial" (McClure–Feltoe 3757, 3800, 3816). The editor's note on a grave at Seleucia (3804–3806) is modern. Corrections:
- Beat 5: "came to Seleucia, where Thecla's book says she ended her days" → "came to Seleucia, where, in one form of Thecla's book, she taught many and fell asleep".
- Ending 1: "now read in full at her tomb" → "now read in full at her memorial".
- Connection: "on to Seleucia as a reading at her tomb" → "on to Seleucia as a reading at her memorial".
- Sheet B3d: add the ANF lines 48117–48122 ("departed to Rome … she is buried about two or three stadia from the tomb of her master Paul") beside the seventy-two years.
- Do not say, add: | "Thecla died / was buried at Seleucia" or "her tomb" | "in one form of her book, she taught many and fell asleep" there; the other form ends in Rome; Egeria saw "the memorial" (B3d; ANF8 48117–48122). |

**B3. Cut first item 4 drops why the claim was made.** It removes "to keep Mary's virginity honoured", the only reason Origen gives for the "some say" claim (ANF9 30607–30608). Without it, the claim about Joseph's earlier wife is named with no why. The approved ruling applies every Cut first item, so the length must be met another way. Kept, item 4 adds about 10 words (509 → about 519, past 510). Correction: replace item 4 with:
> 4. Serapion's promise to come again ("He wrote that he would hurry back: expect me soon"). Leave out. (−10)

Keep the Do not say line "Serapion returned and…", since the writer may still reach for it.

**B4. The beats carry five quoted spans outside the three Quotes, one of them on a doubtful reading.** Beat 1 quotes "lurking in some heresy" and "expect me soon". Beat 2 quotes Thecla's "In the name of Jesus Christ I am baptized on my last day" and Paul's "Go and teach the word of God". Beat 4 quotes "as absurd and impious". Part 2 allows 2 or 3 quotes, each with its rendering, source and job, and the writer uses the outline's quotes word for word, so these would reach the story as unchecked quotes. "Lurking" (ἐφώλευεν) is a reading Schwartz's apparatus thinks wrong ("Serapion schrieb wohl ἐχώλευεν", 2541). Quoting it word for word presents a disputed text as settled. Corrections (narrated, no quotation marks):
- Beat 1: "had a mind "lurking in some heresy", a teaching he held false. He wrote that he would hurry back: "expect me soon"." → "had minds caught up in some heresy, a teaching he held false." (The promise goes out per B3.)
- Beat 2: "In the arena she threw herself into a pit of water: "In the name of Jesus Christ I am baptized on my last day." Paul later told her, "Go and teach the word of God."" → "In the arena she threw herself into a pit of water and baptized herself in the name of Jesus Christ, on what she called her last day. Later Paul sent her to teach the word of God."
- Beat 4: "to be cast aside, in his words, "as absurd and impious"" → "to be thrown out entirely as absurd and godless".
- Heart, "What they believed": add "(narrate; not a story quote)" after Paul's line.

## MINOR

1. **"A pilgrim from the West"** (Beat 5) rests on the editors' argument from her mention of the Rhone (McClure–Feltoe 196–222, which itself weighs it against Valerius). Replace with "a pilgrim who had come, as a bishop told her, from far-distant lands" (3419–3421, her own report). Add to Do not say: | "from Spain / Gaul / the West", "a nun" | "from far-distant lands" (3419–3421) |.
2. **Opening 1, "a small church"**: "small" is not in the source (παροικία, A3). → "a church on the Syrian coast".
3. **Beat 3 ages and children.** The book's Joseph says "I have children" (B4f), so use "children", not "sons" (the B1 replacement already does). "At twelve" varies between copies (ANF8 note 1578: "Or, fourteen. Postel's Latin version has ten"). The B1 replacement drops the age. Add to Do not say: | "at twelve" as the book's fixed age | leave the age out; the copies differ (ANF8 1578) |.
4. **Eusebius's νόθοι glossed "read by many".** Eusebius says they "are yet at the same time known to most ecclesiastical writers" (npnf201 18254–18256). Words to explain: "read by many, but not genuine" → "known to most church writers, but not genuine". Beat 4: "among books read but not genuine" → "among books known to most church writers but not genuine".
5. **Connection, "the same love of the apostles drove both the writing and the refusing".** Serapion gives no motive of love. He receives the apostles "as Christ" (B1b). → "The presbyter wrote for love of Paul; Serapion refused in the apostles' name, receiving them as Christ. The listener must understand that the apostles' names drove both the writing and the refusing, and that the verdicts did not stop the reading."
6. **Ending 1's identification** ("the book whose writer had left his office") cites only B2e, a modern note. Jerome, Lives of Illustrious Men 7 (npnf203 39502–39515; PL23 49226–49230), an old later witness, ties the Acts of Paul and Thecla to Tertullian's presbyter. Cite it beside Ending 1 and add it to Who tells it ("Jerome, De viris 7: old, later witness, c. 392; ties the Acts of Paul and Thecla to Tertullian's presbyter; not credited aloud"). Beat 2 should also say the book "as its later copies tell it", since Tertullian attests only Thecla's baptizing and teaching (B2b). Add to Beat 2 after "told of Thecla": "(the details from its later copies, B3; Tertullian attests her example only)".
7. **Library sweep misses two held old witnesses.**
   - Irenaeus, Against Heresies 1.20.1 (anf01 32806–32822, c. 180): the Marcosians "adduce an unspeakable number of apocryphal and spurious writings, which they themselves have forged", with the story of the boy Jesus and the letter Alpha. That makes the sheet's Infancy Thomas set-aside ("a modern question") incomplete: an old witness holds the story.
   - Clement of Alexandria, Stromata 7.16 (anf02 52530–52533): "some say that, after she brought forth, she was found, when examined, to be a virgin", an old witness c. 200 to a story the surviving Book of James tells. It is Alexandria's, a clause at most.
   Add both to the sheet under Used or Set aside, with reasons. The story need not carry them.
8. **Heart "What came of them" order** puts the test of what was handed down last; the ranked list puts it first (defended). Reorder the heart line to: the test of what was handed down kept by Eusebius (B5); the Acts of Thecla read in full at her memorial (B6c); a book under James's name kept in many later copies (B4g).
9. **Do not say, add:** | "the church at Rhossus was heretical" | only "those who put the book forward", "the backers" (B1d; Rufinus 2584: "hi, qui codicem illum legi debere adserunt") |.
