## 1. The test

- **Who these people were:** filled. "Christians wrote and read gospels and tales of the apostles under the apostles' own names, books that never entered the New Testament." The readers (the Rhossus church), a writer (the presbyter), judges (Serapion, Tertullian, Eusebius) and a pilgrim (Egeria) all appear.
- **What happened to them:** filled. Serapion allows the gospel, then refuses it; the presbyter is found out and leaves his office; Eusebius casts the books aside; Thecla's book is still read at her church late in the fourth century.
- **What their faith was about:** filled, but thin on the readers' side. I can say what the judges held ("we receive Peter and the other apostles as we receive Christ"; what was "handed down"), and that some cited Thecla "as leave for women to teach and baptize." I cannot say why the Rhossus church wanted Peter's gospel, or what Mary's book meant to those who read it. Not counted as unfilled.
- **Why the connection matters:** filled through what came of it: Mary's book "kept in many later copies", Thecla's book read whole at her church.

## 2. The opening (first three sentences)

Who (Serapion, bishop of Antioch; the church at Rhossus), where (Rhossus, coast of Syria near Antioch), when (about 200) and why there (a visit; a quarrel over a gospel) are all there. Missing for this listener, MINOR:
- What a gospel is. "a gospel that carried Peter's name" means little without "a book about Jesus".
- Who Peter was. He is first glossed nowhere; "apostles" comes only in sentence 4, also unglossed.

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing (MINOR)

- "These are the Christian Apocrypha, the books that were never canon." Meaning lost: "canon" is the gloss, and it is itself unknown, so the definition is circular (see 4).
- "In its later copies she hears Paul at Iconium..." Meaning lost: why "later copies"? It suggests early copies say something else, and the story never answers. Iconium is not placed.
- "Some Christians cited her example as leave for women to teach and baptize." Meaning lost: "leave" as permission is rare for a second-language listener; I heard "leave" as going away.
- "The Acts of Paul he counted among books known to most church writers but not genuine." The object first, then "he counted", is hard to follow by ear; and I was not sure "the Acts of Paul" is the Thecla book ("a book of Paul's travels and deeds"). Suggest: "He counted the Acts of Paul, the book of Thecla, among books..."
- "In one form of Thecla's book, this was where she taught many and fell asleep." Meaning lost: "fell asleep" read literally; I did not hear that she died. "One form" raises the same unanswered question as "later copies".
- "On a low hill outside the city stood her church, with cells of men and women around it." Meaning lost: "cells" heard as prison cells.

## 4. Bare statements (BLOCKING)

- "the books that were never canon." The label "canon" is never said in plain words. Change: "These are the Christian Apocrypha: books the churches never took in as scripture, to be read out beside the Gospels." (Wording for the reviser; no claim added beyond "never entered the New Testament".)
- "He set the gospels of Peter and Thomas and the Acts of Andrew and John lower still." Three books (Thomas, Andrew, John) are named and never said to be anything. Change: cut the extra titles: "He set the gospel of Peter, and other gospels and acts under apostles' names, lower still."
- "Serapion was told that the book's backers had minds caught up in some heresy, a teaching he held false." The gloss says what "heresy" means, never what this teaching was; the craft check also flags it. Change: say in plain words what the teaching was, as the source gives it; if the source does not, keep only what it gives, with no gloss pretending to explain it.

Not counted but noted: "some things had been added, and he set those out for them" leaves me asking what was added. If the source does not say, it stands.

- Not counted: the check's "stock word" in sentence 4 is "Testament" in "New Testament", a proper name. No dash, no "not X but Y", no question answered by the narrator.
