## BLOCKING

**S10. "His letter, which Eusebius of Caesarea kept in his Church History a century later, lays down his rule: …"** SOFTENED OR CHANGED.
- Source: Eusebius lists Serapion's writings and sets this one apart from his letters: "xal ἄλλαι πρὸς ἕτέρους ἐπιστολαί, ἕτερός τε συντεταγμένος αὐτῷ / λόγος Περὶ τοῦ λεγομένου κατὰ Πέτρον εὐαγγελίου" (Schwartz 2502–2504: "and other letters to others, and another work he composed, On the so-called Gospel according to Peter"). Rufinus: "sed et ille liber venit ad nos … de euangelio Petri" (Schwartz 2566–2569), and "de eius libello" (2572).
- Brief: "His letter".
- Change: say "What he wrote to them, which Eusebius of Caesarea kept in his Church History a century later, lays down his rule:". Or say "His book on that gospel". The passage does speak to "brothers" and says "expect me soon", so "wrote to them" holds. "Letter" names a kind of writing that Eusebius sets apart from this one.
- Outline: it says "Serapion's letter" in Who tells it, and so leaves room for the error. Suggested Do not say line: "his letter" → "what he wrote to them" or "his book on that gospel" (Eusebius calls it a λόγος and lists it apart from the letters, Schwartz 2502–2504).

**S19. "Then he told of the book's writer, a presbyter, an elder of the church: [Quote 3]."** SOFTENED OR CHANGED (the order is reversed).
- Source order: in Tertullian, the presbyter comes first ("sciant in Asia presbyterum … loco decessisse"), and the argument from Paul follows it ("quam enim fidei proximum uideretur, ut is docendi et tinguendi daret feminae potestatem qui ne discere quidem constanter mulieri permisit?") (CSEL 20 12262–12267).
- Brief: S18 gives the Paul argument and then says "Then he told of the book's writer", so the sequence runs the wrong way.
- Change: drop the sequence word, keeping everything else: "He told them, too, of the book's writer, a presbyter, an elder of the church: …". Or put the presbyter first and the Paul argument after it, as Tertullian does.
- Outline: the error starts in beat 2 ("answered that Paul did not even let a woman learn too boldly. Then he told of the writer"). Suggested Do not say line: "Then he told of the writer" after the Paul argument → no sequence word, or the presbyter first (CSEL 20 12262–12267).

## SUMMARY HOLDS (not blocking)

- **S1** "About the year 200, on a visit to Rhossus, a town on the coast of Syria near Antioch, Bishop Serapion … found the church quarreling over a gospel that carried Peter's name."
  - Visit and gospel: "ἐγὼ γὰρ γενόμενος παρ᾽ ὑμῖν … τὸ ὑπ᾽ αὐτῶν προφερύμενον ὀνόματι Πέτρου εὐαγγέλιον" (Schwartz 2514–2516).
  - Place: modern note, used to clarify: "a city of Syria, lying on the Gulf of Issus, a little to the northwest of Antioch" (npnf201 33698–33700).
  - Date: Serapion was bishop in Commodus's time (npnf201 31060–31061) and died before the persecution under Asclepiades (Schwartz 2369–2371). "About 200" sits within that span.
  - "Quarreling" renders μικροψυχίαν, which Rufinus gives as "simultatem" (2583).
- **S4** The essence (books under the apostles' names, outside the New Testament, second to fourth centuries). It compresses B1b ("τὰ δὲ ὀνόματι αὐτῶν ψευδεπίγραφα"), CSEL 20 12260 ("quae Pauli perperam inscripta sunt") and Eusebius's sorting (npnf201 18031–18036, 18256–18333).
- **S5** "These are the Christian Apocrypha, the books that were never canon." This is a name for the subject. Jerome counts the Acts of Paul and Thecla "among the apocryphal writings" (npnf203 39502–39505).
- **S6** "Congregations read them aloud and argued over them, and bishops and teachers had to judge them against what had been handed down."
  - Read aloud: "ἀναγινωσκέσθω" (Schwartz 2517); Egeria's reading at the shrine (McClure–Feltoe 3815–3817).
  - The test: "τὰ τοιαῦτα οὐ παρελάβομεν" (Schwartz 2513–2514).
  - Judging: Tertullian (CSEL 20 12260–12267) and Eusebius (npnf201 18256ff.). The compression is fair.
- **S8** "He borrowed the book from the group that had used it from the first, and he read it through."
  - Source: "παρ᾽ ἄλλων τῶν ἀσχησάντων αὐτὸ τοῦτο τὸ εὐαγγέλιον, τοῦτ᾽ ἐστὶν παρὰ τῶν διαδόχων τῶν καταρξαμένων αὐτοῦ … χρησάμενοι παρ᾽ αὐτῶν διελϑεῖν" (Schwartz 2622–2626).
  - "Used it from the first" compresses "the successors of those who began it". Meaning and force are intact.
- **S11** "In the same years, a book of Paul's travels and deeds told of a girl named Thecla." Tertullian, c. 200, already knows the writing and "exemplum Theclae" (CSEL 20 12260–12261). Jerome glosses the title as "Acts = Journeyings" (npnf203 39502).
- **S17** "Some Christians cited her example as leave for women to teach and baptize."
  - Latin: "quodsi quae Pauli perperam inscripta sunt exemplum Theclae ad licentiam mulierum docendi tinguendique defendunt, sciant …" (CSEL 20 12260–12262).
  - Grammatically the writings are the subject. But "sciant" ("let them know") shows that people were pressing the claim, and the context names a woman "quae usurpauit docere" (12256–12257). The summary holds.
- **S20** "Other books told what the Gospels leave untold." The Book of James (ANF8 36784–36799). Origen also reports a story about Jesus's brothers drawn from "the Gospel according to Peter, or 'The Book of James'" (anf09 30600–30610).
- **S23** "Early in the fourth century, Eusebius … set these books in order." npnf201 18031–18036, 18256ff.
  - A note for the reviser, not blocking: "these books" follows straight after the Book of James, which Eusebius does not list. "Such books" would avoid hearing that he ranked it.
- **S24** "The Acts of Paul he counted among books known to most church writers but not genuine."
  - "Among the rejected writings [ἐν τοῖς νόθοις] must be reckoned also the Acts of Paul" (npnf201 18031–18033).
  - The same chapter calls these "not canonical but disputed, … yet at the same time known to most ecclesiastical writers" (npnf201, the 3.25.6 passage before "we have felt compelled to give this catalogue").
- **S26** "In his judgment they carried the apostles' names but had not been handed down, and they should be cast aside entirely as absurd and godless."
  - "Cited by the heretics under the name of the apostles … which no one belonging to the succession of ecclesiastical writers has deemed worthy of mention … cast aside as absurd and impious" (npnf201 18256–18333).
  - The test is set against what is "true and genuine" by church tradition (same passage). "Not handed down" for Peter's gospel: "οὐδ᾽ ὅλως ἐν καθολικαῖς ἴσμεν παραδεδομένα" (npnf201 14566–14569).
  - "Fictions of heretics" is left out under the approved Cut first item 3. The force survives in "absurd and godless".
- **S27** "Late in the fourth century, a pilgrim named Egeria, from far-distant lands, came to Seleucia."
  - Seleucia: "I arrived at a city which is called Seleucia in Hisauria" (McClure–Feltoe 3767–3768).
  - "From far-distant lands": the bishop of Edessa's words to her (3419–3421).
  - Name and date are a widely accepted identification. It rests on Valerius's seventh-century letter and on Férotin's argument, which "met with very general acceptance" (intro 147–149, 163–167). The edition itself prefers the spelling "Etheria" (180–182). The claim stays inside Part 1 as Widely Accepted. It is not resting on a modern work alone, so it is not blocking.
- **S30** "There, Egeria writes, prayer was made at the memorial, and then the whole of the Acts of Thecla was read, the book whose writer had left his office."
  - "Prayer was made at the memorial, and the whole of the acts of saint Thecla having been read" (McClure–Feltoe 3815–3817).
  - Tying this book to the presbyter rests on Jerome, a later old witness of the same years: "the Acts of Paul and Thecla … Tertullian … mentions a certain presbyter in Asia … the author of the book … resigned his office" (npnf203 39502–39515; PL23 49226–49230 "loco excidisse").

## The heart

- **Right heart:** yes. The sources show these books meeting the people who read them, and those people judging them by what was handed down: Serapion (Schwartz 2510–2627), Tertullian (CSEL 20 12260–12267), Eusebius (npnf201 18031–18333). They also show the reading going on in spite of those verdicts (McClure–Feltoe 3815–3817). The heart holds, as told.
- **On time:** yes. The essence (paragraph 2) ends at word 139, inside the 150-word mark.
