## BLOCKING

**S19, SOFTENED OR CHANGED (order moved).** "Then he told of the book's writer, a presbyter, an elder of the church: …"
- The brief puts Paul's rule first (S18) and the presbyter second ("Then"). Tertullian argues in the reverse order. CSEL 20 12260–12264 comes first: "quodsi … exemplum Theclae … defendunt, sciant in Asia presbyterum … loco decessisse". Then 12264–12267: "quam enim fidei proximum uideretur … qui ne discere quidem constanter mulieri permisit?" Oehler and ANF3 57780–57794 have the same order.
- This moves a detail onto the wrong place in the record (Part 1, the four faults).
- **Exact change:** "Then he told of the book's writer" → "He also told of the book's writer". The quote stays as it is.
- Outline beat 2 carries the same order ("answered that Paul … Then he told of the writer"). Do not say line to add: "Tertullian gave Paul's rule, then told of the presbyter" | "He told of the presbyter, then gave Paul's rule (CSEL 20 12260–12267); or give no order".

## SUMMARY HOLDS (not blocking; evidence)

- **S1.** "About the year 200 … found the church quarreling over a gospel that carried Peter's name." Serapion was bishop from Commodus's reign (npnf201 31060–31061) until Asclepiades succeeded him (Schwartz 2369–2371), so "about 200" is as rough as the sources allow. The place comes from the translator's note (npnf201 33698–33700), which clarifies and does not testify. "Quarreling" stands on μικροψυχίαν (2516) and Rufinus's "simultatem" (2582).
- **S4.** The essence. The presbyter's writing went under Paul's name ("quasi titulo Pauli", CSEL 20 12263). The gospel went under Peter's ("ὀνόματι Πέτρου", Schwartz 2515). Eusebius's sorting (npnf201 18031–18036, 18256–18333) shows these books stood outside the accepted ones.
- **S5.** A naming line. The old term is Jerome's: "we reckon among the apocryphal writings" (npnf203 39503–39505).
- **S6.** The essence. Read aloud: ἀναγινωσκέσθω (2516), and Egeria (3815–3819). Argued over: 2516. Judged against what was handed down: "τὰ τοιαῦτα οὐ παρελάβομεν" (2512–2513) and Eusebius's "according to ecclesiastical tradition" (npnf201 3.25.6). Congregations reading the books rests mainly on Rhossus. The plural is a fair compression given Eusebius's "cited by the heretics".
- **S8.** "from the group that had used it from the first". The source reads "παρ᾽ ἄλλων τῶν ἀσκησάντων αὐτὸ τοῦτο τὸ εὐαγγέλιον, τοῦτ᾽ ἐστὶν παρὰ τῶν διαδόχων τῶν καταρξαμένων αὐτοῦ … χρησάμενοι παρ᾽ αὐτῶν διελθεῖν" (Schwartz 2622–2625), that is, the successors of those who began it. The compression is fair. Note that these are "others" (ἄλλων), not the Rhossus backers, and the brief does not merge the two. The letter does not say whether he borrowed the book before or after the report. The brief only sets the sentences in sequence and gives no "because", which matches the outline's Do not say.
- **S10.** "His letter". Eusebius calls it a λόγος (Schwartz 2502–2503). It addresses "ἀδελφοί" and says "expect me soon", so "letter" is fair. "A century later": Eusebius wrote about 100–125 years after. Quote 2 checked against the Greek (2510–2513): "ἀποδεχόμεθα ὡς Χριστόν … ψευδεπίγραφα ὡς ἔμπειροι παραιτούμεθα … οὐ παρελάβομεν". It holds.
- **S11.** "In the same years … told of a girl named Thecla". Tertullian, writing about 200, already has "exemplum Theclae" (CSEL 20 12261). "Travels and deeds" glosses "Acts" (πράξεις). Jerome glosses it "Acts = Journeyings" (npnf203 39502).
- **S17.** "Some Christians cited her example as leave for women to teach and baptize." Tertullian frames it as a condition, with the writings as subject: "quodsi quae Pauli perperam inscripta sunt exemplum Theclae ad licentiam mulierum docendi tinguendique defendunt" (12260–12262). But "sciant" addresses real people, and the chapter answers a real "mulier quae usurpauit docere" (12256–12257). The compression is fair.
- **S18.** "writing in Carthage in Roman Africa". Jerome: "from the city of Carthage in the province of Africa" (npnf203 40496). "did not even let a woman learn too boldly": "ne discere quidem constanter mulieri permisit" (12266–12267). This sentence holds once S19 is fixed.
- **S20.** "Other books told what the Gospels leave untold." The plural rests on the Protevangelium (B4) and on Origen's report of the Gospel of Peter and a Book of James (anf09 30600–30610).
- **S21.** "One under James's name, kept in many later copies". The book's own claim: "I James that wrote this history" (ANF8 37167). The editor's manuscript note, "eleventh century (Paris). At least seventeen other forms exist" (ANF8 36602–36603), clarifies the text's transmission.
- **S23.** "Early in the fourth century" is Widely Accepted for Eusebius's Church History. "Set these books in order": the catalogue at npnf201 3.25.
- **S24.** "known to most church writers but not genuine". Eusebius places the Acts of Paul among the νόθοι (18031–18033). Then 3.25.6 (npnf201 18240–18245): "those which, although not canonical but disputed, are yet at the same time known to most ecclesiastical writers". The sentence is a fair compression.
- **S26.** "In his judgment they carried the apostles' names but had not been handed down, and … cast aside entirely as absurd and godless." The source: "cited by the heretics under the name of the apostles … which no one belonging to the succession of ecclesiastical writers has deemed worthy of mention … cast aside as absurd and impious" (18256–18333). "Not handed down" compresses the succession test of 3.25.6. Style and thought went to the card under Cut first 3. The force stays, and the judgment is credited.
- **S27.** "from far-distant lands". A bishop says this to her: "coming to these places from far-distant lands" (McClure–Feltoe 3419–3421). It is fair as a description. The date is Widely Accepted only as "late fourth century". Seleucia: 3779–3780.
- **S30.** "prayer was made at the memorial, and then the whole of the Acts of Thecla was read" (3815–3817, Bernard 9579–9581). This is HELD. The tag "the book whose writer had left his office" rests on Jerome, about 392 and an old later witness. He ties "the Acts of Paul and Thecla" to Tertullian's presbyter, "who, confessing that he did this for love of Paul, resigned his office" (npnf203 39502–39515). This fits Part 1's rule for what came of the subject.

## The heart

The heart is right for what the sources show. The books were written under apostles' names, read in congregations, and judged against what was handed down. Rhossus (Schwartz 2510–2627), Tertullian (CSEL 20 12260–12267) and Eusebius (npnf201 3.25) all carry it. The essence (S4–S6) runs from about word 69 to word 140, so it is told within the first minute.
