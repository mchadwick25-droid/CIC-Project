## Sentence by sentence

**P1**
1. "About the year 200, on a visit to Rhossus … found the church quarreling over a gospel that carried Peter's name." SUMMARY HOLDS. Schwartz 2513–2515: "ἐγὼ γὰρ γενόμενος παρ᾽ ὑμῖν … τὸ ὑπ᾽ αὐτῶν προφερόμενον ὀνόματι Πέτρου εὐαγγέλιον … εἰ τοῦτό ἐστιν μόνον τὸ δοκοῦν ὑμῖν παρέχειν μικροψυχίαν". "Quarreling" is fair for the ill feeling his answer names. Place: npnf201 33698–33700, the translator's note ("on the Gulf of Issus, a little to the northwest of Antioch"), which clarifies and is allowed. The date: Serapion is bishop under Commodus (npnf201 31060–31061) and dead before Asclepiades (Schwartz 2369–2371). "About 200" is as rough as that allows.
2. "He had not read it, and he thought them all sound in faith." HELD. Schwartz 2513–2514: "ὑπενόουν τοὺς πάντας ὀρθῇ πίστει προσφέρεσθαι. καὶ μὴ διελθὼν".
3. "So he gave a short answer: 'If this is the only thing that seems to be causing bad feeling among you, let it be read.'" HELD. Schwartz 2515–2516: "εἰ τοῦτό ἐστιν μόνον τὸ δοκοῦν ὑμῖν παρέχειν μικροψυχίαν, ἀναγινωσκέσθω". Rufinus 2582–2583: "si hoc est solum, quod inter vos simultatem videtur inferre, legatur codex". The rendering keeps "seems" and "only".

**P2**
4. "In the second to fourth centuries, Christians wrote and read gospels and tales of the apostles under the apostles' own names … the Christian Apocrypha." SUMMARY HOLDS. It compresses Serapion (Schwartz 2511: "ὀνόματι αὐτῶν ψευδεπίγραφα"), Tertullian (CSEL 20 12260: "quae Pauli perperam inscripta sunt") and Eusebius (npnf201 18256–18260: "cited by heretics under the name of the apostles … Gospels of Peter … Acts of Andrew … and John"). The name is the census shortName, a label.
5. "Congregations read them aloud and argued over them, and bishops and teachers had to judge them against what had been handed down, received in the churches from the apostles." SUMMARY HOLDS. Rhossus (Schwartz 2516, "ἀναγινωσκέσθω"), Egeria (3816–3817) and the test "οὐ παρελάβομεν" (Schwartz 2512–2513), with Eusebius's "handed down among the catholic" (npnf201 14566–14569).

**P3**
6. "After he left Rhossus, Serapion was told that the book's backers had minds caught up in a teaching he held false." HELD. Schwartz 2516–2518: "νῦν δὲ μαθὼν ὅτι αἱρέσει τινὶ ὁ νοῦς αὐτῶν ἐφώλευεν, ἐκ τῶν λεχθέντων μοι, σπουδάσω πάλιν γενέσθαι πρὸς ὑμᾶς". "Backers": Rufinus 2584–2585: "hi, qui codicem illum legi debere adserunt". "After he left" follows from "come to you again".
7. "He wrote that he saw what one Marcianus held: a man who contradicted himself, not understanding what he said." HELD. Schwartz 2519, 2621: "καταλαβόμενοι ὁποίας ἦν αἱρέσεως ὁ Μαρκιανός, (ὃς) καὶ ἑαυτῷ ἐναντιοῦτο, μὴ νοῶν ἃ ἐλάλει". It does not say the backers followed him, and neither does Serapion.
8. "He borrowed the book from the group that had used it from the first, and he read it through." SUMMARY HOLDS. Schwartz 2622–2625: "παρ᾽ ἄλλων τῶν ἀσκησάντων αὐτὸ τοῦτο τὸ εὐαγγέλιον, τοῦτ᾽ ἐστὶν παρὰ τῶν διαδόχων τῶν καταρξαμένων αὐτοῦ … χρησάμενοι παρ᾽ αὐτῶν διελθεῖν". The Greek has "the successors of those who began it". "The group that had used it from the first" is a fair short form of that line of users. The name Docetae is rightly left unglossed.
9. "Most of it, he found, agreed with the Savior's true teaching, but some things had been added, and he set those out for them." HELD. Schwartz 2626–2627: "τὰ μὲν πλείονα τοῦ ὀρθοῦ λόγου τοῦ σωτῆρος, τινὰ δὲ προσδιεσταλμένα. ἃ καὶ ὑπετάξαμεν ὑμῖν".
10. "What he wrote to them, which Eusebius of Caesarea kept in his Church History a century later, lays down his rule: 'Brothers, we receive Peter … not handed down to us.'" HELD. Schwartz 2510–2513: "ἡμεῖς γάρ, ἀδελφοί, καὶ Πέτρον καὶ τοὺς ἄλλους ἀποστόλους ἀποδεχόμεθα ὡς Χριστόν, τὰ δὲ ὀνόματι αὐτῶν ψευδεπίγραφα ὡς ἔμπειροι παραιτούμεθα, γινώσκοντες ὅτι τὰ τοιαῦτα οὐ παρελάβομεν". Rufinus 2575–2578 agrees. Every clause is there and nothing is added. "As people with experience" renders ὡς ἔμπειροι. "A century later": Eusebius writes into the 320s.

**P4**
11. "In the same years, a book of Paul's travels and deeds told of a girl named Thecla." HELD. Tertullian c. 200 knows a writing under Paul's name with Thecla's example (CSEL 20 12260–12262). "Travels": Jerome calls the book the "Acts" (= "Journeyings", περίοδοι) of Paul and Thecla (npnf203 39502 and note 2371).
12. "In its later copies she hears Paul at Iconium, refuses the man she was promised to, and is condemned to the fire for it." HELD. ANF8 47762–47767 ("betrothed to a man named Thamyris … listened night and day"); 47884–47892 ("burn in the midst of the theatre her that will not marry").
13. "The book says a storm of rain and hail puts the fire out, and she is saved." HELD. ANF8 47907–47911: "a cloud … full of water and hail … And the fire was put out, and Thecla saved." The wonder is in the book's voice.
14. "Later a governor sends her to the beasts for shaming a powerful man who had seized her in the street." SUMMARY HOLDS. ANF8, in 47944–47957: "a certain Syriarch, Alexander … being of great power, himself embraced her in the street. But she would not endure it … made him a laughing-stock … he condemned her to the wild beasts." Given "she would not endure it", "seized" is fair for "embraced".
15. "In the arena she throws herself into a pit of water and baptizes herself in the name of Jesus Christ, on what she calls her last day." HELD. Gebhardt 10407–10413: "εἶδεν ὄρυγμα μέγα πλῆρες ὕδατος … ἔβαλεν ἑαυτὴν λέγουσα· Ἐν τῷ ὀνόματι Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ ὑστέρᾳ ἡμέρᾳ βαπτίζομαι."
16. "Afterward Paul sends her to teach the word of God." HELD. Gebhardt 10538–10540: "Ὕπαγε καὶ δίδασκε τὸν λόγον τοῦ θεοῦ."

**P5**
17. "Some Christians cited her example as permission for women to teach and baptize." SUMMARY HOLDS. CSEL 20 12260–12262: "quodsi quae Pauli perperam inscripta sunt exemplum Theclae ad licentiam mulierum docendi tinguendique defendunt, sciant…". Grammatically the writings "defend". But "sciant" ("let them know") speaks to people who rely on them, and the sentence before speaks of the woman "quae usurpauit docere" (12257). So the "if" is Tertullian's rhetoric, not real doubt.
18. "Tertullian, writing in Carthage in Roman Africa, answered them that Paul did not even let a woman learn too boldly." HELD. CSEL 20 12264–12266: "qui ne discere quidem constanter mulieri permisit". Carthage: Jerome, npnf203 40496–40499, a reliable later witness, the Outline's A4.
19. "He also told of the book's writer, a presbyter, an elder of the church: 'Let them know that the presbyter in Asia … and he left his office.'" HELD. CSEL 20 12262–12264: "sciant in Asia presbyterum, qui eam scripturam construxit, quasi titulo Pauli de suo cumulans, conuictum atque confessum id se amore Pauli fecisse loco decessisse". "Found out" for conuictum and "left his office" for loco decessisse are faithful. There is no "before John" (Junius's conjecture and Jerome's addition), rightly.

**P6**
20. "Other books told what the Gospels leave untold." SUMMARY HOLDS. This is a judgment from the Protevangelium's content (ANF8 36612–36827), none of which is in the four Gospels.
21. "One under James's name, kept in many later copies, tells of Mary's childhood." SUMMARY HOLDS. ANF8 37167: "And I James that wrote this history"; the manuscripts are in ANF8's note at 36602–36606 ("a manuscript of the eleventh century … At least seventeen other forms exist"). Note, not blocking: the sentence states no date, and "later copies" names its witness honestly. It sits among the second-to-fourth-century people, but it does not claim that they read this text. Whether Origen's "Book of James" is the same book is a Dominant Modern Reconstruction, and the story rightly leaves Origen out of it.
22. "She is born to a childless couple, and from the age of three she lives in the temple, fed, it says, by an angel's hand, until she is betrothed to Joseph." HELD. ANF8 36715 ("I the childless shall conceive"); 36784 ("the child was three years old"); 36797–36798 ("she received food from the hand of an angel"); 37038 ("betrothed to me", Joseph). The wonder is in the book's voice ("it says").

SUMMARY HOLDS. npnf201 18256–18257 ("cited by heretics under the name of the apostles"); 14566–14569 (the Gospel of Peter, "οὐδ᾽ ὅλως ἐν καθολικαῖς ἴσμεν παραδεδομένα"); 18333 ("all of them to be cast aside as absurd and impious").

## The heart

It is the right heart. The sources show these books through the people who met them: a congregation reading one, a bishop judging it by what was handed down, a presbyter who wrote one for love of Paul and lost his office, a historian sorting them, and a pilgrim hearing one read in full. That matches the shape ruling. The heart is told by about word 135, the end of P2: who, when, what the books were, and the judging. That is inside the first minute.
