## 1. The test (tradition)

- **Who these people were:** filled. Christians who wrote, read and judged books under apostles' names: a village church, a bishop, a presbyter, Tertullian, Eusebius, a pilgrim.
- **What happened to them:** filled. Serapion allows the gospel, then refuses it. The presbyter is found out and leaves his office. Eusebius ranks the books. Thecla's book is still read at her shrine.
- **What their faith was about:** filled, but thinly. The judges' side is clear: what was "handed down" from the apostles. The readers' side rests on plot (Thecla, Mary's childhood) and one motive ("for love of Paul"). The Gospel of Peter's fault is never said (see item 4).
- **Why the connection matters:** filled. Rejected books were still read in church: the Acts of Thecla read in full at her memorial.

## 2. The opening

After three sentences I have who (Bishop Serapion, the church at Rhossus), where (Rhossus, Syrian coast near Antioch), when (about 200) and why there (a quarrel over a gospel under Peter's name). Nothing missing. That this gospel is outside the New Testament comes only in paragraph two, which is in time.

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing (MINOR)

1. "Some Christians cited her example as leave for women to teach and baptize." "Leave" as permission is lost on a second-language ear; it is heard as "depart". Change: "as permission for women to teach and baptize."
2. "In one form of Thecla's book, this was where she taught many and fell asleep." "Fell asleep" is a church euphemism for death; heard literally, it means she took a nap. Change: "...where she taught many and died."
3. "On a low hill outside the city stood her church, with cells of men and women around it." "Cells" is heard as prison cells. Change: "with the small rooms of monks and nuns around it", or whatever plain gloss the source allows.
4. "The Acts of Paul he counted among books known to most church writers but not genuine." Nothing ties "the Acts of Paul" to "a book of Paul's travels and deeds" two paragraphs back, and the close then calls it "the Acts of Thecla". Three names; the listener does not know Eusebius is judging the Thecla book. Change: "The Acts of Paul, the book that told of Thecla, he counted among..." (as the outline allows).

## 4. Bare statements (BLOCKING)

1. "After he left Rhossus, Serapion was told that the book's backers had minds caught up in some heresy, a teaching he held false." "A teaching he held false" glosses the word, not the teaching. The listener cannot say what it was or why it mattered. Change: name the teaching in plain words, if the outline holds it; otherwise cut "had minds caught up in some heresy, a teaching he held false" and tell only what the source gives.
2. "Most of it, he found, agreed with the Savior's true teaching, but some things had been added, and he set those out for them." What was added is never said, so the story's first quarrel resolves on a blank. Change: say what was added, if the outline holds it; if the source does not say, keep "some things had been added" but cut nothing else, and do not fill the gap.

## 6. Tells

**Lines that sound machine-made (not blocking):**
- Glossing by apposition, six times: "Rhossus, a town on the coast...", "some heresy, a teaching he held false", "the Christian Apocrypha, the books that were never canon", "handed down, received in the churches from the apostles", "a presbyter, an elder of the church", "Seleucia, on the south coast of today's Turkey". Each is fine alone. Together they make a pattern a skeptic hears as a template. Work two or three into the action instead.
- "These are the Christian Apocrypha, the books that were never canon." It repeats the sentence before it ("books that never entered the New Testament") as a definition. Cut it, or merge the name into the sentence before.
- The last clause, "the book whose writer had left his office." This is a crafted callback, the narrator tying a bow. It is not a moral close, but it is the narrator's irony, not the record's. Ending on "...the whole of the Acts of Thecla was read." ends on the subject.
