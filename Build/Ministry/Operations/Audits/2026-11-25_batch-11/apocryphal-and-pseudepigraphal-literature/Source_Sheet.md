## C. Impact, heaviest first (what later witnesses carried on and used)

1. **The test of what was handed down.** Serapion's rule (B1b) is the line Eusebius keeps a century later, and Eusebius builds his own sorting on the same ground: what was "handed down" and what church writers used (B5a, B5d). Athanasius in 367 calls the apocrypha "an invention of heretics, who write them when they choose, … assigning to them a date, that so, using them as ancient writings, they may find occasion to lead astray the simple" (`npnf204_athanasius-select-works-letters.xml` : 68824–68829). Defended: the apostles' teaching as received. (Athanasius's live story tells his Festal Letter 39: not told here.)
2. **Thecla's book read at her shrine.** The book whose writer left his post is read in full at her memorial in Egeria's day (B6c), with many cells of men and women (B6a). Jerome (c. 392) still knows "the Acts of Paul and Thecla and all the fable about the lion baptized by him" and counts them apocryphal (npnf203 : 39502–39507).
3. **A book of James and Mary's childhood.** Origen reports Joseph's earlier wife from a "Book of James" (B4a); the surviving book tells Mary's birth, temple childhood and betrothal (B4d–f), kept in many later manuscripts (B4g). Later Latin retellings (Pseudo-Matthew, the Gospel of the Nativity of Mary) are, in the translator's view, compilations from it (ANF8 intro : 35944–35950). These are later books; told only as what came of it.
4. **Jerome's warning (c. 403).** "Let her avoid all apocryphal writings, and if she is led to read / such not by the truth of the doctrines which they contain but out of / respect for the miracles contained in them; let her understand that / they are not really written by those to whom they are ascribed, that / many faulty elements have been introduced into them, and that it / requires infinite discretion to look for gold in the midst of dirt." (`npnf206_jerome-principal-works.xml` : 20705–20710). English only (1893); Latin not held.
5. **The Gospel of Peter found.** A fragment in Peter's voice ("But I Simon Peter and Andrew", ANF9 : 784) was found in a grave at Akhmim in 1886, published 1892 (ANF9 : 435–441). That it is Serapion's book is a modern identification (Dominant Modern Reconstruction).

## E. Where the sources disagree or come late

- **The presbyter before John.** Tertullian names no judge (B2b). Jerome, about 190 years later, says the presbyter was "convicted by John" (npnf203 : 39511–39514; PL23 Latin `jerome_opera-pl23-part1-with-regula-pachomii-lat_migne1883.txt` : 49226–49228: "convictum apud Joannem"). The CSEL apparatus shows "apud Ioannem" entered Tertullian's text only as Junius's conjecture (B2c). Later legend; Leave out.
- **"Removed" or "left".** Tertullian: "loco decessisse" (left his place); Jerome: "loco excidisse" (fell from his place, PL23 49229–49230) and "resigned his office" (npnf203 39515). Whether he was forced is not said.
- **Thecla's ending.** Two forms (B3d).
- **Docetae.** Serapion gives the name only. McGiffert's note argues the sect so named by Hippolytus held that Christ had a true body (npnf201 : 33743ff., the long note 1838). Modern; do not gloss the name from it.
- **Origen's Book of James = Protevangelium?** Modern editors disagree (B4b).
- **Egeria's date and name** are editors' reckoning (McClure–Feltoe intro 382, 1068).

## F. Explain it (each thing or act the story could name)

| Thing or act | Explanation in its own terms | Source lines |
|---|---|---|
| Gospel of Peter | A gospel "put forward under the name of Peter" and read at Rhossus. Serapion: "most of it" of the Saviour's right teaching, "some things added". Eusebius: not handed down; used by heretics; to be cast aside. | Schwartz 2514–2515, 2626–2627; npnf201 14462–14572, 18256–18333 |
| Heresy (Serapion's word) | A teaching he held false; their mind "lurking in some heresy". Eusebius's gloss: people turned aside "into other teachings" (ἑτεροδόξους διδασκαλίας). | Schwartz 2505, 2517 |
| Writings "falsely put under their names" (ψευδεπίγραφα) | Books bearing an apostle's name that the apostle did not write. Serapion: not handed down. Athanasius (enemy's view): heretics write them and give them a date to pass as ancient. | Schwartz 2511; npnf204 68824–68829 |
| "Handed down" | What the churches had received from the apostles; Serapion's and Eusebius's test. | Schwartz 2512–2513; npnf201 14566–14569 |
| Presbyter | A church elder; Tertullian calls the writer "presbyterum". | CSEL 20 12262 |
| Acts of Paul / Thecla's example | A writing under Paul's name in which Thecla baptizes herself and is sent by Paul to teach; cited as licence for women to teach and baptize. | CSEL 20 12260–12262; Gebhardt 10412–10413, 10540 |
| Tertullian's objection | Paul would not let a woman even learn boldly (1 Cor 14:34–35). | CSEL 20 12264–12267 |
| His leaving office | Because he was proved the writer and confessed. Tertullian gives the reason he confessed: love of Paul. | CSEL 20 12263–12264 |
| Book of James | A book under James's name; Origen: some held from it that Jesus's brothers were Joseph's sons by an earlier wife, wishing to keep Mary's virginity honoured. The surviving book has James claim to write it. | ANF9 30600–30610; ANF8 37167 |
| Eusebius's "rejected" (νόθοι) | Books known and read but not genuine (Acts of Paul). Below them, books under apostles' names used by heretics, "absurd and impious". | npnf201 18031–18036, 18256–18333 |
| Egeria's reading at the shrine | Prayer at the memorial and the whole of the Acts of Thecla read, as part of a pilgrim's visit. | McClure–Feltoe 3815–3819 |

The book's own side: the book never calls itself false. Its side of the act is Thecla's own word: "In the name of Jesus Christ I am baptized on my last day" (Gebhardt 10412–10413), and Paul's commission (10540). The presbyter's side, as Tertullian reports it: he wrote "for love of Paul".
