## 1. The test

- **Who these people were:** filled. Christians of Phrygia in the second century, who called themselves the New Prophecy, led by Montanus, Priscilla and Maximilla.
- **What happened to them:** filled. Tested and shut out by the churches, they lasted, and under Justinian they burned in their own churches.
- **What their faith was about:** filled. The Helper, the Holy Spirit, spoke through their prophets now, and called for a stricter life.
- **Why the connection matters:** filled, but thin. Tertullian taking up their cause is clear. The listener hears that "the main church" opposed them, but cannot say why that quarrel matters to anyone after them, except that it was about strictness and prophecy and not belief in God. Not blocking.

## 2. The opening (first three sentences)

- **Where: BLOCKING.** "In a village called Ardabau, where Mysia meets Phrygia" gives three names this listener has never heard. The country only comes in sentence 5 ("what is now western Turkey"). Change: "In a village called Ardabau, in the uplands of what is now western Turkey, where Mysia meets Phrygia, ..."
- **When:** missing until sentence 5 ("in the second century"). MINOR; fixed if the time comes in a clause in sentence 1 or 2.
- **Who:** "a recent convert" to what? The listener does not learn Montanus is a Christian until sentence 5. "what an enemy calls" also leaves open whose enemy. MINOR: "a recent convert to the Christian faith named Montanus".
- **Why there:** not given; not required at this point.

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing

1. **BLOCKING.** "Apollonius ... says Montanus named two small Phrygian towns, Pepuza and Tymion, Jerusalem, to gather people there..." Heard once, this sounds like a list of three towns. Lost: that he gave the two towns the name Jerusalem. The later Pepuza scenes ("the Jerusalem from above came down there") hang on it, so the thread is lost. Change: "...says Montanus gave the name Jerusalem to two small Phrygian towns, Pepuza and Tymion, to gather people there from everywhere, ..."
2. **BLOCKING.** "The faithful of Asia met often..." A second-language listener hears the continent of Asia (China, India). Lost: the place; the story suddenly seems to move far away. Change: "The faithful of the Roman province of Asia, the churches around them in western Turkey, met often..."
3. MINOR. "Epiphanius, who wrote against the movement about 375, kept those words." followed by "Around 200, at Carthage..." The listener jumps from 375 back to 200 and loses where we are in time. Change: drop "about 375" here, and give it at Epiphanius's later appearance: "Epiphanius, writing about 375, says that in his day Pepuza lay razed and deserted, ..."
4. MINOR. "The prophets' side called the bishops killers of the prophets the Lord had promised." Lost on one hearing: whether the Lord promised the prophets or the killing. Change: "The prophets' side called the bishops killers of prophets, the prophets the Lord had promised."
5. MINOR. "The elder dared that spirit to prove its power on the bishops." Lost: what the dare was, and why the elder speaks here. Understood only as "he challenged her". Leave, or the reviser checks the outline for its plainer sense.

Long but understood, not counted: the Paraclete sentence in paragraph 3; "barred from communion: shut out of the church's fellowship and its shared meal".

## 4. Bare statements (Part 1, Explain it)

1. **BLOCKING.** "prophesied against the church's settled custom." Which custom? The listener hears a charge with no content. Change: say what custom the outline gives, or cut "and prophesied against the church's settled custom".
2. **BLOCKING.** "He defended their rule of one marriage only." This listener hears "against having two wives at once". Lost: that it means no second marriage, even after a husband or wife has died, if the outline holds that. Change: "their rule of one marriage only, with no second marriage even after a husband or wife died," as the outline gives it.
3. **BLOCKING.** "urged facing martyrdom rather than flight." "Martyrdom" is not explained, and nothing in the story says Christians were being hunted or killed, so the listener cannot see what "flight" is from. Change, as the outline holds it: "and, when Christians were arrested for their faith, urged them to face death rather than run."

## 6. Tells

1. **BLOCKING.** "The end did not come." A short narrator's line that only says what did not happen, for effect. Change: cut it; the move to Tertullian and the centuries that follow already show it.

No dashes, no stock words, no "not X but Y" in the narrator's voice (the "not because ... It is because" turn is Tertullian's own quote), no moral close. The ending is a scene. Good.

## 8. Pull and attention

- **Pull:** yes. A man in a village falling into a frenzy, some telling him to be quiet and others urging him on, is a strong first image.
- **Attention first drifts:** paragraph 2, "That enemy, a church elder whose name is lost, wrote within living memory, and the historian Eusebius later copied his account..." The scene stops for a lesson on who wrote what. It drifts again at the Apollonius sentence (3.1), where the town names pile up.
- **Would listen to the end:** yes. The wolf saying of Maximilla, the woman-shaped Christ at Pepuza, and the burning churches carry it.
