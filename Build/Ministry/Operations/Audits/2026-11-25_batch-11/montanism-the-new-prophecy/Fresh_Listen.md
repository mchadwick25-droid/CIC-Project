## 1. The test

- **Who these people were:** filled. "Christians of Phrygia, in the second century", with Montanus, Priscilla and Maximilla, who called themselves "the New Prophecy."
- **What happened to them:** filled. The churches of Asia examine and expel them; Tertullian takes up their cause around 200; the faithful still go to a ruined Pepuza around 375; under Justinian they burn themselves in their churches.
- **What their faith was about:** filled. "The Paraclete ... spoke through their prophets now, in ecstasy", and "called the church to a stricter life": more fasts, one marriage only, no flight from death.
- **Why the connection matters:** filled, though leaner than the rest. Tertullian taking up their cause, and his own words on what the quarrel was and was not about, carry it. The House Church and imperial church links show only as "the main church" and "the empire."

## 2. The opening

After the first three sentences I know who (Montanus, a new convert, and those who heard him) and where (Ardabau, in the uplands of western Turkey). **When** is missing until sentence 4 ("in the second century"), which is still early and within the first minute. **Why there** is never given; the old sources may not give it, and I was not lost. Not blocking. The essence (who, when, where, what held them: the Paraclete speaking now, a stricter life) is told by about word 140, inside the first minute.

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing (MINOR)

1. "The prophets' side called their opponents killers of the prophets, for refusing the prophets the Lord had promised to send." Three "prophets" in one breath; I lost which prophets were promised, and when. Meaning lost: that Jesus had promised prophets, and to refuse these was to refuse his promise. Change, if the source carries it: "for refusing the prophets the Lord had promised" becomes "for refusing the prophets that the Lord, they said, had promised to send." (Trust reader to confirm the source's wording.)
2. "The elder dared that spirit to prove its power by forcing the bishops who had been there to confess it." On one hearing, "confess it" has no clear object, and "who had been there" does not tie back to the bishops who were stopped. Meaning lost: what the bishops were to confess. Change: "the bishops who had been there" becomes "the bishops who had come to test her", and "confess it" is given what the source says they would confess. (Trust reader to supply from the source; no wording added here.)

Near-miss, understood after the following sentences: Tertullian's "fasting more often than marrying" and "divide Jesus Christ." The next sentences explain the first; the second stays opaque to me but does not cost the thread.
