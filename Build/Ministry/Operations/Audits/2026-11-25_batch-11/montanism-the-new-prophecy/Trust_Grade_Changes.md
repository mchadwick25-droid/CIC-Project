**1. "There, in the second century, a recent Christian convert named Montanus fell into what an enemy calls a frenzy and began to say strange things."** HELD. HE 29617-29627: "a recent convert, Montanus by name ... being suddenly in a sort of frenzy and ecstasy, he raved, and began to babble and utter strange things." "In the second century" matches every held dating: PanH 17019 (the nineteenth year of Antoninus Pius); HE 30456-30458 (Apollonius, forty years on); HE 26706-26707 with 27655-27671 (word reaching Gaul in 177). The start stays unstated within the century, as the outline requires.

**1a. "Montanus and his followers were Christians of Phrygia, and two women, Priscilla and Maximilla, prophesied as he did."** HELD. This is the old sentence with "in the second century" removed; that phrase now comes earlier. HE 29692, 29707-29709 ("he stirred up besides two women ... like the person already mentioned"); HE 29266-29269 (the names); HE 29714 ("those of the Phrygians that were deceived").

**2. "The faithful of the Roman province of Asia met often, in many places, to examine the new sayings, whose manner, the elder says, broke the church's settled custom."** SUMMARY HOLDS. HE 29721-29736: "the faithful in Asia met often in many places throughout Asia to consider this matter, and examined the novel utterances". The added clause is credited to the elder and rests on HE 29626-29629, "prophesying in a manner contrary to the constant custom of the Church handed down by tradition from the beginning", which the elder extends to the women at HE 29707-29709. It is told as his description of the sayings. It is not given as the meetings' reason, which the source does not state (sheet B4).

**2, context. "Finding them novel and profane, they drove the prophets' followers out, barred from communion ..."** Unchanged. With the new clause before it, the order of the two sentences does not make the manner the meetings' stated reason. No change needed.

**3. "If the spirit had such power, the elder answered, let it make those bishops acknowledge it."** SUMMARY HOLDS. HE 29831-29849: after the oracle "I am word and spirit and power", the elder writes: "But let him show clearly and prove the power in the spirit. And by the spirit let him compel those to confess him who were then present for the purpose of proving and reasoning with the talkative spirit,—those eminent men and bishops, Zoticus ... and Julian". "Those bishops" correctly points back to the bishops of the sentence before, who came to refute the spirit (HE 29823-29826). "Acknowledge" for "confess him" and "make" for "compel" keep the challenge's force.

**4. "That enemy, a church elder, argued with them while the movement was new, and most of the record, like his, comes from their opponents."** SUMMARY HOLDS. HE 29544 and 29556 (note, Greek συμπρεσβύτερος): "our fellow-presbyter Zoticus", so he was an elder. HE 29519-29541: "being recently in Ancyra ... I found the church there greatly agitated by this novelty ... we disputed in the church many days". That most of the record is hostile is shown by the held witnesses themselves (Serapion HE 30557-30562; Apollonius HE 30225-30289; Hippolytus; Epiphanius), with Tertullian the only one on their side. Removing Eusebius's copy takes nothing out of the record and adds nothing to it. The outline sends that detail to the card.

**5. "He claims the emperor wanted blood and riches."** SUMMARY HOLDS. Proc 2164-2166: "he also endeavoured, by various devices, to drench the earth in human blood, to carry off more riches for himself, and to murder many of his subjects. He proceeded as follows." Procopius names this as the motive behind the order that follows (Proc 2167-2173), and the brief credits it to him as a claim. "He" plainly means Procopius, named in the sentence before.

**6. "Epiphanius, that later opponent, writing about 375, says that in his day Pepuza lay deserted, yet the believers still went there for their rites ..."** HELD. PanH 18466: "Τιμῶσι δὲ οἱ τοιοῦτοι καὶ τόπον τινὰ ἔρημον ἐν τῇ Φρυγίᾳ" ("they honour a certain deserted place in Phrygia"), and PanH 18512-18514: "Πέπουζάν ποτε καλουμένην πόλιν ... καί φασιν ἐκεῖσε κατιέναι τὴν ἄνωθεν Ἱερουσαλήμ. ὅθεν ἐκεῖ ἀπερχόμενοι μυστήριά τινα ἐπιτελοῦσιν". Dropping "razed" (PanH 18512, "νῦν δὲ ἠδαφισμένην") takes out a word whose cause no held source gives. The rest of the sentence is unchanged.
