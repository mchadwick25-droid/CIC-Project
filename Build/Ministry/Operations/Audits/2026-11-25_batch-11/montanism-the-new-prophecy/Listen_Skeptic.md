## 1. The kind kit's test

- **Who they were:** filled. "Christians of Phrygia... in the second century... the New Prophecy".
- **What happened to them:** filled. Examined, "drove the prophets' followers out", then under Justinian "died in the flames".
- **What their faith was about:** filled. "the Paraclete... spoke through their prophets now... and called the church to a stricter life", plus fasts, one marriage, martyrdom.
- **Why the connection matters:** filled, thinly. Tertullian "took up their cause in Latin" carries it. The listener hears that he defended them, but not what he carried on from them.

## 2. The opening (BLOCKING)

After sentences 1-3, the listener has who (Montanus, "a recent convert", with "Holy Spirit" hinting at Christians). **Where** is lost: "where Mysia meets Phrygia" means nothing to a listener with no map. **When** is missing until paragraph 3 ("in the second century"). Paragraph 2 then breaks the scene with a sentence about the record. The essence is not complete until about word 195, past the first minute.

**Change:** Sentence 1: "In a village called Ardabau, in the hills of what is now western Turkey, in the second century, a recent Christian convert named Montanus fell into what an enemy calls a frenzy and began to say strange things." Then cut "in the uplands of what is now western Turkey, in the second century" from paragraph 3. Move or recast paragraph 2 (see 5).

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing

1. "Apollonius... says Montanus named two small Phrygian towns, Pepuza and Tymion, Jerusalem, to gather people there from everywhere..." Lost: that he *called the towns "Jerusalem"*. The appositive pile hides it, and the later Pepuza and Jerusalem paragraph rests on it. MINOR, close to losing the thread. Change: "...says Montanus gave the name Jerusalem to two small Phrygian towns, Pepuza and Tymion, so that people would gather there from everywhere. He also says Montanus set up collectors of money and paid his preachers."
2. "The elder dared that spirit to prove its power on the bishops." Lost: what the dare was, or what it shows. MINOR. Change: say plainly what the elder challenged it to do, as the outline gives it, or cut.
3. "That enemy, a church elder whose name is lost, wrote within living memory..." Lost: within living memory *of what*. MINOR. Folded into the fix at 5.
4. "The prophets' side called the bishops killers of the prophets the Lord had promised." Lost: nobody has been killed in the story, so "killers" is puzzling, and the listener can't tell what "the Lord had promised" belongs to. MINOR. Change: "The prophets' side called the bishops killers of the prophets, the prophets the Lord had promised to send."

Near miss, not counted: "They held that the Paraclete, the Helper Jesus had promised, the Holy Spirit, spoke through their prophets now..." (35 words, three stacked appositives). It is understood, but only just.

## 4. Bare statements (BLOCKING each)

1. "prophesied against the church's settled custom": what custom? Give the custom as the elder names it, or cut the clause.
2. "after her there would be no prophet any more, but the end": a listener with no church background will not know that "the end" means the end of the world. Change: "...but the end of the world."
3. "saying the Jerusalem from above came down there": the teaching is named but never explained. Add a clause in plain words, for example: "...the heavenly city of God, which they believed would come down to earth there." Use the outline's own wording.
4. "their rule of one marriage only": heard as plain monogamy. Change, if the outline holds it: "their rule that no one marry again, even after a husband or wife had died."
5. "ordered groups like theirs to give up their beliefs": "groups like theirs" is a label that is not explained, and no reason is given. Name the groups as Procopius gives them, and his reason if he gives one. Otherwise tell it as he gives it.

## 5. Citations read aloud not called for (MINOR)

1. Paragraph 2, whole sentence: "That enemy... wrote within living memory, and the historian Eusebius later copied his account, which like most of the record comes from the movement's opponents." This is a stock how-we-know sentence, a report about the record, and it stalls the opening. Narrator's voice, folded into the paragraph 7 charge: "The elder, a churchman who wrote while some who had known Montanus still lived, says Montanus longed to lead..." The fact that the sources are hostile then carries through "enemy" and "opponent" as it already does.
2. "Epiphanius, who wrote against the movement about 375, kept those words." This sentence only cites. Change: "Maximilla also said, as Epiphanius records two centuries later, that after her there would be no prophet any more, but the end of the world."
3. "Her words survive in the enemy's book:" Change: "Maximilla answered:"

Craft-check lines: "The elder says", "Apollonius... says" (an enemy's charge), "Epiphanius writes" (he wrote long after) and "says Procopius, a writer hostile to Justinian" (blame) are all called for by Part 1. They stay.

## 6. Tells

- **BLOCKING:** "The end did not come." This is a narrator's punchline, the narrator's drama that Part 1 forbids, and the dry-irony cadence a skeptic hears as machine-made. Cut it.
- Machine-sounding lines, not separately blocking:
  - "which like most of the record comes from the movement's opponents": a reflexive source caveat (cut at 5.1).
  - "whose name is lost": a clause that only says what is missing.
  - "the Paraclete, the Helper Jesus had promised, the Holy Spirit": a gloss chain.
  - The colon-and-define device used three times ("barred from communion: shut out of...", "fasts of their own: Stations, ...", "the short creed he set out: ..."). It sounds like glossary entries pasted in. Work at least one into the sentence as part of what happens.
  - Paragraph 3, sentence 1 packs who, where, when and the two women into one encyclopedia line. This is the kind kit's "encyclopedia entry" trap. The fix at 2 relieves it.

## 7. Words, runs, tone

- Unexplained: "Mysia" (fixed at 2), "profane" (say "irreverent" or "an insult to God"), "rites" (say "worship"). MINOR.
- Short run: "...kept those words. The end did not come." (fixed at 4.2, 5.2 and 6).
- Talking down: none.

## 8. Pull, drift, finish

The opening pulls: a man in a frenzy, a crowd split over whether to silence him. Attention first drifts at paragraph 2 ("That enemy, a church elder whose name is lost..."), where the scene stops for a note on sources. It drifts again in the Tertullian paragraph, where the fast list and the creed list run back to back. I would listen to the end; the Phrygian churches burning is a strong close on the community itself.
