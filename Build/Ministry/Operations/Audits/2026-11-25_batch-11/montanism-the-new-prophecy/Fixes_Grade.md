## What the sources hold

1. **"Novel and profane": what.** The anonymous (contemporary, hostile) does not list the sayings the meetings condemned (HE 29735-29736). What his own account faults is the manner: Montanus prophesied "in a sort of frenzy and ecstasy ... in a manner contrary to the constant custom of the Church handed down by tradition from the beginning" (HE 29626-29629). The women "talked wildly and unreasonably and strangely" (HE 29707-29709). The spirit "taught them to revile the entire universal Church" (HE 29716-29717). Second English, ANF07 11308: "novel doctrines ... declared them to be spurious". Greek not held. Documented that he says it. That the meetings judged on the manner is his sequence, not his statement (Inferential-Thin), so the brief gives the manner as the elder's description of the sayings, not as the meetings' reason. Added to sheet B4 and outline beat 3.
2. **Justinian's order: why.** Procopius gives his own hostile reason. Proc 2164-2167: "he also endeavoured, by various devices, to drench the earth in human blood, to carry off more riches for himself, and to murder many of his subjects. He proceeded as follows." The heretics' churches were "rich beyond belief" (Proc 2173-2174), and Justinian "sequestrated all the property of these churches" (Proc 2184-2185). Contemporary, hostile. Documented that he says it; Contested as the real motive. No other held source gives a reason for this order. Novel 45 (59038-59039) is a different law. Michael's late charge (Mich 20587-20588) is given for the burning at Pepuza. Added to sheet E6 and outline beat 7. The line changes (change 5).
3. **When.** "In the second century" (outline Setting; Contested start, HE 29591-29592, PanH 17019) moves into sentence 2 of the opening. Outline Setting notes it.

**Sources for the new wording.**
- Change 3: HE 29852-29858, "let him show clearly and prove the power in the spirit. And by the spirit let him compel those to confess him who were then present ... those eminent men and bishops". "Those bishops" points back to "When bishops came to refute the spirit in Maximilla". "Such power" points back to her "word and spirit and power". It names who must acknowledge what, and it says nothing past the source.
- Change 2: HE 29626-29629, credited to the elder, so "frenzy" stays his word.
- Change 5: Proc 2164-2167. "He" is Procopius; the emperor is named apart.
- Change 4: "church elder", HE 29540 ("our fellow-presbyter Zoticus"). It keeps the one sentence on the hostile record, which Part 1 allows.

## Cuts, and where each goes

- **1a.** "in the second century" in paragraph 2: Leave out. It moves to the opening and is not lost.
- **4.** The witness sentence loses Eusebius's copying and "whom Eusebius does not name": card ("the elder's account survives in Eusebius's Church History V.16"). This also answers the listener's drift flag. The hostile-record clause stays.
- **3.** The dare sentence is shorter. Nothing goes to the card.
- **6.** "razed": card. Epiphanius's "now razed to the ground" (PanH 18512-18513) goes to the card. No held source says who razed it, and bare_B asked who. The town's desertion and the rites there stay.

None of these cuts touches the heart, the record's force (the condemnation, the "killers of the prophets", Maximilla's wolf oracle, the fire), or a gloss Part 1 requires.
