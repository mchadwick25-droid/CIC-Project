# Outline check: Montanism, the New Prophecy

## My account of the heart (written before reading Outline.md)

From the census entry, the slate job, the source sheet and what the subject is known for:

- **Essence:** In second-century Phrygia, in the uplands of what is now western Turkey, a convert named Montanus and two women, Priscilla and Maximilla, began to prophesy in ecstasy. They and their followers called it the New Prophecy: the Paraclete Jesus promised was speaking now, through them, and calling the church to a stricter life. They were held together by that claim of present prophecy and the discipline it brought.
- **Who they were:** the New Prophecy (their own name; "Phrygians", "Cataphrygians", "Montanists" were their opponents' labels).
- **When and where:** second century, from Ardabau on the Mysia-Phrygia border and Pepuza/Tymion in Phrygia; active and known as far as Gaul by the 170s; later Carthage (Tertullian), Rome; still numerous in Phrygia into the fifth century and present in the sixth.
- **What they faced:** the churches of Asia testing and condemning the utterances (repeated meetings, expulsion from communion); bishops coming to refute or exorcise the spirit in the prophetesses and being stopped; the failed end Maximilla foretold; then imperial law (Constantine takes their meeting houses; Justinian's order, and the Phrygian Montanists burning themselves in their churches, per Procopius).
- **What they believed:** the same rule of faith about God and Christ (Tertullian; Hippolytus agrees for most); the Paraclete leading discipline to maturity; ecstasy as the mark of true prophecy (the point of dispute); the heavenly Jerusalem coming down at Pepuza. Contested: whether Montanus was called the Paraclete (hostile witnesses) or the Paraclete spoke through the prophets (Tertullian).
- **How they practiced:** extra fasts and dry eating, one marriage only, no flight from martyrdom, prophecy in the Sunday gathering tested afterward (Carthage sister), pilgrimage and rites at Pepuza; women prophets and (per Epiphanius, late) women clergy; patriarchs of Pepuza over bishops (Jerome).
- **What held them together:** present prophecy of the Paraclete, and the stricter discipline it commanded.
- **What came of them:** heaviest, the case against them, gathered by Eusebius, which fixed the church's tests for a prophet (not out of his senses; examined by the churches); then Tertullian's Latin defence; then imperial suppression; survival at Pepuza and in Phrygia for centuries. No church descends from them.

Sources hold: all of this as the hostile witnesses and Tertullian give it. Not held: Pepuza found in 2000 (stays out), any Montanist writing, "c. 165".
