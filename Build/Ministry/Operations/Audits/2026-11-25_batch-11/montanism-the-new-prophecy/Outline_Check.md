# Outline check: Montanism, the New Prophecy

## My account of the heart (written before reading Outline.md)

From the census entry, the slate job, the source sheet and what the subject is known for:

- **Essence:** In second-century Phrygia, in the uplands of what is now western Turkey, a convert named Montanus and two women, Priscilla and Maximilla, began to prophesy in ecstasy. They and their followers called it the New Prophecy: the Paraclete Jesus promised was speaking now, through them, and calling the church to a stricter life. They were held together by that claim of present prophecy and the discipline it brought.
- **Who they were:** the New Prophecy (their own name; "Phrygians", "Cataphrygians", "Montanists" were their opponents' labels).
- **When and where:** second century, from Ardabau on the Mysia-Phrygia border and Pepuza/Tymion in Phrygia; active and known as far as Gaul by the 170s; later Carthage (Tertullian), Rome; still numerous in Phrygia into the fifth century and present in the sixth.
- **What they faced:** the churches of Asia testing and condemning the utterances (repeated meetings, expulsion from communion); bishops coming to refute or exorcise the spirit in the prophetesses and being stopped; the failed end Maximilla foretold; then imperial law (Constantine takes their meeting houses; Justinian's order, and the Phrygian Montanists burning themselves in their churches, per Procopius).
- **What they believed:** the same rule of faith about God and Christ (Tertullian; Hippolytus agrees for most); the Paraclete leading discipline to maturity; ecstasy as the mark of true prophecy (the point of dispute); the heavenly Jerusalem coming down at Pepuza. Contested: whether Montanus was called the Paraclete (hostile witnesses) or the Paraclete spoke through the prophets (Tertullian).
- **How they practiced:** extra fasts and dry eating, one marriage only, no flight from martyrdom, prophecy in the Sunday gathering tested afterward (Carthage sister), pilgrimage and rites at Pepuza; women prophets and (per Epiphanius, late) women clergy; patriarchs of Pepuza over bishops (Jerome).
- **What held them together:** present prophecy of the Paraclete, and the stricter discipline it commanded.
- **What came of them:** heaviest, the case against them, gathered by Eusebius, which fixed the church's tests for a prophet (not out of his senses; examined by the churches); then Tertullian's Latin defence; then imperial suppression; survival at Pepuza and in Phrygia for centuries. No church descends from them.

Sources hold: all of this as the hostile witnesses and Tertullian give it. Not held: Pepuza found in 2000 (stays out), any Montanist writing, "c. 165".

## BLOCKING

1. **Opening 1 misreads its source.** HE 29678-29685 says the others were "imagining themselves possessed of the Holy Spirit and of a prophetic gift" and that they "challenged" (egged on) the spirit. It does not say that they believed the Spirit was speaking in Montanus. Replace "Others believed the Holy Spirit was speaking, and from then on he could not be stopped." with: "Others, thinking themselves filled with the Holy Spirit and a gift of prophecy, urged him on, and from then on he could not be kept silent. (HE 29678-29685)"

2. **Beat 5, Rome: the reason for the recall is missing, and "letters" should be "letter".** Tertullian gives the reason (ANF03 51038, 51044-51053): Praxeas, "the first to import into Rome from Asia" his teaching, pressed false accusations against the prophets and their churches and urged the authority of the bishop's predecessors. Explain it requires that reason. Replace "had recognized the prophets and sent letters of peace (gloss) to the churches of Asia and Phrygia, then was talked into recalling them." with: "had acknowledged the prophets' gifts and given his peace to the churches of Asia and Phrygia, with a letter of peace (gloss). Then a man newly come from Asia pressed false charges against the prophets and their churches, and urged the authority of the bishops before him, and the bishop recalled his letter (ANF03 51038, 51044-51053)." Give the man by role; the census has no Praxeas entry. Cut first item 4 still takes the whole event to the card.

3. **Beat 6, Constantine: the reason for the punishment is missing.** The edict gives its own reason: their teachings are "destructive and venomous errors" (HE 66081-66082), and "it is no longer possible to bear with your pernicious errors" (HE 66112-66113). Add to beat 6, after "(HE 66112-66128)": "*Reason* (the edict's own): he called their teachings destructive errors he would no longer bear (HE 66081-66082, 66112-66113)." This finding goes away if blocking finding 4 moves Constantine to the card.

## MINOR

5. **Beat 5, one marriage.** "Elsewhere he defends one marriage only (ANF04 9765)" is wrong: ANF04 9765 is the same chapter, De ieiunio 1, and gives the main church's charge. Replace it with: "In the same book he answers the charge that they allow only one marriage, and points to his earlier defence of it (ANF04 9763-9765, 9777-9778)."

6. **Heart, How they practiced.** The words "tested by the elders" are not in the source: the sister reports "to us" and her visions "are examined" (ANF03 15816-15820). Replace "tested by the elders after the people left" with "told after the people left to Tertullian and those with him, and examined with care."

7. **Heart, What they believed.** ANF03 28749-28762 (the city over Judaea) does not place Jerusalem at Pepuza. Replace "(PanH 18512-18513, 18758-18764; ANF03 28749-28762)" with "(PanH 18512-18513, 18758-18764, of the fourth century; Tertullian's hope of the city 'let down from heaven', ANF03 28736-28739, names no place)."

8. **Heart, What they faced / What came of them.** "No held source shows them after Procopius's fire" is wrong: Michael the Syrian (held, late, Mich 20584-20653) tells of a later burning at Pepuza. Replace it with: "The held record ends in the sixth century: Procopius's fire, and the burning at Pepuza that Michael the Syrian tells six centuries later."

9. **Beat 4, martyrs.** "Both sides had martyrs" is the narrator taking a side. The anonymous calls theirs "so-called martyrs" (HE 29911-29915, 29935-29937). Replace it with: "They claimed many martyrs, the anonymous writer says;" (this goes with Cut first item 3 in any case).

10. **Beat 4 and Do not say: the two prints differ inside Maximilla's oracle.** Holl reads προφήτης, "prophet" (PanH 17218). Oehler reads προφῆτις, "prophetess" (PanO 1417). So PanO does not confirm "no prophet". Replace "PanO 1416-1420" in beat 4 with: "Holl's reading; Oehler (PanO 1417) reads 'prophetess': card." Correct the sheet's B6 note to match.

11. **Beat 6, order.** Sozomen (440s) comes before Epiphanius (about 375). Tell Epiphanius first and then the fifth-century historian, or drop the historian per Cut first item 6.

12. **Beat 7.** Add what Procopius sets just before the fire: agents were "sent out to all parts of the Empire to force whomsoever they met to change the faith of his forefathers" (Proc 2187-2189). "Give up their beliefs" should also carry Procopius's "in favour of the old religion", the established faith (Proc 2170-2171).

13. **Do not say: add these.**

    - "the village of Pepuza" → "Pepuza, a small town" (HE 30275; πόλιν, PanH 18512). The census card says "village".
    - "thirteen years" → "more than thirteen years" (HE 29887).
    - "Montanus had been a pagan priest" → no held source; leave it out.

14. **Library sweep: one later witness is missing.** Pseudo-Tertullian, Against All Heresies 7 (ANF03 55827-55834), is held and not on the sheet: "the Paraclete has spoken in Montanus more things than Christ brought forward into ... the Gospel". Add it to G3 as a third voice, saying the Paraclete spoke *in* Montanus, and to the first Do not say line. The rest of the sweep checks out. The corpus-map bucket matches, and these are confirmed not held: the Greek of HE V.16 (not in Routh vol. 1; Schwartz holds only VI-X), Heine and Trevett, and the John of Ephesus passage (the Part 3 hit at Payne Smith 15042 is an editor's note on Manichees).
