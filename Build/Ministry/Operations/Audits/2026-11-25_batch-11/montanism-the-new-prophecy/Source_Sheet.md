Greek is given as the print reads, with the OCR's ϑ, χ-for-κ and stray marks normalized for reading (for example PanH "ἐχκλησίᾳ" given as "ἐκκλησίᾳ"); where the OCR changes a word, the sheet says so. English is copied as printed.

Witness kinds: **own words** (a member of the movement), **contemporary** (alive while the events ran), **later** (with a rough gap), **late legend**. Confidence uses the project's five levels.

Almost every witness below is hostile. The members' own words survive only as short oracles quoted by enemies (the anonymous writer, Epiphanius) and in Tertullian, who took the movement's side at Carthage. No writing by Montanus, Priscilla or Maximilla is held.

## A. Setting

**A1. The name they used: the New Prophecy.** Documented.
- Serapion of Antioch, a contemporary bishop, hostile, letter quoted by Eusebius. HE 30557-30562: "That you may see that the doings of this lying band of the new prophecy, so called, are an abomination to all the brotherhood throughout the world, I have sent you writings of the most blessed Claudius Apolinarius, bishop of Hierapolis in Asia."
- Tertullian, own side, contemporary. CSEL20 15495: "propter hoc nouae prophetiae recusantur"; ANF04 9773: "it is on this account that the New Prophecies are rejected". ANF03 50885-50886: "through the new prophecy, which descends in copious streams from the Paraclete." CSEL47 20425: "qui apud fidem nostram est nouae prophotiae sermo" (OCR "prophotiae").

**A2. The opponents' names: the Phrygian heresy, Cataphrygians.** Documented.
- HE 29421, Eusebius (later, c. 300+): "Against the so-called Phrygian heresy". HE 30184-30186: "As the so-called Phrygian heresy was still flourishing in Phrygia in his time, Apollonius..."
- Constantine's edict, HE 66071-66073: "ye Novatians, Valentinians, Marcionites, Paulians, ye who are called Cataphrygians". (This span is quoted on the tree in the imperial-juridical edge note; tell it in narrator's words.)
- Hippolytus at Rome (contemporary, hostile), ANF05 15494-15498: "The Phrygians or Montanists. The Phrygians, however, derive the principles of their heresy from a certain Montanus, and Priscilla, and Maximilla".

**A3. Place: Ardabau on the Mysia-Phrygia border; Pepuza and Tymion, small towns in Phrygia.** Documented (as the hostile witnesses give it).
- Anonymous, HE 29577-29579: "There is said to be a certain village called Ardabau in that part of Mysia, which borders upon Phrygia."
- Apollonius, HE 30257, 30275: "who named Pepuza and Tymion, small towns in Phrygia, Jerusalem". Greek, ApolGr 25253-25255: "ὁ Πέπουζαν καὶ Τύμιον Ἱερουσαλὴμ ὀνομάσας, (πόλεις δὲ εἰσὶν αὗται μικραὶ τῆς Φρυγίας)" (OCR "lepov-σαλὴμ").
- Plain spoken words: "Phrygia, in the uplands of what is now western Turkey" for the region (census `modernCountries`: Turkey, Phrygia). Do not name a modern site for Pepuza (see G6).

**A4. Time.** Contested for the start; Documented that the movement was active by the 170s.
- Anonymous, HE 29591-29592: "There first, they say, when Gratus was proconsul of Asia". No year is given; the proconsul is otherwise unknown (HE note 29592-29596, modern).
- Epiphanius (later, c. 375, hostile), PanH 17019: "γὰρ γεγόνασι περὶ τὸ ἐννεακαιδέκατον ἔτος ᾿Αντωνίνου τοῦ εὐσεβοῦς" ("they arose about the nineteenth year of Antoninus Pius", i.e. c. 156/7).
- Apollonius, HE 30456-30458: "at the time of his writing, it was the fortieth year since Montanus had begun his pretended prophecy." Jerome, NPNF203 40318-40319, puts Apollonius "in the reigns of Commodus and Severus".
- Eusebius sets the Gallic letter, which names "the followers of Montanus, Alcibiades and Theodotus in Phrygia" as "now first giving wide circulation to their assumption in regard to prophecy" (HE 27655-27671), in the time of the Lyons martyrs, "In the seventeenth year of the Emperor Antoninus Verus" (HE 26706-26707; Marcus Aurelius, 177).
- Narrator's safe wording: "in the second century"; "by the 170s, Eusebius says, word of them had reached the churches of Gaul". The census "c. 165" is an editor's reckoning between Epiphanius and the Chronicle date (McGiffert's note, HE 29592-29617), not a source's: Do not say.

**A5. Spread beyond Phrygia.** Documented by later hostile witnesses.
- Epiphanius, PanH 18515-18517: "ἔστι γὰρ καὶ τὸ γένος ἐν τῇ Καππαδοκίᾳ καὶ Γαλατίᾳ καὶ ἐν τῇ προειρημένῃ Φρυγίᾳ ... ἀλλὰ καὶ ἐν Κιλικίᾳ καὶ ἐν Κωνσταντινουπόλει τὸ πλεῖστον" ("the sect is in Cappadocia and Galatia and in Phrygia ... but also in Cilicia, and most of all in Constantinople").
- Anonymous at Ancyra in Galatia, HE 29519-29541 (see B4).
- Tertullian at Carthage (C-section); Hippolytus at Rome (C3).

## B. Events, in order

**B1. Montanus begins to prophesy at Ardabau.** Documented as the anonymous gives it; his motive ("desire for leadership") is the enemy's charge.
- Anonymous (contemporary, hostile, presbyter writing to Avircius Marcellus), HE 29617-29629: "a recent convert, Montanus by name, through his unquenchable desire for leadership, gave the adversary opportunity against him. And he became beside himself, and being suddenly in a sort of frenzy and ecstasy, he raved, and began to babble and utter strange things, prophesying in a manner contrary to the constant custom of the Church handed down by tradition from the beginning."
- HE 29673-29686: "Some of those who heard his spurious utterances at that time were indignant, and they rebuked him as one that was possessed, and that was under the control of a demon ... and they forbade him to talk ... But others imagining themselves possessed of the Holy Spirit and of a prophetic gift, were elated ... In consequence of this, he could no longer be held in check, so as to keep silence."
- *Explain, from the anonymous:* the fault was the manner: frenzy and ecstasy "contrary to the constant custom of the Church" (HE 29626-29629). *From the movement's side* (Tertullian): ecstasy belongs to true prophecy, see C4.
- *Explain "the constant custom of the Church" (plain sense):* the custom is how prophets had always spoken. The anonymous, HE 30106-30119: "They cannot show that one of the old or one of the new prophets was thus carried away in spirit" (he names Agabus, Judas, Silas, the daughters of Philip, Ammia in Philadelphia and Quadratus). A work he cites, HE 30064-30066: "a prophet ought not to speak in ecstasy". Contemporary, hostile. Documented that the anonymous says it. Spoken plain sense: "prophesied in a frenzy, as no prophet before him had done, the elder says".

**B2. Two women, Priscilla and Maximilla, prophesy the same way.** Documented (names); the description is the enemy's.
- Anonymous, HE 29692, 29707-29714: "And he stirred up besides two women, and filled them with the false spirit, so that they talked wildly and unreasonably and strangely, like the person already mentioned ... But those of the Phrygians that were deceived were few in number."
- Eusebius, HE 29266-29269: "boasting that Montanus was the Paraclete, and that the women that followed him, Priscilla and Maximilla, were prophetesses of Montanus."
- Apollonius, HE 30303-30305: "We show that these first prophetesses themselves, as soon as they were filled with the Spirit, abandoned their husbands. How falsely therefore they speak who call Prisca a virgin." (Charge.)
- Tertullian calls her Prisca (ANF03 47251; ANF04 5505); Hippolytus and Eusebius Priscilla. Census spelling: Priscilla.

**B3. What Montanus set up: Pepuza named Jerusalem; fasting laws; offerings.** Documented as Apollonius's charges.
- Apollonius (contemporary, hostile, writing forty years on), HE 30225-30289: "His actions and his teaching show who this new teacher is. This is he who taught the dissolution of marriage; who made laws for fasting; who named Pepuza and Tymion, small towns in Phrygia, Jerusalem, wishing to gather people to them from all directions; who appointed collectors of money; who contrived the receiving of gifts under the name of offerings; who provided salaries for those who preached his doctrine, that its teaching might prevail through gluttony." Greek ApolGr 25247-25263.
- *Explain "named Jerusalem":* Apollonius gives the reason: "wishing to gather people to them from all directions" (HE 30276). The movement's own reason, the heavenly city coming down there, is in Epiphanius (E3) and Tertullian (C6).
- Apollonius, HE 30309-30312: "When therefore I see the prophetess receiving gold and silver and costly garments, how can I avoid reproving her?" HE 30441-30445: "does a prophet dye his hair? Does a prophet stain his eyelids? ... Does a prophet lend on usury?" (Charges.)

**B4. The churches of Asia test them and cast them out.** Documented.
- Anonymous, HE 29716-29738: "And the arrogant spirit taught them to revile the entire universal Church under heaven, because the spirit of false prophecy received neither honor from it nor entrance into it. For the faithful in Asia met often in many places throughout Asia to consider this matter, and examined the novel utterances and pronounced them profane, and rejected the heresy, and thus these persons were expelled from the Church and debarred from communion." (ANF07's version of this span is quoted in the post-apostolic-house-church edge note; tell it in narrator's words.)
- The anonymous at Ancyra, HE 29519-29541: "But being recently in Ancyra in Galatia, I found the church there greatly agitated by this novelty, not prophecy, as they call it, but rather false prophecy ... we disputed in the church many days concerning these and other matters separately brought forward by them".
- Apolinarius of Hierapolis wrote against them: HE 29432-29434 ("a strong and invincible weapon, Apolinarius of Hierapolis"); HE 26111-26112 ("which he subsequently wrote against the heresy of the Phrygians"). Not held: his text.
- *Explain "debarred from communion":* shut out of the church's fellowship and its shared meal (the anonymous's own words, HE 29737-29738).
- *Explain the condemnation from each side:* the anonymous: the utterances were novel and "profane" (HE 29735-29736), and the spirit taught them to revile the whole church (HE 29716-29719). The movement's side: "they called us slayers of the prophets because we did not receive their loquacious prophets, who, they say, are those that the Lord promised to send to the people" (HE 29745-29754).
- *What was "novel and profane" (grade, 2026-11-25):* the anonymous (contemporary, hostile) does not list the sayings the meetings condemned. What his own account faults is the manner: Montanus prophesied "in a sort of frenzy and ecstasy ... in a manner contrary to the constant custom of the Church handed down by tradition from the beginning" (HE 29626-29629); the women "talked wildly and unreasonably and strangely, like the person already mentioned" (HE 29707-29709); and the spirit "taught them to revile the entire universal Church" (HE 29716-29717). Second English, ANF07 11308: "subjected those novel doctrines to examination, and declared them to be spurious". Greek not held. Documented that the anonymous says it; that the meetings judged on the manner is his sequence, not his statement (Inferential-Thin), so the manner is told as his description of the sayings, not as the meetings' reason.

**B5. Bishops try to refute the spirit in Maximilla, and in Priscilla, and are stopped.** Documented as the hostile witnesses give it.
- Anonymous, HE 29823-29826: "the holy bishops of that time attempted to refute the spirit in Maximilla, but were prevented by others who plainly co-operated with the spirit."
- Anonymous, HE 29831-29878 (Maximilla's oracle inside it): "And let not the spirit ... say through Maximilla, 'I am driven away from the sheep like a wolf. I am not a wolf. I am word and spirit and power.' But let him show clearly and prove the power in the spirit. And by the spirit let him compel those to confess him who were then present for the purpose of proving and reasoning with the talkative spirit,—those eminent men and bishops, Zoticus, from the village Comana, and Julian, from Apamea, whose mouths the followers of Themiso muzzled, refusing to permit the false and seductive spirit to be refuted by them."
- Second English, ANF07 11336: "“I am chased like a wolf from the sheep; I am no wolf. I am word, and spirit, and power.”" Greek of HE V.16 not held (Routh vol. II and Schwartz's Books I-V are not in the Library).
- Apollonius, HE 30461-30464: "Zoticus, who was mentioned by the former writer, when Maximilla was pretending to prophesy in Pepuza, resisted her and endeavored to refute the spirit that was working in her; but was prevented by those who agreed with her." Greek ApolGr 25651-25660 ("ἐν Πεπούζοις").
- Serapion's letter, a bishop's signature, HE 30595-30601: "Ælius Publius Julius, bishop of Debeltum, a colony of Thrace. As God liveth in the heavens, the blessed Sotas in Anchialus desired to cast the demon out of Priscilla, but the hypocrites did not permit him." Greek ApolGr 24595-24603.
- *Explain:* the bishops came to drive out what they took for a demon (HE 30600, "cast the demon out"); the prophet's followers would not let them. Maximilla's oracle gives her side: she was hunted as a wolf, and was none.

**B6. Maximilla's death, and the end she foretold.** Documented that the anonymous counts the years; the oracle is Documented as Epiphanius quotes it.
- Anonymous, HE 29880-29890: "he indicates the time when he wrote these accounts, and mentions her predictions in which she prophesied wars and anarchy ... 'For it is to-day more than thirteen years since the woman died, and there has been neither a partial nor general war in the world; but rather, through the mercy of God, continued peace even to the Christians.'"
- Maximilla's oracle, Epiphanius (later, c. 375), PanH 17169-17170, 17218-17219: "φάσκει γὰρ ἡ λεγομένη παρ᾽ αὐτοῖς Μαξίμιλλα ἡ προφῆτις ὅτι, φησί, »μετ᾽ ἐμὲ προφήτης οὐκέτι ἔσται, ἀλλὰ συντέλεια«" (OCR at 17218 "wire" and "οὐχέτι"). The prints differ here: Oehler, PanO 1417, reads "προφῆτις", "prophetess", for Holl's "προφήτης", so PanO does not confirm "no prophet". "After me there will be no prophet any more, but the end." Not used on the tree outside this story's own card.
- Epiphanius counts the years against it, PanH 17224-17228: about 290 years "to our own time, the twelfth year of Valentinian and Valens and the eighth of Gratian", "and the end has not yet come". The figure is his own and does not fit his other dates: card only.
- *Explain "the end" (συντέλεια, "consummation"; Oehler's Latin "finis sequetur", PanO 1471-1472):* Epiphanius's own reply reads it as the close of the age: PanH 17222-17224, "αὕτη δὲ εἶπε μετ᾽ αὐτὴν εἶναι συντέλειαν, καὶ οὔπω συνετελέσθη, μάλιστα τοσούτων βασιλέων γενομένων καὶ τοσούτου χρόνου ὑπερβεβηκότος" ("she said that after her would come the end, and it has not yet been brought to its end, above all when so many emperors have come and so much time has passed"); PanO 1501-1503 confirms. Later (c. 375), hostile. Documented that Epiphanius reads it so. Spoken plain sense: "the end of the age".
- Miltiades (quoted by the anonymous), HE 30122-30127: "let them show who among them received it from Montanus and the women ... But they cannot show it, though this is the fourteenth year since the death of Maximilla."

**B7. The deaths of Montanus and Maximilla: a rumour the writer will not vouch for.** Inferential-Thin.
- Anonymous, HE 29775-29782, 29818-29821: "the report is that, incited by the spirit of frenzy, they both hung themselves ... But as we did not see them, O friend, we do not pretend to know. Perhaps in such a manner, perhaps not, Montanus and Theodotus and the above-mentioned woman died."
- Jerome, NPNF203 40301-40303, gives the hanging as Apollonius's assertion: a later writer moving the rumour onto a different witness. Leave out.

**B8. Martyrs on both sides.** Documented.
- Anonymous, HE 29911-29915: "they try to take refuge in their martyrs, alleging that they have many martyrs". HE 29935-29967: "When those called to martyrdom from the Church for the truth of the faith have met with any of the so-called martyrs of the Phrygian heresy, they have separated from them, and died without any fellowship with them, because they did not wish to give their assent to the spirit of Montanus and the women. And that this is true and took place in our own time in Apamea on the Mæander, among those who suffered martyrdom with Gaius and Alexander of Eumenia, is well known."
- Apollonius on Themiso, HE 30317-30319: "Themiso ... could not endure the sign of confession, but threw aside bonds for an abundance of possessions." Apollonius names "a certain Thraseas among the martyrs of that time" (HE 30465-30469).

**B9. Rome: a bishop acknowledges the prophecies, then recalls his letters.** Documented as Tertullian tells it (one witness, a partisan).
- Tertullian, Against Praxeas 1, ANF03 51044-51054: "For after the Bishop of Rome had acknowledged the prophetic gifts of Montanus, Prisca, and Maximilla, and, in consequence of the acknowledgment, had bestowed his peace on the churches of Asia and Phrygia, he, by importunately urging false accusations against the prophets themselves and their churches, and insisting on the authority of the bishop's predecessors in the see, compelled him to recall the pacific letter which he had issued, as well as to desist from his purpose of acknowledging the said gifts." Latin CSEL47 from 11686 (Adversus Praxean). The next sentence (ANF03 51054-51057) is quoted in the census's Praxeas entry: barred.
- *Explain "bestowed his peace":* a letter of peace recognized the churches as in fellowship with Rome (ANF03 51048-51053, "the pacific letter"). Which bishop is not named: Do not name one.

## C. Belief and practice, from inside (Tertullian) and from outside

**C1. The quarrel was not about God or Christ: Tertullian.** Documented that Tertullian says it.
- De ieiunio 1, CSEL20 15493-15499 (clean): "quinam isti sint, semel nominabo: exteriores et interiores botuli psychicorum, hi paracleto controuersiam faciunt; propter hoc nouae prophetiae recusantur; non quod alium deum praedicent Montanus et Priscilla et Maximilla, nec quod Iesum Christum soluant, nec quod aliquam fidei aut spei regulam euertant, sed quod plane doceant saepius ieiunare quam nubere".
- ANF04 9770-9777: "they are the exterior and interior botuli of the Psychics. It is these which raise controversy with the Paraclete; it is on this account that the New Prophecies are rejected: not that Montanus and Priscilla and Maximilla preach another God, nor that they disjoin Jesus Christ (from God), nor that they overturn any particular rule of faith or hope, but that they plainly teach more frequent fasting than marrying."
- *Explain "rule of faith":* Tertullian's own short summary, ANF04 2701-2708: "the rule, to wit, of believing in one only God omnipotent, the Creator of the universe, and His Son Jesus Christ, born of the Virgin Mary, crucified under Pontius Pilate, raised again the third day from the dead, received in the heavens, sitting now at the right (hand) of the Father, destined to come to judge quick and dead through the resurrection of the flesh as well (as of the spirit)."
- *Explain "psychics":* Tertullian's name for the main church, those who have the soul (psyche) but not the Spirit: ANF04 5750 "But to the Psychics, since they receive not the Spirit". In Against Praxeas the English has "carnally-minded" (ANF03 51074-51076; quoted in the tertullian edge note and told in tertullian-s-voice: narrator's words only).

**C2. Same belief, new fasts: Hippolytus, an enemy, agrees in part.** Documented; Contested for the whole movement (see G2).
- Hippolytus, Refutation VIII.12 (ANF numbering), ANF05 13127-13136: "These acknowledge God to be the Father of the universe, and Creator of all things, similarly with the Church, and (receive) as many things as the Gospel testifies concerning Christ. They introduce, however, the novelties of fasts, and feasts, and meals of parched food, and repasts of radishes, alleging that they have been instructed by women. And some of these assent to the heresy of the Noetians, and affirm that the Father himself is the Son".
- ANF05 13115-13126: "they assert that into these the Paraclete Spirit had departed ... And they allege that they have learned something more through these, than from law, and prophets, and the Gospels. But they magnify these wretched women above the Apostles". ANF05 13117-13119: "being in possession of an infinite number of their books". (Their books are lost.)
- ANF05 15498-15505 (Book X): "In respect, however, of what appertains to the origin and creation of the universe, the Phrygians are supposed to express themselves correctly; while in the tenets which they enunciate respecting Christ, they have not irrelevantly formed their opinions."

**C2a. Their own stated reason, as Epiphanius reports it.** Documented that Epiphanius says it (c. 375, hostile).
- PanH 17024-17084: "Οὗτοι γὰρ οἱ κατὰ Φρύγας καλούμενοι δέχονται καὶ αὐτοὶ πᾶσαν γραφὴν παλαιᾶς καὶ νέας διαθήκης καὶ νεκρῶν ἀνάστασιν ὁμοίως λέγουσι ... περὶ δὲ πατρὸς καὶ υἱοῦ καὶ ἁγίου πνεύματος ὁμοίως φρονοῦσι τῇ ἁγίᾳ καθολικῇ ἐκκλησίᾳ, ἀπέσχισαν δὲ ἑαυτούς ... λέγοντες ὅτι »δεῖ ἡμᾶς, φησί, καὶ τὰ χαρίσματα δέχεσθαι«" ("These so-called Phrygians also accept all the Scripture of the Old and New Testament, and speak of the resurrection of the dead in the same way ... about the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit they think as the holy catholic church does; but they cut themselves off ... saying, 'We must also receive the gifts'").
- *Explain "the gifts":* the gifts of the Spirit, prophecy above all (PanH 17084-17085, Epiphanius's answer: the church too receives the gifts, "but the true gifts").

**C3. What the Paraclete was, for them.** Documented (Tertullian's own words).
- De virginibus velandis 1, ANF04 2715-2722: "the reason why the Lord sent the Paraclete was, that, since human mediocrity was unable to take in all things at once, discipline should, little by little, be directed, and ordained, and carried on to perfection, by that Vicar of the Lord, the Holy Spirit. 'Still,' He said, 'I have many things to say to you, but ye are not yet able to bear them: when that Spirit of truth shall have come, He will conduct you into all truth'". ANF04 2736-2741: "righteousness ... was first in a rudimentary state, having a natural fear of God: from that stage it advanced, through the Law and the Prophets, to infancy; from that stage it passed, through the Gospel, to the fervour of youth: now, through the Paraclete, it is settling into maturity." ANF04 2709-2711: "This law of faith being constant, the other succeeding points of discipline and conversation admit the 'novelty' of correction".
- *Explain "Paraclete":* the Helper Jesus promised in John's Gospel, the Holy Spirit, who would lead into all truth (ANF04 2715-2722). The hostile side: Eusebius says they boasted "that Montanus was the Paraclete" (HE 29267-29268); Basil (later, 374) that they applied "to Montanus and Priscilla the title of the Paraclete" (NPNF208 34872-34874). Tertullian himself speaks of the Paraclete speaking "by the mouth of the prophetess Prisca" (ANF03 47250-47251), i.e. through her. The sides differ: tell each in its witness's voice.

**C4. Ecstasy: the point of dispute.** Documented on both sides.
- Tertullian, Against Marcion IV.22, ANF03 32338-32351: "Or was it on the principle which we maintain in the cause of the new prophecy, that to grace ecstasy or rapture is incident. For when a man is rapt in the Spirit, especially when he beholds the glory of God, or when God speaks through him, he necessarily loses his sensation, because he is overshadowed with the power of God,—a point concerning which there is a question between us and the carnally-minded." Latin CSEL47 23761-23762: "qua defendimus in causa nouae prophetiae gratiae exstasin, id est amentiam, conuenire?"
- Miltiades (anti-Montanist), as the anonymous reports, HE 30064-30071: a work "in which he shows that a prophet ought not to speak in ecstasy". The anonymous, HE 30101-30104: "But the false prophet falls into an ecstasy, in which he is without shame or fear."
- Jerome, NPNF203 40315-40318: "Tertullian added to the six volumes which he wrote On ecstasy against the church a seventh, directed especially against Apollonius". Not held: On Ecstasy (lost).
- *Explain "ecstasy":* being carried out of one's own senses while God speaks through one (Tertullian's "amentiam", CSEL47 23762; ANF03 32347-32349).

**C5. Their stricter life: fasts, dry food, one marriage, no flight from martyrdom.** Documented (Tertullian, own side; the charge as he reports it).
- De ieiunio 1, ANF04 9781-9787: "They charge us with keeping fasts of our own; with prolonging our Stations generally into the evening; with observing xerophagies likewise, keeping our food unmoistened by any flesh, and by any juiciness, and by any kind of specially succulent fruit; and with not eating or drinking anything with a winey flavour; also with abstinence from the bath, congruent with our dry diet." Latin CSEL20 15515-15520.
- *Explain "Stations":* fast days, which they kept on into the evening (ANF04 9782-9783). *"Xerophagies":* "dry eating", food with no meat, no juice, no wine (ANF04 9783-9786).
- One marriage: ANF04 9765 "through allowing but one marriage"; CSEL20 15490 "per unicas nuptias".
- *Explain "one marriage only" (plain sense):* no second marriage after becoming a Christian, even after a husband or wife has died. On Monogamy 13, ANF04 6790-6801: "he reduces you to (subjection to) the contrary condition, that you are *not* to marry when you have lost your husband ... he *does* prejudge you (guilty) of adultery if, after the death of your husband, you do marry another". On Modesty 1, ANF04 7265-7269: "it is not permitted, after believing, to know even a second marriage ... we excommunicate digamists"; Latin CSEL20 12615-12620 (clean): "dum nec secundas quidem post fidem nuptias permittitur nosse ... digamos foris sistimus". Tertullian, own side, contemporary. Documented.
- Martyrdom, an oracle of the Spirit, De fuga 9, ANF04 11454-11466: "if you ask counsel of the Spirit ... it incites all almost to go and offer themselves in martyrdom, not to flee from it ... If you are exposed to public infamy, says he, it is for your good; for he who is not exposed to dishonour among men is sure to be so before the Lord ... seek not to die on bridal beds, nor in miscarriages, nor in soft fevers, but to die the martyr's death, that He may be glorified who has suffered for you." Latin OehT1 46469-46477 (damaged OCR: "Pnblicaris", "iectulis", "iebribus").
- *Explain "martyrdom rather than flight" (plain sense):* the book is "De Fuga in Persecutione" (ANF04 10975), written because Fabius asked "whether or not we ought to flee in persecution" (ANF04 10994-10996), and "As persecutions in increasing number threaten us" (ANF04 11000-11001); "who will flee from persecution, but he who fears?" (ANF04 11451-11452). Martyrdom is dying for the faith: "to die the martyr's death, that He may be glorified who has suffered for you" (ANF04 11464-11466). Tertullian, own side, contemporary. Documented. Spoken plain sense: "when Christians were hunted for their faith, to face death rather than flee".
- Prisca's oracle on purity, De exhortatione castitatis 10, ANF04 5504-5511: "through the holy prophetess Prisca the Gospel is thus preached: that 'the holy minister knows how to minister sanctity.' 'For purity,' says she, 'is harmonious, and they see visions; and, turning their face downward, they even hear manifest voices, as salutary as they are withal secret.'" Latin OehT1 68404-68407 (damaged OCR).
- Apollonius's charge (B3): "taught the dissolution of marriage; who made laws for fasting" (HE 30226-30238).

**C6. The heavenly Jerusalem: Tertullian's sign in the sky.** Documented that Tertullian reports it; the sight itself is a wonder in his voice.
- Against Marcion III.24, ANF03 28749-28762: "the word of the new prophecy which is a part of our belief, attests how it foretold that there would be for a sign a picture of this very city exhibited to view previous to its manifestation. This prophecy, indeed, has been very lately fulfilled in an expedition to the East. For it is evident from the testimony of even heathen witnesses, that in Judæa there was suspended in the sky a city early every morning for forty days. As the day advanced, the entire figure of its walls would wane gradually, and sometimes it would vanish instantly." Latin CSEL47 20425-20430: "qui apud fidem nostram est nouae prophotiae sermo testatur ... in Iudaea per dies quadraginta matutinis momentis ciuitatem de caelo pependisse".
- ANF03 28736-28739: "a kingdom is promised to us upon the earth ... after the resurrection for a thousand years in the divinely-built city of Jerusalem, 'let down from heaven'".

**C7. A Carthage service: the sister who sees visions.** Documented (Tertullian, eyewitness, partisan).
- De anima 9, ANF03 15802-15820: "seeing that we acknowledge spiritual charismata, or gifts, we too have merited the attainment of the prophetic gift ... We have now amongst us a sister whose lot it has been to be favoured with sundry gifts of revelation, which she experiences in the Spirit by ecstatic vision amidst the sacred rites of the Lord's day in the church ... After the people are dismissed at the conclusion of the sacred services, she is in the regular habit of reporting to us whatever things she may have seen in vision (for all her communications are examined with the most scrupulous care, in order that their truth may be probed)."
- This scene is this story's own census documented story; it is not quoted in any other story on the tree. If used, narrator's words.

**C8. Women as prophets and clergy, by the later witnesses.** Documented that Epiphanius reports it, c. 375; Contested as to which group (he cannot tell Quintillians from Phrygians).
- PanH 18826-18834: "πολλάκις δὲ ἐν τῇ αὐτῶν ἐκκλησίᾳ εἰσέρχονται λαμπαδηφοροῦσαι ἑπτά τινες παρθένοι, λευχείμονες δῆθεν ἐρχόμεναι, ἵνα προφητεύσωσι τῷ λαῷ ... κλαίειν τε ποιοῦσιν πάντας ... ἐπίσκοποί τε παρ᾽ αὐτοῖς γυναῖκες καὶ πρεσβύτεροι γυναῖκες καὶ τὰ ἄλλα· ὧν μηδὲν διαφέρειν φασίν· »ἐν γὰρ Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ οὔτε ἄρσεν οὔτε θῆλυ«" ("often in their church seven virgins come in carrying lamps, dressed in white, to prophesy to the people ... they make everyone weep ... women among them are bishops and women presbyters and the rest; they say there is no difference, 'for in Christ Jesus there is neither male nor female'").
- PanH 18764-18768: "διό φασι καὶ ἄχρι τῆς δεῦρο μυεῖσθαί τινας οὕτω γυναῖκας ἐκεῖσε ἐν τῷ τόπῳ καὶ ἄνδρας, πρὸς τὸ ἐπιμεινάσας αὐτὰς ἢ αὐτοὺς τὸν Χριστὸν θεωρῆσαι ... οὐ πάνυ δὲ σαφῶς οἶδα εἰ παρ᾽ αὐτοῖς ἢ παρὰ τοῖς κατὰ Φρύγας" ("so they say that to this day some women and men are initiated there in the place, so that by waiting there they may see Christ ... I do not know clearly whether among these or among the Phrygians").
- PanH 18835-18837: they are called Artotyrites "from setting bread and cheese in their mysteries". Card.
- *Which group:* Panarion 49 is headed against the Quintillians, "also called Pepuzians, Artotyrites and Priscillians", whom Epiphanius calls "the same as the Phrygians and sprung from them, but divided in some way" (PanH 18703-18705: "οἱ αὐτοὶ μὲν ὄντες (τοῖς) κατὰ Φρύγας καὶ ἐξ αὐτῶν ὁρμώμενοι, διήρηνται δὲ κατά τινα τρόπον"). Women clergy confirmed by PanO 3311-3322 (Greek) and 3378 (Latin "Apud eosdem mulieres episcopi"). Spoken: "a branch of them in his day", in Epiphanius's voice.

## D. Church structure, by later witnesses

**D1. Patriarchs of Pepuza over bishops.** Documented that Jerome reports it (384, hostile, of the Montanists of his own day).
- Jerome, Letter 41, NPNF206 8336-8344: "With us, again, the bishops occupy the place of the apostles, but with them a bishop ranks not first but third. For while they put first the patriarchs of Pepusa in Phrygia, and place next to these the ministers called stewards, the bishops are relegated to the third or almost the lowest rank."
- NPNF206 8326-8331: "They suppose a repetition of marriage a sin so awful that he who has committed it is to be regarded as an adulterer ... fast through one Lent yearly; whereas they keep three in the year". NPNF206 8345: "they close the doors of the Church to almost every fault".
- Sozomen (c. 440s), NPNF202 41786-41790: in some nations "a bishop serves as priest even over a village, as I have myself observed in Arabia, and in Cyprus, and among the Novatians and Montanists of Phrygia."
- Anonymous, HE 29804-29807: Theodotus, "the first steward, as it were, of their so-called prophecy". Apollonius, HE 30276-30277: "who appointed collectors of money".

## E. The centuries after

**E1. Constantine's law takes their meeting houses.** Documented.
- HE 66068-66073, 66112-66131 (Life of Constantine III.64-65): "Understand now, by this present statute, ye ... who are called Cataphrygians ... we give warning by this present statute that none of you henceforth presume to assemble yourselves together. We have directed, accordingly, that you be deprived of all the houses in which you are accustomed to hold your assemblies ... not in public merely, but in any private house or place whatsoever. Let those of you, therefore, who are desirous of embracing the true and pure religion, take the far better course of entering the catholic Church". (Edge note quotes 66071-66073 and 66124-66126: narrator's words.)

**E2. In Phrygia they stayed many, to Sozomen's day.** Documented (Sozomen, c. 440s, of his own time).
- Sozomen II.32, NPNF202 30574-30577: "The Phrygians suffered the same treatment as the other heretics in all the Roman provinces except Phrygia and the neighboring regions, for here they had, since the time of Montanus, existed in great numbers and do so to the present day."
- Sozomen VII.18, NPNF202 41731-41736: "The Montanists, who are called Pepuzites and Phrygians, celebrate the Passover according to a strange fashion which they introduced ... affirm that it is right to attend exclusively to the cycles of the sun." Card.

**E3. Pepuza razed, still visited; the vision of Jerusalem coming down.** Documented that Epiphanius reports it, c. 375; the vision is an oracle in a prophetess's voice, and Epiphanius cannot say which prophetess.
- PanH 18466, 18512-18515: "Τιμῶσι δὲ οἱ τοιοῦτοι καὶ τόπον τινὰ ἔρημον ἐν τῇ Φρυγίᾳ, Πέπουζάν ποτε καλουμένην πόλιν, νῦν δὲ ἠδαφισμένην, καί φασιν ἐκεῖσε κατιέναι τὴν ἄνωθεν Ἱερουσαλήμ. ὅθεν ἐκεῖ ἀπερχόμενοι μυστήριά τινα ἐπιτελοῦσιν ἐν τῷ τόπῳ καὶ ἁγιάζουσιν ἑαυτούς, ὡς ὑπολαμβάνουσιν." ("These people honour a deserted place in Phrygia, a town once called Pepuza, now razed to the ground, and they say the Jerusalem from above comes down there. So they go there, perform certain rites in the place, and make themselves holy, as they suppose.") PanO 3032-3037 confirms ("ἠδαφισμένην").
- The vision, PanH 18755-18764: "ἐν τῇ Πεπούζῃ ἢ Κυΐντιλλαν ἢ Πρίσκιλλαν (οὐκ ἔχω γὰρ ἀκριβῶς λέγειν), μίαν δὲ ἐξ αὐτῶν ὡς προεῖπον ἐν τῇ Πεπούζῃ κεκαθευδηκέναι καὶ τὸν Χριστὸν πρὸς αὐτὴν ἐληλυθέναι συνυπνωκέναι τε αὐτῇ τούτῳ τῷ τρόπῳ, ὡς ἐκείνη ἀπατωμένη ἔλεγεν· »ἐν ἰδέᾳ, φησί, γυναικός, ἐσχηματισμένος ἐν στολῇ λαμπρᾷ ἦλθε πρός με Χριστὸς καὶ ἐνέβαλεν ἐν ἐμοὶ τὴν σοφίαν καὶ ἀπεκάλυψέ μοι τουτονὶ τὸν τόπον εἶναι ἅγιον καὶ ὧδε τὴν Ἱερουσαλὴμ ἐκ τοῦ οὐρανοῦ κατιέναι«." PanO 3273-3287 confirms (reads "τύπον" for "τόπον", an OCR or print slip; Oehler's Latin 3354-3358 "hunc locum sanctum esse").
- Plain sense: "Either Quintilla or Priscilla (I cannot say exactly) ... one of them, as I said, had slept at Pepuza, and Christ came to her and slept beside her, in this way, as she in her deception said: 'In the form of a woman,' she says, 'clothed in a shining robe, Christ came to me, and put wisdom into me, and revealed to me that this place is holy, and that here Jerusalem comes down from heaven.'" "Slept beside her" (συνυπνωκέναι) is Epiphanius's sneer, not the prophetess's words: Do not say.
- Tertullian, decades earlier, on the same hope: C6.

**E4. Their baptism refused by later bishops.** Documented.
- Firmilian of Caesarea in Cappadocia (c. 256), ANF05 39619-39640: "they who are called Cataphrygians, and endeavour to claim to themselves new prophecies, can have neither the Father, nor the Son, nor the Holy Spirit ... All which we some time back confirmed in Iconium, which is a place in Phrygia, when we were assembled together with those who had gathered from Galatia and Cilicia". Card.
- Basil (374), NPNF208 34871-34881: "Now the Pepuzeni are plainly heretical, for, by unlawfully and shamefully applying to Montanus and Priscilla the title of the Paraclete, they have blasphemed against the Holy Ghost ... What ground is there, then, for the acceptance of the baptism of men who baptize into the Father and the Son and Montanus or Priscilla?" Card.

**E5. Later charges of child murder.** Contested; the witnesses themselves doubt it.
- Cyril of Jerusalem (c. 350), NPNF207 17999-18005: "And having taken possession of Pepuza, a very small hamlet of Phrygia, he falsely named it Jerusalem; and cutting the throats of wretched little children, and chopping them up into unholy food, for the purpose of their so-called mysteries".
- Jerome, NPNF206 8355-8358: "I pass over their sacraments of sin, made up as they are said to be, of sucking children subjected to a triumphant martyrdom. I prefer, I say, not to credit these; accusations of blood-shedding may well be false."
- Cyril himself says the same charge was laid on all Christians in persecution (NPNF207 18005-18008). Leave out: a charge one later enemy makes and another declines to credit; telling it in one hearing needs both and adds nothing the story needs. Card.

**E6. Justinian's order, and the burning of their churches.** Documented that Procopius says it (contemporary of Justinian, hostile to him).
- Proc 2167-2173: "There prevail in the Roman Empire many Christian doctrines which are known as heresies, such as those of the Montanists and Sabbatians and all the others by which men's minds are led astray. Justinian ordered all these beliefs to be abandoned in favour of the old religion, and threatened the recusants with legal disability to transmit their property to their wives and children by will."
- Proc 2195-2198: "The Montanists, who were settled in Phrygia, shut themselves up in their churches, set them on fire, and perished in the flames; and, from this time forth, nothing was to be seen in the Roman Empire except massacres and flight."
- Both spans are quoted in the Justinianic edge note: narrator's words.
- *Why the order (grade, 2026-11-25):* Procopius gives a reason, his own hostile one. Proc 2164-2167: "he also endeavoured, by various devices, to drench the earth in human blood, to carry off more riches for himself, and to murder many of his subjects. He proceeded as follows." Then the heretics' churches "were rich beyond belief" (Proc 2173-2174), and "the Emperor Justinian first of all sequestrated all the property of these churches" (Proc 2184-2185). Contemporary, hostile to Justinian. Documented that Procopius says it; as Justinian's real motive, Contested (one hostile witness). No other held source gives a reason for this order: Novel 45 (below) is a different law, on heretics serving on town councils, and calls them men "whom even now our right and whole faith has not enlightened, who sit in darkness" (59038-59039, "quos ne nunc quidem recta et integra nostra fides collustraverit, sed qui in tenebris sedeant"); Michael the Syrian's late charge that at Pepuza "ils y tuaient les chrétiens" (Mich 20587-20588) is given for John of Asia's burning, not for the order. Neither is used.
- Justinian's Novel 45 names "Montanistas" (Latin, edge note, `justinian_novellae-corpus-iuris-civilis-v3-lat-grc_schoell-kroll1895.txt` 59038). Card.

**E7. The bones at Pepuza.** Late (Michael the Syrian, 12th century, about 600 years on; he does not name his source at this point). Inferential-Thin as a narrated event; French translation only.
- Mich 20584-20596, 20640-20653: "Dans le pays de Phrygie, il y a un lieu appelé Pépouza où les Montanistes avaient un évêque et des clercs; ils l'appelaient Jérusalem ... Jean d'Asie s'y rendit et fit brûler leur synagogue, sur l'ordre de l'empereur. On trouva dans cette maison un grand reliquaire de marbre ... Sur le dessus était écrit: « De Montanus et de ses femmes ». On l'ouvrit et on y trouva Montanus et ses deux femmes, Maximilla et Priscilla, qui avaient des lames d'or sur la bouche ... Et on brûla les ossements. — Les Montanistes firent entendre des gémissements et des pleurs. « Maintenant, disaient-ils, le monde est ruiné et va périr. »" (OCR cleaned for reading: the file has ".lérusalem", "M^ximilla", "[324]" page markers and a column break between "M^ximilla" and "et Priscilla".)
- Not quotable (no original held; a French translation). Ending 2 only, in narrator's words, credited to Michael the Syrian, "writing some six hundred years later". Card if not used.

## F. Impact, ranked heaviest first (what later witnesses carried on and used)

1. **The case against them, gathered and passed down.** The anonymous, Apollonius, Serapion and Miltiades survive only because Eusebius copied them (HE 29421-30613); Jerome draws on them (NPNF203 40297-40319). What was defended: that a prophet must not speak out of his senses (HE 30070-30071, 30101-30104), and that the churches judge a prophet by examining him (HE 29721-29738). Documented.
2. **Tertullian's defence at Carthage,** the only sympathetic writer held, who carried their oracles and rules into Latin (C1, C3-C7); Jerome records his lost seven books On Ecstasy against Apollonius (NPNF203 40315-40318). Documented.
3. **Imperial law against them:** Constantine (E1), Justinian (E6). Documented.
4. **Refusal of their baptism** (E4). Documented. Card.
5. **The survival at Pepuza and in Phrygia** into the fourth and fifth centuries (E2, E3, D1). Documented.

## G. Where sources disagree or come late

- **G1. Date of the start.** The anonymous: under the proconsul Gratus (no year). Epiphanius: about 156/7. Apollonius: forty years before his own writing. Contested.
- **G2. Their belief about God.** Tertullian (C1) and Hippolytus for most of them (C2) say they held the church's belief about God and Christ. Hippolytus says "some of these" followed Noetus (ANF05 13136-13138). Jerome, of the Montanists of 384, says they "force the Trinity into the narrow limits of a single personality" (NPNF206 8318-8325). Epiphanius (later) says that on the Father, Son and Holy Spirit they think as the holy catholic church does (PanH 17081-17082), then quotes Montanus's oracles as claims to be God (PanH 18239-18240). Contested across time: the story tells Tertullian's claim in his voice and Hippolytus's agreement in his.
- **G3. Montanus as Paraclete.** Eusebius and Basil say they called Montanus (or Montanus and Priscilla) the Paraclete (HE 29267-29268; NPNF208 34872-34874); Tertullian speaks of the Paraclete speaking through Prisca (ANF03 47250-47251); the later Against All Heresies printed with Tertullian's works (Pseudo-Tertullian, ch. 7; ANF03 55827-55838) says the Cataphrygians hold "that the Paraclete has spoken in Montanus more things than Christ brought forward into (the compass of) the Gospel" (ANF03 55836-55838), a third voice, with the Paraclete speaking *in* Montanus. Contested; each in its witness's voice.
- **G4. Their deaths.** The anonymous gives hanging as rumour and disowns it (HE 29818-29821); Jerome makes it Apollonius's assertion (NPNF203 40302-40303). Leave out.
- **G5. Which prophetess saw Christ at Pepuza.** Epiphanius: Quintilla or Priscilla, he cannot say (PanH 18755-18756). Contested; say so.
- **G6. Pepuza's site.** No held source places Pepuza on a modern map. The census `why` and `legacy` text "located by archaeologists only in 2000" rests on no held source: Leave out (slate ruling).
- **G7. Errors in this subject's existing census card (for the card step, not the story):**
  - The documented story "Zoticus Tries to Silence Maximilla's Spirit" gives Maximilla's oracle "After me there will be no more prophecy, but the end" as from Eusebius HE V.16. It is not in Eusebius; it is Epiphanius, Panarion 48.2.4 (PanH 17218-17219), and reads "no prophet", not "no more prophecy". The same story says Zoticus and Julian "traveled to Pepuza"; the anonymous names no place (HE 29855-29878), and only Apollonius puts Zoticus at Pepuza (HE 30461-30464).
  - The documented story "The Sister Who Saw Visions" quotes "in order that their truth may be proved"; ANF03 15819-15820 reads "probed".
  - The `relationsSummary` and House Church edge: "met many times" is the anonymous's "met often in many places" (HE 29721-29722): close, keep the source's wording.
  - The census `end` 400 and `dates` "c. 165-400s": Sozomen (E2) has them numerous in Phrygia in the 440s, and Procopius (E6) in the sixth century.
- **G8. Phrygian inscriptions** (census `sourcing`): none held. Not used.
