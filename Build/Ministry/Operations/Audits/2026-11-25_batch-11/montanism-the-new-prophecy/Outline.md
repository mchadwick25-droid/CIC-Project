# Outline: Montanism, the New Prophecy (`montanism-the-new-prophecy`, tradition)

Lines are file lines in `cic/texts/`; file abbreviations and the full passages are in `Source_Sheet.md` (HE, ANF07, ApolGr, PanH, PanO, ANF03, ANF04, CSEL20, CSEL47, OehT1, ANF05, NPNF202, NPNF203, NPNF206, Proc, Mich). Section references (B1, C5 ...) are to the sheet.

## The heart

- **The essence:** Christians of Phrygia, in the uplands of what is now western Turkey, who from the second century followed three prophets, Montanus, Priscilla and Maximilla, and called their movement the New Prophecy. They held that the Paraclete, the Spirit Jesus promised, was speaking through their prophets now and calling the church to a stricter life; the churches of Asia tested them and cast them out, and in Phrygia they held on for centuries. (Documented: HE 29266-29269, 29716-29738, 30557-30559; ANF04 2715-2722, 9772-9777; NPNF202 30574-30577)
- **Who they were:** "the New Prophecy" (census informal name; their own word, CSEL20 15495, HE 30558); "Montanists", "Phrygians" and "Cataphrygians" were their enemies' names (HE 29421; HE 66071-66073). The prophets and their followers in Phrygia; in Carthage, Tertullian's congregation. Documented.
- **When and where:** Ardabau, a village where Mysia meets Phrygia (HE 29577-29579); Pepuza and Tymion, small towns in Phrygia (HE 30257, 30275). The start: under a proconsul of Asia called Gratus (HE 29591-29592), about 156/7 by Epiphanius (PanH 17019): Contested. Active by the time of the Lyons martyrs, 177 (HE 26706-26707, 27655-27680): Documented. Carthage around 200-220 (Tertullian). Still numerous in Phrygia in the 440s (NPNF202 30574-30577) and burning their churches under Justinian (Proc 2195-2197): Documented.
- **What they faced:** frenzied speech that others called possession, and orders to be silent (HE 29625-29686); the meetings of the churches of Asia that examined them and expelled them (HE 29721-29738); bishops who came to drive out the spirit in Maximilla and Priscilla (HE 29823-29878; HE 30595-30601); their prophetess's promised end that did not come (HE 29886-29890; PanH 17218-17219); Constantine's law taking their meeting houses (HE 66112-66131); Justinian's order to give up their faith (Proc 2167-2171). What ended them: the record held ends with Procopius's fire. Documented.
- **What they believed:** the same God and Christ as the church, in Tertullian's words (Q2; CSEL20 15495-15499) and, for most of them, in Hippolytus's (ANF05 13127-13130); that the Paraclete leads the church on "into all truth" and that righteousness "is settling into maturity" through him (ANF04 2715-2741); that true prophecy comes in ecstasy, out of one's senses (ANF03 32344-32351); that Jerusalem would come down from heaven at Pepuza (PanH 18512-18513, 18758-18764; ANF03 28749-28762). What they fought over: the prophets' authority and the stricter rules (ANF04 9770-9793). Documented; their belief about God Contested in the later centuries (G2).
- **How they practiced:** more fasts, "Stations" kept into the evening, dry food without meat or wine (ANF04 9781-9787); one marriage only (ANF04 9765); facing martyrdom rather than fleeing (ANF04 11454-11466); visions in the Sunday service, tested by the elders after the people left (ANF03 15802-15820); in the fourth century, rites at deserted Pepuza and women prophets, bishops and presbyters (PanH 18512-18515, 18826-18834). Documented.
- **What held them together:** the prophets' voice, which they took for the Paraclete speaking now (ANF05 13115-13116; ANF03 47250-47251); and the stricter life that voice asked of them (ANF04 9776-9777; CSEL20 15498-15499). Documented.
- **What came of them:** no church continues from them (census: direct line none). The case the churches built against them was copied by Eusebius and carried by later writers (HE 29421-30613; NPNF203 40297-40319); Tertullian carried their cause into Latin (C1-C7). Documented.

## Length

**About 4¼ minutes, 640 words.** Must carry: three quotes (about 92 words); seven glosses (New Prophecy, Paraclete, ecstasy, barred from communion, letter of peace, rule of faith, Stations and dry food: about 75); credited witnesses (the anonymous writer copied by Eusebius, said once early; Apollonius; the bishop's signature in Serapion's letter; Tertullian; Epiphanius with his date; the fifth-century historian; Procopius: about 85); the uncertainty of the start and of which prophetess saw the vision (about 20). About 270 words fixed; the opening scene and seven beats carry the rest at about 50 words each. Tradition range 450-675.

## Setting

- **Time:** "in the second century" for the start (Contested: HE 29591-29592; PanH 17019; HE 30456-30458). "By the 170s" for word reaching Gaul (HE 26706-26707, 27655-27680). "Around 200" and "in the early third century" for Tertullian. "About 375" for Epiphanius (PanH 17224-17226: the twelfth year of Valentinian and Valens). "In the fifth century" for Sozomen; "in the sixth century, under Justinian" for the end.
- **Places:** Ardabau, "in that part of Mysia, which borders upon Phrygia" (HE 29577-29579); Pepuza and Tymion, "small towns in Phrygia" (HE 30275); Ancyra in Galatia (HE 29519-29522); Apamea on the Maeander (HE 29955); Carthage; Rome. Spoken word for the region: "Phrygia, in the uplands of what is now western Turkey".
- **Tradition:** a movement of prophets among the churches of Roman Asia; spoken word "the New Prophecy".

## Words to explain

- **the New Prophecy:** their own name for the prophets and what they said (HE 30557-30559; CSEL20 15495).
- **the Paraclete:** the Helper Jesus promised, the Holy Spirit, who would lead his followers "into all truth" (ANF04 2715-2722).
- **ecstasy:** being carried out of one's own senses while God speaks through one (ANF03 32347-32349; CSEL47 23762 "amentiam").
- **barred from communion:** shut out of the church's fellowship and its shared meal (HE 29737-29738).
- **letter of peace:** a bishop's letter recognizing other churches as in fellowship with his own (ANF03 51047-51053).
- **rule of faith:** the short creed Tertullian sets out: one God the Creator, his Son Jesus Christ, born of the Virgin, crucified, risen, coming to judge (ANF04 2701-2708).
- **Stations; dry food:** fast days kept on into the evening; food with no meat, no juice, no wine (ANF04 9782-9786).

## Shape

A story through time in four places: Phrygia at the start; the churches of Asia that judged them; Carthage, where Tertullian took their side; Pepuza and Phrygia in the centuries after.

## Sibling

- **Latin Apologists** (batch, outline read): its job is the Latin writers who argued the faith to the educated; Tertullian's turn to the New Prophecy is a clause there and this story's job. Here Tertullian is the New Prophecy's voice at Carthage: what he wrote for it, not his life or his Apology.
- **House Church** (batch, researched later): argued against it, and leaves the condemnation out; the testing by the churches of Asia is this story's.
- **tertullian-s-voice** (live): his life, why he turned (Jerome against Augustine), his break, and the Tertullianists are his story's; here only a short turn. **eusebius-of-caesarea** (live): his life and History are his; here he is only the man who copied the anonymous writer. Barred: every quote in both.

## Openings

1. **Ardabau (recommended).** In a village called Ardabau, where Mysia meets Phrygia, a recent convert named Montanus fell into a kind of frenzy and began to speak strange things. Some who heard him said he was possessed and told him to be silent. Others believed the Holy Spirit was speaking, and from then on he could not be stopped. (HE 29577-29579, 29617-29629, 29673-29686.) Credit the account in the next sentence: it comes from an enemy writing within living memory.
2. **The wolf.** Bishops came to Pepuza to drive the spirit out of the prophetess Maximilla, and her followers would not let them near her. Her own words survive in an enemy's book: Q1. (HE 29831-29878; HE 30461-30464.) This reaches ahead to beat 3; use only if Ardabau is cut.

## Story beats

1. **Montanus and the two women.** Two women, Priscilla and Maximilla, began to prophesy in the same way, and their followers said the Paraclete was speaking through them. Almost all of this comes through their enemies: say once here that the fullest account is by a presbyter whose name is lost, copied a century later by Eusebius. *Reason* (the enemy's): Montanus wanted to lead, and spoke "contrary to the constant custom of the Church" (HE 29617-29629). *Explain* the Paraclete (gloss); ecstasy (gloss). HE 29266-29269, 29692, 29707-29714; ANF05 13115-13117. Documented (as the enemy tells it); date Contested.
2. **Pepuza, "Jerusalem".** Montanus named Pepuza and Tymion, small towns in Phrygia, Jerusalem, to gather people there from everywhere; he made laws for fasting, set up collectors of money, and paid his preachers. So says Apollonius, an opponent writing forty years later, who also charged that the prophetesses left their husbands and took gold and fine clothes. *Explain:* Apollonius gives the reason for the name; the movement's own reason, the heavenly city, comes in beat 6. HE 30225-30289, 30303-30312, 30456-30458; ApolGr 25247-25263. Documented as charges.
3. **The test.** The faithful of Asia met often, in many places, examined the new sayings, called them profane, and drove the prophets' followers out of the church, barred from communion (gloss). Bishops came to refute the spirit in Maximilla, and her followers stopped them; a Thracian bishop signed a letter swearing that another bishop wanted to drive the demon out of Priscilla and was not allowed. Q1. *Reason, each side:* the churches judged the sayings novel and the spirit hostile to the whole church (HE 29716-29736); the prophets' side called the bishops "slayers of the prophets" for refusing those the Lord had promised to send (HE 29745-29754). HE 29721-29738, 29823-29878, 30461-30464, 30595-30601. Documented.
4. **The end that did not come.** Maximilla, the last of the three, said that after her there would be no prophet any more, but the end (PanH 17218-17219, Epiphanius's quotation). The anonymous writer counted more than thirteen years since her death, with no war anywhere and peace even for Christians. Both sides had martyrs; at Apamea on the Maeander, he says, martyrs from the church would not die in fellowship with the Phrygians'. HE 29880-29890, 29911-29915, 29935-29967; PanO 1416-1420. Documented.
5. **Rome and Carthage.** A bishop of Rome, Tertullian says, had recognized the prophets and sent letters of peace (gloss) to the churches of Asia and Phrygia, then was talked into recalling them. At Carthage Tertullian took up their cause. Q2, with the rule of faith (gloss). He names what they were blamed for: more fasts, Stations kept into evening, dry food (gloss), one marriage, and martyrdom faced, not fled. In his church a sister saw visions in the Sunday service and told them after the people had gone; every word was examined. ANF03 51044-51054, 15802-15820; ANF04 9770-9787, 11454-11466; CSEL20 15495-15499, 15515-15520. Documented; one witness, a partisan, for Rome and for the sister.
6. **Pepuza after two centuries.** Constantine's law took away their meeting houses and forbade them to meet even in private (HE 66112-66128). In Phrygia, a fifth-century historian says, they stayed many down to his own day (NPNF202 30574-30577). About 375 Epiphanius found Pepuza razed and deserted, yet they went there to hold rites, saying the Jerusalem from above came down there. Q3, credited to Quintilla or Priscilla: he could not say which. PanH 18466, 18512-18515, 18755-18764; PanO 3032-3037, 3271-3283. Documented that he says it.
7. **The fire.** Under Justinian, in the sixth century, the empire ordered such groups to give up their beliefs, Procopius says, and threatened those who held out with being unable to leave property to their wives and children. In Phrygia the Montanists shut themselves inside their churches, set them alight and died in the flames. Proc 2167-2171, 2195-2197. Documented that Procopius says it; he wrote against Justinian.

## What came of it (heaviest first)

1. **The case against them, kept.** Eusebius copied the anonymous writer, Apollonius, Serapion and Miltiades (HE 29421-30613); Jerome drew on them (NPNF203 40297-40319). *Defended:* that the churches test a prophet (HE 29721-29738) and that a prophet ought not to speak out of his senses (HE 30070-30071). Beats 1-4.
2. **Tertullian's defence,** the only sympathetic voice held, with seven lost books On Ecstasy, the last against Apollonius (NPNF203 40315-40318). *Defended:* prophecy in ecstasy, and the stricter rule as the Paraclete's. Beat 5; the lost books card.
3. **Imperial law:** Constantine (HE 66112-66131), Justinian (Proc 2167-2171). Beats 6-7.
4. **Their baptism refused:** Firmilian at Iconium (ANF05 39619-39640), Basil (NPNF208 34871-34881). Card.
5. **Pepuza's patriarchs over bishops** (NPNF206 8336-8344), three Lents, no second marriage (NPNF206 8326-8331). Card.

## Quotes

1. **Maximilla's oracle** (the anonymous, in Eusebius, Church History V.16.17). Rendered from the English, HE 29849-29851 (McGiffert 1890), confirmed by ANF07 11336 (1886); the Greek is not held. *"I am driven away from the sheep like a wolf. I am not a wolf. I am word and spirit and power."* English printed before 1931. Plain sense alongside, from the anonymous's own reply (HE 29852-29853): the spirit in her claimed power, and he dares it to prove that power on the bishops. Job: the condemned prophet's own voice, inside the enemy's book; carries beat 3.
2. **Tertullian on the quarrel** (On Fasting 1). Rendered from CSEL20 15495-15499 (Reifferscheid-Wissowa 1890, clean), beginning after "propter hoc"; ANF04 9772-9777 agrees. *"The new prophecies are rejected, not because Montanus and Priscilla and Maximilla preach another God. Nor is it because they divide Jesus Christ, or overturn any rule of faith or hope. It is because they plainly teach fasting more often than marrying."* Original in a clean held print. Plain sense alongside: they taught nothing false about Christ (as Hippolytus also allows, ANF05 13128-13130). Job: what the quarrel was about, from the one insider held.
3. **The vision at Pepuza** (Epiphanius, Panarion 49.1). Rendered from PanH 18758-18764 (Holl 1922), confirmed by PanO 3277-3283 (Oehler 1860). *"Christ came to me in the form of a woman, clothed in a shining robe. He put wisdom into me. He showed me that this place is holy, and that Jerusalem comes down here from heaven."* Original in a held print with OCR slips, confirmed by a second held print. Job: the movement's hope in its own prophetess's words; ties the name "Jerusalem" (beat 2) to its meaning.

None is used elsewhere on the tree (census longDescriptions, card quotations, documented stories and edge notes searched for each span); none is in tertullian-s-voice or eusebius-of-caesarea. The quotes in Q2 hold Tertullian's own "not because ... it is because" turn: it is his, and stays in quotation marks.

## Connection to carry into the story

What passed from the New Prophecy was a quarrel the whole church had to settle: whether prophets who spoke out of their senses could bind the churches to a stricter life. The churches of Asia answered by meeting, examining and casting out (HE 29721-29738), and their writers' books were copied into Eusebius's History (HE 29421-30613); the prophets' side passed into Latin through Tertullian, who says the quarrel was over fasting and marriage, not over God (CSEL20 15495-15499). The listener needs to hear that the two sides held the same creed by Tertullian's account, so that the fight is plainly about whose voice the church obeys. It comes through the bishops at Pepuza and Tertullian's words, not a summing-up.

## Ending

1. **The churches on fire (recommended).** In Phrygia, where the prophets had first spoken, the Montanists shut themselves inside their churches, set them alight and died in the flames, Procopius says. Proc 2195-2197. Narrator's words: the span is quoted in the Justinianic edge note.
2. **The bones at Pepuza.** A Syriac chronicler writing some six hundred years later tells how a bishop, on the emperor's order, burned their house at Pepuza and the bones found there, labelled as those of Montanus and his women, while they wept that the world was ruined and would perish. Mich 20584-20601, 20640-20654. Late; narrator's words only; credit Michael the Syrian and his distance.

## Do not say

- "Montanus claimed to be the Holy Spirit" / "said he was the Paraclete" as fact → his enemies say so (HE 29267-29268); Tertullian says the Paraclete spoke through the prophets (ANF03 47250-47251). Each in its witness's voice.
- "around 165", "in 172", "in 156" as fact → "in the second century"; the sources disagree.
- "Montanus founded a church" / "the Montanist church" → their own name was the New Prophecy; the churches cast its followers out.
- "they held the same doctrine as the church" as the narrator's fact → Tertullian says it; Hippolytus allows it for most; later writers differ.
- "frenzy", "raved", "babble" as the narrator's words → the anonymous's words; credit him.
- "collected money", "the prophetess took gold", "they left their husbands" as fact → Apollonius's charges.
- "Montanus and Maximilla hanged themselves" → a rumour the writer would not vouch for; leave out.
- "Zoticus and Julian traveled to Pepuza" → the anonymous names no place; Apollonius puts Zoticus at Pepuza.
- "After me there will be no more prophecy" → "no prophet any more" (PanH 17218); it is Epiphanius's quotation, not Eusebius's.
- "Christ slept with her" → Epiphanius's sneer; the vision is told in Q3's words only.
- "Priscilla's vision" as settled → "Quintilla or Priscilla, Epiphanius could not say which".
- "the bishop of Rome (Eleutherus, Victor, Zephyrinus) accepted them" → Tertullian names no bishop.
- "Tertullian became a Montanist because ..." → his motive is the tertullian-s-voice story's, and the sources disagree.
- "Pepuza was found in 2000" / "lost for a thousand years" → no held source; leave out.
- "women were bishops among the first Montanists" → Epiphanius reports it of the groups of his own day, c. 375, and cannot tell which group.
- "Christians were blamed for child murder because of the Montanists" → Cyril's charge alone; leave out.
- "No church descends from them" said as a closing line → no summing-up close; it is a census fact for the card.
- Any barred quote: every quote in tertullian-s-voice (among them "not the church which consists of a number of bishops", Against Praxeas's "he drove away prophecy ... crucified the Father") and eusebius-of-caesarea; the edge-note spans "by the mouth of the prophetess Prisca", "We indeed, on our part, subsequently withdrew from the carnally-minded", "ye who are called Cataphrygians", "shut themselves up in their churches, set them on fire, and perished in the flames", "met together often and in many places of Asia" → narrator's words.

- Apollonius on Alexander the robber-martyr and Themiso's "catholic epistle" (HE 30317-30409): side stories. Card.
- The Lyons letter's judgment (HE 27655-27680): the letter's content is not given. Card, as dating only.
- Montanus's later oracles of God speaking in the first person and the lyre oracle (PanH 17401-17402, 18239-18240): Epiphanius reads them as claims to be God, and telling them fairly needs more room than the story has. Card.
