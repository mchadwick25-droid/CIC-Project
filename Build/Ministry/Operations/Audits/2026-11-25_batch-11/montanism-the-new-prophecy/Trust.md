## BLOCKING

**1. P5 s2: ASSUMPTION ADDED.** Brief: "Finding them novel and profane, and the spirit behind them hostile to the whole church, they drove the prophets' followers out, barred from communion..." Source, HE 29716-29738: "the arrogant spirit taught them to revile the entire universal Church under heaven, because the spirit of false prophecy received neither honor from it nor entrance into it. For the faithful in Asia ... examined the novel utterances and pronounced them profane, and rejected the heresy, and thus these persons were expelled from the Church and debarred from communion." In the source, the spirit reviled the church *because* the church refused it. The churches' finding was "novel" and "profane". The brief makes the spirit's hostility a reason for the expulsion, which reverses the cause.
Change: "Finding them novel and profane, they drove the prophets' followers out, barred from communion: shut out of the church's fellowship and its shared meal."
Do not say (outline, beat 3): "the churches found the spirit hostile to the whole church" as their reason → the anonymous says the spirit reviled the church because the church refused it (HE 29716-29720); the finding was "novel" and "profane" (HE 29736).

**2. P5 s3: SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** Brief: "The prophets' side called the bishops killers of the prophets the Lord had promised." Source, HE 29746-29754: "they called us slayers of the prophets because we did not receive their loquacious prophets, who, they say, are those that the Lord promised to send". "Us" is the anonymous, a presbyter, and his side, not the bishops.
Change: "The prophets' side called their opponents killers of the prophets the Lord had promised."
Do not say (outline, beat 3): "called the bishops 'slayers of the prophets'" → "they called us" (HE 29746), the anonymous's own side.

**3. P9 s3: SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** Brief: "In Phrygia, where the prophets had first spoken, the Montanists shut themselves inside their churches..." Source for where the prophets first spoke, HE 29577-29579: "a certain village called Ardabau in that part of Mysia, which borders upon Phrygia". The brief's own opening says "where Mysia meets Phrygia". Procopius (Proc 2195-2196) says only "The Montanists, who were settled in Phrygia". The clause moves the first prophecy onto Phrygia and links the two places, which no source does.
Change: "In Phrygia, the Montanists shut themselves inside their churches, set them alight and died in the flames."
Do not say (outline, Ending 1): "Phrygia, where the prophets had first spoken" → Ardabau was in Mysia, on the Phrygian border (HE 29577-29579).

**4. Heart: told late.** It is the right heart for the subject. The sources centre on the prophets' claim that the Paraclete spoke through them now (ANF05 13115-13116; ANF03 47250-47251), and on the stricter rule that claim brought (ANF04 9770-9787). The kind kit's essence is who they were, when and where, and what held them together. Here it starts at word 94 ("Montanus and his followers were Christians of Phrygia") and closes at word 179 ("called the church to a stricter life"), about 30 words past the 150-word mark. The 32-word witness paragraph (P2) pushes it there.

With this move the essence ends near word 147. The witness is still named once, early, inside a sentence about him.

## SUMMARY HOLDS (not blocking)

- **P1 s3**, "Others, who thought themselves filled with the Holy Spirit, urged him on, and he could no longer be kept quiet." HE 29679-29686: "others imagining themselves possessed of the Holy Spirit and of a prophetic gift ... challenged the mad and insidious and seducing spirit ... he could no longer be held in check, so as to keep silence." "Challenged" means called the spirit out (ANF07 has the same wording). "Urged him on" is fair.
- **P2 s1**, the elder, his lost name, Eusebius copying him, "most of the record" from opponents. HE 29544: "our fellow-presbyter Zoticus"; HE 29440: "A certain one of these" (no name given); he disputed with them "recently in Ancyra" and wrote "more than thirteen years since the woman died" (HE 29886-29888). "Within living memory" fairly compresses a contemporary who argued with them in person. Every witness Eusebius copies is hostile (HE 29421-30613).
- **P3 s1**, "Christians of Phrygia ... in the second century", with Priscilla and Maximilla prophesying as he did. HE 29692-29711: "he stirred up besides two women ... so that they talked wildly ... like the person already mentioned". For the names, ANF05 13112-13114. For the century, PanH 17018-17019 (the nineteenth year of Antoninus Pius) and HE 30456-30458 (Apollonius's fortieth year). "In the second century" stays inside where these disagree.
- **P3 s3**, the Paraclete speaking through the prophets now, in ecstasy, calling the church to a stricter life. ANF05 13115-13116: "they assert that into these the Paraclete Spirit had departed"; ANF03 32344-32349: "when God speaks through him, he necessarily loses his sensation" (Latin "excidat sensu", "amentiam"); ANF04 2715-2741: the Paraclete sent so that "discipline should ... be ... carried on to perfection".
- **P5 s4**, "When bishops came to refute the spirit in Maximilla, her followers stopped them." HE 29824-29826: "the holy bishops of that time attempted to refute the spirit in Maximilla, but were prevented by others who plainly co-operated with the spirit"; HE 29877: "whose mouths the followers of Themiso muzzled".
- **P5 s6**, "The elder dared that spirit to prove its power on the bishops." HE 29852-29858: "But let him show clearly and prove the power in the spirit. And by the spirit let him compel those to confess him who were then present ... those eminent men and bishops".
- **P7 s1**, "Around 200, at Carthage ... Tertullian took up their cause in Latin." CSEL20 15495 ("nouae prophetiae"); ANF04 9770-9777. No old source fixes the year. "Around 200" is the outline's Widely Accepted wording, told roughly.
- **P7 s4**, "The main church blamed them for fasts of their own: Stations, fast days kept on into the evening, and dry food, with no meat, juice or wine." ANF04 9781-9787: "They charge us with keeping fasts of our own; with prolonging our Stations generally into the evening; with observing xerophagies ... unmoistened by any flesh, and by any juiciness ... not eating or drinking anything with a winey flavour". The charge is the *prolonging* of Stations. The appositive carries it, so the meaning holds. A reviser may prefer "Stations kept on into the evening".
- **P7 s5**, one marriage, martyrdom rather than flight, as the Paraclete's teaching. ANF04 9777-9778: "we have already published a defence of monogamy"; ANF04 5939: "competent to the Paraclete to preach unity of marriage"; ANF04 11454-11457: the Spirit's utterance "incites all almost to go and offer themselves in martyrdom, not to flee from it".
- **P9 s1**, Justinian's order, "says Procopius, a writer hostile to Justinian". Proc 2167-2171: "such as those of the Montanists ... Justinian ordered all these beliefs to be abandoned in favour of the old religion". The same passage shows his hostility: "to drench the earth in human blood" (Proc 2165).
