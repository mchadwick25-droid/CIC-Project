## Findings

**B1 (BLOCKING). Beat 7: "Valentinus, who, he scoffed, stacked heaven on heaven".** The relative "who" attaches to its nearest noun, Valentinus. A listener hears that Valentinus himself did the stacking. Tertullian does not say that. At anf03 43233–43237 the "storeys upon storeys … as a dwelling for each several god of theirs" belong to "*our* heretics". At 43250 it is "the god even of the Valentinians". Chapter 4 (43105–43108) separates the followers from their founder ("they have departed … from their founder"). So the scorn falls on the followers, not on Valentinus. Put it on them:

- Beat 7 planned wording and `Fixes_Shape.md` planned telling. Replace "He wrote against the followers of a teacher, Valentinus, who, he scoffed, stacked heaven on heaven for their gods." with "He wrote against the followers of a teacher, Valentinus. They stacked heaven on heaven for their gods, he scoffed." (19 words for 19.)
- Do not say, the line "no unexplained opponents for Tertullian". Replace "→ 'the followers of a teacher, Valentinus, who, he scoffed, stacked heaven on heaven for their gods' (anf03 43105, 43233–43237)." with "→ 'the followers of a teacher, Valentinus. They stacked heaven on heaven for their gods, he scoffed.' (anf03 43105, 43233–43237, 43250); the scorn is for the followers, not told of Valentinus himself."

**M1 (MINOR). Beat 4: "The godly there, they say, were driven about".** The plural "they say" sounds like rumour, and the claim is Documented. The words are Melito's own, as Eusebius copied them ("he records", npnf201 25818–25823). Replace "The godly there, they say, were driven about by new decrees, new written orders." with "The godly there, he wrote, were driven about by new decrees, new written orders." (Same count.) The sentence before it already credits Eusebius.

## What holds

- **Reign-by-reign order.** The held record gives it. Hadrian follows Trajan (npnf201 20992–20993). Antoninus, "called the Pious", follows Hadrian after 21 years (22120–22121). Marcus follows Antoninus after his 22nd year (23032–23036). So "Long before" (from Athenagoras's co-emperors back to Hadrian, with a 22-year reign between) and "Under the next emperor" are supported. Each writer now enters as an answer to an event: Quadratus with Eusebius's "because" (21048), Justin to the atheist charge (1apol-grc 186–188), Melito to the decrees (25818–25838), Athenagoras to the rumours (athen-grc 179–198). "So" carries only Eusebius's reason.
- **No order claim the sources lack.** There is no time word between Justin's death and Melito, none placing Theophilus, and none placing Tertullian or Diognetus. Melito is "wrote to Marcus Aurelius", not "him". The Do not say lines added for this are kept.
- **Athenagoras, Plea ch. 7 split** (athen-grc 311–318; anf02 11728–11735). "Almost all" is ὡς ἐπὶ τὸ πλεῖστον. "Wrote freely" is ἐπ᾿ ἀδείας … λέγειν καὶ γράφειν. "Yet one stood against Christians" is ἐφ᾿ ἡμῖν δὲ κεῖσθαι νόμον. "No law stood against them" is the plain contrast the sentence itself draws ("with impunity" against "a law lies"). Turning his question into statements adds nothing. The three sentences keep the thread the listener lost.
- **Tertullian, *Against the Valentinians* 4, 5, 7.** "Why we call them Valentinians" (43105) names them. "A teacher" fits the chief teachers of 43153 and the founder of 43107. "Named Justin first among those who had refuted them" holds: "the opinions and their refutations … for instance Justin" (43157–43163), Justin first in the list. It is placed against heresy, as the Do not say line requires. The barred phrase is not used. B1 is the only fault.
