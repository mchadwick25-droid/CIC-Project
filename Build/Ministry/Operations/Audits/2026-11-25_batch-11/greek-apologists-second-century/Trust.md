## BLOCKING

**1. ASSUMPTION ADDED.** "He was one of a circle of Christians who wrote in Greek in the second century, from Athens and Rome to Sardis and Antioch."
"Circle" says these writers were joined to one another. No held source shows Athenagoras, Justin, Melito, Theophilus, Quadratus or the Diognetus writer knowing each other or working together. Eusebius gives them as separate writers to separate emperors (npnf201 20994–20995, "To him Quadratus addressed…"; 25471–25476, "Each of them on his own part addressed apologies…"). The outline's own line is "A circle of writers, not one church", but the sources give only writers.
**Change:** "He was one of a number of Christians who wrote in Greek in the second century, from Athens and Rome to Sardis and Antioch."
**Do not say (add):** "a circle", "a group", "a movement" for the apologists → "a number of Christians who wrote"; no source links them to one another.

**2. SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** "In the reign of Hadrian, Quadratus gave the emperor a defense because, Eusebius says, certain wicked men were troubling the Christians."
Source, npnf201 21045–21049 (Eusebius IV.3.1): "because certain wicked men had attempted to trouble the Christians." The story turns an attempt into something being done.
**Change:** "…because, Eusebius says, certain wicked men were trying to trouble the Christians."
**Do not say (add):** "wicked men were troubling the Christians" → "were trying to trouble" (Eusebius: "had attempted").

**3. SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** "Under Marcus Aurelius, Justin was condemned and put to death for refusing to sacrifice to the gods."
Source, Acts of Justin 5, anf01 29397–29398: "Let those who have refused to sacrifice to the gods and to yield to the command of the emperor be scourged…"; and earlier, anf01 29271: "Obey the gods at once, and submit to the kings." The sentence gives the reason with two parts. The story keeps one and drops "to yield to the command of the emperor". In a story about Christians who said they gladly served the emperors in all else, the dropped half is the conflict itself. The outline's Do not say line prescribes the shortened wording. The source does not support cutting it.
**Change:** "Under Marcus Aurelius, Justin was condemned and put to death for refusing to sacrifice to the gods and to obey the emperor's command."
**Do not say (revise):** replace "only 'condemned for refusing to sacrifice to the gods'" with "only 'condemned for refusing to sacrifice to the gods and to obey the emperor's command' (Acts 5)".

**4. SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** "At Antioch, in Syria, the bishop Theophilus wrote to a pagan friend who had heard the rumors, and been told that Christian books were new."
Source, anf02 10155–10157 (To Autolycus III.1): "[you] suppose that our writings are recent and modern". The rumours came to him from others ("moved by senseless men… to give credit to the prevalent rumor", anf02 10239–10241). The age of the books is his own opinion, and no source says anyone told him. "Been told" adds a source for that opinion that the text does not give.
**Change:** "…wrote to a pagan friend who had heard the rumors, and thought that Christian books were new."
**Do not say (add):** "had been told the books were new" → "thought the books were new" (Theophilus: "you suppose").

## SUMMARY HOLDS (not blocking; evidence for the second reader)

- "Only Christians, he told them, were hunted for their name, and slaughtered on the word of false accusers." Athen-grc 104–108: "ἐλαύνεσθαι καὶ φέρεσθαι καὶ διώκεσθαι, ἐπὶ μόνῳ ὀνόματι… ὑπὸ τῶν συκοφαντῶν σφαττόμενοι". In the Greek, "alone" goes with the name. The story's "only Christians" comes from the contrast in ch. 1: every other people is allowed its gods (athen-grc 88–95), but the name is hated. That is a fair compression.
- "They put written defenses of their faith into the hands of emperors, and of pagans who asked about it." Eusebius on Quadratus, Melito and Apolinarius addressing emperors (npnf201 20994, 25473–25476). Autolycus "inquired who was my God" (anf02 8241). Diognetus was "exceedingly desirous to learn" (anf01 2556).
- "They are known as the apologists, from apology, a defense like a speech in court." Eusebius's chapter heading reads "The Apologists that wrote in Defense of the Faith" (npnf201 20988–20989). Athenagoras calls himself "ἀπολογούμενον" (athen-grc 652).
- "Accused of being godless, of eating human flesh at secret meals and of incest, they answered…" Athen-grc 179–180: "ἀθεότητα, Θυέστεια δεῖπνα, Οἰδιποδείους μίξεις". "Secret" rests on Trypho's question, "after the feast, having extinguished the lights" (anf01 17948–17949), which supports it. One God, maker of all: 1apol-grc 186–188 and athen-grc 311–313. Prayer for the emperors: 1apol-grc 514 ("εὐχόμενοι") and athen-grc 1601.
- "At Rome, under Antoninus Pius, Justin, a Christian teacher of philosophy, wrote to the emperor and his sons." The heading at 1apol-grc 68–70 names Pius, "Verissimus his son" and Lucius, "son… of Pius by adoption". Acts of Justin, anf01 29308–29310: "I live above one Martinus… I am now living in Rome for the second time", and whoever came to him there he taught. Eusebius IV.11.8 has "in the guise of a philosopher" (npnf201 22442).
- "Christians were called atheists, he said, because they refused the cities' gods and would not sacrifice to them." 1apol-grc 186 ("Ἔνθεν δὲ καὶ ἄθεοι κεκλήμεθα") and 683–685: "ὅτι μὴ τοὺς αὐτοὺς ὑμῖν σέβομεν θεούς, μηδὲ… θυσίας φέρομεν."
- "Everywhere, he wrote, they took care to pay their taxes…" 1apol-grc 506–507: "πανταχοῦ πρὸ πάντων πειρώμεθα φέρειν, ὡς ἐδιδάχθημεν παρ᾿ αὐτοῦ". "Took care" for "we try" is fair, and the story follows the Do not say line on πρὸ πάντων.
- "His petition survives only because Eusebius copied it." Anf08 70962–70966 prints the Apology fragment as "In Eusebius, Hist. Eccl.". No independent witness is held. The modern edition is used only to show where the text comes from.
- "On godlessness, the poets and philosophers, he said, had searched for God and spoken freely of one God, yet a law lay against Christians alone." Athen-grc 311–318: they may "ἐπ᾿ ἀδείας… λέγειν καὶ γράφειν περὶ τοῦ θείου ἃ θέλουσιν, ἐφ᾿ ἡμῖν δὲ κεῖσθαι νόμον", and "ζητῆσαι, εἰ δυνατὸς εὑρεῖν".
- "At Carthage, Tertullian took up the same task in Latin." Jerome, Lives 53 (npnf203 40496): "from the city of Carthage". Tertullian's Apology, anf03 1434ff., is addressed to the "Rulers of the Roman Empire".
- "Writing against teachers…, he said he drew on earlier writers who had already answered them, and named Justin first." Anf03 43156–43162: "that we have of our own mind fashioned our own materials, since these have been already produced, both in respect of the opinions and their refutations… for instance Justin…"
- "One defense, a letter to a man named Diognetus, has come down with no writer's name and no date." The letter names neither. The ANF note confirms "no statement as to the authorship… having descended to us from antiquity" (anf01 2480–2483).
