## What the sources hold

1. **What the decrees ordered (Supporting).** No held source says. Melito, as Eusebius copies him, says only that Christians were "driven about in Asia by new decrees", and that informers, "taking occasion from the decrees", plundered the innocent night and day (npnf201 25818–25826). Own words, quoted by Eusebius about 150 years later; English 1890; Documented. The Greek, held in Otto's 1872 print (OCR, a legible second witness, not quotable verbatim: otto1872 line 9), agrees: καινοῖς ἐλαυνόμενον δόγμασι κατὰ τὴν Ἀσίαν, and the informers τὴν ἐκ τῶν διαταγμάτων ἔχοντες ἀφορμήν, "having their pretext from the ordinances" (otto1872 20996–21029). Melito himself did not know whether the decree was the emperor's (npnf201 25834–25838), and he judges it "not fit to be executed even against barbarian enemies", but he never gives its terms. So the fix states the limit plainly and ties the informers to the decrees in the very next sentence. *Absence (kind: absence): the terms of the decrees are not attested.*
2. **Who the "wicked men" were, and what they did (Passing).** No held old source says. Eusebius gives only "because certain wicked men had attempted to trouble the Christians" (npnf201 21048–21049; later, about 190 years; Documented as Eusebius's reason). His Greek, quoted in Otto's notes, is πονηροί τινες ἄνδρες τοὺς ἡμετέρους ἐνοχλεῖν ἐπειρῶντο, "certain wicked men were trying to trouble our people" (otto1872 17518–17520), so the brief's "were trying" stands. Two held texts were weighed and set aside:
   - Jerome, De viris 19 (npnf203 39898–39901; later, about 270 years, dependent on Eusebius): "those who hated the Christians took opportunity without instructions from the Emperor to harass the believers". It names no one and no act beyond "harass", and Jerome's additions about Quadratus are on the outline's Do not say list (Inferential-Thin, sheet). Not used.
   - The governor Serennius Granianus's letter and Hadrian's reply to Minucius Fundanus (npnf201 21916–21934, 22064–22080): under Hadrian, Christians put to death on the crowd's outcry, without trial, and informers. No source ties them to Quadratus's "wicked men", the reply's genuineness is disputed in the modern editor's notes (McGiffert, npnf201 21942–21954, 22079–22093: "condemn it as a forgery"), and the outline already keeps the edicts out (Do not say; sheet §6, "Did the emperors answer?"). Joining them here would reach past the sources. Not used.
   So the fix says plainly, in Eusebius's own place, that he names neither the men nor their deeds. *Absence (kind: absence).* The other choice the ruling allows, leaving the sentence as it is (0 words), is the fallback if a reader hears the limit as a gap-only tell.

## Cuts, and where each goes

- **2a.** "a church historian" moves up to Eusebius's first mention, not lost; "certain" (change 2): Leave out ("wicked men were trying" keeps the Greek's sense).
- **3.** "condemned and": Leave out; "put to death" carries the outcome, and his trial stays justin-martyr's.
- **4.** "Everywhere" (1apol πανταχοῦ): **for the card**; the taxes and Christ's teaching stay.
- **5.** "the gods of the cities" told as "the cities' gods", the outline's own wording (beat 2): Leave out (wording only).
- **6.** "back" and "the books of Moses" told as "Moses's books", the outline's own wording (beat 6): Leave out (wording only).
- **7.** "also" and "many": Leave out; beat 7's own planned wording has neither.
- **8.** "the" before "Christians": Leave out (wording only).
