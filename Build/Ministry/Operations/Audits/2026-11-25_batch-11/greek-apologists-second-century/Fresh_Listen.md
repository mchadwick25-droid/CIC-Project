## 1. The kind kit's test

- **Who these people were:** filled. "Christians who wrote in Greek in the second century… known as the apologists, from apology, a defense like a speech in court."
- **What happened to them:** filled. They were "hunted for their name", "driven about by new decrees", and "Justin was condemned and put to death for refusing to sacrifice to the gods".
- **What their faith was about:** filled. "they worshipped the one God who made all things and prayed for the emperors", "we hold that to watch a man being killed is close to killing him", and the Diognetus close.
- **Why the connection matters:** filled, thinly. "At Carthage, Tertullian took up the same task in Latin. He said he drew on earlier Christian writers, and named Justin first." The listener knows the work passed on to Latin writers. The listener does not learn why Justin's quarrel with Marcion mattered (see 4).

## 2. The opening

After three sentences the listener has who (Athenagoras, an Athenian Christian, writing to the emperors), where (Athens, then "from Athens and Rome to Sardis and Antioch"), when ("in the second century", since a newcomer cannot date Marcus Aurelius alone), and why ("Only Christians, he told them, were hunted for their name"). Nothing is missing.

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing

1. "He also named Marcion, still teaching another god above the Creator, whose followers, also called Christians, were not persecuted for their teaching." The listener cannot tell who Marcion is, what "another god above the Creator" means, or why Justin raised him. The point of "were not persecuted" is lost. Is it a complaint, an accusation, or a contrast with Christians being punished for the name? This is the same fault as the bare statement in 4, so it is counted there.
2. "If this is done at your command, well and good. For a just ruler will never take unjust measures, and we gladly accept the honour of such a death." So far the listener has heard only about robbery, so "such a death" comes without warning. The general sense survives: "if you ordered it, we accept it". MINOR.

## 4. Bare statements

1. **BLOCKING.** The Marcion sentence quoted above. It names a teaching and a teacher, but it does not say in plain words why the teaching mattered to Justin or to the Christians, so it breaks Explain it. **Exact change:** give, in one clause and from what the outline holds, why Justin named him: what Justin charged Marcion with, or what contrast Justin drew from his followers going unpunished. If the outline holds no reason, cut the sentence. The census card line about Marcionism can carry it.

The scorer's other two flags are not bare. Justin's death gives its reason ("for refusing to sacrifice to the gods and obey the emperor's command"). Theophilus's answer is explained by the friend's charge that "Christian books were new".

## 8. Pull, drift, staying

- **Opening pull:** yes. An outsider hailing emperors as philosophers while his people are slaughtered is a real hook.
- **First drift:** at "In the reign of Hadrian, Quadratus addressed a defense to the emperor because, says Eusebius, who still had the book in the early 300s, certain wicked men were trying to trouble the Christians." A new name arrives with no act of his own, a second, unplaced name, and the vague "certain wicked men", all in one sentence. It reads as the start of a list. Attention comes back at Justin's "atheists of such gods".
- **Second soft spot:** the Theophilus to Tertullian to Diognetus run. After the Athenagoras games passage, the high point, each paragraph opens on a new name, so the close starts to feel like a roll call. The Diognetus quote earns the ending back.
- **Would listen to the end:** yes.
