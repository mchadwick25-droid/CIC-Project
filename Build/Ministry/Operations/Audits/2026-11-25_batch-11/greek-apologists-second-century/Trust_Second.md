## Blocking findings

**1. ASSUMPTION ADDED.** "He was one of a circle of Christians who wrote in Greek in the second century, from Athens and Rome to Sardis and Antioch."
"Circle" implies people tied to one another. No held source connects these writers to each other (Quadratus, Justin, Melito, Athenagoras, Theophilus each appear alone in Eusebius, npnf201 20992–20995, 22442, 25471–25476, 25228–25231). The only tie held is Tatian hearing Justin (anf01 33664), and Tatian is not in the story. The connection comes from the outline's heart ("A circle of writers"), not from a source.
*Change:* "He was one of a number of Christians who wrote in Greek in the second century, from Athens and Rome to Sardis and Antioch."
*Do not say:* "a circle" of apologists → "a number of Christians" (no old source ties them to one another).

**2. NOT FOUND.** "Accused of being godless, of eating human flesh at secret meals and of incest, …"
"Secret" is not in the sources. Athenagoras: Τρία ἐπιφημίζουσιν ἡμῖν ἐγκλήματα, ἀθεότητα, Θυέστεια δεῖπνα, Οἰδιποδείους μίξεις ("Thyestean *dinners*", athen-grc 179–180). Theophilus: "that we eat human flesh" (anf02 10239–10244). Searched: athen-grc chs. 3 and 35 (179–199, 1534–1550); anf02 Theophilus III.4 (10234–10248); anf01 Justin 1 Apol. 26 and 2 Apol. 12 (14955–14960, 17178–17190; Justin's "upsetting of the lamp" is said of heretics, and 2 Apol. 12 says the accusers "openly perpetrate" such acts); npnf201 V.1 (27015–27024). The outline's Words to explain carries "secret meals" but cites anf02 10239–10244, which does not say it.
*Change:* "Accused of being godless, of eating human flesh at their feasts and of incest, they answered …"
*Do not say:* "secret meals" → "at their feasts" (Athenagoras's δεῖπνα; no source says secret).

**3. SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** "In the reign of Hadrian, Quadratus gave the emperor a defense because, Eusebius says, certain wicked men were troubling the Christians."
Source: "To him Quadratus addressed a discourse containing an apology for our religion, because certain wicked men had attempted to trouble the Christians" (npnf201 20994–21046). The brief turns an attempt into something done. "Gave" is also stronger than the held "addressed" (Eusebius's Greek is not held).
*Change:* "In the reign of Hadrian, Quadratus addressed a defense to the emperor because, Eusebius says, certain wicked men were trying to trouble the Christians."
*Do not say:* "were troubling the Christians" → "were trying to trouble" (Eusebius: "had attempted").

**4. SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** "His petition survives only because Eusebius copied it."
Eusebius copies extracts, not the petition: "But in his book addressed to the emperor he records…", "And a little further on he says…", "Again he adds…" (npnf201 25818–25838). ANF's Melito fragments are the same extracts, marked "In Eusebius, Hist. Eccl." (anf08 70962–70966). The sentence implies the whole petition survives.
*Change:* "Only the passages Eusebius copied from his petition survive."
*Do not say:* "his petition survives" → "only the passages Eusebius copied survive."
