# Outline check: The Second-Century Greek Apologists (`greek-apologists-second-century`)

## My own account of the heart (written before reading `Outline.md`)

From the census entry (card `why`, `legacy`, `voices`; the approved brief), the source sheet, and what the subject is known for.

- **The essence:** Through the second century, from Hadrian's reign to about 180, Greek-speaking Christians, some of them philosophers and some bishops, wrote formal defences of their faith and put them into the hands of emperors and educated pagans. They answered the charges laid on them, godlessness, cannibal feasts, incest and disloyalty, by asking to be judged by how they lived and not by their name; and several of them, Justin above all, went further and claimed the best of Greek thought for the church.
- **Who they were:** "the apologists": Quadratus, Aristides, Justin, Melito, Athenagoras, Theophilus, the writer to Diognetus. Not a church or a school: a run of writers doing the same thing.
- **When and where:** reign of Hadrian to Marcus Aurelius and Commodus; Athens, Rome, Sardis in Asia, Antioch in Syria.
- **What they faced:** punishment for the name alone; popular rumours (the "Thyestean feasts" and "Oedipean unions"); informers and new decrees in Asia (Melito); philosophers who called them atheists in public (Crescens, through Justin); death (Justin). No held old source records an emperor's answer.
- **What they believed (as they argued it):** one God, maker of all, with his Word (Son); atheists only of the cities' gods; loyal to the emperors in everything but worship (taxes paid, prayer for the throne); Moses older than the Greek poets; against Marcion's "other god". The major contribution, which the census names as the heart of the effort ("the second move"): that Greek philosophy had been reaching toward the truth the church now held, so that Christians were the heirs of reason, not its enemies (Justin's seeds of the Word; Athenagoras: the philosophers too spoke of one God; Melito: "our philosophy"; Theophilus: Moses older than the Greeks).
- **How they practiced:** what the defences describe: sharing with the poor and taking in strangers (Aristides), no exposure of infants, shunning the gladiator shows, monogamy, living in every city as residents and strangers (Diognetus). Sunday worship belongs to the House Church sibling.
- **What held them together:** the demand to be judged by deeds, not a name; and the claim that the faith is the true philosophy, open to examination by reason.
- **What came of them:** their books kept and quoted by Eusebius (without whom Quadratus and Melito would be names); the Latin defenders took up the work and knew the Greek ones; Alexandria (Clement, then Origen) carried the philosophy argument much further; the fight against Marcion and the Valentinians.
