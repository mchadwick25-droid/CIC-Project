## Heart

It is the right heart. The sources show a number of Greek writers, not one church, who addressed defences to emperors (Eusebius on Quadratus and Melito; Justin's and Athenagoras's own headings) and to pagan enquirers (Theophilus to Autolycus, the letter to Diognetus). They answer the same charges, godlessness, cannibal feasts and incest (athen-grc 179–180; anf02 10239–10244), with the one God and an offer to be judged by their lives (athen-grc 196–198). The essence is complete at about word 130, inside the first minute.

## SUMMARY HOLDS (with the lines they rest on)

1. "Only Christians, he told them, were hunted for their name alone…" The empire lets every people keep its own customs, "μὴ προνενόησθε καὶ ἡμῶν… ἐπὶ μόνῳ ὀνόματι… ὑπὸ τῶν συκοφαντῶν σφαττόμενοι" (athen-grc 102–110). The contrast with every other people carries "only".
2. "…put written defenses of their faith into the hands of emperors, and of pagans who asked them about it." Eusebius says they "addressed" (npnf201 20994, 25474–25476). Athenagoras: "μηνύσαι τὰ καθ᾿ ἑαυτοὺς ἐτολμήσαμεν" (athen-grc 106–107). Autolycus "inquired who was my God" (anf02 8241–8242). Diognetus is "desirous to learn" (anf01 2556). "Into the hands" is at the edge: no source records a defence being received. It reads as "addressed", so it is not marked.
3. "Such a defense was called an apology, the kind of speech made in court." Athenagoras: "Ἀνάγκη δὲ ἀπολογούμενον" (athen-grc 652). Eusebius: "an apology for our religion" (npnf201 20994). The court sense is a gloss on the word's meaning, which Part 1 allows.
4. "Under the next emperor, Antoninus Pius, Christians were called atheists, because they refused the gods of the cities and would not sacrifice to them." Justin: "Ἔνθεν δὲ καὶ ἄθεοι κεκλήμεθα" (1apol-grc 186). Justin again: "Ὅπερ μόνον ἐγκαλεῖν ἡμῖν ἔχετε, ὅτι μὴ τοὺς αὐτοὺς ὑμῖν σέβομεν θεούς, μηδὲ… θυσίας" (1apol-grc 683–686). "Of the cities" is Athenagoras's wording (athen-grc 521–525), and it is fair as a gloss.
5. "Justin, a Christian teacher of philosophy at Rome…" Eusebius: "In the guise of a philosopher" (npnf201 22442). Justin in the Acts: "I am now living in Rome… if any one wished to come to me, I communicated to him the doctrines of truth" (anf01 29309–29312). The outline cites only 1apol-grc 68–75, which gives neither the city nor the teaching.
6. "Everywhere, he wrote, they took care to pay their taxes, as Christ had taught them." The Greek: "πανταχοῦ πρὸ πάντων πειρώμεθα φέρειν, ὡς ἐδιδάχθημεν παρ᾿ αὐτοῦ" (1apol-grc 506–507). "Took care" stands for πειρώμεθα, and leaving out πρὸ πάντων is right.
7. "Poets and philosophers, he said, wrote freely about God, and almost all spoke of one God." The Greek: "ὡς ἐπὶ τὸ πλεῖστον… τοῖς πᾶσι συμφωνῆται… ἐπ᾿ ἀδείας… λέγειν καὶ γράφειν περὶ τοῦ θείου ἃ θέλουσιν… Ποιηταὶ μὲν γὰρ καὶ φιλόσοφοι" (athen-grc 311–318). The brief drops "even against their will", which is minor.
8. "No law stood against them." This is ἐπ᾿ ἀδείας, "with impunity" (athen-grc 315).
9. "…the emperors' own games, the gladiator shows, fights to the death between armed men, or with wild beasts, staged for crowds." The Greek: "τὰς δι᾿ ὅπλων ἀγωνίας καὶ διὰ θηρίων, καὶ μάλιστα τὰς ὑφ᾿ ὑμῶν ἀγομένας" (athen-grc 1541–1543). The deaths come from "τὸν φονευόμενον" (1544).
10. "But the friend believed the rumors, and thought Christian books were new and their teaching nonsense." Theophilus: "to give credit to the prevalent rumor" (anf02 10240). Again: "the word of truth is an idle tale, and suppose that our writings are recent and modern" (anf02 10156–10157). There is no "because" between the two views, as the outline asks.
11. "At Carthage, Tertullian took up the same task in Latin." Jerome: "was from the city of Carthage" (npnf203 40496). Tertullian's *Apology* opens "Rulers of the Roman Empire…" (anf03 1448–1452).
12. "They stacked heaven on heaven for their gods, he scoffed, and he named Justin first among the earlier writers who had refuted them." Tertullian: "storeys upon storeys and… heights upon heights… as a dwelling for each several god of theirs" (anf03 43233–43237). Again: "the opinions and their refutations… not only those who have lived before us… for instance Justin" (anf03 43155–43163).
13. "One defense, a letter to a man named Diognetus, has no known writer or date." The editor clarifies the text: "no statement as to the authorship of the Epistle having descended to us from antiquity" (anf01 2488–2490). The date is Contested, and saying it is unknown keeps to that.
