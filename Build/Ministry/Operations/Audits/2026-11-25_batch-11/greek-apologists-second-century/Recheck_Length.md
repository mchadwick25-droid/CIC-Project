## FIX 2. The taxes wording says more than its source

The Greek is πανταχοῦ πρὸ πάντων (1apol-grc 506–507). In Greek, πρὸ πάντων commonly means "above all", and ANF's "more readily than all men" is one reading of it. "More readily than anyone else" adds a comparison with everyone else that the Greek does not settle. The new Do not say line goes further still and asserts which sense is right. The passage itself does settle one thing: they paid "as we were taught by him", meaning Christ (1apol-grc 506–508).

- `Outline.md`, beat 2: replace "Everywhere they tried to pay their taxes more readily than anyone else (πανταχοῦ πρὸ πάντων, 1apol-grc 506–507; ANF \"more readily than all men\", anf01 14580–14581);" with "Everywhere, he said, they took care to pay their taxes, as Christ had taught them (1apol-grc 506–508; ANF anf01 14580–14581);"
- `Outline.md`, Do not say: replace the line "- 'paid their taxes first of all' / 'before anyone else' → 'more readily than anyone else' (πρὸ πάντων: ahead of all others in readiness, not first in time or first among duties; 1apol-grc 506–507, anf01 14580–14581)." with "- 'first of all', 'before anyone else', 'more readily than anyone else' for the taxes → 'they took care to pay their taxes, as Christ had taught them' (πρὸ πάντων may mean 'above all' or 'before all others'; the text does not settle it; 1apol-grc 506–508)."
