## 3. The charges: each explained from each side the held texts give

| Charge | What it meant | The accusers' side (as held) | The apologists' answer | Lines |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Atheism** | Not "no belief in God": refusing the gods of the cities and their sacrifices | Crescens, a philosopher at Rome, "publicly bears witness against us … saying that the Christians are atheists and impious" (through Justin); Athenagoras: accusers "measure piety by the rule of sacrifices" | Justin: atheists of such gods, not of the most true God; Athenagoras: the poets and philosophers spoke of one God and were not called atheists | anf01: 16856–16859; anf02: 11973–11979; 1apol-grc: 186–188; anf02: 11728–11735 |
| **Cannibal feasts** ("Thyestean feasts", after a man in Greek legend served his own sons to eat) | Eating human flesh at their meetings | Theophilus: "the prevalent rumor wherewith godless lips falsely accuse us"; Justin: slaves, "children or weak women", were tortured to admit it (anf01: 17182–17186); the Lyon letter (npnf201: 27011–27024): heathen servants, fearing torture, "accused us falsely of Thyestean banquets and Œdipodean intercourse" | Athenagoras: no one claims to have seen it; slaves never said it; Christians will not even watch a man killed | anf02: 10239–10244; athen-grc: 1534–1550 |
| **Incest** ("Oedipean unions", after the king in Greek legend who married his mother) | Shared wives, incest with sisters | Theophilus's report of the rumour | Theophilus: "monogamy is observed" (anf02: 10625); Athenagoras: even beasts pair by nature's law (athen-grc: 183–186; anf02: 11562–11564) | anf02: 10239–10244 |
| **Novelty** | Their teaching was new, their books recent | Theophilus's report of Autolycus | Theophilus reckons the age of Moses's books | anf02: 10155–10160 |
| **The name** | Punished for being called Christian, with no crime shown | Justin and Athenagoras both say it | Judge deeds, not names | 1apol-grc: 124–141; athen-grc: 93–95 |
| **Disloyalty** (implied) | That Christians endangered the emperors' house | Athenagoras asks to be examined on "loyalty and obedience to you and your house" | Justin: taxes paid first, God alone worshipped, rulers served gladly; Athenagoras: prayer for the throne to pass son from father | athen-grc: 196–198; 1apol-grc: 506–516; athen-grc: 1601–1602 |

The myths behind the Greek names: McGiffert's note, npnf201: 27024–27028 ("i.e. of cannibalism and incest; for according to classic legend Thyestes had unwittingly eaten his own sons … and Œdipus had unknowingly married his own mother") — a modern note clarifying the old words. No old source here gives a reason why the rumour began beyond Athenagoras's "virtue is opposed by its very nature to vice" (anf02: 11567–11569) and Justin's demons (anf01: 17181–17186). The ANF note tying the charge to the Eucharist (anf02: 11555–11557) is a modern opinion: it stays out.

Other things the story may name:
- **Apology**: a speech for the defence in court (Athenagoras's own ἀπολογούμενον, athen-grc: 652).
- **Informers**: people who laid charges, here to seize property (Melito, npnf201: 25823–25826).
- **Decree**: a new written order of government, which Melito could not say came from the emperor (npnf201: 25833–25838).
- **Gladiator shows**: armed fights and fights with wild beasts, staged by the emperors themselves (athen-grc: 1543–1546).
- **Trinity**: Theophilus's own terms, God, his Word and his Wisdom (anf02: 8990–8996).

## 4. What came of them (heaviest first: what later witnesses carried on and used)

1. **Their books were kept and catalogued, and the lost ones quoted.** Eusebius (early 300s) held Quadratus's book and quotes its one passage; quotes Melito's petition; lists Justin's works and Theophilus's (npnf201: 21049–21068, 25818–25859, ch. IV.18 from 24203, 25228–25231). Jerome repeats them (npnf203: 39892–39919, 40004–40030). Without Eusebius, Quadratus and Melito's petition would be names only. Documented.
2. **The Latin defenders took up the same work, and knew the Greek ones.** Tertullian at Carthage names "Justin, philosopher and martyr" and "Miltiades, the sophist of the churches" among earlier writers (anf03_tertullian.xml: 43160–43166); Jerome reports Tertullian mocked Melito (npnf203: 40019–40022). (Census edge `greek-apologists-second-century--latin-apologists`.) Documented.
3. **The argument from Moses's age passed to Alexandria.** Clement of Alexandria: "This has been discussed with accuracy by Tatian in his book To the Greeks" (anf02: 29724–29727); Theophilus makes the same argument (anf02: 10155–10160). (Census edge to `alexandria-catechetical`.) Documented.
4. **Against Marcion and the Valentinians** (defended: the one God who made the world). Justin names Marcion alive and teaching (anf01: 14943–14945); Dial. 35 lists Marcians and Valentinians (census edge, anf01: 19254–19255). Documented.
5. **Athenagoras read later**: a summary of Methodius's On the Resurrection says Methodius spoke of the devil "as was said also by Athenagoras" (`anf06_…methodius-arnobius.xml`: 35907–35910). Later, thin. Documented that the summary says so.
6. **Aristides carried on unknowingly** inside the romance of Barlaam and Josaphat, then found in Syriac at Sinai in 1889 (anf09: 18398–18403, 18416–18421, 18451–18462). Modern event; Documented.

What was defended: that Christians worship one God, the maker of all, and are neither godless nor dangerous; that their way of life could bear examination.

## 6. Where sources disagree or come late

- **Which emperor Aristides addressed**: Eusebius and the Syriac's short heading say Hadrian; the Syriac's longer heading names "Titus Hadrianus Antoninus", the style of Antoninus Pius (anf09: 19005–19008; the editor's note lays out three readings, 19008–19025). Contested.
- **Quadratus's witnesses**: Quadratus says some of those healed "lived even to our day" (npnf201: 21067); Jerome, 270 years on, says Quadratus had seen them (npnf203: 39904–39906). Follow Quadratus.
- **Aristides's text**: the Syriac has things the Greek lacks (fasting, slaves made brothers); the Greek survives only inside a romance (anf09: 18451–18467). Use what both carry, or credit the Syriac.
- **Did the emperors answer?** Eusebius's own Chronicle (not held) claimed a favourable edict after Quadratus; McGiffert calls it "incorrect" and the Antoninus edict "unauthentic" (npnf201: 21009–21013). Melito himself claims Hadrian and Antoninus wrote against new measures (npnf201: 25890–25897). No held old source records any reply to these petitions. Leave the edicts out; Melito's claim may stand credited to him.
- **Justin's death and Crescens**: Justin expected a plot, perhaps by Crescens (own words); Eusebius says the plot happened (later). Not told here.
- **Dates**: "124" for Quadratus and "c. 165" for Justin rest on Eusebius's Chronicle and Rusticus's term, neither held as an old source here: tell "in the reign of Hadrian", "under Marcus Aurelius". Diognetus: undated (Contested, 2nd–3rd c.).
- **"First to use the word Trinity"**: only the ANF editor's note (anf02: 8994). The word itself is Theophilus's.
