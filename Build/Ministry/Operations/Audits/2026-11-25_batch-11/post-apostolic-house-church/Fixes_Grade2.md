## What the sources hold

1. **Opening: when.** No held old text gives a year for Pliny's letter. The old texts place it by reign. Pliny writes to Trajan (mirbt 2768, "C. Plinius Traiano imperatori"). Eusebius puts Rome's letter in Clement's time, from Domitian's twelfth year (npnf201 16634–16636), then Nerva, then Trajan (npnf201 16977, "After Nerva had reigned a little more than a year he was succeeded by Trajan"), and Pliny's letter under Trajan (npnf201 19443–19448). Jerome's Chronicle sets it just after the entry for Trajan's tenth year (jerome-chron 42193–42262). Later (Eusebius, ~200 years; Jerome, ~270). The century count is the same step the outline already takes for "near the end of the first century". So "early in the second century" is **Widely Accepted**, not Documented. No year number is told (Setting rule).
2. **Opening: where.** Still not held. Jerome confirms the old texts leave the province unnamed: "cum quandam prouinciam regeret" (jerome-chron 42246–42247, "when he ruled a certain province"). Tertullian: "ruler of a province" (anf03 1567). Eusebius: "one of the most noted of governors" (npnf201 19448). "Bithynia" appears only in modern notes (npnf201 19457, 74724; Harnack; Mansi's editor), so under Part 1 (Rigor) it stays out. The plainest held where is now the first sentence's own: Pliny governed a Roman province, and Christians had spread through its cities, villages and countryside (Source Sheet L4). A listener may still want the province's name; no held old source gives it. Note: the live `pliny-the-younger` story names Bithynia and "around the year 112". That is outside this pass.
3. **Guilty of what, and why a crime.** Pliny's own letter gives no law. It says he did not know what was punished: "nomen ipsum, si flagitiis careat, an flagitia cohaerentia nomini puniantur" (mirbt 2772–2775, OCR as printed). English 1890: "whether the mere name, if unaccompanied with crimes, or crimes associated with the name, should be punished" (npnf201 19463–19469). Contemporary, hostile, own words of the questioner. Documented that he says so. Why the name was a crime is not held beyond this. This is new Source Sheet L7. It is not a barred quote; it is told as narration. The `pliny-the-younger` story covers the same doubt in its own words ("he did not know what was punished"), not this clause.
4. **Whose day.** Irenaeus: the elders before Soter "neither observed it [the fourteenth day] themselves … And yet though not observing it, they were none the less at peace with those who came to them from the parishes in which it was observed" (npnf201 31530–31534). "The presbyters before thee who did not observe it, sent the eucharist to those of other parishes who observed it" (npnf201 31549–31553). Later (Irenaeus's own words, quoted by Eusebius). Documented (Source Sheet V1). "A share of the thanksgiving" glosses "sent the eucharist" from Justin's own terms: the food "is called among us eucharist" (1apol-grc 1730; J4), and deacons carry "a portion" to the absent (J3). McGiffert's note gives Valesius's other reading, that it went to other churches, not to visitors (npnf201 31553ff.); the brief keeps the outline's reading, unchanged.

## Cuts, and where each goes

- **Trajan's name** (1): **Leave out**. The outline's Names list already gives Trajan by role, "the emperor".
- **4.** "They were called, he wrote, ministrae, servers." (mirbt 2795–2796; L3): **for the card**. It is not on the Never cut list, which keeps the two women tortured to find the truth. That stays. The slate allowed the word; it did not require it.
- **5.** "and the hope they all shared" (anf01 4703; S4): **Leave out**. Never cut keeps "Ignatius as a prisoner for the name". That stays.
- **6.** Justin's presider speaking and all standing to pray (1apol-grc 1762–1767): **Leave out**. Not on Never cut. The bread, wine and water, the Amen, the deacons' share and the Sun's-day reason all stay.
- **7.** "the letter Rome had sent" becomes "Rome's letter". Nothing lost.
- **8, 9.** "once" and "himself": nothing lost. Justin is still marked as a Christian, an inside witness.

None of these touches the heart, the record's force (the dawn hymn, the two women tortured, Rome's letter as the only side, Ignatius a prisoner, the Sunday, the Easter peace), or a gloss Part 1 requires. The Ending is reworded, not cut, as the grade requires.
