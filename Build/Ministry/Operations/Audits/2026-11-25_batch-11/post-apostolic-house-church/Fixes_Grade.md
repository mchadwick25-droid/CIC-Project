## What the sources hold

1. **Opening: where.** No held old text names Pliny's province. Tertullian says only "ruler of a province" (anf03 1566–1567), Eusebius "one of the most noted of governors" (npnf201 19448). "Bithynia" appears only in modern notes: McGiffert's (npnf201 19457) and an early-modern editor's note in Mansi (`mansi_sacrorum-conciliorum-collectio-tomus-2-305-346-lat_welter-facsimile1901.txt` 43261–43262). Under Part 1 (Rigor) it stays out. The plainest where the letter itself gives is the spread through his province: "Neque civitates tantum sed vicos etiam atque agros superstitionis istius contagio pervagata est" (mirbt 2800–2801, OCR as printed). English 1890: "not only the cities, but also the villages and even the country districts" (npnf201 19511–19512). Contemporary, hostile. Documented. This is Source Sheet L4. Added to Outline Setting (Places) and Opening 1. The listener may still want the province's name; no held source gives it.
2. **Irenaeus's line: the thread.** The elders before Soter are named in his letter as Anicetus, Pius, Hyginus, Telesphorus and Xystus (npnf201 31527–31530). The same five stand in his list of Rome's bishops (anf01 39847–39849; "Sixtus" for Xystus), and "by this succession, the ecclesiastical tradition from the apostles, and the preaching of the truth, have come down to us" (anf01 39851–39853). Later (Irenaeus, his own words in an 1885 English of a Latin version). Documented that he says so (Source Sheet V2, V6). The new wording names them as "these same men" and Irenaeus as the speaker, so it ties back to the elders of the sentence before. Outline Ending 1 now carries it.
3. **Corinth: why.** Rome's letter gives its own reason. "Every kind of honour and happiness was bestowed upon you … Hence flowed emulation and envy, strife and sedition … the young against those advanced in years" (anf01 605–615). The sedition was "kindled" by "a few rash and self-confident persons" (anf01 531–535), "on account of one or two persons" (anf01 2053–2055), against men who served "blamelessly and with honour" (anf01 1941–1943). Own words of Rome's church, one side only. Documented that the letter says so. Corinth's own reason is not held. Added as Source Sheet C7 and to Outline Beat 2. Credited in the brief as the letter's blame, so it stays Rome's view.
