## BLOCKING findings

**1. SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** "This, they told him, was the whole of their guilt, or their error: 'On a fixed day they used to meet before dawn. They sang a hymn to Christ as to a god, taking turns among themselves.'"
- Source, mirbt 2787–2792: "Adfirmabant autem hanc fuisse summam vel culpae suae vel erroris, quod essent soliti stato die ante lucem convenire carmenque Christo quasi deo dicere secum invicem seque sacramento … obstringere … rursusque coeundi ad capiendum cibum". The English cross-check is npnf201 19493–19500.
- The "whole" in Pliny is four things: the meeting and hymn, the oath against theft, robbery and adultery, and the later meal. After Cut first 9, the story gives "the whole" as the hymn alone, which claims a completeness the quote no longer has.
- **Change:** "Part of their guilt, or their error, they told him, was this:" (the quote is unchanged).

**2. SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** "Near the end of the first century, some at Corinth, in Greece, removed their elders, the senior men who led the church together."
- Source, anf01 1940–1943: "ye have removed **some men** of excellent behaviour from the ministry". The same letter speaks of a "sedition against its presbyters" (anf01 2053–2055).
- The letter says some were removed. "Their elders" reads as all of them.
- **Change:** "removed some of their elders".

**3. SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** "At Corinth's request, with the quarrel spreading, the church of Rome wrote."
- Source, anf01 2055–2059: "this **rumour** has reached not only us, but those also who are unconnected with us". The letter's own reasons for writing are Corinth's consultation and its own delay (anf01 523–528).
- In the letter, the report spread to outsiders. It never says the quarrel itself spread, and it never gives that as a reason for writing.
- **Change:** cut "with the quarrel spreading," so the sentence reads "At Corinth's request, the church of Rome wrote."

**4. ASSUMPTION ADDED.** "…Christians in Antioch, in the cities of Asia Minor and in Rome met in small gatherings, and their letters greet one another household by household."
- No source gives the size of their gatherings. Justin has "all … gather in one place" (ἐπὶ τὸ αὐτὸ συνέλευσις, 1apol-grc 1758–1759). Ignatius writes "there let the multitude [of the people] also be" (anf01 8285). Pliny has "multi enim omnis aetatis" (mirbt 2797). The household greetings (anf01 8503–8508, 8868–8869) hold.
- **Change:** "met together" in place of "met in small gatherings".
- This wording is in the approved heart and in the outline's own Do not say ("→ 'met in small gatherings'"). Per the role prompt, the story is cut to the source and the outline is not matched to the story. **Do not say** line for the outline: "'small gatherings' or any size for their meetings → 'met together'; no source gives their size."

**5. ASSUMPTION ADDED.** "On the road he wrote ahead to the churches of Asia, a Roman province in today's western Turkey."
- Ignatius wrote to Ephesus, Magnesia and Tralles from Smyrna (anf01 6221, 6786, 7309). He wrote to Philadelphia and Smyrna from Troas, after he had passed them (anf01 7895, 8467; "From Smyrna he came to Troas", 4647). He is not on his way to any of these churches, so "ahead" adds a route the letters do not give.
- **Change:** cut "ahead", so the sentence reads "On the road he wrote to the churches of Asia, …".

**6. MODERN ONLY.** "One community's manual, whose writer, date and place no one knows, opens on the Two Ways."
- The text calls itself "The Lord's Teaching Through the Twelve Apostles to the Nations" (anf07 12690). Its editor writes that it represents "only a very small fraction of the Christians", following the theory that it "was written for a community of Christians in some obscure locality" (anf07 12664–12674). "Writer, date and place unknown" is a fair use of that editor's note as a tool. "One community's" is his opinion.
- **Change:** "A short manual, whose writer, date and place no one knows, opens on the Two Ways." The later sentence "It also has them appoint…" still reads.
- **Do not say** line for the outline: "'one community's manual' → 'a short manual'; the text gives no community, and calls itself the apostles' teaching to the nations."
