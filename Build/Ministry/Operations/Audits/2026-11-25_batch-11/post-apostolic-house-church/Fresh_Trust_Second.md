## BLOCKING

**T2-1. SOFTENED OR CHANGED: a detail moved onto the wrong letter.**
Brief: "Later, Dionysius, bishop of Corinth, wrote, in words the church historian Eusebius keeps, that his church still read aloud on the Lord's day the letter Rome had sent in the quarrel, written through Clement."
Source (npnf201 25113–25117, Dionysius as Eusebius quotes him): "To-day we have passed the Lord's holy day, in which we have read your epistle. From it, whenever we read it, we shall always be able to draw advice, as also from the former epistle, which was written to us through Clement."
What Corinth read on that Lord's day was Rome's *new* letter, from Soter's church. Dionysius says only that they draw advice from the old letter as well. Eusebius's own gloss (npnf201 25111–25112) is "the custom from the beginning to read it in the church", and gives no day. The brief moves the Lord's-day reading from the new letter onto Clement's. That is one of Part 1's four faults: a detail moved onto the wrong event.
**Exact change** (told as the source gives it, 3 words shorter): "Later, Dionysius, bishop of Corinth, wrote, in words the church historian Eusebius keeps, that his church still drew counsel from the letter Rome had sent in the quarrel, written through Clement."
**Do not say, for the outline:** "Corinth read Clement's letter on the Lord's day" → "drew counsel from" it. Dionysius's Lord's-day reading was Rome's new letter (npnf201 25114–25116).

## SUMMARY HOLDS (not blocking; notes only)

1. "Part of their guilt, or their error, they told him, was this: …" Latin (mirbt 2789–2790): "hanc fuisse summam vel culpae suae vel erroris", "this was the sum of their guilt or error", and the sum also takes in the oath and the meal (mirbt 2791–2795). "Part" is accurate for the two clauses quoted, and the outline's Do not say rules out "the whole" here. It does drop the force of *summa*, though: this was all they would admit to. That holds as a compression. A note, not a change.
2. "In the hundred years after the apostles, Christians in Antioch, in the cities of Asia Minor and in Rome met together, and their letters greet one another household by household." The greetings to households are Ignatius's: "I salute the families of my brethren, with their wives and children … I salute the house of Tavias" (anf01 8503–8507); "the wife of Epitropus, with all her house and children" (anf01 8867–8868). Fair for the period's letters.
3. "Letters and messengers carried between their churches held them together." Rome sends three messengers so that they may "announce to us the peace and harmony we so earnestly desire" (anf01 2395–2398). Ignatius asks for messengers and letters (anf01 8854–8863). The heart's own summary, and the letters' stated aim.
4. "A short manual, whose writer, date and place no one knows, opens on the Two Ways." The text names no writer or place. The editor's note says only that attempts to find the author are "futile" and that "even the region … cannot be determined", with dates from 80 to 190 (anf07 12668–12672). That is a modern note, used here only to clarify, which Part 1 allows.
5. "Each church, he urged, should act as one around its bishop, with its elders and deacons." "do all things with a divine harmony, while your bishop presides in the place of God, and your presbyters in the place of the assembly of the apostles, along with your deacons" (anf01 5853–5856).
6. "What was given went to orphans and widows, the sick, prisoners and strangers." Greek (1apol-grc 1774–1779): those well off who wish give as each chooses. What is gathered is left with the one who presides, and he helps ὀρφανοῖς τε καὶ χήραις, those in want διὰ νόσον, τοῖς ἐν δεσμοῖς, τοῖς παρεπιδήμοις … ξένοις. The brief drops the free giving and the president's charge, but the meaning holds.
7. "Near the end of the century, the churches of Asia kept Easter on the Passover date, the moon's fourteenth day; others kept it only on the Lord's day." "the parishes of all Asia, as from an older tradition, held that the fourteenth day of the moon … should be observed"; the others ended the fast "on no other day than on that of the resurrection" (npnf201 31136–31140, 31217–31222). The date is Victor's time (npnf201 31003).
8. "Elsewhere he counts these men among its bishops, and says the apostles' teaching came down to his day through their line." Sixtus, Telesphorus, Hyginus, Pius and Anicetus all stand in the line of "the episcopate", and "by this succession, the ecclesiastical tradition from the apostles, and the preaching of the truth, have come down to us" (anf01 39842–39851).
9. "They kept a different day, yet lived at peace with visitors who kept Asia's day, and sent them the thanksgiving." "They neither observed it … were none the less at peace with those who came to them from the parishes in which it was observed"; "the presbyters before thee who did not observe it, sent the eucharist to those of other parishes who observed it" (npnf201 31530–31534, 31549–31551). "Them" stands for "those of other parishes". That is fair, since the Greek ἀπὸ τῶν παροικιῶν ("from the parishes") allows it.
