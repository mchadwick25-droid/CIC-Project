## BLOCKING findings

**F1. MODERN ONLY (paragraph 1, sentence 1).** "Under the emperor Trajan, Pliny, the Roman governor of Bithynia on the Black Sea, questioned people…"
- "Governor" holds: Eusebius, "Plinius Secundus, one of the most noted of governors" (npnf201 19448); Tertullian, "the younger Pliny, when he was ruler of a province" (anf03_tertullian.xml 1566–1567). "Under Trajan" holds: "C. Plinius Traiano imperatori" (mirbt 2768).
- "Bithynia" stands in the held texts only on McGiffert's modern note, "The epistle was written from Bithynia" (npnf201 19457). The letter itself (mirbt 2768–2800) names no province. Searched for "Bithyn" in mirbt, npnf201 and every anf volume, and for Pliny in anf03. The Source Sheet rates S7 Documented, but its only line for the place is that note.
- **Change:** "Under the emperor Trajan, Pliny, a Roman governor ruling a province, questioned people…" (−3 words). Or, if the batch rules the province a Widely Accepted identification (Part 1 grants that exemption by name only to dates), keep the sentence and record the ruling in the outline's Setting. This read does not make that ruling.

**F2. SOFTENED OR CHANGED (Justin paragraph).** "What was given went to orphans and widows, the sick, prisoners and strangers."
- Source: "Οἱ εὐποροῦντες δὲ καὶ βουλόμενοι κατὰ προαίρεσιν ἕκαστος τὴν ἑαυτοῦ ὃ βούλεται δίδωσι, καὶ τὸ συλλεγόμενον παρὰ τῷ προεστῶτι ἀποτίθεται, καὶ αὐτὸς ἐπικουρεῖ ὀρφανοῖς τε καὶ χήραις…" (those who are well off and willing each give what they wish; what is collected is left with the one who presides, and he helps orphans and widows…; 1apol-grc 1772–1776).
- The brief drops the givers and the free choice. Coming straight after "Deacons gave to each and carried a share to those absent", "What was given" reads as the shared bread and cup going to the needy. That is a misleading implication.
- **Change:** "The well-off gave what they chose, for orphans, widows, the sick, prisoners and strangers." (+1 word)

**F3. SOFTENED OR CHANGED: a detail moved onto the wrong letter (Dionysius paragraph).** "…that his church still read aloud on the Lord's day the letter Rome had sent in the quarrel, written through Clement."
- Source: "To-day we have passed the Lord's holy day, in which we have read your epistle. From it, whenever we read it, we shall always be able to draw advice, as also from the former epistle, which was written to us through Clement" (npnf201 25113–25117). Eusebius: "showing that it had been the custom from the beginning to read it in the church" (npnf201 25111–25113).
- The Lord's-day reading Dionysius reports is of Soter's new letter. That the older letter was read in church rests on his "as also from the former epistle" and on Eusebius's gloss, with no day named. The brief moves the Lord's day onto the older letter.
- **Change:** "…that his church still read aloud, and drew advice from, the letter Rome had sent in the quarrel, written through Clement." (±0 words). The outline's Beat 7 and What came of it 1 ("Corinth read Rome's on the Lord's day") left room for this. **Do not say line for the outline:** "Corinth read Clement's letter on the Lord's day" → the Lord's-day reading was of Soter's letter; the older one is read and drawn on, with no day given (npnf201 25113–25117).

## SUMMARY HOLDS (with evidence)

1. "Part of their guilt, or their error, they told him, was this:". Latin: "hanc fuisse summam vel culpae suae vel erroris, quod essent soliti stato die ante lucem convenire…" (mirbt 2787–2788). The summa also took in the oath and the meal (mirbt 2789–2792), which are left out here. So "part" is accurate, and the Outline's Do not say requires it.
2. Essence, sentence 1 ("met together… greet one another household by household"). Ignatius: "I salute the families of my brethren, with their wives and children… I salute the house of Tavias" (anf01 8503–8507); "the wife of Epitropus, with all her house and children" (anf01 8867–8868).
3. Essence, sentence 2 ("taught newcomers a way of life before baptism…"). Justin at Rome: those who "undertake to be able to live accordingly" are taught to pray and fast before they are led to the water (1apol-grc 1580–1584). Also the Didache (anf07 12914), Justin 67 (bread and cup on the Sun's day), Ignatius (anf01 5851–5856) and 1 Clement (anf01 1855–1858).
4. Essence, sentence 3 ("Letters and messengers… held them together"). Rome's messengers were sent "that they may the sooner announce to us the peace and harmony" (anf01 2394–2397). Ignatius asks churches to send "messengers" and "letters" (anf01 8858–8860). A fair compression.
5. "A short manual, whose writer, date and place no one knows, opens on the Two Ways." The editor (a tool, not named): "All attempts to discover the author are… futile. Even the region… cannot be determined"; scholars' dates range from 80 to 190 (anf07 12668–12670). First words: "There are two ways" (anf07 12700).
6. "Newcomers learned this way of life before being baptized…". The source: "Having first said all these things, baptize into the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit" (anf07 12914).
7. "At Corinth's request, the church of Rome wrote." The source: "the points respecting which you consulted us" (anf01 527–528).
8. "On the road he wrote to the churches of Asia…". He wrote "from Smyrna" (anf01 6219) and from Troas (anf01 7914) before he "must suddenly sail from Troas" (anf01 8855).
9. "Each church, he urged, should act as one around its bishop, with its elders and deacons." The source: "do all things with a divine harmony, while your bishop presides… your presbyters… along with your deacons" (anf01 5851–5856), and "follow the bishop… the presbytery… the deacons" (anf01 8274–8276).
10. "Justin wrote to the emperor Antoninus Pius what Christians did each week". The source: "Αὐτοκράτορι Τίτῳ Αἰλίῳ Ἀδριανῷ Ἀντωνίνῳ Εὐσεβεῖ" (1apol-grc 68), with his sons, the Senate and the people. The weekly Sun's day: 1apol-grc 1759.
11. "Then the one who presides spoke, and all stood to pray." The source: "ὁ προεστὼς διὰ λόγου τὴν νουθεσίαν… ποιεῖται. Ἔπειτα ἀνιστάμεθα κοινῇ πάντες καὶ εὐχὰς πέμπομεν" (1apol-grc 1763–1765). His call to follow these good things is left out.
12. "Elsewhere he counts these men among its bishops, and says the apostles' teaching came down to his day through their line." The source: "Sixtus… Telesphorus… Hyginus… Pius… Anicetus… In this order, and by this succession, the ecclesiastical tradition from the apostles, and the preaching of the truth, have come down to us" (anf01 39845–39851).
13. "They kept a different day, yet lived at peace with visitors who kept Asia's day, and sent them the thanksgiving." The source: "they were none the less at peace with those who came to them from the parishes in which it was observed… sent the eucharist to those of other parishes who observed it" (npnf201 31531–31553). "Them" follows the outline's "those from other churches", not "the churches of Asia". Eusebius's Greek for Book V is not held, so it could not be checked.
