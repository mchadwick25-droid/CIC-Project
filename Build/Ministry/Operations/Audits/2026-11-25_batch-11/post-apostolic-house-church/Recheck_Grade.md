## Change 3 (Corinth, why): FIX

Rome's letter gives two things. Envy is the source of the sedition (anf01 605–608: "Hence flowed emulation and envy, strife and sedition"). One or two people are why the church rose against its elders (anf01 2053–2055: "on account of one or two persons, engage in sedition against its presbyters"; anf01 531–535, "a few rash and self-confident persons have kindled"). "blames envy, stirred by one or two people" goes further: it says those people stirred the envy, and the letter does not say that. Heard aloud, "envy, stirred by…" also makes envy the thing aimed "against men". The new text keeps the two apart. It uses "Corinth", not "the church", so "the church" can't be heard as Rome's church from the sentence before. The sentence runs 22 words.

- Old: `Its letter, the only side that survives, blames envy, stirred by one or two people against men who had served blamelessly.`
- New: `Its letter, the only side that survives, blames envy, and one or two people who stirred Corinth against men who had served blamelessly.`
