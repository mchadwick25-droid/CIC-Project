## 3. Impact, ranked heaviest first (what later witnesses carried on and used)

1. **Their letters went on being read in the churches.** Corinth read Rome's letters on the Lord's day (R2); Eusebius, in the early 300s, says Clement's letter "has been publicly used in a great many churches both in former times and in our own" (npnf201 16683–16684); Polycarp collected and sent on Ignatius's letters (P5); Irenaeus called Rome's letter "most powerful" (anf01 39835). Documented. Defended: the order received from the apostles (C3, C4).
2. **Rome's church continued, and its leaders were counted.** Irenaeus lists Rome's leaders in a line from the apostles (V2) and, writing to Victor, calls the earlier ones "presbyters … who presided" (V1); census line: continued as the Roman Church in the Third Century (Widely Accepted). Rome's custom of sending gifts "from the beginning" (R1) and the thanksgiving sent to members of other churches (V1) are practices passed on.
3. **Their manuals became books for newcomers.** Athanasius in 367 puts "that which is called the Teaching of the Apostles, and the Shepherd" among books "appointed by the Fathers to be read by those who newly join us" (npnf204 68817–68823). Later, ~200 years. Documented.
4. **The Sunday assembly as Justin describes it** is the fullest early account and is told on the tree here (slate). Later use: not traced in held sources for this sheet; not claimed.

## 5. Where sources disagree or come late

- **Who led.** Ignatius: one bishop presiding with presbyters and deacons (I1–I3). Rome's letter: the same leaders called episcopate and presbyters (C4). Polycarp writes with "the presbyters with him" and never calls himself bishop, though Ignatius addresses him as bishop (P1, S6). Hermas: "the presbyters who preside" (H1) and also "bishops" (H2). Irenaeus: Rome's earlier leaders listed in the episcopate (V2) and called "presbyters … who presided" (V1). All old; the narrator tells each as its witness gives it, without settling the order of development.
- **The thanksgiving.** The Didache: cup first, then bread; prayers with no words of institution (D8). Justin: bread and the cup together, with Jesus's words from the Gospels (J3, J6). Ignatius: only one the bishop leads or entrusts (I3). Different witnesses, places and possibly times; none is said by its writer to be universal.
- **Authorship of Rome's letter.** The letter is in the name of the church (S1); Dionysius (c. Soter's time) first names Clement (R2); Irenaeus and Eusebius follow (S2, S3).
- **Ignatius's letters.** The old witnesses accept them (P5, Eusebius). Their authorship and date are disputed only by modern scholars (I9): card.
- **The Didache.** Undated, unplaced (S11); one short manual, which names no community (anf07 12690).
- **Pliny.** A hostile outsider; what he knows of the meetings came from people who had left (L5) and from two women under torture (L3).
- **Late:** Dionysius and Hegesippus survive only as Eusebius quotes them (written ~150 years before Eusebius); the Acts of Justin is anonymous and undated.
