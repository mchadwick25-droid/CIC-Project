## BLOCKING

**1. ASSUMPTION ADDED (essence, sentence 1).** "...Christians in Antioch, in the cities of Asia Minor and in Rome met in small gatherings, and their letters greet one another household by household."
- No held line gives the size of their meetings. Justin: "πάντων κατὰ πόλεις ἢ ἀγροὺς μενόντων ἐπὶ τὸ αὐτὸ συνέλευσις γίνεται", all in towns or country gather in one place (1apol-grc 1756–1757). Ignatius: "Wherever the bishop shall appear, there let the multitude [of the people] also be" (anf01 8282–8283). Pliny: "Multi enim omnis aetatis, omnis ordinis, utriusque sexus" (mirbt 2797–2798). The household greetings hold: "I salute the house of Tavias" (anf01 8505); "the wife of Epitropus, with all her house and children" (anf01 8868–8869).
- Change: "met in small gatherings, and their letters greet" → "met together, and their letters greet".
- Do not say (outline left room: its essence and first Do not say entry both give "small gatherings"): "met in small gatherings" → "met together". No source gives the size of their meetings.

**2. SOFTENED OR CHANGED (Beat 2, sentence 1).** "Near the end of the first century, some at Corinth, in Greece, removed their elders, the senior men who led the church together."
- Source: "But we see that ye have removed some men of excellent behaviour from the ministry" (anf01 1941–1943); "should, on account of one or two persons, engage in sedition against its presbyters" (anf01 2053–2055). The letter says the church removed *some* of its leaders. "Removed their elders" says all of them.
- Change: "some at Corinth, in Greece, removed their elders," → "the church at Corinth, in Greece, removed some of its elders,".
- Do not say: "removed their elders" or "removed its elders" → "removed some of its elders" ("some men", anf01 1942).

**3. ASSUMPTION ADDED (Beat 3, sentence 2).** "On the road he wrote ahead to the churches of Asia, a Roman province in today's western Turkey."
- "Ahead" is not in the source, and it is wrong for most of the letters. He wrote to Ephesus, Magnesia and Tralles from Smyrna, to churches whose people came to him: "Ephesians from Smyrna (whence I also write to you)" (anf01 6221). He wrote to Philadelphia and Smyrna from Troas, after he had passed them: "The love of the brethren at Troas salutes you; whence also I write to you" (anf01 7914, 8467). And to Polycarp: "I must suddenly sail from Troas to Neapolis" (anf01 8854–8856). Only Rome lay ahead.
- Change: "On the road he wrote ahead to the churches of Asia," → "On the road he wrote to the churches of Asia,".
- Do not say: "wrote ahead to the churches of Asia" → "wrote to the churches of Asia". He wrote from Smyrna and Troas.

**4. ASSUMPTION ADDED (Beat 6, sentence 1).** "Justin wrote to the emperor Antoninus Pius what Christians at Rome did each week:"
- Justin addresses the emperor: "Αὐτοκράτορι Τίτῳ Αἰλίῳ Ἀδριανῷ Ἀντωνίνῳ Εὐσεβεῖ" (1apol-grc 68). That holds. But he gives the Sunday as what "we" do, all who live "κατὰ πόλεις ἢ ἀγροὺς", in towns or country (1apol-grc 1756), and "Τὴν δὲ τοῦ ἡλίου ἡμέρα κοινῇ πάντες τὴν συνέλευσιν ποιούμεθα" (1apol-grc 1777–1778). He never ties it to Rome. That he wrote at Rome is held elsewhere ("your city under Urbicus", 2 Apology, anf01 16739). That his account is Rome's practice alone is a modern reading. The quote that follows already carries Justin's own scope.
- Change: "what Christians at Rome did each week:" → "what Christians did each week:".
- Do not say: the right side of "Justin describes how all churches worshipped" → "what Christians did", in his words "all who live in the towns or in the country"; do not narrow it to Rome, and do not widen it beyond his words.

## The heart

The heart is right for this subject. The sources show local churches, each the "Church of God" in its city (anf01 519–521). They taught before baptism, gave thanks on the Lord's day, and were led by bishops, elders and deacons, linked by letters and messengers. The essence closes at about word 155, at about one minute. That is on time.
