# Outline check: The Post-Apostolic House Churches (`post-apostolic-house-church`)

## My own account of the heart (written before reading `Outline.md`)

From the census entry, the card, the source sheet and what the subject is known for:

- **Essence:** In the century or so after the apostles died (about 70 to 200), Christians in Antioch, the province of Asia and Rome met in ordinary households. They were held together by one way of entering (teaching on the Two Ways, then baptism), one weekly meal of thanksgiving on the Lord's day, care for their poor, and letters carried by hand from church to church, while the question of who should lead them was still being worked out.
- **Who they were:** the households of believers / the house churches after the apostles (Didache, 1 Clement, Ignatius, Polycarp, Hermas, Justin speak from inside; Pliny from outside).
- **When and where:** about 70 to 200; Antioch in Syria, Smyrna and the province of Asia, Rome, with Corinth, Philippi and Bithynia on the edges.
- **What they faced:** the apostles gone and leadership unsettled (Corinth removes its elders, Rome writes; Ignatius urges one bishop with elders and deacons; Polycarp writes with "the presbyters with him"); outside pressure: a governor's inquiry, his ban on clubs, two women tortured; Ignatius bound "for the common name and hope".
- **What they believed:** the Two Ways of life and death; God sent Christ, Christ the apostles; a hymn to Christ "as God" (Pliny); the bread and cup "not as common bread"; Sunday kept for creation and the resurrection.
- **How they practiced:** teaching then baptism with fasting; the Lord's day thanksgiving for the baptized only, after confession and reconciliation; readings from the apostles' memoirs and the prophets; the presider's talk, prayer standing, the deacons carrying shares to the absent; a collection for widows, orphans, the sick, prisoners and strangers; fasting and daily prayer.
- **What held them together:** the shared Lord's-day table with its way in through teaching and baptism, and the letters (and messengers) passing between churches.
- **What came of them:** their letters went on being read in the churches (Corinth reading Rome's letters on the Lord's day; Polycarp's collection of Ignatius); Rome's church continued, its leaders counted (continued as the Roman Church in the Third Century); the Didache and Hermas kept as books for newcomers.

**B2. The essence says more than its sources.** "met in small gatherings, some in the houses of their own members" rests on three passages, and none of them says a church met in a member's house. In the later anonymous trial account (anf01 29302–29312), Justin lives "above one Martinus" and knows of no "other meeting than his". Hermas's bishops "received into their houses the servants of God" (anf02 4946–4952), which is hospitality, not a meeting place. Ignatius greets "families" and "the house of Tavias" (anf01 8503–8508), which are households, not meeting places. The trial account is also one the outline puts in Leave out. Correction:
- In the essence, replace "met in small gatherings, some in the houses of their own members." with "met in small gatherings, and their letters greet one another household by household."
- Replace the confidence note "(Documented, except "some in the houses of their own members", Widely Accepted: anf01 29302–29312, the later trial account of Justin, and anf02 4946–4952; also anf01 8503–8508; …" with "(Documented: anf01 8503–8508, 8868–8869; …", keeping the rest of the list.
- Replace the first Do not say entry's right side with: "→ "met in small gatherings"; their letters greet whole households (anf01 8503–8508, 8868–8869). "House churches" is the tree's name, not theirs; their word is "the church of God" in a city. Do not say where they met."
- In Leave out (Acts of Justin), replace "supports the essence's "some in houses", not told." with "not told."

The spoken name "The Post-Apostolic House Churches" was set by the slate and may stand, as long as the story never claims they all met in houses.

**B3. "What they faced" implies a change in leadership that the old sources do not state.** "What changed them: … Irenaeus wrote to Rome's bishop of "the presbyters before Soter…"; the old sources do not say how the change came." This asserts that a change happened. That is the modern reading (Lampe, V2: Contested, card only), which the outline's own Do not say bars. It is also a line that only says what is missing. Correction: replace from "What changed them:" to the end of the line with:

"Where it ends: near the end of the second century, Rome's bishop tried to cut off the churches of Asia over Easter, and Irenaeus pointed him to the elders who had presided at Rome before Soter and kept peace with churches of another custom (npnf201 31527–31553). Irenaeus also counts those same men in the bishop's office in a line from the apostles (anf01 39823–39851). Documented."

**B4. Beat 6 splices two of Justin's occasions into one.** "then they were brought to the brothers for prayer and a kiss. Quote 2. Then bread, wine and water were brought…" joins two different occasions. The kiss and the prayer belong to the thanksgiving after a baptism (First Apology 65, anf01 16401–16436). The readings, the talk, the standing prayer and the collection belong to the weekly Sunday (ch. 67, anf01 16490–16526). Told this way, the newly baptized seem to walk into the Sunday readings: a detail moved onto the wrong event. Correction:
- Replace Beat 6 from "Justin wrote to the emperor what Christians did." through "Quote 2." with: "Justin wrote to the emperor what Christians at Rome did each week. Quote 2."
- Replace Cut first item 5 with: "5. Justin's account of baptism and the kiss (First Apology 61, 65), since Beat 1 carries baptism: **card**." This item is already struck by the correction above. It stays in the list so the card keeps it.

**B5. Beat 1 tells only the master's half of the Didache's rule on slaves.** The same passage also says "ye bondmen shall be subject to your masters as to a type of God" (anf07 12831–12835). Telling only "both hope in the same God" softens the record by leaving out its force. Correction: Cut first item 8 (B1) leaves the clause out entirely. If the writer keeps it, both halves must be told.
