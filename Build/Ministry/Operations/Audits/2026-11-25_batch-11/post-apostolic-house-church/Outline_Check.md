# Outline check: The Post-Apostolic House Churches (`post-apostolic-house-church`)

## My own account of the heart (written before reading `Outline.md`)

From the census entry, the card, the source sheet and what the subject is known for:

- **Essence:** In the century or so after the apostles died (about 70 to 200), Christians in Antioch, the province of Asia and Rome met in ordinary households. They were held together by one way of entering (teaching on the Two Ways, then baptism), one weekly meal of thanksgiving on the Lord's day, care for their poor, and letters carried by hand from church to church, while the question of who should lead them was still being worked out.
- **Who they were:** the households of believers / the house churches after the apostles (Didache, 1 Clement, Ignatius, Polycarp, Hermas, Justin speak from inside; Pliny from outside).
- **When and where:** about 70 to 200; Antioch in Syria, Smyrna and the province of Asia, Rome, with Corinth, Philippi and Bithynia on the edges.
- **What they faced:** the apostles gone and leadership unsettled (Corinth removes its elders, Rome writes; Ignatius urges one bishop with elders and deacons; Polycarp writes with "the presbyters with him"); outside pressure: a governor's inquiry, his ban on clubs, two women tortured; Ignatius bound "for the common name and hope".
- **What they believed:** the Two Ways of life and death; God sent Christ, Christ the apostles; a hymn to Christ "as God" (Pliny); the bread and cup "not as common bread"; Sunday kept for creation and the resurrection.
- **How they practiced:** teaching then baptism with fasting; the Lord's day thanksgiving for the baptized only, after confession and reconciliation; readings from the apostles' memoirs and the prophets; the presider's talk, prayer standing, the deacons carrying shares to the absent; a collection for widows, orphans, the sick, prisoners and strangers; fasting and daily prayer.
- **What held them together:** the shared Lord's-day table with its way in through teaching and baptism, and the letters (and messengers) passing between churches.
- **What came of them:** their letters went on being read in the churches (Corinth reading Rome's letters on the Lord's day; Polycarp's collection of Ignatius); Rome's church continued, its leaders counted (continued as the Roman Church in the Third Century); the Didache and Hermas kept as books for newcomers.
