## 1. The test

- **Who these people were:** filled. "The Marcionites were the churches that followed Marcion after the church of Rome cast him out."
- **What happened to them:** filled. The martyrs Metrodorus and Asclepius, Constantine's edict, the meetings that went on, the eight villages Theodoret won over.
- **What their faith was about:** filled. "Jesus came from a good Father, above the God who made the world and gave the law and the prophets."
- **Why the connection matters:** filled, but thin. I can say they were a rival church that the other church argued with, outlawed and slowly took back (Cyril, Theodoret). Nothing tells me what they left the wider church beyond that rivalry. I can still fill the line, so no finding.

## 2. The opening

After three sentences I have who (Pionius, then Metrodorus), where (the stadium at Smyrna), when (March 250) and why there (Pionius refused to sacrifice). "A presbyter of the Marcionites" is not explained until sentence 5. That holds up, because "rival churches" comes right after it. Nothing is lost.

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing

1. **BLOCKING.** The last paragraph: "There John Chrysostom had once urged the bishop to drive out Marcion's followers, offering the aid of the imperial edicts. He worked to bring them to the one Son... He was stoned many times by the heretics he fought." Who is "He"? The last man named is Chrysostom, so on one hearing he is the one working and being stoned. "The bishop" could be Theodoret or someone earlier. Only "I led eight villages" makes me guess back. This loses the thread of the closing scene. Change: "There John Chrysostom had once urged an earlier bishop to drive out Marcion's followers, offering the aid of the imperial edicts. Theodoret worked to bring them to the one Son... Theodoret was stoned many times by the heretics he fought."
2. MINOR. "Tertullian gives the reason as a restless curiosity that spread to others." I lose whose curiosity it was and what it is the reason for. Tertullian is named here with no role, and his role comes two paragraphs later. Change: "Tertullian, a Christian writer at Carthage who wrote against him, says the reason was Marcion's restless curiosity, which spread to others." Then the later "At Carthage" sentence needs no second introduction.
3. MINOR. "...says Marcion trimmed both books of whatever made the world's maker Jesus' Father." On one hearing, "the world's maker Jesus' Father" runs together. Change: "...of every passage that showed the God who made the world as Jesus' Father."
4. MINOR. "Like every witness to them, he was their enemy, for no line of theirs survives in their own hand." The "for" sounds as if he was their enemy *because* nothing survives. Change: "No line of theirs survives in their own hand, so every witness to them, Irenaeus among them, was their enemy."
5. MINOR. "...the law and the prophets, which Jesus set aside." Did Jesus set aside the law and the prophets, or that God? Make the antecedent plain (the trust read should confirm which one the source means).

The "So Tertullian pressed them on persecution" chain also needs one inference: a God who never punishes means denial costs nothing. I got it, so it does not count.

## 4. Bare statements

1. **BLOCKING.** "...a law read out to the heretics by name, the Marcionites among them." Part 1 names "heresy" as a label that must be explained. "Heretics" comes three times (edict, Cyril, Theodoret) and is never put in plain words. A listener with no church background does not know what it means or why it mattered. Change: "...a law read out by name to the heretics, the groups the church judged to teach falsely, the Marcionites among them."

The checker flags the edict (s 24–25) as a possible bare statement. It is explained: what it did (forbade meetings, gave away their houses) and why (their teaching "made the healthy soul sick"). No other finding.

## 5. Citations read aloud

1. MINOR. "...the historian Eusebius records that Asclepius was burned in his own city..." The burning is a plain fact. The naming is needed only for the next sentence's blame ("as he thought"), and that sentence names him already. In the narrator's voice: "In the persecution that began under the emperor Diocletian, Asclepius was burned in his own city, Caesarea in Palestine. He was supposed to be, the historian Eusebius says, a bishop of the Marcionites, with zeal for God, as he thought."

Irenaeus, Tertullian and Rhodon are named for an enemy's charge or because they act in the story. That is called for. Cyril and Theodoret act in the story.

## 6. Tells

None of the Part 1 tells: no moral close, no dash, no question asked only to answer it, no stock word. "Not only good but just" (checker s 32) is Theodoret's own reported point against a God who is only good. It carries content and is not the narrator's turn, so it does not count.

**Lines that sound machine-made** (notes, not Part 1 tells):
- "For about three hundred years, from Rome to Persia and down to the villages of Syria, they gathered with their own presbyters, teachers and martyrs." A sweeping "from X to Y" span with a list of three. This is the most generated-sounding line in the piece.
- "They held one belief above all:" A colon set-up before the reveal. Plainer: "Above all, they held that Jesus came from a good Father..."
- Paragraphs 3 to 6 run as a chain of witness frames: "Rhodon found", "Tertullian reported", "In Tertullian's report", "Eusebius records", "Eusebius says". Most are justified as enemy charges. Together they drift toward a report about the record. Cutting the Eusebius frame (item 5) eases this.

## 7. Words, runs, tone

Unexplained (MINOR): "creed" (Cyril), "consul", "catholic" in the edict sentence (explained only later, through Cyril), and "Marcionists" next to "Marcionites" (a listener may hear a second group; add "as he called them"). There are no runs of short one-act sentences, and nothing talks down.

## 8. Pull and drift

The opening pulls me in: two rival presbyters nailed up side by side is a strong, concrete scene.

Attention first drifts at "Irenaeus, a bishop who wrote against them within a generation, says Marcion trimmed both books...". Why: paragraph 2 holds the scene back for seven summary sentences, names a second enemy witness in three sentences, and stacks clauses. **Heart late (BLOCKING, for the trust read to confirm):** what held them together ("They held one belief above all") starts at about word 212, past the first minute (about 150 words). Move that sentence up to follow "The Marcionites were the churches that followed Marcion...". Let the Scripture and Irenaeus sentences come after it.

The Rhodon quote and Tertullian's "Never, never!" pull me back in. Yes, I would listen to the end.
