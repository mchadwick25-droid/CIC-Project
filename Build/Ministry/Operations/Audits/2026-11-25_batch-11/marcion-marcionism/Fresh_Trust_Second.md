Presbyter: Pion 4807.
2. "He had refused to sacrifice to the gods, saying he feared the living God." **HELD.** Pion 5561-5562: "Θύεις; ἀπεκρίνατο· Οὔ ... ζῶντα θεὸν φοβοῦμαι."
3. "Beside him they raised Metrodorus, a presbyter of the rival Marcionites." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** Pion 5601-5602: "πρεσβύτερόν τινα Μητρόδωρον τῆς αἱρέσεως τῶν Μαρκιωνιστῶν". "Rival" fairly carries *hairesis* beside the Acts' "presbyter of the catholic church" (Pion 4808-4809).
4. "Both faced east, eyewitnesses wrote, and the wood was heaped around them." **HELD.** Pion 5604-5606: "ἀμφότεροι ἔβλεπον πρὸς ἀνατολάς ... τὰ ξύλα κύκλῳ"; eyewitnesses 5646: "εἴδομεν οἱ παραγενόμενοι".
5. "The Marcionites were the churches that followed Marcion after Rome's church cast him out, in the middle of the second century." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** ANF3 21333-21340: "in the reign of Antoninus for the most part ... in the church of Rome ... they were more than once expelled."
6. "For about three hundred years, from Rome to Persia and down to the villages of Syria... presbyters, teachers and martyrs." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** Epi 7470-7472 (Rome ... "ἐν τῇ Περσίδι"); Thdt 31511 (villages); Pion 5601-5602; E-gr 24595-24598; span from ANF3 21333 to Theodoret's fifth century.
7. "Their Scripture was two books, a Gospel cut down from Luke's and ten letters of Paul." **HELD.** Epi 8273-8278: "τῷ κατὰ Λουκᾶν εὐαγγελίῳ ... ἐπιστολὰς ... δέκα ... ταύταις δὲ ταῖς δυσὶ βίβλοις κέχρηται."
8. "They held one belief above all: Jesus came from a good Father, above the God who made the world, and Jesus abolished that God's law and prophets." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** ANF1 33577-33582: "Jesus being derived from that father who is above the God that made the world ... abolishing the prophets and the law". "Good": ANF1 33570 ("benevolent"), ANF3 24467 ("your most good and excellent god"). Irenaeus is credited in sentence 10, and sentence 11 says every witness was their enemy.
9. "Tertullian, a writer at Carthage, says Marcion was cast out for his restless curiosity, which spread to others." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** ANF3 21338-21339: "on account of their ever restless curiosity, with which they even infected the brethren, they were more than once expelled" (Oe 1775-1776, "ob inquietam semper curiositatem, qua fratres quoque vitiabant"). Said of Marcion and Valentinus together; no object added.
10. "Irenaeus... says Marcion trimmed both books of what called the world's maker Jesus' Father." **HELD.** ANF1 33585-33590: the Gospel passages "in which the Lord is recorded as most clearly confessing that the Maker of this universe is His Father"; the Epistles, "removing all that is said ... respecting that God who made the world, to the effect that He is the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ".
11. "Like every witness this story draws on, he was their enemy." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** True of each witness used (Acts of Pionius, Irenaeus, Epiphanius, Tertullian, Rhodon, Eusebius, Constantine, Cyril, Theodoret); the wording is the outline's (Who tells it).
12. "Late in the second century, a writer from Asia named Rhodon found Marcion's followers divided over how many first sources... there are." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** E-en 28896-28926: "At this time Rhodo, a native of Asia ... He says that this heresy was divided in his time into various opinions"; "two principles", "three natures" (29073-29105).
13. "Apelles, one of their teachers, held there was only one, one God, where Marcion held two." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** E-en 29153-29155: "he spoke of one principle"; 29196: "one unbegotten God"; 29197: "calling himself a teacher"; 29073-29095: others "hold to two principles, as does the mariner ... Marcion himself".
14. "Rhodon, who argued with him, set down his words:" + Q2. **HELD.** E-en 29119-29123: "he said that it was not at all necessary to examine one's doctrine, but that each one should continue to hold what he believed. For he asserted that those who trusted in the Crucified would be saved, if only they were found doing good works." Q2 renders every clause and adds nothing.
15. "About 207, Tertullian reported that their churches baptized only virgins, widows, the unmarried and the divorced, a rule he said condemned marriage." **HELD.** Kr 16349-16352: "non tingitur apud illum caro, nisi virgo, nisi vidua, nisi caeleps, nisi divortio baptisma mercata ... sine dubio ex damnatione coniugii institutio ista constabit." Date: I.15's "fifteenth year of Severus" (ANF3 898-903), Widely Accepted.
16. "Their good God, he said, punished no one, and only cast the sinner away out of sight." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** ANF3 24459-24464: "prohibiting a thing without annexing the sanction of punishment ... that he is to be cast away out of sight."
17. "So he pressed them on persecution." **HELD.** Kr 16281-16286: "qui deum non times quasi bonum ... quid non ... in persecutionibus ... negatione lucraris?" The "so" is Tertullian's own argument.
18. "An accused Christian was told to sprinkle incense from a box to the gods, and whoever did it denied Christ and went free." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** Kr 16286: "oblata acerra animam negatione lucraris"; Pion 5562-5563: "Ἄλλοι πολλοὶ ἔθυσαν καὶ ζῶσι". The custom is the outline's gloss.
19. "He put it to his Marcionite reader:" + Q1. **HELD.** Against the Latin, Kr 16286-16287: "quid non et in persecutionibus statim oblata acerra animam negatione lucraris? absit, inquis, absit!" (Oe 4956-4958 the same). Every clause is rendered; "Never" carries *absit*.
20. "In the persecution that began under the emperor Diocletian, Asclepius was burned at Caesarea in Palestine, the historian Eusebius's own city." **HELD.** E-en 47042-47057: "in the same city of Cæsarea ... ended his life on one and the same funeral pyre"; E-gr 24598: "μιᾷ καὶ τῇ αὐτῇ πυρᾷ"; the persecution "in the nineteenth year of the reign of Diocletian" (E-en 45893).
21. "He was supposed to be, Eusebius says, a bishop of the Marcionites, with zeal for religion, as he thought, but not with true knowledge." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** E-gr 24596-24597: "τῆς κατὰ Μαρκίωνα πλάνης ἐπίσκοπός τις εἶναι δοκῶν Ἀσκληπιὸς ζήλῳ μέν, ὡς ᾤετο, εὐσεβείας, ἀλλ᾽ οὔτι γε τῆς κατ᾽ ἐπίγνωσιν". Both hedges and both halves kept; "true knowledge" is a fair English for *epignōsis*.
22. "When the persecutions ended, the emperor Constantine issued an edict, a law addressed by name to the heretics, those who, their enemies said, chose error, the Marcionites among them." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** E-en 66064-66070: "Victor Constantinus ... to the heretics. Understand now ... ye Novatians, Valentinians, Marcionites"; gloss from ANF3 20274-20279. See the MINOR note on "When".
23. "Their teaching, it said, made the healthy soul sick." **HELD.** E-en 66083: "through you the healthy soul is stricken with disease".
24. "It forbade their meetings, even in private houses, and gave their houses of prayer to the catholic Church." **HELD.** E-en 66113-66146: "not in public merely, but in any private house ... all the houses of prayer ... be made over without delay to the catholic Church".
25. "Yet their meetings went on." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** Cyril, a generation later, speaks of them in the present: "the meetings of the heretics, the Marcionists and Manichees" (Cyr 20352-20354); Theodoret's villages (Thdt 31511).
26. 27. "He told them to ask in a strange city for the Catholic Church, the church over all the world." **HELD.** Cyr 20357-20359: "if ever thou art sojourning in cities, inquire ... where is the Catholic Church"; 20271-20272: "called Catholic then because it extends over all the world".
28. "In the fifth century Theodoret was bishop of Cyrrhus, in northern Syria." **HELD.** Thdt 17542 ("our district i.e. at Cyrus"), 31501 ("consecrated bishop"); century and region Widely Accepted.
29. "There John Chrysostom had once urged an earlier bishop to drive out Marcion's followers, offering the aid of the imperial edicts." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** Thdt 17542-17545: "in our district some men were infected with the plague of Marcion he wrote to the then bishop charging him to drive out the plague, and proffering him the aid of the imperial edicts."
30. 31. "He was stoned many times by the heretics he fought." **HELD.** Thdt 31511-31516: Marcionists, Eunomians, Arians, then "many a time was I stoned by them".
32. "After twenty-five years as bishop, he wrote:" + Q3. **HELD.** Thdt 31502: "Five and twenty years I so lived"; Q3 against Thdt 31511-31512: "I led eight villages of Marcionists with their neighbourhood into the way of truth." Faithful, nothing added.

## MINOR (noted, not blocking)

- Sentence 22, "When the persecutions ended": Eusebius gives the edict no date and sets it late in Constantine's reign, after Nicaea (E-en 66040-66062), some years after the Great Persecution ended, though close to the end of Licinius's persecution in the East. "When" can read as "at once". If the reviser wants it exact, word for word: "After the persecutions ended," in place of "When the persecutions ended,". Not a fault the bar blocks.
