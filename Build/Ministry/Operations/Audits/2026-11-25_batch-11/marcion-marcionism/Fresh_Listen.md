## 1. The test, for a tradition

- **Who these people were:** filled. "The Marcionites were the churches that followed Marcion after Rome's church cast him out," and they had "their own presbyters, teachers and martyrs."
- **What happened to them:** filled. They had martyrs in 250 and under Diocletian, Constantine's edict forbade their meetings and took their houses of prayer, "Yet their meetings went on," and finally Theodoret "led eight villages of Marcion's followers ... into the way of truth."
- **What their faith was about:** filled. "Jesus came from a good Father, above the God who made the world," they kept a shortened Scripture, Apelles held to one God, and they baptized only the unmarried. One gap: "law and prophets" is not explained (see 7). The listener gets the shape of the belief but not what was being thrown out.
- **Why the connection matters:** filled, though thinly. This was a rival church with its own martyrs, dying beside the others at Smyrna. Every witness is its enemy, and the larger church argued against it, outlawed it and won it back. The listener can say that much.

## 2. The opening (first three sentences)

- **Who:** Pionius and Metrodorus. Clear.
- **When:** 250. Clear.
- **Why there:** clear for Pionius ("refused to sacrifice to the gods"). Not said for Metrodorus, and nobody says who is putting them to death. "The soldier" is the only hint that Rome is doing it.
- **Where:** "the stadium at Smyrna." The listener knows it is a city with a stadium, but not what part of the world it is in. This is not enough to leave the listener lost, so it is MINOR.
- Suggested change (MINOR): "In 250, in the stadium at Smyrna, a Greek city of the Roman Empire, a presbyter, or church elder, named Pionius..." This only adds a place-gloss. The trust reader should confirm that the outline holds it.

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing

1. "Irenaeus, a bishop writing within a generation, says Marcion trimmed both books of what called the world's maker Jesus' Father."
   - Meaning lost: "of what called" does not parse when heard. The listener misses that the cut passages were the ones calling the maker of the world the Father of Jesus.
   - Change: "says Marcion cut from both books every passage that called the maker of the world the Father of Jesus."
2. "Like every witness this story draws on, he was their enemy."
   - Meaning lost: "he" could be Marcion, the last man named inside the sentence before it. A listener can hear "Marcion was their enemy."
   - Change: "Irenaeus, like every witness this story draws on, was their enemy."
3. "When the persecutions ended, the emperor Constantine issued an edict, a law addressed by name to the heretics, those who, their enemies said, chose error, the Marcionites among them."
   - Meaning lost: three clauses are stacked inside each other. The definition of "heretics" gets lost, and so does the fact that the Marcionites were named.
   - Change: "When the persecutions ended, the emperor Constantine issued an edict, a law addressed by name to the Marcionites and other heretics, those who, their enemies said, chose error."
4. "He was supposed to be, Eusebius says, a bishop of the Marcionites, with zeal for religion, as he thought, but not with true knowledge."
   - Meaning lost: "as he thought" has no clear owner, and "zeal" is hard for this listener. The listener misses that Eusebius grants his devotion but denies his faith was true.
   - Change: "He was said to be, Eusebius writes, a bishop of the Marcionites, eager for religion as he understood it, but without true knowledge."
5. "Theodoret worked to bring them to the one Son, who was, he wrote, not only good but just."
   - Meaning lost: "the one Son" means nothing without church background. "Just" never came up earlier, so the listener cannot connect it back to the good God and the maker God.
   - Change: "Theodoret worked to bring them to his own faith, that Christ was, as he wrote, not only good but just."
   - Note: the checker flagged this sentence as "not X but Y." Theodoret's attributed words "not only good but just" are not the narrator's dramatic turn that Part 1 bars, so I do not count it as a tell.
