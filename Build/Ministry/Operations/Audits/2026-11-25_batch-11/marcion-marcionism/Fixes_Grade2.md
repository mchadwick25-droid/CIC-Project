## Fix 1. Thread-losing sentence (craft only)

**Finding:** "So he pressed them on persecution." | lost why a God who punishes no one leads to a question about persecution.

**What the sources hold.** Tertullian's own link is fear: a God who does not punish is not feared, so why not deny him? ANF3 24445-24446 (Against Marcion I.27, ANF 3, 1885): "what can be more direct flattery than not to punish sins? Come, then, if you do not fear God as being good ..."; then 24452-24454: "Why in persecutions also do you not, when the censer is presented, at once redeem your life by the denial of your faith?" Latin, Kr 16282-16283: "delicta non exequi? age itaque, qui deum non times quasi bonum ..."; 16288, "quid non et in persecutionibus statim oblata acerra animam negatione lucraris?" Witness kind: contemporary, an enemy (Tertullian reporting the Marcionite view and reply). Confidence: Documented as Tertullian's report. Already held at `Source_Sheet.md` 87 and `Outline.md` 11, 67.

**Decision: reorder, not add.** The weak sentence goes. The incense test now comes straight after the good God who punishes no one, and the link is said where the quote is introduced: the reader feared no punishment, so why not deny? "Who feared no punishment" restates the brief's own "punished no one" in Tertullian's terms ("if you do not fear God"); it adds no claim.
