# Outline: The Marcionites (`marcion-marcionism`, tradition)

Lines are file lines in `cic/texts/`; abbreviations as in `Source_Sheet.md` (E-en, E-gr, VC-gr, Pion, ANF1, ANF3, Kr, Oe, ANF5, CypLat, Epi, Cyr, Ephr, Chrys, Thdt). Beat codes (E1-E8) point to the sheet.

## The heart

- **The essence:** The Marcionites were the churches that followed Marcion after the church of Rome cast him out, in the middle of the second century. For about three hundred years, from Rome to Persia and down to the villages of Syria, they gathered with their own presbyters, teachers and martyrs around one Gospel and ten letters of Paul, and around one belief: that the Father Jesus made known was a good God, other than the God who made the world. (Documented: ANF1 33572-33591; Epi 7470-7474, 8274-8282; Pion 5601-5602; E-gr 24595-24599; Thdt 31511.)
- **Who they were:** "the Marcionites" (census `informalName` "Marcion and the Marcionites"). Their enemies called them a *hairesis*, a party with its own teaching (E-gr 9663; Pion 5601-5602); they called their meetings churches and the Lord's house (Cyr 20356-20358). Ordinary members in the record: a presbyter (Pion 5601-5602), an unnamed woman (E-gr 9662-9664), a man Eusebius calls "supposed to be" a bishop (E-gr 24595-24599), whole villages (Thdt 31511). Documented.
- **When and where:** mid-second century to the fifth (ANF1 33572; Thdt 31501, 31511); Rome, Asia, Carthage, Smyrna, Caesarea in Palestine, Jerusalem, Edessa, the district of Cyrrhus (S2). Epiphanius, about 375, lists Rome and Italy, Egypt, Palestine, Arabia, Syria, Cyprus, the Thebaid and Persia (Epi 7470-7474). Documented; "the fifth century" Widely Accepted.
- **What they faced:** what made them: Rome's expulsion of Marcion (ANF3 21338-21344; the founding is `marcion-of-pontus`'s job). What pressed on them: the persecutions they shared with every Christian (Pion 5561-5563; E-gr 9662-9664; E-gr 24595-24599); their own quarrels over how many first sources there are (E-en 28946-29105); a stream of books against them; then Constantine's edict taking their meeting houses (E-en 66113-66146), imperial edicts offered against them in Syria (Thdt 17542-17545), and a bishop, Theodoret, bringing whole villages into his own church (Thdt 31511, 35164-35166). What ended them: the held record does not say; it stops with Theodoret. Documented.
- **What they believed:** Jesus came from "that father who is above the God that made the world", abolishing "the prophets and the law" (ANF1 33577-33582, Irenaeus); a good God who does not punish, the sinner only "cast away out of sight" (ANF3 24462-24464, Tertullian); for Apelles, salvation for "those who trusted in the Crucified", if found "doing good works" (E-en 29122-29123). What they fought over: one source of all things, or two, or three (E-en 28967-29105). All as their enemies report them. Documented as reported.
- **How they practiced:** baptism only for the unmarried, widowed, celibate or divorced (ANF3 24533-24537; Kr 16349-16351); washing with water, anointing with oil, honey and milk for the newly baptized, bread for Christ's body (ANF3 23412-23419; Kr 15351-15355); refusal to deny under persecution (Kr 16287-16288); long prayer and fasting (Ephr 1585; Epi 7742-7748). Documented as Tertullian's, Ephrem's and Epiphanius's reports.
- **What held them together:** Marcion's two books, one Gospel and ten letters of Paul, "the only ones he uses" (Epi 8274-8279); and faith in a good God, Father of Jesus, other than the maker of the world (ANF1 33577-33582). Documented.
- **What came of them:** writer after writer answered them for three centuries, and the mainstream church sharpened its own name against them: Cyril told the newly baptized to ask in a strange city for "the Catholic Church", since Marcionists also called their meetings churches (Cyr 20348-20359). Their martyrs were kept in the record by the other side (Pion; E-en 23679-23681; E-gr 9662-9664, 24595-24599). Nothing in the held record descends from them. Documented.

## Setting

- **Time:** the founding "in the middle of the second century" (from Justin and Irenaeus writing in and just after Marcion's life; no year held: ANF1 33572). Rhodon "at this time" in Eusebius's Book V, after 177 (E-en 28896-28897, 26707): "late in the second century". Tertullian about 207 (ANF3 898-901, Widely Accepted). Smyrna, March 250, under Decius (Pion 5655-5658; Widely Accepted for the year). Caesarea under Valerian (E-en 40459). Caesarea in the persecution that began "in the nineteenth year of the reign of Diocletian" (E-en 45893). Constantine's edict, no date in the text (E-en 66068-66150). Cyril and Ephrem: the fourth century (Widely Accepted). Theodoret: after twenty-five years as bishop (Thdt 31501), the fifth century (Widely Accepted).
- **Places:** Smyrna, a city of the province of Asia, the stadium (Pion 5584-5585, 5655-5656). Caesarea in Palestine (E-en 40459-40460). Carthage (Tertullian). Asia (Rhodon, E-en 28922). Jerusalem (Cyril). Edessa (Ephrem). Cyrrhus, Theodoret's see, with "eight hundred churches" in its district (Thdt 33165-33166); "in northern Syria" as the plainest spoken place (Widely Accepted).
- **Tradition:** spoken word "the Marcionites".

## Words to explain

- **presbyter:** an elder of a congregation; the Acts use the same word for Pionius and for Metrodorus (Pion 4807, 5601-5602).
- **Marcion's two books:** a Gospel cut down from Luke, and ten letters of Paul (ANF1 33582-33591; Epi 8274-8282).
- **the two Gods:** a good Father of Jesus, "above the God that made the world" and gave the law (ANF1 33577-33582).
- **the incense test:** an accused Christian was told to sprinkle incense from a box (*acerra*) to the gods; to do it was to deny and go free (Kr 16287-16288; Pion 5562-5563, "Many others have sacrificed and live").
- **honey and milk:** given to the newly baptized as food for newborn children (ANF3 23415-23416; Kr 15353-15354 "qua suos infantat").
- **the Catholic Church:** the name for the church "over all the world" (Cyr 20271-20273), against meetings that also called themselves churches (Cyr 20356-20359).
- **the edict:** an emperor's law, read out to the heretics by name (E-en 66068-66080).
- **Cyrrhus:** Theodoret's see in Syria, with eight hundred parish churches (Thdt 33165-33166).

The kind kit's one form, a story, in time order: Rome (the founding, short), Asia, Carthage, Smyrna, Caesarea twice, Jerusalem and Edessa, Cyrrhus. The opening at Smyrna reaches ahead; the beats then run in order.

## Openings

1. **Smyrna, 250 (recommended).** On a March day in 250, in the stadium at Smyrna, a presbyter named Pionius stretched himself out on a stake and let the soldier drive in the nails. Beside him they raised a presbyter of the Marcionites, Metrodorus. Pionius was on the right and Metrodorus on the left, the eyewitnesses wrote, but both faced the east, and the wood was heaped around them. (Pion 5584-5591, 5598-5607, 5646, 5655-5658; E-en 23679-23681. Narrator's words: the Greek print is damaged and not confirmed word for word.) Leads into the essence: the two belonged to rival churches.
2. **The teacher who believed without knowing.** Late in the second century, a writer from Asia named Rhodon pressed an old Marcionite teacher, Apelles, to say how he knew there was one God. Apelles swore that he did not know; he believed it. (E-en 29160-29199.) Reaches the essence through belief; use if Smyrna is cut.

## Story beats

1. **The founding, in short.** After Rome cast him out (ANF3 21338-21344, Tertullian; reason: his "restless curiosity" spread to others), Marcion taught that Jesus came from a Father "above the God that made the world", and gave his churches a Gospel cut down from Luke and ten letters of Paul, trimmed of what tied Jesus to the maker (ANF1 33577-33591, Irenaeus; Epi 8274-8282, Epiphanius). *Explain:* the two Gods; the two books. *Reason for the cuts, as Irenaeus gives it:* to remove what says the maker is Jesus' Father (ANF1 33583-33586, 33589-33591). Documented. No year.
2. **A generation on: the teachers divide.** Rhodon, a writer from Asia, found Marcion's followers split: Apelles held one first source, others two as Marcion did, Syneros three (E-en 28922-28928, 28967-29105). He spoke with "the old man Apelles". Q2. Pressed for how he knew there was one God, Apelles swore he did not know, but believed it; Rhodon laughed at a teacher who could not prove what he taught (E-en 29160-29199). *Explain:* a first source (Greek *archē*), where all things come from (E-en 29002, 29073-29074). *Reason:* Rhodon gives Apelles' own reason, that the prophecies contradict themselves (29161-29191). Documented (Rhodon's account, in Eusebius).
3. **Carthage, about 207: how they lived.** Tertullian, writing five books against them, reports that their churches baptized only virgins, widows, the unmarried and the divorced (ANF3 24533-24537; Kr 16349-16351); that they washed with water, anointed with oil, gave honey and milk to the newly baptized and used bread for Christ's body, all things of the maker's world (ANF3 23412-23419; Kr 15351-15355). Their God was good and judged no one, so he asked why they did not deny under persecution. Q1. A writer of the same years granted that the Marcionites claimed "a multitude of martyrs for Christ" (E-en 29926-29930). *Explain:* the incense test; honey and milk. *Reason for the marriage rule:* Tertullian says it condemns marriage (Kr 16352-16353); the reason from their side, as Clement reports it, was not to fill the maker's world (card). Documented as Tertullian's report.
4. **Smyrna and Caesarea: their martyrs.** At Smyrna the wood was heaped around Pionius and Metrodorus (Pion 5607-5614; the scene opened the story). At Caesarea in Palestine, under Valerian, after three men were given to the beasts, a woman met the same end; Eusebius says she was "reported" to be a Marcionite (E-gr 9662-9664; E-en 40459-40474). In the persecution that began under Diocletian, in his own city, Eusebius records an ascetic, Peter, burned, and with him Asclepius, "supposed to be" a Marcionite bishop, with zeal "as he thought" for God, who ended his life "on one and the same pyre" (E-gr 24595-24599; E-en 47046-47059). *Reason for their deaths:* they would not sacrifice (Pion 5561-5563; E-en 47049-47052 for Peter). Documented; both hedges are Eusebius's.
5. **After the persecutions: the edict, and churches still standing.** Constantine wrote to the "Novatians, Valentinians, Marcionites" and others, forbidding their meetings, even in private houses, and gave their houses of prayer to the catholic Church; some, Eusebius says, frightened by the threats, crept into the Church hiding what they thought (E-en 66071-66080, 66113-66146, 66161-66164; VC-gr 13038). *Reason, the edict's own:* their teaching made "the healthy soul" sick (E-en 66083). Yet their meetings went on: at Jerusalem, Cyril told those about to be baptized that in a strange city they should not ask for the church, since Marcionists also called their meetings churches, but for the Catholic Church (Cyr 20348-20359). In Edessa, Ephrem heard them say they prayed the whole day (Ephr 1585). *Explain:* the edict; the Catholic Church. Documented.
6. **Cyrrhus, the fifth century: the last villages.** In Theodoret's district, John Chrysostom had urged the bishop to drive out Marcion's followers, offering "the aid of the imperial edicts" (Thdt 17542-17545). Theodoret, bishop there for twenty-five years, wrote: Q3. Elsewhere he says he brought more than ten thousand of them to baptism (Thdt 35164-35166), and that he was stoned many times by the heretics he fought (Thdt 31514-31516). *Reason:* to bring them to the one Son, "not only good but just" (Thdt 35009-35010). Documented as Theodoret's own claims.

## What came of it (heaviest first)

1. **The mainstream church named itself against them.** Writer after writer answered them, from Rhodon to Theodoret (E-en 28925-28926, 26633-26637; Cyr 17970-17971; Ephr 1585; Thdt 35004-35010), and Cyril taught the newly baptized to ask for "the Catholic Church" because Marcionists also called theirs churches (Cyr 20348-20359). *Defended:* one God, maker and Father; the church "over all the world". In the story through Cyril (beat 5).
2. **Their baptism became a test case.** A letter Cyprian answered held that those who came from Marcion need not be baptized again; Cyprian said they must be (ANF5 38511-38550; CypLat 14584-14586). *Defended:* one baptism in the one church. Card.
3. **Their martyrs stayed in the other side's record** (beats 4 and opening). Documented.
4. **The empire turned on them** (beats 5-6). Documented.
- Who keeps it today: no held source; leave out.

## Connection to carry into the story

What passed was a rival church with its own Scripture, and the mainstream church named itself against it. A listener must understand that the Marcionites were congregations, with presbyters, a man called a bishop, rites and martyrs, living beside the other churches and dying in the same fires (Pion 5598-5604; E-gr 24595-24599). And that because their meetings were also called churches, Cyril told the newly baptized to ask for the Catholic Church (Cyr 20348-20359, where Cyril gives this reason himself), and at last Theodoret's bringing their villages into his church (Thdt 31511-31512, 35164-35166). It comes through what Cyril told the newly baptized and what Theodoret did, never a sentence saying why it matters.

## Do not say

- "Metrodorus appears to have been a Marcionite" as the only wording, or "Metrodorus was crucified" → the Acts call him "a presbyter of the sect of the Marcionites"; Pionius was nailed to a stake and both were burned. Eusebius's hedge goes to the card.
- "They died side by side as brothers" / "in communion" → the record says only right and left, both facing east. Add nothing on what they felt or shared.
- "The church's martyrs refused to die beside the Marcionites" → Eusebius V.16.22 says that of the Montanists' martyrs, not the Marcionites'.
- "Asclepius, a Marcionite bishop" alone → "supposed to be" a bishop, with zeal "as he thought": Eusebius's own words.
- "a Marcionite woman" as fact → "reported" to be one.
- "Theodoret was stoned by the Marcionites" → he was stoned by the heretics he fought, Marcionites, Eunomians and Arians together.
- "Theodoret converted them by force" → he says he led them; the force in the record is the edicts Chrysostom offered and Constantine's law. Tell those as they stand.
- "The Marcionites died out in the fifth century" / "disappeared" → the held record stops with Theodoret; no source tells their end.
- "Constantine's edict ended them" → a century later Theodoret still found whole villages.
- "used water instead of wine" → Tertullian names water, oil, honey and milk, and bread; Epiphanius says water was used "in the mysteries". Neither says "instead of wine". Leave out.
- "women priests" / "women led their churches" → Epiphanius, an enemy, says Marcion gave women leave to baptize. Card, in his voice.
- "Marcionites baptized people three times" as fact → Epiphanius "heard from many"; card only.
- "rejected the Old Testament" without its sense → say what the record gives: they kept a Gospel and ten letters, and held the God of the law and the prophets was not Jesus' Father.
- "Marcion's churches had bishops from the start" → the only held Marcionite bishop is Asclepius, "supposed to be" one, in the persecution that began under Diocletian.
- "Apelles founded his own sect" / "left Marcion" as fact → Rhodon counts him in Marcion's school; Tertullian says he stopped being a Marcionite. Card.
- Any year for Marcion's break with Rome (144 or another) → no held old source fixes it.
- "Rhodon wrote in 180" → "late in the second century".
- "northern Syria" is fine for Cyrrhus; do not add "near Antioch" or a modern country.
- "eight villages" and "more than ten thousand" are Theodoret's own claims; do not merge them.
- Any barred quote: Justin's "There is a certain Marcion, from Pontus…", Tertullian's "Separating the law from the gospel…", Polycarp's "firstborn of Satan", Tertullian's "By what right, Marcion, do you cut my timber?", Justin's "in every race of people", Tertullian's "the defenders of Judaism", and every other quote in `marcion-of-pontus`, `tertullian-s-voice`, `polycarp-of-smyrna`, `irenaeus-of-lyon`, `justin-martyr`.
