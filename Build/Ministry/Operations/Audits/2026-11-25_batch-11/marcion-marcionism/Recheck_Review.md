## What holds

- **B10, heretics.** ANF3 20274-20279 gives "called in Greek *heresies*, a word used in the sense of that *choice* which a man makes when he either teaches them ... or takes up with them". Oe 461 gives "haereses dictae Graeca voce, ex interpretatione electio[nis]". E-en 66079-66080 gives "devise and support heresies by means of your private assemblies". VC-gr 13039-13040 gives the same in Greek. The gloss "those who, their enemies said, chose error" is 7 words and says no more than Tertullian: the false teachings are the error, and the word means a choice. Documented as the opponents' word. The cites are right.
- **B11, the first sources.** Apelles "spoke of one principle, as also our doctrine does" (E-en 29154-29155). He is asked how he can "assert that there is one principle" (29161), and answers about "one unbegotten God" (29197). Marcion's followers "like him ... asserted two principles" (29093-29095). Potitus and Basilicus "hold to two principles, as does the mariner Marcion himself" (29073-29085). The gloss "one God, where Marcion held two" holds. That Marcion's two are Gods comes from Irenaeus (ANF1 33577-33582). That join is rated Widely Accepted, which the kit allows in a story line. McGiffert's note 1543 is rightly set aside: it runs 29002-29029, with "an angel" and "Satan" in it.
- **B4 and S4, the Lebaba inscription.** Harnack 1906 holds it as stated. The frame and date "318/19 (630 aer. Seleuc.)" are at 5606-5609. The Le Bas-Waddington note "III 1870 nr. 2558" is at 5633. The damaged Greek is at 5639-5641, and the inference "eine marcionitische Gemeinde" at 5643-5645. Leaving it out is right: the only print is modern and damaged. "Every witness this story draws on was their enemy" holds.
- **B6, the zeal.** E-gr 24596-24597 gives "ζήλῳ μέν, ὡς ᾤετο, εὐσεβείας, ἀλλ᾽ οὔτι γε τῆς κατ᾽ ἐπίγνωσιν". E-en 47056-47057 gives "zeal for religion, but 'not according to knowledge'". The Romans 10:2 note is right. Both halves are kept.
- **B7, Words to explain.** The edict is addressed by name at E-en 66068-66080 and VC-gr 13035-13038. "Let this edict be made public" is at 66149. The correction from 66146 is right.
- **B9, the curiosity.** The line "ob inquietam semper curiositatem, qua fratres quoque vitiabant" is at Oe 1775-1776. "Stoicae studiosus" is at Oe 1722, and the English at ANF3 21331-21332. Chapter 7 is at ANF3 20327-20328 and 20380-20381, and Oe 685-686. Joining the two chapters is rightly rated Inferential-Thin and kept out of the story.
- **Beats.** They run once and in order: Rhodon, late in the second century; Tertullian, about 207; Asclepius, under Diocletian; the edict; Cyril; Theodoret. The opening at Smyrna reaches ahead in time, as the Shape section allows. B9's "About 207, Tertullian reported" is right, because Carthage is now in the essence.
- **Length.** I rebuilt the live brief (`marcion-marcionism.brief.txt`, 674 words) with every `Review.md` change, the new opening, B10 at 7 words and B11 at 6. It comes to **643 words** (`wc -w`), inside 675. The fixer's figure of about 640 stands, and no cut is needed.

## The heart

My count, using the same assembly:

- The opening is 64 words.
- The kind kit's three essence fields close at **word 153**. These are who (the expulsion), when and where (three hundred years, from Rome to Persia), and what held them together (the two books and the one belief).
- Tertullian's reason and Irenaeus's trimming follow, and close at **word 191**.
- The witness sentence closes at word 202.

**Judgment: this meets the test.** Kit Part 1 asks for the essence "as its kind kit defines it" within the first minute, which is about 150 words. The tradition kit defines the essence as exactly these three fields. They land at word 153, about one minute in. Tertullian's reason and Irenaeus's trimming are not among the three fields. They are the approved heart's own explanations, and they follow at once.

I agree that the full approved essence cannot fit inside 150 words. As worded, it runs about 107 words, and the brief's version runs about 127. The opening would have to drop to about 25-45 words. That would cut "stretched himself", Pionius's reason or the eyewitness credit, which is the scene the kit protects.

I considered keeping Tertullian's reason next to the expulsion ("Tertullian, a writer at Carthage, blames his restless curiosity, which spread to others", 13 words). That keeps the time order inside the essence, but it pushes the three fields to word 166. The fixer's order is the better trade. The sentence "says Marcion was cast out for" gives a reason, not a second telling of the event.

The change of order, and "Rome's church" in the plan, are listed for Mark in `Fixes_Review.md`. The wording under "The heart" is not touched. No change is needed.

Replace:
