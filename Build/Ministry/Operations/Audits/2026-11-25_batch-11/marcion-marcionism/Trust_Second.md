## Non-HELD sentences

**S2. "A presbyter was an elder of a congregation, and Pionius had refused to sacrifice to the gods, saying he feared the living God."** SUMMARY HOLDS. Pion 5559-5562: "Θύεις; ἀπεκρίνατο· Οὔ" and, to "why are you mad?", "ζῶντα θεὸν φοβοῦμαι". The fear is his answer to the charge of madness, which comes straight after the refusal. That is a fair compression.

**S5. "The two men belonged to rival churches."** SUMMARY HOLDS. Pion 4807 calls Pionius's fellow prisoner Limnus "πρεσβύτερος τῆς καθολικῆς ἐκκλησίας". Pion 5601-5602 calls Metrodorus a presbyter "τῆς αἱρέσεως τῶν Μαρκιωνιστῶν". Cyr 20356-20358 shows that Marcionite meetings were also called churches.

**S8. "For about three hundred years, from Rome to Persia and down to the villages of Syria, they gathered with their own presbyters, teachers and martyrs."** SUMMARY HOLDS. The sources: ANF3 21333 ("in the reign of Antoninus"), Epi 7470-7472 ("Ῥώμῃ … καὶ ἐν τῇ Περσίδι") and Thdt 31511 ("eight villages of Marcionists"). Presbyters: Pion 5601. Teachers: E-en ("calling himself a teacher", Rhodon on Apelles). Martyrs: E-gr 24595-24599.

**S10. "Irenaeus … says Marcion trimmed both books of whatever made the world's maker Jesus' Father."** SOFTENED OR CHANGED (overstated). ANF1 33584-33586 says that for the Gospel he set aside "a great deal of the teaching of the Lord, in which the Lord is recorded as most clearly confessing that the Maker of this universe is His Father". Only for Paul does Irenaeus say "removing all that is said … that He is the Father" (33589-33591). "Whatever" claims a completeness for the Gospel that Irenaeus does not give. **Change:** "of whatever made" → "of what made".

**S11. "Like every witness to them, he was their enemy, for no line of theirs survives in their own hand."** NOT FOUND. I searched the Source_Sheet witnesses and the outline. No old source makes this claim. It is a judgment about the whole surviving record, and it rests only on what the Library holds. The Source_Sheet itself lists a Marcionite text in their own hand that it does not hold: the Lebaba inscription, a Marcionite "synagogue" dedication by a presbyter, Paul, dated 318/19. A reader who knows the period would catch this. **Change:** "Like every witness this story draws on, he was their enemy." **Do not say (add to outline):** "no line of theirs survives in their own hand" or "every witness to them was an enemy" → "every witness this story draws on was their enemy". The Lebaba inscription survives, though it is not held.

**S12. "They held one belief above all: Jesus came from a good Father, above the God who made the world and gave the law and the prophets, which Jesus set aside."** SUMMARY HOLDS. ANF1 33577-33582: "Jesus being derived from that father who is above the God that made the world … abolishing the prophets and the law". 33567-33569 calls the unknown Father "benevolent", the teaching Marcion "developed".

**S20. "So Tertullian pressed them on persecution."** SUMMARY HOLDS. Kr 16261-16262 / Oe 4952-4953: "qui deum non times quasi bonum … quid non et in persecutionibus …". The question follows from their unpunishing God.

**S21. "An accused Christian was told to sprinkle incense from a box to the gods, and whoever did it denied Christ and went free."** SUMMARY HOLDS. Kr 16266-16267: "statim oblata acerra animam negatione lucraris". Pion 5562-5563: "ἄλλοι πολλοὶ ἔθυσαν καὶ ζῶσι".

**S24. "… the historian Eusebius records that Asclepius was burned in his own city, Caesarea in Palestine."** ASSUMPTION ADDED, as a misleading implication. In "Asclepius was burned in his own city", "his" binds to Asclepius, so the sentence gives him a home city. E-en 47046-47059 says only "in the same city of Cæsarea … ended his life on one and the same funeral pyre". It gives no home city for Asclepius. Caesarea was Eusebius's own city (Widely Accepted). **Change:** "In the persecution that began under the emperor Diocletian, Asclepius was burned at Caesarea in Palestine, the historian Eusebius's own city."

**S25. "He was supposed to be, Eusebius says, a bishop of the Marcionites, with zeal for God, as he thought."** SUMMARY HOLDS. E-gr 24597-24599: "τῆς κατὰ Μαρκίωνα πλάνης ἐπίσκοπός τις εἶναι δοκῶν Ἀσκληπιὸς ζήλῳ μέν, ὡς ᾤετο, εὐσεβείας, ἀλλ᾽ οὔτι γε τῆς κατ᾽ ἐπίγνωσιν". Both hedges are kept. "As he thought" carries the force of the dropped "not according to knowledge".

**S26. "When the persecutions ended, the emperor Constantine issued an edict, a law read out to the heretics by name, the Marcionites among them."** SOFTENED OR CHANGED (a detail changed). E-en 66068-66080 has "to the heretics … ye Novatians, Valentinians, Marcionites" (VC-gr 13034-13038: "αἱρετικοῖς … Μαρκιωνισταί") and ends "Let this edict be made public". It is addressed to them by name, and no source says it was read out. **Change:** "read out to" → "addressed to". **Do not say (add to outline; its gloss left room):** "read out to the heretics" → "addressed to the heretics by name".

**S29. "Yet their meetings went on."** SUMMARY HOLDS. Cyr 20355-20358 speaks of present "meetings of the heretics, the Marcionists".

**S34. "He worked to bring them to the one Son, who was, he wrote, not only good but just."** SOFTENED OR CHANGED, as a misleading attribution. The sentence before has John Chrysostom as its subject, so "He" is heard as Chrysostom. The source is Theodoret about himself, Thdt 35004-35010: "I have never ceased to struggle … Marcion's mad adherents that He is not only good but just … worship the one Son". **Change:** "He worked" → "Theodoret worked". Once this is fixed, S35 ("He was stoned many times", Thdt 31514-31516: "many a time was I stoned by them") holds.
