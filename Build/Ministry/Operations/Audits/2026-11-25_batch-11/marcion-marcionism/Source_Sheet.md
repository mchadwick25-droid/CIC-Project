Witness kinds: **own words** (the community or its own teachers), **contemporary** (at or near the time), **later (gap)**, **late legend**. Every witness to the Marcionites below is an opponent unless marked. One line written by Marcionites is held, but only in a modern work's damaged print: the Lebaba inscription of 318/19 (S4). Every other word of theirs in the Library comes only as their enemies report them.

## 1. Setting

**S1. The founding, as the old sources give it (short essence only; the founding is the `marcion-of-pontus` story's job).**
- Irenaeus, AH I.27.2, ANF1 33572-33594. Contemporary (c. 180, within living memory of Marcion). Documented as Irenaeus's account.
  - 33572-33573: "Marcion of Pontus succeeded him, and developed his doctrine."
  - 33577-33582: "But Jesus being derived from that father who is above the God that made the world ... was manifested in the form of a man to those who were in Judæa, abolishing the prophets and the law, and all the works of that God who made the world".
  - 33582-33584: "he mutilates the Gospel which is according to Luke, removing all that is written respecting the generation of the Lord".
  - 33589-33591: "In like manner, too, he dismembered the Epistles of Paul, removing all that is said by the apostle respecting that God who made the world".
- Epiphanius, Panarion 42.9.1-4, Epi 8272-8282. Later (c. 375; about 220 years after Marcion). Documented as his account.
  - 8274-8279: "μόνῳ δὲ κέχρηται τούτῳ τῷ χαρακτῆρι, τῷ κατὰ Λουκᾶν εὐαγγελίῳ. ἔχει δὲ καὶ ἐπιστολὰς παρ᾽ αὐτῷ τοῦ ἁγίου ἀποστόλου δέκα, αἷς μόναις κέχρηται ... ταύταις δὲ ταῖς δυσὶ βίβλοις κέχρηται" (OCR: "Πουχᾶν" for Λουκᾶν). Plain sense: he uses only this form, the Gospel according to Luke; he has ten letters of the apostle, the only ones he uses; he uses these two books.
  - 8279-8282: the ten listed: Galatians, 1 and 2 Corinthians, Romans, 1 and 2 Thessalonians, Ephesians, Colossians, Philemon, Philippians, with parts of one "to the Laodiceans".
- The break with Rome: Tertullian, Prescription 30, ANF3 21331-21348 (contemporary-later, c. 200; about 50 years). "more than once expelled ... when banished at last to a permanent excommunication, they scattered abroad the poisons of their doctrines" (21338-21344). Reason given: "on account of their ever restless curiosity, with which they even infected the brethren" (21338-21339). Documented as Tertullian's account. Its quotes and details sit in the `marcion-of-pontus` story; here a clause only.
  - Latin, Oehler 1853, Oe 1775-1776: "donec ob inquietam semper curiositatem, qua fratres quoque vitiabant, semel et iterum eiecti" (OCR "ioqiiieUni", "curio-r sitaton", "Titiabant", "itenim"). Clean enough to confirm the English.
  - What the curiosity was about (Review B9, 2026-11-25): chapter 30 names no object. The same chapter calls Marcion "that shipmaster of Pontus, the zealous student of Stoicism" (ANF3 21331-21332; Oe 1722, "Ponticus nauclerus, Stoicae studiosus"). Chapter 7 of the same book: "We want no curious disputation after possessing Christ Jesus, no inquisition after enjoying the gospel!" (ANF3 20380-20381; Oe 685-686, "Nobis curiositate opus non est post Christum Iesum, nec inquisitione post evangelium"), and "From the same source [philosophy] came Marcion's better god, with all his tranquillity; he came of the Stoics" (ANF3 20327-20328). Contemporary-later, an opponent. Documented that Tertullian says each; that chapter 30's curiosity means chapter 7's is our joining of two chapters: Inferential-Thin. The story names no object.
- Confidence: Documented (several witnesses agree on two Gods, the cut Luke and Paul's letters). The year of the break is not fixed in the held old sources: leave it out.

**S2. Where they were.**
- Epiphanius, Panarion 42.1.2, Epi 7470-7474, later (c. 375): "ἡ δὲ αἵρεσις ἔτι καὶ νῦν ἔν τε Ῥώμῃ καὶ ἐν τῇ Ἰταλίᾳ, ἐν Αἰγύπτῳ τε καὶ ἐν Παλαιστίνῃ, ἐν Ἀραβίᾳ τε καὶ ἐν τῇ Συρίᾳ, ἐν Κύπρῳ τε καὶ Θηβαΐδι, οὐ μὴν ἀλλὰ καὶ ἐν τῇ Περσίδι καὶ ἐν ἄλλοις τόποις εὑρίσκεται." Plain sense: the sect is found even now in Rome and Italy, Egypt and Palestine, Arabia and Syria, Cyprus and the Thebaid, and in Persia too. Documented as his report. (The `marcion-of-pontus` story already paraphrases this list: a clause at most here.)
- Places named in the record of the community: Rome (S1), Asia (Rhodon, E-en 28922), Carthage (Tertullian), Smyrna (Pion; E-en 23679-23681), Caesarea in Palestine (E-gr 9662-9664; E-gr 24595-24599), Jerusalem (Cyr), Edessa (Ephr), the district of Cyrrhus in Syria (Thdt 31511, 17542-17545).

**S3. When.** Mid-second century (Justin and Irenaeus write against Marcion's followers in his lifetime and just after: ANF1 33572; the founding's dates belong to `marcion-of-pontus`) to the fifth century (Theodoret's letters, after "Five and twenty years" as bishop, Thdt 31501; "Six and twenty years", Thdt 33158). Documented that witnesses run across that span; Widely Accepted for "the fifth century" as Theodoret's time.

**S4. A line in their own hand: the Lebaba inscription (Review B4, 2026-11-25).**
- Harnack, *Die Mission und Ausbreitung des Christentums*, vol. 2 (2nd ed., 1906), `harnack_mission-und-ausbreitung-des-christentums-band2-deu_2e-aufl1906.txt` 5606-5641, printing the inscription from Le Bas and Waddington, *Inscriptions grecques et latines* III (1870), no. 2558 (his note, 5633). Harnack's frame (5606-5609): an inscription "auf das J. 318/19 (630 aer. Seleuc.) datierten", found "Zu Deir Ali (Lebaba), etwa drei Meilen südlich von Damascus". The text as held (OCR, damaged; 5639-5641): "Svvayoiyi] Magyucoviorcov xwjufijg) Aeßaßcov tov x(vqio)v xai oco(xr])Q(og) Irj(oov) Xqtjotov TiQOvoia(i) IlavXov JioeoßfvTSQOv) — tov 1% erovg." Read as Greek: Συναγωγὴ Μαρκιωνιστῶν κώμ(ης) Λεβάβων τοῦ κ(υρίο)υ καὶ σω(τῆ)ρ(ος) Ἰη(σοῦ) Χριστοῦ προνοίᾳ Παύλου πρεσβ(υτέρου) — τοῦ λχʹ ἔτους. Plain sense: "The meeting house of the Marcionites of the village of Lebaba, of the Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ, by the forethought of Paul, presbyter, in the year 630" (of the Seleucid era: 318/19). Harnack's inference (5643-5645): "Es gab also im J. 318/19 bei Damascus eine marcionitische Gemeinde."
- Witness kind: their own words, contemporary (an inscription of 318/19). Confidence: Documented that the inscription was published and reads "Μαρκιωνιστῶν"; the Library holds it only in a modern work's print, with the Greek damaged by OCR and no second held print, and the reading above is reconstructed from that damage. Under Part 1's modern-work rule it stays out of the story and the card's claims: Leave out. It does show that "no line of theirs survives in their own hand" is wrong.

## 2. Events

**E1. A generation on: the teachers divide (Rhodon on Apelles).**
- Eusebius, Church History V.13, E-en 28893-29236, quoting Rhodon. Rhodon is contemporary with Apelles (he spoke with him); Eusebius later (c. 310-325; about 130 years). Eusebius places Rhodon "At this time" (28896-28897) in Book V, which opens in the seventeenth year of Marcus Aurelius (177; editor's note at E-en 26707 converts Eusebius's year). Greek of Book V not held.
  - 28922-28928: "a native of Asia, who had been instructed, as he himself states, by Tatian ... published among the rest one against the heresy of Marcion. He says that this heresy was divided in his time into various opinions".
  - 28946-28947: "Therefore also they disagree among themselves, maintaining an inconsistent opinion."
  - 28967-29068: "For Apelles, one of the herd, priding himself on his manner of life and his age, acknowledges one principle, but says that the prophecies are from an opposing spirit, being led to this view by the responses of a maiden by name Philumene, who was possessed by a demon."
  - 29070-29086: "But others, among whom are Potitus and Basilicus, hold to two principles, as does the mariner Marcion himself."
  - 29096-29105: "Others ... consider that there are not only two, but three natures. Of these, Syneros is the leader and chief, as those who defend his teaching say."
  - 29111-29123 (Q2): "For the old man Apelles, when conversing with us, was refuted in many things which he spoke falsely; whence also he said that it was not at all necessary to examine one's doctrine, but that each one should continue to hold what he believed. For he asserted that those who trusted in the Crucified would be saved, if only they were found doing good works."
  - 29153-29155: "his opinion concerning God was the most obscure of all. For he spoke of one principle, as also our doctrine does."
  - 29160-29163, 29190-29199: "When I said to him, Tell me how you know this or how can you assert that there is one principle, he replied that the prophecies refuted themselves, because they have said nothing true; for they are inconsistent, and false, and self-contradictory. But how there is one principle he said that he did not know, but that he was thus persuaded. As I then adjured him to speak the truth, he swore that he did so when he said that he did not know how there is one unbegotten God, but that he believed it. Thereupon I laughed and reproved him because, though calling himself a teacher, he knew not how to confirm what he taught."
  - 29233-29236: "this Apelles wrote many things, in an impious manner, of the law of Moses".
- Confidence: Documented (Rhodon's own account of a conversation, kept by Eusebius). Philumene "possessed by a demon" is Rhodon's charge: keep in his voice or leave out.
- Where sources differ: Tertullian, Prescription 30, ANF3 21358-21369: Apelles "forsook the continence of Marcion, by resorting to the company of a woman, and withdrew to Alexandria ... Returning therefrom, after some years, unimproved, except that he was no longer a Marcionite, he clave to another woman, the maiden Philumene". Tertullian makes Apelles leave the Marcionites; Rhodon counts him among Marcion's divided school. Contested which; card.
- Explain it, each side: *principle* (Greek *archē*) here means a first source of all things; Marcion "two", Apelles "one" (29002, 29070-29086). (Corrected 2026-11-25: line 29002 holds Rhodon's "acknowledges one principle" and then the start of McGiffert's note 1543, "This means that Apelles accepted only one God"; "a good God and the maker" as Marcion's two rests on Irenaeus, ANF1 33577-33582, not on Rhodon.)
- What was at stake (Review B11, 2026-11-25), in Rhodon's own words: the number of first sources is the number of Gods. Apelles's "opinion concerning God ... spoke of one principle, as also our doctrine does" (29153-29155); asked "how can you assert that there is one principle" (29161), he "did not know how there is one unbegotten God, but ... believed it" (29196-29197). Marcion's followers "like him ... asserted two principles" (29093-29095), "as does the mariner Marcion himself" (29070-29086). Documented (Rhodon). That Marcion's two are the two Gods of S1 joins Rhodon to Irenaeus: Widely Accepted. McGiffert's note 1543 (29002-29029: the maker "an angel", a third "Satan") is a modern editor's claim: not used. Apelles' own side: he held that faith in the Crucified and good works save, and that teaching need not be examined (29119-29123). Rhodon's side: a teacher who cannot prove his teaching is laughable (29198-29199).
- Also of the same years: Hippolytus, Refutation VII.31, ANF5 12054-12060: "in our times, a certain follower of Marcion, (namely) Prepon, an Assyrian, has endeavoured to introduce something more novel, and has given an account of his heresy in a work inscribed to Bardesanes, an Armenian". Contemporary (early third century). Documented. Card (link to `bardaisanite-current`).

**E2. The martyrs they claimed (the anonymous writer against the Montanists).**
- Eusebius, Church History V.16.21, E-en 29926-29930, quoting an anonymous writer of the late second century (contemporary; Eusebius later). "For some of the heresies have a great many martyrs; but surely we shall not on that account agree with them or confess that they hold the truth. And first, indeed, those called Marcionites, from the heresy of Marcion, say that they have a multitude of martyrs for Christ; yet they do not confess Christ himself in truth." Documented as the writer's words.
- Note: V.16.22 (E-en 29935-) goes on to say the church's martyrs separated from the martyrs of the *Phrygian* (Montanist) party. It does not say this of the Marcionites. See Do not say.
- Sibling note: the Montanism story (this batch) will likely use V.16; this story narrates V.16.21 and does not quote it.

**E3. How they lived, at Carthage (Tertullian, c. 207).**
- Tertullian, Against Marcion, contemporary (he wrote the work "in the fifteenth year" of Severus by his own count, I.15, per the ANF note at ANF3 898-901; Widely Accepted c. 207-208). Enemy witness, and the fullest.
- I.14, ANF3 23412-23419; Latin Kr 15351-15355. "Indeed, up to the present time, he has not disdained the water which the Creator made wherewith he washes his people; nor the oil with which he anoints them; nor that union of honey and milk wherewithal he gives them the nourishment of children; nor the bread by which he represents his own proper body". Kr: "sed ille quidem usque nunc nec aquam reprobauit creatoris, qua suos abluit, nec oleum, quo suos ungit, nec mellis et lactis societatem, qua suos infantat, nec panem, quo ipsum corpus suurn [suum] repraesentat". Oe 4013 confirms "mellis et lactis societatem qua suos infantat". Documented (Tertullian says it of present Marcionite practice, "usque nunc").
  - Explain it: Tertullian's point is that a God who scorns the Creator's world still uses the Creator's water, oil, honey, milk and bread in his rites. Their side: no Marcionite account of the rites survives. The honey and milk: "gives them the nourishment of children" (*infantat*, makes them infants), given to the newly washed.
- I.29, ANF3 24533-24537; Kr 16349-16351: "The flesh is not, according to Marcion, immersed in the water of the sacrament, unless it be in virginity, widowhood, or celibacy, or has purchased by divorce a title to baptism". Kr: "non tingitur apud illum caro, nisi uirgo, nisi uidua, nisi caeleps, nisi diuortio baptisma mercata". Documented as Tertullian's report; Tertullian infers "the proscription of marriage" (24541-24542).
  - Explain it, each side: Tertullian's reading, the condemnation of marriage (Kr 16352-16353 "sine dubio ex damnatione coniugii institutio ista constabit"). The reason from their side as the opponents give it: Clement of Alexandria, Stromata III.12.1, quoted in Holl's apparatus (Epi 7713-7715): "οἱ μὲν ἀπὸ Μαρκίωνος ... μὴ βουλόμενοι τὸν κόσμον τὸν ὑπὸ τοῦ δημιουργοῦ γενόμενον συμπληροῦν ἀπέχεσθαι γάμου βούλονται" (the followers of Marcion, not wishing to fill up the world the maker made, will abstain from marriage). Clement's Greek is not held as a text; this is an editor's quotation of it, so card-level only. The Latin version in ANF2 (`anf02_...xml` 35335-35362) gives the same in Latin. Hippolytus, Refutation X.19.4 (quoted in Holl's apparatus, Epi 7710-7712) says Marcion calls marriage corruption, thinking to grieve the maker by abstaining.
- I.27, ANF3 24446-24455; Kr 16283-16288; Oe 4952-4958 (Q1). Context: Marcion's God is good and does not punish; the sinner is only "cast away" (ANF3 24462-24464; "cast away out of sight", 24464). Tertullian: "if you do not fear God as being good, why do you not boil over into every kind of lust ... Why in persecutions also do you not, when the censer is presented, at once redeem your life by the denial of your faith? God forbid, you say with redoubled emphasis." Kr 16287-16288: "quid non et in persecutionibus statira [statim] oblata acerra animam negatione lucraris? absit, inquis, absit!" Oe 4956-4958: "Quid non et in persecationibus statim oblata acerra animam negatione lucraris? Absit, inquis, absit." Two prints agree; Kroymann's "statira" is OCR for "statim", as Oehler reads. Documented: Tertullian reports the Marcionite's reply.
  - Explain it: the *acerra* is the box of incense held out to an accused Christian; to sprinkle incense to the gods or the emperor's image was to deny (ANF editor's note 24455-24457 clarifies the word; the old record of the test: Pion 5562-5563, the proconsul: "Many others have sacrificed and live"). The Marcionite side: they did not fear their God as a judge, yet would not deny him.
- IV.5, ANF3 29323-29330: "It too, of course, has its churches ... Even wasps make combs; so also these Marcionites make churches." Documented; already told in the `marcion-of-pontus` story (narrator's words): leave out here.
- Confidence: Documented as Tertullian's report.

**E4. Smyrna, 250: Metrodorus, a Marcionite presbyter, burned beside Pionius.**
- Martyrdom of Pionius 21-23, Pion 5584-5662. Contemporary: written by those present, "εἴδομεν οἱ παραγενόμενοι" ("we who were present saw", 5646). Greek, one held print with OCR damage.
  - 5590-5591: Pionius "ἥπλωσεν ἑαυτὸν ἐπὶ τοῦ ξύλου καὶ παρέδωκε τῷ στρατιώτῃ πεῖραι τοὺς ἥλους" (OCR "xoi", "παρέδωχε", "πεῖραν"). Plain sense: he stretched himself out on the wood and gave the soldier leave to drive in the nails.
  - 5594-5595: "Διὰ τοῦτο σπεύδω ἵνα θᾶττον ἐγερθῶ" (OCR "σπείδω"). Plain sense: "I hurry for this reason, so that I may rise sooner."
  - 5598-5604: "ἀνώρθωσαν οὖν αὐτὸν ἐπὶ τοῦ ξύλου, καὶ λοιπὸν [OCR 'Aowrov'] ... μετὰ ταῦτα καὶ πρεσβύτερόν τινα Μητρόδωρον τῆς αἱρέσεως τῶν Μαρκιωνιστῶν [OCR 'ΜΜαρχιωνιστῶν']. ἔτυχεν δὲ τὸν μὲν Πιόνιον ἐκ δεξιῶν, τὸν δὲ Μητρόδωρον ἐξ ἀριστερῶν, πλὴν ἀμφότεροι ἔβλεπον πρὸς ἀνατολάς." Plain sense: they set him upright on the wood, and after this also a certain presbyter, Metrodorus, of the sect of the Marcionites. It happened that Pionius was on the right and Metrodorus on the left, but both faced the east.
  - 5607-5614: they heaped up the wood around them; Pionius closed his eyes, so that the crowd thought he had died.
  - 5655-5658: the date: under the proconsul of Asia Julius Proculus Quintilianus, in the consulship of the emperor Decius for the second time and Vettius Gratus, four days before the Ides of March. Plain year 250 (editor's note E-en 23650-23660 gives 250 under Decius; Widely Accepted).
- Eusebius, Church History IV.15.46, E-en 23677-23681 (later, about 75 years; Greek of Book IV not held): "Among them also Metrodorus, who appears to have been a proselyte of the Marcionitic sect, suffered death by fire." Eusebius wrongly sets these deaths in Polycarp's time; his editor notes the Acts date them under Decius (E-en 23650-23660, modern note: clarifies).
- Where sources differ: the Acts call Metrodorus plainly "a presbyter of the sect of the Marcionites"; Eusebius hedges, "appears to have been". Tell the Acts' wording; card the hedge.
- Confidence: Documented (an eyewitness account and Eusebius agree he was a Marcionite burned with Pionius). The right-and-left detail rests on the Acts alone, in a damaged print no other held print confirms: tell it in the narrator's words, not as a quote.
- Explain it: *presbyter*, an elder of a congregation (the Acts call Pionius the same, Pion 4807 "Πιόνιος πρεσβύτερος"). Why they died: the proconsul demanded sacrifice (5561-5563: Pionius, "I fear the living God"; the proconsul, "Many others have sacrificed and live"). The record gives Metrodorus no words.

**E5. Caesarea, under Valerian: a Marcionite woman.**
- Eusebius, Church History VII.12, E-gr 9658-9664; E-en 40460-40474. Later (Eusebius writing about fifty years on, in the same city). Greek: "ἔτι πρὸς τούτοις γύναιόν τι κατὰ τὸν αὐτὸν διωγμὸν ἐν τῇ αὐτῇ πόλει τὸν ὅμοιον ἱστοροῦσιν ἀγῶνα διηθληκέναι· τῆς δὲ Μαρκίωνος αὐτὴν αἱρέσεως γενέσθαι κατέχει λόγος." English: "They relate that besides these, in the same persecution and the same city, a certain woman endured a similar conflict. But it is reported that she belonged to the sect of Marcion." The three men before her, Priscus, Malchus and Alexander, were given to wild beasts (E-en 40460-40464).
- Confidence: Documented that Eusebius reports it; her Marcionite belonging is "reported" (κατέχει λόγος), keep the hedge. No name, no words. The persecution is Valerian's (VII.10-12; Widely Accepted c. 257-260).

**E6. Caesarea, in the persecution begun under Diocletian: Asclepius, a Marcionite bishop, on the same pyre.**
- Eusebius, Martyrs of Palestine 10.2-3, E-gr 24589-24599; E-en 47044-47059. Contemporary: Eusebius lived in Caesarea through this persecution (it began "in the nineteenth year of the reign of Diocletian", E-en 45893). Greek: "τούτῳ δὲ τῆς κατὰ Μαρκίωνα πλάνης ἐπίσκοπός τις εἶναι δοκῶν Ἀσκληπιὸς [OCR '“Ἰσκληπιὸς'] ζήλῳ μέν, ὡς ᾤετο [OCR 'Gc ῴετο'], εὐσεβείας, ἀλλ᾽ οὔτι γε τῆς κατ᾽ ἐπίγνωσιν, ὅμως δ᾽ οὖν [OCR 'ovr'] μιᾷ καὶ τῇ αὐτῇ πυρᾷ τὸν βίον ἐξελήλυθεν." English (47053-47059): "A certain Asclepius, supposed to be a bishop of the sect of Marcion, possessed as he thought with zeal for religion, but 'not according to knowledge,' ended his life on one and the same funeral pyre." The man burned with him: "Peter an ascetic, also called Apselamus, from the village of Anea" (47046), who would not spare himself though the judge begged him (47049-47052).
- Confidence: Documented. Keep both hedges, Eusebius's own: "supposed to be" a bishop and zeal "as he thought". The phrase "not according to knowledge" is Paul's (Romans 10:2).
- Exact lines (Review B6, 2026-11-25): Greek E-gr 24595-24598, the zeal clause at 24596-24597 ("ζήλῳ μέν, ὡς ᾤετο, εὐσεβείας, ἀλλ᾽ οὔτι γε τῆς κατ᾽ ἐπίγνωσιν"); English E-en 47053-47058, the zeal clause at 47056-47057 ("possessed as he thought with zeal for religion, but “not according to knowledge,”"). *εὐσεβεία* is piety or religion, not "God". The zeal is granted and blamed in one breath: keep both halves.
- Damaged Greek print, confirmed by the 1890 English of the same passage.

**E7. After the persecutions: the edict, the travellers' warning, the Syriac answers.**
- Constantine's edict against the heretics, quoted by Eusebius, Life of Constantine III.64-66. E-en 66071-66080 (address), 66114 (no assembly), 66124-66128 (houses taken), 66141-66150 (houses of prayer to the catholic Church), 66160-66165 (III.66); VC-gr 13038 ("Μαρκιωνισταί" in the address). Contemporary (an imperial document; Eusebius alive). "Understand now, by this present statute, ye Novatians, Valentinians, Marcionites, Paulians, ye who are called Cataphrygians, and all ye who devise and support heresies by means of your private assemblies" (66071-66080, with the editor's note removed). "we give warning by this present statute that none of you henceforth presume to assemble yourselves together" (66113-66114). "We have directed, accordingly, that you be deprived of all the houses in which you are accustomed to hold your assemblies ... not in public merely, but in any private house or place whatsoever" (66124-66128). "all the houses of prayer, if such be worthy of the name, which belong to heretics, and that these be made over without delay to the catholic Church; that any other places be confiscated to the public service" (66143-66146). III.66: "Of those whom they had deceived, some, intimidated by the emperor's threats, disguising their real sentiments, crept secretly into the Church" (66161-66164). Address and close (Review B7, 2026-11-25): "Victor Constantinus, Maximus Augustus, to the heretics" (E-en 66068-66069; VC-gr 13035, "αἱρετικοῖς"; the chapter title, VC-gr 13032-13033, "πρὸς τοὺς ἀθέους αἱρεσιώτας"); it closes "Let this edict be made public" (66149). The Greek address adds "all who, to speak simply, fill up the heresies through your own assemblies" (VC-gr 13039-13040, "οἱ τὰς αἱρέσεις διὰ τῶν οἰκείων πληροῦντες συστημάτων"). No held line says it was read out to them. Documented. No date in the text; Eusebius places it after Nicaea (Widely Accepted: under Constantine, after 325). Reason given: the edict's own: through them "the healthy soul is stricken with disease" (66083), and long patience has let "some who were sound" become tainted (66100-66101).
- Cyril of Jerusalem, Catechetical Lecture 18.26, Cyr 20348-20359. Contemporary (mid-fourth century, lectures to those about to be baptized; Widely Accepted). "since one might properly and truly say that there is a Church of evil doers, I mean the meetings of the heretics, the Marcionists and Manichees, and the rest ... And if ever thou art sojourning in cities, inquire not simply where the Lord's House is (for the other sects of the profane also attempt to call their own dens houses of the Lord), nor merely where the Church is, but where is the Catholic Church." 18.23, Cyr 20271-20273: "It is called Catholic then because it extends over all the world". Also 16.7, Cyr 17970-17971: "Let the Marcionists also be abhorred, who tear away from the New Testament the sayings of the Old". Documented.
  - Explain it: in a city a stranger could walk into a Marcionite meeting that called itself a church and the Lord's house; *Catholic* was the name of the church "over all the world", by Cyril's gloss.
- Ephrem, Against Marcion I (vol. II, 1921), Ephr 1585. Contemporary (fourth century, Edessa). "when all the scattered groups (lit. fragments) of the followers of Marcion are gathered together they cannot keep the fast of Ezekiel ... If they say, 'We are praying the whole day,' let us see whether their prayer is accepted." Also "the Marcionites, because they pray more than Daniel, as they say". Documented as Ephrem's report of their claim. The Syriac sibling quotes Ephrem (its Q3, Ephr 1205); this story does not quote him.
- Epiphanius, Panarion 42.3-4, Epi 7687, 7742-7753, 7893-7897. Later (c. 375). Enemy, and partly hearsay.
  - 7687, 7742: "παρ᾽ αὐτῷ δὲ τῷ Μαρκίωνι καὶ παρθενία κηρύσσεται, νηστείαν δὲ κατὰ τὸ σάββατον κηρύττει." Virginity is preached, and a fast on the Sabbath.
  - 7744-7748 (OCR "ν»ηστεύσωμεν"): "τὸ δὲ σάββατον νηστεύειν διὰ τοιαύτην αἰτίαν φάσκει· ἐπειδή, φησί, τοῦ θεοῦ τῶν Ἰουδαίων ἐστὶν ἡ ἀνάπαυσις τοῦ πεποιηκότος τὸν κόσμον καὶ ἐν τῇ ἑβδόμῃ ἡμέρᾳ ἀναπαυσαμένου, ἡμεῖς νηστεύσωμεν ταύτην, ἵνα μὴ τὸ καθῆκον τοῦ θεοῦ τῶν Ἰουδαίων ἐργαζώμεθα." Plain sense: he gives this reason for fasting on the Sabbath: since the rest belongs to the God of the Jews, who made the world and rested on the seventh day, let us fast on it, so that we do not do what is due to the God of the Jews.
  - 7742-7744: "μυστήρια δὲ δῆθεν παρ᾽ αὐτῷ ἐπιτελεῖται τῶν κατηχουμένων ὁρώντων· ὕδατι δὲ οὗτος ἐν τοῖς μυστηρίοις χρῆται." Their rites are performed with the catechumens (learners not yet baptized) looking on; he uses water in the rites.
  - 7750-7753: up to three washings or more are allowed, "ὡς παρὰ πολλῶν ἀκήκοα" ("as I have heard from many"); Epiphanius explains this as Marcion's cover for his own fall (7753-7765), an enemy's charge.
  - 7893-7896: "δίδωσι καὶ ἐπιτροπὴν γυναιξὶ βάπτισμα διδόναι" (he gives women leave to baptize).
  - Confidence: Documented as Epiphanius's claims; the three washings hearsay by his own word (Contested). One damaged print, no second held print: narrator's words only, credited, or card.
- Chrysostom, Homily 40 on 1 Corinthians, Chrys 24419-24429. Contemporary (Antioch, late fourth century). An enemy's charge told for laughter, by his own word ("I know indeed that I shall excite much laughter", 24419-24420): "when any Catechumen departs among them, having concealed the living man under the couch of the dead, they approach the corpse and talk with him, and ask him if he wishes to receive baptism; then when he makes no answer, he that is concealed underneath saith in his stead that of course he should wish to be baptized; and so they baptize him instead of the departed". Documented as Chrysostom's charge. Card.

**E8. Cyrrhus, fifth century: the last villages (Theodoret).**
- Theodoret, Letter 81 to the consul Nomus, Thdt 31440 (heading), 31501, 31511-31516. Own words of the bishop who acted (contemporary). "Five and twenty years I so lived" (31501). Q3 span: "I led eight villages of Marcionists with their neighbourhood into the way of truth; another full of Eunomians and another of Arians I brought to the light of divine knowledge, and, by God's grace, not a tare of heresy was left among us. All this I did not effect with impunity; many a time I shed my blood; many a time was I stoned by them and brought to the very gates of death." Note: "by them" follows the whole list (Marcionists, Eunomians, Arians); it does not single out the Marcionites.
- Letter 113 to Leo, Thdt 33031 (heading), 33158, 33162-33164: "more than a thousand souls did I rescue from the plague of Marcion".
- Letter 146 to the monks of Constantinople, Thdt 34942 (heading), 35004-35010: "against the victims of the corruption of Marcion, I have never ceased to struggle; ... Marcion's mad adherents that He is not only good but just; and Saviour not, as they fable, of another's works, but of His own." 35164-35166: "of whom, by God's grace, I have converted more than ten thousand, and brought them to Holy Baptism."
- Theodoret, Church History V.31, Thdt 17542-17545 (contemporary of Chrysostom's last years; his own district): "On hearing that in our district some men were infected with the plague of Marcion he [John Chrysostom] wrote to the then bishop charging him to drive out the plague, and proffering him the aid of the imperial edicts."
- Where the same witness differs: eight villages (Ep. 81); more than a thousand souls (Ep. 113); more than ten thousand brought to baptism (Ep. 146). Tell the villages; card the counts.
- Explain it, each side: Theodoret's aim, to bring them to "Holy Baptism" in his church (35165-35166) and to teach that the Son is "not only good but just" and Saviour of his own works (35009-35010). Their side: no held word. The force: imperial edicts offered against them (17544-17545); Theodoret stoned "many a time" (31515), by heretics of all three kinds.
- Confidence: Documented as Theodoret's own claims. The held record goes no later: no old source in the Library tells their end.
- The `theodoret-of-cyrrhus` story (live) tells the villages and the stoning in narrator's words and quotes "unwillingly"; it does not quote the villages line. Here the line does a new job: the last sight of the Marcionite community.

## 3. What came of them (ranked by what later witnesses carried on and used)

1. **Answering them shaped the church's teaching and its word "Catholic".** Writer after writer answered the Marcionites into the fifth century: Rhodon (E-en 28925-28926), Tertullian, Bardaisan's dialogues "against Marcion's followers" in Syriac (E-en 26633-26637), Cyril (Cyr 17970), Ephrem, Epiphanius, Theodoret (Thdt 35004-35010). Cyril taught the newly baptized to ask, in a strange city, for "the Catholic Church" because the Marcionists also called their meetings churches (Cyr 20348-20359). *Defended:* one God, maker and Father; the name Catholic for the church spread through the world. Documented.
2. **Their baptism became a test case.** A letter Cyprian answered held that "even those that came from him did not need to be baptized, because they seemed to have been already baptized in the name of Jesus Christ" (ANF5 38511-38513; CypLat 14584-14585, "etiam Marcionis fieri mentionem, ut nec ab ipso uenientes ..."). Cyprian answered that Marcion did not hold "the same Father, the Creator" and so his baptism gave nothing (ANF5 38541-38550). Contemporary (c. 256; Widely Accepted). *Defended:* one baptism in the one church. Shows people coming over from Marcionite congregations in Africa. Card or clause.
3. **Their martyrs were recorded by the other side.** The Acts of Pionius and Eusebius kept Metrodorus, the woman of Caesarea and Asclepius (E4-E6). Documented.
4. **The empire turned on them.** Constantine's edict named them, forbade their meetings and gave their houses of prayer to the catholic church (E7). Chrysostom offered imperial edicts against them near Cyrrhus (E8). Documented.
- Who keeps it today: no held old source; leave out.

## 5. Where sources disagree or come late

- Apelles a Marcionite (Rhodon) or no longer one (Tertullian, Prescr. 30). Card.
- Metrodorus: "presbyter of the sect of the Marcionites" (Acts) vs "appears to have been a proselyte" (Eusebius). Tell the Acts; card the hedge.
- Eusebius puts the Smyrna deaths in Polycarp's time; the Acts date them under Decius. Tell the Acts' date.
- Theodoret's counts differ letter to letter.
- Ambrose, Origen's patron: Jerome says Origen converted him "from the heresy of Marcion" (Thdt 40704); Eusebius, Church History VI.18, says he held Valentinus's views; Epiphanius says some called him a Marcionist (Epi 31380). Leave out.
- Epiphanius (c. 375) writes over two centuries after Marcion and partly from hearsay ("as I have heard from many").
