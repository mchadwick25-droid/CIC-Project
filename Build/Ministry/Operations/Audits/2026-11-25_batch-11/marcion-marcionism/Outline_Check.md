# Outline check: Marcionism (`marcion-marcionism`, tradition)

## My account of the heart (written before reading `Outline.md`)

From the census entry (`Card.existing.json`), the slate's job line and `Source_Sheet.md`.

- **Essence:** The Marcionites were a church of their own, gathered from the mid-second century out of Marcion's teaching after his break with Rome: congregations with presbyters and bishops, one Gospel and ten letters of Paul, and a good Father of Jesus held apart from the God who made the world. They lasted, under attack from every side, from Rome to Syria into the fifth century.
- **Who they were:** "the Marcionites", their opponents' name for them (they called their meetings churches, the Lord's house: Cyr 20356-20358).
- **When and where:** mid-second century to the fifth; Rome and Italy, Asia, Carthage, Smyrna, Caesarea, Jerusalem, Edessa, the district of Cyrrhus; Epiphanius's list as far as Persia (Epi 7470-7474).
- **What they faced:** the break with Rome (short essence only); division among their own teachers within a generation (Rhodon on Apelles, Potitus, Basilicus, Syneros); persecution by the empire, shared with the catholics (Metrodorus at Smyrna in 250, the woman at Caesarea under Valerian, Asclepius at Caesarea under Diocletian's persecution); then, after the persecutions, Constantine's edict taking their meeting houses, catholic warnings against their meetings (Cyril), and the bishop who brought their last villages over (Theodoret, with imperial edicts offered).
- **What they believed:** a good God, Father of Jesus, other than the just maker and law-giver; Scripture as a cut Luke and ten letters of Paul (Irenaeus, Epiphanius). Their own words survive only as enemies report them: Apelles's "those who trusted in the Crucified would be saved, if only they were found doing good works" (Rhodon); the Marcionite's "absit, absit" at the incense box (Tertullian).
- **How they practiced:** baptism with water, anointing with oil, milk and honey for the newly washed, bread for Christ's body (Tertullian I.14); baptism kept for the unmarried, virgins, widows, celibates (I.29); a Sabbath fast, women allowed to baptize (Epiphanius, later, enemy); claims to pray more than Daniel (Ephrem). Refusing to deny under persecution though they feared no judging God.
- **What held them together:** Marcion's two books, and the good God who does not punish yet would not be denied; martyrdom for the name of Christ alongside the church that refused them communion.
- **What came of them:** nothing descends. Ranked: (1) answering them shaped the church's teaching on one God, maker and Father, and its use of the name "Catholic" (Cyril); (2) their baptism as a test case (Cyprian); (3) their martyrs recorded by the other side; (4) the empire's turn against them. Defended against them: one God, maker and Father; the one Catholic church; one baptism. The canon question rests on modern debate (census `legacy`): out.

My heart for the story: a church that lived, worshipped and died like the church that refused it, told through its martyrs, its divided teachers, its rites and its last villages, and the founding only as a short essence.
