**BLOCKING: 4. Verdict from this read: REVISE.**

## 1. The test

- **Who they were:** filled. "The Marcionites were the churches that followed Marcion after the church of Rome cast him out." I do not learn who Marcion was beyond a name, but for a tradition this is enough.
- **What happened to them:** filled. Martyrs at Smyrna and Caesarea, Constantine's edict, Cyril's warning, Theodoret's eight villages.
- **What their faith was about:** filled. "Jesus came from a good Father, above the God who made the world." Their Scripture of two books is clear.
- **Why the connection matters:** filled, thinly. I can say they were a rival church beside the main one, dying beside it on the stakes and later forbidden and absorbed by it. I cannot say what they left the wider church. The opening image carries most of it.

## 2. The opening

After the first three sentences I have who (Pionius, Metrodorus), where (the stadium at Smyrna), when (March 250) and why for Pionius (he refused to sacrifice). Missing:
- Why Metrodorus is on the stake. He is raised beside Pionius with no reason.
- Where Smyrna is. A listener abroad has no country or region for it.
Neither leaves me lost about who or where. Not blocking.

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing

1. **BLOCKING.** "Tertullian gives the reason as a restless curiosity that spread to others." Whose curiosity, and curious about what? Tertullian has not been introduced yet. The reason for the break that founds the whole church is lost. Change: say whose curiosity it was and, if the outline holds it, what it was about; introduce Tertullian at this point by role (for example, "a writer at Carthage who wrote five books against him").
2. **BLOCKING.** The Theodoret paragraph's pronouns: "There John Chrysostom had once urged the bishop to drive out Marcion's followers… He worked to bring them to the one Son… He was stoned many times… After twenty-five years as bishop, he wrote…" I cannot tell whether "the bishop" is Theodoret or an earlier one, or whether "He" is Chrysostom or Theodoret. The ending's thread is lost. Change: name Theodoret as the subject of "worked", "was stoned" and "wrote", and say which bishop Chrysostom urged.
3. MINOR. "Irenaeus… says Marcion trimmed both books of whatever made the world's maker Jesus' Father." "Made" first hears as "created". Meaning lost: he cut the passages that called the world's maker Jesus' Father. Change: "…trimmed from both books whatever called the world's maker Jesus' Father," if the outline's wording allows it.
4. MINOR. "Like every witness to them, he was their enemy, for no line of theirs survives in their own hand." The "for" sounds as though he was their enemy because nothing survives. Meaning lost: we know them only through enemies, because their own writings are lost.
5. MINOR. "He was supposed to be, Eusebius says, a bishop of the Marcionites, with zeal for God, as he thought." "As he thought" — who thought? Asclepius or Eusebius? The sneer is lost.
6. MINOR. "Cyril named the Marcionists first…" After "Marcionites" all through, "Marcionists" sounds like a different group to a second-language ear. Change: one name throughout, unless the quoted source's own word needs keeping, in which case say it is the same people.

## 4. Bare statements (Part 1, Explain it)

1. **BLOCKING.** "…found Marcion's followers divided over how many first sources, or beginnings of all things, there are. Apelles, one of their teachers, held there was only one." The quarrel is named and glossed, but not why it mattered to them. The next quote, about not examining teaching, does not connect to the number of beginnings, so I lose the point of the whole paragraph. Change: say in one clause what was at stake in "one or two beginnings" for people who believed in a good Father above a world-maker (one God or two), from the outline; or cut the quarrel and keep only Apelles' words.
2. **BLOCKING.** "…a law read out to the heretics by name, the Marcionites among them." "Heretics" is a label the kit names. It is never said in plain words what it means or who used it. Change: "…read out by name to the groups it called heretics, those it held had left the true teaching, the Marcionites among them," or similar the outline supports.

(The checker's flags on "Constantine issued an edict" and "Their teaching, it said, made the healthy soul sick" are not bare: the edict is glossed as a law, and its reason is given.)

## 8. Pull, drift, finish

- **Opening:** pulls me in strongly. The nails, two rival elders on the right and left, both facing east.
- **First drift:** "found Marcion's followers divided over how many first sources, or beginnings of all things, there are." It is abstract, its stakes are never said, and the quote after it is about something else. For the reviser: tie it to the two Gods, or trim it.
- **Second dip:** the Theodoret paragraph, where I lose who is doing what.
- **To the end:** yes. Tertullian's incense box and "'Never,' you say, 'never!'" win me back, and the eight villages make a clear end.
