## 1. The test, for a tradition

- **Who these people were:** filled. "Jews who believed that Jesus was the Christ and went on keeping the Law God gave through Moses: circumcision, the Sabbath, the food laws." They were called Ebionites and Nazoraeans, and the later writers place them "in Syria and beyond the Jordan."
- **What happened to them:** filled. Irenaeus counted them among the heresies. The Jews "cursed them three times a day." The Pharisees "still condemned it." Jerome says they "are neither Jews nor Christians."
- **What their faith was about:** filled. They believed Jesus was the Christ and kept the Law. They rejected Paul. The writers disagree on whether Jesus was born of a virgin, and the story shows that disagreement through Irenaeus, Origen, Epiphanius and Jerome.
- **Why the connection matters:** thin, but filled. I can say that they stood between church and synagogue and were rejected by both, and that we know them only through their opponents. Jerome's last line carries this. Nothing is missing that the bar blocks.

I was not lost, because the scene is a conversation and the community's place comes in sentence 6 ("in Syria and beyond the Jordan"). ## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing (MINOR)

1. "Trypho asked whether some said no." On one hearing I could not tell what "no" answers. The quote that follows ("do not dare to share even company or a home with such people") is about sharing a home, not about being saved, so the link is lost. Change: "Trypho asked whether some Christians said no."
2. "There are some, Trypho, I answered, who do not dare..." The narrator suddenly says "I". For a second-language listener the speaker shifts mid-sentence. Change: say who speaks just before the quote, for example "Justin answered:", and keep the quote word for word.
3. "...who had sworn not to eat bread from the hour he drank the Lord's cup until he saw him risen." Without church background I do not know what "the Lord's cup" is, or when he drank it. Change: add its plain sense alongside, from the outline, for example "the cup at the Lord's last meal before his death", if the outline holds it.

None of these loses the thread of the story.
