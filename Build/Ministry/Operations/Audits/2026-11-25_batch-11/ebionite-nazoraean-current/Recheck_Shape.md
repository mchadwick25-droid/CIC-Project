## Findings

### BLOCKING 1. Origen's "poverty of their Law" says more than its source

Words to explain (row "the poverty of their Law") and Beat 2 say Origen took the name "to fit a Law he held poor" / "the Law they kept, which he held a poor thing". The cited English (ANF4 39116–39121) says only "receiving their very name from the poverty of the law, according to the literal acceptation of the word". No held line says Origen held the Law of Moses poor. The scorn the held texts give is aimed at their understanding: "poor in understanding (deriving their name from the poverty of their intellect ...)" (ANF4 34023–34025). As written, the narrator would hand Origen a judgment on the Law itself that no held line gives him.

Replace, in Beat 2, the sentence "The plain sense follows at once, in Origen's voice: he took the name to fit a Law he held poor; it was scorn, and in another book he calls them poor in understanding (ANF4 39116–39121; ANF4 34023–34025; see Words to explain)." with:

> The plain sense follows at once, in Origen's voice: in another book he turned the name to scorn, and called them poor in understanding, as if the name came from the poverty of their minds (ANF4 39116–39121; ANF4 34023–34025; see Words to explain).

Replace, in the Words to explain row, "Plain sense, in Origen's voice: ebion is Hebrew for "poor", and Origen took the name to fit the Law they kept, which he held a poor thing; it is scorn, not praise, and in another book he scorns them as poor in understanding." with:

> Plain sense, in Origen's voice: ebion is Hebrew for "poor", and he tied their name to "the poverty of the law"; he does not say there what was poor. In another book he makes it scorn: they are poor in understanding, named from the poverty of their minds. Never say that Origen held the Law itself poor.

(The rest of the row, from "Rests on Against Celsus 2.1", stays.) In Do not say, the row "Their Law was poor" stays, and gets one more wrong form: "Origen held their Law a poor thing".

### BLOCKING 2. Beats 4 and 6 open on the witness, against the outline's own Shape

Shape says each beat opens on the community as the grammatical subject, with the witness credited inside, never as the beat's subject. That rule is the fix for the grade's "chain of outsiders' descriptions". Beat 4 still opens "About 375 Epiphanius, a bishop on Cyprus, wrote of them". Beat 6 still opens "In 404, writing to Augustine, Jerome said". For a first-time listener, those two openings bring back the roll of witnesses.

Beat 4: replace "About 375 Epiphanius, a bishop on Cyprus, wrote of them as two heresies, side by side: the Nazoraeans and the Ebionites (E11; Pan 12578). Then the people as subject, every line in his voice ("he says"): the Nazoraeans named themselves, he says, after Nazareth (C6, Pan 24398–24399, "οὗτοι γὰρ ἑαυτοῖς ὀνομα ἐπέϑεντο ... Ναζωραίων"; their own act of naming, as he reports it)." with:

> The beat opens on the people: the Nazoraeans named themselves after Nazareth, says Epiphanius, a bishop on Cyprus, writing about 375 (C6, Pan 24398–24399, "οὗτοι γὰρ ἑαυτοῖς ὀνομα ἐπέϑεντο ... Ναζωραίων"; their own act of naming, as he reports it; A6). He wrote of them and the Ebionites as two heresies, side by side (E11; Pan 12578). Then every line in his voice ("he says"):

Beat 6: replace "In 404, writing to Augustine (Cut first 12: no gloss on him), Jerome said that to this day, among the Jews in all the synagogues of the East, there was a sect commonly called the Nazoraeans, which the Pharisees still condemned (A12, D4; CSEL34 23235–23237): their place and what was done to them, in his voice." with:

> The beat opens on the people: in 404, among the Jews in all the synagogues of the East, there was still a sect commonly called the Nazoraeans, and the Pharisees still condemned it. So Jerome wrote that year to Augustine (Cut first 12: no gloss on him) (A12, D4; CSEL34 23235–23237, "usque hodie ... huc usque"): their place and what was done to them, in his voice.

### BLOCKING 3. The essence ends past the first minute

The kit (Part 1) says the opening and the essence both come within the first minute, at about 150 words. In the current brief the essence ends at about word 182. The shape plan (hook 110, essence 80) moves it to about word 190, about 1:16. The essence's span-and-place clause comes first, so the essence's who-and-what ("Jews who believed that Jesus was the Christ and went on keeping the Law") arrives only after about word 135.

In Story beats, hook paragraph, replace "The essence carries their place: the later writers place them in Syria and beyond the Jordan (A9, A11, A14)." with:

> Order inside the essence: who they were and what they kept first ("Jews who believed that Jesus was the Christ and went on keeping the Law ..."), then the one-witness line, then the span and the place, last: church writers wrote of them from Justin's day to about 400, and the later ones place them in Syria and beyond the Jordan (A9, A11, A14). The essence's wording in The heart is unchanged; only its order in speech changes, and its two lead sentences must end by about word 160.
