## 1. The test (tradition)

- **Who they were:** filled. "They were Jews who believed that Jesus was the Christ and went on keeping the Law God gave through Moses: circumcision, the Sabbath, the food laws."
- **What happened to them:** filled. Counted "among the heresies" by Irenaeus; cursed "three times a day" in the synagogues; Jerome's "neither Jews nor Christians."
- **What their faith was about:** filled. Jesus as the Christ, the Law kept, and the open question of his birth ("son of Joseph"; Origen's two kinds; Epiphanius "cannot tell").
- **Why the connection matters:** filled, but thin. What they left the wider church comes only through Symmachus's Greek version in Origen's "great book of columns" and the Hebrew gospel Jerome copied. The listener can say it, but has to assemble it.

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing (MINOR)

1. "There are some, Trypho, I answered, who do not dare to share even company or a home with such people." Just before this the narrator said "Justin answered" in the third person. Heard aloud, "I answered" sounds like the narrator speaking. Change: "Justin replied: 'There are some, Trypho, who do not dare...'" (keep the quote's words, and move the speech tag outside the quote).
2. "About 375, Epiphanius ... wrote of them under two names, Nazoraeans and Ebionites." Then only the Nazoraeans are described. One hearing leaves me unsure whether these are one people or two. The thread holds, because the doubt is the story. Change: state, as Epiphanius gives it, whether he treats them as two groups.
3. "What they held about Christ himself, he says, he cannot tell: whether he was a mere man..." Three "he"s point to two men, Epiphanius and Christ. Change: "What they held about Christ himself, Epiphanius could not tell: whether Christ was a mere man, or born of Mary by the Holy Spirit."

## 4. Bare statements (BLOCKING)

## 5. Citations read aloud not called for (MINOR)

2. "What they held about Christ himself, he says, he cannot tell". This is his own admission, so "he says" doubles it. The narrator's form is in 3.3 above.

The checker's other flags are called for. "On Jesus, Irenaeus writes..." credits a point where the sources differ. "He calls it ... and says he put it into Greek and Latin" credits a claim that only Jerome makes. "Trypho asked..." is a person acting in the scene, not a citation.

Lines that sound machine-made (MINOR):
1. "He set down what they believed about Jesus as the church's own faith, and in the same breath gave his verdict." This announces the quote and then the quote says it. That is commentary linking quotations, and the "in the same breath" setup is a stock pattern. Change: "Of their faith in Jesus, and of them, he wrote:"
2. Template cadence. Five paragraphs in a row open on date, witness, role: "About 180, Irenaeus, bishop of...", "In his last years Origen, the Alexandrian teacher...", "About 375, Epiphanius, a bishop on Cyprus...", "In 392 the Latin scholar Jerome...", "In 404 Jerome...". The ending is a hostile witness's verdict, given as a quote. That is not the narrator's moral. The header records the ending as ruled, so this is noted only.
