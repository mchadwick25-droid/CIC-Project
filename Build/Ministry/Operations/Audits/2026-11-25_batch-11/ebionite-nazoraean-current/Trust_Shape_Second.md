## Sentence by sentence

**1.** "In the middle of the second century, at Ephesus, as Eusebius later recorded, a Jew named Trypho questioned the Christian teacher Justin." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** HE 24310–24311: "which he held in the city of Ephesus with Trypho, a most distinguished man among the Hebrews". ANF1 17344–17351: "I am a Hebrew of the circumcision". The date is the tree's Widely Accepted one for Justin (Eusebius puts his Apology under Antoninus, HE 22605). Eusebius is credited for the place only, which is right.

**2.** "Suppose a man believes that Jesus is the Christ, the Messiah God had promised, and obeys him, but still wants to keep the Law of Moses: will he be saved?" **SUMMARY HOLDS.** JusG 2152–2154: "μετὰ τοῦ καὶ τοῦτον εἶναι τὸν Χριστὸν ἐπίστασθαι ... καὶ πεπιστευκέναι καὶ πείθεσθαι αὐτῷ, βούλεται καὶ ταῦτα φυλάσσειν, σωθήσεται;" "These things" are the Mosaic institutions (ANF1 19913–19918, "such institutions as were given by Moses"). "The Messiah God had promised" explains the word "Christ". It is not a claim added to Trypho's question.

**3.** "In my view he will, Justin answered, if he does not press the Law on non-Jewish believers." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** JusG 2155–2157: "Ὡς μὲν ἐμοὶ δοκεῖ ... σωθήσεται ὁ τοιοῦτος, ἐὰν μὴ τοὺς ... ἀπὸ τῶν ἐθνῶν ... ἐκ παντὸς πείθειν ἀγωνίζηται ταὐτὰ αὐτῷ φυλάσσειν". "Press" is a fair word for "strive in every way to persuade".

**4.** "Trypho asked whether some said no." **HELD.** JusG 2161–2162: "εἰ μήτι εἰσὶν οἱ λέγοντες ὅτι οὐ σωθήσονται οἱ τοιοῦτοι;"

**5.** Quote: "There are some, Trypho, I answered, who do not dare to share even company or a home with such people. I do not agree with them." **HELD**, checked against the Greek. JusG 2166–2167: "Εἰσίν, ἀπεκρινάμην, ὦ Τρύφων, καὶ μηδὲ κοινωνεῖν ὁμιλίας ἢ ἑστίας τοῖς τοιούτοις τολμῶντες· οἷς ἐγὼ οὐ σύναινός εἰμι." The words "company" (ὁμιλία) and "home" (ἑστία, hearth) are right, and so is "even" (μηδέ).

**6.** "Such people were Jews who believed that Jesus was the Christ and went on keeping that Law: circumcision, the Sabbath, the food laws." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** Justin names circumcision and the Sabbath (JusG 2170–2171, "μήτε περιτέμνεσθαι ... μήτε σαββατίζειν") and calls them "of your race" (ANF1 19925–19926). The food laws are Origen's (ANF9 31918–31922, "meat clean and unclean, for the transgression of which we are accused by ... the Ebionites"). Pan 24969–24971 gives "περιτομῇ τε καὶ σαββάτῳ καὶ τοῖς ἄλλοις". This is a fair summary across the witnesses.

**7.** "Their own books are lost, so we meet them only through other church writers, nearly all of whom argued against them, and those writers do not agree on what they believed about Jesus." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** This is an absence. No work of theirs is held in `cic/texts/` except as quoted by others: their gospel only through Jerome (PL23 48081–48136) and Epiphanius (Pan 26376, 26666), and Symmachus only through Origen's columns. Every witness argues against them except Justin, who would take them in (JusG 2167–2173, "ὡς ὁμοσπλάγχνοις καὶ ἀδελφοῖς"), so "nearly all" is exact. On the disagreement: Irenaeus says "begotten by Joseph" (ANF1 43299–43300); Origen gives a "twofold sect" (ANF4 53650–53653); Epiphanius says "οὐχ οἶδα εἰπεῖν" (Pan 25020–25023); Jerome says "in quem et nos credimus" (CSEL34 23272–23273).

**8.** "Church writers wrote of them from Justin's day to about 400, and the later ones place them in Syria and beyond the Jordan." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** Jerome wrote in 404 (CSEL34 23235, "usque hodie"). Epiphanius gives the places: Pan 25023–25025, "ἐν τῇ Βεροιαίων περὶ τὴν Κοίλην Συρίαν καὶ ἐν τῇ Δεχαπόλει περὶ τὰ τῆς Πέλλης μέρη καὶ ἐν τῇ Βασανίτιδι", and Pan 25030, "τὴν Περαίαν οἰκήσαντες" (Peraea, the land beyond the Jordan). Jerome gives Beroea "urbe Syrie" (PL23 48161–48162). None of the earlier writers names a place, so "the later ones" is right. Epiphanius's Cyprus for the Ebionites (Pan 26879) is left out, which softens nothing.

**9.** "By about 180 they were called Ebionites, and Irenaeus, bishop of Lyon in Gaul, counted them among the heresies, the teachings he held false." **HELD.** ANF1 33533–33534: "Those who are called Ebionites". The chapter title, ANF1 33509–33510, is "Doctrines of Cerinthus, the Ebionites". For the see, HE 27953–27955: "Pothinus having died with the other martyrs in Gaul ... Irenæus succeeded him in the episcopate of the church at Lyons." "About 180" is Widely Accepted. The editor's note puts the work "subsequently to a.d. 181" (ANF1 43298), so "by about 180" is as rough as the record allows.

**10.** "They were circumcised, he writes, and lived so much as Jews that they revered Jerusalem 'as if it were the house of God'." **HELD.** ANF1 33539–33542: "they practise circumcision ... and are so Judaic in their style of life, that they even adore Jerusalem as if it were the house of God". In the Latin (Holl's apparatus, Pan 24979–24981) it is "uti et Hierosolymam adorent quasi sit domus dei". "Revered" for *adorent* keeps the force, and the quoted words are exact.

**11.** "They rejected Paul as 'an apostate from the Law', one who had abandoned it." **HELD.** ANF1 33537–33538: "repudiate the Apostle Paul, maintaining that he was an apostate from the law."

**12.** "They held, he writes, that Jesus was the son of Joseph." **HELD.** ANF1 43299–43300: "The Ebionites ... assert that He was begotten by Joseph".

**13.** "One of them, Symmachus, put the Hebrew Scriptures into Greek, and their opponents read his version." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** HE 34737–34738: "As to these translators it should be stated that Symmachus was an Ebionite". HE 34444–34449 shows Origen working through the "translators of the Sacred Scriptures besides the Seventy". Jerome uses Symmachus's readings too (JP 46683, "the true rendering is that of Symmachus"). "Their opponents" fairly covers Origen and Jerome.

**14.** "In his last years Origen, the teacher from Alexandria, said that their very name came from the poverty of their Law, for Ebion is Hebrew for 'poor'." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** ANF4 39116–39119: "receiving their very name from the poverty of the law ... for Ebion signifies 'poor' among the Jews". On the date: HE 37093 says "Origen, being ... over sixty years old", and HE 37104–37105 that "He also at this time composed a work ... in answer to ... Celsus". HE 39163 gives "Origen died, being sixty-nine years of age". "In his last years" is as rough as the old sources allow.

**15.** "In another book he scorned them as poor in understanding, named from the poverty of their minds." **HELD.** ANF4 34023–34025: "the Ebionites ... who are poor in understanding (deriving their name from the poverty of their intellect—'Ebion' signifying 'poor' in Hebrew)". The scorn is credited to Origen.

**16.** "He knew two kinds of them: some confessed with the church that Jesus was born of a virgin, and some denied it and said he was born like other men." **HELD.** ANF4 53650–53653: "the twofold sect of Ebionites, who either acknowledge with us that Jesus was born of a virgin, or deny this, and maintain that He was begotten like other human beings".

**17.** "The Nazoraeans named themselves after Nazareth, says Epiphanius, a bishop on Cyprus, who about 375 wrote of them and the Ebionites as two heresies, side by side." **HELD.** Pan 24398–24399: "οὗτοι γὰρ ἑαυτοῖς ὀνομα ἐπέϑεντο ... For the order, Pan 12578: "ἐνάτη Ναζωραῖοι, δεκάτη Ἐβιωναῖοι". "About 375" is the tree's Widely Accepted date. *δῆθεν* ("as it seems") is carried by "says Epiphanius".

**18.** "They were Jews in everything, he says: they read the Law and the prophets in Hebrew, and believed in one God and in his child Jesus Christ." **HELD.** Pan 24954–24955: "τὰ πάντα δέ εἰσιν Ἰουδαῖοι". **19.** "What they held about Christ himself, he says, he cannot tell, whether a mere man, or born of Mary by the Holy Spirit." **HELD.** Pan 24971 and 25020–25023: "περὶ Χριστοῦ δὲ οὐχ οἶδα εἰπεῖν, εἰ ... ψιλὸν ἄνϑρωπον νομίζουσιν ἢ ... διὰ πνεύματος ἁγίου γεγεννῆσϑαι ἐκ Μαρίας διαβεβαιοῦνται."

**20.** "In the synagogues, he says, the Jews cursed them three times a day, praying God to curse the Nazoraeans, because they were Jews who preached that Jesus is the Christ." **HELD.** Pan 25161–25166: "τρὶς τῆς ἡμέρας ὅτε εὐχὰς ἐπιτελοῦσιν ἑαυτοῖς ἐν ταῖς συναγωγαῖς ἐπαρῶνται αὐτοῖς ... »ἐπικαταράσαι ὁ ϑεὸς τοὺς Ναζωραίους« ... διὰ τὸ ἀπὸ Ἰουδαίων αὐτοὺς ὄντας Ἰησοῦν κηρύσσειν εἶναι (τὸν) Χριστόν". The reason is Epiphanius's own, and it stays in his voice.

**21.** "In Beroea, a city of Syria, the Nazoraeans used a gospel written in Hebrew letters, and they let Jerome copy it, as he wrote in 392." **HELD.** PL23 48156–48162: "Evangelium Christi Hebraicis litteris verbisque composuit ... VI 38784–38785 gives the date: "fourteenth year of the Emperor Theodosius". Leaving out "Matthew" is a Cut first item, and no force is lost.

**22.** "Elsewhere he calls it the Gospel according to the Hebrews, and says he put it into Greek and Latin." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** PL23 44505–44509: "In Evangelio juxta Hebraeos, quod ... PL23 48081–48082: "Evangelium quoque quod appellatur secundum Hebreos, et a me nuper in Grecum Latinumque sermonem translatum est". The link between the two passages is Jerome's own. ## Observations (not findings)

- Justin's welcome comes with a slight: he says such people keep the Law "through weak-mindedness" (JusG 2167, "διὰ τὸ ἀσθενὲς τῆς γνώμης"). The brief never says Justin approved of them, only that he would not shun them, and "nearly all" leaves him as the exception. So nothing is misstated. The condescension could go on the card.
- Epiphanius uses *δῆθεν* ("as it seems") on both the Nazareth name and the curse's reason. Each is told in his voice, so the doubt he signals is carried.

## The heart

The heart is right for this subject, given what the sources show. These were Jews who believed in Jesus as the Christ and kept the Law. Every witness but Justin argued against them, and the witnesses part on what they believed about Jesus: Irenaeus says son of Joseph; Origen says two kinds; Epiphanius says he cannot tell; Jerome says as we believe. The brief makes that disagreement its spine and ends on Jerome's own words.
