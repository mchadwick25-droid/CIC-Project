# Outline check: The Ebionites and Nazoraeans

## My own account of the heart (written before reading Outline.md)

From the census entry, the slate job, the source sheet and what the subject is known for:

- **The essence:** Jews who believed Jesus was the Christ and went on keeping the Law of Moses (circumcision, Sabbath, food laws), in Palestine and Syria from roughly the second to the early fifth century, known to us only through church writers who opposed them, and who describe them differently from one another. What held them together was keeping the Law while believing in Jesus.
- **Who they were:** "the Ebionites" (from the Hebrew for "poor", Origen) and "the Nazoraeans / Nazarenes" (Epiphanius, Jerome); whether one people or two is itself open in the old sources (one name, two kinds in Origen and Eusebius; two chapters in Epiphanius).
- **When and where:** Justin's mid-second-century Law-keeping believers (unnamed); Irenaeus about 180 first names Ebionites; Beroea in Syria, the Decapolis near Pella, Kokaba (Epiphanius, c. 375); "all the synagogues of the East" (Jerome, 404); still few, c. 400 (Augustine).
- **What they faced:** pressed from two sides: some Christians would not share a table with them (Justin), the church writers named them heretics (Irenaeus, Eusebius's "evil demon", Jerome's "neither Jews nor Christians"); the synagogue cursed them (Epiphanius; Jerome's "condemned by the Pharisees"). Fading by about 400.
- **What they believed:** that the Law still bound a Jew who believed in Christ; they refused Paul as "an apostate from the law" (their own charge against him kept by Epiphanius); about Jesus the witnesses differ: born of Joseph (Irenaeus, Eusebius's first kind), virgin-born in one of two kinds (Origen, Eusebius), Epiphanius cannot say for the Nazoraeans, and Jerome says they believe as "we" do. This disagreement is the scene the slate asks for, told in the old witnesses' own words.
- **How they practiced:** circumcision, Sabbath, clean foods, Jerusalem revered (Irenaeus); Lord's day kept too (Eusebius); Hebrew reading of the Scriptures, synagogue and elders (Epiphanius); their own gospel in Hebrew letters, with its James scene (Jerome).
- **What held them together:** the Law kept, with faith in Jesus as the Christ; James honored and Paul refused.
- **What came of them:** heaviest first: Symmachus's Greek version, set in Origen's columns and used by Jerome; their gospel, quoted and translated by Jerome, used by Origen and listed by Eusebius; "Ebion" as a later byword; a small remnant by c. 400. No community descends from them.
- **Descent:** Epiphanius's line from the Jerusalem believers at Pella only in his voice, one late hostile witness.

The major contributions a correct outline must carry: the Law kept with faith in Jesus; the double pressure (church and synagogue); Paul refused; the witnesses' disagreement on Jesus as its own scene; their gospel (and James); Symmachus.

## Heart comparison

The outline's heart matches mine in substance: the Law kept with faith in Jesus as the Christ, the pressure from church and synagogue, Paul refused, the witnesses' disagreement about Jesus as its own scene, their gospel with James at its centre, Symmachus, and a small remnant about 400. No major contribution is missing. The hook and the essence come within the first minute (about 150 words). The essence's span ("from the middle of the second century") rests on Justin's unnamed Law-keeping believers. It holds because Origen defines the name as "those Jews who have received Jesus as Christ" (ANF4 39119–39121).

## Verdict: FIX

## BLOCKING

**B1. Beat 6 is a roll call and goes back in time (the roll-call risk the ruling names).** Beat 6 is set at 404 but goes back to retell what Irenaeus (about 180), Origen, Eusebius and Epiphanius (375) wrote. Each of those writings is already a beat of its own (Beats 1, 2, 3, 4). Telling the same writing in two beats, or jumping back after the essence, is a failure to understand on one hearing (Part 3). The beat is also a list of sources, with no people acting. Its opening, "What did they believe about Jesus himself? Here the witnesses part.", is a question asked only to answer it (Part 1, Craft). The correction is the outline's own alternative under Shape, plus a scene made from what Jerome himself wrote:
- Beat 1, replace "Irenaeus's words on Jesus are kept for Beat 6." with: "On Jesus, he writes, they held that he was the son of Joseph (E2; ANF1 43299–43300)."
- Beat 2, replace "Origen's two kinds are kept for Beat 6." with: "Origen knew two kinds of them: some confessed with the church that Jesus was born of a virgin, and some denied it and said he was born like other men (E4; ANF4 53648–53653)."
- Beat 4, add after the sentence on Christians and the Law: "What they held about Christ himself, he says, he cannot tell: whether a mere man, or born of Mary by the Holy Spirit (E8; Pan 25023–25026)."
- Eusebius's line ("not God before he was born", E6) goes to Beat 3 if Beat 3 stays. Otherwise it goes to the card, as Cut first 6 already plans.
- Replace Beat 6 with: "**6. The doubt, in Jerome's letter of 404.** In 404, writing to Augustine, Jerome said that to this day, in all the synagogues of the East, there was a sect the Pharisees still condemned, commonly called Nazoraeans. What they believed about Jesus he set down as the church's own faith, and in the same breath gave his verdict on them (Quote 2). The listener has already heard each earlier witness's answer in its own time; the scene does not repeat them, and the narrator gives no verdict. (A8, A12, D4, E10; Documented as Jerome's report; the community's belief Contested.)"
- Shape: replace the paragraph with: "The kind kit's form: a story in time order, following the witnesses as they meet the community from about 155 to about 404. Each witness's answer on Jesus is told in its own beat, where that witness acts. The doubt comes to its turn as a scene at Jerome's letter of 404 (Beat 6), where the fullest report of their faith and the hardest verdict come in one breath. No earlier answer is told again."
- Never cut: replace "the doubt scene with Irenaeus, Origen, Epiphanius and Quote 2" with "each witness's answer on Jesus (Beats 1, 2, 4) and the doubt scene with Quote 2".

**B2. Beat 1 gives Paul's rejection a reason no old source gives.** "Reason, from Paul's own side: he had written that circumcision would profit a non-Jewish believer nothing (B6)." B6 (Pan 25108–25109) is Epiphanius quoting Galatians 5.2 at the Nazoraeans as his own argument against them. No witness says it was why they rejected Paul. As written, it adds a "because" the record does not hold. The reason the record does give is their own charge, as Epiphanius reports it: Paul "wrote against circumcision, the Sabbath and the Law" (Pan 26744–26745), and Irenaeus's "apostate from the law" (ANF1 33538–33539).
- Replace the sentence with: "Reason, as the record gives it: they held that he had abandoned the Law (Irenaeus, B5). Their own charge, as Epiphanius reports it, was that he wrote against circumcision, the Sabbath and the Law (Pan 26744–26745). The rest of that charge goes to the card (B5b)."
- Do not say, add the row: "They rejected Paul because he wrote that circumcision profits nothing" | "No witness gives Galatians 5.2 as their reason; Irenaeus: 'an apostate from the law'; Epiphanius: their charge that he wrote against circumcision, Sabbath and Law (B5, B5b)."

**B3. The Connection and the heart claim more than the record holds.**
- The Connection says "the only words of their own that survive are James's bread and the risen Lord's, in Jerome's Latin". Beat 5 says "This is the one place their own book speaks". The heart ("What they believed") says "Their own words survive only in their gospel as Jerome quotes it (F6)". All three are contradicted by the sheet. Jerome quotes the gospel a second time (the baptism scene, F9; JP 45243–45247). Epiphanius quotes the Ebionites' gospel (F3–F4; Pan 26376–26378, 26666–26668) and their charge against Paul (B5b). Jerome also says only that the Nazoraeans used this gospel, not that they wrote it (F8).
- The Connection's chain, "Symmachus's books went to Juliana and from her to Origen, who set his translation in his columns", implies that the translation came through Juliana. Eusebius says she passed on Symmachus's commentaries "and other interpretations of the Scriptures" (HE 34756–34760; HEg 3309–3314). He does not say she passed on the version. G1 and G2 are separate rows on the sheet.
- Replace the Connection's second and third sentences with: "Symmachus's Greek version stood in Origen's columns (G1), and Origen had Symmachus's own writings on the Scriptures from Juliana, who had them from Symmachus himself (G2). The Nazoraeans of Beroea let Jerome copy the gospel they used, and he translated and quoted it (F5–F8). The listener must understand that the writers who called them heretics were also the ones who kept and used their books, so that what survives of the gospels they used survives in their opponents' quotations."
- Beat 5: delete "This is the one place their own book speaks."
- Heart, "What they believed", replace the last sentence with: "Words from the gospels they used survive only as their opponents quote them (Jerome, F6, F9; Epiphanius, F3–F4)." This narrows the approved heart to its sources and changes no substance.
- Do not say, add the rows: "the only words of their own that survive" / "their own book" | "the gospel they used, as Jerome quotes it (F6, F8)"; and "Juliana gave Origen Symmachus's translation" | "Juliana gave him Symmachus's commentaries and other writings on Scripture (G2); the version stood in his columns (G1)."

**B4. A thing is named without its explanation: Symmachus's commentaries (Beat 2).** Beat 2 says "Origen had Symmachus's own commentaries from a woman, Juliana". It never says what they were. Cut first 4 ("Symmachus's books came to Origen") leaves the books unexplained too.
- Beat 2, replace that sentence with: "Origen had Symmachus's own writings on the Scriptures, among them commentaries that, Eusebius says, argued against Matthew's Gospel, from a woman, Juliana, who had the books from Symmachus himself (G2; HE 34746–34756)."

**B5. A link line rests on a modern claim.** Links: "`irenaeus-of-lyon`: wrote against them; first to name them." That he was "first" is McGiffert's claim (C1; HE 18519–18520), not an old source's.

**B6. The Library sweep missed a held later witness.** Augustine, On Heresies 9–10, is held in `augustine_arian-sermon-collatio-maximinum-pl42-lat_migne1886.txt`, lines 1337–1352. - Add a row to the sheet, in E and J: "Augustine, On Heresies 9–10 (late, about 428 per its preface, PL42 995–1000; credits Epiphanius by name, 1347–1352): the Nazaraeans confess Christ the Son of God but keep all the old Law; the Ebionites say Christ was only a man and keep circumcision and the Law's carnal commands.
