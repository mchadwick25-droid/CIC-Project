## Heart

The heart is the right one. All the witnesses show Jews who believed Jesus was the Christ and kept the Law (Origen ANF4 39116–39121; Irenaeus ANF1 33534–33543; Epiphanius Pan 24952–24971). Every report on them is an outsider's, and the witnesses part on Jesus (ANF1 43299–43300; ANF4 53648–53653; Pan 25023–25026; CSEL34 23237–23272). The essence's core is told by about word 137 ("...the food laws"), and its one-witness line ends at word 166. That is about 66 seconds, inside "about 150". It is not late.

## BLOCKING findings

**1. ASSUMPTION ADDED.** "Trypho asked whether some Christians said no."
- Source: JusG 2161–2162: "Διὰ τί οὖν εἶπας· Ὡς μὲν ἐμοί δοκεῖ, σωθήσεται ὁ τοιοῦτος, εἰ μήτι εἰσὶν οἱ λέγοντες ὅτι οὐ σωθήσονται οἱ τοιοῦτοι;" (ANF1 19909–19912: "unless there are some who affirm that such will not be saved?").
- Trypho does not say who those people are. "Christians" is added. It comes from ANF's chapter heading ("Not a few Catholics do otherwise"), which is the editor's, not Justin's. Opening 1 in the outline doesn't say it either: "And are there some who say no?"
- Change: "Trypho asked whether some said such a man would not be saved."
- Do not say: "some Christians said no" | Trypho asks only whether "there are some who say" such a man will not be saved (B1, JusG 2161–2162).

**2. ASSUMPTION ADDED.** "Such people lived in Palestine and Syria from Justin's day to about 400."
- Sources: the last witnesses say they were still there. Jerome, 404: "usque hodie per totas orientis synagogas ... haeresis est" (CSEL34 23235–23236). Augustine "still continues" (I4) is no longer in the story.
- "Lived ... to about 400" gives an end that no source gives. A listener hears that they died out, which is the outline's own Do not say ("They died out" | "still continues", "Nothing after").
- Justin also gives no place (the places come from Epiphanius, Pan 25026–25029, and Jerome, PL23 48161–48162).
- Change: "Such people were still living in Palestine and Syria about 400, two and a half centuries after Justin."
- Do not say: "lived ... to about 400" | "were still living ... about 400" (CSEL34 23235; I4).

**3. SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** "Their own books are lost, so we meet them only through church writers who argued against them, and those writers do not agree on what they believed about Jesus."
- Source: Justin is one of those church writers, and he did not argue against them. "οἷς ἐγὼ οὐ σύναινός εἰμι ... καὶ προσλαμβάνεσθαι καὶ κοινωνεῖν ἁπάντων, ὡς ὁμοσπλάγχνοις καὶ ἀδελφοῖς, δεῖν ἀποφαίνομαι" (JusG 2166–2173; ANF1 19913–19925). The outline itself, under Who tells it, has "friendly to them on terms".
- The sentence contradicts the hook the listener has just heard. Its last clause holds (E2, E4, E8, E10).
- Change: "Their own books are lost, so we meet them only through other Christians' writings, most of them by writers who argued against them, and those writers do not agree on what they believed about Jesus."
- Do not say: "only through church writers who argued against them" | "through other Christians' writings, most of them by writers who argued against them" (Justin, B1b–B1c).

**4. SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** "In 404 Jerome wrote to Augustine that in all the synagogues of the East, to this day, the Jewish teachers, the Pharisees as he called them, still condemned a sect commonly called the Nazoraeans."
- Source: CSEL34 23235–23237: "usque hodie per totas orientis synagogas inter Iudaeos haeresis est, quae dicitur Minaeorum et a Pharisaeis huc usque damnatur, quos uulgo Nazaraeos nuncupant". AugE 33602–33605: "there exists a sect among the Jews throughout all the synagogues of the East ... and is even now condemned by the Pharisees."
- Jerome puts the sect itself among the Jews, in the synagogues. The brief moves "in all the synagogues" from where they were to where they were condemned. That drops the record's point that they were still inside the synagogue.
- Change: "In 404 Jerome wrote to Augustine that to this day, among the Jews in all the synagogues of the East, there was a sect commonly called the Nazoraeans, which the Jewish teachers, the Pharisees as he called them, still condemned."
- Do not say: "in all the synagogues ... the Pharisees condemned a sect" | "among the Jews in all the synagogues of the East there was a sect ... which the Pharisees still condemned" (A12, D4).

## SUMMARY HOLDS (with the line each rests on)

- "In the middle of the second century, a Jew named Trypho put a question to the Christian teacher Justin." The date is A2, the tree's sourced entry (Widely Accepted). For Trypho, ANF1 17344–17351: "I am a Hebrew of the circumcision".
- "Suppose a man believes ... will he be saved?" JusG 2152–2154: "τοῦτον εἶναι τὸν Χριστὸν ... πεπιστευκέναι καὶ πείθεσθαι αὐτῷ. βούλεται καὶ ταῦτα φυλάσσειν, σωθήσεται;" "The Law of Moses" is a fair word for ταῦτα (JusG 2168, "ἐκ τῶν Μωσέως"). "The Messiah God had promised" is the outline's gloss.
- "In my view, yes, Justin answered, if he does not press the Law on non-Jewish believers." JusG 2155–2157: "Ὡς μὲν ἐμοὶ δοκεῖ ... σωθήσεται ὁ τοιοῦτος, ἐὰν μὴ τοὺς ἄλλους ἀνθρώπους ... τοὺς ἀπὸ τῶν ἐθνῶν ... πείθειν ἀγωνίζηται ταὐτὰ αὐτῷ φυλάσσειν".
- "They were Jews who believed ... the food laws." ANF4 39116–39121 ("those Jews who have received Jesus as Christ"); ANF1 33540 (circumcision); Pan 24971 ("περιτομῇ τε καὶ σαββάτῳ"); ANF9 31918–31922 (meats clean and unclean).
- "About 180, Irenaeus ... called them Ebionites." ANF1 33509–33512 and 33534 ("Those who are called Ebionites"), in his catalogue of heresies. "About 180" is the tree's date; the editor gives "subsequently to a.d. 181" (ANF1 43297–43298).
- "Ebion, he says, is Hebrew for 'poor,' ..." ANF4 39119–39121. Note, not blocking: Origen's reason, "from the poverty of the law", is left out. The meaning is kept, and nothing is distorted.
- "One of them, Symmachus, ... side by side." HE 34736–34737 ("Symmachus was an Ebionite", with Jerome agreeing, VI 40556–40561); HE 34715–34719 ("placed them opposite each other ... Aquila and Symmachus and Theodotion with the Septuagint").
- "About 375, Epiphanius ... under two names, Nazoraeans and Ebionites." Pan 24391–24394 (Panarion 29 and 30, two heresies, "σύγχρονοι"). The date is A6, the tree's entry.
- "He calls it the Gospel according to the Hebrews, and says he put it into Greek and Latin." PL23 48081–48083 ("secundum Hebreos ... a me nuper in Grecum Latinumque sermonem translatum"). The name is tied to the Nazoraeans' book only in Against the Pelagians (PL23 44505–44510, "quo utuntur usque hodie Nazareni"; undated in the held texts). In the 392 passage itself, Jerome calls the Beroea volume Matthew's Hebrew gospel (PL23 48155–48162). This is not blocking: it is Jerome's own naming, and "Matthew's" went to the card by Cut first 5.
- "He set down what they believed about Jesus as the church's own faith, and in the same breath gave his verdict." CSEL34 23237–23275: "in quem et nos credimus, sed dum uolunt ...", all in one sentence.
