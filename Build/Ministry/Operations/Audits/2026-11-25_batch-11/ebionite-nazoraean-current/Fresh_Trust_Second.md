### 1. SOFTENED OR CHANGED — BLOCKING

> "Origen, the Alexandrian teacher, said in his last years where the name came from. Ebion, he says, is Hebrew for "poor," and Jews who received Jesus as the Christ were called Ebionites."

The sentence says Origen explained where the name came from, then gives only the word's meaning. Origen's own explanation is that the name came "from the poverty of the law". That is a jibe, and it carries his hostility:
- Against Celsus 2.1, ANF4 39116–39121: "receiving their very name from the poverty of the law, according to the literal acceptation of the word; for Ebion signifies "poor" among the Jews, and those Jews who have received Jesus as Christ are called by the name of Ebionites."
- Same jibe again, On First Principles 4.1.22, ANF4 34023–34025: "the Ebionites ... who are poor in under­standing (deriving their name from the poverty of their intellect—"Ebion" sig­nifying "poor" in Hebrew)".

As it stands, the listener hears Origen as a neutral explainer of a word. This is an enemy's charge dropped (Part 1, "Give an enemy's charge in its own content"), and the record's force is softened by leaving it out (the fourth fault). The outline's Do not say bars only "poor in understanding" *as the meaning*; it allows the jibe in Origen's voice.

**Exact change:** replace "said in his last years where the name came from." with "said in his last years that the name came from the poverty of their Law." The next sentence stays as it is. (+5 words, about 677. If the 675 cap must hold, also cut "the Alexandrian teacher," and write "Origen, the teacher of Alexandria," — or take the 2 words from elsewhere as the reviser chooses. Do not drop the jibe to save words.)
