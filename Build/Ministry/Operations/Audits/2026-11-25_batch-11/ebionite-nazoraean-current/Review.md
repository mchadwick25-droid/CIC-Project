## BLOCKING

### B1. "Such people lived in Palestine and Syria from Justin's day to about 400." (Trust: ASSUMPTION; Trust second: ASSUMPTION)

The two reads find two faults in it:
- **The place.** Justin, Irenaeus and Origen give no place. The first old source to place them is Epiphanius, about 375: Beroea in Coele Syria, Pella in the Decapolis, Basanitis (Pan 25026–25028). Jerome follows in 392 and 404 (PL23 48161; CSEL34 23235). "Palestine" rests only on the census `region`, and the sentence carries fourth-century places back two centuries.
- **The end.** "Lived ... to about 400" gives an end that no source gives. Jerome, in 404, says they were there "usque hodie" (CSEL34 23235). As written, the sentence tells the listener they died out, which the outline's Do not say bars ("They died out").

Change:

> "Church writers wrote of such people from Justin's day to about 400, and the later ones place them in Syria and beyond the Jordan."

Trust's own wording used "met", which risks the Do not say line "Epiphanius visited them". "Wrote of" avoids it. The reviser's wording gets a targeted check.

Outline: **the outline itself carries the error, in the heart.** The essence reads "From the middle of the second century to about 400, in Palestine and Syria, there were Jews who...". **This changes the approved heart's wording.** The researcher rewords the essence to match, and the outline check confirms it from Pan 25026–25028, PL23 48161 and CSEL34 23235. Mark is told.

Do not say, to add:
- "lived in Palestine and Syria from Justin's day" → "the later writers place them in Syria and beyond the Jordan" (Pan 25026–25028; Justin gives no place).
- "lived ... to about 400" → "church writers wrote of them to about 400" (Jerome, "usque hodie", CSEL34 23235).

### B2. "...we meet them only through church writers who argued against them..." (Trust: SOFTENED; Trust second: SOFTENED)

Justin, the story's first witness, did not argue against them. He would take them in "as kinsmen and brethren" (JusG 2172–2173, ὡς ὁμοσπλάγχνοις καὶ ἀδελφοῖς; ANF1 19922–19925). As written, the sentence contradicts the scene the listener has just heard. "Their own books are lost" and the last clause hold.

Change:

> "Their own books are lost, so we meet them only through other church writers, nearly all of whom argued against them, and those writers do not agree on what they believed about Jesus."

Trust second worded it "other Christians' writings, most of them by writers who argued against them". The Trust wording is shorter and keeps "church writers".

Outline: **the outline itself carries the error, in the heart.** The essence reads "We meet them only through church writers who argued against them", and the hook beat repeats it. Yet the outline's Who tells it marks Justin "friendly to them on terms". **This changes the approved heart's wording.** It goes to the researcher and the outline check, and Mark is told.

Do not say, to add: "only through church writers who argued against them" → "through other church writers, nearly all of whom argued against them" (JusG 2166–2173).

### B4. "...wrote of them under two names, Nazoraeans and Ebionites." (Trust: SOFTENED; Trust second: SUMMARY HOLDS; Skeptic: failing sentence, MINOR)

Epiphanius counts two heresies, side by side: "ἐνάτη Ναζωραῖοι, δεκάτη Ἐβιωναῖοι" (Pan 12578; Panarion 29 and 30). "Under two names" tells one side of the one-group-or-two question as fact, and gives Epiphanius the opposite of his own view. The skeptic heard the same doubt ("one people or two").

Change:

> "About 375, Epiphanius, a bishop on Cyprus, wrote of them as two heresies, side by side: the Nazoraeans and the Ebionites."

Outline: **the outline itself carries the error.** Beat 4 is titled "two names" and worded "wrote of them under two names", although the Do not say line on one group or two says "Epiphanius two". The researcher corrects Beat 4, and the outline check confirms it from Pan 12578.

Do not say, to add: "under two names, Nazoraeans and Ebionites" → "as two heresies, side by side: the Nazoraeans and the Ebionites" (Pan 12578).

### B5. "He calls it the Gospel according to the Hebrews, and says he put it into Greek and Latin." (Trust: SOFTENED; Trust second: SUMMARY HOLDS)

The sentence sits under "In 392". In the 392 passage on Beroea, Jerome calls the book Matthew's Hebrew gospel (PL23 48155–48162). The name "the Gospel according to the Hebrews", and the translation, come from another chapter, which does not tie that gospel to the Nazoraeans (PL23 48081–48083). Jerome links the two only in an undated later work (PL23 44505–44509). As written, the sentence moves that later link onto 392.

Change:

> "Elsewhere he calls it the Gospel according to the Hebrews, and he says he put it into Greek and Latin."

The Trust read's wording also adds "which he took for Matthew's own". That would bring back Matthew, which Cut first 5 sent to the card under Mark's length ruling. So it is not recommended here. If it is wanted, it is a change to a ruled cut, and comes to Mark.

Outline: **the outline left room for it.** Beat 5 is headed "(writing in 392)" and puts the naming inside it, and its cites run F5–F8 together. The researcher separates the 392 copying (F7) from the naming (F8). The outline check confirms it from PL23 48155–48162 and 44505–44509.

Do not say, to add: "In 392 ... He calls it the Gospel according to the Hebrews" → "Elsewhere he calls it the Gospel according to the Hebrews" (PL23 44505–44509; in 392, Matthew's Hebrew, PL23 48155–48162).

### B6. "In 404 Jerome wrote to Augustine that in all the synagogues of the East ... still condemned a sect..." (Trust second: SOFTENED; Trust: SUMMARY HOLDS)

Jerome puts the sect itself among the Jews, in the synagogues: "per totas orientis synagogas inter Iudaeos haeresis est ... et a Pharisaeis huc usque damnatur" (CSEL34 23235–23237). The brief moves "in all the synagogues" to where they were condemned, and drops the point that they were still inside the synagogue.

Change (split in two, since one sentence would run 40 words):

> "In 404 Jerome wrote to Augustine that to this day, among the Jews in all the synagogues of the East, there was a sect commonly called the Nazoraeans. The Jewish teachers, the Pharisees as he called them, still condemned it."

Outline: **holds it.** Beat 6 reads "in all the synagogues of the East, there was a sect the Pharisees still condemned". The split wording gets a targeted check.

Do not say, to add: "in all the synagogues ... the Pharisees condemned a sect" → "among the Jews in all the synagogues of the East there was a sect ... which the Pharisees still condemned" (CSEL34 23235–23237).
