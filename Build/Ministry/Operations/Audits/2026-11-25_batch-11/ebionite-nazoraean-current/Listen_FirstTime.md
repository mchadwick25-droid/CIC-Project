## 1. The test

- **Who they were:** filled. "Jews who believed that Jesus was the Christ and went on keeping the Law God gave through Moses: circumcision, the Sabbath, the food laws." Named Ebionites and Nazoraeans; Palestine and Syria, Justin's day to about 400.
- **What happened to them:** filled. Church writers counted them among "the heresies"; "the Jews cursed them three times a day"; Jerome: "they are neither Jews nor Christians." I can say they were pushed out by both sides. What finally became of them after about 400 I only hear as a date, but nothing in the hearing makes me feel cheated of it.
- **What their faith was about:** filled. Jesus as the Christ plus the Law; rejecting Paul; and the split on whether Jesus was "born of a virgin" or "born like other men."
- **Why the connection matters:** filled, through what people did: Justin's question (can a Law-keeping believer be saved?) and Jerome's verdict frame them as the Jewish believers the rest of the church would not own.

## 2. The opening

I was not lost, because paragraph 2 gives "Palestine and Syria" within the first minute, and the essence is told by about word 155. MINOR.

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing

- "There are some, Trypho, I answered, who do not dare to share even company or a home with such people." The "I" comes with no warning. On one hearing I took it as the narrator speaking and lost for a moment who was talking. MINOR. Change: frame it in the narrator's voice before the quote, e.g. "Trypho asked whether some Christians said no, and Justin, who wrote the talk down himself, set down his answer:" and keep the quote as it stands.
- "On Jesus, Irenaeus writes, they held that he was the son of Joseph." Without church background I do not know who Joseph is, so the point (an ordinary human birth) is lost until Origen's paragraph says "born like other men." MINOR. Change: "they held that he was the son of Joseph, Mary's husband, born as other men are" (only if the outline holds it).
- "In it, after the resurrection, the Lord appears to James, his brother, who had sworn not to eat bread from the hour he drank the Lord's cup until he saw him risen." "Drank the Lord's cup" means nothing to me; I lose when the vow began. MINOR. Change: gloss in a clause, e.g. "from the hour he drank the cup at the Lord's last supper," if the outline holds it.
- "He set down what they believed about Jesus as the church's own faith, and in the same breath gave his verdict." I could not tell whether Jerome said this was the church's faith, or the Nazoraeans believed what the church believes. The quote ("We too believe in him") then clears it. MINOR.
