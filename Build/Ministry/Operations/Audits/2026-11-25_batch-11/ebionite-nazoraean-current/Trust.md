## Heart

The heart is right for what the sources show. Every witness gives Jews who took Jesus as the Christ and kept the Law (Origen, ANF4 39114–39121, "the Jewish converts have not deserted the law of their fathers… those Jews who have received Jesus as Christ are called by the name of Ebionites"). The witnesses also part on what they held about Jesus: Irenaeus says begotten by Joseph, Origen gives two kinds, Epiphanius says he cannot tell, Jerome gives the church's creed. The essence is told by word 137 (who, when, where, Law with faith in Christ), and the doubt by word 166. Both fall within the first minute.

## Sentences not HELD

**1. ASSUMPTION ADDED.** "Such people lived in Palestine and Syria from Justin's day to about 400."
- Justin gives no place for the Law-keeping believers (ANF1 19913–19928; JusG 2166–2173), and neither do Irenaeus or Origen. The first old source to place them is Epiphanius, about 375: "ἐν τῇ Βεροιαίων περὶ τὴν Κοίλην Συρίαν καὶ ἐν τῇ Δεκαπόλει περὶ τὰ τῆς Πέλλης μέρη καὶ ἐν τῇ Βασανίτιδι" (Pan 25026–25028). Jerome follows in 392 and 404: Beroea, "per totas orientis synagogas" (PL23 48161; CSEL34 23235).
- "Palestine" rests only on the census `region`. The kind kit says the census is a starting list, never a source. The sentence carries fourth-century places back two centuries.
- Change: "Church writers met such people from Justin's day to about 400, and the later ones place them in Syria and the lands beyond the Jordan." Or cut the place.

**2. SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** "Their own books are lost, so we meet them only through church writers who argued against them, and those writers do not agree on what they believed about Jesus."
- The story's own first witness did not argue against them. Justin: "I hold that we ought to join ourselves to such, and associate with them in all things as kinsmen and brethren" (ANF1 19922–19925; JusG 2172–2173, "ὡς ὁμοσπλάγχνοις καὶ ἀδελφοῖς").
- As written, the sentence contradicts the scene the listener has just heard. "Their own books are lost" stands: no work of theirs is held in the Library, and HE 34750 notes the commentaries "have vanished" (an edition fact). The last clause is HELD.
- Change: "Their own books are lost, so we meet them only through other church writers, nearly all of whom argued against them, and those writers do not agree on what they believed about Jesus."

**3. SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** "About 375, Epiphanius, a bishop on Cyprus, wrote of them under two names, Nazoraeans and Ebionites."
- Epiphanius counts them as two separate heresies, not one people under two names: "ἐνάτη Ναζωραῖοι, δεκάτη Ἐβιωναῖοι" (Pan 12578). That is the ninth and tenth heresies, Panarion 29 and 30.
- "Under two names" tells as fact one side of the one-group-or-two question, and gives Epiphanius the opposite of his own view. The outline's Do not say rules this out ("The witnesses differ: … Epiphanius two").
- Bishop on Cyprus is HELD: "ἐπίσκοπον Κύπρου πόλεως Κωνσταντίας" (Pan 1248).
- Change: "About 375, Epiphanius, a bishop on Cyprus, wrote of them as two heresies, side by side: the Nazoraeans and the Ebionites."

**4. SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** "He calls it the Gospel according to the Hebrews, and says he put it into Greek and Latin." This sentence is framed by "In 392" in the sentence before it.
- In 392, Jerome calls the Beroea book Matthew's own Gospel in Hebrew: "Evangelium Christi Hebraicis litteris verbisque composuit… ipsum Hebraicum… Mihi quoque a Nazaraeis, qui in Beroea urbe Syriae hoc volumine utuntur, describendi facultas fuit" (PL23 48155–48162; VI 39352–39364). The name "the Gospel according to the Hebrews", and the translation into Greek and Latin, come from a different chapter, which does not tie that gospel to the Nazoraeans (Lives 2, PL23 ~48125 ff.; VI 39327–39329, "which I have recently translated into Greek and Latin").
- Jerome links the two only in an undated later work: "In Evangelio juxta Hebraeos, quod Chaldaico quidem Syroque sermone, sed Hebraicis litteris scriptum est, quo utuntur usque hodie Nazareni… sive, ut plerique autumant, juxta Matthaeum" (PL23 44505–44509).
- As written, the sentence moves that later identification onto 392 and drops Jerome's own label for the book, Matthew's. It also leaves the outline's "most take it for Matthew's" untold.
- Change: replace the two sentences with "In 392 the Latin scholar Jerome wrote that the Nazoraeans of Beroea, in Syria, had let him copy the gospel they used, written in Hebrew letters, which he took for Matthew's own. Elsewhere he calls it the Gospel according to the Hebrews, and he says he put it into Greek and Latin." Matthew stays in Jerome's voice, as the Do not say requires.

## SUMMARY HOLDS (no change needed)

- "Trypho asked whether some Christians said no." Trypho: "εἰ μήτι εἰσὶν οἱ λέγοντες ὅτι οὐ σωθήσονται οἱ τοιοῦτοι;" (JusG 2163–2164). "Christians" is the context's meaning: Justin's own side, set against "the Christians and the faithful" these believers choose to live with (ANF1 19921). Optional tightening, not blocking: "Trypho asked whether some said no."
- "They were Jews who believed… circumcision, the Sabbath, the food laws." Irenaeus, "they practise circumcision" (ANF1 33540); Eusebius, "The Sabbath and the rest of the discipline of the Jews they observed" (HE 18726); Origen, the food laws, "for the transgression of which we are accused… by the Ebionites" (ANF9 31918–31922).
- "In his last years Origen…" Eusebius: Against Celsus was written when Origen was "over sixty years old" (HE 37093–37105), and he died at sixty-nine (HE 39163).
- "One of them, Symmachus… a great book of columns…" "Symmachus was an Ebionite" (HE 34741). Origen "placed them opposite each other, with the Hebrew text itself… the so-called Hexapla" (HE 34731–34733).
- "In 404 Jerome wrote… the Pharisees… still condemned a sect commonly called the Nazoraeans." "a Pharisaeis huc usque damnatur, quos uulgo Nazaraeos nuncupant" (CSEL34 23236–23237). Jerome's name for the sect, "Minaeorum", is left out, which is fair. "Jewish teachers" is a gloss on the word. 404 is the NPNF date and the tree's census edge (AugE 33142).
- "He set down what they believed about Jesus as the church's own faith…" "in quem et nos credimus" (CSEL34 23270–23271).
