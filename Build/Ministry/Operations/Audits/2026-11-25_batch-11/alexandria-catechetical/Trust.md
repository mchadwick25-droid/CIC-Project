## BLOCKING findings

### 1. SOFTENED OR CHANGED: "bodily plenty"

Brief (para. 6): "As his opponents describe it, he taught that Christ would reign on this earth for a thousand years of bodily plenty."

Source: E-gr 10578, "χιλιάδα ἐτῶν τρυφῆς σωματικῆς ἐπὶ τῆς ξηρᾶς ταύτης"; E-en 41290, "a certain millennium of bodily luxury upon this earth". The "reign of Christ upon earth" is Dionysius's, E-en 41309-41310.

Τρυφή means luxury or indulgence. That is the opponents' charge, and the sentence credits it to them. "Plenty" is a neutral word, so it takes the charge's force away. The kit says an enemy's charge is given in its own content.

Change: "...for a thousand years of bodily luxury." The outline has room for this error. In Words to explain and in the Do not say line on Nepos, change "bodily plenty" to "bodily luxury". Add a Do not say line: "bodily plenty" → "bodily luxury" (E-gr 10578; E-en 41290).

### 2. SOFTENED OR CHANGED: three groups where the source names two; "examination of the book"

Brief (para. 7): "In his own account, the only one left to us, Dionysius called together the presbyters, the elders, and the teachers of the villages, and asked for a public examination of the book."

Source: E-en 41352-41354, "I called together the presbyters and teachers of the brethren in the villages,—such brethren as wished being also present,—and I exhorted them to make a public examination of this question." In the Greek, E-gr 10721-10723: "συγκαλέσας τοὺς πρεσβυτέρους καὶ διδασκάλους ... δημοσίᾳ τὴν ἐξέτασιν ποιήσασθαι τοῦ λόγου". The book comes only in the next clause: "when they brought me this book" (E-en 41355; E-gr 10723-10724).

There are two problems:
- (a) Heard once, "the presbyters, the elders, and the teachers" names three groups. Dionysius names two.
- (b) Dionysius asked them to examine the question (τοῦ λόγου), meaning the teaching. The villagers were the ones who brought the book.

Change: "...Dionysius called together the presbyters, or elders, and the teachers of the villages, and asked for a public examination of the teaching. They brought him the book like a weapon and a fortress no one could take, and he sat with them for three days, from morning to evening." The second sentence changes only "it" to "the book".

Do not say lines to add: "the presbyters, the elders, and the teachers" → "the presbyters, or elders, and the teachers" (E-en 41352). And "a public examination of the book" → "of the teaching" (E-gr 10722; E-en 41353-41354).

One was a catechumen, a learner not yet baptized: "Among the women, too, Herais was still a catechumen. She received baptism, as he himself says somewhere, the baptism by fire, and left this life.""

With this hook, the essence starts at about word 100, its first sentence is told by about 133, and the whole essence by about 169. ## Other non-HELD sentences (SUMMARY HOLDS, with evidence)

1. "Around 200, persecution under the emperor Severus had driven every teacher of the faith out of Alexandria, in Egypt." E-en 32672-32673, "there was no one at Alexandria to give instruction in the faith, as all were driven away by the threat of persecution"; E-gr 1217-1218, "ὑπὸ τῆς ἀπειλῆς τοῦ διωγμοῦ". The date is the tenth year of Severus (E-en 32523). The persecution was real, with martyrs, so the meaning holds. "The threat of" is closer to the source (it is in finding 3's hook).
2. "Pagans came instead ... took charge of the instruction." E-en 32673-32674, "some of the heathen came to him to hear the word of God"; E-en 32730-32731, "He was in his eighteenth year when he took charge of the catechetical school."
3. "Eusebius of Caesarea, writing about a century later from letters and from people who had known Origen, gives nearly all we know of these classrooms." E-en 32515-32517, "gathering them from certain letters, and from the statement of persons still living who were acquainted with him." "About a century later" is Widely Accepted: Eusebius writes of his own day at E-en 43201-43204. "Nearly all we know" fits the Library sweep in `Source_Sheet.md`, where every classroom event rests on Eusebius.
4. "From about 180 ... as the city's pagans learned grammar and philosophy, in what Eusebius calls a school." E-en 28488-28490 and 28561-28562, "the school of the faithful in Alexandria ... which continues to our day". The time is the first year of Commodus (E-en 28467-28476). For the city's own teachers: Origen's grammar school, E-en 32796-32813, and Heraclas "with the teacher of philosophic learning", E-en 35270-35276. Eusebius uses one word, διατριβή, for both kinds of school: the grammar school at E-gr 1215 and "τῆς αὐτῆς διατριβῆς" for the martyrs at E-gr 1608. So the likeness rests on the source's own wording.
