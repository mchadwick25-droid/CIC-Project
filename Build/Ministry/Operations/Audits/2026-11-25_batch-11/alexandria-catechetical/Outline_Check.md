# Outline check: Alexandrian Christianity (`alexandria-catechetical`)

## My own account of the heart (written before reading `Outline.md`)

From the census entry, the slate job, the built world (`cic-website/data/worlds/alexandria-catechetical.json`), the card and `Source_Sheet.md`.

- **Essence:** From about 180 into the early 300s, the Christians of Alexandria learned their faith the way their city learned everything, as a school. Teachers, from Pantaenus and Clement to Origen, Heraclas and Dionysius, led inquirers, catechumens (learners not yet baptized) and curious pagans step by step through Scripture, read for its plain sense and for a deeper sense about Christ and the soul. Almost all of it reaches us through Eusebius, writing a century on.
- **Who they were:** the Christians of Alexandria, teachers and learners together: catechumens, the newly baptized, educated pagans, women as well as men (Sheet C5, C6, C8).
- **When and where:** Alexandria, Egypt, from about 180 (Pantaenus, B1) through Origen's teaching from about 202 to 231 (C1, C10), Dionysius's headship and episcopate (C11, C12), to the villages of the Arsinoite district in the 250s or so (C15), and the school still running in Eusebius's day (C18).
- **What they faced:** the persecution under Severus (about 202): the teachers fled, an eighteen-year-old took the instruction, and his pupils died (B7, C1-C5); crowds too many for one teacher (C7); a quarrel inside Egypt over how to read Scripture, Nepos's book against the allegorists, which had split whole village churches (C13-C15). The teacher-bishop tension (Origen's departure, built world gravity) belongs mostly to the Origen story.
- **What they believed:** the Word, Jesus, is the one Instructor, who first urges, then trains, then teaches (D1, D2); faith is taught even without letters, its handbook is love (D3); Scripture has flesh, soul and spirit, for the simple, the advancing and the perfect (D5); the hope of resurrection and being made like Christ, against a thousand years of plenty on earth (C14).
- **How they practiced:** instruction before baptism; graded teaching (Heraclas the beginners, Origen the advanced; geometry and philosophy for the gifted, the basics for the less learned, all toward Scripture) (C7, C8); public examination of a dispute from Scripture over three days, minds changed when the reasons satisfied (C15-C17). The built world also names a second way (baptism, the Eucharist and the fasts reaching those who could not read); the sheet holds no lines for it, and Clement's "taught without letters" (D3) is the held line that keeps the unlettered in view.
- **What held them together:** the Word as the one teacher, and Scripture read as something that forms the reader in stages (built world gravities `logos-unity`, `scripture-formative`).
- **What came of them:** pupils became the city's teachers and bishops (Heraclas, Dionysius) (F1); the way of reading carried on through Basil and Gregory of Nazianzus and the Philocalia (F2); the school still running in Eusebius's day (F3). The census link to Desert Monasticism is not carried by the old sources tested (E, last item).

## Verdict: FIX

Seven BLOCKING findings, sixteen MINOR. The heart matches mine in substance. The sources hold the outline's account: the learners, the graded teaching, the reading in three senses, the pupils who became bishops, the three days at Arsinoe. Two parts of it go further than the sources (B4, and B1's "everything"). Every quote checks out. The beats follow the story once, in order, with the hook reaching ahead as the kit allows. The length cannot hold at 445 (B5).

Heart comparison. The outline's essence, who, when and where, what they faced, believed and practiced, and what came of them all match my account. Two differences. (a) Under "What held them together" it adds "a habit of settling a quarrel over reading by public argument rather than by decree". The record has one such event, told by Dionysius, and no source sets it against a decree (B4 below). (b) It leaves out the built world's first gravity, the Word as the one center (`alx.gravity.logos-unity`), though "What they believed" carries the Word as teacher (M15). Neither leaves out a major contribution. The census card's "why" puts the reading's later use first; the outline sends it to the card (M14).

## BLOCKING

**B1. The essence says more than any source: "the way their city learned everything".** None of the cited lines (B1, C1, C7, D5, C15) is about how the city learned, and no held line says "everything". The record does show the city's pagans learning grammar and philosophy from teachers: Origen ran a grammar school (E-en 32796-32813), Heraclas studied five years with "the teacher of philosophic learning" (E-en 35270-35276), and men "that followed learning and philosophy" came to Origen (E-en 32879-32881).
Correction, in The heart, essence: replace "learned their faith the way their city learned everything, from teachers, in what Eusebius calls a school." with "learned their faith from teachers, as the city's pagans learned grammar and philosophy, in what Eusebius calls a school." Add "C3, C4, C9" to its citation list. Add to Do not say: "the city of the great Library", "the Museum", "the learning capital of the world" → no held source; "as the city's pagans learned grammar and philosophy, from teachers" (E-en 32796-32813, 35270-35276).

**B2. Opening 1 adds a detail: "a young woman, Herais".** Eusebius writes "And of women, Herais died while yet a catechumen" (E-en 32921-32925). The Greek is "καὶ γυναικῶν δὲ Ἡραῒς ἔτι κατηχουμένη" (E-gr 1613). Neither gives her age.
Correction, in Openings 1: replace "and a young woman, Herais, who had not yet been baptized." with "and a woman, Herais, who had not yet been baptized." Add to Do not say: "a young woman" / "a girl" for Herais → "a woman, Herais" (E-gr 1613).

**B3. Beat 1 implies something the source does not say: "He wrote for those who could not read as well."** Clement answers the excuse of someone who says, "I have not learned letters". He tells that person that faith "is taught without letters" (Cl 26367-26372). He does not write for people who cannot read. A listener would hear a contradiction.
Correction, in Beat 1: replace "He wrote for those who could not read as well." with "He wrote that faith was taught to those who could not read as well." Add to Do not say: "Clement wrote for the unlettered" → "Clement wrote that faith is taught without letters" (Cl 26369-26371).

**B4. A link no source draws, in the Connection field and the heart.** The Connection field says Beat 4 shows "the classroom's habit of question and answer carried into a quarrel" (citing E-en 41322-41324, 41361-41369). Those lines are Dionysius on the three days. No held source says Origen's classroom taught by question and answer, or that Dionysius took his way of arguing from it. The heart's "a habit of settling a quarrel over reading by public argument rather than by decree", and What came of it's "against settling it by decree", make one event into a habit and add a contrast no source holds. (The outline's own rule for the reading and the division, "draw no link between them", applies here too.) The Connection field's second item, "the same pattern of teaching reached the countryside", is the same kind of inference from the single word "teachers" (E-en 41352-41353).
Corrections:
- In The heart, What held them together: replace "and a habit of settling a quarrel over reading by public argument rather than by decree (C14-C16)." with "and, when a book against that reading split the village churches, three days of public examination of it from Scripture, as Dionysius tells it (C14-C16)."
- In What came of it: replace "Marked as defended: the public examination of a disputed reading, against settling it by decree (C14, C16)." with "Marked as defended: the deeper reading of the promises, and the hope of the resurrection and of being made like Christ, against Nepos's thousand years (E-en 41322-41341); defended by a public examination from Scripture (E-en 41349-41372)."
- In Connection, first item: replace "say it in Beat 3, so Beat 4 shows the classroom's habit of question and answer carried into a quarrel (E-en 41322-41324, 41361-41369)." with "say it in Beat 3, so the listener knows, when Beat 4 comes, that the bishop who sat three days with the villages had been a pupil in that classroom. Draw no link between the classroom's teaching and how he argued; no source makes it."
- In Connection, second item: delete "the same pattern of teaching reached the countryside." and keep "Let the word stand; do not explain it further."
- Add to Do not say: "the Alexandrians always settled quarrels by argument" / "Dionysius argued as he had learned in Origen's classroom" / "rather than by decree" → tell what Dionysius did and said (E-en 41343-41381).

**B6. Beat 4 names the quarrel but not why it mattered to the side the record gives.** Part 1 (Explain it) asks why a quarrel mattered to the people in it. ## MINOR

**M1.** The hook: "from Origen's letters and from people who had known him". Eusebius says "from certain letters, and from the statement of persons still living who were acquainted with him" (E-en 32515-32517); he does not say whose letters. In Hook, replace "from Origen's letters and from people who had known him" with "from letters and from people who had known Origen". Make the same change in Who tells it, Eusebius.

**M2.** Dates. Opening 1 and Shape say the pupils died "about 202". Eusebius dates Leonides's death to Severus's tenth year (E-en 32523-32533). He puts the pupils' deaths after Origen, in his eighteenth year, took the instruction (E-en 32732-32735), "during the persecution under Aquila". In Openings 1, replace "The pupils who died, about 202." with "The pupils who died, soon after 202." In Shape, replace "about 202" with "around 200". Add to Do not say: "in 202 his pupils died" → "around 200" / "in those years".

**M3.** Beat 2 places the reading in three senses in Origen's Alexandrian years but gives no source for the time. Eusebius says Origen wrote On First Principles "before leaving Alexandria" (HE 6.24.3, E-en 36068-36092). Add that citation after "(Ph 76; Documented)" in Beat 2.

**M4.** Quote 1 puts a name inside the quotation marks: "as Origen himself says somewhere". The Greek is αὐτός, "he himself" (E-gr 1614). Replace it in the rendering with "as he himself says somewhere". The narrator names Origen just before the quote (Opening 1 already does). My own rendering: "And among the women, Herais, still a catechumen, received baptism (as he himself says somewhere), the baptism through fire, and departed this life." It agrees with the outline's in sense.

**M5.** Quote 3. My rendering of E-gr 10734-10735: "nor were we ashamed, if the reasoning prevailed, to change our minds and agree." It matches the outline's. Schwartz's apparatus (E-gr 10747, "ἐποιούμεϑα τὸ μὲν] ἐποιοῦντο μὲν … TrRe") shows that some witnesses have "they" for "we" in the governing verb. Add to the Quote 3 source line: "Some manuscripts read 'they' (the brethren) for 'we' in the governing verb (E-gr 10747); Schwartz prints 'we'." The Do not say line "do not say who changed" already covers the risk.

**M6.** Quote 2, "for hearing is not taught". Wilson's English reads "for it is not taught" (Cl 26368), and the Greek is not held. Rendering "it" as "hearing" settles an ambiguity. If item 10 of B5 is applied, the span drops out. If not, add to its source line: "'it' read as hearing; Greek not held".

**M7.** Beat 4: "the psalms the brethren still sang". The source says "his extensive psalmody, with which many of the brethren are still delighted" (E-en 41314-41315). Replace it with "the many psalms of his that the brethren still delighted in". Add Do not say: "the brethren still sang Nepos's psalms" → "still delighted in" (E-en 41315).

**M8.** Beat 4: "had split whole churches from the rest". The source says "schisms and apostasies of entire churches" (E-en 41349-41351). Delete "from the rest".

**M9.** Ending: "Coracion, the leader of Nepos's party". The source says "the author and mover of this teaching" (E-en 41374-41375). In Ending and Beat 4, replace "the leader of Nepos's party" with "who had led this teaching in the villages".

**M10.** What came of it, item 2, says "about 382 Gregory sent a friend". The date comes from the editor's heading (GN 47442-47443; modern), as the sheet says (F2). Part 1 keeps a date made only by a modern work off the card. Delete "about 382". Mark the line "Documented (that Gregory sent it); the date is the editor's, not used".

**M11.** The approved-length line cites `Length_Count.md` as if it were in this folder. It is at `Build/Ministry/Operations/Audits/2026-11-25_batch-11/Length_Count.md`. Give the path.

**M12.** Beat 3: "When Origen left for Caesarea". That is Eusebius's own wording at HE 6.26 (E-en 36330-36332), so it is right not to give a reason. Add to Do not say: "Origen moved on to new work" or any reason for the move → "Origen left for Caesarea" only; the break with his bishop is the Origen story's.

**M13.** Hook: "every teacher of the faith". The source says "there was no one at Alexandria to give instruction in the faith, as all were driven away" (E-en 32671-32673). That holds. Add to Do not say: "the teachers fled in fear" → "all were driven away by the threat of persecution" (E-en 32672-32673).

**M14.** Ranking in What came of it. The kit weighs what later witnesses carried on and used. A later witness used the reading itself: Socrates says Basil and Gregory "drew from them the right interpretation of the sacred Scriptures" (Soc 13913-13915). The pupils' succession is only reported, by Eusebius. Swap items 1 and 2, keeping the "Told in Beat 3" and "Card" marks as they are. If B5's length ruling allows, carry a clause of it in the essence (the essence may reach ahead). Otherwise it stays on the card.

**M15.** What held them together: the note "(The built world's gravities: Scripture as formation, learning as formation.)" leaves out the world's first gravity, `alx.gravity.logos-unity`. Replace the note with "(The built world's gravities: the Word as the one center, Scripture as formation, learning as formation.)" and cite D1, D2.
