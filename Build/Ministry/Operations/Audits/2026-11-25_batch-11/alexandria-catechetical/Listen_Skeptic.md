## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing

- **BLOCKING (event told twice).** "When village churches rejected that reading, Dionysius, once a pupil there, sat with them for three days and argued it through." This tells the ending in the essence. The story then tells the same three days in full at Arsinoe. The bar counts an event told twice as a failure on one hearing, and Dionysius is introduced twice ("once a pupil there", then "another of Origen's pupils"). **Change:** cut the sentence. Make the link to the school where Nepos's book is told, for example "against those who, like Alexandria's teachers, read Scripture for hidden meanings", if the trust read finds the sources hold it.
- MINOR. "in Origen's own phrase: 'Among the women, too, Herais was still a catechumen. She received baptism, as he himself says somewhere, the baptism by fire...'" Meaning lost: who is "he"? The lead-in says Origen is speaking, but the quote cites Origen in the third person. "Baptism by fire" also sounds like the English idiom for a hard first try, so a second-language listener can miss that she was burned. **Change:** "He tells of one of the dead, Herais, a catechumen... She received, in Origen's phrase, 'the baptism by fire', and left this life", or keep the quote but credit Eusebius, and state plainly that she was burned.
- MINOR. "Faith is taught without letters. Its handbook, unpolished and divine at once, is called love, a spiritual book." Meaning lost: the story has just described a school of letters, and the listener hears a contradiction with no key. Give its plain sense next to it (Part 1 allows that), or cut it.
- MINOR. "called together the presbyters, the elders, and the teachers of the villages." On one hearing this sounds like three groups. **Change:** "the presbyters, or elders, and the teachers of the villages."
