### B1. Heart told late; stock how-we-know sentence (Trust, Trust second, Skeptic)

The hook runs five sentences and 118 words, against the outline's plan of 75. The essence is not finished until about word 188. The Eusebius sentence is also a stand-alone how-we-know sentence (Part 1). Fix both with one three-sentence hook:

> "Around 200, the threat of persecution under the emperor Severus had driven every teacher of the Christian faith out of Alexandria, in Egypt, and with no one else left to teach, pagans came to Origen, then in his eighteenth year, to hear the word of God. Nearly all we know of these classrooms comes from Eusebius of Caesarea, writing about a century later from letters and from people who had known Origen, and he tells of the pupils who were put to death. One was a catechumen, or learner not yet baptized, and Eusebius borrows Origen's own phrase for her death: "Among the women, too, Herais was still a catechumen. She received baptism, as he himself says somewhere, the baptism by fire, and left this life.""
