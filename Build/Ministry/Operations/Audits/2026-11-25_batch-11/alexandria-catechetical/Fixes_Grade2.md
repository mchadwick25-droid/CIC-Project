## What the sources hold

1. **That her death was her baptism.** Eusebius, Church History 6.4.3, citing Origen. E-gr 1613-1614: "γυναικῶν δὲ Ἡραΐς ἔτι κατηχουμένη τὸ βάπτισμα, ὥς πού φησιν αὐτός, τὸ διὰ πυρὸς λαβοῦσα, τὸν βίον ἐξελήλυθεν" (OCR normalized: "Hoais", "χατηχουμένη"). E-en 32923-32925: "Herais died while yet a catechumen, receiving baptism by fire, as Origen himself somewhere says." Rufinus's Latin, E-gr 1698-1700: "Hera quaedam catechumena, de qua ipse in quodam loco ait, quod ignis baptisma consecuta sit." Later (Eusebius a century on), quoting Origen's own phrase. Widely Accepted. She was still unbaptized; the baptism she received was the one "through fire", and with it she left this life. So Origen counted her death as her baptism: the plain sense of the clause, adding nothing to it.
2. **Whether she was burned to death.** Not stated. Eusebius gives a manner of death for the others in the list (Heraclides and Hero "beheaded", E-en 32914-32916; the second Serenus, E-en 32917-32918), but none for Herais beyond the phrase. In the same passage he uses the same words, διὰ πυρὸς, "through fire", for Serenus's proof of faith (E-gr 1607; E-en 32905-32906, "gave through fire a proof") and for Potamiaena and her mother, "perfected through fire" (E-gr 1621; E-en 32959-32961, "was put to death by fire"). The phrase points to burning; Eusebius does not say it outright. Inferential-Thin (the sheet's own rating, C5, kept). The brief says this plainly as an inference, in the narrator's voice, and does not state it as fact.

No held source says more about how she died.

## Cuts, and where each goes

- **1a.** "own" and "for her death": Leave out. The new sentence after the quote says what the phrase was for; Origen stays credited ("Origen's phrase", "as he himself says").
- **1b.** "one after another": Leave out; "the crowds came from morning to evening" keeps the press of pupils.
- **1c.** "or to study Scripture himself" (E-en 34408-34409): card. "Scarcely left him time to breathe" keeps the reason he divided them.
- **1d.** "for his faith and his work on Scripture" (E-en 41312-41320): card, beside the psalms (Cut first 5). "He loved Nepos" stays, as Cut first 5 requires.
- **1e.** "of the question": Leave out; the next sentence names what was examined (the book).
