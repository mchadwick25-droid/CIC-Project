## alexandria-catechetical
Heart: In Alexandria, from about 180 to the mid-200s, teachers such as Clement and Origen led pagans and catechumens step by step through Scripture, read for a deeper sense beneath the plain story; Origen's pupils carried the school on, and Dionysius defended that reading against Nepos's earthly thousand-year kingdom.

## antiochene-church-third-century
Heart: The third-century church of Antioch, one church under a bishop bound by letters to others, was troubled by its bishop Paul of Samosata, who taught that Christ was from below, an ordinary man, until the bishops of the East put him out; later Arius's opponents traced a line from Paul through Lucian to Arius.

## roman-church-third-century
Heart: The large, mixed church of Rome in the early 200s argued in public over Father and Son, over taking back grave sinners, and over marriage, until in 251 a stricter party under Novatian broke away over whether the fallen could be taken back.

## apocryphal-and-pseudepigraphal-literature
Heart: From the second to fourth centuries Christians wrote and read gospels and acts under the apostles' names that never entered the New Testament; congregations read and argued over them, and bishops and teachers judged them against what had been handed down from the apostles.

## ebionite-nazoraean-current
Heart: From Justin's day to about 400, church writers described Jews who believed Jesus was the Christ yet kept the Law of Moses; known only through their opponents, who disagreed on what they believed about Jesus, they were called Ebionites and Nazoraeans and held to be neither Jews nor Christians.

## greek-apologists-second-century
Heart: Second-century Christians writing in Greek put defenses of their faith before emperors and pagans, answering charges of godlessness, cannibalism and incest by pointing to the one God, their prayers for the emperors, and how they lived.

## marcion-marcionism
Heart: For about three hundred years, the Marcionite churches that followed Marcion after Rome cast him out kept their own Scripture, clergy and martyrs, holding that Jesus came from a good Father above the God who made the world, until emperors' laws and bishops worked them away.

## post-apostolic-house-church
Heart: In the century after the apostles, Christians met household by household, taught newcomers a way of life before baptism, gave thanks over bread and cup on the Lord's day under elders, bishops and deacons, and were held together by letters and messengers between churches.

## syriac-edessa-nisibis
Heart: The Syriac-speaking Christians of Edessa and Nisibis, frontier cities between Rome and Persia, from 201 to 410, sang their teaching in hymns, counted men and women vowed to God for life, and endured persecution in Persia until the synod of 410.

## latin-pastoral-congregational-christianity
Heart: From the 250s to 430, the Latin Christians of Roman Africa lived as congregations around one bishop, at Carthage under Cyprian and Hippo under Augustine, and under persecution, plague and a rival church kept asking what a bishop and his people owed each other in danger.
