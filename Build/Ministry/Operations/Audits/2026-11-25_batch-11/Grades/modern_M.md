## alexandria-catechetical
Heart: In Alexandria, teachers such as Clement and Origen led pagans and learners step by step through Scripture toward its deeper sense, and Dionysius carried that teaching into a patient public examination that won over Nepos's followers.
Findings: 0

## antiochene-church-third-century
Heart: The church of Antioch, bound to churches across the empire, judged and finally put out its own bishop Paul for denying that the Son came down from heaven, and its later presbyter Lucian left both martyrs' witness and pupils tied to Arius.
Findings: 0

## roman-church-third-century
Heart: Third-century Rome was a large, mixed church that argued in public over God, forgiveness and marriage, and under persecution split over whether the fallen could come back.
Findings: 0

## apocryphal-and-pseudepigraphal-literature
Heart: Christians wrote and read books under the apostles' names, and bishops and teachers judged them against what had been handed down from the apostles, even as some, like Thecla's story, went on being read.
Findings: 0

## ebionite-nazoraean-current
Heart: Jews who believed Jesus was the Christ and kept the Law of Moses are known only through church writers who mostly argued against them and did not agree on what they believed.
Findings: 0

## greek-apologists-second-century
Heart: Second-century Christians writing in Greek answered charges of godlessness, cannibalism and incest by pleading to emperors and pagans that they worshipped the one God, prayed for the rulers, and should be judged by how they lived.
Findings: 0

## latin-apologists
Heart: Latin-speaking Christians of Rome and Africa defended their faith to the educated who despised it, answering the charges out of the pagans' own books.
Findings: 0

## marcion-marcionism
Heart: The Marcionites, known only through their enemies, kept their own churches, Scripture and martyrs for some three centuries, holding that Jesus came from a good Father above the God who made the world.
Findings: 0

## post-apostolic-house-church
Heart: In the century after the apostles, household churches taught a way of life before baptism, gave thanks over bread and cup on the Lord's day under elders, bishops and deacons, and were held together by letters and messengers.
Findings: 0

## syriac-edessa-nisibis
Heart: The Syriac-speaking Christians of Edessa and Nisibis, on the frontier between Rome and Persia, sang their faith in hymns, kept vowed lives for God, and endured flood, siege and persecution.
Findings: 0

## latin-pastoral-congregational-christianity
Heart: The Latin Christians of Roman Africa lived as congregations around one bishop who taught, fed, judged and stayed with them through persecution, plague, schism and invasion.
Findings: 0
