## alexandria-catechetical
Heart: From about 180, Alexandria's Christians learned their faith from teachers in a school, led step by step and taught to read Scripture for a deeper sense beneath the plain story, as Clement and Origen did. Their heirs settled a dispute over Nepos's earthly-reign teaching by open, patient examination.

## antiochene-church-third-century
Heart: Antioch's third-century church was one church bound to others by letters. Its bishops met again and again and finally cut off their own bishop, Paul of Samosata, for teaching that Christ was an ordinary man, and a later bishop traced a line from Paul through Lucian to Arius.

## roman-church-third-century
Heart: Rome's large, mixed third-century church argued in public over Father and Son, over taking back grave sinners, and over marriage. The question of the fallen split it in 251, when Novatian's stricter party set up a rival bishop and Rome's synod chose to heal the fallen through repentance.

## marcion-marcionism
Heart: For about three hundred years the Marcionites gathered as their own churches, with a cut-down Luke and ten letters of Paul, holding that Jesus came from a good Father above the God who made the world. Every witness to them was an enemy.

## post-apostolic-house-church
Heart: In the century after the apostles, Christians met by households, taught a way of life before baptism, gave thanks over bread and cup on the Lord's day, were led by elders, bishops and deacons, and were held together by letters and messengers.

## syriac-edessa-nisibis
Heart: Syriac-speaking Christians of Edessa and Nisibis, on the frontier between Rome and Persia, sang their teaching in hymns and counted members vowed to God for life. They lived through persecution in Persia until its church met in synod in 410.

## latin-pastoral-congregational-christianity
Heart: From the 250s to 430, Latin-speaking African Christians lived as congregations around one bishop who taught, fed, judged and reconciled them. Under persecution, plague and a rival church, they kept asking what a bishop and his people owed each other in danger.
