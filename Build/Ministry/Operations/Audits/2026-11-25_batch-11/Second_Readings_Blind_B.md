# Second Readings, Blind (B)

Range B11-14 to B11-26. Each passage was rendered from the lines given, and from the original language where the source has one.

## B11-14
Latin (an Irenaeus quotation in Holl's apparatus), lines 24980-24981: "uti et Hierosolymam adorent quasi sit domus dei".

Rendering: "so that they even worship Jerusalem as if it were the house of God."

Scan note: "Hierosolymam" is split across the line break ("Hiero- / solymam"). The text is otherwise clear.

## B11-15
Latin (Jerome, quoting the Gospel according to the Hebrews), lines 48133-48136.

Rendering: "He took bread, blessed it and broke it. He gave it to James the Just and said to him: 'My brother, eat your bread, for the Son of Man has risen from among those who sleep.'"

Scan note: the "a" before "dormientibus" is printed as the Greek "ἃ". It reads plainly as Latin "a" ("from").

## B11-16
Latin (Jerome to Augustine), lines 23272-23275.

Rendering: "...Christ, the Son of God, born of the Virgin Mary. They say he is the one who suffered under Pontius Pilate and rose again, the one in whom we also believe. But because they want to be both Jews and Christians, they are neither Jews nor Christians."

Note: the passage opens mid-sentence. "Christum" is the object of a verb that comes before line 23272, so it is not part of the given lines. The sentence goes on after "Christiani" with a new sentence ("oro ergo te..."), which falls outside the passage.

## B11-17
English (NPNF translation of Melito, quoted by Eusebius), lines 25826-25829.

Rendering: "If you have ordered these things, so be it. A just ruler would never take unjust measures. And we gladly accept the honor of such a death."

## B11-18
Greek (Athenagoras, Plea), lines 1546-1550.

Rendering: "But we hold that watching a man being killed is close to killing him, so we have given up such shows. We do not even watch, so that we bring no guilt or pollution on ourselves. How, then, could we commit murder?"

## B11-19
Greek (Letter to Diognetus 5.5), lines 312-317.

Rendering: "They live in their own home countries, but as resident foreigners. They take part in everything as citizens, and they put up with everything as strangers. Every foreign land is their homeland, and every homeland is a foreign land."

## B11-20
Latin (Minucius Felix, Octavius), lines 826-827.

Rendering: "a people that hides away and shuns the light, silent in public, chattering in corners."

## B11-21
Latin (Jerome's Chronicle, quoted in a footnote by Monceaux), lines 21713-21718.

Rendering: "...when he could not obtain from the bishop admission to the faith he had always attacked, he worked late into the night and wrote brilliant books against his former religion. At last, with these as a kind of hostages for his devotion, he won the bond of the faithful."

Scan notes: "ah" reads as "ab" ("from"), "religionein" as "religionem", "fœdus" as "foedus". The quotation opens with "Cum..." and an ellipsis in the footnote itself, so the subject is not named in these lines. "fidem" here means being received into the faith; "pietatis foedus" is literally "the covenant of piety".

## B11-22
Latin (Arnobius, Against the Nations 1.39), lines 1132-1135.

Rendering: "Whenever I caught sight of a stone made slippery and smeared with olive oil, I flattered it as if a living power were inside it. I spoke to it and asked favors of a stump of wood that felt nothing."

## B11-23
Latin (Augustine, Letter 29), lines 6521-6524.

Rendering: "I did not move them to tears with my own tears. But as these things were said, I admit, their weeping came first and I could not hold back mine. And when we had wept together, I brought my sermon to an end, full of hope that they would mend their ways."

Scan note: a stray mark ("•r>") stands before "plenissima" at the line start; it is a scan artifact, not text.

## B11-24
Latin (Cyprian, Letter 14), lines 2293-2296.

Rendering: "I could not write back on my own. From the start of my time as bishop I resolved to do nothing by my own private judgment, without your advice and without the consent of the people."

## B11-25
Latin (Augustine, Letter 228, quoted in Possidius' Life of Augustine), lines 4556-4561.

Rendering: "But if the ministers are present, everyone is helped, as far as the strength the Lord supplies them allows. Some are baptized, some are reconciled, and none is cheated of communion in the Lord's body. All are comforted, built up and urged to pray to God, who is able to turn aside everything they fear."

Scan note: "X5" at the start of a line is a misread line number, not text.

## B11-26
English (Sachau's translation of al-Biruni), lines 29121-29122.

Rendering: "This man has come forward and is calling on people to destroy the world."
