## 1. The test (a tradition)

- **Who these people were:** filled. "the Christians of Antioch, in Roman Syria, were one church under a bishop, with presbyters, its priests, and deacons, bound by letters to churches across the empire."
- **What happened to them:** filled. Their bishop was charged, the bishops met three times, Malchion questioned him, he was cut off, the emperor gave the house to the other side, Lucian was killed.
- **What their faith was about:** filled, by contrast. They sang psalms to Christ and held that "the Son of God came down from heaven"; Paul called him "a man from below".
- **Why the connection matters:** **NOT FILLED. BLOCKING.** The story ends on Arius, but I never learn who Arius was, what he taught, or what his quarrel was about. "the bishop of Alexandria who later fought Arius", "Arius and his party", "A historian on Arius's side": what side? What fight? So "fellow-Lucianist" lands as a curious name. I cannot say why it matters that Arius was Lucian's pupil. Fixed by the changes under item 4.

## 2. The opening

After the first three sentences (the narrator's line and the first two of the quote), I know **who**: Paul of Samosata, bishop of Antioch, and the bishops who judged him. I have **where** only as the name Antioch. "Roman Syria" comes in the second paragraph. **When** is missing until "In the later third century". **Why** they judged him is missing; I hear only the complaint about the singing. I am not lost, because the second paragraph fills the gaps at once. Not blocking.

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing

1. **BLOCKING:** "The bishop of Alexandria who later fought Arius charged that Lucian was Paul's successor and stayed out of communion for many years, and that Arius and his party came from the same stock." What I lose: who Arius is (first heard here); whether "successor" means Lucian became bishop after Paul (that is what I first heard); what "same stock" means. This sentence carries the link to the ending, so the thread breaks here. Change: tell "successor" in its plain sense, so it cannot be heard as the next bishop. Tell "same stock" in its plain sense. The trust read must confirm both wordings against the source. Introduce Arius as item 4 says.
2. MINOR: "The letter's other charges are the bishops' own." I do not know what this means. I guess at "these are only their accusations". Change: cut the sentence, and begin the next one "The bishops also wrote that Paul built himself a high throne and a curtained private room, like a magistrate."
3. MINOR: "Everyone grieved in private, and no one dared accuse him." I have just heard bishops meeting again and again to accuse him, so this seems to contradict it. Change: "In his own church, they wrote, people grieved in private, and no one dared accuse him."
4. MINOR: "The emperor Aurelian was petitioned, and he ordered the house given to those to whom the bishops of Italy and Rome wrote." I lose "to those to whom" and get back only part of the meaning from the next sentence. Change: "...and he ordered that the house go to the side the bishops of Italy and Rome wrote to."
5. MINOR: "Lucian ... studied the Scriptures so closely that long after ... copies still bore his name." Copies of what? Change: "copies of the Scriptures still bore his name."
6. MINOR: "Driven out of Alexandria by his bishop, Arius wrote to one of them, Eusebius of Nicomedia, ..." I have already met a Eusebius, the historian, so I think it is the same man. Change: "...Arius wrote to one of them, a different Eusebius, of Nicomedia, ..."
