## The heart

The heart is the right one for this subject. The sources show a church under a bishop with presbyters and deacons, its neighbouring churches round it (E-Eng 42025-42026), bound by letters to "all the provinces" (E-Eng 41993-42001). Its crisis is the long fight with its own bishop, settled by those letters (E-Eng 42274-42314) and by the emperor's ruling that turned on them (E-Gk 12116-12120). The essence is complete by the end of paragraph 2, at word 145, inside the first minute (150).

## BLOCKING findings

**1. Paragraph 2, sentence 2: ASSUMPTION ADDED (also changed).**
"When their own bishop was charged with denying that the Son of God came down from heaven, the bishops of the East met again and again to put him out."
- "To put him out" gives a purpose the source does not give. Eusebius says they met "at different times and frequently to consider these matters" (E-Eng 41894-41896; E-Gk 11457-11462). Firmilian, at those earlier meetings, believed Paul's promise "and hoped that ... what was necessary would be done. So he delayed the matter" (E-Eng 42056-42060). Only the last meeting put him out (E-Eng 41951-41954).
- "Denying" is stronger than the charge. The letter says "he is unwilling to acknowledge that the Son of God has come down from heaven" (E-Eng 42173-42175), in Greek "οὐ βούλεται συνομολογεῖν ἐξ οὐρανοῦ κατεληλυθέναι" (E-Gk 11903-11904): he would not confess it.
- **Change:** "When their own bishop was charged with refusing to confess that the Son of God came down from heaven, the bishops of the East met again and again over him, until at last they put him out."

**2. Paragraph 3, sentence 3: SOFTENED OR CHANGED.**
"They wrote that Paul called Jesus Christ a man from below, and Eusebius says he held that Christ was by nature an ordinary man."
- The bishops quote him as saying "'Jesus Christ is from below'" (E-Eng 42177-42178); the Greek is "Ἰησοῦν Χριστὸν κάτωθεν" (E-Gk 11907). There is no "man" in it. The word comes from Eusebius's separate frame (T1), and here it is moved into the bishops' quotation. The second clause is HELD: "in his nature he was a common man" (E-Eng 41838-41840; E-Gk 11433-11435, "κοινοῦ τὴν φύσιν ἀνθρώπου").
- **Change:** "They wrote that Paul said Jesus Christ was from below, and Eusebius says he held that Christ was by nature an ordinary man."

**3. Paragraph 5, sentence 5: SOFTENED OR CHANGED.**
"Everyone grieved in private, and no one dared accuse him."
- The source: "Because of these things all mourn and lament by themselves; but they so fear his tyranny and power, that they dare not accuse him" (E-Eng 42243-42245). The reason the bishops give, fear of his tyranny and power, is dropped, and with it the charge's force.
- **Change:** "Everyone grieved in private, they wrote, but feared his tyranny and power too much to accuse him."

**4. Paragraph 7, sentence 3: ASSUMPTION ADDED.**
"He left the teaching alone and let those bishops' letters decide, and Paul was driven out by the worldly power."
- No source says the emperor left the teaching alone. Eusebius gives only the order: the house to go to those "οἷς ἂν οἱ κατὰ τὴν Ἰταλίαν καὶ τὴν Ῥωμαίων πόλιν ἐπίσκοποι τοῦ δόγματος ἐπιστέλλοιεν" (E-Gk 12119-12120), "to whom the bishops of the teaching in Italy and the city of Rome should write". The ruling itself names the bishops "of the teaching". Saying he set the teaching aside is the outline's reading (Source_Sheet 2e, "He judged who held communion, not what was true about Christ"), not Eusebius. It reads as an attitude or reason. The rest holds: "driven out of the church, with extreme disgrace, by the worldly power" (E-Eng 42329-42330; E-Gk 12120-12122).
- **Change:** "So those bishops' letters decided it, and Paul was driven out of the church by the worldly power."
- **Do not say** line for the outline: "The emperor left the teaching alone / did not judge the teaching" → "He ordered the house given to those to whom the bishops of Italy and Rome wrote" (A1). Replace the outline's own "the emperor did not judge the teaching" in beat 5 to match.

Do not say lines for the outline from findings 1-3:
- "Paul called Christ a man from below" → "the bishops quote him: 'Jesus Christ is from below'" (T2); "ordinary man" is Eusebius's word only (T1).
- "Paul denied that the Son came down" → "the bishops charge that he would not confess it" (T2). (The outline's essence says "denying": change it to match.)
- "The bishops met to put him out" for the earlier meetings → "they met often over him; the last meeting put him out" (G5, G6, M1).
- "No one dared accuse him" without the reason → "they so feared his tyranny and power" (C9).
