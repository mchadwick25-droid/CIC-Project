# Outline check: The Church of Antioch in the Third Century

## My own account of the heart (written before reading Outline.md)

From the census entry (Card.existing.json), the slate job and Source_Sheet.md, to the tradition kind kit's fields.

- **The essence:** The Christians of Antioch, the great city of the Roman East, in the later third century: one city church with its bishop, presbyters and deacons, tied by letters of communion to the town churches round it and to churches across the world. Their story is the fight with their own bishop, Paul of Samosata, whom they and the visiting bishops judged to have denied that the Son of God came down from heaven, and who held the church house until an emperor ruled on it.
- **Who they were:** the church of Antioch; its people (who had their own word for the women in clergy houses, S4), clergy, Malchion the teacher of rhetoric made presbyter (M2), Dorotheus the Hebrew-reading presbyter (L16), Lucian the presbyter and his pupils.
- **When and where:** Antioch on the Orontes and its Syrian hinterland, from Paul's accession (S1) to Lucian's death at Nicomedia under Maximinus (L2, L3). No year for the last synod (D1).
- **What they faced:** a bishop with worldly power and a following (C1-C9), whom many feared to accuse (C9); bishops coming again and again, Firmilian twice and dying on the third journey (G6, G7); Paul hiding his teaching until Malchion drew him out before shorthand writers (G5, M2); deposition and Domnus appointed (M6); Paul refusing to leave the house until Aurelian gave it to those the bishops of Italy and Rome wrote to (A1); later, persecution in which Lucian died (L2, L3).
- **What they believed:** that the Son of God came down from heaven (T2, the charge's ground); psalms sung to Christ (C6); a church that listened "reverently and orderly as in the house of God" (C4). Paul's side survives only in enemies' words (T1-T4).
- **How they practiced:** psalms to Christ, the Easter feast (C6); synods of bishops; letters of communion sent and received (M6); preaching and expounding Scripture (L16), Lucian's study of the Scriptures (L1).
- **What held them together:** the communion of churches, shown by letters, which is both what the bishops cut Paul off from and what Aurelian's ruling turned on; and the confession of the Son come down from heaven.
- **What came of them, heaviest first:** Lucian's name and circle, claimed by Arius and Eusebius of Nicomedia as "fellow-Lucianists" (L5) and the pupils who became bishops (L6), teachers of Aetius (L7), and invoked against "consubstantial" (L14, L15); the council's refusal of homoousios, cited in the 350s and answered by Athanasius, Hilary and Basil with differing reasons (Contested in reason); the Paulianists as a separate body to Nicaea's canon 19; Paul's name as a byword. Lucian's text passing to the later Antiochene exegetes is Inferential-Thin: out.

## Verdict: FIX

One BLOCKING finding (the Connection field and beat 6 misstate and halve Alexander's charge). The rest are MINOR.

**Heart.** The outline's heart matches mine in substance: the church as one body bound by letters of communion, the fight with Paul, the ruling on the house, and Lucian's circle as what came of it. No major contribution is left out. The council's refusal of "homoousios" and the Paulianists are rightly held for the card under the slate's ending. The beats tell the heart in the first minute (opening Q1, then the essence), then follow the story once, in order: accession, the bishops' visits, the charges, Malchion and the deposition, the house, Lucian. No event falls in two beats, and the ending is not told early.

**Quotes, against my own renderings.** Q1 (E-Gk 11896-11900) is faithful. "As if" fairly renders ὡς δή, and "trains" renders παρασκευάζων. Q2 (Epiph-Gk 45744-45746; Thdt 6063-6064) is faithful. It stops before "ἀληθῶς Εὐσέβιε" ("truly Eusebius", a pun on the name), which is an acceptable stop. Q3 (Chrys 15435-15440) is faithful to the Latin. "What is your homeland?" compresses "From what homeland were you born?" without changing the sense. Each quote stands on what the outline says stands behind it.

**Length.** Less its seven Cut first items, the plan runs about 531 words (Length_Count.md; my spot count of the three quotes gives about 101). That fits the approved 535. No cut drops what a named thing was about or why it happened.

## Findings

### 1. BLOCKING: the Connection field and beat 6 misstate Alexander's charge

The Connection field says "no old source says Lucian taught what Arius taught". Alexander does say so, as an enemy. Right after the Lucian sentence (Thdt 5759-5770), he writes that Arius and Achillas "have greedily sucked down the dregs of this impiety, offsets of the same stock" (Thdt 5772-5775). That puts Arius in a line running from Paul through Lucian. Beat 6 gives only the first half of the charge. Part 1 requires an enemy's charge to be told in its own content.

**Corrections:**

- **Connection.** Replace "The listener needs to understand that the link is the teacher, through the pupils' own word for themselves: no old source says Lucian taught what Arius taught, and the story must not say so." with:

  "The listener needs to understand that the link is the teacher, through the pupils' own word for themselves. Only Arius's enemy Alexander says the teaching ran on from Paul through Lucian to Arius. He calls Arius and his party 'offsets of the same stock' (Thdt 5772-5775). The story tells this as his charge, never in the narrator's voice."

- **Beat 6.** Replace "(L4; an enemy's charge, credited, told in its own content)" with:

  "(L4), and that Arius and his party sprang from 'the same stock' (Thdt 5772-5775; an enemy's charge, credited, told in its own content, in about six words)"

- **Do not say.** In the row for "Lucian taught Arius's doctrine", append to the Right column:

  "; Alexander charged that Arius sprang from the same stock as Paul and Lucian (Thdt 5772-5775), told as his charge"

- **Source_Sheet.md.** Add Thdt 5772-5775 to L4 and D5.

### 5. MINOR: the beat 5 plain sense conflicts with Do not say

Beat 5 says "the side the bishops of Italy and Rome were in communion with". Replace it with "the side the bishops of Italy and Rome wrote to (A1, ἐπιστέλλοιεν); the listener links this to the letters of communion of beat 4." Do not say bars the "communion with Rome" framing, and the Greek says "write to".

Also, "most equitably" is McGiffert's word. The Greek αἰσιώτατα (E-Gk 12118) means "most fittingly". If it is used at all, render it that way and credit it to Eusebius.
