## 1. The test

- **Who these people were:** filled. "the Christians of Antioch, in Roman Syria, were one church under a bishop, with presbyters, its priests, and deacons."
- **What happened to them:** filled. The synods, Malchion's questioning, Aurelian's ruling, Lucian's death.
- **What their faith was about:** filled, though thinly. I hear only what they fought over, given as a denial: "denying that the Son of God came down from heaven", "a man from below", "by nature an ordinary man". The opening psalms give some practice. That is enough to fill the line.
- **Why the connection matters: UNFILLED. BLOCKING.** The story ends on Arius and "fellow-Lucianist". But it never tells me who Arius was, what he taught, or why it matters that his circle came from Lucian. "The bishop of Alexandria who later fought Arius" names a quarrel and never explains it. A listener with no church background hears a new name in the last 120 words and cannot say what passed from Antioch to anyone. **Change:** the first time Arius comes in, add one clause from the outline's sources saying what he was and what the fight was about (for example, a presbyter of Alexandria whose teaching about the Son divided the church). Use only wording the old sources hold. If the outline does not hold it, the trust read decides what can be said.

## 2. The opening

After sentence 3: **who** (Paul, bishop of Antioch, and the bishops who judged him) and **where** (Antioch) are there. **When** is missing until paragraph 2 ("the later third century"). So is **why there**: why bishops were judging him at all. The essence answers both within about 145 words, so the listener is not lost. Not blocking.

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing (MINOR)

- "He ordered the house given to those to whom the bishops of Italy and Rome wrote." Heard once, "those to whom ... wrote" does not land. The meaning lost is that the house went to whichever side the bishops of Italy and Rome were in communion with. The next sentence partly rescues it. **Change:** "he ordered the house given to the side that the bishops of Italy and Rome wrote to as their own" (or the outline's plainest wording).
- "The bishop of Alexandria who later fought Arius charged that Lucian was Paul's successor and stayed out of communion for many years, and that Arius and his party came from the same stock." "Paul's successor" sounds as if Lucian became bishop after Paul. "Stayed out of communion" has no clear subject. Three claims and two unexplained names sit in one sentence. **Change:** split it at "and that". Say what "successor" means here, if the source shows it (heir to his teaching). Make Lucian the plain subject of "stayed out of communion".

## 4. Bare statements (BLOCKING)

- "The bishop of Alexandria who later fought Arius": a quarrel named and not explained. This is the same gap as test line 4, and one fix covers both.
- "Driven out of Alexandria by his bishop, Arius wrote to one of them": an exile with no reason given. If the old sources give the reason, tell it in a clause. If they give only the act, the trust read should confirm that, and it stands as told. **Change:** "Driven out of Alexandria by his bishop for [the reason the source gives], Arius wrote ..."

## 5. Citations read aloud (MINOR)

- "The letter's other charges are the bishops' own." This is a note about the record, not a moment in the story. It reads as a source flag. The "they wrote" and "they held" that follow already credit the bishops. **Change:** cut it and open the paragraph on "Paul, the bishops wrote, built himself a high throne ..."

The other times a witness is named are ones Part 1 calls for, and I do not count them. One witness is named early (Eusebius copied the letter). The others are blame ("ravaging Christ's flock"), praise (Malchion "alone could catch out"; "judged most fittingly"), a late writer (the fourth-century Latin writer, Chrysostom), an enemy's charge (the bishop of Alexandria) and a partisan witness (the historian on Arius's side).

## 6. Tells and machine-made lines

**Tells Part 1 names: 0.** There is no dash for drama, no question answered by the narrator, no stock word and no moral close. The story ends on Arius's own words, which is the story's point.

**Lines that sound machine-made (MINOR):**
- "The letter's other charges are the bishops' own." It is clipped and editorial, the cadence of a model hedging its sourcing (see item 5).
- "He left the teaching alone and let those bishops' letters decide, and Paul was driven out by the worldly power." The first half is a contrast pivot, close in shape to "not X but Y": he did not judge the doctrine, he let letters decide. It is narrator interpretation laid over the ruling. "The worldly power" is translationese. **Change:** tell only the ruling and the eviction as the source gives them, for example "and Paul was driven out of the church by the ruler of this world's power", if that is the source's phrase, or else plainly: "and the emperor's officers drove Paul out."
- "bound by letters to churches across the empire." This is a smooth summary phrase in the essence. It passes because "letters of communion" are explained later. Noted, no change needed.
