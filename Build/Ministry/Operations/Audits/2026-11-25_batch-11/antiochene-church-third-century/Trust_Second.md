## BLOCKING findings

**1. SOFTENED OR CHANGED (paragraph 3):** "They wrote that Paul called Jesus Christ a man from below, and Eusebius says he held that Christ was by nature an ordinary man."
- Source: E-Gk 11906-11907, "ὅπου λέγει Ἰησοῦν Χριστὸν κάτωθεν"; E-Eng 42177-42178, "where he says 'Jesus Christ is from below.'" Neither the Greek nor the English nor ANF6 has "man" in the bishops' report of his words. "Man" is Eusebius's word (T1, E-Gk 11433-11435, "κοινοῦ τὴν φύσιν ἀνθρώπου"), and the sentence gives it to him separately. Putting it into the bishops' quote merges the two witnesses into one stronger saying.
- Change: "They wrote that Paul said Jesus Christ was from below, and Eusebius says he held that Christ was by nature an ordinary man."
- The outline left room for this. Its beat 1 Explain it reads "a Christ who did not come down from heaven but was a man from below". Do not say line: *Wrong:* "Paul called Christ a man from below". *Right:* the bishops quote him as saying "Jesus Christ is from below" (T2); "man" is Eusebius's word and stays with Eusebius (T1).

**2. ASSUMPTION ADDED (paragraph 2):** "...the bishops of the East met again and again to put him out."
- Source: E-Eng 41894-41899, they "assembled at different times and frequently to consider these matters ... the others striving zealously to lay bare and make manifest his heresy". E-Eng 42044-42066 / E-Gk 11682-11692 (the letter): Firmilian condemned the teaching but, when Paul "promised to change his opinions, he believed him and hoped that ... what was necessary would be done. So he delayed the matter." The sources say the bishops met to examine and expose his teaching, and that a leading bishop held off in hope of a change. Removal comes only at the last meeting (E-Eng 41936-41954). Giving every meeting the purpose of putting him out adds an aim the record does not give, and it clashes with paragraph 4.
- Change: "...the bishops of the East met again and again over his case, until at last they put him out."
- This is the approved essence wording, so the same change belongs in the outline's heart. Do not say line: *Wrong:* "the bishops met again and again to put him out". *Right:* they met often over his teaching (G5), and only the last meeting cut him off (M1).

## Notes on sentences that hold (not blocking)

- Paragraph 3, "around 260": JerChr 44213-44215 places it at Gallienus VII ("Antiochiae xv coostituitur episcopus Paulus Samosatenus").

- Paragraph 4, "died at Tarsus": the Greek (E-Gk 11788-11790) says he came as far as Tarsus, and that "meanwhile" (μεταξύ) he died while they waited. Rufinus (E-Gk 11848-11850) says "cum Tarsum usque pervenisset ... nuntiatus est nobis excessisse de mundo". This is a fair reading.
- Paragraph 5: "scolded" compresses "rebukes and insults" (ἐπιτιμῶν καὶ ἐνυβρίζων, E-Gk 11893). "Curtained" comes from an editor's note that explains the word (E-Eng note 2414). - Paragraph 7, "He left the teaching alone and let those bishops' letters decide": this is a fair account of what the ruling says (E-Gk 12119-12120, "οἷς ἂν ... ἐπίσκοποι τοῦ δόγματος ἐπιστέλλοιεν"). It claims nothing about Aurelian's mind. "Most fittingly" renders αἰσιώτατα.
