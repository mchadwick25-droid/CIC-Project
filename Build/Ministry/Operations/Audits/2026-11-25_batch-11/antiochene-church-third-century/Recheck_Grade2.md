## Sources, checked at the cited lines

- **A1 (E-Eng 42324-42330; E-Gk 12116-12122).** Paul is the one who refuses: "μηδαμῶς ἐκστῆναι τοῦ Παύλου ... ϑέλοντος". The petition is passive ("ἐντευχϑεὶς") and names no petitioner, so "the case went to" adds no one. The house goes "τούτοις ... οἷς ἂν ... ἐπίσκοποι ... ἐπιστέλλοιεν", "to those to whom the bishops should write", so "whichever side ... wrote to" is right. "Driven out of the church ... by the worldly power" follows "οὕτω δῆτα". Later witness, Documented. Holds.
- **M5 (E-Eng 42003-42005) and M6 (E-Eng 42274-42314; E-Gk 12018-12026).** The letter is addressed to Dionysius of Rome, to Maximus, and "to all our fellow-ministers throughout the world". The bishops appointed Domnus "that you may write to him ... But let this man write to Artemas". So the bishops of Italy and Rome were among those asked to write to the new bishop, not to Paul. This is contemporary and Documented, and the brief names the new bishop by role only. Holds. "Refused to give up" in the correction below is Eusebius's own verb: "refused to surrender".
- **Fix 3 (M2, E-Eng 41977-41984).** "Alone able to detect" is Eusebius's judgment, not an event. The Never-cut item "Malchion's questioning" stays, as does the questioning before shorthand writers. The heart, the record's force and the Part 1 explanations are untouched. Holds.
- **L4-ANF (ANF6 28778-28779).** The quotation is exact: "To whom Lucian succeeding, remained for many years separate from the communion of three bishops." Its note 2429 (28779-28781) is a modern editor's note and gives no reason. Theodoret's text (Thdt 5759-5762) gives none either. The sheet row is accurate and may stand as sheet evidence.

## BLOCKING 1. "but his letter does not say why" is a gap-only clause

Kit Part 1, Explain it, covers this case. Lucian keeping out of communion is a break. For an act of conflict whose reason the sources leave out, the rule is "tell it as they give it, with no reason added and no word on the missing one". The kit also says "do not announce it unless the gap is itself the story". Here the gap is not the story. The story is Alexander's charge, heard as a charge. Craft says "No sentence that only says what is missing". The clause is exactly that kind of sentence, attached to Alexander's own words. The Alexandria recheck in this batch cut a similar line, "but Eusebius never says so outright", on the same rule.

The listener's "why?" cannot be answered from any held source, so the right response is to leave the act as the record gives it. The Lucian sentence stays as it was before this fix. Fix 2 is withdrawn in full, including its split into three sentences.
