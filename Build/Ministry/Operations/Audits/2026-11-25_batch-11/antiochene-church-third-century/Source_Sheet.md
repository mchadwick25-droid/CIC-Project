**The main witness, stated once.** Nearly the whole story of Paul comes from the letter the bishops sent out after they deposed him. It is an enemy's document: the bishops are prosecutors writing their own verdict. Eusebius of Caesarea, writing within living memory, copied extracts of it into his history (E-Eng 41997-42001) and adds his own short frame. ANF6 gives the same letter in fuller form (17312-17499; its translator says Eusebius holds extracts of it, 17301-17302). Paul's own voice survives only in what his opponents quote.

## 2. Events, in order

### 2a. Paul's teaching, as his enemies give it

| # | Claim | File, lines | Witness | Confidence | Text verbatim |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | Eusebius: Paul held that Christ was in his nature a common man | E-Eng 41838-41840; E-Gk 11433-11435 | later (Eusebius), hostile frame | Documented that Eusebius says so; what Paul himself taught is Contested | "low and degraded views of Christ, namely, that in his nature he was a common man" ; "ὡς κοινοῦ τὴν φύσιν ἀνϑρώπου γενομένου" |
| T2 | The bishops: Paul would not confess that the Son of God came down from heaven; his "Jesus Christ is from below" | E-Eng 42173-42178; E-Gk 11903-11907 | contemporary, enemy's charge | Documented as the charge | "he is unwilling to acknowledge that the Son of God has come down from heaven ... and not least where he says 'Jesus Christ is from below.'" |
| T2-note | An editor thinks "from below" was the bishops' own inference, not Paul's words | E-Eng note 2415, 42178-42190 | modern, makes a claim of its own | Not usable as record | "The words quoted in the epistle can hardly have been used by Paul himself." |
| T3 | Malchion's disputation: Paul said Wisdom dwelt in Christ as we dwell in houses, without becoming part of him | ANF6 17611-17616 (fragment kept in Peter the Deacon, 6th century; ANF6 note 1419 at 17583-17585) | later (fragment, c. 250 years), enemy's report of Paul | Contested (which fragments are genuine is disputed; corpus-map notes on the Loofs and Bardy editions say so) | "And this assertion you also make, that the Wisdom dwelt in Him, just as we also dwell in houses, the one in the other, and yet not as if we formed a part of the house, or the house a part of us." |
| T4 | Athanasius: Paul held the Son was not before Mary but took his beginning from her | Ath 59102-59104 | later (c. 359), opponent | Documented as Athanasius's report | "since the Samosatene held that the Son was not before Mary, but received from her the origin of His being" |
| T5 | Alexander of Alexandria says Arius's teaching "rivals" that of Paul of Samosata | Thdt 5752-5760 | later (c. 320s), enemy of Arius | Documented as Alexander's charge | "is the same as that propagated by Ebion and Artemas, and rivals that of Paul of Samosata, bishop of Antioch, who was excommunicated by a council of all the bishops." |

Explain it, "his teaching about Christ", from each side the texts hold:
- The bishops' side: Paul would not say the Son of God came down from heaven; he made Christ "from below" (T2). Eusebius: a common man by nature (T1). Malchion: Wisdom lived in Christ as a man lives in a house (T3). Athanasius: the Son began at Mary (T4).
- Paul's own side: no text of his survives except through enemies. His singers, the bishops say, praised him as an angel come down from heaven (E-Eng 42191-42192), which the bishops set against his refusal to say the Son came down. The Loofs and Bardy volumes print fragments under his name; the corpus map notes that which are his own is disputed. Nothing in the held texts gives his own reasons in his own words that is not disputed.

On "three times": the letter itself gives two visits by Firmilian and a third journey cut short by his death, with the final meeting assembled waiting for him (G6, G7). Eusebius adds that they met "at different times and frequently" (G5). The story may say the bishops came and came again, that Firmilian came twice and died on the third journey. It may not give "three councils" as a count from the record; that count is an editor's.

Explain it, each thing the story could name:
- **ducenarius**: the letter sets it against "bishop" as one of his "worldly dignities" (C2). An editor explains it as the title of an imperial official, named from his salary (E-Eng note 2412, 42105-42110; modern, clarifies the word). The same note's claim that Paul was Zenobia's official is a modern claim (42105, 42110-42116): it stays out. ANF6 renders the word "procurator" (17366).
- **secretum**: the letter says "like the rulers of the world" (C3). Rufinus's Latin: "secretarium quoque sterni et parari, sicut iudicibus saeculi solet" (E-Gk 11875-11876), a private room set up as worldly judges have. An editor's note explains it as the railed and curtained place where magistrates sat (E-Eng note 2414, 42147-42154; modern, clarifies). Plain word: "a curtained private chamber, as a magistrate had".
- **subintroductae / synesaktoi**: the people of Antioch's word for women brought in to live in a clergyman's house (C8, S4). Rufinus: women "quae cum ipso simul manebant" (E-Gk 11960-11961). The bishops' charge is the suspicion and the example, and they grant he may have done nothing wanton.
- **Paul's side on the conduct**: none survives. The letter itself says all feared to accuse him (C9), which shows he held real power and a following. An editor's note doubts the portrait (E-Eng 42117-42122; modern opinion: stays out).

Explain it:
- **synod**: a meeting of bishops from many churches to settle a question together (G3-G5, M1 show it in action).
- **excommunicate**: in the letter's own terms, to put him out of the church's communion, so that no church writes to him or receives his letters (M6). The reason the letter gives: "since he sets himself against God, and refuses to obey" (M6), and his teaching (T2).
- **letters of communion**: letters a bishop sent and received to show his church shared one fellowship with the others (M6: "receive letters of communion from him"). This is what held the network of churches together, and it is what Aurelian's ruling turned on.
- **Artemas**: named by the bishops as Paul's "father" in heresy (E-Eng 42263-42268). The held old texts do not say who Artemas was beyond Alexander's pairing him with Ebion (Thdt 5754-5757); the who-was-he gloss is a modern note (Thdt note 294). No room to explain: Leave out.

Explain it, "Aurelian's ruling":
- In Eusebius's Greek, the house goes to those to whom the bishops "of the teaching" in Italy and Rome "should write letters" (A1, ἐπιστέλλοιεν). McGiffert's "adjudge" narrows it. Plainly: the emperor gave the building to whichever side the bishops of Italy and Rome were in correspondence with. He judged who held communion, not what was true about Christ.
- Why Paul held on: Eusebius gives only the act ("refused to surrender"). Why the emperor ruled as he did: Eusebius gives only that he was petitioned and judged "most equitably". No reason is given for either; none is added.
- Zenobia: the link between her rule and Paul's holding the house is a modern reconstruction (E-Eng notes 41787, 41819, 42105-42116). Athanasius's line (A3) is the only old witness, late and hostile. Not told as cause.

## 3. Impact, heaviest first (what later witnesses carried on and used)

1. **Lucian's name and school, carried by the party that opposed Nicaea's word.** Arius names himself and Eusebius of Nicomedia fellow-Lucianists (L5); Philostorgius lists the pupils who became bishops of Nicomedia, Chalcedon, Nicaea, Antioch and Tarsus (L6), and three of them taught Aetius (L7). The bishops at Antioch in 341 claimed a creed of Lucian (L14); Asian bishops in Caria still invoked him against "consubstantial" (L15). Lucian's enemies used the same link: Alexander tied him to Paul (L4). Defended: his memory was kept by the Nicene side too, in Chrysostom's feast sermon (L11) and Constantine's Helenopolis (L10).
2. **The council's refusal of "homoousios".** In the 350s, bishops who balked at Nicaea's word said the council against Paul had rejected it. Three later witnesses answered them, each giving a different reason the council had refused it:
   - Athanasius (Ath 58970-58981): "since, as they allege (for I have not the Epistle in question), the Bishops who condemned the Samosatene have said in writing that the Son is not coessential with the Father". Reason, Ath 59053-59057: "they who deposed the Samosatene took Coessential in a bodily sense, because Paul had attempted sophistry and said, 'Unless Christ has of man become God, it follows that He is Coessential with the Father; and if so, of necessity there are three essences, one the previous essence, and the other two from it'". Athanasius counts "the seventy who deposed the Samosatene" (Ath 58995). Later (c. 359), opponent of the objectors. Documented as his account.
   - Hilary (Hil 9607-9611): "our fathers, when Paul of Samosata was pronounced a heretic, also rejected the word ὁμοούσιον, on the ground that by attributing this title to God he had taught that He was single and undifferentiated, and at once Father and to Himself." Hilary counts eighty: "Eighty bishops once rejected it; but three hundred and eighteen recently accepted it." (Hil 9772-9773). Later (c. 359). Documented as his report.
   - Basil (Bas 27366-27368, 27403-27407): "the members of the synods which met to discuss the case of Paul of Samosata did find fault with the term as an unfortunate one. For they maintained that the homoousion set forth the idea both of essence and of what is derived from it". Later (c. 370s).
   - None of the three had the council's letter before him in the passage (Athanasius says so outright). They disagree on the reason and on the number of bishops. Contested in its reason; Documented that the claim was made and answered.
   Explain it, **homoousios**: Nicaea's word that the Son is "of one being" with the Father (Bas 27357-27358, 27417-27424 for Nicaea's use). Plain word: "of one being".
3. **Paul's followers survived as a church of their own.** Nicaea in 325 ruled that Paulianists coming over must be rebaptized, their blameless clergy ordained again, their deaconesses counted as laity (Nic 5296-5306): "Concerning the Paulianists who have flown for refuge to the Catholic Church, it has been decreed that they must by all means be rebaptized". Later (about 55 years on). Documented.
4. **Paul's name as a byword.** Alexander set Arius beside Paul (T5); Hilary and Athanasius use "the Samosatene" as a fixed label (Hil 9633-9634; Ath 59102). Documented.
5. **Lucian's text of Scripture.** Copies bore his name in Jerome's day (L1). That this reading of Scripture passed to the later Antiochene teachers is Inferential-Thin (census edge to `antiochene-exegetical-christianity-chrysostom-ce`): stays out.
