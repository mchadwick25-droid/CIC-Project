## Sentence by sentence

**Opening (Hippo, Letter 29)**
1. "In the 390s, at Hippo ... which they called the Gladness." SUMMARY HOLDS. AugEp34 6260-6262: "ut illa sollemnitas prohiberetur, quam laetitiam nominantes"; 6461-6462: "totum tam magnae basilicae spatium turbis epulantium ebriorumque complerent". The decade: Augustine writes as "Presbyter" (AugEp34 6279), and Possidius gives "almost forty" years "as a priest or bishop" before his death in 430 (Pos 4919-4920). That puts the letter in the 390s.
2. "Their priest Augustine read them Scripture against drunken feasts and pleaded with them." HELD. AugEp34 6402-6403 (1 Cor 11 "recitato"), 6417-6422 (Gal 5, "ebrietates"), 6473 "obsecraui".
3. Q1. HELD against the Latin. AugEp34 6521-6524: "non ego illorum lacrimas meis lacrimis moui, sed cum talia dicerentur, fateor, eorum fletu praeuentus meum abstinere non potui, et cum iam pariter fleuissemus, plenissima spe correctionis illorum finis sermonis mei factus est." Every clause is rendered and nothing is added.
4. "On the feast day itself, from the rival church, whose people he called heretics, came the sound of their feasting." HELD. AugEp34 6656-6657: "in haereticorum basilica audiebamus ab eis solita conuiuia celebrata", on the afternoon of the feast day ("Pomeridiano autem die", 6650).

**Essence**
5. "From the 250s to 430 ... at Hippo under Augustine." SUMMARY HOLDS. It frames the beats below (CyL 2292-2296; Pos 4917-4920).
6. "Their bishop taught them, fed their poor, bought back their captives, judged their quarrels and decided with them whom to take back after sin." SUMMARY HOLDS. CyL 10893-10896: "misimus autem sestertia centum milia nummorum ... cleri et plebis ... collatione collecta"; Pos 3149-3150 (heard their cases); CyL 2922-2925 (Ep. 19.2): "praepositi cum clero conuenientes praesente etiam stantium plebe ... disponere omnia consilii communis religione".
7. "Persecution, plague and a rival church ... owed each other in danger." SUMMARY HOLDS. This is a frame for what follows: K1 (CyL 2968-2972), Q2, and Ep. 228 (Pos 4286-4292).

**Carthage, 250**
8. "In 250 the emperor Decius began a persecution, and his edict called for sacrifice to the gods." SUMMARY HOLDS. Eus 37414: "commenced a persecution of the churches"; CyLap 1050-1051: "seruiuit saeculari domino, obtemperauit eius edicto", said of those who sacrificed. The year 250 is Widely Accepted.
9. "At Carthage ... the crowd kept shouting violently for Cyprian, and he withdrew, as he wrote, to keep his people in peace." HELD. CyL 2971-2973: "cum me clamore uiolento frequenter populus flagitasset, non tam meam salutem quam quietem fratrum publicam cogitans interim secessi". Leaving out "not so much my own safety" keeps the reason he puts first.
10. "From hiding he led his church by letter, and told his clergy:" Q2. HELD. CyL 2974: "absens tamen corpore nec spiritu nec actu nec monitis meis defui". Ep. 14 is addressed "ad presbyteros et diaconos" (CyL 2173). Q2 against CyL 2292-2296: "solus rescribere nihil potui, quando a primordio episcopatus mei statuerim nihil sine consilio uestro et sine consensu plebis mea priuatim sententia gerere." Faithful.
11. "Many had given way and sacrificed, and now these fallen Christians asked to come back." HELD. CyLap 346-348: "ante aciem multi uicti ... sacrificare idolis"; CyL 2926: "ipsis quoque festinantibus", the fallen pressing to be received.
12. "Cyprian held that no one should be barred from penance ... or from the hope of peace." HELD. CyL 8428: "neminem putamus a fructu satisfactionis et spe pacis arcendum".
13. "Peace was the church's word for taking someone back, reconciling them, to share the bread and cup." SUMMARY HOLDS. CyL 2593-2598 (Ep. 16.2): "ad communicationem admittuntur ... eucharistia illis datur, cum scriptum sit: qui ederit panem aut biberit calicem Domini". "Public" repentance: the same lines, "nondum exomologesi facta, nondum manu eis ab episcopo et clero inposita".
14. "At Rome a stricter party held that sharing communion with the fallen stained the rest." HELD, as Cyprian reports their view. CyE 34075-34077: "if they say that one is polluted by another's sin ... the idolatry of the delinquent passes over to one who is not guilty".
15. "They set up Novatian as a second bishop of Rome, against the one already chosen." HELD. CyL 6186-6189: "Nouatianum episcopum factum"; CyL 6217-6218: "collegae nostri iam delecti et ordinati".
16. "When his envoys came to Carthage ... since Novatian had been made bishop against the Catholic Church." HELD. CyL 6186-6193: "Venerunt ad nos ... missi a Nouatiano ... inlicitae et contra ecclesiam catholicam factae ordinationis prauitate commoti a communicatione eos nostra statim cohibendos esse censuimus"; 6195: "ego et collegae plurimi".

**Plague**
17. "A few years later plague struck." SUMMARY HOLDS. PonL 40781: "erupit postmodum lues dira". Pontius sets it after the persecution of 250 and before the exile and death, so "a few years" stays within what the old sources fix. No year is told.
18. "Pontius, Cyprian's deacon and admirer, says the bishop gathered the people and told them that caring only for their own was nothing great." HELD. PonL 40798-40803: "adgregatam primo in loco plebem de misericordiae bonis instruit ... non esse mirabile, si nostros tantum debito caritatis obsequio foueremus". That Pontius was Cyprian's deacon: Jerome, On Illustrious Men 68, a later witness ("Pontius, diaconus Cypriani", per Source_Sheet P6), and the Life's own praise shows the admirer.
19. "The tasks were shared out, and the poor, who had no money to give, gave their work." HELD. PonL 40848-40851: "distributa sunt ergo continuo ... ministeria. multi qui paupertatis beneficio sumptus exhibere non poterant, plus sumptibus exhibebant, compensantes proprio labore".

**Cyprian's death**
20. "Then the emperors ordered sacrifice to the Roman gods." SUMMARY HOLDS. PonL 41416-41418: "sacratissimi imperatores Valerianus et Gallienus ... praeceperunt eos qui Romanam religionem non colunt, debere Romanas caeremonias recognoscere"; 41534 "iusserunt te ... caeremoniari". Cyprian's answer, "nullos alios deos noui", shows the rites meant the gods' worship. "Sacrifice" fairly names those ceremonies.
21. "Cyprian was arrested, and all his people gathered in the street outside where he was held." HELD. PonL 41510-41515: "illuc uniuersus populus fratrum conuenit ... omnes in uico ante ianuam hospitii principis manserant".
22. "He refused, and the sentence called him an enemy of the Roman gods and their rites, and the leader of men gathered in a wicked conspiracy." SUMMARY HOLDS. PonL 41535: "non facio"; 41567-41572: "plurimos nefariae tibi conspirationis homines adgregasti et inimicum te diis Romanis et religionibus sacris constituisti ... nequissimorum criminum auctor et signifer".
23. "He was led out and beheaded on 14 September 258." HELD. PonL 41575: "gladio animaduerti placet"; 41578: "decollemur"; 41580: "in agrum Sexti productus est"; 41518: "octaua decima kalendarum Octobrium"; consuls "Tusco et Basso" (41474), so 258, Widely Accepted.

**The split**
24. "Soon after the great persecution of the early 300s, the people of Carthage chose a new bishop." HELD. Opt 207: "The storm of persecution passed over ... by the vote of the whole people, Caecilian was chosen". The early 300s: Opt 177, "sixty years and more" since the persecution.
25. "A party said the bishop who had made him bishop had handed over the Scriptures to be burned, so they made a rival bishop and set up altar against altar." SUMMARY HOLDS. Opt 217: "his Consecrator being falsely alleged to have been a Betrayer"; Opt 229: "In this manner they went forth, and altar was raised against altar"; burning: Opt 5559, "in accordance with the Emperor's command, any Scriptures of your Law were burnt". The "so" gives the party's own stated ground, in Optatus's order of events. Optatus's further charges of greed and ambition (Opt 207) are left out without softening the split. Not a finding.
26. "The rival side, the Donatists, held that one who was himself stained could not wash away sins, and they baptized again those who came over." HELD. Opt 153 (Parmenian quoted): "It could not be that one who was stained should wash away sins"; Pos 2023-2024 / R5 English: "the rebaptizing Donatist party".
27. "A Catholic writer some sixty years later said the Donatists had split away, yet shared his church's true sacraments, such as baptism." HELD. Opt 169: "to you schismatics, although you are not in the Catholic Church ... you have shared true Sacraments with us"; Opt 177: "sixty years and more"; Opt 3601-3603: "the Sacrament of Baptism".
28. "So he did not call them heretics: to him, heretics taught falsely about God." SUMMARY HOLDS. Opt 155: "heretics alone, since they have falsified the creed, for amongst them one has said that there are two Gods ... how wide is the gulf between schismatics and heretics".

**Hippo, the bishop**
29. "Later, as bishop of Hippo, Augustine judged the disputes that Christians brought him, because Paul had told believers not to go to law before unbelievers." HELD. Pos 3137-3151: "According also to the teaching of the Apostle ... brother goeth to law with brother, and that before the unbelievers. Accordingly when he was importuned by Christians ... he heard their cases".
30. "Against the rival church he had first wanted argument only." HELD. NP1 38242-38244: "originally my opinion was, that no one should be coerced ... we must act only by words, fight only by arguments".
31. "Then, he wrote, his fellow bishops pointed to his own town, brought over ... by fear of the emperors' laws of exile and fines, and he came to back those laws." SUMMARY HOLDS. NP1 38240-38241: "I have therefore yielded to the evidence ... which my colleagues have laid before me"; 38248-38251: "my own town, which, although it was once wholly on the side of Donatus, was brought over to the Catholic unity by fear of the imperial edicts"; 38003-38004, the same letter: "by punishments, such as exile and fines". The town is not named, as the source does not name it.

**The end**
32. "In his last years the Vandals, enemies who came by sea, overran Africa." HELD. Pos 3982-3985: "Vandals and Alans ... came by ship from the regions of Spain across the sea and poured into Africa and overran it."
33. "Of countless churches, only three still stood: Carthage, Hippo and Cirta." HELD. Pos 4025-4028: "vix tres superstites ex innumerabilibus ecclesiis, hoc est Carthaginensem, Hipponensem et Cirtensem, quae Dei beneficio excisae non sunt".
34. "A fellow bishop wrote to ask whether bishops and clergy should leave their churches." HELD. Pos 4167-4170: "a sancto viro nostro coepiscopo ... Honorato, litteris fuisse consultum, utrumnam illis adventantibus ex ecclesiis episcopis vel clericis recedendum esset".
35. "Augustine answered:" Q3. HELD against the Latin. Pos 4556-4561: "Si autem ministri adsint, pro viribus quas eis Dominus subministrat, omnibus subvenitur; alii baptizantur, alii reconciliantur, nulli Dominici corporis communione fraudantur, omnes consolantur, aedificantur, exhortantur, ut Deum rogent, qui potens est omnia quae timentur avertere". Every clause is rendered, and "ministri" is rightly "the clergy".

## Findings

None BLOCKING, and no MINOR finding to apply. These notes need no change:
- Sentence 20: "sacrifice" names what the Acta call "caeremoniae". The fit is fair, and the Acta's own word, "rites", follows two sentences later.
- Sentence 25: the "so" rests on Optatus's order of events and on the party's own charge. The story does not take up Optatus's claim that the charge was false (Opt 217), which keeps it out of the narrator's voice, as Part 1 requires.
