## tile

**Current (none in the census; the world page's `skim.tile.text`):** "From about 246 to 430, this was the ordinary church of two cities in Roman North Africa, Carthage and Hippo. Each congregation had one bishop, given a particular people and answerable for them. When a persecution in 250 made many of them give way, their bishop did not write them off. He set a road back, examined and walked in the open. Cyprian of Carthage held that road, and he was put to death in 258. A century and a half later, Augustine of Hippo argued that Cyprian had been wrong about baptism, but never called him an outsider. This church argued hard about the font, about councils and about grace, but always inside a bond it would not break. Both bishops speak at length in their own words. Almost everyone else is missing: the ordinary believer, the women, and the people who gave way."

**New:** "From the 250s to 430, the Latin-speaking Christians of Roman Africa lived as congregations around one bishop, at Carthage under Cyprian and later at Hippo under Augustine. Their bishop taught them, fed their poor, bought back their captives and judged their quarrels. In 250 the emperor Decius began a persecution, and many gave way and sacrificed. From hiding, Cyprian led his church by letter and held that no one should be barred from penance and the hope of being taken back. When plague struck, he told his people that caring only for their own was nothing great, and the poor, who had no money to give, gave their work. In 258 he refused the Roman rites and was beheaded. Soon after the great persecution of the early 300s, a rival church, the Donatists, set up altar against altar and baptized again those who came over. Augustine first wanted only argument with them, then came to back the emperors' laws of exile and fines. As the Vandals overran Africa, a fellow bishop asked whether the clergy should leave their churches. Augustine answered that if the clergy are there, everyone is helped."

**Why:** the brief's dates ("From the 250s to 430", where the world text has "about 246", a modern reckoning of Cyprian's election). Every claim is the brief's, in its order, and the essence comes first. The world text's "argued that Cyprian had been wrong about baptism", "never called him an outsider", "about councils and about grace" and its "Almost everyone else is missing" are not in the brief, so they are not carried; the brief tells Augustine's people and the rival church instead. The world text's "not X but Y" turns ("but never called him", "but always inside a bond") are gone (Kit Part 1, Craft). About 190 words.
