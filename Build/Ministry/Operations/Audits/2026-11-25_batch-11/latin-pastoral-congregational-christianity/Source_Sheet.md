Witness kinds: **own words** (the people in the story), **contemporary**, **later (gap)**, **late legend**. Confidence: Documented / Widely Accepted / Dominant Modern Reconstruction / Contested / Inferential-Thin.

**The witnesses, stated once.** For Carthage, the record is Cyprian's own letters and treatises (250-258), the Life written by his deacon (Pontius) as an admirer soon after his death, and the Acta, a court-style record of his trials joined to a Christian account of his death. For Hippo, it is Augustine's own letters, sermons and books, and the Life by his friend Possidius, who lived beside him and shared the siege of 430. Every main witness is a churchman of this church. The rival church speaks only through its enemies' quotations (Optatus, Augustine). No story claim rests on one witness alone.

## 5. Where sources disagree or come late

- **Cyprian's flight.** His own letters give his reason (K1); the Roman clergy's letter and Pontius's defense show it was charged as fear (card story; `pontius-the-deacon`). Both are on the tree already; this story gives his reason in his words and does not settle the charge.
- **Caecilian's consecrator.** The rival church charged he was a Betrayer; Optatus, a Catholic bishop two generations later, says the charge was false (R2). The story gives the split as the rival church's charge and does not judge it.
- **The year of Caecilian's election** (311 or 312): Contested; say "soon after the great persecution ended" or "early in the 300s".
- **The heretics' church in Letter 29** is not named there; Possidius places the Donatists in Hippo (R5, E6).
- **The Acta and Pontius** differ on who bound Cyprian's eyes (`cyprian-of-carthage` tells this); not used here.
- **Dates resting on modern reckoning**: Cyprian's election (about 248), the plague (about 252), Augustine's ordination (391) and consecration as bishop (395 or 396), Letter 29 (395, NPNF editor), Letter 228 (428 or 429, NPNF editor). Told only as roughly as the old sources allow.
