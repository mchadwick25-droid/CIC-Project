**Paragraph 1**

1. "At Hippo, a town in Roman Africa, on the feast day of a former bishop, the people wanted their old feast in the church, with eating and drinking, which they called the Gladness." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** AugEp34 6260-6262: "tumultuari homines et dicere se ferre non posse, ut illa sollemnitas prohiberetur, quam laetitiam nominantes"; 6461-6462: "totum tam magnae basilicae spatium turbis epulantium ebriorumque complerent". The day comes from the manuscript heading, AugEp34 6279-6281: "de die Natalis Leontii quondam episcopi Hipponensis" (Goldbacher: "inscriptionis loco positum m. 2"). NP1 25319-25320 has the same heading. "The people" stands for the letter's "homines" ("some", NP1 25343). That is a fair compression.
2. The heading, "epistola Presbyteri", supports "priest".
3. Q1, "I did not move their tears with my own ... that they would mend their ways." **HELD.** AugEp34 6521-6524: "non ego illorum lacrimas meis lacrimis moui, sed cum talia dicerentur, fateor, eorum fletu praeuentus meum abstinere non potui, et cum iam pariter fleuissemus, plenissima spe correctionis illorum finis sermonis mei factus est." Each clause is rendered and nothing is added.
4. The sermon came the day before the feast (NP1 25518: "Next morning ... when the day dawned, which so many were accustomed to devote to excess"), so the order is right.

**Paragraph 2 (the essence)**

5. "From the 250s to 430, the Latin-speaking Christians of Roman Africa lived as congregations around one bishop, at Carthage under Cyprian and later at Hippo under Augustine." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** It compresses the whole record: CyL 2968-2976 (Cyprian in 250); Pos 4147-4148 (the siege and Augustine's last illness, with 430 Widely Accepted).
6. "Their bishop taught them, fed their poor, bought back their captives, judged their quarrels and decided with them whom to take back after sin." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** Taught: Pos 2013 ("Augustine taught and preached"). Poor: CyE 28962-28972; Pos 3597-3604. Captives: CyL 10893-10896 ("misimus autem sestertia centum milia ... cleri et plebis ... collatione collecta"); Pos 3600-3602. Judged: Pos 3146-3148. Decided with them: CyL 2922-2925 ("praepositi cum clero conuenientes praesente etiam stantium plebe ... disponere omnia consilii communis religione"). The people were "present" and gave "consent" (CyL 2295). Saying the bishop decided "with them" is a fair compression.
7. "Persecution, plague and a rival church in the same towns pressed on them, and each time they had to work out what a bishop and his people owed each other in danger." **ASSUMPTION ADDED.** The first clause holds. "Each time they had to work out" claims that every crisis posed this question to the congregation itself, and no held line says so of the rival church. Optatus's account of the split (Opt 207-229) and Augustine's account of the laws (NP1 38245-38258) concern who held the true altar and whether force was right, not what a bishop and his people owed each other in danger. The approved essence in `Outline.md` reads "and the record keeps asking what a bishop and his people owed each other in danger", which the sources carry through K1, Q2, P2 and V3. **Change:** "Persecution, plague and a rival church in the same towns pressed on them, and the record keeps asking what a bishop and his people owed each other in danger."

9. "At Carthage, in today's Tunisia, the crowd kept shouting for Cyprian, and he withdrew, for the brethren's peace, as he wrote." **HELD.** CyL 2972-2975: "cum me clamore uiolento frequenter populus flagitasset, non tam meam salutem quam quietem fratrum publicam cogitans interim secessi".

The rendering is faithful.

**Paragraph 4**

**Paragraph 7**

23. "Soon after the great persecution of the early 300s ended, the people of Carthage chose a new bishop." **HELD.** Opt 207: "The storm of persecution passed over, and subsided ... Then, by the vote of the whole people, Caecilian was chosen".
24. "A party said the bishop who had consecrated him had handed over the Scriptures during the persecution, so they made a rival bishop and set up altar against altar." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** Opt 217: "they imagined, however, that he might be blackened by his Consecrator being falsely alleged to have been a Betrayer"; Opt 229: "altar was raised against altar; and there was an unlawful consecration". The charge was raised "so that his Consecration might be declared void" (Opt 211), so "so" follows the party's own stated ground. Optatus's other motives (anger, ambition, avarice) are an enemy's judgment, and leaving them out softens nothing.
25. "The rival side held that one who was himself stained could not wash away sins, and they baptized again those who came over to them." **HELD.** Opt 153: "'It could not be that one who was stained should wash away sins'"; Pos (R5) "the rebaptizing Donatist party".
26. "A Catholic writer of the next generation said the two churches held the same sacraments, and he kept the charge of breaking the creed for heretics." **SOFTENED OR CHANGED** on the date. The content holds: Opt 143 ("the Sacraments which you and we alike possess") and Opt 155 ("heretics alone, since they have falsified the creed"). But "of the next generation" puts him too close to the split. Optatus himself writes, Opt 177: "sixty years and more have passed since the storm of persecution spread abroad throughout the whole of Africa" (again at Opt 2142). The outline's own "Who tells it" says "writing two generations after the split". Only its Names line has "the next generation", and that line left room for this error. **Change:** "A Catholic writer, some sixty years after the persecution, said the two churches held the same sacraments, and he kept the charge of breaking the creed for heretics." **Do not say:** "a Catholic writer of the next generation" | "some sixty years after the persecution" (Opt 177).

They also show care beyond their own walls and a second altar in the same towns, and the essence names those things.
