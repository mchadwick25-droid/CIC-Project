## Non-HELD sentences

### BLOCKING

**9. ASSUMPTION ADDED.** "Persecution, plague and a rival church in the same towns pressed on them, and each time they had to work out what a bishop and his people owed each other in danger."
- The sources show the bishop and people settling what they owed each other under persecution. Cyprian decides "nihil sine consilio uestro et sine consensu plebis" (`cyprian_epistulae-lat_hartel1871-csel3-tei.txt` 2292-2295), and the fallen are to be handled "cum clero conuenientes praesente etiam stantium plebe" (same file, 2917-2919). They show it again as the Vandals come: "hi qui aliis indigent, non deserantur ab his quibus indigent" (`possidius_vita-augustini_weiskotten1919.txt`, Latin of Ep. 228, ch. 30).
- For the plague, the held line is about mercy reaching beyond their own people: "nothing wonderful in our cherishing our own people only" (`anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian.xml` 27963-27965). For the rival church, it is about coercing the other side (`npnf101_augustine-confessions-letters.xml` 38246-38254). Neither is a question of what bishop and people owed each other, so "each time" adds a pattern the record does not give. (The outline's approved essence reads "the record keeps asking"; the brief has reworded it.)
- **Exact change:** "and each time they had to work out" → "and in persecution and invasion they had to work out".
- **Do not say (add to outline):** "each time" (persecution, plague, rival church) they worked out what bishop and people owed each other → only persecution (K5, K6) and the Vandals (V3) are shown that way.

**21. SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** "Then a new emperor ordered the Roman rites kept, the sacrifices of the Roman gods."
- In the Acta, two emperors give the order: "sacratissimi imperatores Valerianus et Gallienus litteras ad me dare dignati sunt, quibus praeceperunt eos qui Romanam religionem non colunt, debere Romanas caeremonias recognoscere" (`cyprian_opera-spuria-vita-pontius-lat_hartel-csel3-pars3.txt` 41416-41420). The sentence names "Valerianus et Gallienus Augusti et Valerianus nobilissimus Caesar" (41562-41563).
- **Exact change:** "Then a new emperor ordered" → "Then the emperors ordered".
- **Do not say (add to outline):** "a new emperor" (singular) for the order of 257 → "the emperors" (Valerian and Gallienus, S6).

**28. SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** "A Catholic writer of the next generation said the two churches held the same sacraments, and he kept the charge of breaking the creed for heretics."
- The content is held. Optatus speaks of "the Sacraments which you and we alike possess" (`optatus_against-the-donatists.txt` 143), and says such charges are "true of heretics alone, since they have falsified the creed" (155).
- The time is wrong. Optatus writes "sixty years and more have passed since the storm of persecution spread abroad throughout the whole of Africa" (177; again at 2142, "sixty years and more ago"). That is two generations after the split, as the outline's own Who tells it says ("writing two generations after the split"). The outline's Names line says "of the next generation", so the outline left room for this error.
- **Exact change:** "A Catholic writer of the next generation said" → "A Catholic writer some sixty years later said".
- **Do not say (add to outline):** "a Catholic writer of the next generation" → "some sixty years later" or "two generations later" (Opt 177). Also correct the Names line, which says "of the next generation".

### SUMMARY HOLDS (not blocking)

**1.** "At Hippo, a town in Roman Africa, on the feast day of a former bishop, the people wanted their old feast in the church, with eating and drinking, which they called the Gladness." The letter's heading reads "de die Natalis Leontii quondam episcopi Hipponensis" (`augustine_epistulae-1-123-lat_goldbacher-csel34.txt` 6279-6280). The feast is "quam laetitiam nominantes uinulentiae nomen frustra conantur abscondere" (6261-6263), and they would fill "totum tam magnae basilicae spatium turbis epulantium ebriorumque" (6461-6462). "Old" rests on the next day's complaint about "those before us" (NP1 25519-25548). A fair compression.

**7.** "From the 250s to 430, the Latin-speaking Christians of Roman Africa lived as congregations around one bishop, at Carthage under Cyprian and later at Hippo under Augustine." A summary of the whole record. The span runs from the persecution of 250 (Eus 37413-37414) to the siege in which Augustine died (Pos 4147-4148; 430 is Widely Accepted).

**8.** "Their bishop taught them, fed their poor, bought back their captives, judged their quarrels and decided with them whom to take back after sin." Each part is held:
- taught them: "On the people assembled together in one place he first of all urged the benefits of mercy" (anf05 27961);
- fed their poor: money for "those who are labouring in poverty and want" (anf05 28962-28972), and the vessels melted for "captives and ... the many who were in need" (Pos 3597-3604);
- bought back their captives: "sestertia centum milia nummorum ... cleri et plebis ... collatione collecta" (CyL 10893-10896);
- judged their quarrels: "he heard their cases carefully and dutifully" (Pos 3149-3150);
- decided with them: CyL 2917-2919.
- Note for the verdict: the captives appear only here, since their beat went to the card. This is a trust note only, not a finding.

**10.** "In 250 the emperor Decius began a persecution of the churches, and his edict called for sacrifice to the gods." Eusebius says "he commenced a persecution of the churches" (`npnf201_eusebius-church-history-life-of-constantine.xml` 37413-37414, ch. XXXIX "The Persecution under Decius"). For the edict, Cyprian writes of one who "obtemperauit eius edicto" (`cyprian_de-lapsis-lat_hartel1868-csel3-tei.txt` 1050-1051), in the setting of those who sacrificed or took certificates. The year 250 is Widely Accepted.

**15.** "At Rome a stricter party held that sharing communion with the fallen stained the rest." Cyprian writes of the "new heretics": "they say that one is polluted by another's sin ... the idolatry of the delinquent passes over to one who is not guilty" (anf05 34072-34076). That is Cyprian's report of the Novatianists, whose bishop was at Rome. Fair.

**16.** "They set up Novatian as a second bishop of Rome, against the one already chosen." Cyprian: "Nouatianum episcopum factum" (CyL 6188). For the one already chosen: "collegae nostri iam delecti et ordinati" (CyL 6230-6231) and "cum post primum secundus esse non possit" (CyL 7704).

**18.** "A few years later plague struck." Pontius has only "Afterwards there broke out a dreadful plague" (anf05 27939-27940). He tells it after the persecution and before the exile of 257, so "a few years" stays inside what the old sources fix.

**23.** "He refused the rites, and the sentence called him an enemy of the Roman gods and their rites, and the leader of men gathered in a wicked conspiracy." The sentence reads: "plurimos nefariae tibi conspirationis homines adgregasti et inimicum te diis Romanis et religionibus sacris constituisti, nec te ... ad sectam caeremoniarum suarum reuocare potuerunt ... nequissimorum criminum auctor et signifer" (PonL 41561-41566). The brief folds "auctor et signifer" of crimes into "leader of men gathered". That is a fair compression with the force intact.

**26.** "A party said the bishop who had consecrated him had handed over the Scriptures during the persecution, so they made a rival bishop and set up altar against altar." The charge: "his Consecrator being falsely alleged to have been a Betrayer" (Opt 217); Felix accused of having "given up nor burned the most sacred Scriptures" (Opt 303). The act: "In this manner they went forth, and altar was raised against altar" (Opt 229). The "so" follows Optatus's own sequence. Optatus also names avarice, ambition and Lucilla as causes (Opt 207). The brief leaves those out and keeps to the rival side's own stated ground, which is fair.

**31.** "Then, he wrote, his fellow bishops pointed to his own town, brought over to the Catholics by fear of the emperors' laws, and he came to back those laws." The letter: "I have therefore yielded to the evidence afforded by these instances which my colleagues have laid before me ... my own town, which, although it was once wholly on the side of Donatus, was brought over to the Catholic unity by fear of the imperial edicts" (NP1 38244-38254). "Came to back" fairly gives "yielded".

## The heart

The heart is right for this subject. The sources do show a bishop deciding with clergy and people (CyL 2292-2295, 2917-2919) and the people gathered at his door (PonL 41509-41512). They show penance and peace (CyL 8427), shared work in the plague (anf05 27981-27986), judging (Pos 3137-3150), and the clergy staying with the people at the last (Pos 4556-4561). The opening scene of a priest and people weeping together fits it well.

It is told in time. The opening runs 116 words, and the essence's first sentence ends at about word 143. Its second sentence ends at about 171, a little past 150 but within the first minute as heard. The story's question ends at about 199. This is not a late heart; not blocking.
