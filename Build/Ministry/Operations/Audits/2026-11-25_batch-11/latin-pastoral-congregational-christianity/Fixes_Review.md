- Held answer inside the story's window (250s-430): Possidius saw that, as the Vandals overran Africa, of countless churches only three still stood, Carthage, Hippo and Cirta. Source: `possidius_vita-augustini_weiskotten1919.txt` 4025-4027 (Latin: "vix tres superstites ex innumera-bilibus ecdesiis, hoc est Carthaginensem, Hipponensem et Cirtensem, quae Dei beneficio excisae non sunt") and 4067-4070 (English). Witness: contemporary, eyewitness. Confidence: Documented. Sheet row W7 (with V9).
- Nothing held inside the window says what came of these churches after 430. Procopius on Cyprian's festival (W2) dates from the 530s and stays on the card. W1 (both churches claimed Cyprian) is not taken into the story: `cyprian-of-carthage` tells it, and the Connection bars making the Donatists Cyprian's heirs in the narrator's voice.
- Words for the story: "Of countless churches, only three still stood: Carthage, Hippo and Cirta." (+11). It goes after "overran Africa", before the fellow bishop's letter. Possidius places what Augustine saw (ch. 28) before the letter (ch. 30). Outline: What came of it ("In the story"), Ending 1, Connection, Do not say.
