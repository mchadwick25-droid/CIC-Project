**Verdict from this read: REVISE.** Six BLOCKING findings: 1 test line, 3 bare statements, 2 failing sentences that lose the thread. Some of these are the same sentence, as noted below.

## 1. The test, for a tradition

- **Who these people were.** Filled. "the Latin-speaking Christians of Roman Africa lived as congregations around one bishop, at Carthage under Cyprian and later at Hippo under Augustine."
- **What happened to them.** Filled. Persecution under Decius, the fallen asking to come back, plague, Cyprian's death in 258, the split at Carthage, the emperors' laws, the Vandals.
- **What their faith was about.** Filled, but thinly. I can say they believed the fallen could repent and come back, that the bishop decided with his people, and that the church cared for the poor and captives. What their own side believed about baptism is never said. I hear only the rival side's view ("one who was himself stained could not wash away sins").
- **Why the connection matters.** **Unfilled, BLOCKING.** I can say a party at Rome broke away under Novatian and was refused, and a rival church split off at Carthage. But the rival church has no name, so I could not find it on the tree or say who they became. The opening calls them "heretics", and a later sentence seems to say they were not heretics (see 3c). So I cannot say clearly what this church defended against them. Nothing tells me what came of these churches after the Vandals, or what they handed on. Change: name the rival church once where it is set up, if the outline holds the name, and let one sentence of what came of them (from the outline's "What came of it") come through what people did. Do not add a moral.

## 2. The opening

The first three sentences give **who** (the people of Hippo and their priest Augustine), **where** (Hippo, "a town in Roman Africa") and **why there** (the people want their feast, the Gladness, inside the church). **When** is missing: no century until "From the 250s to 430" in sentence 7. I am not lost about who or where, so this is not BLOCKING. A clause like "around the year 395" would help, if the outline holds a date.

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing

a. **BLOCKING.** "On the feast day itself, from the church of those he called heretics, came the sound of their feasting." (s6) I do not know who the heretics are, what a heretic is, or why the sound matters. Did his own people give up the feast? The image is lost. It also uses a label Part 1 says must be explained (counted again under 4).

b. MINOR. "At Carthage, in today's Tunisia, the crowd kept shouting for Cyprian, and he withdrew, for the brethren's peace, as he wrote." (s11) Shouting for him could mean cheering. I only guessed that the crowd wanted him dead. "For the brethren's peace" is old wording, and I did not know what it meant: to keep his people safe? To stop riots? The reason for the withdrawal is lost.

c. **BLOCKING.** "A Catholic writer of the next generation said the two churches held the same sacraments, and he kept the charge of breaking the creed for heretics." (s29) Meaning lost: I could not tell what "kept the charge ... for heretics" means, and it seems to contradict the opening, where Augustine calls the rival church heretics. "Sacraments" and "creed" are not explained. This is the one place that says what the split was about, so the thread breaks here. Change: say plainly what the writer held, for example that the rival church kept the same baptism and the same faith, and broke only with the unity of the church; and he did not call them heretics. Check the wording against the outline.

d. MINOR. "Cyprian was arrested, and the whole people of the brethren gathered in the street outside the door where he was held." (s23) Understood, but "the whole people of the brethren" sounds like a translation, not a narrator. Say "all his people gathered in the street".

## 4. Bare statements (all BLOCKING)

a. "those he called heretics" (s6). The label is named and not explained in place. See 3a.

b. "A party said the bishop who had consecrated him had handed over the Scriptures during the persecution" (s27). I do not know why handing over the Scriptures was so bad: to whom, and what happened to them. "Consecrated" is not explained. This charge is the cause of the whole split, so it needs its plain sense: handed the holy books to the persecutors to be burned, if the outline holds it.

c. "his own town, brought over to the Catholics by fear of the emperors' laws, and he came to back those laws" (s32). Which laws, and what did they do to the rival church? I hear "fear", but not what was feared. This is the coercion in the story, and it is left vague. Change: say in one clause what the laws did, as the outline gives it.
