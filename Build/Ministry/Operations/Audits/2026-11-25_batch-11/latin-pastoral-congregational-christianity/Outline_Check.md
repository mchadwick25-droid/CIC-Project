# Outline check: Latin Pastoral Christianity in Africa

## My own account of the heart (written before reading Outline.md)

From the census entry, the slate job, the source sheet and what the subject is known for.

- **The essence:** the Latin-speaking Catholic congregations of Roman North Africa, from about the 240s to 430, chiefly at Carthage under Cyprian and at Hippo under Augustine. What held them together was the bishop living among one local people: he decided with clergy and people, taught, disciplined, cared and judged, and stayed with them in danger, inside one Catholic Church he would not see divided.
- **Who they were:** the Christians of the African churches, who called their church the Catholic Church (S2); spoken as "the Latin churches of North Africa" or "the African Catholics".
- **When and where:** Carthage, about 250-258; Hippo Regius, about 391-430; Roman Africa from Decius to the Vandals.
- **What they faced:** persecution (Decius, then Valerian), and the fallen who wanted back; plague; raiders taking captives; rival churches (Novatian's party; then from early in the 300s the Donatist church in the same towns); quarrel with Rome over baptism; the old feasting customs of the people; and at the end the Vandal invasion and siege of Hippo, which ended this church's life there.
- **What they believed:** one Catholic Church with one bishop in each place; penance and "peace" open to the fallen (against the Novatianist view that communion with them stains the rest); Cyprian's view that washing outside the church is no baptism (against Stephen); Augustine against the Donatists' rule that the stained cannot cleanse, against the Manichaean two principles, and against the Pelagians on grace.
- **How they practiced:** the Eucharist (even in prison), penance and reconciliation, catechumen teaching before baptism, preaching several times a week, martyrs' feasts (and Augustine's fight against drunken ones), alms, ransom of captives, the bishop as judge, singing (the psalm against the Donatists).
- **What held them together:** the bishop and his people bound to each other (Cyprian's "nothing without your counsel and the people's consent"; Augustine's "live together or suffer together"), and the unity of the Catholic Church against rival altars.
- **What came of them:** heaviest, both sides of the Donatist split claimed Cyprian (Augustine answers the Donatists from him); Cyprian's cult kept at Carthage into the 500s (Procopius); Augustine's letter on clergy not deserting their people, kept by Possidius for every priest; the sermons and books copied and the library kept; Byzantine Africa honoring both (card). The later Western use (Calvin, Jansenists) is on census edges and belongs to the card.
- **Major contributions to check the outline keeps:** the fallen and penance; the rival-church question (Novatian, then the Donatists); the plague care beyond their own; the bishop as decided-with-the-people; staying with the people at the end.

## Heart compared

The outline's heart agrees with mine in substance: one bishop with clergy and people, through persecution, plague and a rival church, deciding and suffering together, at Carthage and then Hippo. No major contribution is left out of the heart; Pelagius and the later Western use go to the card, which fits the job (the congregation, not each bishop). The essence and opening come first, then the beats run in time order from 250 to 430, with no event in two beats and the ending not told early (the Hippo opening of 395 reaches ahead as a hook, which Part 2 allows).
