# Outline: Latin Pastoral Christianity in Africa

Census id `latin-pastoral-congregational-christianity` (census name: Latin Pastoral-Congregational Christianity). Kind: tradition (`kinds/tradition.md`). Source codes (K1, E3, V5 ...) are rows in `Source_Sheet.md`, which gives each file and line. Job (slate, 2026-11-25): the week-in, week-out life of the Latin churches of North Africa; the bishop and his people through persecution, plague, rival churches and the ordinary round of teaching, penance, care and judging, told at Carthage under Cyprian and at Hippo under Augustine. It tells the congregation, not each bishop's life.

## The heart

- **The essence:** From the 250s to 430, the Latin-speaking Christians of Roman Africa lived as congregations around one bishop, at Carthage under Cyprian and later at Hippo under Augustine. He taught them, fed their poor, bought back their captives, judged their quarrels and decided with them whom to take back after sin. Persecution, plague and a rival church in the same towns pressed on them, and the record keeps asking what a bishop and his people owed each other in danger. (K1, K3-K6, L1, P2-P5, R3, B2-B5, V3.)
- **Who they were:** the church of each town with its bishop, clergy and people; they called it the Catholic Church, and at Hippo they were "the Catholics" (S2). Cyprian calls the whole body the brethren (K1, K4). Spoken name: "the Latin-speaking churches of Roman Africa"; title "Latin Pastoral Christianity in Africa" (S1).
- **When and where:** Carthage, from the persecution of 250 to Cyprian's death in 258 (S4, D5); Hippo, from Augustine's ordination as priest to the Vandal siege of 430 (H1, V7). Between them, the split of the early 300s (R1-R3).
- **What they faced:** an emperor's order to sacrifice, and many who gave way (S4, S5); a stricter party with a second bishop (L2, L4); plague (P1); raiders who took Christians captive (P4); the Roman rites ordered again, and their bishop's death (S6, D3-D5); a second church that baptized again (R3-R5); the Vandals (S7, V6, V9).
- **What they believed:** that no one should be barred from penance and the hope of peace (L1); that mercy must reach beyond their own (P2); that one church's captivity was every church's (P4); that clergy must not leave their people when all share the danger (V3). They fought over who could give baptism (D1, R4, H5).
- **How they practiced:** the Eucharist taken to confessors in prison by priests in turn (K4); the bishop deciding with clergy and people (K5); penance and return to communion (L1, V4); shared work in the plague (P3); collections for captives (P5); hymns and psalms through the day (E6); first teaching of newcomers (B2); the bishop as judge of their disputes (B3); the church's silver melted for the poor (B5); the martyr's feast kept at his altar (B6, B7).
- **What held them together:** one bishop with his clergy and people, deciding and suffering together (K5, V3, D3); and care for one another as one body, captives and the poor first (P3-P5, B5).
- **What came of them:** the rival church that broke with them, and both churches' claim on Cyprian (W1); Carthage's festival of Cyprian into the 530s (W2); Augustine's letter on staying with the people, kept by Possidius for every priest (W3).

## Openings

1. **The Gladness at Hippo (recommended).** At Hippo, on the feast day of a former bishop, the people wanted their old feast in the church, with eating and drinking, which they called the Gladness. Their priest Augustine, serving under the aged bishop, read them Scripture against drunken feasts and pleaded with them; then Q1. On the feast day itself, a greater crowd came, men and women sang psalms and hymns in the church until the light failed, and from the church of those he called heretics came the sound of their feasting. Then the essence. (E1-E3, E6.) Keep the opening to these three sentences and Q1, with nothing added before the essence. It opens on the people themselves, a priest and his flock weeping together, with the rival church across the town in a single sound.
2. **Carthage in the plague.** The dead lay in the streets, and people fled their own sick; the bishop gathered his people and told them that to care only for their own was nothing great (P1, P2; Pontius's account). Weaker: it is a bishop's speech, and the plague is close to the barred plague questions.

## Story beats

2. **250-251: the fallen, and a second bishop's envoys.** Many gave way, some running to the forum of their own accord (S5), and then asked to come back. Cyprian held that no one should be barred from penance and the hope of peace (L1)~~; the bishops met and set a long penance (L3, narrated only)~~ [cut: card]. ## What came of it

1. **The rival church broke with it, and both claimed Cyprian.** The Donatists brought Cyprian's authority forward for baptizing again; Augustine answered that they should keep Cyprian's example of unity instead (W1). Told in `cyprian-of-carthage` and `donatism`; Not told in the story (no beat carries it after the cuts); card, with B6.
2. **Carthage's festival of Cyprian.** A century after Augustine, the people of Carthage revered Cyprian above all saints, kept a festival called the Cypriana at his great church by the sea, and named a storm after it; the Vandals had taken the church from the Christians (W2). For the card.
3. **The letter on staying.** Possidius copied Augustine's letter into his Life as necessary for every priest (W3). Told in the Ending. Defended.
4. **Byzantine Africa honored both bishops** (W5, from the census edge). For the card.
5. Who keeps this today: no held source says. Leave it out.

## Connection to carry into the story

What passed from this church to the Donatists was its own split: in the same towns, a second church with its own bishops, which said a bishop who had handed over the Scriptures could give nothing, and which baptized again those who came to it (R2-R5). The listener needs to understand that the quarrel was over who could rightly give baptism, not over what Christians believed of God: Optatus, a Catholic writer, says the two churches held the same sacraments, and keeps the creed-breaking charge for heretics (Opt 143, 155), while the rival side held that one who was stained could not wash sins away, and baptized again (R4, R5). It must come through what people did: a party making a rival bishop and raising altar against altar, the rival church feasting within earshot of Augustine's people at the Gladness, and Augustine's change of mind on the emperors' laws (R2-R3, E6, B9). The story must not say Cyprian caused the split or that the Donatists were his heirs in the narrator's voice; Augustine's answer that both sides claimed him is for the card (W1).

## Ending

1. **Recommended: the letter on staying (428-430).** As the Vandals overran Africa, a fellow bishop wrote to ask whether bishops and clergy should leave their churches (S7, V1). Augustine answered that ~~one man sought in particular may flee, if others stay with the church; but~~ [cut: card] when the danger falls on bishops, clergy and people alike, those who need others must not be abandoned by those they need (V2, V3). ~~At such times, he wrote, men and women of every age crowd into the church, some asking baptism, some reconciliation, some to do penance (V4).~~ [cut: card] Then Q3 (V5). ~~Possidius, who shared the siege of Hippo, says he preached in the church until his last illness, which came in the third month (V6-V8).~~ [cut: card] It ends on the congregation at its last, and answers the opening's question in the bishop's own letter. (Pos 4167-4592, 4897-4899, 4147-4148.)
2. **Alternative: the hymns fall silent.** After Q3, close on Possidius's sight of the province: churches stripped of their clergy, "the hymns and praises of God" gone from them, only Carthage, Hippo and Cirta standing, and Hippo itself, after Augustine's death, left by its people and burned (V9). It sees the opening's psalms again, ending in silence; it ends on an end, and adds about 35 words.
