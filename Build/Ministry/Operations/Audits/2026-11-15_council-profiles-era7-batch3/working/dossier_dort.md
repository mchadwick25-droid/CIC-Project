## 1. Held sources

**Acts (the synod's own record, and the States' acts on it)**

- **SCOTT** (Thomas Scott, Calvinist translator; preface signed Aston Sandford 15 March 1818, header l.8). English of the Latin Acta. Holds: the synod's own "Preface to the Reformed Churches" (history of the quarrel, published "by the authority of the States-General", from l.703; it stops at the opening and refers the reader to the acts for what was done, 4473-4479); the Canons, titled "The Judgment of the National Synod ..." with its Preface (4589-4965), Chapters I-V each with Rejection of Errors (5026-6935), Conclusion (6936-7056); "The Decision of the Synod concerning the Remonstrants" (7057-7581); "The Approbation of ... the States General", dated the Hague 2 July 1619 (7582-7672). Scott's footnotes and remarks (e.g. 4750-4800, 5589-5635, 7676ff) are his, and take the synod's side. Scott does **not** print the subscription list: he summarizes it in his own words (5589-5622).
- **NIEM** (H. A. Niemeyer, editor, 1840). **The Latin text of the Canons** ("24. Canones Dordraceni", editor's note 3748-3770; the Preface ending 44030-44040; "Sententia de divina praedestinatione ... quam Synodus Dordrechtana Verbo Dei consentaneam ... iudicat", 44045-44056; Caput I from 44058; Caput II at about 44760) **with a subscription list after Chapter I** (44505-44760): president, assessors and scribes, then "Ex Magna Britannia", Palatinate, Hesse, Helvetia, Wetteravia, Geneva, Bremen, Emden, the Dutch professors, and the provinces. The OCR loses lines at page turns (South and North Holland run together at 44654-44699), so **I have not counted the names** (rule 5). Second witness for wording.
- **BRANDT**, as carrier of a state act: the States-General's resolution banishing the cited Remonstrants, "Done at ... the Hague, this 15th of July" 1619 (50840-50935, Brandt's English; OCR garbled).

**Participants**

- **HALES**: John Hales "of Eaton-College", the English ambassador's chaplain, letters to Sir Dudley Carleton datelined Dort, late November 1618 to early February 1619 (header l.2, l.8; first letter 74-125, last near 6095). He arrived after the opening (75-79: "We arrived at Dort this last night"). Observer, not a member; he writes for Carleton. Datelines and many day-numbers are OCR-garbled ("Upon Monday the Fr of January", 4748).
- **Balcanqual** (the Scottish member of the British delegation): one letter to Carleton, quoted in English by BRANDT 17512-17537. Participant, but reached only through Brandt.
- **Episcopius's oration** (7 December 1618), given at length in BRANDT 6740-7586 (Brandt's English; Brandt says it is "inserted word for word in the Synodical Acts" for a later paper, 8219-8220, not for this speech). Hales summarizes it (2706-2800).

- **CONF-R**: the Remonstrant Confession of 1621 in an anonymous English of 1676 (header l.8). Its preface speaks of Dort (2855-2945). Post-synod.

## 2. Candidate opening scenes

**A. The empty chair (13 November 1618), BRANDT 2263-2302** [OCR: verify]. The procession, then the seating: "On the left side, over against them sate the English Divines; the next place was kept void for the French" (2295-2297). Lydius's Latin prayer: "'Thou hast confounded our language, and the conclusion of our foreign war is become almost the beginning of a civil one'" (2333-2334). Pairs with QUICK 13311-13314: the French deputies Chamier, Du Moulin, Chauve and Rivet, "on their way to the Synod of Dort ... were frighted back again by a Prohibition issued out against them by the King then Reigning, Louis XIII" (OCR reads "Uort, in the year 1648" and "Lois": the year is an OCR or print slip for 1618, so verify). Brandt is a hostile (Remonstrant) narrator, though the seating is neutral detail.

**B. The long empty table (6 December 1618), HALES 1877-1883**, eyewitness [OCR: verify]: "There is in the midst of the synod house a long table, set, as it seems, for them; for it hath hitherto been void, no man sitting at it: here chairs and forms being set, they were willed to sit down." Then the clash over why they had come. Episcopius said they came "ad collationem instituendam" (1897; my tr.: "to hold a conference"). Polyander answered (1913-1927): "They were called not to conference, but to propose their opinions with their reasons, and leave it to the synod to judge of them. The synod would be a judge and not a party." This puts the whole quarrel in one exchange. **Strongest scene for an opening.**

**C. "Exite" (14 January 1619, 57th session)**: HALES 4893-4921 (eyewitness, relayed in a letter dated "F5 ... Jan. 1619", 4931) and BRANDT 17402-17480 (Brandt's English of the speech). Bogerman: "quo coepistis pede eodem cedite, with a lie you made your entrance into the synod, with a lie you take your leave of it" (HALES 4897-4899; he attributes the gibe to "one of the foreigners", 4896). It ends: "Quamobrem vos delegatorum et synodi nomine dimitto, exite" (4916-4917; my tr.: "Therefore in the name of the delegates and of the synod I dismiss you: go out"). Episcopius, leaving: "Dominus Deus judicabit de fraudibus et mendaciis" (4919-4920; my tr.: "The Lord God will judge of the frauds and the lies"). Best as a middle beat, not the opening, since it is the dismissal, not the room before it.

**D. The Canons read aloud (6 May 1619), BRANDT 43019-43078** [OCR: verify]. A procession to the Great Church, a vast crowd, then the reading: "the Synodical Scribe, Sebastian Damman, ascended the same Pulpit, and read the Introduction ... till his voice began to fail, by reason of the bigness of the church, and the thick steam proceeding from the great multitudes of spectators" (43061-43064). Festus Hommius took over until he too was "tired and hoarse" (43066-43067). A strong closing-side scene.

## 3. Issue

- **The five points.** The canons' Preface (acts) names the opponents: "James (Jacobus) Arminius and his followers, holding out the name of Remonstrants, by various errors, old as well as new; at first covertly, and then openly assaulted" the Belgic church (SCOTT 4663-4667). The synod judged "the known five heads of doctrine" (4716-4717).
- **The Remonstrance of 1610** (the Remonstrants' five articles) is held only in Calder's English after Chamberlayne's English of Brandt (CALDER 5054-5058, 5115-5160). Two points matter for a fair telling. Article I: God "hath decreed to elect to everlasting life, all those who, through his grace, believe in Jesus Christ, and ... persevere to the end" (5117-5119). Article V leaves perseverance open: whether believers "might not, through their negligence, apostatize ... must first be more fully inquired into, under the guidance of the holy scriptures" (5153-5159).
- **Toleration or a synod.** Remonstrant own words (SECREM 166-178, telling the 1611 Hague conference) [OCR: verify]: "Remonstrantes declararunt nullam concordiae rationem sibi certiorem videri, quam ut inter partes statuatur mutua Tolerantia: Reliqui Pastores, qui Contra-Remonstrantes postea etiam appellari coeperunt, responderunt nullam sibi videri convenientiorem rationem, quam ut negotium referatur ad judicium Ecclesiarum Reformatarum ... ut Synodus Nationalis ... convocari ... possit." My tr.: "The Remonstrants declared that no surer way to concord seemed to them than mutual toleration between the parties. The other pastors, who later also came to be called Contra-Remonstrants, answered that no fitter way seemed to them than to refer the matter to the judgment of the Reformed churches, ... so that a National Synod could be called." But the Remonstrants too had asked for "a lawful synod" in 1610, on their terms (CALDER 5112-5118: they asked not to be deposed "until a lawful synod be convoked by the authority of the illustrious States"). Do not tell it as "Remonstrants against any synod".
- **Who rules the church: the province or the States General.** The synod's history: delegates of the States of Holland favouring the Remonstrants protested that the decree injured "the majesty, the right, and finally, the liberty of that province" (SCOTT 4173-4178). Maclaine, in a note quoted by Scott: Oldenbarnevelt "maintained that the States General had no sort of authority in matters of religion" (SCOTT 4545-4548). Politics: in Scott's own note, "the Remonstrants favored his [Maurice's] opponents, and the Contra-Remonstrants were attached to him" (4581-4583).
- **The conditions of the synod.** Remonstrants (Brandt's report of their September 1618 paper) objected that only "Thirteen or Fifteen" were allowed to appear for a whole body (BRANDT 3814-3826). Their 7 December complaint and Polyander's "judge and not a party" are in §2 B.

Sides: synod majority (Dutch Contra-Remonstrant delegates and the foreign divines), witnessed by the acts (SCOTT, NIEM), Hales and the canons' prefaces. The Remonstrants are witnessed by SECREM (own words, 1617), the speeches in Brandt and Hales, CONF-R (1621/1676), and their historians Brandt, Calder, Tideman and Knappert.

## 4. Called by

- **The States General** (acts). The synod's history: they "determine, that the city Dordrecht (or Dort) should be the place of its meeting; the day, the first of the next November" (SCOTT 4171-4172). The Approbation (a state act): "it seemed proper to us ... to convene at Dordrecht a national Synod of all Federated Belgium" (7590-7592). The canons' Preface: the States General, "with the counsel and direction of the most Illustrious and valiant the Prince of Orange, ... called a Synod at Dordrecht by their own authority" (4682-4689).
- **Contested emphasis.** Mosheim (quoted by Scott 4537-4538): "a Synod was convoked at Dordrecht in the year 1618, by the counsels and influence of prince Maurice". Maclaine (4538-4545) answers that Mosheim "always forgets to mention the order issued by the States General". Calder (Remonstrant side): "the Calvinistic party, with Maurice at their head, carried their measures in obtaining the appointment of a national synod" (10121-10123).
- **Invitations abroad**: letters to James I, "the deputies of the reformed churches of the kingdom of France", the Elector Palatine, the Elector of Brandenburg, the Landgrave of Hesse, the four Swiss republics (Zurich, Bern, Basel, Schaffhausen), the counts of the Wetterau, and Geneva, Bremen and Emden (SCOTT 4453-4462).

## 5. Attended

- **The foreign delegations, by name** (BRANDT 2310-2321) and **signed** (NIEM 44525-44637). Great Britain: George Carleton, bishop of Llandaff; John Davenant; Samuel Ward; Thomas Goad; Walter Balcanqual "Scoto-Britannus" (NIEM 44527-44540; SCOTT 5591-5618). Joseph Hall came first and was replaced by Goad on 17 January (BRANDT 24992-24996: Goad "was sent by the King of Great Britain to supply the place of Joseph Hall, then Dean of Worcester"; HALES 5056-5081). Palatinate: Scultetus, Tossanus, Alting. Hesse: Cruciger, Steinius, Angelocrator, Goclenius. Switzerland, five: Breitinger (Zurich), Rütimeyer (Bern), Beck and Meyer (Basel), Koch (Schaffhausen) (NIEM 44566-44590). Wetterau: Alsted, with Bisterfeld (BRANDT 2316-2317) or Georgius Fabricius (NIEM 44591-44597). **The two lists differ here; leave it open.** Geneva: Diodati and Tronchin. Bremen: Martinius, Isselburg, Crocius. Emden: Eilshemius, Grimershemius.
- **France absent**: the place "kept void for the French" (BRANDT 2296-2297); the king's prohibition (QUICK 13311-13314). Scott's heading, "Theologians of ... Great Britain, Germany, and France, were present" (SCOTT 4597-4598), is his English of the title. Held lines say the French did not come, and I did not trace the Latin title in NIEM, so leave the heading's "France" untold.
- **Counts, each with its witness (keep apart):**
  - Brandt: "instead of six and twenty home Members, there came besides the five Professors, six and thirty Ministers, and twenty Elders, making together fifty-eight persons, besides two Remonstrant Ministers, and one Elder ... out of the Province of Utrecht" (2325-2329). Foreigners "not more than the appointed number of eight and twenty" (2329-2331). **The sum does not close as read** (5 + 36 + 20 = 61, not 58), so verify the numbers on the page image.
  - Knappert: five professors, then "37 predikanten en 19 ouderlingen" (HEER 4055-4059; my tr.: "37 ministers and 19 elders").
  - Tideman: "haar 107 Hoog Ed. Mog., Hoog Geleerde en Hoog Eerw. leden" (TID 1341; my tr.: "its 107 members, the Noble and Mighty, the Learned and the Reverend", that is with the lay commissioners) on 5 March 1619.
  - Scott's own count: British signers "with very many others ... amounting to above eighty" (SCOTT 5619-5620; Scott's words, not the acts).
  - A signed list **is held** (NIEM 44505-44760), not counted (OCR breaks).
- **Officers**: "Johannes Bogermannus, Pastor Ecclesiae Leovardiensis, Synodi Praeses" (NIEM 44508-44509); assessors Rolandus and Faukelius; scribes Damman and Hommius (44511-44518; HEER 4026-4028). Lay commissioners presided by Martinus Gregorius (BRANDT 44466-44467); their secretary Daniel Heinsius (HEER 4044-4045, Knappert).

## 6. Dates

- **Summoned for 1 November 1618** (SCOTT 4172).
- **Opened Tuesday 13 November 1618** (SCOTT 4473-4475, the synod's history; BRANDT 2263; CALDER 10127). Lydius's sermon in Dutch and de Peurs's in French (BRANDT 2263-2266).
- **Remonstrants arrive 6 December** (HALES 1836-1840; BRANDT 5617-5620); **Episcopius's oration Friday 7 December** (HALES 2654, 2706-2711; BRANDT 6757ff).
- **Dismissal Monday 14 January 1619, the 57th session** (BRANDT 17151; TID 1315 "14 Jan."). Hales's day number is OCR-garbled (4748).
- **Collecting their views from their books**: finished by the 99th session, Thursday 5 March; the colleges' judgments 6-21 March; the Canons settled "tot in de 131e zitting (den 13den April)" (TID 1316-1340; Tideman's dates).
- **Canons published 6 May 1619** in the Great Church (BRANDT 43019; the States' Approbation, "published, on the sixth of May last past", SCOTT 7604-7605). But Scott's heading to the Judgment reads "Published on the fifth of May, A. D. 1619" (SCOTT 4602). **Two dates in one held book; tell 6 May with the witness, and note the heading.**
- **Foreign divines dismissed** at the 154th session, 9 May (BRANDT 44463-44475). Calder: "On the ninth of May, the synod closed its labours" (15266). Brandt has sessions going on after that.
- **Last session: the 180th, Wednesday 29 May 1619** (BRANDT 48640; Knappert: sessions "tot 180", HEER 4070). Rutgers cites a private minute of the "170e zitting, van Dinsdag 28 Mei 1619" (RUTG 22430). The numbering differs from Brandt's (179th on the 28th, 48635-48638); possibly OCR, so leave it open.
- **State acts, kept apart**: the States' Approbation of the decrees, 2 July 1619 (SCOTT 7663-7664); the banishment resolution, 15 July 1619 (BRANDT 50934-50935). Oldenbarnevelt executed 13 May 1619 (BRANDT 53914). Grotius and Hogerbeets "condemned on the 12th of May to a perpetual Imprisonment" (BRANDT 54284-54286; Brandt's date), moved to Loevestein on 5-6 June (54287-54291). **None of these is the synod's act.**

## 7. Decided (acts first)

1. **The Canons, five heads, each with a Rejection of Errors** (SCOTT 5026-6935; Latin NIEM 44045ff).
   - I.7, election: "election is the immutable purpose of God, by which, before the foundations of the world were laid, he chose, out of the whole human race ... a certain number of men, neither better nor worthier than others" (SCOTT 5123-5129). Latin begins NIEM 44058.
   - I.9: election "is not made from any foreseen faith ... but unto faith" (SCOTT 5158-5161).
   - I.15 (OCR "16"), reprobation: some are "passed by in the eternal election of God", left "in the common misery, into which they had, by their own fault, cast themselves" (SCOTT 5250-5257).
   - II.3: Christ's death is "of infinite value and price, abundantly sufficient to expiate the sins of the whole world" (SCOTT 5661-5664). Latin: "abunde sufficiens ad totius mundi peccata expianda" (NIEM 44772-44773, OCR "abonde suiEciens ... lotias mnndi"). Then II.8: its saving efficacy is for "all those, and those only, who were from eternity chosen to salvation" (SCOTT 5702-5711).
   - V.8: the elect "neither totally fall from faith and grace, nor finally continue in their falls and perish" (SCOTT 6610-6612).
2. **Unanimity claimed** for the canons: "confirmed by the unanimous consent of all, and every one of, the members of this whole Synod" (SCOTT 7013-7016). In the synod's history: all subscribed, "not one indeed excepted, or declining to do it" (4494-4495).
3. **Sentence on the Remonstrants** (SCOTT 7057-7181). It asks all to cast away "the five known articles of the Remonstrants, which are erroneous, and mere hiding places of errors" (7068-7070). The names "Remonstrants" and "Contra-Remonstrants" should "be blotted out by a perpetual oblivion" (7089-7091). The cited ministers are declared "convicted and guilty persons" (7130-7131): "the Synod interdicts the before cited persons from every ecclesiastical service, and abdicates them from their offices ... until by earnest repentance ... they satisfy the church" (7140-7144). Others are left to the provincial synods, classes and consistories, and none may be admitted "who refuse to subscribe these synodical constitutions" (7149-7170). It asks the States to make the doctrine "alone, and publicly heard in these regions" (7204-7205).
   - **Not all foreign delegates joined this personal censure.** BRANDT 39385-39388 [badly garbled, verify]: "The English and the He[ssians?] neither agreed to, nor [sig]ned it, for they did not think fit to concern themselves with those personal matters". Do not use until checked against the page image.
4. **Church business after the doctrine**: Brandt gives later sessions on the church order and liturgy (48586-48625). The synod's share in the Bible translation (HAAK 325-380, citing "Acta Synodalia, Sess. 7"). Hales on the decree that children of non-Christian parents not be baptized until of age (1738-1747), with his comment "A strange decision" (1747).
5. **The oath**: the synod bound itself "that it would take the Holy Scriptures alone as the rule of judgment" (SCOTT 4710-4712).

## 8. Outcome

- **The States ratify** (state act, 2 July 1619): "no other doctrine concerning the aforesaid five heads of doctrine be taught or propagated in the churches of these regions" (SCOTT 7635-7638).
- **Banishment** (state act, 15 July 1619): the cited ministers are to be "brought and conducted ... out of all the United Provinces" (BRANDT 50913-50917), "excepting only Henricus Leo, who subscribed the said Act" (50905-50906; the "Act" binds them to cease all ministry, 50856-50865). Knappert: 3 July summons, refusal, and "Den 6en Juli werden 11 predikanten naar Waalwijk, in Brabant gebracht" (HEER 5510-5518; my tr.: "On 6 July eleven ministers were taken to Waalwijk in Brabant"; OCR "ben").
- **The Remonstrants organize during the synod.** On 5-6 March 1619, "in het naburig Rotterdam een 15, 16 leeraars en opzieners ... in een burgerhuis" (TID 1346-1348; my tr.: "in nearby Rotterdam some 15 or 16 ministers and elders, in a private house") laid the ground of a separate Remonstrant body (1353-1360). Tideman's account.
- **Reach beyond the Dutch church:**
  - **France**: the National Synod of Alez (1 October 1620, QUICK 13405) "decreed that the Articles of the said National Council at Dort, should be read in full Synod", received them "by a common unanimous consent", and required ministers to swear to them. ## 9. The condemned party's own voice

Truth "ought to be dearer and more acceptable to us than the whole World" (7585; OCR "W099") [OCR: verify].
- **At the synod, as Hales heard it**: Episcopius on reprobation, "quod maxime nos aegre habet" (HALES 3748; my tr.: "the thing that most troubles us"). ## 11. Contested points

1. **Why the Remonstrants were dismissed.** Synod: they "rejected the decision of the Synod, and refused to answer to their interrogatories" (SCOTT 4722-4724, acts). Remonstrants: they were treated as defendants by their own adversaries. Brandt writes of their objection to the judges (3808-3826), and the CONF-R preface (2940-2944). Inside the majority's own camp: Crocius objected (HALES 4981-5003; BRANDT 17543-17553); Balcanqual (BRANDT 17512-17537); Hales: "some exceptions taken by the deputies themselves" (4963-4964).
2. **Episcopius's status: member or party.** Hales: Episcopius "was to have been of the synod if he would have brought his credential letters ... But he refused it, because in the remonstrants cause he was to be a party" (2015-2019). Knappert (Remonstrant): the synod cited him "alsof hij geen afgevaardigde der hoogeschool ware" (HEER 3985-3986; my tr.: "as if he were not a deputy of the university").
3. **Thirteen or fifteen.** Thirteen cited (HEER 3986-3998; BRANDT 5592; States' decree BRANDT 50849); fifteen dismissed (BRANDT 17486; ARMAG 2607), counting the two Utrecht members who joined. Not a real contradiction; tell both.
4. **The dismissal formula.** Hales: "vos ... dimitto, exite" (4916-4917). Calder: "Dimittimini, exite, depart, begone" (14426-14427). Brandt: "I therefore dismiss you in the name of the Lords Commissioners, and of this Synod; Be gone" (17470-17471). Who said the parting line: Hollingerus "Depart from the Assembly of the wicked", which "Hales ascribes ... to Sapma" (BRANDT 17479-17481; HALES 4920: Sapma, "Exeo ex ecclesia malignantium").
5. **Length of the oration.** Brandt: "lasted an hour and half" (7592). No held line gives the census's "nearly two hours". Brandt's "above two hours" (8221) is the reading of a later Remonstrant paper.
6. **Date of publication**: 6 May (BRANDT 43019; States' Approbation, SCOTT 7604-7605) or "fifth of May" (SCOTT heading, 4602).
7. **When the synod ended**: 9 May per Calder (15266); 29 May, 180th session, per Brandt (48640) and Knappert (HEER 4070). Rutgers's "170e zitting ... 28 Mei" (RUTG 22430) does not match Brandt's numbering.
8. **Home members' count**: 36 ministers and 20 elders (BRANDT 2326-2327; sum garbled) or 37 and 19 (HEER 4059).
9. **Who called it**: the States General (acts) or Maurice (Mosheim; Calder). See §4.
10. **Wetterau signer**: Bisterfeld (BRANDT 2317) or Georgius Fabricius (NIEM 44595).
11. **"Calvinist."** The acts call their doctrine that of "the reformed churches" (SCOTT 6942-6944, 7028-7030). The word "Calvinian" is Heylin's, a hostile Englishman quoted by Scott: "a representative of all the Calvinian churches of Europe, (except those of France,) some few Divines of England being added to them" (SCOTT 4974-4977). Scott calls Heylin a "decided Anti Calvinist" (4986). Knappen calls the canons "highly Calvinistic" (KNAP 2995), and Calder speaks of "the Calvinistic party" (10121).
12. **Lies.** Bogerman's charge that Episcopius lied about having no other copy of his speech (BRANDT 17402-17406) is answered by Knappert: Episcopius was accused of untruth "op grond van eene stellig verkeerd gehoorde uitdrukking" (HEER 4080-4083; my tr.: "on the ground of an expression certainly misheard").

## 17. Length

**The full 2:30 ceiling (about 400 words) is carried, without padding.** The held record has the synod's own canons and sentence (English and Latin, with a signed list) and the States' approbation. It has an eyewitness outside both parties for the decisive weeks (Hales), the condemned side in its own words before, at and after the synod (SECREM, the speeches, CONF-R), and the reach through events (Alez 1620, North America). Two limits shape it. The minutes are not held, so the sessions come through Hales and the Remonstrant historians; say so once, early. Hales stops in February, so 6 May rests on Brandt. Do not tell the "two hundred deposed" figure.
