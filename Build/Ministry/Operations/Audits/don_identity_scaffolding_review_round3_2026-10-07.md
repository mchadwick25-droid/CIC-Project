## Verdict: REVISE

## Finding A. SUBSTANTIAL: the heresy charge did not begin with the law of 405

The corrected text in both records says the charge was schism and "never heresy" for the first ninety years, and that the word heretic "arrived only with the emperor's law of 405" (creed) or "came with the emperor's law of 405 and Augustine's pen" (Jesus). The creed text adds "Augustine adopted that word." The divergence_notes say "until the emperor's law of 405" in both records, and the creed record's second position says "the anti-Donatist literature before 405 charges schism and not heresy".

The vendored file contradicts this:
- Answer to Petilian, dated A.D. 400 in the volume's contents (npnf104 lines 661-665), Book II: "And yet we are Catholics ... but you are heretics, who, on account of charges, whether true or false, which you have brought against certain men, are unwilling to maintain Christian charity" (line 16579); "when the very object of our negotiations with you is that you should cease to be heretics ... it is dissension and division that make you heretics" (line 17835); "When, then, you come over from your heresy to us" (lines 17835-17836). The creed record already cites `don.source.augustine-answer-to-petilian`.
- Letter 185 ch. 7, the passage the records cite: before the laws were sent into Africa, Augustine and other bishops asked that "heresy should absolutely cease to be" not be ordained (line 19624), complained of "the furious violence of the heretics" (line 19625), and sought Theodosius' law "against heretics of all kinds" confirmed "against the Donatists, who denied that they were heretics" (lines 19625-19626). The editor's note dates that council to 401 (line 19631); Boyd dates the petition to 404 (line 6769). The denial the records quote is itself evidence that the charge was being made before 405.

What the sources support: the early polemic (Optatus, c. 366-393) charges schism and not heresy; by about 400 Augustine was calling the Donatists heretics because of their separation; the law of 405 declared them heretics and named them beside the Manichaeans; throughout, the charge rested on separation, not on the creed. The rest of the correction (separation, not creed; Augustine's 417 concession; the Donatist denial) stands.

Fix: in both `text` fields, replace the "first ninety years ... never heresy" sentence and the 405 sentence with the supported sequence, in the world's voice. For example (creed): "For most of a century our opponents called us schismatics, not heretics, because the creed was identical on both sides. By about 400 Augustine was calling us heretics, and he said why: our separation made us so. The emperor's law of 405 then placed us beside the Manichaeans." For the Jesus text: "For most of a century the charge against us was schism, not heresy, because both sides said the same creed. Then Augustine's pen and the emperor's law of 405 called us heretics." Delete "Augustine adopted that word." In both divergence_notes, replace "until the emperor's law of 405" with "until Augustine's writing around 400 and the emperor's law of 405". In the creed record's second position, replace "before 405" with "of the first decades (Optatus)" and "after 405" with "from about 400". Add `don.source.augustine-answer-to-petilian` with locus "Book II (lines 16579, 17835) - 'you are heretics'; 'it is dissension and division that make you heretics'" to the Jesus record, and add the same loci to the creed record's existing Petilian entry. Re-run readability after the edit. OG-25 in `Open_Gaps_Tracking.md` currently says the correction closes OG-23 item 12; it should not close until this finding is fixed and rechecked.

## Finding B. MINOR: "Augustine himself declared that most of us held the same belief"

Line 19321 has the Donatists declaring it: "the greater part of them declare that they hold entirely the same belief". Augustine's own statement in that passage is that the Donatists "acknowledge the unity of substance in the Trinity", with the qualification that "some even of them have said that the Son was inferior to the Father". The creed text makes Augustine the declarer of the majority belief. The creed record's second position, "Augustine conceded the creed was the same", drops "most of" and the qualification.

Fix: in the creed text, "Yet in 417 he himself wrote that we confessed one substance in the Trinity, and that most of us said we held the same belief as his church." In the second position, "Augustine conceded that the confession of the Trinity was the same for most of us."

## Finding C. MINOR: "not on anything we said about Christ"

The new sentence in the Jesus text is correct about what the heresy charge rested on. But Optatus Book I.8 (lines 133-135, and line 85) charges Parmenian with saying "untrue things concerning the Flesh of Christ". That was a criticism, not a heresy charge, so the sentence does not need to change. It does bear on the same record's "word for word, creed for creed", which this diff did not touch.

Fix: none in this diff. Log the Optatus I.8 criticism in the world's gap file against `who-jesus-was-among-us` (see below).
