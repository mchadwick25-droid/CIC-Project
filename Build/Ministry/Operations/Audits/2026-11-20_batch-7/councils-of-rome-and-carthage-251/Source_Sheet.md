# Source sheet: Councils of Carthage and Rome on the lapsed (251)

Researcher's sheet for batch 7 (2026-11-20). The job: what to do with the lapsed after Decius: Cyprian's wait for a council, the African middle way of long penance case by case, Cornelius's sixty bishops at Rome, and the split it set off with Novatian's church. The spine is the lapsed, not the rival bishop. Shape: Council. **Conventions.** Line numbers are each file's own. XML quotes are given with tags and notes removed; Latin as printed (u for v). Square brackets are the translator's or editor's.

**Language.** Cyprian and the African bishops wrote in Latin: held clean (EP, LAPS) and in English (ANF). Cornelius wrote in Greek; his letters survive only as Eusebius quotes or sums them up, held in a damaged Greek scan (EUSG), with Rufinus's Latin in the same volume, and in English (EUS). Socrates wrote in Greek: held in scan OCR (SOCG2) and English (SOC).

**Witness kinds and gaps.** The council's own record is lost: Cyprian says the heads of its decisions were written in "a little book" (E6), and Leclercq: "De ce concile, rien ne reste" (HEFL 145). What Carthage decided reaches us through Cyprian, who called the council and defends it (Ep. 55, written after both councils of 251, to a bishop shaken by Novatian's letters). What Rome decided reaches us through Eusebius (about 325), summing up Cornelius's lost letters; Cornelius wrote as Novatian's rival. Novatian's own rule reaches us through Socrates, about 439, a historian kind to his church (SOC-ed; see the `novatian` sheet, D10). No letter of the lax party or of the lapsed themselves survives except as Cyprian quotes or describes it; the confessors' one short letter (E3) is the exception.

## 1. Setting

**S1. Time.** The emperor Decius's order that all should sacrifice, at the start of 250: "Soon after the commencement of the persecution of Decius (at the beginning of the year 250)" (HEF 6284–6289). Cyprian away from Carthage "from February 250 until the month of April or May 251" (HEF 6307–6310). The councils: Carthage in spring 251, Rome in autumn 251 (S4). Documented (year); months Dominant Modern Reconstruction. Plain words: "in 250 and 251".

**S2. Place.** Carthage, chief city of Roman Africa (today's Tunisia), and Rome (CEN `modernCountries`). Plain words: "Carthage, in what is now Tunisia, and Rome".

**S3. Tradition.** The Latin-speaking churches of Africa (tree: "Latin Pastoral-Congregational Christianity", short "Latin Pastoral") and Rome (tree: "The Roman Church in the Third Century", short "Third-Century Rome"); the council condemned Novatian's followers (tree: "Novatianism") (CEN; ENTRY `traditions`). Say "the churches of Africa and of Rome" aloud; never the tree's long labels.

**S4. Dates.** Hefele: Cyprian "returned to Carthage, a year after the festival of Easter in 251; and he wrote his book de lapsis as a preparation for the Synod which he assembled soon afterwards, probably during the month of May 251" (HEF 6365–6369). "A year after" is the English print's reading; Leclercq's French has "revint à Carthage après les fêtes de Pâques de 251" (HEFL 103–104), and Cyprian himself expected to come "after Easter-day" (Ep. 43.7, ANF 32560–32561). Say "after Easter 251". Rome: "Cornelius assembled at Rome in the autumn—probably in the month of October 251—a synod composed of sixty bishops" (HEF 6482–6486). McGiffert follows Hefele: "The Carthaginian synod met in the spring of 251 ... Hefele thinks about October" (EUS-ed 38278–38281). Leclercq: *On the Lapsed* and *On Unity* were read at the council of May 251, "Cette date est absolument certaine" (HEFL 138–141; his claim, after Monceaux). No held source of the time names either month (ENTRY `datesNote`). Dominant Modern Reconstruction.

## 2. Events

**E1. 250: the fall.** Cyprian, *On the Lapsed* 8 (written spring 251): "ante aciem multi uicti, sine congressione prostrati nec hoc sibi reliquerunt ut sacrificare idolis uiderentur inuiti. ultro ad forum currere, ad mortem sponte properare, quasi hoc olim cuperent ... quot illic a magistratibus uespera urgente dilati sunt, quot ne eorum differretur interitus et rogauerunt!" (LAPS 345–352). Paraphrase: many were beaten before the battle, thrown down without a fight; they did not even leave themselves the excuse of sacrificing unwillingly. They ran to the forum of their own accord. How many were put off by the magistrates as evening pressed on, and how many begged that their ruin not be put off! Also: "ad prima statim uerba minantis inimici maximus fratrum numerus fidem suam prodidit" (LAPS 303–304: at the enemy's first threatening words, a very great number of the brothers betrayed their faith). Contemporary, Cyprian's own words; he was in hiding at the time, so this is a bishop's account of his people, not an eyewitness's (Cyprian sheet E4). Documented as his account. The Cyprian sheet cites the phrase "ultro ad forum currere" (its E6) but its outline does not quote it.

**E2. Two kinds of fall: sacrifice and the bought certificate.** Ep. 55.14: one who took a certificate may say he had learned he must not sacrifice, and so "ad magistratum uel ueni uel alio eunte mandaui, Christianum me esse, sacrificare mihi non licere, ad aras diaboli me uenire non posse, dare me ob hoc praemium, ne quod non licet faciam" (EP 7860–7881; ANF 33705–33708: "I either went or charged some other person going to the magistrate, to say that I am a Christian, that I am not allowed to sacrifice, that I cannot come to the devil's altars, and that I pay a price for this purpose, that I may not do what is not lawful for me to do"). "Etsi manus pura sit et os eius feralis cibi contagia nulla polluerint, conscientiam tamen eius esse pollutam" (EP 7882–7884: his hand is clean, but his conscience is stained). Hefele: the *sacrificati* had "really sacrificed to the gods"; the *libellatici* had, "without making a formal act of apostasy, ... made them give them false attestations" (HEF 6431–6435). Documented. Plain words: "some had offered the sacrifice; others had paid an official for a certificate saying they had".

**E3. The pressure to take them back at once: the letters of peace.** Christians who had suffered for the faith (confessors and martyrs) were giving the fallen written pardons.
- Ep. 20.2 (Cyprian to the Roman clergy, 250): those who had sacrificed or taken certificates were going round the martyrs and pressing the confessors, "ut sine ullo discrimine adque examine singulorum darentur cotidie libellorum milia contra euangelii legem" (EP 3006–3011; ANF 30201–30205: "so that, without any discrimination or examination of the individuals themselves, thousands of certificates were daily given, contrary to the law of the Gospel"). Documented, his own words; "thousands" is his figure.
- Ep. 15.4 (to the martyrs and confessors): "audio enim quibusdam sic libellos fieri ut dicatur communicet ille cum suis ... et possunt nobis et uiceni et triceni et amplius offerri" (EP 2467–2473; ANF 29937–29944: certificates given saying "Let such a one be received to communion along with his friends", so that "twenty or thirty or more" might be presented). He asks them to name each person (EP 2473–2476). Documented.
- Ep. 23, the confessors to Cyprian, whole text: "Scias nos uniuersos quibus ad te ratio constiterit quid post commissum egerint dedisse pacem, et hanc formam per te et aliis episcopis innotescere uolumus. optamus te cum sanctis martyribus pacem habere. praesente de clero et exorcista et lectore Lucianus scripsit." (EP 3389–3393; ANF 30382–30393: "Know that, to all, concerning whom the account of what they have done since the commission of their sin has been, in your estimation, satisfactory, we have granted peace ... We bid you to have peace with the holy martyrs. Lucianus wrote this"). Contemporary, the lax side's own words. Documented. Not chosen as a quote (see Outline); told in paraphrase.
- Leclercq: the confessors gave *libelli* "impliquant un pardon complet, sans conditions, sans le visa des évêques" (HEFL 119–124, after Batiffol). Editor's summary.

**E4. Cyprian's wait.** From hiding he refused to settle the question alone.
- Ep. 55.4: "de eo tamen quod statuendum esset circa causam lapsorum distuli, ut cum quies et tranquillitas data esset et episcopis in unum conuenire indulgentia diuina permitteret, tunc communicato et librato de omnium conlatione consilio statueremus quid fieri oporteret, si quis uero ante consilium nostrum ... lapsis temere communicare uoluisset, ipse a communicatione abstineretur" (EP 7530–7537; ANF 33476–33487: "I put off deciding what was to be arranged about the case of the lapsed, so that when quiet and tranquillity should be granted, and the divine indulgence should allow the bishops to assemble into one place, then the advice gathered from the comparison of all opinions being communicated and weighed, we might determine ... But if any one, before our council ... should rashly wish to communicate with the lapsed, he himself should be withheld from communion"). Documented, his own later account.
- Ep. 43.3 (to the people, early 251): it had been agreed by him, the confessors, the clergy of Rome and the bishops of Africa and overseas "ut nihil innouetur circa lapsorum causam, nisi omnes in unum conuenerimus et conlatis consiliis cum disciplina pariter et misericordia temperatam sententiam fixerimus" (EP 5964–5969; ANF 32425–32429: "that no novelty should be introduced in respect of the case of the lapsed unless we all assembled into one place, and our counsels being compared, should decide upon a moderate sentence, tempered alike with discipline and with mercy"). Documented.
- The exception for the dying, agreed with Rome: the Roman clergy's letter (written by Novatian) said "that peace ought to be granted to the lapsed who were sick and at the point of departure" (ANF 33498–33502; EP 7570–7575). Documented. That letter is Novatian's opening and Quote 1 in his outline; here one clause, no quote.
- He could not come back before Easter because of "the malignity and treachery of certain of the presbyters" (Ep. 43.1, EP 5902–5904; ANF 32369–32371). Documented.

**E5. The lax party at Carthage: Felicissimus and five elders.** Cyprian, Ep. 43.2–3: the faction of Felicissimus had stirred some confessors against their bishop and now invited the lapsed "by the falsehood of a fallacious peace, to a fatal rashness, leaving off prayers and supplications" (ANF 32383–32399; EP 5917–5947); "persecutio est haec alia et alia est temptatio" (EP 5953: "This is another persecution and another temptation", ANF 32403). Cyprian's own words, as their opponent. Hefele: the party took up "the severity of the bishop with regard to the lapsi" as its "war-cry" (HEF 6356–6360); ENTRY `issue` 3. Hostile witness only (Documented as Cyprian's charge). Whether the presbyter Novatus was one of the five: "By some it is asserted, by others denied" (HEF 6362–6364); Leclercq says he was (HEFL 133, after Monceaux). Contested; leave out.

**E6. Spring 251: the council at Carthage and its middle way.** Ep. 55.6 (the core text): "persecutione sopita, cum data esset facultas in unum conueniendi, copiosus episcoporum numerus ... in unum conuenimus et scripturis [diu] ex utraque parte prolatis temperamentum salubri moderatione librauimus, ut nec in totum spes communicationis et pacis lapsis denegaretur, ne plus desperatione deficerent et eo quod sibi ecclesia cluderetur secuti saeculum gentiliter uiuerent, nec tamen rursus censura euangelica solueretur, ut ad communicationem temere prosilirent, sed traheretur diu paenitentia et rogaretur dolenter paterna clementia et examinarentur causae et uolontates et necessitates singulorum, secundum quod libello continetur quem ad te peruenisse confido, ubi singula placitorum capita conscripta sunt" (EP 7580–7612; "m unum" at 7581 is OCR for "in unum"). ANF 33508–33524: "a large number of bishops ... we met together; and the divine Scriptures being brought forward on both sides, we balanced the decision with wholesome moderation, so that neither should hope of communion and peace be wholly denied to the lapsed, lest they should fail still more through desperation, and, because the Church was closed to them, should, like the world, live as heathens; nor yet, on the other hand, should the censure of the Gospel be relaxed, so that they might rashly rush to communion, but that repentance should be long protracted, and the paternal clemency be sorrowfully besought, and the cases, and the wishes, and the necessities of individuals be examined into, according to what is contained in a little book ... in which the several heads of our decisions are collected." Participant (the bishop who called it). Documented. **Quote 1.**
- **Case by case.** Ep. 55.13: not all who sacrificed are equal: "neque enim aequandi sunt ille qui ad sacrificium nefandum statim uoluntate prosiliuit et qui reluctatus et congressus diu ad hoc funestum opus necessitate peruenit, ille qui et se et omnes suos prodidit et qui ipse pro cunctis ad discrimen accedens uxorem et liberos et domum totam periculi sui pactione protexit" (EP 7840–7845; ANF 33679–33686: "we must not place on a level one who has at once leapt forward with good-will to the abominable sacrifice, and one who, after long struggle and resistance, has reached that fatal result under compulsion; one who has betrayed both himself and all his connections, and one who, himself approaching the trial in behalf of all, has protected his wife and his children, and his whole family, by himself undergoing the danger"); and one who sheltered fleeing brothers in his house (EP 7845–7849). Documented. Paraphrase only.
- **The terms.** Ep. 55.17: "placuit ... examinatis causis singulorum libellaticos interim admitti, sacrificatis in exitu subueniri, quia exomologesis apud inferos non est" (EP 7978–7981; ANF 33780–33785: "it was decided ... the case of each individual having been examined into, that the receivers of certificates should in the meantime be admitted, that those who had sacrificed should be assisted at death, because there is no confession in the place of the departed"). "Si proelium prius uenerit, corroboratus a nobis inuenietur [armatus] ad proelium: si uero ante proelium infirmitas urserit, cum solacio pacis et communicationis abscedit" (EP 7982–7984; ANF 33786–33790). Documented.
- **Not for those who wait for their deathbed.** Ep. 55.23: those who do not repent with their whole heart "are to be absolutely restrained from the hope of communion and peace if they begin to beg for them in the midst of sickness and peril; because it is not repentance for sin, but the warning of urgent death, that drives them to ask" (ANF 33962–33970). Documented. Keep the force: the terms were not soft.
- **If they recover.** Ep. 55.13: peace given in danger stands if the sick man lives (ANF 33663–33675; EP 7811–7820). Documented.
- **Judgment left to God as well.** Ep. 55.18: "Neque enim praeiudicamus Domino iudicaturo" (EP 7988; ANF 33791–33797: "we do not prejudge when the Lord is to be the judge ... God ... will judge of those things which we have imperfectly looked into"). Documented.
- **The lax leaders cut off.** Ep. 59.9: Fortunatus "is one of the five presbyters who some time back deserted from the Church, and were lately excommunicated by the judgment of our fellow-bishops"; Felicissimus "was excommunicated by them here" (ANF 34756–34770). Hefele places this at the council of 251 (HEF 6369–6372). Documented.
- **The book.** Hefele: the decrees "were collected into one book, which may be considered as the first penitential book which had appeared in the Church; but unfortunately it is lost" (HEF 6374–6378). The book and its loss: Documented (Cyprian, Leclercq). "First penitential book": Hefele's view (editor's).
- **Hefele's summary differs on one term.** Hefele: "the libellatici must be reconciled immediately" (HEF 6436–6437). Cyprian says "in the meantime" and "the case of each individual having been examined into" (EP 7978–7979). Give Cyprian's wording (D2).

**E7. 251: two bishops at Rome; Novatian's rule.** Cornelius chosen bishop of Rome in 251 by sixteen bishops in Cyprian's count (`novatian` sheet E5; ANF 33981–33984). A stricter party set up Novatian against him (`novatian` sheet E5; ENTRY `issue` 4). Told here only as the split; the oath, the drunk bishops and the book are Novatian's. His rule, Socrates IV.28 (about 439, from Novatian's letters): Novatian separated "because Cornelius the bishop received into communion believers who had sacrificed during the persecution"; elevated by like-minded bishops, "he wrote to all the churches that 'they should not admit to the sacred mysteries those who had sacrificed; but exhorting them to repentance, leave the pardoning of their offense to God, who has the power to forgive all sin'" (SOC 14069–14090). Greek (SOCG2 4371–4377): "ταῖς πανταχοῦ ἐκκλησίαις ἔγραφε, μὴ δέχεσθαι τοὺς ἐπιτεθυκότας εἰς τὰ μυστήρια· ἀλλὰ προτρέπειν μὲν αὐτοὺς εἰς μετάνοιαν, τὴν δὲ συγχώρησιν ἐπιτρέπειν Θεῷ, τῷ δυναμένῳ καὶ ἐξουσίαν ἔχοντι συγχωρεῖν ἁμαρτήματα" (OCR "avy-εχωρεῖν" read as συγχωρεῖν). Valesius's Latin on the same page confirms the opening: "ne eos qui daemonibus immolaverant, ad sacra mysteria admitterent" (SOCG2 4402). Later (about 190 years), sympathetic, reporting Novatian's own letters. Documented as Socrates's report. **Quote 2; allocated to this story by the batch README.**
- Socrates on how it was received: "this appeared to some a cruel and merciless course: but others received the rule as just and conducive to the maintenance of discipline" (SOC 14099–14101). Documented as Socrates's report.
- Envoys from Novatian reached Carthage and were refused communion, while Cyprian and "several of my colleagues, who had come together to me" waited for others (ANF Ep. 40, 32589–32600). Documented; one clause (Cyprian's and Novatian's stories hold it).

**E8. Autumn 251: the council at Rome.** Eusebius VI.43.2, from Cornelius's letters: "There upon a very large synod assembled at Rome, of bishops in number sixty, and a great many more presbyters and deacons; while the pastors of the remaining provinces deliberated in their places privately concerning what ought to be done. A decree was confirmed by all, that Novatus and those who joined with him, and those who adopted his brother-hating and inhuman opinion, should be considered by the church as strangers; but that they should heal such of the brethren as had fallen into misfortune, and should minister to them with the medicines of repentance" (EUS 38272–38299). Greek (EUSG 6606–6612, 6699–6701, damaged scan): "συνόδου μεγίστης ἐπὶ Ῥώμης συγκροτηθείσης ἑξήκοντα μὲν τὸν ἀριθμὸν ἐπισκόπων ... τῇ μισαδέλφῳ καὶ ἀπανθρωποτάτῃ γνώμῃ ... ἐν ἀλλοτρίοις τῆς ἐκκλησίας ἡγεῖσθαι. τοὺς δὲ τῇ συμφορᾷ περιπεπτωκότας τῶν ἀδελφῶν ἰᾶσθαι καὶ θεραπεύειν τοῖς τῆς μετανοίας φαρμάκοις" (OCR "ixxAmolac", "φαρμάχοις" read as above). Rufinus's Latin in the same volume confirms: "alienum esse ab ecclesia, eos vero, qui lapsi in certamine fuerant, fraterna miseratione curandos et fomentis paenitentiae medicandos" (EUSG 6772–6774). Later (about 75 years), from a participant's lost letters. Documented. The phrases "strangers" and "the medicines of repentance" are this story's (batch README; `novatian` Do not say).
- "Most inhuman" is ἀπανθρωποτάτῃ, a superlative (EUSG 6612); McGiffert's "inhuman" flattens it.
- Eusebius on the letters: Cornelius's to Fabius of Antioch "show what was done at the synod at Rome, and what seemed best to all those in Italy and Africa and the regions thereabout"; with Cyprian's Latin letters, they "show that they agreed as to the necessity of succoring those who had been tempted, and of cutting off from the Catholic Church the leader of the heresy and all that joined with him" (EUS 38324–38359). Documented. McGiffert: the council "was, of course, only a local synod" (EUS-ed 38284): editor's.
- Cyprian on the same council: "etiam Romam super hac re scripsimus ad Cornelium collegam nostrum, qui et ipse cum plurimis coepiscopis habito concilio in eandem nobiscum sententiam pari grauitate et salubri moderatione consensit" (EP 7612–7616; ANF 33525–33529: Cornelius, "himself also holding a council with very many bishops, concurred in the same opinion as we had held, with equal gravity and wholesome moderation"). Participant, Carthage's side. Documented. Cyprian says Carthage wrote "lest perchance the number of bishops in Africa should seem unsatisfactory" (ANF 33524–33526).

**E9. The charge that Cyprian had gone soft, and his answer (Ep. 55, after both councils; no held source dates it more closely).** Ep. 55.3: Antonianus, a bishop in Africa, had been shaken by "Novatiani litteris" (EP 7473–7474); Cyprian must clear himself, lest anyone think that, having defended the gospel's rigour at first, he had later "turned my mind from discipline and from its former severity of judgment, so as to think that those who have stained their conscience with certificates, or have offered abominable sacrifices, are to have peace made easy to them" (ANF 33449–33457; EP 7483–7490). His image, Ep. 55.19: "Lo! a wounded brother lies stricken by the enemy in the field of battle. There the devil is striving to slay him whom he has wounded; here Christ is exhorting that he whom He has redeemed may not wholly perish. Whether of the two do we assist? On whose side do we stand?" (ANF 33831–33835). And his attack on the strict view, Ep. 55.15: "those whom a hateful persecution has not destroyed, we ruin by our hardness and inhumanity" (ANF 33729–33731). Documented, Cyprian's side. Not chosen as quotes; the wounded brother may be paraphrased.
- Earlier African bishops who would not pardon adultery "did not withdraw from the assembly of their co-bishops, nor break the unity" (Ep. 55.21, ANF 33870–33884). Documented; for the card.

**E10. Where the sources differ on Cornelius's practice (D1).** Socrates: Cornelius "received into communion believers who had sacrificed" (SOC 14069–14071), and his letters promised "indulgence to delinquents after baptism" (SOC 14102–14103). Cyprian: the report "that Cornelius communicates everywhere with those who have sacrificed" is a false rumour of the apostates (Ep. 55.12, ANF 33654–33662; EP 7800–7806); Cornelius "has merely associated with us those whose cause had been heard, and whose innocence was approved" (Ep. 55.10, ANF 33626–33631). Both Documented as each witness's claim.

**E11. 252: peace for all who kept repenting.** The African bishops to Cornelius (Ep. 57.1): "Statueramus quidem pridem ... ut qui in persecutionis infestatione subplantati ab aduersario et lapsi fuissent et sacrificiis se inlicitis maculassent, agerent diu paenitentiam plenam et si periculum infirmitatis urgueret, pacem sub ictu mortis acciperent" (EP 8632–8636); now, "cum uideamus diem rursus alterius infestationis adpropinquare coepisse ... necessitate cogente censuimus eis qui de ecclesia Domini non recesserunt et paenitentiam agere et lamentari ac Dominum deprecari a primo lapsus sui die non destiterunt, pacem dandam esse et eos ad proelium quod inminet armari et instrui oportere" (EP 8663–8673; ANF 34273–34299). Ep. 57.2: "nunc non infirmis sed fortibus pax necessaria est nec morientibus sed uiuentibus communicatio a nobis danda est" (EP 8684–8685; ANF 34309–34311). Ep. 57.4: "hoc in tempore pacem nos non dormientibus sed uigilantibus damus, pacem non deliciis sed armis damus, pacem non ad quietem sed ad aciem damus" (EP 8721–8724; ANF 34332–34335: "In these times we grant peace, not to those who sleep, but to those who watch. We grant peace, not amid indulgences, but amid arms. We grant peace, not for rest, but for the field of battle"). Synodal letter, forty-two bishops named in its heading (EP 8630; ANF 34251–34258). Documented. Date: Hefele, "a fresh council at Carthage on the Ides of May 252" (HEF 6504–6507); ANF's argument gives no year. Year Dominant Modern Reconstruction. **Quote 3.**
- The same letter keeps the distinction: no peace for those who went back to pagan life or to the heretics (Ep. 57.3, ANF 34324–34331). Documented.

**E12. 252: rival bishops at Carthage too.** Cyprian, Ep. 59.9: Fortunatus, one of the five, set up as "that pseudo-bishop, constituted by a few", and "Novatian's party is also said to have now made Maximus the presbyter—who was lately sent to us as an ambassador for Novatian ... their false bishop in that place" (ANF 34751–34780). Hefele: "they gave the priest Fortunatus as bishop to the lax party at Carthage" (HEF 6580–6588); "The Novatians of Carthage even succeeded in putting at their head a bishop of their party named Maximus" (HEF 6500–6503). Documented, Cyprian's hostile account. Carthage had a bishop for each side of the quarrel besides its own.

## 3. Impact (heaviest first)

**I1. The split outlived the councils: Novatian's church and its rule.** Socrates IV.28: the "strict discipline which is maintained in their church, even to the present day, in the provinces of Phrygia and Paphlagonia" (SOC 14059–14061; about 439, so nearly two centuries on). Allocated to this story by the `novatian` outline. Documented as Socrates's report. Its further life (Acesius, the ladder, canon 8 of Nicaea, Rome until the 420s) is `novatian`'s; here one clause at most: at Nicaea in 325 the bishops set terms for receiving clergy of "those who call themselves Cathari", including that "they will communicate ... with those who having lapsed in persecution have had a period [of penance] laid upon them" (NIC 3769–3777; `novatian` sheet I1). Documented. The listener must hear that the two churches then kept the same faith and divided on discipline (Sozomen, SOC 30565–30566, via the `novatian` sheet; Documented).

**I2. The shape of penance the councils set down: long, graded, case by case, with the dying not refused.** Nicaea's canons on those who fell under Licinius, 325: canon 11, those who fell "without compulsion" are to pass three years as hearers, seven as kneelers ("prostrators"), two sharing prayers (NIC 4124–4131); canon 12, "in all these cases it is necessary to examine well into their purpose and what their repentance appears to be like" (NIC 4353–4355); canon 13, "Concerning the departing, the ancient canonical law is still to be maintained, to wit, that, if any man be at the point of death, he must not be deprived of the last and most indispensable Viaticum", and if he recovers, "let him remain among those who communicate in prayers only" (NIC 4503–4509). Documented (the canons). That these came from 251: **no held source says so.** Bright's note on canon 12 (in Percival) explains it from "the Roman Presbyters ... to Cyprian" and *On the Lapsed* (NIC-ed 4488–4492): an editor's use of Cyprian to read the canon, not a statement of descent. Hefele calls the 251 book "the first penitential book" (E6; editor's). Inferential-Thin as a line of descent. The outline allows only the canons' own words and the editors' readings, credited, never "Nicaea followed Carthage".

**I3. Alexandria, the same years: peace for the dying.** Dionysius of Alexandria to Fabius of Antioch, who "seemed to lean somewhat toward this schism" (EUS 38828–38829): "as I had commanded that persons at the point of death, if they requested it, and especially if they had asked for it previously, should receive remission, that they might depart with a good hope", a presbyter sent the eucharist by a boy to the dying Serapion, an old man who had sacrificed (EUS 38840–38883). Contemporary, Dionysius's own account. Documented. Valesius ties it to "the African Synod, which met about the time that Dionysius wrote" (ANF6 10428–10432): editor's link. Optional, one sentence at most; `dionysius-of-alexandria` leaves it to this story.

**I4. Who keeps this today.** No held source says. Leave out.

## 5. Where sources disagree or come late

**D1. Did Cornelius take back those who had sacrificed?** Socrates says he did (E10). Cyprian says that is a false rumour; at Carthage the sacrificed got peace only at death, and Rome agreed (E6, E8, E10). Eusebius: "heal ... with the medicines of repentance", no terms given (E8). Credit each; the story says Socrates, writing for a later reader kind to Novatian's church, gives Novatian's reason as Cornelius's receiving the sacrificed.

**D2. The certificate-holders' terms.** Cyprian: admitted "in the meantime", after each case was examined (E6). Hefele: "reconciled immediately" (E6). Use Cyprian's.

**D3. What Novatian's rule held.** Socrates: no communion; urge them to repent; pardon is God's (E7). Eusebius's own words: "as if there was no longer for them a hope of salvation" (EUS 38265–38268). The Rome decree: a "brother-hating and most inhuman opinion" (E8). Cyprian: they are told to weep and then die outside the church (`novatian` sheet E8). Credit each; the rule did not say the lapsed were damned.

**D4. The months.** Hefele and McGiffert: May and October 251; no source of the time (S4).

**D5. Two councils or four.** Hefele: older editors "turned the two Synods of Carthage and Rome (251) into four councils"; he doubts a third at Antioch reported only by the *Libellus Synodicus* (HEF 6488–6497). McGiffert thinks the Antioch synod probably met; Hefele doubts it (ENTRY `uncertain`). For the card.

**D6. Was the lax party only about the lapsed?** Hefele: it began as a quarrel over Cyprian's alms commissioners and Felicissimus's unlawful ordination, and only then took the lapsed as its "war-cry" (HEF 6309–6360). Cyprian's letters are the only witness (E5). Say "Cyprian says".

**D7. Alexandria and the martyrs' pardons.** Dionysius did not overrule the martyrs: "These divine martyrs ... received some of the brethren who had fallen away ... and had fellowship with them in prayers and feasts" and asks whether the bishops should overturn their judgment (EUS 38138–38145; EUSG Rufinus 6650–6660). Cyprian held such pardons to the bishop's examination (E3, LAPS 733–740). A real difference of practice between two churches; for the card.

## 6. Wanted but not held

- The "little book" of the Carthage decisions: lost (E6).
- Cornelius's letters to Fabius on the Rome synod: lost except Eusebius's summary (EUS 38368–38370).
- Novatian's letters "to all the churches": lost (Socrates's source).
- Any letter from the lapsed themselves, or from Felicissimus's party, in its own words: none survives.
