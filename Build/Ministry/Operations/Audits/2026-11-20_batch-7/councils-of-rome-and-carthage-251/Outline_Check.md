## My own account of the heart

Order note: the role asks for this account before `Outline.md` is read. In this check the outline was opened first, so this account is not fully independent. It was drawn from the census entry's fields (`teaser`, `council.issue`, `decided`, `outcome`, `record`) and from the source sheet, not from the outline.

- **Essence.** After the persecution under Decius (250), many who had sacrificed or bought certificates asked to come back. Cyprian would not decide alone; in 251 the African bishops at Carthage, and then about sixty bishops at Rome under Cornelius, set a middle way. Hope of return was not denied, but return came through long penance with each case examined, and the dying who repented were not sent away without peace. A stricter party would give no communion back to those who had sacrificed, set up Novatian against Cornelius, and was cut off. Its church lasted for centuries.
- **Also told.** The pressure for return at once (the confessors' pardons; Felicissimus and five elders, by Cyprian's charge). Carthage's terms: certificate-holders admitted after examination; the sacrificed given peace at death; deathbed-only penitents refused; the lax leaders cut off; the book of decisions lost. Rome's decree ("strangers", "the medicines of repentance"), through Eusebius from Cornelius. Novatian's rule, through Socrates. In 252 (Hefele's year), with a new persecution expected, peace for all who had kept repenting.

**Against the outline.** The outline's essence and Also told match this account and the heart Mark approved (`Hearts.md`, "Councils of Carthage and Rome, 251"). No major contribution is left out. The spine is the lapsed. Novatian's election is one clause.

## Verdict: FIX

There are two BLOCKING findings: one detail the source does not hold, and one beat out of time order. The other findings are MINOR.

## BLOCKING

**B1. Beat 1 turns Cyprian's hypothetical plea into a real man.** The outline says "one such man, Cyprian writes, sent word that he was a Christian and could not sacrifice, and paid a price so as not to". In Ep. 55.14 this is what a certificate-holder "may say" in his own defence ("when he by whom the certificate has been received may say ...", ANF 33693–33708; EP 7860–7881). It is a plea Cyprian imagines, not an event or a person he reports. Told as "one such man", it is an invented anecdote.
*Correction:* "A certificate-holder, Cyprian writes, might plead that he had sent word to the official that he was a Christian and could not sacrifice, and had paid so as not to (E2, ANF 33693–33708; paraphrase)." Add to **Do not say**: "Not 'one man sent word ...' or any named or single certificate-holder. Cyprian gives the plea a certificate-holder might make (ANF 33693)."

**B2. Beat 5 goes back in time.** Beat 5 opens "At Rome, Cornelius was chosen bishop in 251", after beat 4 has told the Carthage council. The held letters show that Cornelius was already bishop, and Novatian already set up against him, while the African bishops were gathered at Carthage. Cyprian "and several of my colleagues, who had come together to me" refused Novatian's envoys and upheld "our colleague, who had already been chosen and ordained" (Ep. 44.1, ANF 32589–32630). Carthage's council then wrote "to Cornelius our colleague" (Ep. 55.6, EP 7612–7616). Heard once, beat 5 implies the split came after Carthage. A jump back is a failure to understand on one hearing (Kit, The bar).
*Correction:* Move the two bishops at Rome and the envoys' refusal into beat 4, before Quote 1. For example: "By then Rome had two bishops: Cornelius, chosen in 251, and Novatian, set up against him by a stricter party. Novatian's envoys reached Carthage while the bishops were gathered there, and were refused (E7; ANF 32589–32630; one clause each)." Beat 5 then starts with Novatian's letter to the churches (Quote 2, D1) and goes on to the Rome council that autumn. Add to **Do not say**: "Not that Cornelius was chosen, or Novatian set up, after the Carthage council. Both had happened by the time the bishops met (ANF 32589–32630)."

## MINOR

**M1. Length does not fit the beats.** The 555 words count only the quotes, the glosses and the credits (about 300). The beats are not counted. A plain telling of what the outline marks as kept runs to roughly 800 words or more. Beat 4 alone is about 200 words with Quote 1 (about 85), the case-by-case passage, the three terms, the cut-off and the lost book. Beat 5 is about 150, beat 6 about 120, and What came of them about 150. The listed cuts save about 60 words. A writer handed this outline will run past 5 minutes. That is batch 6's failure, which the Length rule ("The kit after batch 6", 2026-11-19) exists to prevent.
*Correction:* Count each beat's minimal words, then cut to fit, or set a longer time and give the reason the kit requires. Suggested cuts, in order:
- What came 3 (Alexandria) and What came 2 to one clause, or to the card.
- Beat 6's charge and the wounded-brother image to one sentence.
- Beat 2's confessors' letter to one clause.
- Beat 4's case-by-case paraphrase. Quote 1 already says that each case was examined.
- Beat 6's rival bishops.

Then restate the length from the count.

**M2. Quote 1, "the Father's mercy".** The outline says that "paterna clementia" is "God's mercy as Father". The Latin says only "fatherly clemency" (ANF 33519: "the paternal clemency"). Whose clemency is not stated. In Ep. 57.1 the bishops set "paterna pietas" beside "diuina clementia" as two things (EP 8637). The capitalised reading says more than the source.
*Correction:* Render it as "and fatherly mercy be begged with grief", and delete the parenthetical claim that it is God's mercy.

**M3. Quote 3 citation.** "hoc in tempore pacem ..." is in Hartel's Ep. 57 section 3 (the div n="3" at EP 8715; section 4 begins at EP 8764), not 57.4. The line numbers (EP 8721–8724) are right.
*Correction:* Change "Ep. 57.4" to "Ep. 57.3", in both the outline and the sheet's E11.

**M4. Opening adds "until the next day".** LAPS 350–351 says only that some were "put off" by the magistrates as evening pressed on ("uespera urgente dilati sunt").
*Correction:* "As evening came on, the officials put some off, and some begged not to be put off."

**M5. Beat 4, "no confession among the dead".** Here "confession" is the penitent's public confession (exomologesis), and the same story uses "confessors" for those who confessed the faith under torture. Heard once, the two words collide.
*Correction:* "since, Cyprian wrote, no one can confess and do penance among the dead (EP 7980)." Or add "confession" to **Words to explain** with that plain sense.

**M6. Words to explain: "forum".** The opening's "ran to the forum" has no gloss. This is a passing word.
*Correction:* Add "**forum**: 'the city's main square, where the officials sat' (E1)."

**M7. Short phrases from damaged scans are not flagged.** "Most inhuman" rests on the Greek superlative in the damaged EUSG scan (6612, legible as ἀπανθρωποτάτῃ). McGiffert's English has "inhuman" (EUS 38296), and Rufinus does not confirm the superlative. "Strangers" and "the medicines of repentance" also come from that scan, but Rufinus confirms them ("alienum ... ab ecclesia", "fomentis paenitentiae medicandos", EUSG 6772–6774).
*Correction:* Under Quotes, add a note: "Rome's decree is held in Greek only in a damaged scan (EUSG); 'strangers' and 'medicines of repentance' are confirmed by Rufinus's Latin (EUSG 6772–6774) and McGiffert (EUS 38296–38299); 'most inhuman' rests on the Greek alone (EUSG 6612); McGiffert has 'inhuman'."

**M8. The Connection field quotes a line that is not in the allowed list.** "we do not prejudge the Lord who will judge" (EP 7988) appears in quotation marks in the Connection field, but not among the short phrases cleared for use.
*Correction:* Mark it "(paraphrase)", or add it to the cleared short phrases after the same overlap search.

**M9. What came 3, Serapion.** Dionysius says the boy was told to soak the bread "and let the drops fall into the old man's mouth" (EUS 38866–38881), not the soaked piece itself.
*Correction:* "... as soon as his grandson let drops from the soaked bread of the Lord's Supper fall into his mouth."

**M10. Overlap with `novatian` on Nicaea's canon 8.** Novatian's outline tells, in What came 1, that Nicaea "set terms for receiving his church's clergy" (`novatian/Outline.md`, Also told and What came 1). This outline tells the same canon in What came 1 and in the Connection. The slate gives "his church's later life" to Novatian.
*Correction:* Keep here only the one term that concerns this story: that the clergy of Novatian's church, if they came over, would share communion with the lapsed who had done their penance (NIC 3769–3777). Add to the Sibling field that `novatian` tells the canon's other terms, so that the two do not both tell it in full.

**M11. Library sweep: a contemporary text is not listed.** The anonymous *Treatise Against the Heretic Novatian* (Ad Novatianum, about 255, probably African) is held in English (ANF 64269ff., corpus-map `novatianism.yaml` row `anf05_...--anonymous-treatise-against-the-heretic-novatian`) and in Latin (`cyprian_opera-spuria-vita-pontius-lat_hartel-csel3-pars3.txt` 3052ff.). It is a near-contemporary witness to the quarrel over the lapsed. The sweep neither uses it nor sets it aside.
*Correction:* Add it under "Set aside, and why". For example: "an anonymous polemic against Novatian's rigour, about 255; it gives no terms of either council; `novatian`'s card." No claim in the outline depends on it.
