2. **What happened:** they chose a middle way. The fallen could come back after long penance, with each case examined, though those who first repented on their deathbed were refused. A stricter party would take none back and set up Novatian as a rival bishop of Rome. A year on, with a new persecution coming, the African bishops gave peace to everyone who had kept repenting.
3. **Why the connection matters:** the councils left a rule for return and a split. Novatian's church kept its strict rule for centuries, and at Nicaea in 325 its clergy who came over had to share communion with the penitent lapsed.

## Findings

### BLOCKING

**B1. "Within a year" after Nicaea misdates 252, the time order jumps back, and Hefele's year is uncredited (P10 to P11).** P10 ends "At Nicaea in 325, its clergy who came over…". P11 opens "Within a year, with a new persecution expected…". Heard once, "within a year" counts from 325. There is also a jump back after the essence (439, then 325, then 252), which the kit bars. And the year is Hefele's (Outline, Setting and Who tells it: "Credit ... the year 252 to him"; heart: "the year is Hefele's"), but nothing in the story credits it.

- **Fix (smallest), in P11:** replace "Within a year," with "In 252, by a historian's reckoning," (+3 words). That names the year, credits it, and makes the return plain.
- **The order itself is set by the outline,** not the writer. The outline places What came 1 (Socrates's day, Nicaea) before an ending held to 252, and bars ending on Socrates, Nicaea or Novatian. No order keeps time, tells Nicaea's term and still ends on Quote 3. With the year named, the listener is no longer lost. If the recheck still counts the marked return as a jump back, the kit says the story goes to Mark with that reason, rather than to a second rewrite.
- **Do not say (add to the outline):** "Not 'within a year', 'the next year' or any date reckoned from the sentence before for the 252 decision when it follows Nicaea or Socrates's day. Say 'in 252', credited to a historian's dating (Hefele)."

**B2. Rome's council is never said to be Cornelius's (P9).** The approved heart and the outline's essence say "sixty bishops at Rome under Cornelius". As told, Cornelius is only one of Rome's "two bishops" (P6) and the author of Eusebius's source (P9). The listener is never told that the council which called Novatian's followers "strangers" was held by his rival. That is a part of the approved heart that has been dropped. The source holds it: Cyprian says Cornelius held the council "cum plurimis coepiscopis habito concilio" (EP 7612–7616). Eusebius's summary comes from Cornelius's letters (EUS 38272–38299).

- **Fix, in P9:** change "Later that year sixty bishops at Rome, by Eusebius's account of Cornelius's letters, agreed with Carthage." to "Later that year sixty bishops under Cornelius at Rome, by Eusebius's account of his letters, agreed with Carthage." (+1 word)
- The outline already holds this, so no Do not say line is needed.
