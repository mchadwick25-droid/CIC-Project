# Second Readings, Blind B (batch 7, B7-21 to B7-40)

## B7-21
Then I understood why they had come. I cried out, begging and pleading with them to go away and leave us alone. I asked them, if they wanted to do me any good, to get ahead of the men who were carrying me off and cut off my head themselves. When I had cried out like this, as my companions and partners in everything know, they lifted me up by force.

## B7-22
So I do not deny that this man was called John, or that this was the writing of a man named John. I also grant that it was the work of some holy, inspired person. But I cannot so easily accept that he was the apostle, the son of Zebedee and brother of James, the same man who wrote the Gospel called "according to John" and the general letter.

## B7-23
So that this kind of death, because of the great devotion and strong faith it showed, seemed to lack nothing of martyrdom.

## B7-24
For I admit that it is beyond my power to produce a perfect and complete history. Since I am the first to take up this subject, I am trying to walk, as it were, a lonely and untrodden path.

## B7-25
We saw with our own eyes the houses of prayer thrown down to their foundations, and the holy Scriptures burned in the middle of the public squares. We saw the shepherds of the churches shamefully hiding here and there, and some of them captured in disgrace and mocked by their enemies.

## B7-26
"Of one substance" (the same being or essence).

## B7-27
Not without examination.

## B7-28
Do you sit as judge, Eusebius, and is Athanasius, innocent as he is, judged by you? Who can put up with such things?

## B7-29
Tell me, were you not in prison with me during the persecution? I lost an eye for the truth, but you seem to have suffered no bodily harm. You were not martyred; you are alive and unmaimed. How were you let out of prison, unless you promised those who pressed us under persecution to do what is forbidden, or actually did it?

## B7-30
Accused by our confessors of sacrificing to idols.

## B7-31
Provoked by their proud ungodliness, by my own awareness of the truth itself, and, as I think, by God, I took on this task.

## B7-32
Religion must be defended not by killing but by dying, not by cruelty but by endurance, not by crime but by faith.

## B7-33
A man more eloquent than anyone of his time, but so poor in this life that he often lacked even the necessities.

## B7-34
Let them knock at the doors, by all means, but let them not break them down.

## B7-35
If you were led on against your will, as you say, you will show it by withdrawing willingly. For it would be better to suffer anything than to split the Church of God.

## B7-36
Pure.

## B7-37
Set up a ladder, Acesius, and climb up to heaven by yourself.

## B7-38
Was there any lover of books who did not find a friend in Pamphilus? If he knew that any of them lacked the necessities of life, he helped them as far as he possibly could. He would not only lend them copies of the Holy Scriptures to read but give them freely, and not only to men but also to women whom he saw devoted to reading. So he kept a supply of manuscripts, to be able to give them to anyone who wanted them whenever the need arose.

## B7-39
Which we produced, working on it together with zeal, for the sake of those who love to find fault.

## B7-40
When he sets these out, he often adds, and declares, that he does not state these things as a fixed ruling or settle them as doctrine. He is inquiring as far as he is able and weighing the meaning of the Scriptures, and he does not claim to have grasped it fully and completely.
