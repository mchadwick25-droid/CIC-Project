## The test, from one hearing

1. **Who:** Peter, bishop of Alexandria, who led the churches of all Egypt through the Great Persecution and was beheaded in 311.
2. **What happened:** in 306 he set graded rules for taking back Christians who had given way, by what each had suffered and chosen; meanwhile one of his bishops, Melitius, broke away, and the witnesses disagree on why.
3. **Why the connection matters:** his rules outlived the persecution as church law (sealed in 692, kept in the Greek books of church law), and the split he left came before the Council of Nicaea, which could not end it.

No line is blank or wrong.

## The heart

**Mine, written before reading `Hearts.md`.** Peter was bishop of Alexandria, over the churches of all Egypt, through the Great Persecution from 303. In 306 he wrote rules for receiving back those who had given way, weighing each case by what was suffered and what was chosen, and in 311 he was beheaded. In those years his bishop Melitius broke away, for reasons the witnesses tell differently; the rival church outlived Peter and came before Nicaea. Also told: the rules themselves, in his words; the break from both held sides; Eusebius on the death; the rules sealed as church law in 692.

**Against the approved heart.** It matches. Every part of the approved essence is told, and every part of "Also told" as the outline now holds it. The heart's "Athanasius and Socrates" was narrowed by the outline to Athanasius alone (Also told, Beat 3, Who tells it); the outline governs, so Socrates's absence is not a finding.

**Where the essence is complete.** All of it but one clause is told by word 123 ("303", in the third paragraph): office and Egypt (word 1 to 59 with the hook), the rules of 306 and the death in 311 (word 96), Melitius and the rival church that "outlived him" (word 114), and the persecution from 303 (word 123). The approved essence's last clause, that the rival church "came before Nicaea", is first heard at word 586, in the ninth paragraph. So the essence is complete only at word 586. See B1.

## Findings

### BLOCKING

- **B1. The essence's Nicaea clause comes at word 586, not in the first minute.** The approved heart and the outline's essence both end "the rival church outlived Peter and came before Nicaea". The story's essence (paragraph 2) stops at "a rival church that outlived him". Smallest fix, in two places, so that Nicaea's plain sense still comes at its first hearing:
  - Paragraph 2: "starting a rival church that outlived him." → "starting a rival church that outlived him and came before the first great council of the whole church, at Nicaea."
  - Paragraph 9: "In 325 the Council of Nicaea, the first great council of the whole church, took up this same quarrel." → "In 325 that council, at Nicaea, took up this same quarrel."
  Net about +5 words (story about 643 words, about 4:17).
  **Do not say line for the outline:** "The essence ending on 'a rival church that outlived him' without Nicaea. Say in the first minute that the rival church came before the Council of Nicaea, the first great council of the whole church; What came 2 then tells the council's terms."

### MINOR

- **M1. "Many had given way" (paragraph 4).** No held line gives a number or "many"; the canons and Epiphanius say "some" and "those who". Change "Many had given way, and now they asked for communion again" → "Those who had given way now asked for communion again". Saves 2 words.
- **M2. Optional cut, the Epiphanius hinge (paragraph 6).** "Epiphanius, writing seventy years on, tells it another way. He says the two were in prison together" → "Epiphanius, writing seventy years on, says the two were in prison together". The contrast still lands, since paragraph 5 has just given the other account. Saves 5 words. Keep "In his telling" in the next sentence: it keeps the prison story in his voice.
- **M3. Optional cut, Lycopolis (paragraph 5).** "Melitius, bishop of Lycopolis in Upper Egypt," → "Melitius, a bishop in Upper Egypt,". The city is heard once and nothing turns on it. Saves 2 words.

### Where held sources differ (Mark, 2026-11-19)

- **Why the split began (X1).** The story credits each: the letters, Athanasius (named as a foe), Epiphanius (named as seventy years on), and Hefele by role. It does not settle the question. This passes.
- **The prison (X2).** The story tells it as Epiphanius's claim. Hefele's denial and Bell's reading are not in the audio. The story frames the prison as one witness's telling and says historians still weigh the two, so this is not a finding. The card must carry X2, credited.
- **The year of the rules (X5).** The story says 306, from Peter's own "fourth Easter". Nikodemos's 304 belongs on the card, credited, under the same ruling.
- **The date of the split (X3).** "During the persecution". This passes.
- **The term for those who fell from fear (X6).** "Longer". This passes.
- **The year of death.** Eusebius's ninth year is 311 for Bell and Hawkins. McGiffert gives February 311 to spring 312, with the death "not long before the spring of 312" (EUS 43213–43226). That range holds late 311, so the held sources do not conflict, and "311" stands.
- No quoted span hides a cut. Each runs continuously in its original: PED 49722–49723; EPI 43852–43853 and EPI-PG 13795–13798; EUS-G 17556–17560.
