# Outline check: Peter of Alexandria

## My account of the heart, written before reading Outline.md

From the census entry (teaser: bishop of Alexandria through the Great Persecution, wrote the rules for the lapsed, broke with Melitius, beheaded 311), the card and the source sheet.

**The essence.** Peter was bishop of Alexandria from about 300 (EUS 43214–43215; the year is McGiffert's reckoning), leading the churches of all Egypt (Epiphanius's statement of custom, EPI 43712–43716), and led them through the Great Persecution that began in 303. In 306, by his own "fourth passover of the persecution" (year Widely Accepted; Nikodemos says 304), he set out rules for taking back Christians who had given way, weighing each case by what they had suffered and whether they chose to fall (own words, Documented). He was beheaded in 311, in the ninth year of the persecution (Eusebius, contemporary, Documented). He is on the tree as the martyr-bishop whose graded rules for the lapsed became church law (ratified by Trullo, 692), and whose break with Melitius left a rival church that reached Nicaea.

**Also told.** The graded rules themselves (the tortured who gave way, those who only were imprisoned, those who fell from fear, the tricksters, slaves and masters, those who fled or paid, clergy who fell kept from office). The break with Melitius, told from both held sides and credited: the letters (ordaining in others' churches while they were in prison or hiding), Athanasius and Socrates (deposed by Peter's council, sacrifice among the charges), Epiphanius (a prison quarrel over the fallen, Peter for mercy); kept to two beats from Peter's side per Mark's ruling. His death as Eusebius tells it (seized "for no cause", beheaded as if by Maximinus's order, many Egyptian bishops with him). The canons received into church law (Trullo, 692; the Byzantine commentators). The split outliving him and coming before Nicaea, a clause.

Confidence: bishop and dates Documented / Widely Accepted; the rules Documented (own); the date of the rules Widely Accepted, sources differ; why the break began Contested; death Documented; the 25 November date from the later Egyptian reckoning.

## The heart compared

The outline's essence and Also told match my account in substance: the bishop through the Great Persecution, the graded rules of 306, the beheading in 311, and the break with Melitius that reached Nicaea. I put the rules becoming church law in the essence. The outline ranks it first under What came, which is equivalent. No major contribution is left out. The hook and essence come first. The essence reaches ahead to 311, as the kit allows. After that the beats run once, and the death is told only at Beat 5.

## BLOCKING

**B1. What came 1 says the rules became "law for the whole church", which goes past the sources.** Line 56: "The listener must hear that the rules he wrote in a persecution outlived it, as law for the whole church." The held sources say less:
- Trullo Canon 2 sets its seal on Peter's canons (NPNF14 26731–26744). That was a council of the Greek church.
- Nikodemos says the ratification gives them ecumenical force only "in a way" (PED 49705–49706, "Οἰκουμενικὴν τρόπον τινὰ ... δύναμιν").
- Lauchert, a held editor, says such letters gained standing "in der ganzen Kirche oder grossen Theilen derselben" (the whole church or large parts of it) and were taken into "die griechischen Codices canonum" (`cic/texts/lauchert_kanones-altkirchlichen-concilien-lat-grc_1896.txt` 391–401).
- The tree's own live Athanasius story scopes the same canon as "Greek copies of that law".

A writer following line 56 would state a qualified claim as settled.

**Correction.**
- Line 56: replace the last sentence with "The listener must hear that the rules he wrote in a persecution outlived it, as church law in the Greek books of canons." Replace "carry, in a way, the force of a council of the whole church" with "carry, in a way, the force of a council of the whole church, as that book's editor saw it". Keep that sentence credited to the book, never in the narrator's voice.
- Do not say: add "'His rules became law for the whole church' as fact. Say: 'a council in 692 set its seal on them, and the Greek books of church law kept them.' The 'in a way' of the Greek book stays with that book."

**M3. What came 3 and Ending 1 tell the same thing twice.** Both carry Athanasius's "blessed, bishop and martyr" and Alexandria keeping his day. **M4. Beat 2 attaches Christ's "flee" to the wrong rule.** "those who fled, he would not blame, since Christ said to flee from city to city (E11)". "flee ye into another" is in Canon 9, on those who rushed forward (ANF6 27066–27074). Canon 13 gives its own reasons: Paul kept from the theatre at Ephesus, Lot's "Escape for thy life", and Peter the apostle escaping prison with no blame to him (ANF6 27536–27603). **M5. Canon 1's three years read as a condition.** Also told says "who had mourned their fall for three years", and Beat 2 says "The tortured who had mourned three years". Canon 1 says only that "some are now, for the third year, bewailing their fault" (ANF6 26619–26621; PED 49723–49724, "ἤδη τινὲς τρίτον ἔτος"). The rule is forty more days from their approach. Correction, in both places: "The tortured who gave way, some already mourning into a third year: forty more days, the days Christ fasted, then communion."

**M6. Beat 1 puts forced sacrifice in 303.** "In 303 the emperors ordered the churches pulled down, and Christians were made to sacrifice to the gods". McGiffert dates the edict ordering all Christians to sacrifice to 304, the "fourth edict" (EUS 46173–46178; EUS 44526–44528, "not until that time were Christians in general called upon to offer sacrifices"). Correction: "In 303 the emperors ordered the churches pulled down; within a year all Christians were ordered to sacrifice to the gods (S3; EUS 46173–46178)." Beat 1's "many bishops of Egypt were jailed" should cite M1 (the four bishops' "imprisonments", ANF6 16756–16757), not D3, which lists deaths.

**M7. Beat 5 cites the wrong lull.** BELL 3198–3200 is the short release around 305. The lull before Peter's death is Galerius's edict of 311 (BELL 3208–3211). Correction: cite "BELL 3208–3211" only.

**M8. The order of Beats 2 and 3.** Every held date for the split's start (301 Robertson, 304–305 Hefele, probably 305 Bell; X3) falls before or at the rules of 306. **M9. Do not say is missing four easy errors.** Add:
