# Review: Tertullian (`tertullian-s-voice`)

Reviewed against the kit (`Brief-Kit/Kit.md`), the reviewer role, the approved heart (`Hearts.md`, 2026-11-20), Mark's batch 7 rulings and the batch 6 and change-order entries of 2026-11-19 in the Decision-Log, the outline, and `cic/texts/`.

## The test, from one hearing

- **Who:** Tertullian of Carthage, a convert who knew Roman law, the foremost early Christian writer in Latin.
- **What happened:** he defended the Christians to Rome's governors, argued that the Scriptures belong to the apostles' churches, and put Latin words for God as one substance in three persons to work. Then he joined the New Prophecy, and his followers were later counted heretics.
- **Why the connection matters:** his Latin words for God became the Latin church's words, and Cyprian, Novatian and Lactantius read him and built on him, though his name carried the mark of heresy.

## Findings

### 1. BLOCKING — the Apology "ends" twice

Paragraph 1: "He ended: "One thing only she asks for now: not to be condemned unknown."" That sentence is in chapter 1. The Apology goes straight on ("What harm can it do to the laws ... to give her a hearing?", ANF3 1465–1466; OEH1 11258), and it closes with "the blood of Christians is seed" in chapter 50 (ANF3 4660–4661). Paragraph 3 then says "He closed defiant", so the listener hears two endings. The first is false.
**Change:** "He ended:" → "He pleaded:"
**Do not say (add to the outline):** "He ended" or "he closed" for Quote 1's last sentence. It comes in chapter 1, and the Apology closes with "the blood of Christians is seed" (chapter 50). Say "he pleaded".

### 2. MINOR — "That letter"

Paragraph 3: "That letter was his Apology". The Apology is a defense addressed to the governors as judges (outline, Also told), not a letter. Thelwall calls it a "book" (ANF3 1458–1459).
**Change:** "That letter" → "That book". No change in word count.

### 3. MINOR — "their cause"

Paragraph 8: "Lactantius said his Apology had already pleaded their cause in full." The nearest plural is Jerome and Cyprian, so "their" is unclear heard once. Lactantius means the Christians' cause (LACT-E 4569).
**Change:** "their cause" → "the Christians' cause". No change in word count.

### 4. MINOR — the Paraclete's plain sense stops short of the Spirit

Paragraph 5: "the Paraclete, the Helper Jesus promised". The outline's gloss goes on to "the Holy Spirit". The story's later "the church of the Spirit" (paragraph 7) relies on the listener knowing that the Paraclete is the Spirit.
**Change:** "the Helper Jesus promised, was speaking" → "the Helper Jesus promised, the Holy Spirit, was speaking" (+3 words; the sentence becomes 27).
