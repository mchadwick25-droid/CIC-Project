# Outline: Tertullian

Sheet items (S, E, T, I, D) are in `Source_Sheet.md`, which gives the file and line for each. ANF3 and ANF4 are the Ante-Nicene Fathers English; OEH1, OEH2, KRO and CSEL20 the held Latin; JER and JERL Jerome in English and Latin; EUS Eusebius; LACT Lactantius; AUG Augustine; VIN Vincent of Lérins; MON Monceaux (modern).

- **The essence:** Tertullian of Carthage, a convert who knew Roman law well, as Eusebius says, was the foremost early writer of the faith in Latin, around the year 200 (S1, S2, S5, S7, D4; Carthage and the reigns Documented; "trained in Roman law" Eusebius's word, Documented as his, Contested that he practised as an advocate; "foremost" is rank, not order: Vincent, "as Origen holds by far the first place among the Greeks, so does Tertullian among the Latins", VIN 13555–13557; Coxe, "the founder of Latin Christianity", ANF4 17041–17042; Widely Accepted). He defended the Christians before Rome's governors, claimed the Scriptures for the churches the apostles founded, and gave Latin its words for God as one substance in three persons; then he turned to the strict New Prophecy, and two centuries later Augustine counted his followers at Carthage, the Tertullianists, among the heretics (E1, E4, T4, T5, E5, I5, E6; the books Documented; "gave Latin its words" Widely Accepted, census and editors; Augustine's list Documented, AUG 2827–2835, 2868–2873).
- **Also told:**
  - **The Apology, about 197.** A defense of the Christians addressed to Rome's governors as judges: the charge is only the name; the crowd blames the Christians for every flood and famine; the more they are cut down, the more they grow (E1, E2; Documented; the year Widely Accepted).
  - **The Prescription, about 200.** A lawyer's plea that throws a case out before it is argued: those who teach against the apostles may not argue from Scripture at all, because the Scriptures belong to the churches that received them from the apostles, and those churches can be visited, such as Ephesus and Rome (E4, T2, S6; Documented as his argument; the date Dominant Modern Reconstruction).
  - **Against Praxeas, after 213.** Against a teacher at Rome who, he says, made the Father and the Son one and the same, so that the Father was born and suffered, he answered that God is one substance and three persons, Father, Son and Spirit, distinct but not divided; the Latin words trinitas, persona and substantia are in his text (E7, T3, T4, T5; Documented as his book and words; that later Latin theology took its words from here Widely Accepted). He wrote it after his turn (E7; Documented).
  - **The turn.** He took up the New Prophecy from Phrygia, which held that the Paraclete, the Helper Jesus promised, was speaking anew through its prophets; it asked for stricter fasts, no second marriage and no flight in persecution, and he broke with those he called the unspiritual (E5, E8; Documented). Why, the later witnesses differ (D1; Contested). In what is probably his last surviving book, the church of the Spirit may forgive sins, "not the church which consists of a number of bishops" (E8; Documented).
  - **What came of him.** Cyprian read him every day and asked for "the master", in one clause (I3; Jerome's report, Documented as his). Novatian's book On the Trinity was, Jerome says, a kind of summary of his, though a modern historian finds that not borne out (I2). Lactantius said his Apology had already pleaded the Christians' cause in full (I4; Documented as his view). His followers at Carthage lasted until Augustine's day and then gave their church building to the Catholics (I5; Augustine's report, Documented as his).
- **Length:** about 4 minutes 10 seconds (about 625 words, at 150 a minute), under Mark's ruling (2026-11-20: about 4:00, 4:30 at most). Count, part by part, as told after the cuts: the opening with Quote 1's first and last sentences, about 63 (quote 28); the essence, about 90, unchanged; beat 2, the Apology, about 45; beat 3, the Prescription, about 82 (Quote 2 trimmed to its Marcion, Valentinus and heir lines, 37); beat 4, the turn with Jerome and Augustine one clause each, about 75; beat 5, Against Praxeas, about 87 (Quote 3, four sentences, 37); beat 6, the last book, about 35; What came 1, the words and Novatian with the historian who doubts it, about 40; What came 2, Cyprian, one clause, about 30; What came 3, Lactantius, one clause, about 15; What came 4, the followers and Augustine's verdict, about 45; the ending, about 20. Inside these: three short phrases (about 20), the plain sense of each word to explain (Apology, prescription, Paraclete, New Prophecy, the unspiritual, person and substance, second marriage, Catholic: about 55), and the witnesses credited (Eusebius, Jerome twice, Augustine twice, Lactantius, the modern historian: about 45). The essence ends by about word 150, inside the first minute. Cut for length (see "For the card, not the story"): beat 1 as its own beat; Vincent and Hilary; the Tiber and Nile list; "kill us, torture us"; the opening and closing questions of Quote 2; the Paraclete-taught clause and the paraphrase of one God with Son and Spirit in beat 5; "from Asia"; the Greek triad as narration; Philippi and Corinth.
- **Setting:**
  - Time: around the year 200: the Apology in about 197, what is probably his last surviving book between 217 and 222; Jerome puts his best years under the emperors Severus and Caracalla (S2, E1, E8). Plain words: "around the year 200". Give a year only for the Apology: "about 197"; held editors give 197 to 204 (ANF3-ed 1443–1446; ANF4 17138), for the card.
  - Place: Carthage, the great city of Roman Africa, today in Tunisia (S1; CEN `modernCountries`). Plain words: "Carthage, in Roman Africa".  - Tradition: the Christians who first made the faith's case in Latin; the tree's name is "The Case Made in Latin" (S3). Do not say "the Latin apologists".
  - Language: Latin; Jerome names only two Latin Christian writers before him (JER 40495); a few works in Greek are lost (EUS-ed 10260–10262).

**Words to explain** (the first time each is heard; plain word or short gloss, worked into the sentence)

- **Apology**: "a defense of the Christians" (the Pliny story calls it "Defense of the Christians"). Say the title once with its plain sense: "his Apology, a defense of the Christians".
- **Rome's governors / "rulers of the Roman Empire"**: "the governors who sat in judgment for Rome" (E1). Not "emperors".
- **prescription**: "a lawyer's plea that throws out a case before it is argued" (S6; ANF3-ed 20037–20038 "a demurrer, or formal objection"). Work it into the sentence; do not use the word bare.
- **heretics**: "teachers he held false", or credit it: "those he called heretics" (T2 context).
- **Paraclete**: "the Paraclete, the Helper whom Jesus promised, the Holy Spirit" (E5, E7). Say "the Paraclete" only with this sense the first time.
- **New Prophecy**: "a movement from Phrygia, in today's Turkey, whose prophets said the Spirit was speaking anew through them" (`montanism-the-new-prophecy`; E5). "Montanists" was their opponents' name; the narrator says "the New Prophecy".
- **psychics / the carnally-minded**: his scornful name for the church members who did not accept the new prophets: "those he called the unspiritual" (E5). Never "psychics" bare.
- **substance and person** (Quote 3): give the plain sense beside the quote: substance is what God is, one and undivided; person is who is distinct, Father, Son and Spirit. Do not explain "persona" as "mask": no held source says so.
- **Trinity**: a grown listener knows it; say once that the Latin word, trinitas, is in his text (T5).
- **second marriage**: "marrying again after a husband or wife had died" (E5, E6).
- **Phrygia**: "inland Asia Minor, in today's Turkey".
- **Catholic** (Augustine, What came 4): "the main church", or "the Catholic church, the main body"; the word is Augustine's.
- **a great trial** (Vincent; cut for length, card only): a great test of the church's faithful, the way a false teacher tests them in Moses's words (VIN 13497–13504, 13618). Give this plain sense beside the phrase, or do not use it.
- **Asia**: dropped with "from Asia" (beat 5).
- **penance / done penance** (beat 6): "done the repentance the church required".
- **his Word** (beat 5): "the Word, the name John's Gospel gives the Son" (ANF3 51888–51892, John 1:1).

- **Shape:** Teacher or thinker. The question he answered: in a language that had hardly been used for the faith, how could a Christian say what the church is, who owns its Scriptures, and how God is one and yet Father, Son and Spirit, before Rome's courts and against rival teachers? What he taught: the Christians' innocence before the law; the apostles' churches as the owners of Scripture; one substance in three persons. Who learned it: Cyprian, Novatian, Lactantius, and the Latin church after them. The trap is a list of books: show each through one line of his own (Quotes 1–3), and keep Against Marcion, Against the Valentinians and the rest out (Leave out).

- **Openings:**
  1. **Chosen.** His own first words in the Apology (Quote 1; E1, T1). In about three sentences: in about 197, Tertullian of Carthage wrote to Rome's governors that if they may not, or dare not, look into the charges openly, the truth should reach them "by the hidden road of silent writing". Close the quote there; then, as a separate quote, the last line of his plea: "One thing only she asks for now: not to be condemned unknown." Never run the two as one quote. Then the essence.
  2. Not chosen: Cyprian, half a century later, asking his secretary every day, "Give me the master" (I3). Strong, but it reaches ahead to the end, and its witness chain (Jerome, Paul, the secretary) needs room the opening does not have.

**Story beats** (the opening and the essence are the first minute, about 150 words; beats 2 to 6 deepen it)

1. **A convert at Carthage.** Cut for length: the essence already carries Carthage, the convert and Eusebius's word on Roman law (S1, S5, S7). Nothing more is told here; the rest is for the card.
2. **The Apology, about 197.** The governors hated and judged the very name of Christian, he argued, without learning what Christians did (E1; ANF3 1472–1478; Documented as his argument). Their crowds blamed the Christians for every flood, drought, famine or plague (E2; ANF3 3997–4003; Documented; one clause, the force kept). He ends defiant: the more you cut us down, the more we grow; "the blood of Christians is seed" (E2; ANF3 4653–4661; OEH1 30954–30955; Documented; short phrase).
3. **The Prescription, about 200.** He wrote against teachers who built new teachings on Scripture, and he used a plea from the law courts, one that throws a case out before it is argued (S6, E4). Their arguments from Scripture should not even be heard, he said, until it was settled whose the Scriptures were (E4; ANF3 20765–20768, 20860–20866). The faith came from Christ to the apostles, and from them to the churches they founded, such as Ephesus and Rome, which anyone could visit (E4; ANF3 20937–20942, 21657–21677; Documented as his argument). Then Quote 2. Do not say who first made the argument; the Irenaeus story tells his.
4. **The turn, from about 207.** He came to believe the New Prophecy from Phrygia: that the Paraclete, the Helper Jesus promised, now spoke through its prophets, and asked more of Christians than the church did: stricter fasts, no second marriage after a husband or wife died, and no flight from persecution (E5; Documented; the years Dominant Modern Reconstruction, MON). Later, he writes, his acceptance and defense of the Paraclete separated his side from those he called the unspiritual (E5; ANF3 51074–51076; KRO 11745–11746). Why he turned, two later writers disagree: Jerome says the envy and insults of the clergy at Rome drove him to it; Augustine says he joined the prophets he had once argued against, then broke from them too and gathered his own followers (AUG 2868–2873, "quos ante destruxerat"; E6, D1; Contested; credit each).
5. **Against Praxeas, after 213.** Praxeas, by Tertullian's account, had come to Rome and taught that the Father himself was born and suffered (E7; ANF3 51014–51016, 51037–51041; his hostile account). Then Quote 3, introduced by the creation scene: God says "Let there be light", and the Word makes it, so the one who commands and the one who makes are two (T4; ANF3 51892–51894). Say it plainly: the book from which, later writers say, the Latin church drew its words for the Trinity was written after his turn (I1; Widely Accepted; credited, not the narrator's).
6. **What is probably his last surviving book.** When a bishop announced he would forgive adultery after penance (ANF4 7187–7188), Tertullian answered that only the church of the Spirit could forgive it, "not the church which consists of a number of bishops" (E8; ANF4 9542–9545; CSEL20 15312–15314; Documented; short phrase). Do not name the bishop; the Hermas story tells the ruling.

**What came of him** (about a third of the story; heaviest first)

1. **The words.** The words he set to work in Against Praxeas, trinitas, persona, substantia, became the Latin church's words for God, the census and later editors say (T5, I1; Widely Accepted; credit "historians" or "later writers" if a source is named). (Do not say he was the first anywhere: see Do not say.) Jerome called Novatian's On the Trinity "a sort of epitome" of Tertullian's work, though a modern historian finds that not borne out (I2; JER 40895–40897; HARN 22546–22548).
2. **Cyprian.** One clause, in narration (Mark, 2026-11-20): Cyprian, the next great bishop of Carthage, never let a day pass without reading him, Jerome heard, and would ask his secretary, "Give me the master" (I3; JERL 55618–55626; JER 40500–40505; Documented as Jerome's report, hearsay through two people, Paul and Cyprian's secretary). Jerome's Latin says the old man Paul of Concordia had seen Cyprian's aged secretary at Rome; Richardson's English wrongly makes Paul the secretary (D2): if the chain is told at all, follow the Latin. Not one of the three quotes.
3. **Read, and judged.** Lactantius, a century on, said his Apology had already pleaded the Christians' cause in full (I4; LACT-E 4569; Documented as his view; one clause, which may join What came 2). Vincent and Hilary: cut for length; for the card (below).
4. **His followers.** A small group at Carthage bore his name down to Augustine's day, when the last few joined the Catholic church, the main body, and handed over their church building (I5; AUG 2829–2835; Documented as Augustine's report). Augustine wrote this in his list of heresies, and he named Tertullian a heretic (E6; AUG 2868–2873): say so, so that the force of the judgment is not lost.

Defended: the Christians' innocence before Rome's courts (Apology); the apostles' churches' title to the Scriptures (Prescription); one God in three persons against Praxeas; later, the New Prophecy.

**Quotes** (3)

Only Quotes 1–3 are quoted aloud. These three short phrases stand inside the narrator's sentences: "the blood of Christians is seed" (beat 2), "not the church which consists of a number of bishops" (beat 6), "Give me the master" (What came 2). "Men are made, not born, Christians", "the Christians to the lion!" and "put to flight the Paraclete, and crucified the Father" and "a great trial in the Church" are cut for length.

1. **Apology 1**, rendered from the Latin (OEH1 11253–11261). **The Latin is held only in Oehler's damaged 1853 scan**; no other held Latin print has it. Thelwall's English (ANF3 1457–1465) confirms every clause. Latin, read through the OCR: "liceat veritati vel occulta via tacitarum litterarum ad aures vestras pervenire. Nihil de causa sua deprecatur, quia nec de condicione miratur. Scit se peregrinam in terris agere, inter extraneos facile inimicos invenire, ceterum genus, sedem, spem, gratiam, dignitatem in caelis habere. Unum gestit interdum, ne ignorata damnetur." Rendering: "Then let the truth reach your ears at least by the hidden road of silent writing. She makes no plea in her own cause, for her lot does not surprise her. She knows she lives as a foreigner on earth, and easily finds enemies among strangers. But her family, her home, her hope, her reward and her honor are in heaven. One thing only she asks for now: not to be condemned unknown." ("Silent writing" is *tacitarum litterarum*; Thelwall has "a noiseless book". "Reward" is *gratiam*; Thelwall has "recompense".) The card prints the Latin as read through the OCR, with the corrected letters listed, or the English alone. The story uses only the first and last sentences. Job: the opening. The Apology's whole case in its first lines.
2. **Prescription 37**, rendered from the Latin (OEH2 2280–2290). The Latin is held only in Oehler's damaged scan, which has lost 'quando et unde' and garbles four words; no held print confirms them. The card prints the English alone, or the Latin as the scan shows it with the restored words in brackets, marked as restored from Holmes's English. Latin, read through the OCR and ANF: "Qui estis? quando et unde venistis? quid in meo agitis, non mei? quo denique, Marcion, iure silvam meam caedis? qua licentia, Valentine, fontes meos transvertis? qua potestate, Apelles, limites meos commoves? Mea est possessio. … Mea est possessio, olim possideo, prior possideo, habeo origines firmas ab ipsis auctoribus quorum fuit res. Ego sum heres apostolorum." Rendering: "By what right, Marcion, do you cut my timber? By whose leave, Valentinus, do you turn my springs aside?" "This property is mine. I have held it long; I held it first." "I am the heir of the apostles." Close the quote after each span. Before (a), the narrator may say he asked who they were and where they came from; between (a) and (b) and between (b) and (c), one narrator clause ('he went on', 'and he ended'). (b) is the second 'Mea est possessio, olim possideo, prior possideo', unbroken in the Latin. The cut opening is "Who are you? When did you come, and from where? You are not mine. What are you doing with what is mine?", cut for length (the narrator may say he asked who they were and where they came from); the next "[…]" is the Apelles sentence ("By what power, Apelles, do you move my boundary stones?"), cut for length; the third is "Why are you, the rest, sowing and feeding here at your own pleasure? The churches' claim to the Scriptures, in a property lawyer's words.
3. **Against Praxeas 12**, rendered from the Latin (KRO 12558–12562), checked against Oehler (OEH2 43865–43868), which agrees in sense, and Holmes's English (ANF3 51894–51902). Latin: "alium autem quomodo accipere debeas, iam professus sum, personae, non substantiae nomine, ad distinctionem, non ad divisionem. ceterum, (etsi) ubique teneo unam substantiam in tribus cohaerentibus, tamen alium dicam oportet ex necessitate sensus eum, qui iubet, et eum, qui facit." Rendering: "I have already said how you should take the word 'another'. It names a person, not a substance. It marks a distinction, not a division. I hold everywhere one substance in three who are joined together. Yet the sense forces me to call the one who commands another than the one who makes." "(etsi)" is Kroymann's own supplement (KRO 12559); Oehler reads "ubique teneam" with no "etsi" (OEH2 43866). The story stops after the fourth sentence. Job: beat 5. His words for God, person and substance, in his own sentence; the narrator gives the plain sense beside it.

**Connection to carry into the story:** what passed from Tertullian was a Latin way of saying the faith: above all the words for God, one substance and three persons, and with them the claim that the Scriptures belong to the churches that received them from the apostles (T2, T4, T5). The listener must understand three things. First, why it was new: Jerome names only two Latin Christian writers before him (JER 40495), and he had to make Latin carry it, in the words of the law courts he knew (a plea that throws out a case, a title to an estate) and in words for God that later Latin writers kept (D4, S5, S6, I1). Second, how it passed: Cyprian, the next great bishop of Carthage, read him every day and called him "the master"; Novatian at Rome wrote a book on the Trinity that Jerome took for a summary of his; Lactantius said he had pleaded the cause already (I2, I3, I4). Third, why it passed with a mark against it: he wrote the book on the Trinity after he had joined the New Prophecy, and Augustine counted his followers at Carthage among the heresies (I5; AUG 2827–2835). Tell it through what people did: Cyprian asking for the book, Novatian writing his own, the last followers handing over their church. Not as a "this matters because" sentence.

**Ending:** close on the person. Either (a) Jerome's last line: he is said to have lived to a great old age; no one records when or how he died (E9; JER 40514–40516; JERL 55634–55635; credit "Jerome says", and "is said" stays); or (b) the opening seen again: the man who asked only not to be condemned unknown is known best, in the end, from his own books (E1, T1; EUS-ed 10267–10268: his own writings and Jerome are the main sources). Recommended: (a), placed after What came, so the story ends on him, not on Augustine.

**Do not say**

- "He was a lawyer" or "an advocate in Rome" as fact. Say "trained in Roman law, Eusebius says", or "who knew the language of the courts". Whether he practised law is not settled (S5, D3).
- "The first Christian to write in Latin." Say "the foremost early writer of the faith in Latin" (Mark, 2026-11-20); never "the first" in any form (D4; rank, not order: Vincent's "first ... among the Latins", Coxe's "founder of Latin Christianity").
- Not "the first Christian whose Latin books survive" as settled. The one book of Minucius Felix, the Octavius, may be older; held editors differ (Coxe: after 205; Wallis: its date keenly disputed, perhaps about 166). For the card.
- Not "Novatian summed up / copied Tertullian's book" as fact: Jerome's view, disputed by Harnack.
- "A great trial" without its plain sense (if the card uses it).
- Not "he left the church at Carthage" as settled. From whom he withdrew is read differently (Coxe: perhaps only from Rome's bishop; Monceaux: the Catholics at Carthage); Augustine says he later broke from the New Prophecy too.
- "His last book" as settled. Say "what is probably his last surviving book": Monceaux, "très probablement le dernier des ouvrages conservés" (MON 17466–17469); Jerome says many small works are lost (JER 40514–40516).
- "The Christians to the lions": the Latin is singular, "ad leonem" (ANF3-ed 4000–4001).
- His followers "for two centuries": Augustine says only "usque ad nostrum tempus" (AUG 2829); say "down to Augustine's day".
- "The Latin church drew its words from this book" as the narrator's own claim: credit it as later writers' view (Widely Accepted, I1).
- "He invented the word Trinity" or "he was the first to call God a Trinity." The Greek word is older; say he put the Latin words trinitas, persona, substantia to work, and later Latin writers kept them (T5).
- "The Council of Nicaea used his words." No held source says so; Nicaea's word is Greek.
- "The blood of the martyrs is the seed of the church." His words are "the blood of Christians is seed" (E2).
- "He wrote Against Praxeas before his turn" or "as a loyal son of the church." He wrote it after his turn (E7).
- "Praxeas taught that the Father died on the cross" as the narrator's fact. It is Tertullian's charge: "by Tertullian's account" (E7). Do not call Praxeas a bishop or name the bishop of Rome.
- "Never counted a saint" / "never made a saint": not said; no held source says it (Mark, 2026-11-20). Give Augustine's verdict instead: he listed the Tertullianists among the heresies and said Tertullian became a heretic by going over to the New Prophecy (AUG 2827–2835, 2868–2873; Documented as Augustine's).
- "The church refused to make him a saint," "he was condemned" or "excommunicated." No held source records a decision; let the later writers' judgments stand (I5).
- "He founded his own church" as fact. Augustine says he broke from the New Prophecy too and gathered his own followers; credit Augustine (E6, I5).
- Why he turned, as fact (envy at Rome, or second marriage). Credit Jerome and Augustine, side by side (D1).
- "Paul of Concordia was Cyprian's secretary." Paul had seen Cyprian's secretary (D2).
- "Montanists" in the narrator's voice for his own side. Say "the New Prophecy"; "Montanists" was the opponents' name.
- "Priest." If his church office comes up at all, it is Jerome's word, "presbyter", an elder; leave it out (E6).
- A year for his death, or "he died a Montanist" (E9).
- Vincent is for the card only; if the card gives him, give both his praise and his "trial" (I6).
- "Persona means mask." No held source says it.
- Do not add how he felt at any point, or that he was bitter, proud or lonely; Jerome's "a man of keen and vigorous character" (JER 40497) is the only word on his temper, and it is optional.

**For the card, not the story:** (cut for length, Mark, 2026-11-20)

- Beat 1 as a beat: Jerome, from Carthage (JER 40496); Eusebius, "well known at Rome" (EUS 10279–10280); "We are of your stock and nature: men are made, not born, Christians" (ANF3 2710–2712; OEH1 18939–18940); that no source says when or why he converted (S7).
- The crowds' list: "If the Tiber rises as high as the city walls, if the Nile does not send its waters up over the fields …" and the cry "Away with the Christians to the lion!" (ANF3 3997–4003; the Latin singular "ad leonem", ANF3-ed 4000–4001).
- "kill us, torture us, condemn us, grind us to dust; your injustice is the proof that we are innocent" (ANF3 4653–4654).
