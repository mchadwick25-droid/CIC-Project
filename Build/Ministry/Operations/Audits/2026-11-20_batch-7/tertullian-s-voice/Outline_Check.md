# Outline check: Tertullian

## The checker's own heart (written before reading Outline.md)

From the census entry (`why`, `legacy`, `voices`), the source sheet and what Tertullian is known for.

**Essence.** Around the year 200 at Carthage, Tertullian, a convert from paganism whom Eusebius calls well versed in Roman law, became the first great Christian writer in Latin whose books survive. He defended the Christians to Rome's governors in the Apology, argued in the Prescription that the Scriptures belong to the churches the apostles founded, and against Praxeas set out God as one substance in three persons, in Latin words (trinitas, persona, substantia) the West kept using. He is on the tree as the voice that opened Latin Christian argument.

**Also told.** The Apology (197) and its "the blood of Christians is seed"; the Prescription's claim that heretics have no title to Scripture; his turn, about 207–213, to the strict New Prophecy (no second marriage, harder fasts, no flight), which he says separated his side from the "psychics"; Against Praxeas written after that turn; On Modesty, his last surviving book, denying that a church "of bishops" can forgive grave sins; why he turned, where Jerome (the Roman clergy's envy and insults) and Augustine (going over to the Phrygians, condemning second marriage, then breaking from them too) differ; who read him after: Cyprian daily ("Give me the master", Jerome's report at several removes), Novatian (Jerome: an epitome of his Trinity work), Lactantius (skilled but obscure), Vincent (first among the Latins, but changed his belief), Augustine (eloquent works still read, yet his followers counted among the heretics, the last of them returning their basilica at Carthage). His death is not recorded.

(Note for honesty: while reading the batch files, a shell command printed the end of `Outline.md`, its Do not say and Leave out lists, before this was written. The heart above is drawn from the census and the sheet, not from those lines.)

**Against the outline.** The outline's essence and Also told match this heart in substance, including Mark's hearts ruling (no "never a saint"; Augustine's verdict told). No major contribution is left out. The beats tell the heart in the first minute, then follow the life once, in order (convert, Apology 197, Prescription about 200, turn from about 207, Against Praxeas after 213, On Modesty 217–222, then what came); no event is in two beats, and the ending is not told early. The one place where the heart itself goes past the sources is "first ... whose books survive" (finding B1).

## Findings

### BLOCKING

**B1. "The first great writer of the faith in Latin whose books survive" is stated as settled where held sources differ** (essence; D4; Do not say line 2). One held Latin Christian book may be older: the *Octavius* of Minucius Felix. Wallis's notice in the same ANF volume the sheet uses says its date "is still a matter of keen dispute": if Tertullian borrowed from Minucius, "the Octavius was written probably about the year 166" (ANF4 17130–17145). Coxe in the same notice puts it after 205, making Minucius "his contemporary" and the copyist (ANF4 17040–17075). The corpus map rates the date Contested, with one pole "before Tertullian's Apology" (`cic/corpus-map/latin-apologists.yaml`, the Octavius row, lines 455–480). Lactantius names Minucius before Tertullian (LACT-E 4470–4472). The sheet's D4 deals only with Victor and Apollonius, so the Library sweep missed this. The approved heart carries the same line, but the sources do not hold it as settled.
*Correction.* Essence: "was the first great writer of the faith in Latin, and the one who left the deepest mark" (rank, not order: Vincent, "as Origen holds by far the first place among the Greeks, so does Tertullian among the Latins", VIN 13555–13557; Coxe, "the founder of Latin Christianity", ANF4 17042). Add to D4 and to Do not say: "Not 'the first Christian whose Latin books survive' as settled. The one book of Minucius Felix, the Octavius, may be older; held editors differ (Coxe: after 205; Wallis: its date keenly disputed, perhaps about 166). For the card." Setting, Language: change "Latin, which until then had little Christian writing (D4)" to "Latin; Jerome names only two Latin Christian writers before him, whose works are lost (JER 40495)". Connection: change "until then the faith had been argued mostly in Greek" to the same Jerome-credited line, or cut it.

**B2. Jerome's "epitome" claim is stated as fact where a held historian disputes it** (Connection, "Novatian at Rome summed up his book on the Trinity"; Links, "Novatian: summed up his book on the Trinity, Jerome says"; What came 1). Harnack: Jerome's claim that On the Trinity is an epitome of a work of Tertullian "bestätigt sich nicht", is not borne out (HARN 22546–22548, `harnack_geschichte-der-altchristlichen-litteratur-chronologie-band2-deu_1904.txt`). The batch 7 `novatian` outline already bars stating it as settled (its line 118).
*Correction.* What came 1: "Jerome called Novatian's On the Trinity 'a sort of epitome' of Tertullian's work, though a modern historian finds that not borne out (I2; JER 40895–40897; HARN 22546–22548)", or cut Novatian to a name. Connection: "Novatian at Rome wrote a book on the Trinity that Jerome took for a summary of his". Add to I2 on the sheet. Add to Do not say: "Not 'Novatian summed up / copied Tertullian's book' as fact: Jerome's view, disputed by Harnack."

**B3. The Connection states an outcome no held source gives, against Mark's ruling and the outline's own Do not say.** "So the same church that took his words would not take him" implies a decision against him, which the outline itself bars ("No held source records a decision", Do not say; Decision-Log, "Batch 7 hearts approved", item 2).
*Correction.* Replace with: "and the writers who kept his words also weighed his turn: Vincent praised him first and then called him 'a great trial in the Church', and Augustine counted his followers at Carthage among the heresies (I5, I6; VIN 13555–13580; AUG 2827–2835)."

**B4. "A great trial in the Church" is a quoted phrase whose modern sense misleads, with no gloss** (What came 3; Quotes' short phrases; Connection). In a story about a man of the law courts, a listener hears "trial" as a court case. Vincent means a testing of the faithful: his chapter on Origen calls such a teacher's error "a trial" and cites Moses, "the Lord your God doth make trial of you" (VIN 13497–13504; chapter title, VIN 13527–13531, "Tertullian a great Trial to the Church"; and later, God "permits it in order to make trial of us, whether or not we love God", VIN 13618). The Latin of Vincent is not held. Huet's quotation of the Origen chapter reads "magna tentatio" (`origen_opera-omnia-t7-pamphilus-apologia-pg17-grc-lat_migne1857.txt` 88768–88769), which confirms the sense.
*Correction.* Add to Words to explain: "**a great trial** (Vincent): a great test of the church's faithful, the way a false teacher tests them in Moses's words (VIN 13497–13504, 13618). Give this plain sense beside the phrase, or do not use it." Add to Do not say: "'A great trial' without its plain sense."

**B5. The Quote 2 Latin is partly reconstructed from the English, and the card plan does not say so** (Quote 2). The scan has lost "quando et unde" (it reads "lo, el mide") and garbles "fontes", "limites", "prior", "fuit" (OEH2 2280–2290). No other held Latin print has Prescription 37: a corpus search for its key phrases finds only Oehler. Holmes's English (ANF3 21742–21751) confirms each clause's sense, but a translation does not confirm the Latin wording. The outline prints a clean Latin text that no held source shows.
*Correction.* Quote 2: "The Latin is held only in Oehler's damaged scan, which has lost 'quando et unde' and garbles four words; no held print confirms them. The card prints the English alone, or the Latin as the scan shows it with the restored words in brackets, marked as restored from Holmes's English." For Quote 1, every Latin word can be read through the OCR errors (OEH1 11253–11261), so see M1.

**B6. The Length does not fit the beats it lists, which repeats the batch 6 overrun** (Length). It counts about 400 carried words out of 550, which leaves about 150 words for the opening, the essence, six beats, four impact items and the ending. The three renderings alone come to 209 words (73, 83, 53 by count). The beat and impact paraphrases need at least 500 to 550 more, so the story as outlined runs about 750 to 800 words, roughly 5:00 to 5:20 at 150 a minute.
*Correction.* Recount, then choose and say which. Either (a) make the standing cuts mandatory and add more: Quote 1 to its first and last sentences; the father (beat 1); the "lion" clause (beat 2); the Paraclete's saying (beat 6); Lactantius and Augustine's "eloquent" (What came 3); Apelles. That lands at about 600 to 650 words, about 4:00 to 4:20, which falls under "very compelling" and needs a line saying why. Or (b) keep the beats and set the time the count gives, about 5:00, with the reason, for Mark. Do not keep 3:40 with the present beats.

### MINOR

**M1. Quote 1 card plan.** The flag is right: the only held Latin is Oehler's damaged scan, and Thelwall's English confirms each clause. Add the plan: "the card prints the Latin as read through the OCR, with the corrected letters listed, or the English alone." Rendering: "her favor" (*gratiam*) is unclear when heard in a list; use "her reward" (Thelwall, "recompense"). My own rendering otherwise agrees clause by clause.

**M2. Quote 2 rendering omits a sentence silently.** Between "This property is mine" and "I have held it long" the Latin and Holmes have "Why are you, the rest, sowing and feeding here at your own pleasure? This is my property" (ANF3 21747–21749). Mark the cut "[…]" in the rendering so the card shows it. Otherwise it is verbatim to the sense, and "by whose leave" for *qua licentia* and "founders" for *auctoribus* hold.

**M3. Quote 3 Latin.** Kroymann prints "(etsi)" as his own supplement (KRO 12559), and Oehler reads "ubique teneam" with no "etsi" (OEH2 43866). Print "(etsi)" with its brackets, and change "Oehler … agrees" to "agrees in sense". The rendering holds.

**M4. Beat 4: the agency in "the Paraclete separated his side from the rest".** The Latin is "agnitio paracleti atque defensio disiunxit a psychicis" (KRO 11745–11746): his acknowledgment and defense of the Paraclete separated them. "In the end" is also not his: *postea* means afterwards. "The rest" goes past *a psychicis*. Correct to: "later, he writes, his acceptance and defense of the Paraclete separated his side from those he called the unspiritual." Add D6 to Do not say: "Not 'he left the church at Carthage' as settled. From whom he withdrew is read differently (Coxe: perhaps only from Rome's bishop; Monceaux: the Catholics at Carthage); Augustine says he later broke from the New Prophecy too."

**M5. Beat 4: Augustine's "Cataphrygas".** "Took up the prophets he had once argued against" should be "went over to the Phrygians, the New Prophecy's followers, whom he had once argued against" (AUG 2868–2870, "quos ante destruxerat").

**M6. Beat 6 and the Do not say list: "the last book".** Monceaux says "très probablement le dernier des ouvrages conservés" (MON 17466–17469), and Jerome says he wrote many small works that are lost (JER 40514–40516). Write "what is probably his last surviving book" in beat 6 and in Also told, and add it to Do not say. For the bishop's edict, cite ANF4 7187–7188 ("I remit, to such as have discharged (the requirements of) repentance, the sins both of adultery and of fornication"). The Paraclete's saying is in the first person: "the church has the power to forgive sins, but I will not do it" (CSEL20 15213–15214). Do not paraphrase it as the church refusing.

**M7. Words to explain: three passing words.** Add: **Asia** (beat 5, "from Asia"): "the Roman province of Asia, in today's western Turkey", or drop "from Asia". **penance / done penance** (beat 6): "done the repentance the church required". **his Word** (beat 5): "the Word, the name John's Gospel gives the Son" (ANF3 51888–51892, John 1:1). The present glosses are right for the time and place, and none is needless.

**M8. Setting, "He knew Rome too".** This is flat narration of a reading. Eusebius says "one of those especially distinguished in Rome" (EUS 10279–10280), and Rufinus and Valesius took the phrase to mean "among the Latin writers" (EUS-ed 10281–10286). Use "well known at Rome, Eusebius says", credited, or cut it.

**M9. Opening.** "Will not look into the charges openly" drops the Apology's condition and its reason ("if you may not … if … you are afraid or ashamed", ANF3 1448–1455; "Si non licet vobis", OEH1 11030–11032). Use "if Rome's governors may not, or dare not, look into the charges openly". Put the name in the hook ("So, in 197, Tertullian of Carthage wrote ..."), not "a man in Carthage" followed by a reveal (Kit Part 1).

**M10. The Apology's year.** Held editors differ (Coxe: "Great diversity … Mosheim says, a.d. 198, Kaye a.d. 204", ANF3-ed 1443–1446; Wallis "about the year 198", ANF4 17138). Say "about 197" in the story and put the range on the card.

**M11. Small Do not say additions.** "The Christians to the lions": the Latin is singular, "ad leonem" (ANF3 3999). The followers "for two centuries": Augustine says only "usque ad nostrum tempus" (AUG 2829); say "down to Augustine's day". "The Latin church drew its words from this book" (beat 5, "Say it plainly"): credit it as later writers' view (Widely Accepted, I1), not the narrator's.

**M12. What came of him does not mark what he defended** (Kit Part 3). Add: "Defended: the Christians' innocence before Rome's courts (Apology); the apostles' churches' title to the Scriptures (Prescription); one God in three persons against Praxeas; later, the New Prophecy."

**M13. Housekeeping.** The sheet has no I7, but the Links and Leave out cite it: add I7 (EUS 10302–10305, McGiffert: Eusebius quotes an existing Greek translation of the Apology) or drop the citations. Make "hearsay at two removes" (What came 2) and "at three removes" (sheet I3) agree; "through two people, Paul and Cyprian's secretary" is accurate. Close the Sibling "question for Mark" on Novatian: the batch 7 `novatian` outline does not use "epitome". Fifteen possible names is many: give Hilary, Paul of Concordia, Apelles and Pliny by role or cut them.

**M14. Library sweep gap, a second Jerome witness.** Jerome's Chronicle (`jerome_eusebii-chronici-canones-lat_fotheringham1923.txt` 43480–43482): "Tertullianus Afer centurionis proconsularis filius omnium ecclesiarum sermone celebratur". It confirms the father and his fame "in the speech of all the churches". Add it to S4 for the card. It changes no beat.
