## The test, from one hearing

- **Who:** Origen, a teacher of the faith at Alexandria and later Caesarea in the third century. He was the son of a martyr and became the early church's great scholar of Scripture.
- **What happened:** He took over teaching at Alexandria at about seventeen, after his father was martyred. He learned Hebrew, set the Hebrew beside the Greek in the Hexapla, and taught that Scripture has body, soul and spirit. He taught Greek-educated pupils at Caesarea. His bolder ideas, chiefly souls living before birth, were attacked and defended, and a council three centuries later named him among heretics.
- **Why the connection matters:** Later readers built on him. Jerome defended his own Latin Job by Origen's marks. The Philocalia opens with his chapters on reading Scripture. Athanasius called on him for the Son's eternity. Two of his pupils became bishops in Pontus. Methodius attacked him, and Pamphilus and Eusebius defended him.

All three lines come clear from one hearing.

**The essence (mine, from the census entry and the story).** A martyr's son who began teaching the faith at Alexandria as a teenager, Origen became the early church's great scholar of Scripture. He set the Hebrew text beside the Greek and taught a layered way of reading the Bible that later readers kept. His bolder ideas were argued over for centuries. That is why he is on the tree: he is the scholar of the Alexandrian teachers, and his pupils, readers and critics spread across the churches.

**Also told (from the census "why", card and refs).** The Hexapla and Jerome's use of its marks. The three senses of Scripture and the Philocalia. The Son always with the Father, with Athanasius. The pupils from Greek learning, Gregory and Athenodorus. The souls-before-birth teaching, attacked by Methodius and defended by Pamphilus and Eusebius, and the name in the council's acts of 553. Late in life he was tortured for his faith under Decius, and he died at Tyre. The census "why" names this last item as one of its four lines.

## Findings

### B1. BLOCKING: the death is told, then the story goes back

- **Where:** paragraph 2, "In 231 he left for Caesarea, and Jerome says he died at Tyre." Then paragraph 4, "Eusebius says two brothers he taught at Caesarea, Gregory and Athenodorus, both became bishops in Pontus."
- **Why:** the kit forbids telling the ending and then going back. The role makes a jump back BLOCKING. The listener hears that he died, and then hears about him teaching again at Caesarea.
- **Change (smallest):** in paragraph 2, cut ", and Jerome says he died at Tyre". The sentence reads: "In 231 he left for Caesarea." Then put "Jerome says he died at Tyre." in the new paragraph given under B2, after paragraph 4 and before "Some of his ideas...". The life then runs in order: 202, his eighteenth year, 231, the work at Caesarea, his suffering and death. The attacks and the defense after his death follow, and then 553.

### B2. BLOCKING: his suffering under Decius is missing (essential material)

- **Where:** nowhere in the text. It was cut to the card in the 2026-11-16 tightening pass (refs, "After the tightening... card-only": "Stretched in the stocks under Decius: VI.39.5, E 37486-37495"), when the length ceiling was about 360 words.
- **Why:** the census "why" for this entry gives it as one of its four lines: "Late in life he was tortured for his faith, and he died a few years after." The story opens on the boy longing for martyrdom and hiding at home. Without his own suffering, the line through his life lacks its turning point, and the opening's image is never seen again. That reason for cutting it is gone: the batch limit is now about 4:30 (about 675 words), and the story runs 317.
- **Change (smallest):** add one paragraph between paragraph 4 and paragraph 5. It carries B1's moved clause:
  > Late in life, under the emperor Decius, Eusebius says he was chained and tortured, his feet stretched in the stocks for many days. Jerome says he died at Tyre.
- **Source:** Eusebius, Church History VI.39.5, `cic/texts/npnf201_eusebius-church-history-life-of-constantine.xml` 37484-37495: "bonds and bodily tortures and torments under the iron collar and in the dungeon; and how for many days with his feet stretched four spaces in the stocks". The chapter title is "The Persecution under Decius, and the Sufferings of Origen" (line 37378). Jerome, Lives 54, `npnf203_theodoret-jerome-gennadius-rufinus.xml` 40589-40591: "until his sixty-ninth year, and died at Tyre". "Late in life" rests on Jerome's "until the time of Gallus and Volusianus... his sixty-ninth year".
- **Check needed:** this wording is mine as fixer. Under the kit it needs a targeted independent check of the new sentence against these lines. Do not say: "he died a martyr", "he died of his tortures" or "he died in the persecution". Eusebius says the judge "strove eagerly... not to end his life", and McGiffert's note at line 37510 calls the later tradition that he died under Decius "certainly an" error.
- **Length after the fix:** about 340 words, about 2:15. Well under 4:00.

### M1. MINOR: card note on the 553 acts (no change to the text)
