**1. MINOR, rigor: "his editor" (line 3).** Heard once, "his editor" could be taken as an ancient editor of Eusebius. It is McGiffert, his English translator of 1890, and the outline gives him as "his English editor" (Outline 56, 115). Change "Many early Christian writings, his editor wrote," to "Many early Christian writings, his English editor wrote,". This adds 1 word. Line 11's "his editor reckons" can then stand as it is.

**2. MINOR, rigor and order: "He had seen Constantine as a young man" (line 13).** Heard once, "as a young man" attaches to Eusebius. In fact it was Constantine who was young, standing at the senior emperor's right hand in 296–297, when Eusebius was about 36 (E12; EUS 60955–60957). The sentence is also the story's one backward step: "had seen" goes back before 303. The outline places it there (beat 4), so it was planned, and it sets up "later knew him". The listener is not lost, so I hold it MINOR, not a jump back of the kit's kind.
