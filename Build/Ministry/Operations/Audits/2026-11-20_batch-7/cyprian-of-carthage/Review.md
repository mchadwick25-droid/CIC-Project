## The test, from one hearing

1. **Who:** Cyprian, a teacher of public speaking at Carthage who was made its bishop while newly baptized, and led the church of Roman Africa from about 248 until his execution in 258.
2. **What happened:** under Decius he hid and led his church by letter, and the clergy at Rome wrote of the hired man who runs. He refused the envoys of Novatian, a rival bishop, and taught that the church is one mother. In the plague he urged care for all. He broke with Stephen of Rome over baptism given outside the church. Exiled under Valerian, he was tried again and put to the sword in 258.
3. **Why the connection matters:** his teaching on the one church and his council's "bishop of bishops" line were claimed by both sides of a later African split. The Donatists used him to defend baptizing again. Augustine used him for unity: wrong on baptism, but never leaving the church or cutting off those who differed.

## Findings

### BLOCKING

**B1. Paragraph 3: his own reason for hiding is gone, and "calling for" is heard as friendly.** The text is: "The crowds were calling for Cyprian, he wrote, so he withdrew from the city and hid, leading his church by letter."

Two problems follow from it. First, the opening's crowd wanted him as bishop, so a listener hears these crowds as admirers too. Second, his stated reason is missing. He says he withdrew "thinking less of my own safety than of the public peace of the brothers, so that my bold presence should not stir up further the riot" (EP Ep. 20.1, 2971–2975). Without it, the "so" makes the clamour itself the cause. The next sentences then give the Roman charge with no content for his side of it. That tilts the record toward the charge, against the outline's Do not say ("give his reason as his (E4)") and the kit's "the narrator never carries any side's view forward".

- **Change** "The crowds were calling for Cyprian, he wrote, so he withdrew from the city and hid, leading his church by letter." **to** "Crowds were shouting for him, he wrote, and to keep the peace he withdrew from the city and hid, leading his church by letter." (+3 words.)
- **Do not say line to add to the outline:** Not "the crowds were calling for him, so he withdrew". The clamour was hostile ("clamore uiolento"), and his reason is his own: to keep the peace among his people, not for his own safety (EP 2970–2975). Give the reason with the withdrawal.

**B2. Paragraph 7: "by a holy command" settles a disputed rendering.** The *Acta* reads "ex sacro praecepto in suis hortis manebat" (KNOPF 4855). The source sheet renders it "by imperial order" (Source_Sheet D4), and the coordinator's check passed that reading as the late-Latin sense of *sacer* (Fixes.md, line 46). The outline's beat 6 and its Do not say line then rendered it "by a holy command". As heard, "holy command" makes the account claim divine direction. The held renderings do not agree that it does, and the story presents one of them as settled. The difference the story needs is only that he stayed at his gardens, where his own letter says he left them.

- **Change** "The account says he stayed at his gardens by a holy command." **to** "The account says he stayed at his gardens." (−4 words.)
- **Do not say line to add to the outline:** Not "by a holy command" or "by imperial order" for *ex sacro praecepto* (KNOPF 4855). The phrase can be read either way. Say only that the account has him stay at his gardens, waiting for them to come.

### MINOR

**M1. A 34-word sentence (paragraph 6).** The text is: At a council of eighty-seven bishops, probably in 256, Cyprian said: "For none of us sets himself up as a bishop of bishops, or drives his fellow bishops by a tyrant's terror to obey."
- **Split:** "Eighty-seven bishops met in council, probably in 256. Cyprian opened it: "For none of us ..."" (+1 word; SENT 105–108, Cyprian's opening at 136–142.)

**M2. Paragraph 7: "When officers came to take him to Utica".** His letter says he was told that men "had been sent" to take him to Utica ("commentarios esse missos qui me Vticam perducerent", EP 17368–17369; ANF 41229–41231, "had been sent"). He left before anyone came. As written, "came" implies he escaped men at the door.
- **Change** "When officers came to take him to Utica" **to** "When officers were sent to take him to Utica" (+1 word).

**M3. Paragraph 6: "washing in groups cut off from the church" is hard to hear.** "Washing in groups" can be heard as washing done in a group. The outline's gloss is "people who had been baptized in a group cut off from the church" (Words to explain).
- **Change** "held that washing in groups cut off from the church was no baptism" **to** "held that a washing given in a group cut off from the church was no baptism" (+2 words).

- **Change** "said only to lay hands on them as a sign of repentance" **to** "said to keep to what was handed down, and only lay hands on them as a sign of repentance" (+7 words).
