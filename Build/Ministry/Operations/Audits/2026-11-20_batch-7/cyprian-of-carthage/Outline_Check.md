## My account of the heart, written before reading the outline

**The essence.** Cyprian, a teacher of public speaking at Carthage, became bishop of the city while still a newly baptized Christian (Pontius; "about two years" is the editors' reckoning, 246 and 248). From about 248 to 258 he led the Latin churches of Roman Africa through the Decian persecution, a plague and two divisions, largely by letter: his letters are the fullest record of how a third-century church was run (census `why`; some eighty letters held). He held that the church is one, a mother outside whom no one has God as Father, and he died by the sword at Carthage in 258, its first bishop to die a martyr (Pontius's claim). He is on the tree because both sides of the later African split, the Donatists and Augustine, claimed him.

**Also told.**
- Under Decius (250) he withdrew into hiding and led by letter; the Roman clergy wrote to Carthage of the hireling who flees (whether it was aimed at him is the editors' reading, Contested), and he answered that his acts had been reported "less than plainly".
- The lapsed: a clause; their return was settled by the councils of 251 (sibling).
- Novatian's envoys refused (a clause); On the Unity of the Church, its images and the "mother" line (ch. 6).
- The plague (252 by the Chronicle): his address On the Mortality, testing "whether the healthy serve the sick"; the care for all, pagans included, is Pontius's report.
- The quarrel with Stephen of Rome over baptism outside the church: Stephen's words only as Cyprian quotes them; the council of 1 September 256, 87 bishops, "none of us sets himself up as a bishop of bishops"; Firmilian's hostile report of Stephen. No settlement is held; Stephen died in 257.
- Exile at Curubis, 257, after his first hearing ("I am a Christian and a bishop"); the edict of 258; his last hiding so that he could confess at Carthage (his own last letter; Pontius says he refused to withdraw).
- The trial on 14 September 258, "Deo gratias", the crowd's "let us be beheaded with him", and the death, where the Acta and Pontius differ on who bound his eyes.
- What came of him: Augustine and the Donatists both claimed him; his feast at Carthage (Augustine, Procopius); his writings read on.

## Verdict: FIX

The heart matches mine and the approved heart in substance: the rhetorician made bishop while still new, ten years through persecution, plague and division, the letters, the one mother, the death in 258, and both sides of the later split claiming him. Beats tell the heart in the first minute and follow the life once, in order; the ending (the burial at night) is held back. The three quotes are verbatim in Hartel's clean TEI (UNIT 364–365, MORT 584–588, SENT 140–142), and my renderings agree in sense with the outline's. No quote is shared with a live story or a batch 7 outline. Five blocking findings, all small to fix.

## BLOCKING

**B1. The exile gloss "under guard" is unsupported.** Words to explain, "exile": "sent to live under guard in a small coastal town, Curubis" (E11). No held text says he was guarded at Curubis. The *Acta* say only that Paternus sent him "in exilium" to "urbem Curubitanam", that he stayed there long, and that Galerius Maximus recalled him (KNOPF 4835–4836, 4850–4853; GEB 6551 the same). Pontius describes the opposite of close custody: "a sunny and suitable spot, a dwelling, secret as he wished", with "the number of the brethren who visited him" and "the kindness of the citizens themselves, which supplied to him everything" (ANF 28046–28050). Pontius's "gentle custody" (ANF 28181) is the single night in the officer's house in 258, not the exile. Benson's "out-of-the-way ... well-walled little coast town" (BEN 27288–27290) is an editor's description of the place, not of his keeping; Duchesne's "interné" (15668) is a modern summary that also misplaces the arrest at Curubis. A search of every held file naming Curubis found no guard, soldiers or custody tied to the exile. "Exile" is also a word a grown listener knows, so it needs no gloss. The `pontius-the-deacon` outline already says "Not 'under guard': the held texts do not say how he was kept" (its Words to explain); the two stories must agree.
*Correction.* Delete the "exile" line from Words to explain. Add to Setting, Place: "Curubis: a small town on the coast, some fifty miles from Carthage (BEN 27288–27290, an editor's description); he was sent there by the governor in 257 (KNOPF 4835–4836, 4850–4853)." Add to Do not say: "Not 'under guard', 'imprisoned' or 'held' at Curubis. The held texts say he was sent there and stayed there; Pontius says the brethren visited him and the townspeople supplied his needs (ANF 28046–28050). Say 'sent away to live in Curubis, a small town on the coast'."

**B2. The last hiding states Cyprian's letter as settled where Pontius and the *Acta* differ** (Mark, 2026-11-19: credit each). Beat 6 tells only Ep. 81: on friends' advice he hid, so that he could confess at Carthage (EP 17369–17377). Do not say then bars "he refused to hide" outright, citing D4. But Pontius tells it differently: eminent friends "repeatedly urged his withdrawal" and "offered places to which he might retire", and "he did not consent" (ANF 28142–28149); then the officer and his soldiers "came unexpectedly on him ... at his gardens" (ANF 28163–28166). The *Acta* say that after his recall he stayed in his gardens "ex sacro praecepto" and "cotidie sperabat ueniri ad se" (KNOPF 4855–4856). The approved heart carries both ("his own last letter; Pontius says he refused to leave", `Hearts.md`). The sheet's D4 says "Credit each". The outline drops Pontius's side, and its Do not say would make the writer correct him silently.
*Correction.* Beat 6, after the Ep. 81 sentence, add: "Pontius tells it differently: friends offered him places to retire to, and he would not go; the officers found him at his gardens (ANF 28142–28149, 28161–28165; Documented as Pontius's account). Credit each in one sentence: his own letter, then his deacon." Replace the Do not say line with: "Not 'he gave himself up', and not 'he refused to hide' or 'he hid again' in the narrator's own voice. His last letter says that on friends' advice he left his gardens for a while to die at Carthage, not Utica; Pontius says he refused his friends' offers of a refuge (D4). Credit each."

**B3. The Tertullian clause breaks Mark's hearts ruling.** Decision-Log, "Batch 7 hearts approved" (2026-11-20), item 5: Jerome's story of Cyprian asking for "the master" goes to Tertullian, and "Cyprian does not tell it". The `tertullian-s-voice` outline records the ruling as "'Give me the master' and the daily reading are one clause in What came 2; the Cyprian story does not tell them". This outline still allows it: Sibling ("Live `tertullian-s-voice` ... carry the daily reading of Tertullian ... one clause at most here"), Names ("Tertullian ... link, optional, one clause at most"), Links ("Tertullian: read him daily ... Not told, or one clause"), and Leave out ("at most one clause without the quote").
*Correction.* Leave out: replace that line with "'Give me the master' and the daily reading of Tertullian (Jerome; I5): `tertullian-s-voice` tells them, by Mark's ruling (2026-11-20, hearts item 5). Not told here, not even as a clause." In Sibling, Names and Links, replace "one clause at most" and "Not told, or one clause" with "not told here (Mark, 2026-11-20)". Tertullian is then dropped from Names.

**B4. The Sibling line keeps the uncorrected Pontius job.** It gives Pontius's job as "why Pontius wrote the first Christian Life of a bishop". The hearts ruling corrected that job line: "Pontius: not 'the first Christian Life of a bishop' as settled; whether it is the oldest surviving Christian biography is Contested" (`Hearts.md`, "Job lines the sources corrected"; Decision-Log item 1, "with the corrected job lines"). The writer gets this line, and the story names Pontius as "his deacon Pontius, who wrote his life". The line leaves room to call it the first.
*Correction.* Sibling: replace "why Pontius wrote the first Christian Life of a bishop" with "why Pontius wrote his *Life and Passion of Cyprian* (whether it is the oldest surviving Christian biography is Contested, and is Pontius's story, not this one)". Add to Do not say: "Not 'the first Christian biography' or 'the first Life of a bishop'. Say 'his deacon Pontius, who wrote his life'."

**B5. Two words the listener needs are missing from Words to explain.**
- **communion.** It carries beat 3 ("Cyprian at once refused them communion", from EP 6190–6191, "a communicatione eos nostra statim cohibendos") and the Connection ("had let those who differed keep communion with him", from SENT 138–139, "a iure communicationis", and AUG 11588). Heard once, "communion" sounds like the bread and cup alone. Add: "**communion**: here, fellowship as members of one church, worshipping and sharing the Lord's table together; to refuse it is to refuse to treat them as brothers in the church (EP Ep. 44.1, 6190–6191; SENT 138–139). Or say it plainly: 'he would not receive them as members of the church'."
- **confess** (beat 6, from Ep. 81: "a bishop should confess the Lord in the city where he leads the church"). This is his stated reason for the last hiding. Today the word means admitting guilt or sins. Add: "**confess / confession**: to declare one's faith openly before the judge, at the risk of death (EP Ep. 81, 17369–17373). Say 'declare his faith before the governor'."

## MINOR

**M1. Length does not fit the beats as counted.** At about 3:40 (550 words), the opening, essence and beat 1 take about 160 words, and "a third" for What came of him takes about 180. That leaves about 210 words for beats 2 to 7. The three quotes alone take about 86 of them, leaving about 125 words for the hiding and the charge, Novatian and the *Unity*, the plague, the Stephen quarrel, the exile and last hiding (now with two witnesses, B2), and the trial and death (with two witnesses). That is the batch 6 overrun the kit's "length counted first" rule exists to stop. *Correction:* either set the time at about 4:00 and say why (five turning points the job requires, each with its own witness), or extend "Cut first" so beats 2 to 7 fit: add beat 1's estates and *To Donatus*, beat 2's public notice and the lion, beat 5's Ep. 74 words on Stephen's letter, beat 7's governor's sentence (keep "Thanks be to God" and the crowd's cry), and What came 3. Keep the Roman letter and his answer, as the outline already says.

**M2. The ending adds a crowd and "the brothers".** The *Acta* say the body was first laid nearby "propter gentilium curiositatem", then "per noctem sublatum cum cereis et scolacibus ... cum uoto et triumpho magno deductum est" (KNOPF 4929, 4950–4952). They name no bearers and no crowd. *Correction:* "his body was taken up by night, with candles and torches, in prayer and great triumph, to a burial ground on the road out of the city (KNOPF 4929, 4950–4952)." Drop "by the brothers". Drop "the crowd at the door ... is seen again in the crowd that carries him"; the opening image may return through the night procession itself, without a crowd the record does not give.

**M3. Beat 1 credits the name to the wrong witness.** "An elder of the church, Caecilius, brought him to the faith, Pontius says, and he took the man's name." Pontius gives the conversion (ANF 27802–27805) but not the name. The name is Jerome's ("from whom he received his surname", NPNF3 40815–40818) and the ANF editor's (ANF-ed 27517–27518). *Correction:* "... Pontius says; he took the man's name, a later writer adds (Jerome, NPNF3 40815–40818)."

**M4. The "laying on of hands" gloss says more than Stephen's words.** Stephen: "ut manus illis inponatur in paenitentiam" (EP 15431–15432). There is no "bishop's" and no "in prayer" in the line. *Correction:* "hands laid on them as a sign of repentance (Stephen's words, as Cyprian quotes them, E10)."

**M5. The "mother" gloss adds "at baptism".** UNIT 353–355 says "illius fetu nascimur, illius lacte nutrimur", with no mention of baptism. *Correction:* "Christians are born from the church and fed by her, as children from a mother (UNIT 353–355)."

**M6. Quote 2's "justice" may mislead.** "explorat iustitiam singulorum" means testing each person's uprightness, not justice as courts or fairness. *Correction (for the Opus rendering pass):* "searches out how upright each one is" or "tests the goodness of each one", keeping the rest.

**M7. "The record of his trial" for the *Acta*.** The eye-binding, the gold pieces and the burial are in the *Acta*'s narrative between and after the hearings, written by Christians ("beatus Cyprianus", KNOPF 4924–4925), not in the hearing record. *Correction:* Who tells it: "a Christian account of his trials and death, built on the court record"; say "the account of his death" for D2, D3 and the ending.

**M8. Two claims want their held lines.** "In 250 the emperor Decius ordered all to sacrifice": cite McGiffert (EUS-ed 37408–37412: edicts "early in the year 250", to make Christians "deny their faith and return to the religion of the state") with LAPS 8 (345–349). "When he came back in 251": cite Ep. 43, his promise to return after Easter (EP 6148), with the year as the editors' (the `councils-of-rome-and-carthage-251` live story also gives spring 251).

**M9. Damaged scans: say it on the outline.** The *Acta* phrases "a Christian and a bishop" and "Thanks be to God" stand in three prints, each an OCR scan with noise: H3 ("Deo logratias"), KNOPF ("hune", "hoe", "deferria") and GEB. Both phrases read cleanly and identically in KNOPF (4828–4829, 4910–4911) and GEB (6489, 6626), so they stand. *Correction:* add to the Quotes note: "The *Acta* phrases are held only in OCR scans (H3, KNOPF, GEB); both read identically in KNOPF and GEB. No quote rests on Pontius's Latin (H3, damaged)."

**M10. Library sweep: two gaps; no claim rests on them.**
- The sheet says Augustine's sermons on Cyprian (309–313) are "held only in part (MORIN)". They are held in full in Migne, PL 38: `cic/texts/augustine_sermones-1-340-lat_migne-pl38.txt`, "In Natali Cypriani martyris" i to v (99049, 99222, 99329, 99754, 99959). Serm. 310.1 gives "Natale Cypriani martyris Africa tota percelebre" (99224–99225), a stronger primary line for What came 2 than MORIN. Several sermons are headed "Habitus ad Mensam sancti Cypriani" (6854, 22176, 45600). *Correction:* fix "Wanted but not held", and cite PL 38 99222–99226 for What came 2 ("kept across Africa", not just at Carthage).
- Vincent of Lérins, *Commonitorium* 6 (`npnf211_sulpitius-severus-vincent-lerins-cassian.xml` 12513–12575), about 434, is a later witness on the baptism quarrel from Stephen's side. He names Agrippinus as "the first who held it" and says Stephen wrote "Let there be no innovation". Sulpicius Severus (same file, 2657–2661, 5401) tells of a visitor worshipping at "the tomb of the martyr Cyprian" at Carthage. *Correction:* add Vincent to D7 (three witnesses: Eusebius, Cyprian, Vincent) and to the sheet's later witnesses. Sulpicius goes to the card at most.
Otherwise the sweep holds. The corpus-map bucket's Cyprian files are all used or set aside with reasons; Prudentius is confirmed not held; and no claim rests on an editor where a primary text carries it.

**M11. What came 3 overlaps Lactantius's job.** Lactantius's judgment of Cyprian's style is a clause in the `lactantius` outline (its Sibling and Links). Here it is one optional clause, already first to be cut. *Correction:* if kept, credit Jerome only, by role ("a later writer said his works shine brighter than the sun", NPNF3 40820–40821), and leave Lactantius to his own story.
