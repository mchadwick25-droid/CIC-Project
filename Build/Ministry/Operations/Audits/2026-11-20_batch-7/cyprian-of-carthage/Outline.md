# Outline: Cyprian of Carthage

Sheet items (S, E, I, D) are in `Source_Sheet.md`, which gives the file and line for each. EP, UNIT, MORT and SENT are Cyprian's own Latin in Hartel's edition (clean TEI texts); KNOPF is the Latin record of his hearings and death (the *Acta*); ANF is the English of Pontius's *Life* and of Cyprian's works; AUG is Augustine's *On Baptism* in English.

- **The essence:** Cyprian was a teacher of public speaking at Carthage who became its bishop while still a new Christian, about two years after his baptism by modern reckoning (E1–E3; rhetoric Widely Accepted, from Jerome and Lactantius; "still a new Christian" Documented, Pontius; "two years" Dominant Modern Reconstruction, editors' dates of 246 and 248). For ten years, 248 to 258, he led the church of Roman Africa through persecution, plague and division, and some eighty letters, from him and to him, show how that church was run (S2; EP, Ep. 1 to 81; Documented). He taught that the church is one mother, and that no one has God as Father without her; he died by the sword at Carthage in 258, the first of its bishops to die a martyr (E8, E13; Documented; "first" is Pontius's claim).
- **Also told:**
  - **The hiding and the charge.** When the emperor Decius ordered everyone to sacrifice, in 250, Cyprian hid outside the city and led his church by letter. He says the crowds were calling for him and he left to keep the peace (E4; Documented, his own words). Clergy at Rome wrote to Carthage about the shepherd who lays down his life and the hired man who runs (E5; Documented that they wrote it; that it was aimed at him is the editors' reading, Contested). He wrote back that his acts had been reported to them "less than plainly and faithfully" (E5; Documented). His deacon later had to argue that fear was not the cause (E5; Documented).
  - **The Unity of the Church.** When a rival bishop, Novatian, was set up at Rome and his envoys came to Carthage, Cyprian refused them (E7; Documented). He wrote that the church is one as the sun's rays are one light; a branch broken off cannot bud; she is one mother, and "whoever does not have the Church as mother cannot have God as Father" (E8; Documented; written in 251, Dominant Modern Reconstruction).
  - **The plague sermon.** When plague struck Carthage, about 252, people fled and left the sick in the streets, Pontius says, and Cyprian urged his people to care for all, not only their own (E9; Documented as Pontius's account). In his own address on the plague, *On the Mortality*, he named its horrors, and said it tested everyone: whether the healthy would serve the sick (E9; Documented).
  - **The quarrel with Stephen.** Cyprian and the African councils baptized those who came over from groups outside the church; Stephen, bishop of Rome, said only hands should be laid on them, as was handed down (E10; Documented, Stephen's words held only as Cyprian quotes them). At a council of 87 bishops in 256 Cyprian said none of them made himself "a bishop of bishops" (E10; Documented; whether aimed at Stephen is Contested, D8). A bishop in Cappadocia reported that Stephen refused the African envoys a roof and called Cyprian a false Christ (E10; Documented as that bishop's hostile claim). Stephen died in 257; no settlement between them is recorded (E10).
  - **Trial and death.** Exiled in 257, recalled, and in 258 under a harder law he hid once more, this time so that he could die before his own people at Carthage, not at Utica (E11, E12; Documented, his last letter). On 14 September 258 he was sentenced to the sword and answered "Thanks be to God" (E13; Documented, the *Acta*). His deacon Pontius, who shared his exile, tells the death (E13; Documented as Pontius's account).
- **Length:** about 3 minutes 40 (about 550 words at 150 a minute). Counted first: three quotes (about 85 words), the plain sense of nine words to explain (about 70), and the witnesses credited (Pontius, the court record, the Roman clergy, the Cappadocian bishop, Augustine, Procopius: about 80). Why: the job names five turning points, each with its own risk and its own witness, and the death has two witnesses who differ on one detail. A third goes to what came of him, where both sides of a later split claimed him. Cut first, if needed: beat 4's symptoms, the Cappadocian bishop's report in beat 5, and What came 3. Never cut the Roman clergy's letter or his answer to it: that is the record's conflict.
- **Setting:**
  - Time: 248 to 258 (S2). Decius's persecution, 250 (S4, E4). The plague, about 252 (E9). The baptism quarrel, 255 to 257 (E10). The emperor Valerian's laws, 257 and 258 (E11, E12). Plain words: "in the middle of the third century".
  - Place: Carthage, the great city of Roman Africa, in today's Tunisia (S1). Curubis, his place of exile, a town on the coast (E11; Pontius ANF 28054). Utica, another city of the province (E12). Plain words: "Carthage, in what is now Tunisia".
  - Tradition: the Latin-speaking churches of North Africa; the tree's name is "the Latin Pastoral Congregations" (S3). Do not say "Latin pastoral congregational Christianity" aloud.

**Words to explain** (the first time each is heard; plain word or short gloss, worked into the sentence)

- **rhetoric / rhetorician**: "a teacher of public speaking", the art of persuading in court and assembly (E1).
- **neophyte**: "a newly baptized Christian". Never "neophyte" bare.
- **the lapsed**: "those who had given way and sacrificed", with "or bought certificates saying they had" (E6). One clause; the full story is the councils of 251.
- **presbyter**: "an elder of the church". Not "priest".
- **proconsul**: "the Roman governor of the province" (E11, E13).
- **exile**: "sent to live under guard in a small coastal town, Curubis" (E11).
- **baptism given outside the church**: "people who had been baptized in a group cut off from the church" (E10). Never "heretical baptism" or "rebaptism" bare; if "baptized again" is used, say that Cyprian held their first washing was no baptism at all (EP Ep. 69.1, "profanum eius lauacrum").
- **laying on of hands**: "the bishop's hands laid on them in prayer, as a sign of repentance" (Stephen's words, E10).
- **bishop of bishops** (Quote 3): the narrator gives the plain sense alongside: no bishop stands over the others to force them to agree; each answers to Christ (SENT 142–148).
- **mother** (Quote 1): the narrator gives the plain sense: Christians are born into the church at baptism as children from a mother (UNIT 353: "from her womb we are born, by her milk we are nourished").

- **Shape:** Leader. What he built was a church held together by letters and councils through ten hard years; how it ran is in his letters; it served the lapsed, the sick and the poor. The trap is titles and offices in place of acts: so each beat is an act (he hid, he wrote, he refused, he gathered, he answered, he chose where to die).
- **Sibling:** `pontius-the-deacon` (in this batch, after this story; job: why Pontius wrote the first Christian Life of a bishop, what he chose to tell and leave out, the dream at Curubis, his grief at being left behind; the death and the plague are clauses there). Here Pontius is credited as the witness of the death and of the plague care, by name, and the death scene is told here. The dream and "I remained behind" are his; not told or quoted here. `councils-of-rome-and-carthage-251` (after this story; job: the lapsed). Here the lapsed are one clause in beat 2: he would not settle their return alone, and the bishops met in 251. Do not tell the council's terms. `novatian` (after this story). Here Novatian is one clause in beat 3: his envoys refused. His oath, book and church are his. The tradition entry `latin-pastoral-congregational-christianity` (live) tells Cyprian in brief and quotes nothing; its `documentedStories` (not a story text) quote the "hireling" letter and "Thanks be to God" (see Quotes). Live `tertullian-s-voice` and `lactantius` (in this batch) carry the daily reading of Tertullian and Lactantius's praise: one clause at most here.
- **Openings:**
  1. **Chosen. The crowd at the door** (E3; Pontius ANF 27826–27837). In about three sentences: in Carthage, the people crowded the doors of a house and filled the streets around it, waiting for one man. He was a newly baptized Christian, and he had tried to step aside for older men. They wanted him as their bishop. Then the name, credit to his deacon Pontius in a clause, and the essence. It is the first event in time, so no beat repeats it.
  2. Not chosen: the "mother" line (Quote 1). Strong, but it belongs in time order at beat 3; opening on it would tell it twice.

**Story beats** (the opening, the essence and beat 1 are the first minute, about 160 words; beats 2 to 7 deepen it)

1. **Teacher of speech, new Christian, bishop** (E1–E3). He had been famous as a teacher of public speaking, a later writer says (E1, Jerome; Widely Accepted). An elder of the church, Caecilius, brought him to the faith, Pontius says, and he took the man's name (E2; E1). In a letter to a friend he wrote that he had once thought a man could not be born again and shed his old habits (E2, ANF 28370–28376; Documented; paraphrase). He sold whole estates for the poor (E2; Pontius's claim). Some resisted his choosing, Pontius admits (E3). Chosen about 248 (S2; Dominant Modern Reconstruction).
2. **Decius: the hiding and the charge** (E4, E5, E6). In 250 the emperor Decius ordered all to sacrifice to the gods. Cyprian says the crowds shouted for him, and he withdrew for a while to keep the peace, leading his people by letters, thirteen of them (E4; Documented). He was named in a public notice, he wrote later, with his title of bishop added, and the crowd at the games called again and again for him to be thrown to the lion (E4, Ep. 59.6; Documented). From Rome, whose bishop Fabian had just died a martyr (ANF 28905–28916, Cyprian's own letter; Fabian by role, "their bishop"), the clergy wrote to Carthage about the good shepherd who lays down his life and the hired man who runs from the wolf (E5; Documented). Cyprian wrote to them that what he had done had been reported "less than plainly and faithfully" (E5; Documented; may stand as a short phrase). Many of his people gave way under the order; he would not decide their return alone, and when he came back in 251 the bishops met on it (E6; one clause).
3. **Novatian, and the Unity** (E7, E8). A stricter party at Rome set up Novatian as a rival bishop. His envoys reached Carthage, and Cyprian at once refused them communion (E7; Documented; one clause). He wrote *On the Unity of the Church*: one sun with many rays, one tree with many branches; break a branch off and it cannot bud; she is one mother, from whose womb we are born (E8; Documented). Then Quote 1, with its plain sense (Words to explain).
4. **The plague** (E9). About 252 plague struck Carthage. Pontius says people fled and left their own sick, and bodies lay in the streets (E9; credit Pontius). Cyprian gathered his people and told them it was nothing great to care only for their own: they should do good to all, as God sends sun and rain on all (E9; Pontius's account). In *On the Mortality* he named the disease plainly: fever in the throat, vomiting, eyes red with blood (E9, MORT 14; Documented; may be cut). Then Quote 2.
5. **Stephen and the baptism quarrel** (E10). Those baptized in Novatian's church and other groups outside it began to come over (EP Ep. 69.1). Cyprian and the African councils held that their first washing was no baptism, and baptized them (E10; Documented). Stephen, bishop of Rome, wrote that nothing new should be done beyond what was handed down: only lay hands on them in repentance (E10; Documented, held only in Cyprian's quotation; paraphrase). Cyprian, sending Stephen's letter on to a colleague, called it proud and self-contradictory, and Stephen's view an error (E10, EP 15426–15428; keep his own sharpness). On 1 September, probably in 256, 87 bishops met at Carthage, with presbyters, deacons and many of the people (E10; Documented; year Dominant Modern Reconstruction). Cyprian opened: let each give his view, judging no one and cutting off no one who differs (SENT 138–139). Then Quote 3, with its plain sense. A bishop in Cappadocia wrote to Cyprian that Stephen would not even let the African envoys talk with him, told the brothers to give them no roof, and called Cyprian a false Christ and a false apostle (E10; credit "a bishop in Cappadocia, on Cyprian's side"; may be cut). Stephen died in 257; no settlement between them is recorded (E10).
6. **Exile, and the second hiding** (E11, E12). In 257 the governor told him the emperors ordered all who did not follow the Roman religion to honour its rites; he answered that he was a Christian and a bishop and knew no other gods but the one true God, and would not name his elders (E11; Documented, the *Acta*; "a Christian and a bishop" may stand as a short phrase). He was sent to Curubis (E11). In 258 a harsher law ordered bishops put to death; at Rome the bishop Xistus was killed in a cemetery (E12; Documented, Ep. 80; Xistus by role, "the new bishop of Rome"). Officers came to take Cyprian to Utica. On friends' advice he hid again, he wrote, because a bishop should confess the Lord in the city where he leads the church, before his own people (E12; Documented, his last letter, in indirect speech).
7. **Trial and death** (E13). Back at his gardens, he was arrested; the people kept watch all night outside the officer's door (E13). Next day, 14 September 258, the governor told him the emperors ordered him to perform the Roman rites. "I will not." "Think of yourself." "Do what you are ordered. In so just a cause there is nothing to think over" (E13; Documented, the *Acta*; narrator's indirect speech, or this exchange told plainly in the narrator's words, not a fourth set-piece quote). The governor's sentence said he had gathered a wicked conspiracy and led it, called him an enemy of the Roman gods, and said his blood would make him an example to his followers (E13, KNOPF 4900–4907; paraphrase; keep the state's force). Sentenced to the sword, he said, "Thanks be to God" (short phrase). The crowd cried that they would be beheaded with him (E13, *Acta*). At the place of death people climbed into the trees to see, Pontius says (E13). The record of the trial says he covered his eyes with his hand, and an elder and a subdeacon, both named Julian, tied the cloth; Pontius says he bound his own eyes (D2; credit each). He had twenty-five gold pieces given to the executioner, the record says; Pontius says the man's hand shook on the sword (D3; Pontius's account, in his voice).

**What came of him** (about a third of the story; heaviest first)

1. **Both sides of a split claimed him.** Later, in Africa, a church called the Donatists split from the rest. They baptized again those who came over to them from the other side, and quoted Cyprian's letters and his council for it (I1; AUG 11588; CEN `relationsSummary`; Documented). Augustine of Hippo, about 140 years after Cyprian's death, answered them: stop citing Cyprian for repeating baptism, he wrote, and hold with us to Cyprian's example of keeping unity (I1, AUG 11416; Documented; paraphrase only). Augustine quoted the "bishop of bishops" line again and again and asked, "What could be more kind? What more humble?" (I1, AUG 11626; may stand as a short phrase). He held that Cyprian was wrong on baptism but never left the unity of the church (I1, AUG 11949). **The connection**: tell it here, at its strongest.
2. **His feast at Carthage.** Augustine preached on the day of his death, which Carthage kept as his feast (I2, MORIN; Documented). In 533 a Greek historian, Procopius, wrote that the people of Carthage honoured Cyprian above all other holy men, with a church by the sea and a festival in his name (I2, PROC 2206–2210; Documented). One sentence.
3. **His books.** Writers after him praised his clear, persuasive style (I3; Lactantius, Jerome; Lactantius by role, "an African teacher half a century later", unless the link is wanted). One clause; may be cut.

Defended: that the church is one and is mother to all Christians (E8); that each bishop is free in his own church and answers to Christ, not to a bishop over bishops (E10). No held source says who keeps his feast today: do not say "today".

**Quotes** (3)

Only Quotes 1–3 are quoted aloud. "Less than plainly and faithfully", "a Christian and a bishop", "Thanks be to God" and "What could be more kind? What more humble?" may stand as short phrases inside the narrator's sentences. "Thanks be to God" also appears in the tradition entry's `documentedStories` (not a story text); it is the record's own two words at the sentence and has no substitute; Mark to confirm. All three quotes are rendered from Hartel's Latin (clean TEI texts), checked against the ANF English. Each was searched against every census `longDescription` but this entry's, all census string fields, and the batch 6 and batch 7 outlines: none is used.

1. Latin (UNIT 6, 364–365): "habere non potest Deum patrem qui ecclesiam non habet matrem." (ANF 42321–42322: "He can no longer have God for his Father, who has not the Church for his mother.") Rendering: "Whoever does not have the Church as mother cannot have God as Father." ("No longer" in ANF is not in the Latin.) Job: beat 3. The "mother" line of the *Unity*, the heart of his teaching on the one church; the narrator gives the plain sense of "mother" alongside.
2. Latin (MORT 16, 584–588): "pestis ista et lues quae horribilis et feralis uidetur explorat iustitiam singulorum et mentes humani generis examinat, an infirmis seruiant sani, an propinqui cognatos pie diligant, an misereantur seruorum languentium domini, an deprecantes aegros non deserant medici" (ANF 47051–47057). Rendering: "This plague and pestilence, which seems horrible and deadly, searches out the justice of each one and tests the minds of the human race. Do the healthy serve the sick? Do relatives love their kin as they should? Do masters pity their sick slaves? Do doctors stand by the sick who beg for help?" (The Latin's "whether" clauses are rendered as questions; "seruorum" is "slaves", not ANF's "servants", whose modern sense misleads. The quote stops before "whether the fierce restrain their violence".) Job: beat 4. The plague sermon: what the plague showed of each person.
3. Latin (SENT, Cyprian's opening, 140–142): "neque enim quisquam nostrum episcopum se episcoporum constituit aut tyrannico terrore ad obsequendi necessitatem collegas suos adigit" (AUG 11292: "For no one of us sets himself up as a bishop of bishops, or, by tyrannical terror, forces his colleagues to a necessity of obeying"). Rendering: "For none of us sets himself up as a bishop of bishops, or drives his fellow bishops by a tyrant's terror to obey." Job: beat 5. His stand in the quarrel with Stephen, and the line Augustine later carried on (What came 1).

**Connection to carry into the story:** what passed from Cyprian was his writing on the one church, read as two things at once. His *Unity* and his letters taught that the church is one mother and that no one outside her has God as Father (E8); his council said each bishop was free and none might force another (E10). Later the Donatists of Africa, who had split from the rest, used his letters and his council to defend baptizing again (I1, AUG 11588). Augustine of Hippo, a bishop of the same African church, answered with Cyprian's own words: Cyprian, he said, had been wrong about baptism but had never left the unity of the church, and had let those who differed keep communion with him (I1, AUG 11416, 11626, 11949). The listener must understand three things. First, why both sides could claim him: he held baptism outside the church to be no baptism (the Donatists' side), and he held to one church even with those who disagreed (Augustine's side). Second, that "bishop of bishops" is the line Augustine turned against the Donatists: Cyprian had judged no one and cut off no one (SENT 138–142; AUG 11626). Third, that Augustine respected him while saying he was wrong: hence "both sides claimed him". Tell it through these acts, the quoting and the answering, not as a "this matters because" sentence.

**Ending:** close on the person. The record of his death ends at night: his body was carried away by the brothers with candles and torches, in prayer and great triumph, to burial on the road out of the city (E13, KNOPF 4950–4952; Documented). Hold this one detail back from beat 7 and tell it as the last sentence, after What came of him; the crowd at the door in the opening is seen again in the crowd that carries him. Do not end on Procopius or Augustine.

**Who tells it**

- **Cyprian himself**, in his letters, his *Unity*, *On the Mortality* and the council's record: the hiding and his reasons, the refusal of Novatian's envoys, the mother line, the plague, his council speech, his last letter (E4, E5, E7–E10, E12). Credit "he wrote" where he defends himself (beat 2).
- **The clergy at Rome**, in 250, writing to Carthage (E5). Their words are held; that they meant Cyprian is the editors' reading: say they wrote "of the good shepherd and the hired man who runs", and let the listener hear the charge. Do not say they called him a coward.
- **Stephen**, known only through Cyprian's quotation of his letter (E10). Say so in a clause: "as Cyprian quotes him".
- **A bishop in Cappadocia** (Firmilian), on Cyprian's side and hostile to Stephen (E10). Credit his report as his.
- **The court record** (the *Acta*), written by Christians from the hearings: the two hearings and the death (E11, E13). Say "the record of his trial".
- **Pontius**, his deacon, who shared his exile and wrote soon after his death, as open praise (E2, E3, E9, E13). Credit him where he praises, where he tells the plague care and where he tells the death. Say once, inside the sentence that brings him in, that he writes as an admirer.
- **Augustine**, about 140 years later, arguing with the Donatists (I1).
- **Procopius**, 533 (I2).

**Do not say**

- Any word in "Words to explain" used without its plain sense the first time it is heard.
- Not "two years after his baptism" as fact. Say "while still a new Christian", or "about two years after his baptism, by modern reckoning" (D9).
- Not "he fled in fear", "he ran away" or "a coward". Say "he withdrew from the city and hid", and give his reason as his (E4).
- Not "Rome accused him of cowardice" or "the Roman church condemned him". The clergy at Rome wrote to his clergy of the good shepherd and the hired man who runs; how sharp it was is debated (E5, D10). Do not soften it either: tell what they wrote, and that he and Pontius both answered it.
- Not "the plague of Cyprian" as the narrator's name for it; no held source here uses the name.
- Not that *On the Mortality* told Christians to nurse pagans. Pontius says he urged care for all; the sermon tests whether the healthy serve the sick (D6).
- Not "Cyprian founded the African councils" or "invented councils". An African council under Agrippinus had ruled on baptism before him (D7).
- Not "Cyprian was the first to rebaptize" as fact. Eusebius says so; Cyprian himself names an earlier council (D7).
- Not "Stephen excommunicated Cyprian" as fact. The record holds Firmilian's report that Stephen refused the envoys a roof and called Cyprian false, and Cyprian's charge of error (E10). Say "Stephen broke off with the African envoys, a bishop in Cappadocia reported".
- Not "Cyprian rejected Rome's authority" or "Cyprian taught the primacy of Rome". The chapter 4 dispute stays on the card (D1).
- Not "the 'bishop of bishops' line was aimed at Stephen" as fact (D8). Tell it as spoken at the council in the months of the quarrel.
- Not "they made peace" or "the quarrel was settled". No reconciliation is recorded (E10).
- Not "he gave himself up" or "he refused to hide". In 258 he hid again, by his own letter, to die at Carthage (D4, E12).
- Not "beheaded in the arena", "thrown to the lions" or "burned". He was put to the sword in an open field outside the city (E13; Theodoret's "fire" is wrong, D5).
- Not "on 13 September" or "14 August". He was tried and died on 14 September 258 (E13).
- Not "he bound his own eyes" as fact. Give both witnesses (D2).
- Not "God strengthened the executioner's hand" in the narrator's voice. That is Pontius's reading (D3).
- Not "a third of Carthage died" or any death toll for the plague. No held source gives one.
- Not "the Donatists were followers of Cyprian". They quoted him (I1).
- Not "Augustine said Cyprian was a heretic". He said Cyprian was wrong on baptism and faithful to unity (I1).
- Not "Pontius wrote the Life" as unqualified fact where the doubt matters; the Life is unsigned and Jerome names him. Here one clause, "his deacon Pontius, who wrote his life", is enough; the doubt is Pontius's own story.
- Not "the most important Latin father before Augustine" or similar ranking. No held source here gives it.
