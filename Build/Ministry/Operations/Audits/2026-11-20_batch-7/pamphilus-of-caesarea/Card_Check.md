**Verdict: FIX** (7 changes below).

## The ending, and Sophronius's Greek

- **Sophronius ends "suffering", not "blood": confirmed.** JER-L 58506-58507 prints "δι’ ὦν, ὡς ἐμο / δοχεῖ, τὰ ἴγνη τοῦ οἰχείου χαταλέλοιπε πάθους". Read by hand, with the scan's χ for κ and γ for χ: τὰ ἴχνη τοῦ οἰκείου καταλέλοιπε πάθους, "he has left the traces of his own suffering". The last word is πάθους. The outline's claim that the Greek "confirms" the last clause was wrong, and the card is right to record the difference.
- **The story's words are Jerome's.** Jerome's Latin (JER-L 58495-58497): "quanto magis tot millia versuum, que mihi videtur sui sanguinis signasse vesti- / giis5 !". The verb is singular, so its subject is Pamphilus: he seems to me to have marked them with the traces of his own blood. The brief's quotation, "He seems to me to have marked them with the traces of his own blood", follows this exactly. Richardson's English (JER 40966-40968) reads "lines which seem to me to be traced in his blood". The closing sentence, "which Jerome had once said seemed marked with his blood", is narration, not quotation; its "blood" is Jerome's (sanguinis), and its "seemed marked" follows Richardson's construal. It holds.
- The card's own description of the Latin, however, says "the volumes seem to him to be marked". That is Richardson's construal, not the Latin's: FIX 6.
