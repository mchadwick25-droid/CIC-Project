# Outline: Novatian

Sheet items (S, E, I, D) are in `Source_Sheet.md`, which gives the file and line for each. ANF is Novatian's own books in English and Cyprian's letters in English; EP is Cyprian's Latin letters (Hartel), including Ep. 30, the letter Novatian wrote for the Roman clergy; EUS is Eusebius in English (with Cornelius's and Dionysius's letters), EUSG the Greek; SOC is Socrates and Sozomen in English, SOCG Socrates's Greek; NIC is the canons of Nicaea.

- **The essence:** Novatian was a leading elder of the church at Rome and a writer on the faith; his *On the Trinity* set out what the church believed of Father, Son and Spirit (E2; the book Documented; "leading" Widely Accepted, from Cornelius's sneer and Harnack, E2, E5). In 251 a stricter party made him a rival bishop of Rome against Cornelius, and he led a church that would not take back to communion those who had denied the faith under persecution; it left their pardon to God (E5, E8; Documented). That church lasted for centuries, and almost all we know of him comes from the men who fought him (S3, I1, I2; Witness kinds; Documented).
- **Also told:**
  - **The book.** *On the Trinity* follows the church's short summary of faith, the "rule of truth": God the Father, maker of all; the Son, truly man and truly God; the Father and Son two, not one person under two names; then the Spirit (E2; Documented). Its date is uncertain: one historian puts it in the 240s, older editors after 256 (D1; Contested). It survived under Tertullian's name (Harnack, HARN 22499–22500), and some took it for Cyprian's (Wallis, ANF-ed 60400–60403). Jerome named Novatian as its author (I3; Widely Accepted).
  - **His own letter of 250.** With no bishop at Rome, he wrote for its clergy that those who had given way must wait, with tears, for a bishop and a council; meanwhile the dying who repented should be helped (E4; Documented; authorship by Cyprian's word, Widely Accepted). Quote 1.
  - **The election and the charges.** In 251 Cornelius was chosen; a stricter party, with confessors who had been jailed for the faith, set Novatian up against him (E5; Documented). Everything on how it happened comes from Cornelius, his rival: sworn oaths never to seek the office, three country bishops brought to Rome and, he says, made drunk; and earlier, a sickbed baptism and a refusal to help in the persecution (E1, E3, E5; Cornelius's charges; McGiffert calls some of them slander). Novatian said he had been pushed into it against his will (E7; his claim, reported by Dionysius).
  - **His rule and the break.** He and his church would not admit to communion those who had sacrificed; they were urged to repent and their pardon left to God (E8, I1; Documented, Socrates and Acesius). Cyprian answered that this told the fallen to weep day and night and then die outside the church (E8; Documented, as his attack). At Rome sixty bishops declared him outside the church (E9; a clause). His church spread to Gaul and the East, called itself "the pure", and baptized again those who joined it (S3, E10; Documented).
  - **Acesius and the ladder.** At Nicaea in 325, Acesius, a bishop of Novatian's church, accepted the creed and the Easter date, and explained his church's rule; Constantine answered with the ladder (I1; Documented as Socrates's report from an old man who was there). Quote 3. The council set terms for receiving his church's clergy (I1; Documented).
- **Length:** about 3 minutes 30 (about 525 words at 150 a minute). Counted first: three quotes (about 85 words), the plain sense of eight words to explain (about 60), and the witnesses credited (Cornelius three times, Cyprian twice, Dionysius, Socrates and his old informant, Sozomen optional, Jerome by role, McGiffert by role: about 100). Why: a person's story near the usual three minutes, stretched because nearly every claim about his life must carry the name of the enemy who made it, and because the job runs from 250 to Nicaea in 325 and Rome in the 420s. A third goes to what came of him. The plan leaves out the sickbed baptism (beat 1), Gaul and Antioch in beat 5, Dionysius's later attack (beat 5) and What came 3; each is marked "only if the count allows" (about 115 words together). Recount of the plan against 525: opening with Quote 1 about 90, essence 75, beat 1 85, beat 2 60, beat 3 with Quote 2 180, beat 4 125, beat 5 35, What came 1 with Quote 3 175, What came 2 45, ending 70: about 940 words, about 6 minutes 15. That is over 525 and over the kit's four minutes. Never cut Cornelius's oath charge or Cyprian's answer: that is the record's conflict.
- **Setting:**
  - Time: before 250 (E1, E2); 250, the persecution under the emperor Decius, Rome without a bishop for over a year (S1, E4); 251, the rival election and the council at Rome (E5, E9); the spread, 251 to about 257 (E10); Nicaea, 325 (I1); Rome, until about 422 (I2). Plain words: "in the middle of the third century".
  - Place: Rome (S2). Then Carthage, Alexandria, Arles in Gaul, Antioch, Phrygia, Nicaea and Constantinople, each a clause (E6, E7, E10, I1, I2). Plain words: "Rome".
  - Tradition: the tree's name is "Novatianism" (S3). His followers called themselves the Cathari, "the pure" (S3). Say "Novatian's church" aloud.

**Words to explain** (the first time each is heard; plain word or short gloss, worked into the sentence)

- **presbyter**: "an elder of the church". Not "priest".
- **the lapsed / those who had sacrificed**: "Christians who had given way under the persecution and offered sacrifice to the gods, or paid for a certificate so as not to have to" (E4, E8; the certificates, ANF 33695–33708). One clause.
- **communion**: "the bread and cup of the Lord's Supper, shared by the church"; to be kept from communion is to be shut out of the church's fellowship (E8, I1).
- **confessors**: "Christians who had been jailed or tortured for the faith and had not given way" (E5).
- **rival bishop**: "a second bishop of Rome, set up against the first". Never "antipope" or "pope" (D12).
- **Trinity / the rule of truth**: "God as Father, Son and Holy Spirit"; "the church's short summary of what it believed, the forerunner of the creed" (E2).
- **Cathari**: "a Greek word meaning 'the pure', the name his followers gave themselves" (S3). Say whose name it is.
- **baptized again**: "they baptized a second time those who came over to them from the larger church, holding its baptism no baptism" (E10). One clause.
- **a sin unto death** (inside Acesius's words, if used): "a grave sin after baptism, such as denying Christ, which the Bible calls a sin that leads to death" (I1).
- **lay hands on him** (beat 3): "the laying on of hands, the act that made him a bishop" (EUS 38531–38533, "imposition of hands").
- **ascetic** (beat 2): "living apart in strict self-denial and prayer" (EUS-ed 38717–38720).
- **penance** (What came 1, canon 8): "a set time of repentance before being taken back" (NIC 3776–3777).
- **Council of Nicaea**: "the great gathering of bishops called by the emperor Constantine in 325" (I1).
- **threshold / camp** (Quote 1): the narrator gives the plain sense: they may come to the church's door and plead, but may not force their way back in before the church has judged (E4).

- **Shape:** Teacher or thinker. The question he was trying to answer: what the church believes of God, and whether the church may take back those who denied Christ. What he taught: the Son truly God and truly man; and a church that does not give back what only God may forgive. Who learned it: a church that kept his rule for two centuries and more, and readers who kept his book under other men's names. The trap is a list of books: so only *On the Trinity* is named, shown through what it argues, and his one idea about the lapsed is shown in his own words (Quote 1).
- **Sibling:** `cyprian-of-carthage` (batch 7, outline done; job: his life as bishop; here Novatian's envoys were refused, one clause there). Here Cyprian acts twice: he names Novatian as the writer of the letter of 250 (the opening) and answers his rule (beat 4); the envoys are one clause. None of Cyprian's quotes is reused ("mother", the plague, "bishop of bishops"). `councils-of-rome-and-carthage-251` (batch 7, after this; job: the lapsed, the African middle way, the sixty bishops, the split). Here the sixty bishops are one clause in paraphrase; the words "strangers" and "medicines of repentance", and Socrates's statement of the rule ("should not admit to the sacred mysteries ..."), are the council's. `dionysius-of-alexandria` (batch 7, outline done; job: his own life; the letter to Novatian is a clause there). Here the letter is quoted (Quote 2, allocated here by the batch README). Links read: `hippolytus-of-rome` (live: Damasus's verse placing Hippolytus in "the split of Novatus"; not told here, its quote not reused), `socrates-of-constantinople` (live: Auxanon, Socrates's kindness to the Novatians; its quotes, such as "a mere youth", not reused).
- **Openings:**
  1. **Chosen. The letter of 250** (E4; EP Ep. 30.6; Cyprian EP Ep. 55.5). In about three sentences: in 250, with the persecution going on and Rome without a bishop, one of the city's elders, Novatian, wrote for its clergy to Carthage about the Christians who had given way (Cyprian names him as the writer, and says he read aloud what he had written, EP 7570–7573). They must wait for a bishop and a council, he wrote, though the dying who repented might be given careful help (ANF 31709–31722, 31820–31827). Quote 1. Then the essence.
  2. Not chosen: Cornelius's charge of the oath at the communion table (E8). Strong, but opening on an enemy's charge makes the charge the frame; it belongs at beat 4.

**Story beats** (the opening, the essence and beat 1 are the first minute, about 160 words; beats 2 to 5 deepen it)

1. **Elder and writer** (E1, E2). By his rival Cornelius's account, he was baptized on what seemed his deathbed, and made an elder by the bishop over the clergy's objection, since one baptized in bed was not thought fit (E1; Cornelius's charge; the baptism Widely Accepted; only if the count allows). He wrote *On the Trinity*: the rule of truth bids us believe first in God the Father, maker of all things; then in the Son, who is both man and God, so that whoever leaves out one part of the truth will never hold the whole of it (paraphrase, no quote marks, ANF 61355–61357); and Father and Son are two, against those who said they were one person (E2; Documented). When he wrote it is uncertain; a German historian, Harnack, put it in the 240s, before the persecution (D1; credit; or say "its date is uncertain").
2. **In the persecution** (S1, E3). Cornelius later charged that in the persecution Novatian shut himself in his room and refused the deacons who begged him to help, saying he no longer wished to be an elder (E3; Cornelius's charge). An American historian who edited Eusebius, McGiffert, calls this slander; another suggests he had withdrawn to live as an ascetic (D2; credit by role).
3. **The rival election, 251** (E5, E6, E7). Cornelius was chosen bishop, by sixteen bishops in Cyprian's count (E5). A stricter party, with confessors who had suffered in prison, set Novatian up against him (E5; Documented). Cornelius, writing to a bishop in Antioch, gave his version: Novatian had sworn terrible oaths never to seek the office, then fetched three simple bishops from a small corner of Italy, shut them in, and at the tenth hour, drunk and sick, made them lay hands on him (E5; Cornelius's charge; told as his; the drunkenness is called slander by McGiffert). Cornelius's letter mocks him as the great defender of the church's teaching (may stand as a paraphrase). Novatian said he had been pushed into it against his will (E7). His envoys to Carthage were refused (E6; one clause). Dionysius of Alexandria wrote to him: Quote 2.
4. **His rule, and the break** (E8, E9). Rome's council of sixty bishops declared him and his followers outside the church (E9; one clause, paraphrase only). His rule: those who had sacrificed would not be given communion again; they were urged to repent and to leave their pardon to God (E8; Documented). Cornelius charged that at the communion table he held each person's hands and made him swear by the body and blood of Christ never to go back to Cornelius, so that instead of "Amen" he said "I will never return to Cornelius" (E8; Cornelius's charge, his gravest; indirect speech, not a quote). Cyprian answered that this was to tell the fallen to mourn and weep day and night, and then die outside the church (E8, ANF 34105–34110; paraphrase). Cyprian also wrote that whoever is not in the church of Christ is not a Christian (E8; paraphrase; keep its force).
5. **The church spreads** (E10). He sent bishops of his own to many cities, Cyprian complained (E10; Documented). A bishop at Arles in Gaul joined him and shut out those who knocked at the church with tears, Cyprian wrote (E10; the opening's doors seen again in an enemy's words; only if the count allows). Antioch's bishop leaned his way (E10; only if the count allows). His followers called themselves the Cathari, "the pure", and baptized again those who came over to them (S3, E10; Documented). Dionysius, a few years later, wrote of hating him for splitting the church and, he said, making out our merciful Lord to be unmerciful (EUS 39714–39722; E10; Dionysius's charge; only if the count allows).

**What came of him** (about a third of the story; heaviest first)

1. **Acesius at Nicaea.** In 325 the emperor Constantine called to the council Acesius, a bishop of Novatian's church; Sozomen, a later historian, says at Constantinople, and that Constantine esteemed him for his life (I1, D7; Sozomen optional, by role). Acesius accepted the creed and the Easter date: the council, he said, had decided nothing new. Asked why he stayed apart, he told what had happened under Decius, and gave the old strict rule: those who commit a sin unto death after baptism are urged to repent, but must look for pardon from God, not from the priest (I1; Documented as Socrates's report). Then Quote 3. Socrates says no other writer recorded it; he heard it from an old man of Novatian's church who had gone to Nicaea as a youth (I1). Nicaea's eighth canon set terms for receiving clergy of "the pure": they must accept the twice married and the lapsed after penance, and where a city already had a bishop, theirs would not be a second one (I1, NIC 3769–3791; Documented). **The connection**: tell it here, at its strongest.
2. **Two centuries at Rome.** Socrates says that until the 420s Novatian's church flourished at Rome, with many churches and large congregations, until a bishop of Rome took their churches and drove their bishop into private houses (I2, SOC 19289–19294; Documented as Socrates's report; the bishop by role). One or two sentences. Keep the force: the churches were taken.
3. **The book under other names.** It survived under Tertullian's name (Harnack, HARN 22499–22500), and some took it for Cyprian's (Wallis, ANF-ed 60400–60403). Jerome named Novatian as its author. One clause; only if the count allows.

Defended: that the church may not give back communion to those who denied Christ, only urge them to repent and leave their pardon to God (E8, I1); that the Son is truly God and truly man, and distinct from the Father (E2). No held source says who keeps him today: do not say "today".

**Connection to carry into the story:** what passed from Novatian was a church and its rule. He and his followers held that the church may not give back communion to those who had denied Christ: they were urged to repent and to look to God alone for pardon (E8; SOC 14088–14090). That church kept the rule and kept the common faith. At Nicaea in 325 its bishop Acesius accepted the creed and said the council had decided nothing new; he stayed apart only because of that rule, which he traced back to Decius's persecution (I1, SOC 3687–3716). Constantine answered with the ladder (Quote 3), and the council's eighth canon set the price of coming back: accept the lapsed after penance and the twice married (NIC 3769–3777). The listener must understand three things. First, that the split was over discipline, not belief: the same creed, a different answer on who may come back. Second, that the rule did not say the lapsed were damned; it said pardon was God's to give, not the church's (D4). Third, the ladder: Constantine told Acesius to set up a ladder and climb to heaven alone. Socrates gives no reading. Sozomen, a later historian, took it as blame of a man who thought himself without sin (D8). The narrator adds no reading of their own. Tell it through Acesius's answers and the canon's terms, not as a "this matters because" sentence.

**Ending:** close on the person, with his death, held back from the beats. How he died is not known for certain (HARN 22550–22551). Socrates, writing almost two centuries later, says he died a martyr under the emperor Valerian, and his church later remembered him so (EUS-ed 38240–38242; HARN 22551–22552). No other early writer confirms it (ANF-ed 60394–60397). One editor reports that the Novatians' own book of his martyrdom made him only a confessor (SOC-ed 14134–14141). Credit each, or drop the book clause. Tell it as the last sentences, after What came of him. Do not end on Celestine or on the ladder.

**Do not say**

- Any word in "Words to explain" used without its plain sense the first time it is heard.
- Not "antipope", "pope" or "the first antipope". Say "a rival bishop of Rome" (D12).
- Not "he taught that the lapsed could never be saved" or "he denied that God could forgive". He held that the church could not restore them, and left their pardon to God (D4).
- Not "he refused peace even to the dying" for 250. His letter of 250 allowed careful help to the dying who repented (E4); the rule came after.
- Not 'he came to the faith as a grown man', 'he was born a pagan' or 'he converted late'. Cornelius gives only a sickbed baptism, and McGiffert says his parents' faith is unknown (EUS-ed 38636–38640).
- Not "he changed his mind out of ambition" or any motive for the change. No held source gives one; McGiffert's "became very decided" is an editor's reading (E4).
- Not "he was a coward", "he hid", "he was drunk", "he bribed", "he forced the oath" as fact. Each is Cornelius's or Cyprian's charge (E3, E5, E8). Do not soften them either: tell each as charged.
- Not "the three bishops were drunk" in the narrator's voice. "Cornelius says".
- Not "he sought the office all along" or "he was forced to accept it" as fact. Give both claims (D3).
- Not "Novatian was a heretic in his teaching on God" as fact. Dionysius charged it; Acesius accepted the creed, and later historians say his church held the same faith (D5).
- Not "Novatian was a Stoic philosopher" (D11), "the first Latin theologian of Rome" or any ranking (D12).
- Not "On the Trinity was written in 257" or "before 250" as fact (D1). Not "Jerome said it copied Tertullian" as settled; Harnack disputes it (I3).
- Not "Novatus" for him aloud, and never confuse him with Novatus of Carthage, a different man who joined the split (S4).
- Not "the pure ones" or "the Cathari" in the narrator's voice as praise. It is the name they gave themselves; Eusebius calls it pride (S3).
- Not "strangers to the church" or "medicines of repentance": the council's words. Say "declared outside the church" (E9).
- Not Socrates's words "should not admit to the sacred mysteries ...": the council's quote. Tell the rule in Acesius's terms or in paraphrase (E8).
- Not "Acesius, bishop of Constantinople" without credit. Socrates says only "a bishop of the Novatians"; Sozomen names the city (D7).
- Not "Constantine mocked him" or "Constantine praised him" in the narrator's voice. Socrates gives no reading; Sozomen took it as rebuke (D8).
- Not "Nicaea welcomed the Novatians back" or "they refused the terms". The canon set terms; the held sources here do not say how many took them.
- Not "Socrates was a Novatian" (D10). Say "a historian kind to Novatian's church".
- Not "the church lasted until the sixth century" or "the fifth" as fact. Say "for centuries" (D9).
- Not "Novatian died a martyr" as fact. "Socrates says" (E12).
- Not "the first split in the Roman church". The earlier quarrel of Hippolytus is not settled as a split (Hippolytus's live text); no held source here ranks it.
- Not "Novatian wrote that the doors should be shut". His letter of 250 says the fallen should knock and wait (Quote 1).
