# Source sheet: Novatian

Researcher's sheet for batch 7 (2026-11-20). The job: the Roman presbyter who wrote *On the Trinity*, was set up as a stricter rival to Cornelius in 251, and founded a church that would not take back those who had denied the faith; it lasted for centuries, down to Acesius at Nicaea and Constantine's "ladder". Told openly as known mostly through enemies. Shape: Teacher. The card (`Card.existing.json`) was used as a starting list only.

**Conventions.** Line numbers are each file's own. XML quotes are given with tags and notes removed; Latin as printed (u for v). Square brackets are the translator's or editor's.

**Language.** Novatian wrote in Latin. *On the Trinity* and *On the Jewish Meats* are held only in Wallis's English: no Latin of either is in the Library. His letter for the Roman clergy (Ep. 30) is held in Latin (EP, clean). Cornelius and Dionysius wrote in Greek; their letters survive only inside Eusebius, held in Greek (EUSG, damaged scan) and English (EUS). Socrates and Sozomen wrote in Greek; Socrates is held in Greek (SOCG) and English (SOC).

**Witness kinds and gaps.** Almost everything about his life comes from his opponents: Cornelius (his rival, 251), Cyprian (who refused him, 251–256), Dionysius of Alexandria (who urged him to step down, 251, and later attacked him, about 257). His side survives in two books (*On the Trinity*, *On the Jewish Meats*), one letter he wrote for the Roman clergy (Ep. 30, 250), and the later report of Socrates (about 439), who had Novatian informants and wrote kindly of them. "We have not one remaining line of his in self-defence or against his adversaries" (Lardner, quoted by Coxe, ANF-ed 60305–60308). No record by his own church of his election survives.

## 1. Setting

**S1. Time.** Rome, about 250 to 251, during and just after the persecution under the emperor Decius. The bishop of Rome, Fabian, died a martyr early in 250 (Cyprian's letter to the Roman clergy, ANF 28905–28916: "the departure of the excellent man, my colleague ... his glorious end"). The see was then "vacant for more than a year" (EUS-ed 38221–38222). Cornelius was chosen bishop in 251: "in March, 251" (EUS-ed 38480–38481); "June 4, a.d. 251" (Coxe, ANF-ed 60290). The editors differ; say "in 251". Documented (year).

**S2. Place.** Rome. The Roman church then had "forty-six presbyters, seven deacons, seven sub-deacons, forty-two acolyths, fifty-two exorcists, readers and janitors, and over fifteen hundred widows and persons in distress" (Cornelius, EUS 38565–38615). Contemporary, hostile witness, but the count is not in dispute. Documented. Plain words: "Rome".

**S3. Tradition.** The census tradition is `novatianism`, named "Novatianism" (CEN). His followers called themselves *Cathari*, Greek for "the pure": "those who, in the pride of their imagination, call themselves Cathari" (Eusebius, EUS 38268–38270, note "καθαροί, 'pure'"). Jerome: "the dogma of the Novatians, or as they are called in Greek, the Cathari" (JER 40888–40890). Documented. Plain words: "Novatian's church".

**S4. His name.** Eusebius and the Greek writers call him "Novatus"; "Cyprian and the Latins write the name Novatianus" (EUS-ed 38199–38201). Not to be confused with Novatus, a presbyter of Carthage who also joined the split (EUS-ed 38207–38209, 38501–38504). Documented.

## 2. Events

**E1. Before 250: convert, sickbed baptism, ordination.** Cornelius: "Satan, who entered and dwelt in him for a long time, became the occasion of his believing. Being delivered by the exorcists, he fell into a severe sickness; and as he seemed about to die, he received baptism by affusion, on the bed where he lay; if indeed we can say that such a one did receive it" (EUS 38630–38643). And: he "was judged worthy of the presbyterate through the favor of the bishop who ordained him ... This had been resisted by all the clergy and many of the laity; because it was unlawful that one who had been affused on his bed on account of sickness as he had been should enter into any clerical office; but the bishop requested that he might be permitted to ordain this one only" (EUS 38728–38755). Contemporary, hostile (Cornelius, 251). The baptism: McGiffert: "There is no reason to doubt that Novatian received clinical baptism, as here stated by Cornelius" (EUS-ed 38635–38636). Baptism on a sickbed: Widely Accepted. "Satan ... dwelt in him": Cornelius's hostile claim only. Ordained by Fabian: Wallis says so (ANF-ed 60379–60381); Cornelius gives "the bishop" without a name.

**E2. On the Trinity: what it says.** Own words (English only).
- Opening: "The Rule of truth requires that we should first of all things believe on God the Father and Lord Omnipotent; that is, the absolutely perfect Founder of all things" (ANF 60470–60472). The "Rule of truth" is the church's short summary of faith (ANF-ed 60435–60436: "Which we call the Creed"). Documented.
- Plan (the translator's preface, ANF-ed 60432–60458): chapters 1–8 on God the Father; 9–28 on the Son, "very man and very God"; chapter 18 against the Sabellians, "the distinction of the Father and of the Son"; chapter 29 on the Holy Spirit; then the unity of God. Chapter titles: "That Jesus Christ is the Son of God and Truly Man" (61227); "That Christ Was Not Only Man, But God Also" (61313); "Against the Sabellians He Proves that the Father is One, the Son Another" (62893); "the Holy Spirit, Whose Operations He Enumerates from Scripture" (63255). Documented.
- One idea in his own words (ch. 11): "We must not then lean to one side and evade the other side, because any one who should exclude one portion of the truth will never hold the perfect truth. For Scripture as much announces Christ as also God, as it announces God Himself as man" (ANF 61355–61359). Documented. Not chosen as a quote (see Outline); may be paraphrased.
- His standing before the split: Harnack: that Novatian had already stood out before the schism as preacher, writer, teacher and defender of the gospel follows from Cornelius's letter, Cyprian's letters and the treatise against him (HARN 22531–22535). Editor's reading of Documented sneers (E5).
- Its standing: McGiffert: "Novatian was not at all heretical in doctrine. His work upon the Trinity is both able and orthodox" (EUS-ed 38244–38245). Coxe: "a most valuable contribution to ante-Nicene theology" (ANF-ed 60318–60319). Editors' views.
- **Date: the editors differ (D1).**

**E3. Cornelius's charge: his conduct in the persecution.** "In the time of persecution, through cowardice and love of life, he denied that he was a presbyter. For when he was requested and entreated by the deacons to come out of the chamber in which he had imprisoned himself and give aid to the brethren ... he paid so little respect to the entreaties of the deacons that he went away and departed in anger. For he said that he no longer desired to be a presbyter, as he was an admirer of another philosophy" (EUS 38709–38717). Contemporary, hostile. McGiffert: "This is certainly a calumny. It is possible, as Neander suggests, that Novatian ... withdrew somewhat from active duty and lived the life of an ascetic" (EUS-ed 38717–38720). Contested (D2).

**E4. 250: his letter for the Roman clergy (Ep. 30).** Own words, by Cyprian's attribution. Cyprian: "It was added also—Novatian then writing, and reciting with his own voice what he had written, and the presbyter Moyses, then still a confessor, but now a martyr, subscribing—that peace ought to be granted to the lapsed who were sick and at the point of departure" (ANF Ep. 51.5, 33498–33502). Latin: "additum est etiam Nouatiano tunc scribente et quod scripserat sua uoce recitante et presbytero Moyse tunc adhuc confessore nunc iam martyre subscribente, ut lapsis infirmis et in exitu constitutis pax daretur" (EP Ep. 55.5, 7570–7575). Contemporary (Cyprian, 251–252), on the other side by then. Authorship Widely Accepted (Wallis, ANF-ed 60411–60414; McGiffert, EUS-ed 38257–38259; Harnack HARN 22538–22540 groups it with his books by style).
- The letter: "Let them indeed knock at the doors, but assuredly let them not break them down; let them present themselves at the threshold of the church, but certainly let them not leap over it; let them watch at the gates of the heavenly camp, but let them be armed with modesty, by which they perceive that they have been deserters" (ANF 31759–31763). Latin: "pulsent sane fores, sed non utique confringant. adeant ad limen ecclesiae, sed non utique transiliant. castrorum caelestium excubent portis, sed armati modestia qua intellegant se desertores fuisse" (EP Ep. 30.6, 4222–4225). Documented.
- Wait for a bishop and a council: "having, since the departure of Fabian of most noble memory, had no bishop appointed as yet ... what you also have yourself declared ... is satisfactory to us, that the peace of the Church must first be maintained; then, that an assembly for counsel being gathered together, with bishops, presbyters, deacons, and confessors, as well as with the laity who stand fast, we should deal with the case of the lapsed" (ANF 31709–31722). Documented.
- The dying: those "as impending death does not suffer to bear the delay, having repented and professed a detestation of their deeds with frequency; if with tears, if with groans ... to them, finally, such cautious and careful help should be ministered, God Himself knowing what He will do with such" (ANF 31820–31827). Documented.
- Its force: "This is not to cure, but, if we wish to speak the truth, to slay" (of too-hasty pardon, ANF 31671–31672). Documented.
- His view hardened later: McGiffert: "He seems originally to have held less rigid notions in regard to the treatment of the lapsed, but before the end of the persecution he became very decided in his opposition" (EUS-ed 38226–38229). Editor's reading; the change itself is Documented by comparing Ep. 30 with his later rule (E8).

**E5. 251: the rival election.** Cyprian on Cornelius: made bishop "by very many of our colleagues who were then present in the city of Rome" (ANF 33573–33574), and later "by sixteen co-bishops" (ANF 33981–33984; EP 8256: "a sedecim coepiscopis"). Cyprian on Novatian: he "strives by bribery to be made an adulterous and extraneous bishop by the hands of deserters" (ANF 33984–33985). Contemporary, hostile.
- Cornelius: "this most illustrious man, who bound himself with terrible oaths in nowise to seek the bishopric, suddenly appears a bishop as if thrown among us by some machine" (EUS 38461–38512). "This dogmatist, this defender of the doctrine of the Church" (EUS 38514–38515; Greek EUSG 6839–6840: "ὁ δογματιστής, ὁ τῆς ἐκκλησιαστικῆς ἐπιστήμης ὑπερασπιστής"). That this mocks *On the Trinity* is Closs's reading, reported by McGiffert (EUS-ed 38515–38517): editor's.
- Cornelius: he sent two companions "to a small and insignificant corner of Italy, that there by some counterfeit argument he might deceive three bishops, who were rustic and very simple men"; "by the tenth hour, when they had become drunk and sick, he compelled them by force to confer on him the episcopate" (EUS 38518–38533). One of the three "came back to the church, lamenting and confessing his transgression" (38536–38538). Hostile. McGiffert accepts the three Italian bishops but calls the drunkenness "baseless calumny" (EUS-ed 38493–38501). Contested.
- The confessors: "using as a cloak for his rebellion those confessors who had adhered to him from the beginning" (Cornelius, EUS 38384–38386). Maximus, Urbanus and Sidonius later returned to Cornelius (ANF Ep. 45, 32969–32984). Documented.
- His own claim, reported by Dionysius: "If, as thou sayest, thou hast been led on unwillingly" (EUS 38923–38924). McGiffert: "There is no reason to doubt ... Novatian's sincerity in declaring that he did not seek the office of bishop" (EUS-ed 38463–38464); "Novatian may well have been urged against his will" (38913–38914). Contested (D3).

**E6. 251: envoys to Carthage, refused (a clause).** "There have come to us, beloved brother, sent by Novatian, Maximus the presbyter, and Augendus the deacon ... that Novatian had been made bishop; disturbed by the wickedness of an unlawful ordination ... we considered at once that they must be restrained from communion with us" (ANF Ep. 40, 32590–32597). Documented. Told in `cyprian-of-carthage`; a clause here.

**E7. 251: Dionysius's letter.** "Dionysius to his brother Novatus, greeting. If, as thou sayest, thou hast been led on unwillingly, thou wilt prove this if thou retirest willingly. For it were better to suffer everything, rather than divide the Church of God. Even martyrdom for the sake of preventing division would not be less glorious than for refusing to worship idols. Nay, to me it seems greater. For in the one case a man suffers martyrdom for the sake of his own soul; in the other case in behalf of the entire Church" (EUS 38922–38929). Greek (EUSG 7362–7367, damaged scan): "Διονύσιος Νοουατιανῷ ἀδελφῷ χαίρειν. εἰ [ἄκων], ὡς φῇς, ἤχϑης, δείξεις ἀναχωρήσας [ἑκών]. ἔδει μὲν γὰρ καὶ πᾶν ὅτι οὖν παϑεῖν ὑπὲρ τοῦ μὴ διαχόψαι τὴν ἐκκλησίαν τοῦ ϑεοῦ, καὶ ἢν οὐχ ἀδοξοτέρα τῆς ἕνεχεν τοῦ μὴ εἰδωλολατρῆσαι γινομένης ἡ ἕνεχεν τοῦ μὴ σχίσαι μαρτυρία, κατ᾽ ἐμὲ δὲ καὶ μείζων" (bracketed words restored from OCR "axzov" and "Exam"). Rufinus's Latin, same volume (EUSG 7433–7436): "Si invitus, ut ais, in hoc venisti, ostendes in eo, si desinas volens. oportuerat quidem etiam pati omnia, ne scinderetur ecclesia dei, et erat non inferior gloria sustinere martyrium pro eo, ne scindatur ecclesia, quam est illa, ne idolis immoletur, immo secundum meam sententiam maius hoc puto esse martyrium." Contemporary, written to him. Documented.

**E8. His rule.** Socrates (IV.28, about 439, from Novatian's letters): Cornelius "received into communion believers who had sacrificed"; Novatian "wrote to all the churches that 'they should not admit to the sacred mysteries those who had sacrificed; but exhorting them to repentance, leave the pardoning of their offense to God, who has the power to forgive all sin'" (SOC 14069–14090). Later, sympathetic. Documented as Socrates's report. **This quote is allocated to `councils-of-rome-and-carthage-251`**; here the rule is told in Acesius's words (I1) or paraphrase.
- Eusebius's hostile summary: Novatus, "being lifted up with arrogance against these persons, as if there was no longer for them a hope of salvation, not even if they should do all things pertaining to a genuine and pure conversion" (EUS 38265–38268). Later (about 325), drawing on Cornelius. "As if there was no longer ... hope of salvation" is Eusebius's charge; Socrates and Acesius say pardon was left to God (D4).
- Cyprian's answer: "to say to our brethren, 'Mourn and shed tears, and groan day and night ... but, after all these things, you shall die without the pale of the Church. Whatsoever things are necessary to peace, you shall do, but none of that peace which you seek shall you receive!'" (ANF Ep. 51.28, 34105–34110). And "he who is not in the Church of Christ is not a Christian" (ANF 33976–33977; EP 8254–8255: "christianus non est qui in Christi ecclesia non est"). Contemporary, hostile. Documented. The second line is in the card's `remembered`.
- Cornelius's charge of an oath at the Eucharist: "when he has made the offerings, and distributed a part to each man, as he gives it he compels the wretched man to swear in place of the blessing ... 'Swear to me by the body and blood of our Lord Jesus Christ that you will never forsake me and turn to Cornelius.' ... instead of saying Amen, as he takes the bread, he says, I will never return to Cornelius" (EUS 38761–38774; Greek EUSG 7134–7145). Hostile, the gravest of Cornelius's charges ("the worst of all the man's offenses", 38758). Documented as Cornelius's charge; not otherwise confirmed.

**E9. 251: the council at Rome (a clause).** "A very large synod assembled at Rome, of bishops in number sixty, and a great many more presbyters and deacons ... A decree was confirmed by all, that Novatus and those who joined with him ... should be considered by the church as strangers; but that they should heal such of the brethren as had fallen into misfortune, and should minister to them with the medicines of repentance" (EUS 38272–38299; Greek EUSG 6606–6612). Later, from Cornelius's letters. Documented. **Its wording belongs to `councils-of-rome-and-carthage-251`**; here a clause in paraphrase.

**E10. The church spreads.** Cyprian: he "is sending his new apostles through very many cities, that he may establish some new foundations of his own appointment ... (this one) dares to create over these other and false bishops" (ANF Ep. 51.24, 33992–33998). Gaul: Marcianus of Arles "has associated himself with Novatian ... holding that most extreme depravity of heretical presumption, that the comforts and aids of divine love and paternal tenderness are closed to the servants of God who repent, and mourn, and knock at the gate of the Church with tears" (ANF Ep. 66, 37258–37264; EP Ep. 68.1, 12906–12912: "ad ecclesiam lacrimis et gemitu et dolore pulsantibus"). Antioch: Fabius, its bishop, "seemed to lean somewhat toward this schism" (EUS 38828–38829). Documented.
- Baptizing again: "the Novatians re-baptize those whom they entice from us" (Cyprian, ANF Ep. 72.2, 38453–38454). Dionysius, about 257: "with good reason do we feel hatred toward Novatian, who has sundered the Church ... and has introduced impious teaching concerning God, and has calumniated our most compassionate Lord Jesus Christ as unmerciful. And besides all this he rejects the holy baptism" (EUS 39714–39723). Contemporary, hostile. Documented as charges; "impious teaching concerning God" disputed (D5).

**E11. His books from the split.** *On the Jewish Meats* opens as a letter from absence: "the fear lest you should think that any disadvantage is suffered by you by reason of my absence; and this I strive to remedy, in labouring to show myself present with you by frequent letters" (ANF 63741–63745). Harnack: written by Novatian as a separated bishop, after spring 251, probably driven from Rome by a persecution (HARN 22503–22508). Coxe: "composed during the Decian persecution" (ANF-ed 60321–60322). Editors differ. For the card.

**E12. His death.** Socrates: "He himself indeed afterwards suffered martyrdom in the reign of Valerian, during the persecution which was then raised against the Christians" (SOC 14132–14144). Later (about 190 years), sympathetic, single witness. Valesius (SOC-ed 14134–14141): the Novatians' book *The Martyrdom of Novatian* "was full of false statements and fables", and in it "the founder of the sect was not represented as suffering martyrdom, but simply as being a 'confessor.'" Wallis/Coxe: "his authority, amid the silence of all others, is not sufficient to guarantee the fact" (ANF-ed 60394–60397). Harnack: when he died "weiß man nicht sicher"; the martyr acts current in the sixth century were forged (HARN 22550–22554). Contested.

## 3. Impact (heaviest first)

**I1. His church outlasted him; Nicaea, 325.** Socrates I.10: Constantine "summoned to the council Acesius also, a bishop of the sect of Novatians. Now, when the declaration of faith had been written out and subscribed by the Synod, the emperor asked Acesius whether he would also agree to this creed to the settlement of the day on which Easter should be observed. He replied, 'The Synod has determined nothing new, my prince: for thus heretofore, even from the beginning, from the times of the apostles, I traditionally received the definition of the faith, and the time of the celebration of Easter.' When, therefore, the emperor further asked him, 'For what reason then do you separate yourself from communion with the rest of the Church?' he related what had taken place during the persecution under Decius; and referred to the rigidness of that austere canon which declares, that it is not right persons who after baptism have committed a sin, which the sacred Scriptures denominate 'a sin unto death' to be considered worthy of participation in the sacraments: that they should indeed be exhorted to repentance, but were not to expect remission from the priest, but from God, who is able and has authority to forgive sins. When Acesius had thus spoken, the emperor said to him, 'Place a ladder, Acesius, and climb alone into heaven'" (SOC 3687–3721). Greek (SOCG 9912–9913): "θὲς ὦ Ἀκέσιε κλίμακα, καὶ μόνος ἀνάβηθι εἰς τὸν οὐρανόν" (OCR "Ἀκέσιεΐ"; Hussey's apparatus "Ἀκέσινε C"); Valesius's Latin facing: "Pone scalam, Acesi, et in coelum solus ascende" (SOCG 9934–9935). Source: "Neither Eusebius Pamphilus nor any other has ever mentioned these things: but I heard them from a man by no means prone to falsehood, who was very old" (SOC 3725–3727); that man is Auxanon, "a very aged presbyter of the Novatian church; who when quite a youth accompanied Acesius to the Synod at Nicæa" (SOC 3855–3857). Later (about a century), one oral witness from Novatian's church, retold by a historian kind to them. Documented as Socrates's report.
- Sozomen (I.22) tells it after him: "O Acesius, take a ladder and ascend alone to heaven." Sozomen's reading: "By this speech I do not imagine the emperor intended to praise Acesius, but rather to blame him, because, being but a man, he fancied himself exempt from sin" (SOC 28087–28090). Sozomen (II.32): "Acesius, who was then the bishop of this heresy in Constantinople, was much esteemed by the emperor on account of his virtuous life" (SOC 30570–30572). Later, dependent on Socrates for the ladder. Socrates I.10 itself does not name Acesius's city; Sozomen does.
- Canon 8 of Nicaea: "Concerning those who call themselves Cathari, if they come over to the Catholic and Apostolic Church, the great and holy Synod decrees that they who are ordained shall continue as they are in the clergy. But it is before all things necessary that they should profess in writing that they will observe and follow the dogmas of the Catholic and Apostolic Church; in particular that they will communicate with persons who have been twice married, and with those who having lapsed in persecution have had a period [of penance] laid upon them" (NIC 3769–3777); a Cathar bishop where there is a catholic bishop is to rank as presbyter or country bishop, "that there may not be two bishops in the city" (3784–3791). Documented. Hefele: "it is generally believed that this canon was made so mild towards the Novatians out of respect for" Acesius (HEF 26318–26320). Editor's.
- Same faith: Sozomen: the Novatians "entertained the same opinions respecting the Divinity as the Catholic Church" (SOC 30565–30566). Socrates V.10: the bishop Nectarius consulted "Agelius bishop of the Novatians, inasmuch as he entertained the same sentiments as himself in matters of faith" (SOC 15290–15292). Documented.

**I2. The church at Rome and in the East, for two centuries.** Socrates VII.11: Celestine, bishop of Rome (from about 422), "took away the churches from the Novatians at Rome also, and obliged Rusticula their bishop to hold his meetings secretly in private houses. Until this time the Novatians had flourished exceedingly in Rome, possessing many churches there, which were attended by large congregations" (SOC 19289–19294). Socrates IV.28: the strict discipline "maintained in their church, even to the present day, in the provinces of Phrygia and Paphlagonia" (SOC 14059–14061; this line belongs to the council of 251). How long it lasted, the editors differ: McGiffert, "until the sixth century" (EUS-ed 38242–38244); Hefele, "until the fifth century" (HEF 26280–26281); Ambrose's editor, "becoming weaker in the fifth century, and gradually fading away" (AMB-ed 33379–33381). Say "for centuries".
- Opposition kept: Ambrose, *On Repentance*, about 384 (AMB 1183–1185): "they say that those should not be restored to communion who have fallen into denial of the faith" (AMB 33503–33505); and "after the fashion of the Stoics, they think that all sins are equal in gravity" (33511–33515). Later, hostile. For the card.

**I3. His book survived under other names.** Jerome (about 392): "a great volume On the Trinity, a sort of epitome of the work of Tertullian, which many mistakenly ascribe to Cyprian" (JER 40895–40897). Jerome against Rufinus (about 402): Rufinus said a book by Tertullian "On the Trinity" was read as Cyprian's; Jerome: "The book in question is not Tertullian's, nor does it pass under the name of Cyprian. It is by Novatian and is called by his name; the peculiarity of the style proves the authorship of the work" (JER 53840–53847). Harnack: "Unter Tertullians Namen haben sich De trinitate und De cibis Iudaicis erhalten" (HARN 22499–22500): both survived under Tertullian's name. Wallis: "formerly attributed by some to Tertullian, by others to Cyprian; but now on all hands allowed to be the work of Novatian, to whom Jerome expressly assigns it" (ANF-ed 60400–60403). Widely Accepted. Harnack: Jerome's "epitome of Tertullian" claim "bestätigt sich nicht" (is not borne out) (HARN 22546–22548) (D1).

**I4. Who keeps him today.** No held source says. Leave out.

## 5. Where sources disagree or come late

**D1. When *On the Trinity* was written.** Coxe: "said by Cave to have been written about a.d. 257" (ANF-ed 60319–60320). Wallis: "written after the heresy of Sabellius, which appeared 256 a.d." (ANF-ed 60404–60405). Harnack: it nowhere suggests it was written by a schismatic, but shows him "standing in the church"; how long before Decius's persecution it was written "weiß man nicht", but one must stay with the 240s (HARN 22536–22546). Closs, via McGiffert, hears it mocked in Cornelius's letter of 251 (EUS-ed 38515–38517), which would put it before 251. Credit each; the story says only that its date is uncertain, or places it before the split by Harnack's reckoning, credited.

**D2. His conduct in the persecution.** Cornelius: cowardice, shut in his room (E3). McGiffert: calumny; Neander: an ascetic withdrawal (E3). Credit each.

**D3. Whether he sought the office.** Cornelius: long ambition, false oaths (E5). Dionysius reports his claim of being "led on unwillingly" and doubts it (E7; EUS-ed 38918–38919). McGiffert: no reason to doubt Novatian (E5). Credit each.

**D4. What his rule held.** Eusebius: as if there were no hope of salvation (E8). Socrates and Acesius: the church does not restore them to communion, but they are urged to repent and their pardon is left to God (E8, I1). Cyprian: they are told to mourn and die outside the church (E8). Ambrose adds that they held all grave sins equal, not only denial (I2). Canon 8 adds second marriage (I1). Credit each; never "he said they could not be saved".

**D5. Was his teaching on God sound?** Dionysius, about 257: "impious teaching concerning God" (E10). McGiffert: "not at all heretical in doctrine" (EUS-ed 38244). Acesius at Nicaea accepted the creed (I1); Sozomen and Socrates say his church held the same faith (I1). McGiffert reads Dionysius's charge as general, from the rejection of the church's baptism (EUS-ed 39749–39756). Credit Dionysius if told.

**D6. His death.** Socrates alone; disputed (E12).

**D7. Acesius's see.** Socrates I.10: "a bishop of the sect of Novatians". Sozomen II.32: in Constantinople (I1). Percival and Hefele follow Sozomen (NIC-ed 3825–3826; HEF 26284–26285).

**D8. The meaning of the ladder.** Socrates gives no reading. Sozomen: blame, not praise (I1). Hefele: Constantine esteemed him for "the austerity of his life" (HEF 26285–26286, after Sozomen). Credit Sozomen if a reading is told.

**D9. How long the church lasted.** Fifth or sixth century, by different editors (I2).

**D10. Was Socrates a Novatian?** Zenos: Baronius and others held so; others put it down to sympathy or indifference to fine points; "a combination of the last two motives comes nearest" (SOC-ed 941–947, 1066–1071). Told in `socrates-of-constantinople`; here one clause at most: "a historian kind to Novatian's church".

**D11. "Stoic".** Wallis: "It is also stated by Cyprian, that he was a Stoic ... but this also has been doubted" (ANF-ed 60375–60377). Cyprian's own line speaks of "the reasoning of philosophers and Stoics" without naming him (EP Ep. 55.16, 7932–7933). Editor's inference. Leave out.

**D12. "First" claims.** Coxe: Novatian "was the earliest contributor to the 'Latin Christianity'" in the Roman church (ANF-ed 60346–60348). Percival quotes Fleury: "the first Anti-Pope" (NIC-ed 3814–3816). Editors' rankings; leave out.

## 6. Wanted but not held

- The Latin of *On the Trinity* and *On the Jewish Meats*: not held. Any quote from them is rendered from Wallis's English.
- *The Martyrdom of Novatian* (the Novatians' own book, known only through Valesius and Eulogius via Photius): lost and not held.
- Eulogius of Alexandria, *Against the Novatians* (Photius, *Library*, codices 182, 208, 280): not held.
- Pacian of Barcelona's letters to the Novatian Sympronianus: not found by name in the Library.
- Novatian's letters to the churches after 251 (Socrates's source): lost.
