# Outline check: Novatian

## The checker's own heart (written before reading `Outline.md`)

**The essence.** A leading presbyter of the church at Rome, Novatian wrote *On the Trinity*, the first large Latin account of the faith written at Rome, setting Father, Son and Spirit against those who blurred Father and Son (Documented, the book; "first" left out as an editor's ranking). In 251 he was made a rival bishop to Cornelius by a stricter party, and he led a church that would not restore to communion those who had denied the faith, leaving their pardon to God (Documented). That church lasted for centuries, holding the same faith as the majority, and nearly all we know of him comes from those who fought him (Documented).

**Also told.** His sickbed baptism and ordination over objections (Cornelius, hostile; baptism Widely Accepted). Cornelius's charge that he hid in persecution (Contested). His letter for the Roman clergy in 250 (Ep. 30), which waited for a bishop and a council and allowed help to the dying: the gentler early view (Documented; authorship by Cyprian's word, Widely Accepted). The rival election and Cornelius's charges, each credited: the oaths, the three Italian bishops, the oath at communion (Contested / Documented as charges). His own claim that he was led on unwillingly, and Dionysius's letter urging him to step down (Documented). The sixty bishops' sentence as a clause (sibling's). The church spreading (Gaul, Antioch, new bishops). Acesius at Nicaea, accepting the creed and Constantine's "ladder" (Documented as Socrates's report from one old Novatian witness); canon 8's terms. The book surviving under Tertullian's and Cyprian's names until Jerome restored it to him. His death: martyrdom under Valerian, which only Socrates reports (Contested).

The outline's heart is compared with this account, and with Mark's approved heart, below.

## BLOCKING

**B1. The letter of 250 is told twice, and the opening holds back the name.** The opening tells the letter (Quote 1). The essence then goes ahead to 251, and beat 1 goes back before 250. Then beat 2 returns to "the rest of the letter". That puts one event in two places, which the kit counts as a failure to understand on one hearing. The outline says itself that "no beat tells it again", and then beat 2 does. The opening also ends "... Cyprian later said: Novatian", which is the held-back reveal the kit and `roles/writer.md` bar. And "read it aloud to them" adds "to them": Cyprian says only "reciting with his own voice what he had written" (ANF 33498–33502; EP 7570–7573).
*Correction.* Openings 1: "In about three sentences: in 250, with the persecution going on and Rome without a bishop, one of the city's elders, Novatian, wrote for its clergy to Carthage about the Christians who had given way (Cyprian names him as the writer, and says he read aloud what he had written, EP 7570–7573). They must wait for a bishop and a council, he wrote, though the dying who repented might be given careful help (ANF 31709–31722, 31820–31827). Quote 1. Then the essence." Beat 2: delete "The rest of the letter: ... and God will judge (E4; Documented)". Retitle it **In the persecution** (S1, E3), carrying only Cornelius's charge and the McGiffert/Neander answer. Keep the Do not say line on the dying.

**B2. Beat 1, "He came to the faith as a grown man", is unsupported.** The only witness is Cornelius, and he says only that Satan "became the occasion of his believing" and that he was baptized on a sickbed (EUS 38630–38643). McGiffert warns against the very inference: clinical baptism "does not imply, as is commonly supposed, that he was of heathen parentage ... We do not know whether his parents were heathen or Christians" (EUS-ed 38636–38640).
*Correction.* Delete that sentence. Beat 1 begins: "By his rival Cornelius's account, he was baptized on what seemed his deathbed ...". Add to Do not say: "Not 'he came to the faith as a grown man', 'he was born a pagan' or 'he converted late'. Cornelius gives only a sickbed baptism, and McGiffert says his parents' faith is unknown (EUS-ed 38636–38640)."

**B3. The ending states one source's reading where held sources differ (Mark, 2026-11-19).** The ending says that his own church's book "made him only a confessor". That is Valesius's report (SOC-ed 14134–14141). Two other held sources say his followers remembered him as a martyr: McGiffert, "according to the tradition of his followers, and also Socrates ... he suffered martyrdom under Valerian" (EUS-ed 38240–38242), and Harnack, "so auch die späteren Novatianer" (HARN 22551–22552). Harnack adds that when he died "weiß man nicht sicher". The ending would leave the listener with his own church denying him martyrdom, which is one editor's reading. The sheet's E12 also omits McGiffert's and Harnack's lines.
*Correction.* Ending: "How he died is not known for certain (HARN 22550–22551). Socrates, writing almost two centuries later, says he died a martyr under the emperor Valerian, and his church later remembered him so (EUS-ed 38240–38242; HARN 22551–22552). No other early writer confirms it (ANF-ed 60394–60397). One editor reports that the Novatians' own book of his martyrdom made him only a confessor (SOC-ed 14134–14141). Credit each, or drop the book clause." Add McGiffert's and Harnack's lines to the sheet's E12 and D6.

**B4. Quote 2: the outline names no held print of the Greek, yet it says the card need not print the English alone.** The only Greek held is the damaged Schwartz scan, and the researcher restored two words of it (ἄκων and ἑκών, from "axzov" and "Exam", EUSG 7362–7363). Rufinus's Latin (EUSG 7433–7436, the same noisy scan: "ecclesia del") and McGiffert's English confirm the sense of those words. They do not confirm the Greek reading. The checker read the Greek by hand: the restored text matches the standard reading, and the rendering is right. But by the kit's rule (Decision-Log, "The kit after batch 6", 2026-11-19, item 4), with no held print the card is planned to print the English alone. The batch's own `dionysius-of-alexandria` outline reaches that plan for this same scan. The sweep also missed a second held English print: Salmond, ANF6 (`anf06_gregory-thaumaturgus-dionysius-julius-africanus-methodius-arnobius.xml` 9907–9925, "If you were carried on against your will, as you say, you will show that such has been the case by your voluntary retirement").
*Correction.* Quotes 2: replace "and by McGiffert's English (EUS 38922–38928), so the card need not print the English alone" with "The sense of the two restored words is confirmed by Rufinus's Latin (EUSG 7433–7436) and by two held English prints, McGiffert (EUS 38922–38928) and Salmond (ANF6 9907–9925). No clean print of the Greek is held, so the card prints the English alone."

## MINOR

**M1. Quote 3, the Greek scan.** SOCG 9912–9913 also has noise at "ao φοὐρανόν" (a line number and a stray letter), not only at "Ἀκέσιεΐ". Hussey prints no comma after θὲς. Valesius's Latin and Zenos's English are translations, not prints of the Greek. The checker read it by hand ("θὲς ὦ Ἀκέσιε κλίμακα, καὶ μόνος ἀνάβηθι εἰς τὸν οὐρανόν"), and the rendering is right. *Correction:* note both noisy spots, drop the comma after θὲς, and plan the card to print the English alone, as in B4.

**M2. The gloss for the lapsed cites lines that do not hold "bought papers".** E4 and E8 say nothing of certificates. *Correction:* cite Cyprian, ANF 33694–33697 ("those who have received certificates, with those who have sacrificed"), or drop "or bought papers saying they had".

**M3. Words to explain: three are missing.** (a) "lay hands on him" (beat 3): add "the laying on of hands, the act that made him a bishop" (EUS 38529–38531, "imposition of hands"). (b) "ascetic" (beat 2): add "living apart in strict self-denial and prayer" (EUS-ed 38717–38720). (c) "penance" (What came 1, canon 8): add "a set time of repentance before being taken back" (NIC 3773–3774). None of these is glossed needlessly, and no common word is glossed.

**M4. "It survived because it was copied as Tertullian's or Cyprian's."** No held source gives "because". Jerome also contradicts himself: in 392 he says "many mistakenly ascribe [it] to Cyprian" (JER 40895–40897), and in 402 he says it does not "pass under the name of Cyprian" (JER 53844–53846). *Correction (Also told and What came 3):* "It survived under Tertullian's name (Harnack, HARN 22499–22500), and some took it for Cyprian's (Wallis, ANF-ed 60400–60403). Jerome named Novatian as its author."

**M5. The Connection gives the ladder a reading that no source holds.** "If no one who falls can be taken back, the pure must climb alone" is not in Socrates, and it is not Sozomen's reading. Sozomen took the words as blame of a man who "fancied himself exempt from sin" (SOC 28087–28090). *Correction:* "Third, the ladder: Constantine told Acesius to set up a ladder and climb to heaven alone. Socrates gives no reading. Sozomen, a later historian, took it as blame of a man who thought himself without sin (D8). The narrator adds no reading of their own."

**M6. Beat 5's Dionysius clause can be heard the wrong way.** As written ("hating him for splitting the church and calling Christ unmerciful"), it can sound as if Dionysius called Christ unmerciful. *Correction:* "for splitting the church and, he said, making out our merciful Lord to be unmerciful (EUS 39714–39720)".

But the beats and What came, as listed, run to about 700 words against the 525 set. The approved time is about 3:30, and the kit's warning past about four minutes applies. *Correction:* make the cut list the plan. Drop the sickbed baptism, Gaul and Antioch, Dionysius's later attack and What came 3 from the outline, or mark them "only if the count allows". Then recount against 525.
