# Outline check: Pontius the Deacon

## My own account of the heart (written before the comparison)

From the census entry (`knownFor`: "Deacon of Cyprian of Carthage, who shared his exile and wrote the Life and Passion of Cyprian"), the existing card, the source sheet and the job line as the hearts entry corrected it.

- **Essence.** A companion of Cyprian of Carthage, a deacon whom Jerome names as Pontius, went with his bishop into exile at Curubis in 257 by choice and was with him on his last night in 258. Soon after, he wrote *The Life and Passion of Cyprian*, so that Cyprian's example would reach later generations. It tells a bishop's whole life as a Christian, from baptism to death, where earlier African writing had told only martyrs' trials and deaths. It is the main narrative witness to Cyprian's years as bishop, beside his letters and the court record. Whether it is the oldest surviving Christian biography (Harnack) or a speech of praise (others) is Contested. The book is unsigned; the name and office are Jerome's (Widely Accepted).
- **Also told.** Why he wrote (his own words); what he chose to tell and leave out (begins at baptism; no councils, no Rome, no baptism quarrel; points to the court record; his version of the last withdrawal against Cyprian's own last letter); the dream at Curubis in Cyprian's words and Pontius's reading of it; his grief at being left behind. What came of it: kept with Cyprian's works by 359, named by Jerome, argued over by modern scholars. His own end is not known.

Note on order: `Outline.md` and `Hearts.md` were opened before this account was written down, so it is not fully blind. It was set from the census fields, the card and the source texts, and it agrees with the outline in substance.

## Findings

**BLOCKING 1. The Library sweep misses a held major witness, Monceaux.** The sheet lists "Monceaux (1902)" under "Not held" and cites him only through Harnack. He is held: `cic/texts/monceaux_histoire-litteraire-afrique-chretienne-tome2_1902.txt`, pp. 190–197, lines about 10970–11290 (legible). He is a direct, held voice on two points the outline treats as disputes:
- (a) What kind of book it is. He says the book is "non une véritable biographie, mais un panégyrique" (11030–11032).
- (b) Why the councils and the baptism quarrel are missing. He calls these "d'étranges lacunes" ("strange gaps") and gives his own reason (11092–11103): fixed on praise, Pontius wanted to paint "moins le politique, le chef d'Eglise, que le chrétien, le clerc accompli" ("less the politician, the church leader, than the Christian, the accomplished cleric"). He also notes "quelques allusions à l'affaire des lapsi".
- (c) He makes the exile-after-plague charge in his own words (11124–11131, "quatre ans au moins", "four years at least").

Correction:
- Sheet: remove Monceaux from "Not held" and add a MON entry.
- I3 and What came 3: credit the speech-of-praise view to Monceaux by role ("a French historian of African Christian writing, in 1902"), beside Harnack. Keep Corssen as Delehaye reports him.
- Beat 6: give the gap's reasons as "scholars differ: one found the silence strange, one thought such things did not belong in a book about God at work in one man, one thought Pontius wished to praise the Christian and the cleric, not the church leader". Credit each, or say "scholars differ" in one clause.
- Do not say (exile after plague): cite MON 11124–11131 for the claim, and note that Harnack answers it (HAR 2043–2046).

**BLOCKING 2. The gloss for "Life" says more than its source.** "A Life, as such books were called" implies the form already had a name and a class when Pontius wrote. No held source says so, and whether there was any such book before his is the story's own Contested point (I3). Correction: "*The Life and Passion of Cyprian*, as Jerome titles it (JL 57618–57620, 'vitae et passionis Cypriani'): the story of his life, and of his suffering and death as a martyr". This glosses "Life" and "passion" together, credits the title to Jerome, and keeps the passion gloss as written.
