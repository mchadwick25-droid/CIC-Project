- **What happened:** in a siege of the city's palace quarter he talked the city council into sending out the "useless", and under that decree got out almost all the starving, his church first, by night to the Roman camp. ## Findings

### B1. BLOCKING. The Latin book and the disagreement over it are missing

The approved heart (`Hearts.md`, under Anatolius) says: "Colman's claim from a Latin book under his name (genuine to McGiffert, forged to Harnack) and Wilfrid's from his own words". The outline says the same in Also told (line 11), What came 3 (line 59: "Credit each; the narrator does not settle it"), the Connection (line 83: "The two claims rest on two different texts") and Who tells it (line 104). The story leaves all of it out.

Without it, the listener hears "both sides claimed him" but has nothing to explain how Colman could. Wilfrid's quote and the king's ruling then read as the settled verdict on what Anatolius taught. That is the narrator carrying one side forward, and it drops a disagreement the outline credits.

The story runs about 3:54, so there was room. The likely cause is that "What came 3" sits on the outline's cut-first list (line 12).

**Change.** In paragraph 8, after "…from the fourteenth day of the moon to the twentieth", add: ", a rule found in a Latin book under his name". After the Wilfrid quote, before "The king ruled for Rome.", add:

"That book may not be his. An American scholar who translated Eusebius takes it as an early translation of his work; a German historian, as a forgery made in the sixth century or earlier."

That adds about 45 words, bringing the story to about 630, or 4:12.

**Do not say line to add to the outline:** "Do not leave out the Latin book under his name, or the disagreement over whether it is his (D4), to fit the length. It is in the approved heart and the Connection. 'Cut first: What came 3' covers only Bede's own doubt and the Elias clause."

### B2. BLOCKING. "The fourteenth day of the moon" is used without its plain sense

Words to explain (outline line 25) lists "Passover / the fourteenth of the moon", glossed as "the Passover, kept on the fourteenth day of the first spring month, counted by the moon, at full moon". Paragraph 7 glosses the Passover moon as "the full moon of the first spring month", but never says that this is the month's fourteenth day. So Colman's "from the fourteenth day of the moon to the twentieth" in paragraph 8 cannot be tied to anything the listener has heard. That is the one point where the Whitby claim needs its terms, so this is a breach of Do not say (line 109) that the listener needs.

**Change.** In paragraph 7, change "the full moon of the first spring month." to "the full moon on the fourteenth day of the first spring month."

**Do not say line to add:** "Not 'the fourteenth day of the moon' at Whitby unless the Passover gloss has already said that it falls on the fourteenth day of the first spring month, at full moon."

### M1. MINOR. Caesarea is left out of "Bishop on the road" (Also told, line 9)

"After the war, Anatolius was called to Antioch" skips his time at Caesarea in Palestine (E5). It is not in the approved heart, so this is not blocking. If room allows, open paragraph 6 with:

"After the war he left Alexandria. At Caesarea in Palestine, its bishop made him a bishop beside himself, meaning him to take over when he died."

Then continue: "Then he was called to Antioch, …". That adds about 30 words, bringing the story to about 660 with B1, or 4:24, which is under the limit.

### M2. MINOR. One part of the reckoning is missing

Also told (line 10) and beat 5 include the claim that he "said the Jews kept the rule even before Christ, and cited their writers for it" (E6). If room allows after B1 and M1, add it to paragraph 7 after "no small mistake". Otherwise send it to the card.

### M3. MINOR. "A cycle of nineteen years" is first heard without its plain sense

The term first comes in paragraph 2, and its gloss only in paragraph 7. In paragraph 2, change "to reckon Easter on a cycle of nineteen years" to "to reckon Easter on a table that repeats every nineteen years". Paragraph 7 keeps the full gloss.

### M4. MINOR. A long sentence, and "ruled"

"Anatolius ruled that this month must not begin before the spring equinox, the day in March when day and night are equal, and that to begin it earlier is no small mistake." is 32 words.

"Ruled" also suggests that he had authority over the date. The source gives his writing (E6: "we maintain"; outline line 53: "he wrote").

**Split:** "Anatolius wrote that this month must not begin before the spring equinox, the day in March when day and night are equal. To begin it earlier, he said, is no small mistake."
