# Outline check: Lactantius

## My heart, written before reading Outline.md

From the census entry (`lactantius`, fields other than `longDescription`), the source sheet and what he is known for.

- **The essence:** Lactantius, a teacher of Latin rhetoric from Africa, was called to Nicomedia under Diocletian and was there when the Great Persecution began in 303. He answered it with the *Divine Institutes*, the first full statement of the Christian faith in Latin, made to educated pagans in their own terms (their poets, philosophers and the Sibyls), and holding that religion must be defended by dying, not by killing, because it cannot be forced. He is on the tree as the writer with whom the Latin Apologists' line ends (CEN `relationsSummary`).
- **Also told:** why he wrote (the two writers who mocked; Tertullian and Cyprian judged); what the seven books argue; *On the Deaths of the Persecutors* (authorship Widely Accepted), the near-contemporary record of the persecution that keeps the edicts of Galerius and Milan, and reads every persecutor's end as God's judgment; his poverty (Jerome's Chronicle); late in life tutor to Crispus in Gaul (Jerome); how he was read: Jerome's praise of the style and fault of the teaching, Augustine's use of his Sibyl texts, Calvin. The address to Constantine is Contested.

## Quotes: my renderings, then the outline's

- **Q1** (V.4.1). Mine: "So when these men I spoke of had set out their sacrilegious writings in my presence, and to my grief, I was spurred by their proud impiety, by my knowledge of the truth itself and, as I think, by God. I took up this task: to refute the accusers of justice with all the strength of my mind." The outline's rendering is faithful. "Godless" for *sacrilegas* and "answer" for *refutarem* are slightly weaker than the Latin, but within the rendering bar. Brandt's apparatus records that Cellarius and Bünemann suspected a word lost after *deo* (for example *duce*, "led by God"; DI-L3 32163–32166). The sense, "by God", stands either way. This is for the card, not for the story.
- **Q2.** Mine: "For religion is to be defended not by killing but by dying, not by savagery but by patience, not by crime but by faith." The outline's rendering is faithful. The citation is wrong: see M1.
- **Q3.** Mine: "Lactantius taught Crispus Latin letters: the most eloquent man of all in his time, but so poor in this life that he very often lacked even the necessities." The outline's rendering is faithful. See B1 and M2.

## BLOCKING

**B1. The ending tells the Crispus event a second time.** Beat 7 tells that Lactantius taught Crispus in Gaul. The chosen ending then returns to it with all of Q3, whose first sentence ("Lactantius schooled one of them, Crispus, in Latin learning") tells the same event again, after What came of him. The kit counts an event told twice as a failure to understand on one hearing.
*Correction:* in **Quotes**, Q3, replace the rendering with: "He was the most eloquent man of his time, but so poor in this life that he often lacked even the necessities." Replace its last two sentences ("'Them' is the new Caesars … sets this up just before. Job: the ending, on the person.") with: "Job: the ending, on the person; the teaching of Crispus is told once, in beat 7, and not again here." In **Ending** 1, replace "return to the old teacher in Gaul with Jerome's line, the most eloquent man of his day and too poor for necessities" with "close on Jerome's verdict on the man, the most eloquent of his day and too poor for necessities, without telling the teaching of Crispus again".

## MINOR

**M1. Q2's citation.** The passage is Brandt V.19.22 (DI-L chapter div n=19 at 26020, section 22 at 26240; the DI-L2 margin reads "22"). In ANF it is ch. XX (AN 5130 heading). It is not "V.20.9".
*Correction:* in Q2, replace "*Divine Institutes* V.20.9" with "*Divine Institutes* V.19.22 (Brandt; ANF ch. XX)". Make the same change in the sheet's Q-src 2.

**M2. Q3's "them", and Prosper.** The three new Caesars are Constantine's sons Crispus and Constantine and Licinius's young son (JC 44979–44982). They are not all "Constantine's sons". Prosper also drops *Latinis litteris* (TE 11250–11252), so he does not copy the line word for word. B1 removes the first point from the story.
*Correction:* in Q3's source line, replace "and, word for word, by Prosper" with "and by Prosper, who copies it without 'Latinis litteris'". In the sheet's Q-src 3, replace "Prosper copies it word for word" with "Prosper copies it, without 'Latinis litteris'".

**M3. "Almost certainly his."** Monceaux calls his authorship "plus vraisemblable", more likely, and reports scholars who deny it (MO 28622–28625). Schanz says "deren Echtheit allerdings bestritten ist" (SH 39060–39062). Neither held source says "almost certain".
*Correction:* in beat 6, replace "it is almost certainly his (Widely Accepted; say once)" with "most scholars now hold that he wrote it, though some have doubted it (Widely Accepted; say once)". In Who tells it, replace "almost certainly his" with "most scholars hold it his". In Do not say, replace "→ 'almost certainly his', said once" with "→ 'most scholars now hold it his', said once".

**M4. "Copied in full".** McGiffert says in one note that the edict of Milan is "given in full" in chapter 48 (EU-ed 48376). In another he says "the greater part of the edict (beginning with § 4)" (EU-ed 50239–50240). The editor differs with himself, and no held source says the edict of 311 is copied in full.
*Correction:* in Also told (the *Persecutors* item), replace "the edicts of 311 and 313 copied in full" with "the texts of the edicts of 311 and 313 copied into it". In beat 5, replace "Lactantius copied it into his book in full" with "Lactantius copied its text into his book". In Connection, replace "with the edicts copied in full" with "with the edicts' texts copied into it". Add to Do not say: "'copied the edicts in full' → 'copied their text into his book'".

**M5. Eusebius on the fire.** Eusebius says "I know not how" (EU 43982–43984). That is his own ignorance, not a claim that no one knew.
*Correction:* in beat 2, replace "Eusebius, a contemporary, says no one knew how it began" with "Eusebius, a contemporary, says he does not know how it began". In Who tells it, replace "the fire's cause unknown" with "he did not know the fire's cause".

**M6. Constantine's lightning is tied to the 303 fire by an editor.** The *Oration* (EU 70320–70326) says the palace and the emperor's chamber were "consumed by lightning", in a chapter on Diocletian's later dread. It does not name the fire that was blamed on the Christians. McGiffert makes the link (EU-ed 43992–43994). The outline may keep Constantine's view, but the writer should not make Constantine name the 303 fire.
*Correction:* in beat 2, replace "Constantine, who was in Nicomedia, later called it lightning" with "Constantine, who was in Nicomedia, later said the palace was destroyed by lightning". In the sheet's D3, add: "That the *Oration*'s lightning is the 303 fire is McGiffert's link (EU-ed 43992–43994)."

**M7. Augustine and the Sibyl verses.** Augustine says Lactantius used the Sibylline testimonies "introducing them bit by bit". It is Augustine who sets them "in one connected series" (AU 35552–35555). "Had gathered" gives Lactantius the gathering.
*Correction:* in What came 1, replace "Augustine took the Sibyl's verses Lactantius had gathered and set them out in the *City of God*" with "Augustine took the Sibyl's verses that Lactantius had quoted bit by bit through his argument, and set them out in one series in the *City of God*". In Do not say, replace "→ 'Augustine took over the Sibyl's verses Lactantius had gathered'" with "→ 'Augustine took the Sibyl's verses Lactantius had quoted and set them out together'". Make the same change in Links ("took over his Sibyl texts" → "took the Sibyl verses he had quoted").

**M8. A partial quote in beat 1.** His words are "not to virtue, but altogether to cunning wickedness" (AN 556). The outline's quoted "not to virtue, but to cunning" drops words inside quote marks and softens his own self-judgment.
*Correction:* in beat 1, replace "\"not to virtue, but to cunning\"" with "\"not to virtue, but altogether to cunning wickedness\"".

**M9. Words to explain.**
- "The Great Persecution": the gloss "the last and fiercest persecution of the Christians by Rome's emperors" has no source, and "fiercest" is a judgment. *Correction:* replace it with "the persecution of the Christians that Diocletian and Galerius began at Nicomedia in 303 (S5, E2), which Lactantius counts as lasting ten years and about four months to the church's restoration (AN 10690)".
- "Cicero" (beat 4, and What came 1's "Ciceronian eloquence") is not glossed. Many listeners hearing in a second language will not know the name at once. It is a passing word, so this is MINOR. *Correction:* add "**Cicero**: 'Rome's great orator' (the census `legacy` glosses Jerome's 'Tully' as Cicero)". In What came 1, say "eloquence like Cicero's" rather than "Ciceronian".
- Beat 4's "Cicero, Virgil and the Sibyls" cites no line for Virgil. *Correction:* add "(Virgil: AN 1015)".
- The other glosses are right for the time and place and say no more than their sources. Common words are not glossed.

**M10. Do not say: the wonder at the sacrifice.** Beat 2 cites E1. E1 includes "the immortal sign on their foreheads", with demons "chased away" (AN 10126). A writer could tell that as fact.
*Correction:* add to Do not say: "The Christians' sign that stopped Diocletian's sacrifice, if told at all → in Lactantius's voice ('Lactantius says'); never 'the demons fled' in the narrator's voice. Better left out (side story)." Add it to Leave out with the other side stories of *the Persecutors*.

**M11. Length against the beat load.** The count of quotes, glosses and credits is done first, as the kit asks. But the 320 words left must carry seven beats that hold the edict and the man burned, two fires with three witnesses, the drownings, the governor, the two mockers and the method, the book's ideas, the move from Bithynia and the *Workmanship*, the address, Galerius's edict, Donatus, Milan, the ten years, the children, and Crispus, plus What came of him. That is closer to 650 to 700 words, so the writer will either run long or cut at random. The length is not padded. The risk runs the other way.
*Correction:* under **Length**, add: "Standing cuts, in order, if it runs long: the governor's joy (beat 2); the *Workmanship*'s opening line (beat 4); 'ten years and about four months' (beat 5); the later list under a pope's name and Calvin (What came 1).
