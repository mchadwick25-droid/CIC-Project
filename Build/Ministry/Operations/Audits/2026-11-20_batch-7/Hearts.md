# Batch 7 hearts

The heart of each story, as its researcher set it in `Outline.md`, in plain words. Confidence uses the project's five levels. Shown to Mark on one page, 2026-11-20. Origen and Methodius are not here: they are reviewed, not researched.

## Carthage

**Tertullian** (Teacher, about 3:40). Around the year 200, Tertullian of Carthage, a convert who knew Roman law well (Eusebius's word, Documented as his; that he practised as an advocate, Contested), was the first great writer of the faith in Latin whose books survive (Widely Accepted). He defended the Christians before Rome's governors, claimed the Scriptures for the churches the apostles founded, and gave Latin its words for God as one substance in three persons (the books Documented; "gave Latin its words" Widely Accepted). Then he turned to the strict New Prophecy.
Also told: the Apology (197), the Prescription (about 200), Against Praxeas (after 213, written after his turn), On Modesty (dates, Dominant Modern Reconstruction); why he turned, where Jerome (envy and insults from Rome's clergy) and Augustine differ; who read him after: Cyprian, Novatian, Lactantius, Augustine.

**Cyprian of Carthage** (Leader, about 3:40). A teacher of public speaking at Carthage (Widely Accepted), he became its bishop while still a new Christian (Documented, Pontius), about two years after his baptism by modern reckoning (Dominant Modern Reconstruction). From 248 to 258 he led the church of Roman Africa through persecution, plague and division, and some eighty letters show how it was run (Documented). He taught that the church is one mother, and no one has God as Father without her. He died by the sword at Carthage in 258, the first of its bishops to die a martyr (Documented; "first" is Pontius's claim).
Also told: his hiding under Decius and the Roman clergy's "hireling" letter (that it was aimed at him, Contested); the plague sermon (Documented); the quarrel with Stephen of Rome over baptism, Stephen's words surviving only as Cyprian quotes them; his last hiding, so that he could die at Carthage (his own last letter; Pontius says he refused to leave); the trial and the death, where the trial record and Pontius differ on who bound his eyes.

**Pontius the Deacon** (Teacher, about 3:00). Cyprian's deacon went with him into exile in 257 by choice, and was with him on his last night in 258 (Documented, the book's own claims; his name and office are Jerome's, Widely Accepted). Soon after, about 259 (Dominant Modern Reconstruction), he wrote the Life and Passion of Cyprian so that Cyprian's example would reach later generations (Documented, his words). Whether it is the oldest surviving Christian biography or a speech of praise is Contested. The book is unsigned.
Also told: why he wrote (his own words); what he kept and left out: no life before baptism, nothing of the councils, Rome or the baptism quarrel (Documented); the dream at Curubis, in Cyprian's words as Pontius reports them; his grief at being left behind (Documented).

**Novatian** (Teacher, about 3:30). A leading elder of the church at Rome, he wrote On the Trinity, setting out what the church believed of Father, Son and Spirit (the book Documented). In 251 a stricter party made him rival bishop against Cornelius, and he led a church that would not take back to communion those who had denied the faith, leaving their pardon to God (Documented). That church lasted for centuries, and almost all we know of him comes from the men who fought him (Documented).
Also told: what the book argues (its date, Contested); his own letter of 250; Cornelius's charges, each credited to Cornelius, including the oath at communion; Dionysius's letter urging him to step down; Acesius at Nicaea and Constantine's "ladder"; his martyrdom, which only Socrates reports.

**Councils of Carthage and Rome, 251** (Council, about 4:15). When Decius's persecution ended, many who had given way asked to come back (Documented). In 251 the bishops of Africa at Carthage under Cyprian, then sixty bishops at Rome under Cornelius, chose a middle way: no one denied all hope of return, no one straight back, long penance and each case examined (Documented, with Cyprian the only witness for Carthage). A stricter party took none back and set up Novatian; his church split off and lasted for centuries (Documented).
Also told: the confessors' pardons and a party pushing for return at once (Cyprian's charge); Cyprian's wait for a council; Carthage's terms, including refusing those who repented only when dying; Rome's "strangers" and "medicines of repentance"; peace for all penitents the next year, as a new persecution seemed near (Documented; the year is Hefele's).

## Origen's line

**Eusebius of Caesarea** (Teacher, about 3:50). Early in the fourth century, Eusebius, bishop of Caesarea in Roman Palestine, set out to write the first history of the whole church, and built it from the church's own letters, lists of bishops and old books, copying their words into his pages (Documented, his words; "first", Widely Accepted). Much of what the early Christians wrote survives only because he copied it (Widely Accepted, his editor).
Also told: in 303 he saw churches thrown down and Scriptures burned, and chose not to record those who gave way (Documented); the Life of Constantine (Documented); at Nicaea he signed "not without inquiry" (Documented, his letter; Socrates and Athanasius tell it differently); at Tyre in 335 Potamo asked how he had left prison unharmed, and Egypt's bishops wrote that he was "accused of sacrificing" (Documented as charges; unproven).

**Pamphilus of Caesarea** (Martyr, about 3:30). A presbyter of the church at Caesarea, he gathered a library of sacred books for his church (Documented, Eusebius and Jerome), and Jerome says he copied most of Origen's books by hand; Jerome held twenty-five of those volumes about eighty years later (Documented as Jerome's report). Arrested in the Great Persecution, he wrote a Defense of Origen in prison with his younger friend Eusebius, and was put to death about 310 (Documented, Eusebius; February 309 or 310, Contested).
Also told: he gave his inheritance to the poor and gave books away; the Defense argued that Origen searched rather than decreed; his torture and death with eleven others; who wrote the Defense, told as a scene of four voices (Eusebius: jointly; Jerome first: Pamphilus, later: Eusebius; Rufinus: "is said"; Photius: five books and one).

**Dionysius of Alexandria** (Leader, about 4:00). A pupil of Origen, he ran the church's school at Alexandria and then led its church as bishop from about 247 to about 264 or 265, through two persecutions and a plague (Documented; the death year, Eusebius and the Chronicle differ). His letters, copied by Eusebius, are the eyewitness record of those years, in his own words (Documented).
Also told: wedding guests carried him off under Decius, as he tells it answering a bishop who said he ran away (his own account); the plague letter (his words and claims); his judgment that the Revelation was by a holy John but not the apostle, though he kept the book (Documented); the quarrel over the Son, which the Arians later claimed and Athanasius answered (Documented through Athanasius; whether the Arians had grounds, Contested).

## Alexandria to the Great Persecution, and the Latin witness

**Peter of Alexandria** (Martyr, about 3:15). Bishop of Alexandria, leading the churches of all Egypt, through the Great Persecution from 303 (Documented). In 306 he set rules for taking back those who had given way, measuring each case by what the person had suffered and chosen, and in 311 he was beheaded (Documented). In those years a bishop of his, Melitius, broke away; the rival church outlived Peter and came before Nicaea (Documented that it happened; why it began, Contested).
Also told: the graded rules (his own words); the break from both held sides, Athanasius and Socrates (deposed for sacrifice) against Epiphanius (a prison quarrel over the fallen) (Contested); Eusebius on the death (Documented); the rules made church law in 692 (Documented).

**Anatolius of Alexandria and Laodicea** (Leader, about 3:20). A learned Christian of Alexandria, he saved most of the starving people trapped in a besieged quarter of the city: he talked its council into letting the "useless" go, and under that cover got out almost everyone, his own church first (Documented as Eusebius's report, "they say"). Passing through Laodicea on his way to a synod, he was kept there as bishop, and he wrote an Easter reckoning on a nineteen-year cycle, the first Christian known to use one (Documented; "first known", Dominant Modern Reconstruction). At Whitby, almost four centuries later, both sides claimed him (Documented, Bede).
Also told: his learning; the disguise and the night exit (Documented); Colman's claim from a Latin book under his name (genuine to McGiffert, forged to Harnack) and Wilfrid's from his own words.

**Lactantius** (Teacher, about 3:30). A teacher of Latin speech from Africa, he was summoned to Nicomedia, Diocletian's capital, and was there when the Great Persecution began in 303 (Documented, his words and Jerome's). He answered it in the Divine Institutes, seven books that argue the faith to educated pagans from their own poets and philosophers, and hold that religion cannot be forced (Documented). When it was over, On the Deaths of the Persecutors told how the persecuting emperors died (content Documented; his authorship Widely Accepted). Late in life, Jerome says, he taught Constantine's son (Documented as Jerome's account).
Also told: why and how he wrote; what the Institutes argue; what the Persecutors holds, including the persecutors' children killed, told as his verdict; the palace fire, where Lactantius, Eusebius and Constantine each give a different cause.

## Job lines the sources corrected
- Pontius: not "the first Christian Life of a bishop" as settled; whether it is the oldest surviving Christian biography is Contested.
- Cyprian: "two years after baptism" is the editors' reckoning, not a source's.
- Tertullian: no held source says he was refused as a saint; see question 1.
- Peter: the census spells "Melitius"; the job said "Meletius".
