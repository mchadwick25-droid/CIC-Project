## 1. The test, from one hearing

- **Who:** a third-century bishop of Alexandria, a pupil of Origen and once head of its church school, whose own letters are the eyewitness record of his years.
- **What happened:** he was seized under Decius and carried off by wedding guests. He judged that the Revelation was by a holy John but not the apostle. He used blunt images against teachers who made the Father and the Son one, and answered in four books. He praised the Christians who died nursing the plague-sick, and he died old, still bishop.
- **Why the connection matters:** a century later the followers of Arius quoted his images for their side. Athanasius, bishop of the same city, answered from his four books, and later readers still argue over whether the Arians had any ground.

All three lines come clear from one hearing.

**My own, before reading the approved one** (from the census entry and what he is known for): Dionysius, Origen's pupil, ran Alexandria's church school and then led its church as bishop from about 247 to about 264 or 265, through the persecutions of Decius and Valerian and a plague. His own letters, kept by Eusebius, are the eyewitness record of those years. Also told: his rescue by wedding guests, which he told to answer a charge of flight; his judgment on who wrote the Revelation; the plague letter; and the quarrel over the Son, which the Arians later claimed and Athanasius answered.

(+4 words). It gives "Then I understood why they had come" its referent before the quote. Source: beat 2, "the wedding guests carried him off"; E3, "they directed me to rise and come away quickly… dragged me away".

As it stands, "wrote against both errors" followed by "Dionysius answered" lets a listener hear that Rome wrote against him. The outline's Do not say ("Rome condemned him" → "…wrote against both errors and told him what had been said against him; he answered in four books") carries both halves. Source: ATH 29881–29882.

**MINOR 3 (Athanasius's claim in the narrator's voice).** Paragraph 8. Replace "He died with honor inside it," with "He died, Athanasius wrote, with honor inside it," (+2 words). The colon frames only the first sentence, so the second, a partisan's verdict, falls to the narrator. The kit says the narrator never carries any side's view forward, and the outline says "paraphrase, credited to Athanasius".

**MINOR 4 ("still": past the Library).** Paragraph 7. Replace "later readers still disagree." with "later readers disagree." (−1 word). This is the outline's wording (What came 1; D3).
