## B7-02
(Lines 8135-8141 begin the speech; the speech runs on to line 8186, and I render it to its end.)

"Anatolius was a very holy, learned and admirable man," said Wilfrid. "But what has he to do with you, when you do not keep his rules? He followed the rule of truth in setting his Easter, and fixed a cycle of nineteen years. Either you do not know it, or you know it and despise it, though the whole Church of Christ keeps it. He reckoned the fourteenth moon of our Lord's Easter so that, by Egyptian custom, he counted it as the fifteenth moon from the evening on. In the same way he assigned the twentieth to Easter Sunday, taking it to be the twenty-first moon once the sun had set. It is clear you do not understand this rule and distinction of his, since you sometimes keep Easter before the full moon, on the thirteenth day.

"As for your Father Columba and his followers, whose holiness you say you imitate, and whose rules you keep, and who have been confirmed by signs from heaven: I could answer that on the day of judgment many will say to the Lord that they prophesied in his name, cast out devils and worked many wonders, and the Lord will reply that he never knew them. But far be it from me to say that of your fathers. It is much more just to believe good than evil of people one does not know. So I do not deny that they were God's servants and loved by him, since in plain simplicity but with devout intent they loved him themselves. Nor do I think their way of keeping Easter did them much harm, so long as nobody came to show them a more perfect rule. And I believe that if a Catholic adviser had come among them, they would have followed his advice as readily as they are known to have kept the commandments of God that they had learned and knew.

"But you and your companions certainly sin if, having heard the decrees of the Apostolic See and of the universal Church, confirmed by holy scripture, you refuse to follow them. Even if your fathers were holy, do you think that their small number, in a corner of the farthest island, should be preferred to the universal Church of Christ throughout the world? And if that Columba of yours (and ours too, if he was Christ's servant) was a holy man, powerful in miracles, could he be set above the most blessed prince of the apostles, to whom our Lord said, 'You are Peter, and on this rock I will build my church, and the gates of hell will not prevail against it, and to you I will give the keys of the kingdom of heaven'?"
