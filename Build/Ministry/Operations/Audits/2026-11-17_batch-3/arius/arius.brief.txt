"We are persecuted because we said: the Son has a beginning, but God has no beginning." So wrote Arius, a priest of Alexandria in Egypt, in the early three-hundreds.

In his letters, God alone was without beginning. The Son, begotten by the Father before time as perfect God, was not eternal beside him. The question split the church: was the Son God in the same full sense as the Father, or a being the Father made before all else? In 325 the council of Nicaea answered: the Son is "begotten, not made", "of one substance with the Father". Yet the teaching lived on, under a name its own bishops refused, as the faith of emperors for a time and of the Gothic peoples for two centuries.

His reason: two beings without beginning would make two first principles, and a Son who was part of the Father would make the bodiless God divisible. Around 318 he clashed with his bishop, Alexander, whose council deposed him and his allies as "enemies of Christ". His words survive only because his opponents copied them, Athanasius to mock his verse.

Nicaea's creed closed by cursing anyone who said "before he was begotten he was not", a phrase from Arius's own letter. The emperor Constantine exiled him and ordered his books burned, with death for anyone who hid one.

Constantine recalled him after he gave a creed without the disputed phrases. In the new capital, Constantinople, the bishop refused to admit him, praying that God would take either Arius or himself. Arius died suddenly the day before his reception, in 336 by the usual dating. Athanasius, who was not there, saw God's judgment in it.

Opponents called the bishops who had accepted him Arians. At Antioch in 341 they replied: "We have not been followers of Arius. For how could we, who are bishops, follow a priest?" Athanasius answered that they disowned a hated name.

In 360, under the emperor Constantius, who favoured them, a council dropped Nicaea's word "substance", since Scripture never uses it, and called the Son "like the Father". Jerome, an opponent, wrote that the world "groaned, and was astonished to find itself Arian". Ulfila, bishop of the Goths, accepted that creed, and under the emperor Valens gave the Goths letters and the Scriptures in their tongue. His creed calls the Father "alone unbegotten", in Arius's words, and the Son our Lord and God, yet "subject and obedient in all things" to the Father.

In 589 the Gothic bishops of Spain entered the catholic church at Toledo. They cursed anyone who still held "the faith and communion that came from Arius", held, they said, until then. Churches that had refused his name gave up their faith under it.
