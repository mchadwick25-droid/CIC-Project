# Outline check: Arius

**Verdict: FIX.** Four blocking findings, all on the witness line. The quotes are verbatim, the sources are mostly read correctly, and the shape and length fit. The outline carries two sides' framing forward in places, and it softens Athanasius's telling of the death.

## BLOCKING

**B1. "The creed answered him in his own words" says more than the texts hold.** The claim appears in Thread, What came of them 1 and Connection. Two of the creed's curses match his surviving letter to Eusebius. "Before he was begotten he was not" matches "πρὶν γεννηθῇ ... οὐκ ἦν" (epiphanius 45729-45736), and "made of things that were not" matches "Ἐξ οὐκ ὄντων ἐστίν" (45738). The curse on "there was a time when the Son of God was not" (ἦν ποτε ὅτε οὐκ ἦν) is not in either surviving letter. It comes from the Thalia as Athanasius quotes it (PG26 1233, npnf204 41484-41487), from Alexander's charges, and from the council's own letter (npnf202 3186-3189). The curses on "a creature" and "subject to change" condemn wording that both his letters reject in those terms. One calls the Son "unchangeable" (ἀναλλοίωτος, epiphanius 45729; npnf203 6043). The other says "perfect creature of God, but not as one of the creatures ... unalterable and unchangeable" (npnf204 57105-57107). Only his opponents give him the Son as "alterable": Athanasius (npnf204 41452, 57000-57001) and the council's letter ("capable either of vice or virtue", npnf202 3189-3190).
Corrections:
- Thread: "He forced the church to say in its creed whether the Son of God had a beginning, and the creed's closing curses took up his circle's own phrases to reject them."
- What came of them 1: "Two of the curses repeat his letter's phrases, 'before he was begotten he was not' and 'of things that were not'. A third, 'there was a time when he was not', is the slogan his opponents and the council's letter give him, close to the Thalia's 'once he was not'."
- Add to Do not say: "Every curse in the creed was a phrase of Arius's" or "Arius taught that the Son could change" → "his own letters call the Son unchangeable; Athanasius and the council's letter say he taught the Son could change."

**B2. The Connection gives the Homoian bishops' self-defence as the narrator's verdict.** The lines are "So the movement ... did not inherit his teaching from him as its founder. It was named after him by the other side." That takes one side's framing as settled. The same Antioch letter goes on, "we admitted him rather than followed him" (npnf204 57420-57422). Athanasius introduces it as written because "they were in general and lasting odium for their heresy" (57413-57415). The heading "His name, refused by his own side" also states as fact whose side they were. The outline also leaves out what Aquileia did to the two bishops: the council condemned and deposed them (Mansi 3, 60853, 61007; Kauffmann 1955-1960).
Corrections:
- Replace those two Connection sentences with: "'Arian' was his opponents' name for them. The bishops it named said they had examined his faith and received him, but did not follow him as bishops follow a priest. Athanasius answered that they said so because the name was hated. Both claims stay in their owners' voices."
- Retitle item 2 "His name, refused by those it was pinned on". Add to I4: "and the council deposed them both."
- Add to Do not say: "the Homoians had nothing to do with Arius" → "they said they received him but did not follow him."

**B3. The death beat softens Athanasius.** Beat 6 and the Do not say entry call his account "plainer" and give the death as "withdrew to relieve himself and died there". Athanasius tells it in the words Acts uses for the death of Judas: "falling headlong he burst asunder in the midst" (npnf204 70100-70106, note Acts i.18). He repeats the charge elsewhere: "Arius perjured himself and burst asunder" (npnf204 39556). The beat also drops the coercion on both sides. Alexander of Constantinople refused, saying "the inventor of the heresy ought not to be admitted". Eusebius and his fellows answered with a threat: "to-morrow, though it be contrary to your desire, Arius shall have communion" (70081-70085).
Corrections:
- In beat 6, add: "The bishop refused to admit him; Eusebius's party threatened to bring him in anyway. Athanasius tells the death in the words the Bible uses for the death of Judas, who betrayed Jesus, and calls it a sign that God hated the heresy (70061-70064)."
- In Do not say, replace "Athanasius's plainer account" with "Athanasius's account, which uses the Judas words; give it as his, and add no gore beyond it."

**B4. The Do not say list has no guard for Arius's own framing.** Opening 1 and Q1 invite a narrator to say "he was persecuted". In the letter that is Arius's own polemic. The same letter calls his opponents "heretics" and "unlearned men", and says they "threaten us with a thousand deaths" (npnf203 6026-6039).
Correction, added to Do not say: "Arius was persecuted" or "driven out as an atheist" in the narrator's voice → "he wrote that his bishop was persecuting him and had driven him out 'as atheists'; Alexander's letter calls Arius's party 'enemies of Christ'."

## MINOR

**M1. Q2 (Thalia).** My rendering from PG26 and npnf204 agrees with the outline's. Three fixes:
- The outline's Greek drops the opening word "Τούτων" (PG26 1194) without marking the cut. Add "Τούτων".
- "Their" has no antecedent for the listener. Tell the writer to set it up in paraphrase: "God's chosen, wise and taught by God" (npnf204 41461-41470).
- The PG26 lines are 1189-1194 and 1221-1223, not 1196-1199 and 1222-1225. Note that the Greek is not checked against the page image. The rendering rests on npnf204.

**M2. Socrates and the oath.** Sheet E18 and "Where sources disagree" say Socrates admits the oath is hearsay. That is the wrong way round. The hearsay is the paper-under-the-arm trick; the oath he says he confirmed "from ... the emperor's own letters" (npnf202 5513-5518). Correct both rows.

**M3. Setting.** "Palestine and Nicomedia in exile" mislabels them. He went there after Alexander expelled him, not into imperial exile. Palestine is at epiphanius 45523, and Epiphanius says he had "not yet come to Nicomedia" when he wrote (45648). Correct it to "after Alexander expelled him".

**M4. What came of them 4.** "Even his friend" misleads: Philostorgius was born about 364, nearly thirty years after Arius died (philostorgius 36). "To the skies" is Photius's phrase. Correct it to "Even the one historian on his side", with "praised him, in Photius's words, 'to the skies'".

**M5. What came of them 3 and Opening 2.** "After the burning order ... because" implies the order is why the rest is lost, which no held text says. Also, the copiers' stated reasons differ from "so readers could see his error". Theodoret copied the letter so "the names of those ... implicated" would be known (npnf203 5996-5997). Epiphanius copied it to show nothing was said against him "calumniose" (45654-45656). Athanasius quotes the Thalia to mock it. Correct accordingly.

**M6. Beat 4, the coercion at Nicaea.** Arius was not condemned alone. The council's letter names the bishops Theonas and Secundus as drawn into his condemnation (npnf202 3196-3198). Add one clause. Do not add Socrates's claim that Eusebius and Theognis were exiled with him (2962-2966): the editor says other witnesses put their exile later and judges Socrates wrong (1484-1487). Put that in Do not say, with "no count of bishops": Epiphanius's "CCCXVIII" (46220-46221) is a traditional number, not a roll.

**M7. Library sweep.** Eusebius of Caesarea's letter to his church is missing, and it was there at Nicaea. It says the curses ban the expressions "of things which do not exist" and "there was a time when he was not" (npnf202 3128-3142). Socrates adds that the council cursed "the very expressions of his tenets" (3262-3264). Both directly support I1 and should be cited there. Athanasius's other accounts of the death (Ep. Aeg. 18-19; npnf204 39556) should be listed as used or set aside. No other held primary text is missing on my spot-check of `homoian-arian-christianity.yaml`.

**M8. Beat 6.** The prayer asks God to take Arius, or take the bishop if Arius is admitted. "First" is not in it. Use "prayed that God would take either Arius or himself" (Robertson, npnf204 4870-4871).

**M9. Beat 2.** The letter to Alexander was sent by "the Presbyters and Deacons", not by Arius alone (npnf204 57094-57096). Correct it to "the letter he and his fellow clergy sent".

**M10. What came of them.** What Arius himself defended is not marked: that God alone is without beginning, and that the bodiless Father is not made compound or divisible (E8). Mark it there, as the template asks.
