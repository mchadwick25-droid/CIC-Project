## The test, from one hearing

- **Who:** Arius, a priest of Alexandria in Egypt in the early 300s. He taught that God alone has no beginning, and that the Son was begotten before time but is not eternal beside the Father.
- **What happened:** His bishop's council deposed him. Nicaea in 325 answered him in its creed and cursed his own phrase, and the emperor exiled him and ordered his books burned. He was taken back, then refused in Constantinople, and died suddenly in 336.
- **Why the connection matters:** A faith his opponents named after him lived on. The bishops of 341 refused his name. The creed of 360 called the Son "like the Father". Ulfila carried that faith to the Goths, using Arius's words for the Father. In 589 the Gothic bishops of Spain gave it up at Toledo as the faith "that came from Arius".

All three lines come clear from one hearing.

## The heart (written from the census entry and what he is known for)

Arius was a priest of Alexandria who taught that the Son of God has a beginning: God alone is without beginning, and the Son, made by the Father before time, is not eternal beside him. That teaching split the church. The Council of Nicaea in 325 condemned it and wrote its creed against it ("begotten, not made", "of one substance"). His name then became the label for a movement whose bishops refused it, and its faith ran through emperors' courts and the Gothic churches until Toledo in 589.

**First minute (the first 150 words):** these reach "…the faith of emperors for a time and of the Gothic peoples for two centuries." ## Findings

### MINOR

**M1. "Constantine recalled him after he gave a creed without the disputed phrases."** In Socrates (npnf202 lines 4825-4860), Constantine first summons Arius to court. Arius then gives the creed, and only after that is he restored. The brief reverses recall and creed. The outline says "took him back after". Change to: "Constantine took him back after he gave a creed without the disputed phrases." Or: "Constantine called him to court, and accepted him after he gave a creed without the disputed phrases."

**M2. The first-minute sentence drops the outline's "in later forms".** The outline asks for one sentence saying his teaching lived on "in later forms and under his opponents' name". The brief says "the teaching lived on, under a name its own bishops refused, as the faith of emperors … and of the Gothic peoples". Without "in later forms", the narrator comes close to saying that the Goths held Arius's own teaching, which the outline marks Contested. The rest of the story balances this, so it is not blocking. Change to: "Yet his teaching lived on, in later forms and under a name its own bishops refused, as the faith of emperors for a time and of the Gothic peoples for two centuries." That adds three words to a 450-word ceiling, so trim three elsewhere, for example "In the new capital, Constantinople," to "In Constantinople,".

**M3. Jerome's line is set against "In 360".** Jerome's "the whole world groaned" (npnf206 lines 32409-32413) is about Ariminum in 359, where "the term Usia was abolished". It is not about the council of 360. The outline's beat 6 covers both (359-360). Change "In 360, … a council dropped" to "In 359 and 360, … councils dropped", or let Jerome's sentence say "of those councils".

**M4. "His reason:" after the forward leap.** The listener is taken from the Goths back to Arius's argument with no time marker. Starting the sentence "His reason, in his letters, was that …" or "He gave a reason: …" would make the return audible. This is a listening note, not a factual fault.

No Do not say additions are needed: none of these is an error that the outline left room for.
