# Review: Arius

Reviewer of `arius.brief.txt` (2026-11-17). I did not write it, and I read no earlier draft, review or card text.

## The test, from one hearing

- **Who:** Arius, a priest of Alexandria in the early 300s, who taught that the Son of God had a beginning and was condemned for it.
- **What happened:** He clashed with his bishop around 318 and was removed. He set his teaching in verse, was condemned at Nicaea in 325 and exiled, and his books were ordered burned. He was later taken back by the emperor, but he died suddenly the day before he was to be received into communion in Constantinople.
- **Why the connection matters:** The Nicene creed's closing curses take up his circle's own phrases as what the church denies. His opponents then pinned his name on everyone who rejected Nicaea, and the bishops it named refused it.

**One-hearing test alone: PASS.** All three lines come clear from one hearing.

Where I got lost, briefly:
- "The Son, he wrote, existed before time, as perfect God, unchanging, yet before he was begotten, he was not." The chain of commas is hard to hold by ear, and "two first principles without beginning" is the most abstract phrase in the piece.
- In the last paragraph, "Athanasius ... told what followed in the Bible's words for the death of Judas" comes before the death itself. For a moment the ear expects the Judas words to follow, and they never come.

Nothing reads as a children's book or talks down.

## Scores

- **Voice: 4.** An adult documentary narrator throughout, and Arius's own voice is given room in Q1 and the Thalia. "Nearly every word of his that survives was copied by men who wanted him condemned" sets the witness problem plainly and early.
- **Craft: 4.** It opens well on his own complaint. Time and place arrive in the first two paragraphs, and the connection gets two full paragraphs, carried through what the council and the bishops did. The death scene is compressed: we hear the prayer without hearing what it answered.
- **Rigor: 3.** Every sentence traces to the outline, and all three quotes and every fragment are verbatim. But the Constantinople beat drops the refusal and the threat that the outline check (B3) required, so the force on both sides at the death is softened (B1 below).
- **Accessibility: 4.** Grade 8.1, reading ease 72.2 (names out), 16.0 words a sentence, longest sentence 28 words: pass. Plain words. The one dense sentence is noted above.

## Source check

Every sentence traces to `Outline.md`. The quotes were re-checked in `cic/texts/`:

- Q2 matches npnf204 41472-41479 and PG26 1194, 1221-1225. The set-up "wise and taught by God" is the outline's.
- Q3 matches Hussey 16656-16658 and npnf204 57418-57419.
- The fragments are verbatim too:
  - "as atheists" (npnf203 6026)
  - "enemies of Christ" (npnf204 19018; the encyclical was issued by Alexander in assembly with his clergy, so "Alexander's council ... calling" holds)
  - "before he was begotten he was not" and "of things that were not" (npnf214 2419-2421)
- The burning order with its death penalty matches npnf202 3378-3385.

Must-tell items:
- **The force** ("Never leave out the force"): present. The deposition, the exile, the burning order with its death penalty and Athanasius's open contempt ("his fiercest opponent", "copied them to mock them", the Judas framing, "saw God's judgment in it") are all told and credited.
- **The coercion at Constantinople** (Outline_Check B3, carried into beat 6): missing. See B1.
- **The Connection:** the creed's curses taking up his circle's phrases are present. "Arian" as the opponents' name, Antioch 341 with Athanasius's answer, and Aquileia with the deposition are also present. The Antioch bishops' "admitted him rather than followed him" is missing (M1).
- **Who tells it:** the listener knows, at the Thalia and at the death, that the teller wanted him condemned. Present.
- **What he defended:** present in part. "God alone has no beginning" is told. The other half, that the bodiless Father is not made compound or divisible, is not told, which is acceptable.
- **The Do not say list:** honoured throughout. "Persecuted" and "as atheists" stay in Arius's voice. No bishop count. No motive. No gore beyond Athanasius. Athanasius was not there. "Either Arius or himself." No narrator verdict on the death.

**Length:** 464 words against the outline's "about 2.5 to 3 minutes (about 400 words)". At a documentary pace of about 150 words a minute this runs about 3.1 minutes, at or just past the outline's top end (M3).

## Verdict: REVISE

## Findings

**B1 (BLOCKING). The coercion at Constantinople is softened to "pressed".** Beat 6, added by Outline_Check B3 because an earlier draft "drops the coercion on both sides", holds two things:
- the bishop's refusal: "the inventor of the heresy ought not to be admitted"
- the threat from Eusebius's party: "to-morrow, though it be contrary to your desire, Arius shall have communion with us" (npnf204 70081-70085)

The brief's "his allies ... pressed to restore him to communion" carries neither. As a result, the bishop's prayer arrives without the conflict it answers. That leaves out the record's force, which the Standard forbids.

*Change:* replace "pressed to restore him to communion" with a paraphrase credited to Athanasius. For example: "The bishop there refused to admit him, and Eusebius's party, as Athanasius tells it, said he would have communion the next day whether the bishop wished it or not." Paraphrase only; the quote count is already three. Offset the words elsewhere (see M3).

*Do not say line for the outline:* "His allies pressed for (or asked for) his reception" with no refusal or threat → "the bishop refused to admit him; Eusebius's party threatened to bring him to communion the next day against the bishop's wishes (Athanasius's account)."

**M1 (MINOR). Antioch 341: "received him" is missing.** The Connection, and the Do not say entry against "The Homoians had nothing to do with Arius", both give the Antioch bishops' own fuller claim: they examined his faith and admitted him, but did not follow him. Athanasius's answer keeps the brief from saying they had nothing to do with him. Even so, the Aquileia line that follows ("did not know who Arius was") leans the ear that way.

*Change:* after Q3, add a clause such as "They had examined his faith, they said, and received him, but did not follow him."

**M2 (MINOR). The Judas line.** "Told what followed in the Bible's words for the death of Judas" announces words the listener never hears, and it comes before the death it describes. Either add "who betrayed Jesus", as the outline gives it, or move the Athanasius sentence after the death sentence so that it reads as his telling of what we have just heard. Keep it credited to him and add no gore.

**M3 (MINOR). Length.** At 464 words the piece runs at or just past the outline's three-minute ceiling, and B1 adds words. Trim to stay near 400 to 430. Two places could give way without losing a beat: the second clause of the Socrates sentence, and the dense "existed before time, as perfect God, unchanging" sentence, which could also be split for the ear.

**M4 (MINOR). Not condemned alone at Nicaea.** Beat 4 and Outline_Check M6 note that the bishops Theonas and Secundus were condemned with him. One clause would carry it, if the length allows. This is not required.
