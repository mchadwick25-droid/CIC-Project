# Source sheet: Arius

All paths are under `cic/texts/` unless stated. Line numbers are the file's own. "Own words" means Arius's own text, even where an opponent preserved it. Confidence uses the five levels.

The witness line for this piece: almost everything about Arius was kept by his opponents. His own words survive only inside their books (Theodoret, Epiphanius, Athanasius, Socrates). His one historian on his side, Philostorgius, survives only in a hostile summary by Photius (800s). Each row names the witness and its side.

OCR note: the Oehler Epiphanius, Hussey Socrates, Migne PG 26, Migne PL 10 and Mansi files are scanned OCR. Their Greek and Latin are quoted here with only mechanical OCR slips corrected (ϑ for θ, lost breathings and accents, misread letters such as "eeclesiae", "trisli", "serpentes inslar", "el" for "et", "ſ" read as "f" in Mansi), and with line-break hyphens joined. No word is changed. The raw text is at the lines given. PG 26's own header calls it a second witness to be checked against the page image; the English of npnf204 is the first witness for the Thalia.

## Where sources disagree or come late

- How it began: Socrates (E1), Constantine (E2), Philostorgius (E3). The editor calls the origin obscure (S4).
- Motive: jealousy (Theodoret, a century later) against Arius giving way to Alexander (Philostorgius via Photius). Robertson rejects such tales (E4).
- The famous slogan "there was a time when the Son was not": Socrates puts it in his mouth a century later (E1); Athanasius gives "once He was not" from the Thalia (E10); the council's letter quotes it (E13). His own letter says "before He was begotten ... He was not" (E7). The exact words are his circle's, the setting of the first utterance is not secure.
- The Thalia: Athanasius's quotations mix verbatim lines and paraphrase; only the opening is plainly set out as verse (E9). Philostorgius's "songs" may be other works (E12).
- The death: Athanasius second-hand from Macarius (E17); Socrates adds lurid detail; he calls the paper-under-the-arm trick hearsay but says he confirmed the oath itself from the emperor's own letters (E18); Sozomen records three readings (E19). Epiphanius puts the death before Nicaea (epiphanius 46094-46218, before the council at 46220), which conflicts with every other witness.
- Epiphanius's portrait and numbers (E5) are hostile and about fifty years later.
- Year of death 336 and start 318/319 are Robertson's dating.
