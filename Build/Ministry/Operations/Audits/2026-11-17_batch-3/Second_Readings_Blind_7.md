## S4-01

- **Source and lines:** `cic/texts/npnf206_jerome-principal-works.xml`, lines 32396-32415 (English, printed).
- **Lines read (the relevant part):** "At that moment the term <i>Usia</i> was abolished: the Nicene Faith stood condemned by acclamation. The whole world groaned, and was astonished to find itself Arian."
- **Phrase:** "The whole world groaned, and was astonished to find itself Arian."
- **Rendering:** **The whole world groaned. It was amazed to discover that it had become Arian.**
- **Note:** The lines read do not name Ariminum. The council is only "the Council" here ("After these proceedings the Council was dissolved"), so the identification rests on the request, not on these lines. The endnote marker 4119 (on Valens) breaks the paragraph earlier but does not touch the phrase. "Find itself Arian" means the world discovered it now stood on the Arian side, not that it chose to be. "Astonished" is kept as "amazed"; "shocked" would add a stronger tone than the text gives.

## S4-02

- **Source and lines:** `cic/texts/justin-martyr_first-apology-grc_rauschen1911.txt`, lines 1230-1234 (Greek).
- **Lines read (the relevant part):** "Τὸν Χριστὸν πρωτότοκον τοῦ θεοῦ εἶναι ἐδιδάχθημεν καὶ προεμηνύσαμεν λόγον ὄντα, οἷ πᾶν γένος ἀνθρώπων μετέσχε. Καὶ οἱ μετὰ λόγου βιώσαντες Χριστιανοί εἰσι, κἂν ἄθεοι ἐνομίσθησαν, οἷον ἐν Ἕλλησι μὲν Σωκράτης καὶ Ἡράκλειτος καὶ οἱ ὅμοιοι αὐτοῖς, ἐν βαρβάροις δὲ Αβραὰμ καὶ Ἀνανίας καὶ Ἀζαρίας καὶ Μισαὴλ καὶ"
- **Phrase:** From "Τὸν Χριστὸν πρωτότοκον" through "κἂν ἄθεοι ἐνομίσθησαν."
- **Rendering:** **We have been taught that Christ is the firstborn of God. We have already explained that he is the Word, in whom every race of people has shared. And those who lived with the Word are Christians, even though they were thought to be godless.**
- **Note:** Three things are delicate. (1) "οἷ" is almost certainly an OCR error for "οὗ" ("of which / in whom"); the rendering reads "οὗ". The same line also prints "Αβραὰμ" with no breathing mark, another sign of OCR damage. (2) The edition prints "λόγον" and "λόγου" in lower case. Justin's argument links the divine Word with human reason, so "lived with the Word" carries both senses; "lived by reason" alone would lose the link to Christ. (3) "προεμηνύσαμεν" points back to Justin's own earlier argument ("we have already explained"), not to prophecy. The sentence continues past the phrase with examples (Socrates, Heraclitus, Abraham, Ananias, Azarias, Mishael); those are not rendered here. "Godless" keeps "ἄθεοι"; "atheists" would mislead, since the charge was refusing the gods, not denying any god.

## S4-03

- **Source and lines:** `cic/texts/anan-isho_paradise-v2-sayings_budge1907.txt`, lines 164-166 (English, printed, Budge's translation of the Syriac).
- **Lines read (the relevant part):** "On one occasion the Fathers in Scete were gathered together, and because certain folk were wishing to see Abbâ Moses, they treated him with contumely, saying, “Why doth this Ethiopian come and go in our midst?” But Moses hearing this held his peace."
- **Phrase:** "Why doth this Ethiopian come and go in our midst?"
- **Rendering:** **Why does this Ethiopian come and go among us?**
- **Note:** "Contumely" means open insult or scorn, so the question is meant as an insult, not as a real question. "Come and go in our midst" suggests he moves freely among them as if he belonged; "among us" keeps that without adding "as if he belonged". The surrounding text frames the insult as a test ("because certain folk were wishing to see Abbâ Moses"); the rendering leaves that framing to the narrative. No brackets in this phrase.

## S4-04

- **Source and lines:** `cic/texts/anan-isho_paradise-v2-sayings_budge1907.txt`, lines 1026-1030 (English, printed).
- **Lines read (the relevant part):** "Now when he went into the sacrarium they rebuked him and drove him out, saying, “Get outside, O Ethiopian”; and having gone forth he began to say to himself, “They have treated thee rightly, O thou whose skin is dark and black; thou shalt not go back as if thou wert a [white] man.”"
- **Phrase:** "They have treated thee rightly, O thou whose skin is dark and black; thou shalt not go back as if thou wert a [white] man."
- **Rendering:** **They have treated you rightly, you whose skin is dark and black. You will not go back as if you were a white man.**
- **Note:** "[white]" is in square brackets in the printed text: it is Budge's own addition to the Syriac, not a word he read in the Syriac. Without the bracket, the line reads "as if you were a man". The added "white" picks up the bishop's earlier words ("thou art wholly white", about his white garment), so the bracket is an interpretation, not a neutral fill. Any use should mark it as the translator's. "Go back" most likely means going back into the sacrarium; the rendering keeps "go back" and adds nothing to it; "thou shalt not" is a statement of what will happen, not a command, so "you will not".
