## Quotes: my own renderings first, then compared

1. SRM2 29492–29493, "vestra me oratione iuvari peto, quae sum minus docta, plus devota." Mine: "I ask to be helped by your prayer, I who am less learned, more devoted." This matches the outline. **Is it her own wording?** Krusch says the prefatory letter is "almost entirely" made from Fortunatus's words in his Lives of Marcellus, Hilary and Radegund (SRM2 28220–28221). I checked all three prologues (AA4 42995–43045, 46449–46520; SRM2 28517–28560) and the whole of AA4 for "devot". The phrase is not there. Krusch's side-by-side ends at "formidatur" (AA4 42140–42158), before this sentence. The nearest thing is an idea, not wording: Fortunatus's prologue to the Life of Albinus, "eligo rusticus agnosci per oboedientiam magis quam indevotus effici per doctrinam" (AA4 44945). So the quote is hers as far as the held texts reach. Safe to use.
2. SRM2 30245, "Quod fecit illa in orientali patria, hoc fecit beata Radegundis in Gallia." Mine: "What she did in the eastern land, blessed Radegund did in Gaul." Matches. A search of the whole corpus finds this sentence only in SRM2. Hers.
3. SRM2 30522, "clausi sunt eius oculi, et obscurati sunt nostri." Mine: "Her eyes were closed, and ours were darkened." Matches. The sentence is in ch. 21. Krusch's note on copying from the Life of Caesarius covers ch. 19–20 (SRM2 30493–30495), not ch. 21. My search of SRM3 found none of ch. 21's wording. The phrase echoes Lamentations 5:16–17, which is scriptural colour, not a borrowed passage. Hers.

## BLOCKING

**B1. Beat 5 turns a passage copied from the Life of Caesarius into her memory of daily life.** "Reading never stopped in Radegund's presence" comes from SRM2 29891: "lectrix ante eam una monacharum legere non desistebat". That is the Life of Caesarius I.45 with the sex changed: "lector aut notarius ante eum legere non desistebat" (SRM3 37520–37521). The whole frame around it is copied too. Its run of "quis … quis" questions (SRM2 29887–29890 = SRM3 37518–37520) and the "numquam de ore illius detractio" that follows (SRM2 29894–29897 = SRM3 37524–37527) are both Caesarius. Sheet 4.7 records this borrowing, but the beat gives the clause as her own report. A writer would tell it as something the nuns saw.
- *Correction:* in beat 5, delete "Reading never stopped in Radegund's presence; once," and begin "Once, meaning to call the portress …". The "alleluia" story (it names the portress, Rodegund) and "I chose you, daughters" are not in the Life of Caesarius or anywhere else in the corpus. Both stay. Add to **Do not say**: "Reading never stopped before her" / "she never spoke ill of anyone" as her memories → copied from the Life of Caesarius I.45 (SRM3 37518–37527). Leave them out.

## MINOR

**M1. Connection field: "calls him 'that apostolic man'" reads as personal reverence.** In her book, "vir apostolicus" is the usual title for any bishop. She gives it to Pientius (SRM2 29745), Germanus (29806), Gregory (30537), Eufronius (30309) and Leifastus (30652).
- *Correction:* replace "she quotes him with respect and calls him 'that apostolic man'" with "she names him with the title she gives every bishop, 'the apostolic man, Bishop Fortunatus', and quotes his own closing line". Add to **Do not say**: "she revered him as 'that apostolic man'" → it is her standard title for bishops.

**M2. Hildebert (What came of her 2, Connection, Who tells it).** This item rests only on Krusch's quotation (SRM2 28227–28233). I confirmed that no held file carries Hildebert's Life of Radegund. Three wordings say more than the quotation does:
- (a) "Fortunatus and the nun" is printed as a quotation, but the Latin names her: "Fortunati … simul et Baudoniviae sanctimonialis scripta". *Correction:* write "the writings 'of Fortunatus and of Baudonivia the nun'".
- (b) "for his authority" drops half of the reason. *Correction:* "he chose to follow Fortunatus, whose authority came 'no less from his life than from his rank as bishop'".
- (c) "added miracles only the nun had recorded" drops a qualifier. *Correction:* "added some miracles that Fortunatus never touched and the nun had recorded, 'not all'" (28232). Also change the confidence to "*Documented as quoted by Krusch; Hildebert's text not held*". Add to **Do not say**: any year for Hildebert (the OCR "1197", SRM2 28228, cannot be trusted; say "in the Middle Ages"); and "Hildebert praised her" or "preferred her" → he followed the bishop and took some of her miracles. In the Connection, change "ranked below the bishop's" to "used, but followed second to the bishop's".

**M3. Beat 6: her account of the bishop is missing.** She says that "the bishop of the place, with all the people," wished to receive the Cross devoutly (SRM2 30257–30258). She then blames the enemy's agents. Without this, a writer may suggest that she accused the bishop. *Correction:* after "it was kept out of the city", add "she says the bishop and people wished to receive it, but". Also delete "at Constantinople", which is not in her text (she writes "the emperor", 30250).

**M4. Beat 2: a date is marked Documented.** "After 600" is Krusch's reconstruction (SRM2 28186–28197). *Correction:* drop "After 600" from the beat, or tag it *Dominant Modern Reconstruction*.

**M5. Opening 1 calls the borrowed phrase "her own words"** (AA4 43014–43015). *Correction:* "Her prologue to her abbess, in a phrase she took from Fortunatus: …".

**M6. The Thread says "in its own plain Latin", but she took whole passages from Fortunatus, the Life of Caesarius and Gregory** (SRM2 28219–28223; she uses Gregory's "rose and lily" face, SRM2 30570–30572, cf. GregLat 61576–61577). *Correction:* "in plain Latin she herself called 'rustic'".

**M7. Beat 1: "later a bishop" is not on the sheet.** *Correction:* "a poet and friend of the house, a bishop by the time she wrote (SRM2 29493)".

**M8. Shape and length.** Teacher or thinker is a fair partial fit, and the outline says so. But beats 4–7 are all events from Radegund's life. Beat 4 alone holds three episodes, and beat 3 names five witnesses. Add a third of the piece for impact, and 300 words cannot hold all of it without turning into a summary of Radegund's life. That is the trap this subject's shape warns against. *Correction:* in beat 4, keep the shrine burning and the peace letters and mark the king's pursuit "optional, if time". In beat 3, keep Leo and one other witness. Add to **Shape**: "Tell each Radegund event as her choice ('she put in what he left out: …'), never as the queen's biography." In beat 4, change "a woman who wrote the same letters" to "Radegund writing the same letters".
