## The heart of her life (checker's account)

Baudonivia is known only from one book: the second book of the Life of Radegund, written soon after 600 (Krusch's dating) by a nun of Radegund's own house, the Holy Cross at Poitiers, at the command of the abbess Dedimia and the whole community (SRM2 29433–29441). Her stated purpose was not to repeat Fortunatus, the bishop-poet who wrote Book I, but to tell what he had passed over (SRM2 29493–29496): Radegund's life inside the monastery, her words to her "daughters", her letters and the house's vigils for peace between warring kings, her winning of a piece of the Cross from the emperor (set beside Helena's finding of it), the struggle to bring it into Poitiers, her death and burial, and the wonders after.

The essence of the work: a community's own memory of its founder, told by one of its members, in "we", with named living witnesses, written for "the ears of her flock" (SRM2 29451–29452, 29900, 30522). It is a humble, borrowed-and-adapted piece of Latin ("less learned, more devoted"; much of the prologue from Fortunatus, passages from the Life of Caesarius), but its own material — the shrine burning, the peace letters, the Cross and Helena, the deathbed and the weeping from the wall — is hers. She defended the Cross as Radegund's gift to the city and Radegund as peacemaker, and she kept silent about who blocked the relic, where Gregory names Bishop Maroveus.

What came of it, heaviest first: the book travelled as Book II after Fortunatus's in most manuscripts, so later readers met Radegund through the bishop's book and the nun's together; Hildebert of Le Mans used it (some of "the nun's" miracles) while following Fortunatus for his episcopal authority; later scholars cited her as the contemporary witness for the Poitiers church.

A writer must not invent her life: no family, age, entry, office or death is held, and "raised from the cradle" is a borrowed formula.

## Findings

**1. BLOCKING — Hildebert's reason is cut in half (heart, "Why it matters that a woman of the house wrote it").** The outline says Hildebert "followed Fortunatus for his 'rank as bishop'". Hildebert's words (SRM2 28229–28231): "Fortunatum sequi disposui, cui non minus ex vita, quam ex dignitate pontificis plurimum auctoritatis accedit": I chose to follow Fortunatus, who gains great authority "no less from his life than from his rank as bishop". Dropping "no less from his life" makes rank his whole reason, and, set right after the woman-writer paragraph, it invites a writer to tell a bishop preferring a bishop over a nun. The source gives two reasons, life and rank. Correction: replace the sentence with "A later bishop, Hildebert, weighed the two books and followed Fortunatus, whose authority, he says, came 'no less from his life than from his rank as bishop'; he added some of the nun's miracles, not all (SRM2 28227–28233)." Add to **Do not say**: "Hildebert followed Fortunatus because he was a bishop" / "because she was a woman" → he gives Fortunatus's life and his rank as bishop; no reason touching her sex is given.

**2. MINOR — the community did not fast (heart, The Cross; beat 4).** The outline says "the whole community fasted and wept every day" / "the community fasting and weeping". SRM2 30304–30306: Radegund put herself to fasts, vigils and tears; "tota congregatio sua in luctu et fletu omnibus diebus": the whole community was in mourning and weeping every day. Correction: "Radegund fasted, kept vigil and wept, and the whole community mourned and wept every day" (row 5.23 has it right).

**3. MINOR — "to be heard by Radegund's 'flock'" is a borrowed formula (heart; row 5.20).** "dum sui gregis auribus" (SRM2 29451) is taken word for word from Fortunatus's prologue to the Life of Hilary, "quatinus dum sui gregis auribus vox ... et vita pastoris antiquissimi resonaret" (AA4 43012), the same sentence the outline already rules out for "raised from the cradle". The audience claim itself holds (she addresses the abbess and the whole community, SRM2 29433–29437), and she has re-aimed the phrase at Radegund's flock. Correction: cite the dedication (SRM2 29433–29437) for the audience, and add to **Do not say**: "her own phrase, 'for the ears of the flock'" → the phrase is Fortunatus's, from his Hilary prologue, re-aimed at Radegund's community; say only that she wrote for her sisters.

**4. MINOR — "saw her from outside it" is the outline's own contrast (heart).** No source sets Fortunatus as an outsider; he was Radegund's familiar ("familiaris scilicet ipsius", SRM2 28174; "Fortunato familiariter usa est", AA4 41897). Correction: "where Fortunatus wrote as her close friend, not as one of the nuns", with those two citations.

**5. MINOR — Fortunatus does mark her death (Do not say).** His Book I ends with the tribune Domolenus's vision "at that hour" she died (SRM2 29376–29382); the census text's "nothing of her death" goes further than this. The outline's own wording ("no death scene") is right. Add to **Do not say**: "Fortunatus said nothing of her death" → he tells of a vision at the hour she died; what he lacks is her death and burial as the house saw them.

**6. MINOR — Link to Gregory of Tours is mislabelled.** She does not quote him; she points readers to his book of miracles for what he saw before the burial (SRM2 30537–30539), and Krusch notes she used Glory of the Confessors 104 (SRM2 30565). Correction: "Gregory of Tours — used his book and sent readers to it (Glory of the Confessors 104); in her story he buries Radegund."
