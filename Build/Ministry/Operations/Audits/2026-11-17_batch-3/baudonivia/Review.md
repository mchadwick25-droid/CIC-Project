## The test, from one hearing

- **Who:** Baudonivia, a nun of the Holy Cross convent at Poitiers in Frankish Gaul, known only from the book she wrote a little after 600 about her founder, the former queen Radegund.
- **What happened:** Her abbess and community ordered her to write. In what she called rustic Latin, she told what the poet Fortunatus's Life had passed over: the burned shrine, the peace letters to rival kings, the relic of the Cross kept out of the city, and the death seen from inside the house.
- **Why the connection matters:** Her book was copied after Fortunatus's as the second book of Radegund's Life, so later readers met Radegund as her own house remembered her. A medieval bishop who rewrote the Life followed Fortunatus but took some of her miracles.

## Findings

**B1. BLOCKING — "After her death in 587, Fortunatus wrote her Life, but it held no relic of the Cross and no death scene."** The paragraph is about Baudonivia, so on one hearing "her" can be taken as Baudonivia. That gives her the date 587, which the outline forbids ("587 as her date → that is Radegund's death"), and it suggests that Fortunatus wrote her Life. "Held no relic" also sounds like an object rather than a book's contents.
- *Change:* name Radegund in that sentence and say what the book lacked, for example: "After Radegund died in 587, Fortunatus wrote her Life, but it said nothing of the relic of the Cross, and nothing of her death."
- *Do not say line to add to the outline:* "After her death in 587" or any 587 sentence where "her" could be heard as Baudonivia → name Radegund in the sentence that gives 587.

**M1. MINOR — the shrine burning loses what it risked.** Beat 4 holds that the Franks came "with swords and clubs" while she stayed on her horse (SRM2 29673–29675). Without this, the act sounds safe. *Change:* add a clause, kept as her report, for example: "...burned, and who sat her horse while the Franks came with swords and clubs."

**M2. MINOR — the Cross episode has no outcome.** It stops at "kept outside the city". Beat 6 says the relic waited at Tours until the king had the bishop of Tours bring it in. *Change:* add a half-sentence saying that it came in at last, on the king's order.

**M3. MINOR — the daily life is absent.** The Connection field lists the daily life among her additions, and it is the part most clearly "from inside her house". This is optional at this length. If room allows, use the alleluia moment with the portress (beat 5).

**M4. MINOR — "Copied after Fortunatus's work as the second book".** The source says "in most manuscripts" (AA4 41918). *Change:* "In most copies, her book followed Fortunatus's as the second book..."

**M5. MINOR — "her editor" is never placed in time.** Twice the listener hears "her editor" with no sign that he is a modern scholar, and could take him for a contemporary. *Change:* say once "her nineteenth-century editor" (Who tells it: Krusch).

**M6. MINOR — Fortunatus is never called a bishop.** The Connection field asks that the listener know Fortunatus wrote the official Life, and Hildebert's choice rests on his "rank as bishop". *Change:* at first mention, call him "the poet, a bishop by the time she wrote" (beat 1, SRM2 29493).
