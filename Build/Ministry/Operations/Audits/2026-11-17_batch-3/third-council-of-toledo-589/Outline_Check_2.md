# Outline check, round 2: the Third Council of Toledo (589)

## The heart, written before reading the outline

From the census entry (`third-council-of-toledo-589`), `Source_Sheet.md` and what the council is known for:

In May 589, in the Visigothic royal city of Toledo, King Reccared, his queen Baddo, eight Gothic bishops with their clergy, and the Gothic nobles stood before sixty-two Catholic bishops of Spain and Visigothic Gaul and gave up the faith their people had held: the Homoian faith every witness calls "Arian", which ranked the Son and the Spirit below the Father (Agilan, H1-H2; anathema V, H3). They signed the Catholic faith of their Roman subjects instead, ending a kingdom with two churches side by side, often two bishops in one city (E12, C3). The king's book of faith confessed Father, Son and Spirit as one substance, and the Spirit "from the Father and the Son" (E4, H4).

What it decided: the Gothic profession and twenty-three anathemas (on "the faith and communion that came from Arius", on rebaptism, on Leovigild's booklet; receiving the four councils); twenty-three canons, chiefly the creed said aloud by the people before the Lord's Prayer at every Eucharist "to strengthen our people's new conversion" (C1-C2), Arian churches to the Catholic bishop (C3), converted clergy to live apart from their wives (C4), plus canons on Jews (C5), idolatry (C6), and a yearly synod (C8); and the king's edict with penalties (C9).

What it changed: the end of the Gothic Homoian church in Spain, as its winners recorded it (I1), with force around it (Sunna's refusal and exile, plots, Argimund; I3, B9) and enforcement afterwards (Seville, Saragossa; I2, I5); the creed at Mass kept in Spain (Toledo VIII, XII; I4); and "and the Son", which an editor (Percival) traces from here through the West into the long East-West dispute (H7), Dominant Modern Reconstruction / editor only. Every witness is Catholic.

(Correction to my own account, found on checking: the sixty-two bishops are not all Catholic. The signatures to the canons include converted Gothic bishops (Gardingus of Tuy, Beccila of Lugo, Argiovitus of Porto; ACTS 38218, 38224, 38230). Read "before the bishops of Spain and Gaul", not "before sixty-two Catholic bishops". This is now a Do not say item below.)

## The outline's heart, compared

The outline's heart agrees with mine in substance: the king, queen, Gothic bishops, clergy and nobles give up a faith that ranked the Son and Spirit below the Father and sign the Catholic faith; the king's book (equal, eternal, one substance, "from the Father and the Son"); the anathemas (Arius, the old doxology, the new one); churches to the Catholic bishop, two bishops in some cities, the creed before communion; then John's "cut out by the roots", Spain's creed at Mass in 653 and 681, and Percival's account of "and the Son", credited to him. It adds what mine lacked, the content of the Gothic faith through Agilan, and keeps the coercion (Sunna, the edict, canons V, XIV, XVI) in the beats. Leaving out Argimund is right: John ties his plot to no faith (I3). No major contribution is missing. The recommended opening (the two prayers) leads straight into the heart, so it is told in the first minute.

## Verdict: FIX

One blocking finding. The rest are minor wording and confidence fixes.

## Findings

**BLOCKING 1. The Connection field tells the writer that "and the Son" answered the Goths.** It says: "The listener needs to hear that the words that answered the Goths' question about the Spirit's rank outlived the Goths' church, without the narrator saying they were written for that purpose." "The words that answered the Goths' question" is itself the purpose claim. No held source gives a reason for the words (H4: Inferential-Thin as a motive), and the outline's own Do not say forbids it. A writer told this is the point the listener must take away will convey it by arrangement even without a "because". Correction, replace that sentence with: "The listener needs to hear that the words 'from the Father and the Son', set down in the king's book of faith at the council that ended the Goths' church, outlived that church: Spain said them at Mass, and a modern editor traces them through the West into a long quarrel with the East. Agilan's ranking of the Spirit and the king's words may stand in the same story; never join them with 'so', 'because' or 'answered'."

**MINOR 2. Confidence of Agilan's words in the heart.** The heart ends "All Documented, as the acts and their opponents record them". The sheet (H1) gives Agilan's words Documented only as Gregory's report, and Widely Accepted as a statement of Gothic belief. Correction: "Documented as the acts and Gregory of Tours record them; as a statement of what the Goths believed, Widely Accepted (Agilan's words come through his opponent, and agree with anathema V)."

**MINOR 3. "Its bishops signed beside Catholic bishops of the same cities" (heart).** Documented for four cities; the general claim is a reconstruction (E12: Dominant Modern Reconstruction). Correction: "in four cities, Tuy, Lugo, Porto and Tortosa, a converted bishop and a Catholic bishop both signed" (Documented as names; the two-churches reading, Dominant Modern Reconstruction, supported by canon IX).

**MINOR 4. Opening 1, "promised to say".** Anathema XIV (ACTS 36959-36961) curses whoever does not say "Glory and honour to the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit"; it is not a promise (the promise in XVIII is to preach the faith). Correction: "those bishops cursed that prayer, and cursed anyone who would not say 'Glory and honour to the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit'."

**MINOR 5. Links: "Reccared: ... wrote and signed its book of faith".** The acts give the book in his voice and say he offered it and a notary read it (E3); Dalton says Leander composed the speech. "Wrote" settles what the outline elsewhere leaves open. Correction: "offered and signed its book of faith".

**MINOR 6. Links and Who tells it: "Isidore ... wrote ... the life of Leander".** It is a short notice in On Famous Men (ISID 122769-122776), not a Life. Correction: "wrote of Leander in On Famous Men". (The outline's own Links say "his brother Isidore wrote his life" under Leander too; same fix.)

**MINOR 7. Do not say: add three items a writer could easily get wrong.**
- "sixty-two Catholic bishops" or "the Goths stood before sixty-two Catholic bishops" → "sixty-two bishops", or "the bishops of Spain and Gaul". The signatures include converted Gothic bishops (ACTS 38218, 38224, 38230).
- "idolaters were put to death" or "hunted down and killed" → "punished short of death" (canon XVI, "salvo discrimine animae", ACTS 37687-37688). The beat says "hunt out", which invites the error.
- "Agilan, a Gothic bishop" → "Agilan, the Gothic king's envoy"; Gregory gives him no church office (B5).

**MINOR 8. Sunna's timing.** Beat 5 and the Do not say ("he refused before it, and was exiled before it") state the timing flatly; it rests on the inference that Paul's Sunna is John's (B9). The beat already names the inference, so this is wording only. Correction to the Do not say line: "→ by John's dating and the sheet's inference that Paul's Sunna is John's, he was exiled before the council; never place him at it."

## Other checks

- **Impact ranking and what they defended:** sound. The end of the Gothic church first, "and the Son" second, the creed at every Eucharist third; "Defended" marked for the council and for Sunna.
- **Shape and length:** Council shape, led by the major issue and kept as its spine, with the canons on clergy, Jews and idolatry kept in so one dispute does not stand for the whole council. Three minutes, about 390 words at Daniel's 130 a minute, is justified by a major council with two things a newcomer cannot infer.
- **Spoken tradition names:** "the Goths' own church" and "the Catholic church of Spain" do not mislead; "Arian" kept in the witnesses' mouths; "Homoian" kept out of the audio; "Roman Catholic" barred.
- **Connection, the rest:** the first half (churches and property to the Catholic bishop, two bishops in four cities, the new doxology) holds at C3, E12 and anathema XIV, with the reconstruction labelled.
- **Do not say:** otherwise thorough (relics tested by fire, no general conversion of Jews, Toledo not the cause of the schism, Percival credited, "Toledo added" not as fact).
- **Library sweep:** spot-checked the `nicene-visigothic-iberia` and `gothic-and-vandal-homoian-christianity` buckets and a search of `cic/texts/` for "Third Council of Toledo" and Toledo with 589. Brehaut's Isidore extracts (`isidore-seville_encyclopedist-of-the-dark-ages-extracts_brehaut1912.txt` 658) say Leander presided over the council in 587: an editor's claim, which the outline rightly does not carry (the acts name no president). Férotin's Liber Mozarabicus and Martin of Braga have no passage on this council; Hefele's English volume IV is a modern summary of the acts, rightly set aside. No held primary text or major later witness is missing, and the "and the Son" history rests on Percival, credited as an editor, with the primary Toledo VIII and XII creeds carrying what they can.
