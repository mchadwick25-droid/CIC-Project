# Outline: the Third Council of Toledo (589)

Line references are to `Source_Sheet.md` items (S, B, E, C, I), each of which gives its file and lines in `cic/texts/`.

- **Length:** stretch toward 3 minutes (about 430 words). A major council whose main issue, the end of a whole church, has to be told from both sides, and whose other business (the creed at every Eucharist, Jews, idolatry, married clergy) must not be lost behind it. The record is rich on all of it.

- **Setting:**
  - Year: 589 (the acts: "the fourth year of King Reccared", "era 627", "the eighth day before the Ides of May", that is 8 May; 589 is the editors' conversion). S1. Spoken: "in May of the year 589".
  - Place: Toledo, the royal city of the Visigothic kingdom, which held Spain and a strip of southern Gaul around Narbonne. S1, S2. Spoken: "Toledo, the Visigothic kings' capital in Spain".
  - Traditions: the Church of Visigothic Spain (the Catholic, Nicene side) and the Gothic church, part of the Gothic, Vandal and Lombard Churches, which every surviving witness calls "Arian". S5. Spoken: "the Goths' own church" and "the Catholic church of Spain".

- **Thread:** At Toledo the Gothic king, his bishops and his nobles gave up the Gothic church's faith for the Catholic faith of their Roman subjects, and the council made the whole kingdom confess that faith aloud before every communion.

- **Shape:** Council.

- **Openings:**
  - 8 May 589, after a three-day fast: the king takes his place among the bishops, and a notary reads his book of faith aloud. E2, E3.
  - A Gothic bishop, Sunna of Mérida, told he may keep a bishop's rank if he does penance and turns Catholic, says no (Q2). B9. He spoke before the council; place it before 589.
  - A question the record raises: what did the Goths' church believe? Its own books are lost; we hear it only through the men who defeated it. I1.

- **Story beats:**
  1. **The Goths' church under Leovigild.** The Gothic kings and their people kept their own church, apart from the Catholic church of their Roman subjects. In 580 King Leovigild's bishops, meeting at Toledo, called their faith "our catholic faith" and the other "the Roman religion". They ruled that Romans joining them need not be baptized again, and gave their prayer of praise: "Glory to the Father, through the Son, in the Holy Spirit." They baptized with three immersions. A Gothic envoy told a Frankish bishop that the Son is less than the Father, and that "we do not curse what you believe". Catholic writers remember exiles and forced rebaptism. B1, B5, B6, B3, B4. Documented (as reports; all by opponents).
  2. **Reccared turns.** Leovigild died in 586; his son Reccared became Catholic within his first year. John of Biclaro says he won the Gothic clergy "by reason rather than command"; Gregory of Tours tells of a debate of bishops; the acts say the converts were received by anointing or laying on of hands, not a second baptism. B7, B8. Documented that each says so; the accounts differ.
  3. **Resistance.** Gothic bishops resisted. Sunna of Mérida and the noble Segga plotted, and were caught; Sunna was exiled, Segga lost his hands. The bishop Uldila plotted with Leovigild's widow, Goswintha. Sunna, offered a see if he repented, refused (Q2) and was put on a small boat out of Spain. B9. Documented that John and Paul of Mérida say so.
  4. **The council meets.** Reccared ordered all the bishops of his realm to Toledo, to give thanks for his conversion and the Goths' renewal, and to restore church order, because, he said, the heresy had kept them from holding councils. Sixty-two bishops came. His book of faith confessed Father, Son and Spirit as one substance, the Spirit proceeding "from the Father and the Son". He offered the Goths and the Sueves to God (Q1). He and Queen Baddo signed. E1-E6, S4. Documented.
  5. **The Gothic church gives itself up.** A Catholic bishop asked the converts to condemn the Arian faith openly, "with its teachings, rules, services, communion and books". The Gothic bishops, clergy and nobles answered that they had done so already, when they followed the king. They pronounced condemnations of the faith "that came from Arius and that we have kept until now", of rebaptism, of Leovigild's 580 booklet and its prayer, and of the Council of Rimini. They confessed that they and those before them had erred (Q3). Eight Gothic bishops signed one by one, then Gothic nobles and "all the elders of the Goths". E7-E11. Documented. "Anathema" means a solemn curse that cuts a person off from the church: work it into the sentence.
  6. **The canons.** Twenty-three. The people of every church were to say the creed of Constantinople aloud before the Lord's Prayer, in the manner of the Eastern churches: the king's reason was to strengthen "our people's new conversion", so that no one could plead ignorance of the faith. The Gothic church's buildings, with their property, went to the Catholic bishop of the place. Converted clergy were to live apart from their wives. Jews might not have Christian wives, concubines or slaves. Idolatry had spread "through almost all Spain and Gaul"; parents in some parts were killing their children; dancing and shameful songs at saints' feasts were banned. Bishops were to meet once a year. C1-C8. Documented.
  7. **The king's edict.** No one might despise what the council decided. A cleric who disobeyed would be excommunicated; a noble would lose half his property; a lesser man would lose all he had and be exiled. C9. Documented.

- **What came of them:**
  1. **The Gothic church in Spain ended.** John of Biclaro: at Nicaea the Arian heresy was condemned with its roots left in; at Toledo, "after long killings of Catholics and slaughter of the innocent", it was cut out by the roots (I1). Three years later the bishops at Saragossa ruled that former Gothic priests of chaste life could serve again after a new blessing, that the Gothic church's relics must be brought to the bishops and tested by fire, and that churches its bishops had consecrated must be consecrated again (I2). Gregory the Great says Reccared barred heretics from his army (B8). Its own texts from Spain do not survive; a later Frankish chronicle says Reccared had the Arian books burned (B8, I1: late and alone). Documented (John, Saragossa); Inferential-Thin (Fredegar). **Defended:** the Catholic side defended the faith of the four councils; Sunna defended "the religion I have held from my youth".
  2. **The creed before communion.** Canon II put the creed in the people's mouths at every Eucharist, aimed at the new converts (C1, C2). A modern editor, Henry Percival, says this was the first creed to carry "and from the Son", and that Spain kept the form; the surviving Latin gives the creed only by its first words (C2, I4). Dominant Modern Reconstruction (editor).
  3. **Did the decrees hold?** The bishops of Gaul met at Narbonne that November as Toledo had ordered. In 590 a council at Seville, with Leander, complained that some bishops were ignoring "the recent decree of the council of Toledo" (I5). Documented.
  4. **Force around the change.** After the council the duke Argimund plotted to take the throne; he was flogged, shorn, lost his right hand and was paraded through Toledo on a donkey (I3). John does not say he was Arian. Documented that John says so.
  5. **Rome's welcome.** Gregory the Great wrote to Leander that he could not fully express his joy, and later to Reccared of "a new miracle in our days": the whole Gothic people brought from Arian error (I6). He told Leander that Spain should baptize with one immersion, so the heretics could not boast that they had won (B6). Documented.
  6. **Leander.** John says the business of the council lay with Leander of Seville and the abbot Eutropius (E14). Isidore, Leander's brother, wrote that through his faith and work the Goths turned from the "Arian madness" (I7). A sermon for the conversion is printed under Leander's name (I8, attributed). Documented that they say so; authorship of the sermon Contested.

- **Quotes, 2 or 3 at most:**
  1. *Rendered from the Latin of the acts, ACTS 36684-36685 (E5):* "So, most holy fathers, these most noble peoples have been brought through me to the Lord, as his gain. Through your hands I offer them to the eternal God, as a holy and pleasing sacrifice." The king's own framing of what the council was for.
  2. *Rendered from the Latin of the Lives of the Fathers of Mérida, PAUL 12065-12070 (B9):* "I do not know what penance is. So let this be plain to you: I know nothing of penance, and I will never be a Catholic. But I will live by the rite I have lived by. Or I will most gladly die for the religion I have held from my youth until now." The Gothic church's voice, in refusal. Paul of Mérida reports it as a hostile witness, writing later; Sunna spoke before the council.
  3. *Rendered from the Latin of the acts, anathema XVIII, ACTS 36981-36986 (E9):* "No one doubts that we and those before us went astray in the Arian heresy. We have now learned the faith of the Gospel and the apostles, within the Catholic church." What the council asked of the Gothic bishops: to judge their own forebears.

- **Connection to carry into the story:** The Gothic church passed into the Catholic church of Spain, and the council said how. Its churches and their property went to the Catholic bishop of each place (canon IX, C3). Its bishops signed the canons as bishops of their cities: in Tuy, Lugo, Porto and Tortosa a Gothic and a Catholic bishop sign side by side (E12), so the listener should understand there had been two churches, with two bishops, in the same cities (the general claim is a reconstruction from these names and canon IX). Its married clergy had to live apart from their wives (C4). Its priests could serve after a new blessing, its relics were tested by fire, its churches consecrated again (I2). And every congregation, Gothic and Roman alike, now said one creed aloud before communion, which the king meant to fix the new faith in the Goths (C1). Tell this through those acts, not as a summary.

- **Links:**
  - Reccared: called the council, wrote and signed its book of faith, confirmed it by edict.
  - Leander of Seville: signed the canons; ran the council's business (John); a sermon on the conversion is printed under his name; Gregory the Great wrote to him; his brother Isidore wrote his life.
  - Masona of Mérida: first bishop to sign; Sunna plotted against him.
  - John of Biclaro: wrote the council's chronicle; had been exiled by Leovigild.
  - Isidore of Seville: wrote the History of the Goths and the life of Leander.
  - Gregory the Great: wrote to Leander and to Reccared about the conversion.
  - Gregory of Tours: told Reccared's conversion and debated Leovigild's envoy.
  - Hermenegild: Gregory the Great says Reccared followed his martyred brother.
  - Councils of Nicaea, Constantinople, Ephesus and Chalcedon: received. Council of Rimini: condemned.

- **Who tells it:**
  - The acts: the council's own record, made by the side that won. The Gothic bishops speak in it, but in words the council required.
  - John of Biclaro: a Gothic Catholic, exiled by Leovigild for ten years, writing his chronicle in Reccared's reign. The listener should know he suffered under the old church.
  - Gregory of Tours: a Frankish bishop of the same years, openly hostile to the Arians; he reports the envoy's words only to refute them.
  - Isidore of Seville: Leander's younger brother, writing in the next generation.
  - Paul of Mérida: a deacon of Mérida writing a generation or more later (Migne heads him "A.D. 610"), who calls Sunna a "false bishop".
  - Gregory the Great: bishop of Rome and Leander's friend.
  - Henry Percival: a nineteenth-century editor, for the "and from the Son" claim only.
  - Name the witness for each praise or blame, and for Sunna. There is no Gothic witness: say so once.

- **Do not say:**
  - "the Arians" in the narrator's voice → "the Goths' own church, which its opponents called Arian", or "the Gothic church". The word "Arian" belongs to the witnesses.
  - "followers of Arius" → "the faith the council traced to Arius".
  - "Homoian means 'like'" or any gloss of the name → no held source here explains it; do not explain it.
  - "Toledo added 'and from the Son' to the creed" → "the king's book of faith says the Spirit proceeds from the Father and the Son; a modern editor says it was the first creed to carry the words".
  - "Reccared forced the Goths to convert" or "converted them peacefully" as fact → give John's "by reason rather than command" as John's, and the edict's penalties and the exiles as recorded.
  - "Sunna spoke at the council" → he refused before it, and was exiled before it.
  - "Argimund, an Arian rebel" → "the duke Argimund"; John gives no faith for him.
  - "Leander wrote the king's speech" as fact → only a modern editor says so; leave it out of the audio.
  - "Leander presided" → the acts name no president; John says the business lay with Leander and Eutropius.
  - "seventy-two bishops" or "sixty-two" without care → "sixty-two bishops", the number the acts and their signatures give.
  - "the Arian books were burned" as fact → "a later Frankish chronicle says" if told at all.
  - "the whole Gothic people converted" as the narrator's claim → it is the king's claim and Gregory the Great's.
  - "Roman Catholic" → "Catholic", the word the council uses for its own Nicene church.
  - Do not add the Sueves' story, Hermenegild's death or Leovigild's deathbed as colour.

- **Leave out:**
  - Leovigild's deathbed charge to Leander (Gregory the Great alone; a story about the conversion, not the council).
  - Hermenegild's revolt and death: a separate story.
  - The letter in Reccared's name to Gregory: its genuineness is doubted.
  - Leander's sermon imagery of heresy as a harlot and concubine: hostile and side-matter.
  - The Frankish war and Claudius's victory: outside the council.
  - Dalton's claim that Leander composed the king's speech: an editor's, unverifiable here.
  - The 62 against 72 dispute and Mommsen's year 590: for the card.
  - Fredegar's secret baptism of Reccared: late, alone, and against the acts.
