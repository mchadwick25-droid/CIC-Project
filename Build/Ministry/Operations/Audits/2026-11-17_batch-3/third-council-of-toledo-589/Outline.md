# Outline: the Third Council of Toledo (589)

Line references are to `Source_Sheet.md` items (S, B, E, C, I, H), each of which gives its file and lines in `cic/texts/`. Daniel's voice, about 130 words a minute.

- **The heart of their life:** At Toledo in May 589, the Visigothic king Reccared, his queen, and the bishops, clergy and nobles of the Goths' own church gave up that church's faith and signed the Catholic faith of their Roman subjects. What they gave up: a faith that ranked the Son below the Father and the Spirit below both. Their envoy Agilan said the Son "is ever less than the Father" and that "there was a time when yet He was not"; that the Spirit, "promised by the Son and sent by the Father", is less than either (H1, H2). Their prayer of praise was "Glory to the Father, through the Son, in the Holy Spirit" (B1, B2). Their opponents called this faith Arian, after Arius, a priest of Alexandria who had taught "there was a time when the Son was not" (H5). What they took up, in the king's book of faith: the Son equal to the Father and eternal with him, the Spirit proceeding "from the Father and the Son" and of one substance with them (H4). They cursed their old prayer and the faith "that came from Arius", and had to say instead "Glory and honour to the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit" (E9, H3). All Documented, as the acts and their opponents record them; no Gothic text survives (I1). What the council did to the churches: the Gothic church's buildings and property went to the Catholic bishop of each place; its bishops signed beside Catholic bishops of the same cities; and every congregation was to say the creed aloud before communion, the king said to strengthen "our people's new conversion" (C1-C3, E12). Documented. What came of it: John of Biclaro wrote that the heresy was "cut out by the roots" (I1, Documented that he says so). The words "and the Son" became Spain's form of the creed, said at Mass in 653 and 681 (I4, Documented). A modern editor says 589 is the first time they appear in the creed, that from Spain they spread through the West, that Rome held out until 1014, and that they gave rise to, or were the pretext for, "bitter reviling between East and West" (H7: Dominant Modern Reconstruction for 589; Widely Accepted, editor, for the rest).

- **Length:** 3 minutes, about 390 words (Daniel, about 130 a minute). A major council with two things a newcomer must be told and cannot infer: what the Gothic church believed, so the giving-up means something, and what "and the Son" was, so it pays off. Enforcement shrinks to two or three sentences. The heart, through what was given up and what was taken up, is told in the first 130 words.

- **Setting:**
  - Year: 589 (the acts: Reccared's fourth year, era 627, the eighth day before the Ides of May, that is 8 May; 589 is the editors' conversion). S1. Spoken: "in May of the year 589".
  - Place: Toledo, the royal city of the Visigothic kingdom, which held Spain and a strip of southern Gaul around Narbonne. S1, S2. Spoken: "Toledo, the Visigothic kings' capital in Spain".
  - Traditions: the Catholic, Nicene church of Spain, and the Goths' own church, part of the Gothic, Vandal and Lombard Churches, which every surviving witness calls "Arian". S5. Spoken: "the Goths' own church" and "the Catholic church of Spain".

- **Shape:** Council.

- **Openings:**
  - The two prayers. The Goths' bishops had taught their people to pray "Glory to the Father, through the Son, in the Holy Spirit". In May 589, at Toledo, those bishops cursed that prayer, and promised to say "Glory and honour to the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit". B1, B2, H3. Plain heard once, and it leads at once into what the difference was. Recommended.
  - Agilan at Tours: a Gothic envoy tells a Frankish bishop, "The Son is always less than the Father" (Q1). Gregory, the bishop, is a hostile reporter: say so. B5, H1.

- **Story beats:**
  1. **Two churches in one kingdom (the heart, part one).** The Gothic kings and their people kept their own church, apart from the Catholic church of their Roman subjects, with its own bishops, sometimes in the same cities (E12, C3). It taught that the Son was less than the Father and the Spirit less than both; its envoy Agilan argued so before Gregory of Tours (Q1; H1, H2). Its opponents named it after Arius, a priest of Alexandria who had taught that "there was a time when the Son was not" (H5). Under King Leovigild, about 580, its bishops set out the prayer "Glory to the Father, through the Son, in the Holy Spirit" and ruled that Catholics coming over need not be baptized again (B1). Catholic writers remember exiles and forced rebaptism (B3, B4). Documented (as reports; all by opponents). Say once that no Gothic witness survives.
  2. **The council meets (the heart, part two).** Leovigild died in 586; within a year his son Reccared became Catholic (B7, B8). He called the bishops of his realm to Toledo. After a three-day fast, 8 May 589, sixty-two bishops sat, and a notary read the king's book of faith aloud: the Son equal to the Father and eternal with him, "without beginning and without lessening"; the Spirit proceeding "from the Father and the Son" and of one substance with them (E1-E4, H4, S4). He and Queen Baddo signed (E6). Documented. Do not say the words were chosen to answer Agilan (H4).
  3. **The Gothic church gives itself up.** The Gothic bishops, clergy and nobles condemned the faith "that came from Arius and that we have kept until now", rebaptism, the booklet of Leovigild's twelfth year with its prayer, and the teaching that the Son and the Spirit are "less than the Father" (E9, H3). They confessed that they and those before them had gone astray (Q2). Eight Gothic bishops signed, then nobles and "all the elders of the Goths" (E10, E11). Documented. "Anathema", the church's solemn curse that cuts a person off: work it into the sentence or say "cursed".
  4. **The creed before communion.** Canon II: in every church, before the Lord's Prayer, the people were to say the creed aloud, as the Eastern churches did. The king's reason: to strengthen "our people's new conversion", so that no one could plead ignorance of the faith (C1, C2). Documented.
  5. **Not everyone turned (one or two sentences).** Before the council, the Gothic bishop Sunna of Mérida refused to turn Catholic (Q3) and was put on a small boat out of Spain (B9). Paul of Mérida, a hostile later witness, tells it; that Paul's Sunna and John's are one man is an inference. Reccared won the clergy "by reason rather than command", says John of Biclaro, a Catholic Leovigild had exiled (B8, B4). Documented that each says so.
  6. **The force behind it (two or three sentences).** The Gothic church's buildings and property went to the Catholic bishop of each place (C3). Its married clergy had to live apart from their wives (C4). The king's edict: a cleric who defied the council was excommunicated, a noble lost half his property, a lesser man lost all and was exiled (C9). Jews might not have Christian wives or slaves; children of such marriages were to be baptized; and each bishop, with the local judge, was to hunt out idolatry, which had spread "through almost all Spain and Gaul" (C5, C6). Documented. One sentence each at most; these must not crowd beats 1 to 3.

- **What came of them:**
  1. **The Goths' church in Spain ended, as its opponents recorded it.** John of Biclaro, comparing Reccared to Constantine: at Nicaea the heresy was condemned with its roots left in; at Toledo, "after long killings of Catholics and slaughter of the innocent", it was cut out by the roots (I1). Three years later, at Saragossa, the bishops were still dealing with its priests and its hidden relics, which were to be "tested by fire" (I2): one clause, if any. Documented. **Defended:** the council defended the faith of Nicaea and the councils after it; Sunna defended "the religion I have held from my youth".
  2. **"And the Son".** The king's book and the third anathema say the Spirit proceeds "from the Father and the Son" (H4, H3). Spain kept the words: at Mass in 653 and 681 its councils said the creed with them (I4). Documented. Henry Percival, a modern editor, says 589 is the first time the words appear in the creed, that Toledo did not know it was adding anything, that from Spain they spread through the West, that Rome resisted until 1014, and that they became the cause, or the pretext, of "bitter reviling between East and West" (H7). Dominant Modern Reconstruction (589 as first); Widely Accepted, editor (the spread and the quarrel). Credit the editor. This is the payoff for the phrase, and the story may end on it.
  3. **The creed at every Eucharist.** Canon II put the creed in the people's mouths before communion, aimed at the new converts (C1, C2). Documented.
  4. **Rome's welcome** (optional, one clause). Gregory the Great wrote to Reccared of "a new miracle in our days": the whole Gothic people brought from Arian error (I6). Documented that he says so; it is his claim.
  5. **Did the decrees hold?** (card only, or one clause). Seville in 590 complained of bishops ignoring Toledo's decree on clergy and women (I5).

- **Quotes, 2 or 3 at most:**
  1. *Rendered from the Latin of Gregory of Tours, History V.43, GT-L 18556-18558 (H1):* "Whoever does another's will is the lesser of the two. The Son is always less than the Father, because he does the Father's will. And it is not shown that the Father does his." Agilan, the Gothic king's envoy, as Gregory, who argued against him, reports him. The job: the Gothic faith in a Gothic voice, the one thing the listener most needs. The first sentence may be dropped; the rest must stay whole.
  2. *Rendered from the Latin of the acts, anathema XVIII, ACTS 36981-36986 (E9):* "No one doubts that we and those before us went astray in the Arian heresy. We have now learned the faith of the Gospel and the apostles, within the Catholic church." What the council asked of the Gothic bishops: to judge those before them.
  3. *Rendered from the Latin of the Lives of the Fathers of Mérida, PAUL 12065-12070 (B9):* "I do not know what penance is. So let this be plain to you: I know nothing of penance, and I will never be a Catholic. But I will live by the rite I have lived by. Or I will most gladly die for the religion I have held from my youth until now." Optional: the last sentence alone may be used. The refusal, as Paul of Mérida, a hostile later witness, reports it, before the council.
  Retired: the king's offering of the Goths and Sueves "as a holy and pleasing sacrifice" (ACTS 36683-36685, E5). It frames the council as the king's, which beats 2 and 3 now carry; keep only if a quote slot is free.

- **Connection to carry into the story:** Two things passed from this council. First, the Goths' church passed into the Catholic church of Spain, and the council said how: its people now prayed to Father, Son and Spirit as equals, not "through the Son, in the Spirit" (H3); its churches and their property went to the Catholic bishop of each place (C3); and in Tuy, Lugo, Porto and Tortosa a bishop from the old church and a Catholic bishop signed side by side (E12), so the listener should understand there had been two churches, with two bishops, in the same cities (a reconstruction from these names and canon IX). Second, the words "and the Son", set down in the king's book here, became Spain's way of saying the creed (I4), and, by a modern editor's account, spread through the Western church and became a point of quarrel with the East (H7). The listener needs to hear that the words that answered the Goths' question about the Spirit's rank outlived the Goths' church, without the narrator saying they were written for that purpose. Tell both through what people did and said.

- **Links:**
  - Reccared: called the council, wrote and signed its book of faith, confirmed it by edict.
  - Leander of Seville: signed the canons; John says the council's business lay with him and the abbot Eutropius; a sermon on the conversion is printed under his name; Gregory the Great wrote to him; his brother Isidore wrote his life.
  - Masona of Mérida: first bishop to sign; Sunna plotted against him.
  - John of Biclaro: wrote the council's chronicle; had been exiled by Leovigild.
  - Gregory of Tours: debated the Gothic envoy Agilan; told Reccared's conversion.
  - Gregory the Great: wrote to Leander and to Reccared about the conversion.
  - Isidore of Seville: wrote the History of the Goths and the life of Leander.
  - Arius: the faith the council condemned is named after him (Socrates, H5).
  - Councils of Nicaea, Constantinople, Ephesus and Chalcedon: received. Council of Rimini: condemned.

- **Who tells it:**
  - The acts: the council's own record, made by the side that won. The Gothic bishops speak in it, but in words the council required.
  - Gregory of Tours: a Frankish bishop of the same years, who reports Agilan's words to refute them. The listener must hear that the Gothic voice comes through its opponent (one clause).
  - John of Biclaro: a Catholic Goth, exiled by Leovigild for ten years, writing in Reccared's reign.
  - Paul of Mérida: a deacon of Mérida writing a generation or more later, who calls Sunna a "false bishop".
  - Socrates: a Greek church historian writing about a century after Arius; for who Arius was. Not to be named in the audio.
  - Henry Percival: a nineteenth-century editor, for 589 as the first creed with "and the Son", and for what came of the words. Credit him as "a modern editor" or "one modern scholar".
  - How-we-know: one sentence, that no Gothic writing survives and the Goths' church is heard only through those who defeated it. The doubt is not the story.

- **Do not say:**
  - "the Arians" in the narrator's voice → "the Goths' own church, which its opponents called Arian", or "the Gothic church". The word "Arian" belongs to the witnesses.
  - "followers of Arius" → "the faith the council traced to Arius"; Arius is named only to say whose name the opponents gave it.
  - "the Goths believed Jesus was not God" → "taught that the Son was less than the Father, and the Spirit less than both". In the held lines Agilan says "he who is sent is no God" of the Spirit only (H2); he never denies that the Son is God. Say only what he says.
  - "Homoian means 'like'" in the audio → leave the word "Homoian" out of the audio. The gloss and the Goths' tie to the creed of Rimini are editors' (H6): for the card.
  - "Toledo added 'and from the Son' to the creed" as the narrator's fact → "the king's book of faith says the Spirit proceeds from the Father and the Son; a modern editor says 589 is the first time the words appear in the creed".
  - "they added 'and the Son' to answer the Goths" or "because the Goths ranked the Spirit lower" → no held source gives the reason. Set Agilan's words and the king's book side by side; no "so" or "because".
  - "Toledo caused the split between East and West" → "the words later became a cause, or a pretext, of bitter quarrel between East and West, one modern scholar writes".
  - "the Goths had always prayed 'through the Son'" → "under Leovigild, about 580, their bishops set out that prayer". Whether it was older is not held.
  - "the council forced the Jews to convert" → "the council ordered the children of Jewish-Christian marriages baptized"; it ordered no general conversion.
  - "the Goths called their own church catholic" → leave it out; the manuscripts and the critical edition disagree on who says "our catholic faith".
  - "the Arian relics were burned" → "tested by fire".
  - "Reccared converted at the council" → he became Catholic within his first year; the council made it public.
  - "pagan child sacrifice" → leave canon XVII out; if told, it is parents killing their children so as not to have more, not tied to idolatry.
  - "Reccared forced the Goths to convert" or "converted them peacefully" as fact → John's "by reason rather than command" as John's, and the edict's penalties and the exiles as recorded.
  - "Sunna spoke at the council" → he refused before it, and was exiled before it.
  - "Leander wrote the king's speech" or "Leander presided" → leave out; the acts name no president.
  - "seventy-two bishops" → "sixty-two bishops".
  - "the Arian books were burned" as fact → "a later Frankish chronicle says", if told at all.
  - "the whole Gothic people converted" as the narrator's claim → it is the king's claim and Gregory the Great's.
  - "Roman Catholic" → "Catholic", the council's word for its own Nicene church.
  - Do not open on "from the Father and the Son" unless the story pays it off (What came of them, item 2). A reader found it left hanging.
  - Do not add the Sueves' story, Hermenegild's death or Leovigild's deathbed as colour.
