## New findings

**BLOCKING 1. Seville is tied to the wrong rule.** The story says: "converted clergy had to live apart from their wives. By 590, bishops at Seville complained that some ignored the rule." A listener hears that the converted clergy were still living with their wives. The Seville canon (ACTS 59273-59291, re-checked) is headed "De clericis cum quibus mulieres cohabitant". It says some bishops were not enforcing the Toledo decree and were lax with their clergy, and it acts against clergy keeping "extranearum faeminarum vel ancillarum" (outside women or slave women). That is canon V's second clause (ACTS 37455-37464), which covers clergy "qui vero semper sub canone ecclesiastico jacuerint", those who had always been Catholic, not the converts. The complaint is about bishops not enforcing a rule, and the rule is not the converts' marriages.
*Change:* replace the sentence, for example: "By 590, bishops at Seville complained that some bishops were not enforcing Toledo's rule against clergy keeping women in their houses." Or cut it, since Saragossa already carries whether the decrees held.
*Do not say line for the outline (the outline left room: its C4 gives only canon V's first clause, and item 3 says "clergy living with women"):* "Seville complained that converted clergy kept their wives" → "bishops at Seville complained that some bishops were not enforcing Toledo's rule against clergy keeping women in their houses".

**MINOR**

1. **"A disobedient cleric was excommunicated".** The past tense reads as an event. The edict sets a penalty: "would be excommunicated ... would lose ... would be exiled".
2. **The creed's later life, and the opening phrase.** "From the Father and the Son" is planted in the opening and never paid off, and item 2's evidence that the creed held (653 and 681) is absent. Its absence misleads no one, so it is not blocking. If room is made, one clause after the creed sentence would carry it: "Sixty years later, bishops at Toledo wrote out the creed they said at Mass, with 'from the Father and the Son'." Keep Percival for the card.
3. **What called it.** One clause would do: Reccared summoned every bishop of his realm, to give thanks for the conversion and restore church order (E1).
