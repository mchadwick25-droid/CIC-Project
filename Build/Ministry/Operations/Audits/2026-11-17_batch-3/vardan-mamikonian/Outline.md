# Outline: Vardan Mamikonian

File keys as in `Source_Sheet.md`: **N** = Neumann's English Elishe (`cic/texts/eghishe_history-of-vartan-and-the-armenian-war_neumann1830.txt`); **L12** = Langlois tome II in `cic/texts/langlois_collection-historiens-armenie-tomes1-2-fra_1867-1869.txt` (Elishe in French, Lazar, Koriun, editors' notes); **O** = Ormanian 1912; **ST** = Stepanos of Taron, German; **K** = Kirakos of Gandzak, French (`cic/texts/brosset_deux-historiens-armeniens-kiracos-oukhtanes-fra_1870.txt`). New lines for this revision are in Source_Sheet section 8.

- **The heart of his life:** In 450 the Persian king ordered every people in his empire, Christian Armenia among them, to worship the sun and serve the fire religion of the Magi (N 1470-1473). Vardan Mamikonian, general of the Armenians (L12 90515), told the king he would give his life and goods, but would not change his faith (L12 90618-90626). He led the princes, priests and people of Armenia in a rising against the order (N 2957-2961; L12 91622-91625), and fell in battle on the plain of Avarayr in the spring of 451 (N 4165-4166; N 4175), with 1,036 Armenians by the historians' count (N 4227-4229; L12 92386-92391). *Documented.* What his death won, as the held texts give it, came in two steps:
  - **At once, and not for long.** Within about a year, both historians say, the Persian side ordered that Christians in Armenia be left free to worship (Lazar, L12 92401-92414, 92424-92437; Elishe, L12 83883-83886, 83909-83920; year 452 from the editor, L12 83858-83860, 92428-92430). Elishe says the governor acted because many princes still held the mountain fortresses (L12 83873-83877; N 4574-4575). *Documented* as each historian's report. It did not hold: the patriarch and priests were taken to Persia and killed (O 2273-2276, 454), and under a later king, Peroz, apostasy bought office and Persia kept pressing its religion (L12 95252-95266; O 2280-2282). *Documented* (Lazar); *Widely Accepted* (Ormanian).
  - **A generation later, for good.** His nephew Vahan, raised among the martyrs' sons, rose again about thirty years on. His first demand was that no Armenian be made a Magus and that Christians worship freely, and the Persian king granted it forever, under seal (L12 99485-99498, 100500-100512). *Documented* (Lazar). The year is about 484 (O 2291-2295); editors differ (L12 99215; 87287-87289): *Contested* for the year. Ormanian, a modern patriarch, says this "ensured the civil and religious peace of Armenia" (O 2294-2295): *Widely Accepted*, and his, not the narrator's.
  - **The church's martyr.** The Armenian church made him and his companions its martyrs and saints and keeps their feast (L12 91072-91076; ST 2789-2810; K 11281-11288; O 2263-2265). *Documented*; feast day *Widely Accepted* (Ormanian).
  What the sources will not hold: that Avarayr "saved" Armenian Christianity, or that the battle itself won anything. Elishe says there was no telling victors from vanquished (N 4182-4183). The freedom of 452 is told by both historians but came with fear of the fortresses (Elishe) and did not last; the lasting grant is Vahan's, a generation later.

- **Length:** about 2½ minutes, 380-420 words (Neo, about 150 a minute). Reason: a major figure (census `major: true`), and the heart runs across two generations, 450 to about 484, which needs telling in order. The earlier version ran to 495 words; the saving comes from the witness comparison, cut to two sentences.

- **Setting:**
  - Time: 450-451; the order in 450 (N margin; N 1477, 2242), the battle in spring 451 (N 4175, "It was then spring time"; year from editors, L12 83415-83417). The settlement about 484. Spoken: "in the year 450", "the spring of 451", "about thirty years later".
  - Place: Armenia, then ruled by the kings of Persia after its own kings had fallen (N 1120-1122); the plain of Avarayr by the river Deghmut (L12 92126-92128). Spoken: "Armenia, then ruled by Persia"; "the plain of Avarayr".
  - Tradition: the Armenian church, taught its faith by Gregory the Illuminator (L12 90831-90832). Census "Armenian Christianity after Avarayr". Spoken: "the Armenian church".
  - The other side: the king's religion of the Magi, fire and the sun (N 1470-1473). Spoken: "the Persian fire religion of the Magi" (Zoroastrian is acceptable).

- **Shape:** Martyr.

- **Openings** (recommend 1, run straight into 2):
  1. **The order, plainly.** In 450 the king of Persia commanded every people in his lands to worship the sun, feed the holy fire, and keep the laws of the Magi (N 1470-1473). Heard once, it sets time, place and stakes in one sentence, and the next sentence is Vardan's answer.
  2. **His answer before the king** (Quote 1), framed as "an Armenian general stood before the king".
  Do not open on Lazar's "action whose fame would resound forever": it is irony that works only on the page.

- **Story beats** (the heart lands by the end of beat 3, about 150 words; the rest deepens it in time order):
  1. **The order and the man, 450.** The king's order (N 1470-1473). Vardan: general of the Armenians and lord of the Mamikonian house (L12 90515), grandson of the patriarch Sahak, who taught him the Scriptures (L12 90889-90891). Summoned to court with other princes (N 2204-2206, 2242). *Documented.*
  2. **His answer.** Offers his strength, life and goods; refuses to change his faith (L12 90612-90626; Quote 1). One sentence on who tells it: Elishe, a churchman who says he saw it, and Lazar, who wrote a generation later at the order of Vardan's nephew (N 1117-1118; L12 87726-87729). *Documented.*
  3. **The heart, told in brief.** He led the rising, fell at Avarayr in 451, and the church keeps him as its martyr; about thirty years later his nephew won from Persia the freedom to keep that faith. One or two sentences only, no scene: the full telling comes in beats 6-7, in order. Do not frame it with "But first".
  4. **The outward yielding and the rising, 450-451** (two sentences for the yielding). The king threatened chains, elephants and the exile of wives and children (N 2372-2383). Both historians say the princes then pretended to accept his religion (N 2444-2446; L12 90969-90971); in Lazar, Vardan refused in anger and gave way in tears only when the others begged him to spare their families from deportation and forced worship (L12 90692-90698, 90796-90799, 90960-90966). Home again, their children ran from them in fear (L12 91123-91126). Magi came to close the churches (L12 80402-80405); when the chief Magus and troops tried to break open a church door, the priest Leont led the people, who stoned them (N 2648-2659). The princes rose, stoned a prince who would not join, burned fire-temples and killed Magi (N 2891-2893, 2957-2961). The governor Vasak went over to Persia (N 3284-3288). *Documented.*
  5. **Avarayr, spring 451.** He read the army the Maccabees, Jews who died rather than break God's law (N 3956-3966); in the night the unbaptized were baptized, at dawn all took communion (N 4009-4011). His words (Quote 2). Armenians fought on both sides (O 2259-2261). He was killed near the elephants (N 4165-4166); the survivors scattered to the strongholds (L12 83402-83411); the historians count 1,036 Armenian dead (N 4227-4229; L12 92386-92391). *Documented.*
  6. **A freedom that did not last, 452 onward.** Both historians: the Persian side ordered Christians left free to worship; Elishe says the governor feared the princes in the fortresses (Source_Sheet §8). Then the patriarch Joseph, Leont and other priests were taken to Persia and killed (O 2273-2276). Under Peroz, apostasy bought office (L12 95252-95266). *Documented* (each historian's report); year 452 the editor's.
  7. **Vahan, about 484.** The martyrs' widows raised their sons (L12 95201-95205). Vahan, son of Vardan's brother Hmayeak (L12 95330-95334), himself bore the name of apostate "in appearance and not in reality", as his forebears had, and it gave him no peace (L12 95786-95789). He rose with princes who said they trusted "in the death of our ancestors" who pleased Christ by martyrdom (L12 95976-95983). His first demand (L12 99485-99498); King Vagharsh's grant under seal (Quote 3), his pardon of a revolt forced by Peroz's violence, and Vahan made general of Armenia (L12 100500-100512, 100545-100552). End here, on what was won. *Documented* (Lazar); year *Contested*.

- **What came of him** (heaviest first; about a third of the story, carried mainly by beats 6-7):
  1. **His nephew's generation won lasting freedom of worship**, about 484. **Defended: the right to keep the faith of their fathers** ("les lois de notre pays et de nos péres", OCR for pères, L12 99487). *Documented*; year *Contested*.
  2. **The church made him its martyr and saint.** Lazar: "the title of martyr" (L12 91072-91076); Stepanos, about 550 years later, "the holy Vardan", who fell "for the holy church and the Christian faith" (ST 2789-2792, 2807-2810); 12th-century hymns for "the holy Vardanans" (K 11281-11288, OCR poor); feast on the Thursday before Lent (O 2263-2265). One sentence in beat 3, a phrase again at the end if room allows.
  3. **His war became a classic of Armenian writing:** Elishe's History of Vartan and Lazar's history (N 1035; L12 87726-87729; ST 191-198). Carried by the one how-we-know sentence in beat 2; not told further.

- **Quotes** (all rendered from Langlois's French; no Armenian original is held; Elishe checked against Neumann's English):
  1. **Vardan before the king** (Lazar, L12 90623-90626). « Mais abandonner une religion que j'ai reçue de Dieu dès mon enfance, et la changer par crainte des hommes, cela n'est pas possible! »
     Rendering: "But I received this religion from God as a child. To abandon it, and change it out of fear of men, is not possible!"
     Job: his stand, in his own reported words; leads into the heart. Credit to Lazar.
  2. **Vardan to his army before Avarayr** (Elishe, L12 82667-82673; OCR "ua", "nous.le", "ordresiniques", "estle" parted). « pendant un certain temps, nous trompâmes ce prince injuste et nous le surprimes comme un faible enfant, comme si, en apparence, nous avions exécuté des ordres iniques. Le Seigneur cependant est le témoin caché de nos pensées; [il sait] que nous avons persévéré sans nous séparer de lui. »
     Rendering: "For a time we deceived that unjust prince. We took him in like a weak child, as if we had outwardly carried out his wicked orders. But the Lord is the hidden witness of our thoughts. He knows we held firm and never parted from him."
     Job: names the outward yielding in his own reported voice and turns it into the reason for the battle, so the yielding needs no longer telling in beat 4. Neumann reads "by seeming apostacy" (N 3930-3933); do not soften it away. Credit to Elishe.
  3. **The Persian king's grant** (Lazar, L12 100503-100506; new). « Qu'aujourd'hui toutes vos demandes vous soient accordées éternellement, confirmées par notre écrit et par notre sceau »
     Rendering: "Today let all your demands be granted to you forever, confirmed by our writing and our seal."
     Job: the ending; what his death won, in the words of the other side, as Lazar gives them. Credit to Lazar ("as Lazar gives the king's words").
  Dropped: Lazar's "God gave him the title of martyr" (L12 91072-91076). It served the old riddle opening; its content is now one plain sentence in beat 3. It may stand as a card quotation.

- **Connection to carry into the story:** From Vardan to his nephew **Vahan Mamikonian**, and through him to the grant of about 484. Told through what people did:
  - Vahan was the son of Vardan's brother Hmayeak, who also died in the war (L12 92519-92523, 95330-95334), and was raised by the martyrs' widows (L12 95201-95205).
  - Like his uncle, he outwardly bore the Persian religion's name, "in appearance and not in reality", and it gave him no peace (L12 95786-95789). The listener should hear the echo without being told it is one.
  - He rose with men who trusted in their ancestors' martyrdom (L12 95976-95983), and his first demand was exactly what Vardan's generation had refused: that no Armenian be made a Magus, and that Christians worship freely (L12 99485-99498).
  - The king granted it forever, under seal (L12 100503-100506). No "this matters because" sentence.

- **Who tells it:**
  - **Elishe**, an Armenian churchman who says he saw these events (N 1117-1118), writing for a priest of Vardan's house (N 1035). His date is discussed by scholars: say "he says he saw it", never "an eyewitness" as settled.
  - **Lazar of Parpi**, writing a generation later at the order of Vardan's nephew Vahan (L12 87726-87729). He alone gives Vardan's personal refusal and tears, and the whole of Vahan's story.
  - **Ormanian**, a modern Armenian patriarch: the feast day, the 484 date, the "religious peace" judgment.
  - **No Persian account is held.** Persia speaks only through these Armenian writers.
  - How-we-know gets one sentence, in beat 2, naming both and the nephew. The doubt is not the story here. The two tellings of the yielding get one sentence between them in beat 4, not a comparison.

- **Do not say:**
  - "Vardan came up with the plan to pretend" → *the princes (Lazar) or a secret Christian at court (Elishe) proposed it; in Lazar he refused, then wept and gave way.*
  - "They all bowed to the fire" → *Lazar: some, not all, seemed to bow* (L12 90970).
  - "66,000 Armenians" as fact → *"some sixty thousand", by Elishe's count* (French 60,000, English 66,000).
  - "1,036 died with him" as a firm total → *"the historians count 1,036"*.
  - "the Persians lost seven times as many" → *leave out; the two renderings disagree.*
  - "on 26 May" or "on 2 June" → *"in the spring of 451"*, or "at Pentecost" with Lazar.
  - "they wrote to the Council of Chalcedon" → *Neumann's gloss; they wrote to the capital.*
  - "Avarayr was really a victory", "a defeat that won the war", "a defeat", or "Avarayr saved Armenian Christianity" as the narrator's verdict → *he fell and his army scattered; Elishe says there was no telling victors from vanquished (N 4182-4183). What came of it is told through the orders of 452 and the grant of about 484.*
  - "Persia gave in because of Vardan's death" or "the king, moved by his death, granted freedom" → *Lazar tells the king's grief and the order in sequence, not as cause (L12 92401-92414); Elishe gives the governor's fear of the princes in the fortresses as his reason (L12 83873-83877).*
  - "In 452 the Armenians won their freedom" as lasting → *an order to leave Christians free, which did not hold; the lasting grant is Vahan's, about 484.*
  - "those forced into the Magi's religion were compelled back to Christianity" → *Neumann's wording (N 4596-4597); the French says they could profess Christianity, and each follow the worship he chose (L12 83909-83920).*
  - "the treaty of Nvarsak" → *the name is in no held text; "the king's grant", "under seal".*
  - "Vahan, his son" → *his nephew, son of his brother Hmayeak.*
  - "Vahan had converted to the fire religion" → *Lazar: he bore the name of apostate "in appearance and not in reality", after his forebears' example.*
  - "a clever trick" or "a sin" in the narrator's voice → *give each witness's word: Elishe's Vardan says they deceived the king; Lazar's Vardan says it was not by his own will or from fear; he "lost himself in appearance" for a time, to save the others and repent after (L12 91184-91194).*
  - "to get home and save their people" as the narrator's motive → *Elishe: a way for some to get out of their plight; Lazar: to spare the deported from forced worship.*
  - "he defied Persian rule" → *at court he offered the king his life and goods, and refused only to change his faith.*
  - "Armenians against Persians" → *Armenians fought on both sides* (O 2259-2261).
  - "Sahak's nephew" → *grandson* (L12 90891).
  - "the people stoned the Magi" with no cause → *when the chief Magus and troops tried to break open a church door* (N 2648-2659).
  - "Vardan the Great", "Elishe, his secretary", "Armenia, the first Christian nation" → *not in these sources; omit.*
  - "the Persians' religion was false/evil" in the narrator's voice → *the witnesses' hostility stays theirs.*
  - Any age, look, inner feeling or family scene not in the outline (no "with a heavy heart" beyond Lazar's tears and Vahan's lack of peace).
  - "Vardan said" as exact words → *"as Elishe/Lazar gives his words".*

- **Leave out:**
  - Lazar's "action whose fame would resound forever" (L12 91056-91064): page-only irony.
  - Vardan's win in Albania and the emperor Marcian's refusal of help (N 3108-3240); his gifts of arms (N 3950-3953): no room.
  - Hmayeak's death and Vasak's disgrace beyond a phrase: side lines; Hmayeak enters only as Vahan's father.
  - Lazar's claim about Vahan Amatuni's hatred of Vasak (L12 91402-91411): a side quarrel.
  - Leont's radiance seen by Mesrop's pupils (L12 92172-92178): a wonder about another man.
  - Elishe's eighth chapter on the captive priests: a different story.
  - Stepanos's "19 years" and Moses's imperial commission: single, late or disputed.
  - The 1527 colophon's "holy Vardans": unclear.
  - His daughter Shushanik (PO18 5964-5966): a second story.
  - The marriage clauses of the royal order (L12 80481-80488): polemical, hard to frame fairly here.
  - Keep, do not soften: the stoning of the Magi and of a dissenting prince, the killing of Magi, the burning of temples. Told as plainly as the courage.
