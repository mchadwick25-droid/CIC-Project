# Outline check: Vardan Mamikonian

**Verdict: FIX.** There are five blocking findings. Once they are corrected, the outline needs an independent recheck.

Quotes and paraphrases were checked against `cic/texts/langlois_collection-historiens-armenie-tomes1-2-fra_1867-1869.txt` (L12) and `cic/texts/eghishe_history-of-vartan-and-the-armenian-war_neumann1830.txt` (N), at the cited lines, plus `ormanian_church-of-armenia_gregory1912.txt` (O), `stepanos-taron_armenische-geschichte-deu_gelzer-burckhardt1907.txt` (ST) and `brosset_deux-historiens-armeniens-kiracos-oukhtanes-fra_1870.txt` (K).

## BLOCKING

**B1. Quote 2's rendering: "caught him out" misleads.** In modern English, to "catch someone out" means to expose them in a mistake. The French "nous le surprîmes comme un faible enfant" (L12 82669) means "we took him in" or "we caught him off guard". Neumann has "treated him as a simple Child" (N 3929-3930). The French is otherwise verbatim. My rendering: "For a time we deceived that unjust prince. We took him in like a weak child, as if we had outwardly carried out his wicked orders. But the Lord is the hidden witness of our thoughts. He knows we held firm and never parted from him."
*Correction:* change "We caught him out like a feeble child" to "We took him in like a weak child". Also change "agrees with N 3930-3933" to: "Neumann's English differs. It says 'by seeming apostacy, though in secret we were true to God, as he himself will testify' (N 3930-3932), where the French has 'as if we had outwardly carried out wicked orders' and 'the hidden witness of our thoughts'." The writer must not soften Neumann's word "apostasy" away (see B3).

**B2. Beat 5, "At home the people reproach the princes": wrong place and wrong people.** In Elishe the reproaches come before the princes go home. In the French they come from "tous ceux qui croyaient au saint Évangile" in "l'immense armée des Perses" (L12 80605-80613), that is, Christians in the Persian camp. Neumann has the news arriving "from the great camp of the Persians to the people who believed", and then "they returned" (N 2569-2601). In the same passage, the priests with the army leave the princes in anger and send a rider to the bishops (L12 80729-80737). The homecoming grief at home belongs to Lazar: the children who ran from their fathers' arms (L12 91110-91126).
*Correction:* "In Elishe, Christians in the Persian camp heap scorn on the princes, quoting 'whoever denies me before men'. The princes cannot explain and weep (L12 80605-80727; N 2569-2600). The priests with the army leave them in anger (L12 80729-80737). Back home, Lazar says, their own children ran from them (L12 91123-91126)."

**B3. The pretended conversion is not told as each witness tells it.** The outline gives it only as the defenders' justified plan, and opens beat 4 with one motive in the narrator's voice ("to get home and save their people"). The record gives three different framings, and none of them reaches the writer:
- **Elishe's narrator** calls it "l'apostasie odieuse des Arméniens" (L12 80607), "the sinful confession" in Neumann (N 2569). His Vardan, in contrast, boasts that they deceived the king (L12 82667-82673).
- **Lazar's Vardan** tells his household that he "denied" Christ ("j'ai renié mon Créateur") and "lost himself in appearance", meaning to "repent afterwards" (L12 91184-91192). He counts it a denial that needs repentance, not a clever trick.
- **The motive differs.** In Elishe, the councillor offers some of the princes a way "to get themselves out of their plight" ("se tirer eux-mêmes de leur triste position", L12 80335-80336). In Lazar, the princes argue that refusing would damn countless deported men, women and children (L12 90689-90698).

*Correction:* open beat 4 with "Both witnesses say the princes pretended to accept the king's religion. They tell it differently." Add both motives, each credited to its witness. Add to Do not say: "a clever trick" or "a sin" in the narrator's voice → *give each witness's word: Elishe's Vardan says they deceived the king; Elishe himself calls it hateful apostasy as the Christians saw it; Lazar's Vardan says he denied Christ in appearance, to repent later.*

**B4. Persia's demands are cut down to "worship the sun", and the stoning at Angegh loses its cause.** Elishe quotes the royal order that came with the 700 Magi and more. Churches were to be closed and sealed and their books taken to the treasury. Psalms and the reading of Scripture were to stop. Nobles' wives were to be taught by the Magi, and children instructed in public (N 2470-2515; L12 80404-80470). The stoning happens because the Mogbed, "with the Magi, and a strong force", tried to break open the church doors (N 2648-2651). As the outline stands, a listener hears that the people stoned Magi at a church door with no reason given. Beyond that, there is no held Persian account. The Persian side is heard only as these Armenian writers report it: the Magi's counsel (N 1199-1201), Mihrnerseh's edict (N 1611ff), and in Lazar the Persian general Nikhor and King Vagharsh, who blame King Peroz's violence for the revolt and pardon it (L12 99604-99612, 100500-100512).
*Correction:*
- Beat 2: add "The king sends hundreds of Magi with orders to close the churches and teach the nobles' wives and children (N 2470-2500)."
- Beat 5: "When the Mogbed and his troops try to force open a church door, the priest Leont leads the people, who stone them (N 2648-2659)."
- Mark the linking line "the king wants one religion" as "as Elishe reports the Magi's counsel".
- Add to Who tells it: "No Persian account is held. Persia speaks only through these Armenian writers."
- Leave out the marriage clauses of the royal order (N 2501-2507): they are polemical and hard to frame fairly in this length.

**B5. Three claims rest on an editor or a modern writer when a primary text carries them. The sweep also misses a major later witness.**
- **Lazar wrote for Vahan.** This is Lazar's own statement, not only the editor's: "le marzban Vahan ... nous oblige d'écrire ... dans l'ordre qu'il a adressé à moi Lazare de Pharbe" (L12 87725-87730), and "Vahan ... m'obligea d'entreprendre une œuvre" (L12 87777-87779). The editor's line that the outline cites is itself hedged: "il est probable que Lazare demeura ... où il rédigea, à sa prière" (L12 87280-87282). *Correction:* cite L12 87725-87730 and 87777-87779 as Documented (Lazar's own word), and drop "editor only".
- **The 484 settlement.** Lazar tells it himself. King Vagharsh grants all the demands, "confirmées par notre écrit et par notre sceau", forever. He pardons the revolt as forced "par la violence de Bérose". Then he names Vahan general-in-chief of Armenia (L12 100500-100552). Ormanian says commander in 484 and governor in 485 (O 2289-2292). Langlois's note dates Vagharsh's reign 486-490 (L12 99215), and his introduction says Lazar's history runs to 485 (L12 87288). *Correction:* cite Lazar for the grant (Documented). Change "made Vahan governor in 484-485" to "made Vahan commander, and later governor". Give the year as "about 484, by modern reckoning" (Contested between editors). In Do not say, replace "33 years later" with "a generation later".
- **The feast.** The 13th-century historian Kirakos, who is held (K), names "S. Vardan, petit-fils de S. Sahak, et ses compagnons" as martyrs crowned by Christ (K 6017-6019). He also says the 12th-century patriarch Nersès wrote hymns "des saints Vardanans" (K 11270-11288). The sheet's sweep does not list this file. *Correction:* add Kirakos to the sweep and to impact item 2 ("by the 12th century the church sang hymns for them, Kirakos records"; Documented as Kirakos's report). Keep Ormanian for the Thursday-before-Lent date only.

## MINOR

1. **A fourth quote, unchecked against the French.** Beat 2 quotes Neumann's "thou hast the sword, we the neck" (N 2144). The French reads "si tu nous présentes le glaive, voici notre tête" (L12 79990-79991). The template allows three quotes at most. *Correction:* paraphrase ("the bishops answer that the king has the sword, and they will give their heads"), or render it from the French and drop quote 3, which repeats Opening 1.
2. **Beat 3, "their church was not founded by man".** The French has "l'Église" (L12 80220-80222); Neumann has "religion" (N 2332). *Correction:* say "their faith" and note that the renderings differ.
3. **Quote 1's context.** In the same speech, Vardan first pledges his life, goods and household to the king (L12 90618-90623). *Correction:* add to Do not say: "he defied Persian rule" → *at court he offered the king his life and goods, and refused only to change his faith.*
4. **The sides at Avarayr.** Ormanian: "a large number of Armenians reinforced the enemy's ranks" (O 2259-2260). The French Elishe counts the 3,544 dead "du côté des apostats" (L12 83451-83452). *Correction:* add to Do not say: "Armenians against Persians" → *Armenians fought on both sides.*
5. **Do not say: "a defeat".** Elishe says "les vainqueurs se trouvaient des deux côtés" (L12 83399-83400; N 4183-4184). *Correction:* replace "→ a defeat" with "→ Vardan fell and his army scattered to the strongholds; Elishe says no one side won the field; never 'a victory'."
6. **Koriun's "neveu".** L12 55967-55968 calls him Sahak's nephew. *Correction:* add to Do not say: "Sahak's nephew" → *grandson* (Lazar, L12 90891; Genealogy, L12 59900-59904).
7. **Wrong line cite.** Vasak's name "not named among the faithful" is at N 4868-4869, not N 4887-4889. *Correction:* fix the cite in beat 7.
8. **Length against the martyr shape.** 450 words is justified (a major figure, a two-witness turn, the 484 connection), but beats 2-7 are overloaded for about 300 words. *Correction:* mark beat 5's Albania campaign and Marcian's refusal, and beat 6's gifts of arms, as the first to compress. Keep the two-witness turn and the impact at full room, so the death does not swallow the life and what came of it.
