# Outline check, round 2: Vardan Mamikonian

## The heart of his life (written before opening Outline.md)

From the census entry, `Source_Sheet.md` and what he is known for.

Vardan Mamikonian, general of the Armenians and lord of the Mamikonian house, grandson and pupil of the patriarch Sahak, led the Armenian church's armed defence of its faith against the Persian king Yazdegerd II, who in 450 ordered Armenia to take up the Magi's religion (L12 90515, 90889-90891; N 1470-1473; L12 80466-80481). His essence is the stand of a Christian people for the right to keep the faith of their fathers, made by a soldier: the refusal at court, then the outward yielding the princes made there (in Lazar, Vardan gave way only in tears, to spare the deported from forced worship, L12 90960-90966), then the rising at home, in which churches were defended, fire-temples burned and Magi killed (N 2643-2659, 2957-2961; L12 91615-91625). He fell at Avarayr in 451 with 1,036 Armenians, Armenians on both sides, and the battle was lost (N 4162-4166, 4227; O 2259-2261).

What it changed: the defeat did not end the cause. Within about a year the governor, fearing princes still in the fortresses, let the bishops and open worship return (Elishe, L12 83866-83921; Lazar, L12 92401-92437), though that did not hold under Peroz (L12 95252-95266). The next generation, raised by the martyrs' widows and trusting in "la mort de nos ancêtres", rose under his nephew Vahan and won freedom of worship under seal, about 484 (L12 95201-95205, 95976-95983, 99485-99498, 100503-100552; O 2283-2294). The church named him martyr and saint, keeps the feast of him and his companions, and his war became the subject of two classic histories, Lazar's written at Vahan's order (L12 91072-91076; O 2263-2265, 8564-8565; ST 191-198; L12 87726-87729).

Contributions to carry: (1) leader of the 450-451 rising in defence of Christian worship; (2) the martyr's death at Avarayr; (3) his memory carried into Vahan's rising and the grant of freedom of worship; (4) the church's martyr and feast; (5) the subject of Elishe's and Lazar's histories.

## The outline's heart, compared

The outline's heart: the 450 order, Vardan's refusal at court, his leadership of the rising, his fall at Avarayr with 1,036 by the historians' count; then what came of it in two steps (the short-lived freedom of 452, and Vahan's lasting grant about 484), and the church's martyr and feast. It matches mine in substance and leaves out no major contribution: the two histories are ranked third in "What came of him" and carried by the how-we-know sentence in beat 2. Its refusal to call Avarayr a victory or a saving of the faith is what the sources hold. Beats 1-3 tell the heart within about 150 words, so it lands in the first minute.

## Findings

**MINOR 1. The Maccabees gloss says more than Elishe does.** Beat 5 reads "Jews who died rather than break God's law". Elishe says they fought King Antiochus for the law of God and, though they died, were remembered (N 3956-3960; L12 82808-82813). Correction: "the Maccabees, Jews who fought the Greek king Antiochus for God's law and died, but were remembered."

**MINOR 2. Lazar's plea is for a countless crowd, not "their families".** Beat 4 reads "begged him to spare their families from deportation and forced worship". In Lazar the princes speak of "une foule innombrable d'hommes et de femmes, de vieillards et d'enfants" who would be carried off and forced into impiety "de génération en génération" (L12 90692-90698). Vardan gives way "pour sauver les trois nations, et la multitude des hommes et des femmes" (L12 90963-90966). Correction: "begged him to spare countless men, women and children from deportation and forced worship."

**MINOR 3. Elishe's "no telling victors from vanquished" is one rendering, not a shared reading.** The heart and the Avarayr line in Do not say both give it as Elishe's view. Neumann has "There was no distinction, whether of the victors or the vanquished" (N 4182-4183). The French has "les vainqueurs se trouvaient des deux côtés", meaning that brave met brave and there were victors on both sides (L12 83398-83400). Elishe also says the general died and the survivors scattered to the fortresses (L12 83401-83411). A writer might quote the phrase as a verdict on the battle. Correction for the Do not say right-wording: "he fell and his army scattered to the fortresses; in Elishe, brave met brave and there were victors on both sides (L12 83398-83400; Neumann reads 'no distinction')."

**MINOR 4. Elishe's actor and his "perhaps" in 452.** The official who sees the captive priests is "le grand intendant" / "the great Chiliarch" at the king's winter residence (L12 83852-83854; N 4570-4572). His fear of the princes in the fortresses is hedged in the French: "vraisemblablement ... peut-être" (L12 83873-83877). Neumann drops the hedge (N 4574-4575). Correction: in the heart and in beat 6, write "the Persian officials, Elishe says, perhaps fearing the princes who still held the mountain fortresses". Add a Do not say entry: "'the Persians gave in out of fear' as fact → Elishe: perhaps from fear of the princes in the fortresses."

**MINOR 5. "Like his uncle" in the Connection.** Lazar says Vahan bore the name of apostate "à l'exemple de ses aïeux", after his forebears' example (L12 95786-95788). He does not say "his uncle". Beat 7 has it right. Correction: in the Connection bullet, replace "Like his uncle, he outwardly bore" with "After his forebears' example, he outwardly bore". Add a Do not say entry: "'as his uncle had' → 'as his forebears had' (Lazar's word)."

**MINOR 6. The princes' trust is put first in God.** Beat 7 and the Connection give only "trusted in the death of our ancestors". In Lazar they trust "tout d'abord dans la miséricorde de Dieu, dans l'intercession de saint Grégoire et dans la mort de nos ancêtres", and finally in their own death (L12 95976-95981). Correction: add a Do not say entry: "'they trusted above all in their ancestors' martyrdom' → first in God's mercy and Saint Gregory's intercession, then in their ancestors' martyrdom."

**MINOR 7. Sweep: list the RHC file as set aside.** Add `rhc-armeniens_documents-armeniens-t1-fra_academie1869.txt` (6402-6406, 32638-32655) to the sheet's "Set aside": a modern editor's summary that adds no witness.
