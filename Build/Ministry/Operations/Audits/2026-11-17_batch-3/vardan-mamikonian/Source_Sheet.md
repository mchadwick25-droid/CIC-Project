No Armenian text of Elishe or Lazar is held. Every rendering below is from a translation. For Elishe, French (L12) and English (N) were read against each other at every point used.

Witness kinds: **own words** (none survive outside the historians), **contemporary** (Elishe, who says he saw the events; Koriun), **later with a gap** (Lazar, a generation later; Stepanos, about 550 years; the Genealogy, about 500 years; the Synaxarion, medieval), **modern** (Ormanian; editors Neumann, Langlois, Ghesarian).

## 1. Who tells it

**Elishe (Eghishe).** Claims to be an eyewitness. N 1117-1118: "Truly, not of our own free will, do we describe, in lamenting tones, all the miseries of which we ourselves have been eye-witnesses." L12 78374-78376: "nous raconterons les tribulations sans nombre qui nous ont frappés, puisque nous en avons été les témoins oculaires." Writes at the request of a priest of Vardan's house: N 1035, "TO THE PRIEST DAVID, OF THE HOUSE OF THE MAMIGONIANS"; L12 78263-78266, "[écrite] à la demande de David Mamigonien". Writes for the Armenian side and calls the fallen martyrs. Confidence on his eyewitness claim: it is his own claim, Documented as a claim; the census dates him "5th c."; the file's provenance header says his dating is "discussed in scholarship", which this sheet has not examined. Treat the date of the History as **Contested** in strength, not settled.

- Editors' claims, not Elishe's: that Elishe was Vardan's secretary. Neumann preface N 574-575: "He was also secretary to the Armenian General Vartan, of the house of the Mamigonians, a grandson of Sahag the Great." Langlois L12 77882 (introduction): "Selon d'autres écrivains, Elisée aurait été le secrétaire de Vartan". **Editor's; Inferential-Thin.**

**Lazar of Parpi (Ghazar Parpetsi).** Later, writing a generation after (census: "late 5th or early 6th c."). He wrote at the order of Vahan Mamikonian, Vardan's nephew, in his own words: L12 87726-87729 "C'est ainsi que le général des Arméniens, le marzban Vahan, le seigneur puissant des Mamigoniens, nous oblige d'écrire rapidement, dans l'ordre qu'il a adressé à moi Lazare de Pharbe."; L12 87777-87779 "C'est ainsi que Vahan, seigneur des Mamigoniens, général et marzban d'Arménie, m'obligea d'entreprendre une œuvre". **Documented (Lazar's own word).** Langlois's introduction says the same, hedged: L12 87280-87282 "il est probable que Lazare demeura depuis lors dans le palais du marzban où il rédigea, à sa prière, l'histoire". Lazar gives Vardan's personal words and choices at much greater length than Elishe, and calls him "saint" throughout.

**Koriun.** Contemporary of Mesrop, in the Langlois tome II text. Only the Constantinople journey and Sahak's funeral concern Vardan.

**Moses of Khoren.** Book III. His own date is disputed (census "date disputed"; corpus-map row `needs-ruling`). **Contested.**

## 5. Impact, ranked heaviest first (what later witnesses carried on and used)

1. **The next generation fought under his memory and won freedom of worship in 484.** (Defended: the right to keep the faith of their fathers.)
   - Widows raised the martyrs' sons: L12 95201-95205, "Les femmes des martyrs et des prisonniers ... élevaient et instruisaient, avec une grande sollicitude, les fils encore jeunes de ceux qui avaient été martyrisés avec saint Vartan" (later; Documented).
   - The sons prospered, they said, "par l'intercession du sang sacré de leurs aïeux": L12 95283-95288 (later; Documented as Lazar's report).
   - Vahan, eldest son of Hmayeak (Vardan's brother), the most eager of them: L12 95330-95334, "le plus âgé des fils de saint Hemaïag, qui se nommait Vahan" (later; Documented).
   - In the new rising (481) the princes tell Vahan they trust "dans la mort de nos ancêtres qui, par leur martyre, plurent au Christ Sauveur": L12 95976-95983 (later; Documented as Lazar's report).
   - Vahan's first condition to Persia: L12 99485-99498, "c'est de nous garantir les lois de notre pays et de nos péres; de ne faire mage aucun Arménien ... d'enlever les adrouschans (temples du feu) de l'Arménie ... de laisser les chrétiens et les prètres pratiquer librement et sans crainte le culte" (later; Documented).
   - Lazar's own telling of the settlement: King Vagharsh grants all demands, L12 100503-100509 "Qu'aujourd'hui toutes vos demandes vous soient accordées éternellement, confirmées par notre écrit et par notre sceau ; que cet acte de révolte que vous avez fait, quand vous y avez été forcés par la violence de Bérose, et non pas par votre volonté, vous soit pardonné en ce jour"; then names Vahan general-in-chief, L12 100550-100552 "lui conférérent le titre de général en chef de l'Arménie avec les mêmes droits qu'avaient exercés ses ancètres" (later; Documented).
   - The year: editors differ. Langlois's note L12 99215 "Vagharsch occupa le trône de 486 à 490"; his introduction L12 87287-87289 "tous les événements accomplis en Arménie jusqu'à l'an 485"; Ormanian 484/485 (below). **Contested** (the year); about 484 by modern reckoning.
   - Modern outcome: O 2283-2294, "Once again the Armenians were obliged to take up arms under the leadership of Vahan Mamikonian, a nephew of St. Vardan ... the new king Valarse ... He wisely proclaimed religious liberty, and nominated Vahan, first as military commandant (484), then as governor-general of Armenia (485)" (modern; Widely Accepted).
   - Lazar wrote his history at Vahan's order, in his own words (L12 87726-87729, 87777-87779). So the fullest portrait of Vardan comes from his nephew's household.
2. **The church named him martyr and saint, and keeps his feast.**
   - Lazar: L12 91072-91076, "Dieu lui accorda le titre de martyr, qui devait durer éternellement parmi toutes les nations" (later; Documented).
   - Lazar calls him "saint Vartan": L12 91330-91331, 92276, 92353-92354; the fallen are "les Vartaniens" (chapter headings L12 92039, 92319).
   - Stepanos, c. 1000: ST 2789-2792 "den heiligen Wardan ... den Enkel des heiligen Sahak", ST 2807-2810 (fell "für die heilige Kirche und den christlichen Glauben"); ST 195-197, Elishe "der die Taten der Wardanier ... berichtet hat" (later, c. 550 years; Documented).
   - Genealogy of St Gregory, 10th c.: "saint Vartan" (L12 59903-59904; dating line L12 56736-56739) (late; Documented).
   - Kirakos (13th c.): describing Elishe's book, K 6017-6019 "les exploita de VArdan, pctit>fi]a de S. Salue, «t de aw compagnons, qui rmt sacrifié leur vie, par amour pour Ii' Cliriît, et ont été par lui couronnés" (OCR as printed: Vardan, petit-fils de S. Sahac, et de ses compagnons). Of the patriarch Nerses, a hymn writer: K 11281-11288 "Il a arrangé pour ré^HM pliuieiira byiniies ... celle des Nini- vites, des archanges, des saiutâ Vardanans" (OCR as printed: plusieurs hymnes ... des saints Vardanans). Later (13th c.); Documented as Kirakos's report.
   - Ormanian: O 2263-2265, "The death of these martyrs is commemorated in the Armenian calendar on Shrove-Thursday." O 8564-8565, "Vardan and his thousand and thirty-five companions" among the saints (modern; Widely Accepted).
   - Armenian Synaxarion notice of his daughter: PO18 5964-5966, "En ce jour sainte Chouchanik, fille de Vartan, chef des troupes arméniennes, fut mise à mort par son mari Vaskene, gouverneur de la Géorgie" (medieval; Documented that the calendar names her as his daughter).
   - A 1527 colophon invokes "the holy Vardans who rest there": RA 1376-1377. Whether this means Vardan's companions is not stated: Inferential-Thin. Not for the story.
3. **His war became the subject of two classic histories** — Elishe's "History of Vartan" (N 1-2, title) and Lazar's — and later Armenian writers named them as the canon: ST 191-198 (Agathangelos, Moses, Elishe, Lazar) (Documented).
4. **The traitor's memory as its counterpart.** Vasak's name is struck from the altar (N 4887-4889); in Lazar, Vasak himself laments "le sang du saint martyr Vartan" (L12 93311-93316) — Lazar says Vasak's servants reported it (L12 93321-93322). Documented as Lazar's report.

## 7. Where the sources disagree or come late

1. **Who proposed the outward yielding, and Vardan's part.** Elishe: a secret Christian councillor advised "some"; the princes as a body sent word (N 2402-2446). Lazar: the princes proposed it, Vardan refused, then gave way in tears under their oath (L12 90786-90966). Lazar only has Vardan's personal refusal, tears, flight to Byzantine land and recall.
2. **The court answer.** Elishe: collective (N 2330-2363). Lazar: Vardan alone first (L12 90600-90636).
3. **Easter day.** N "great festival day of Easter"; French "samedi de Pâques" (N 2242; L12 80104-80105).
4. **"Religion" or "the Church" was not founded by man** (N 2332; L12 80220-80222).
5. **The night council**: "prayed to him" (God) vs "prayed for him and received him back into communion" (N 2847; L12 81041-81042). Langlois identifies the general as Vardan (editor's).
6. **Letter "to Chalcedon"** is Neumann's gloss; French says the great capital (N 3360; L12 81832-81833).
7. **Army size** 66,000 (N, Ormanian) vs 60,000 (French) (N 3874; L12 82604; O 2257).
8. **The dead**: 1,036 in both witnesses, but the parts sum to 1,027 (N), 1,127 (French Elishe) or 1,026 (Lazar). Ormanian: "Vardan and eight other generals, as well as one thousand and twenty-seven men" (O 2261-2262), and "thousand and thirty-five companions" (O 8564-8565).
9. **Persian losses or strength** "seven times" (N 4231-4232 vs L12 83456-83458).
10. **Date**: 2 June 451 (Langlois notes, Neumann margin) vs 26 May 451 (Ormanian). Neither witness gives a calendar date. Lazar: the armies met at Pentecost (L12 92132-92133).
11. **Elishe's dedication.** N 1080 reads it as about a general: "when they behold the victorious general, who never contemned a foe". French L12 78335-78338 reads it as God: "Celui qui commande la victoire, qui ne se réjouit pas, comme un ennemi, de la défaite des autres". Do not use as a description of Vardan.
12. **Grandson or nephew.** French Koriun and Moses say "neveu" in some places, "petit-fils" in others (L12 55968, 76543); Lazar and the Genealogy are clear on grandson through Sahak's daughter (L12 90891, 59900-59904).
13. **Sides at Avarayr.** Armenians fought on both sides: O 2259-2261 "a large number of Armenians reinforced the enemy's ranks"; the French Elishe counts the 3,544 "du côté des apostats" (L12 83451-83452), Neumann "the unbelievers" (N 4229). Elishe: "les vainqueurs se trouvaient des deux côtés" (L12 83399-83400); N 4182-4183 "There was no distinction, whether of [the] victors or the vanquished" (OCR "lie").
14. **The pretended conversion's framing.** Elishe's narrator: hateful apostasy as the Christians heard it (L12 80607); Elishe's Vardan: we deceived the king (L12 82667-82673); Lazar's Vardan: I denied in appearance, to repent afterwards (L12 91184-91194). Motive: Elishe, a way out for some (L12 80335-80337); Lazar, to spare the deported from forced worship (L12 90692-90698).
15. **Late or editor-only claims**: secretary Elishe (editors); "Vardan the Great" (editor's note); 19 years of leadership (Stepanos alone); emperor naming him general (Moses, disputed date).
