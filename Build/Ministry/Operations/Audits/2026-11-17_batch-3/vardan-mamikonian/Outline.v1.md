# Outline: Vardan Mamikonian

File keys as in `Source_Sheet.md`: **N** = Neumann's English Elishe (`cic/texts/eghishe_history-of-vartan-and-the-armenian-war_neumann1830.txt`); **L12** = Langlois tome II in `cic/texts/langlois_collection-historiens-armenie-tomes1-2-fra_1867-1869.txt` (Elishe in French, Lazar, Koriun, Moses, editors' notes); **O** = Ormanian 1912; **ST** = Stepanos of Taron, German.

- **Length:** about 2½ to 3 minutes (400-450 words). Reason: a major figure, and the story needs the room. Its turn, the princes' outward yielding, is told two ways by the two witnesses and must be told fairly. And the connection, his nephew Vahan's settlement of 484, needs explaining.

- **Setting:**
  - Time: 450-451. The king's order and the court scene fall in 450 (N margin; N 1477, 2242); the battle in spring 451 (N 4175, "It was then spring time"; year from the editors, L12 83415-83417). Spoken: "in the year 451".
  - Place: Armenia under the Persian (Sasanian) kings, after its own kings had fallen (N 1120-1122). The court of Yazdegerd II; the battle on the plain of Avarayr by the river Deghmut (L12 92126-92128). Spoken: "Armenia, then ruled by Persia"; "the plain of Avarayr".
  - Tradition: the Armenian church, taught its faith by Gregory the Illuminator, as Vardan himself says in Lazar (L12 90831-90832; also L12 92189, 92211-92212). Census name "Armenian Christianity after Avarayr". Spoken: "the Armenian church".
  - The other side: the king's religion of the Magi, fire and the sun (N 1470-1473). Spoken: "the Persian fire religion of the Magi" (Zoroastrian is acceptable).

- **Thread:** He left the Armenian church the memory of a general who would not, in the end, trade his faith for safety. His nephew's generation fought under that memory until Persia let them keep their faith.

- **Shape:** Martyr.

- **Openings:**
  1. Lazar's double-edged promise. After the outward yielding, Vardan tells the Persian king he will do "an action whose fame will resound forever", as far as the emperor's court. The king hears loyalty. Lazar adds that God gave him the title of martyr instead (L12 91056-91064, 91072-91076).
  2. Elishe: on the eve of battle the general reads the Maccabees aloud to his army (N 3956-3958; L12 82806-82808).
  3. Lazar: when the princes came home seeming to have changed faith, their children ran from their arms in fear and did not know their faces (L12 91123-91126).

- **Story beats:**
  1. **Who he was.** General of the Armenians and lord of the Mamikonian house (L12 90515). Grandson of the patriarch Sahak, who taught him the Scriptures (L12 90889-90891). As a younger man he travelled with Mesrop, maker of the Armenian alphabet, to the emperor at Constantinople (Koriun, L12 55966-55968). *Documented* (the journey: Koriun; grandfather: Lazar and a 10th-century genealogy, L12 59900-59904).
  2. **The order, 450.** King Yazdegerd II commands every people to worship the sun and feed the holy fire (N 1470-1473). The bishops answer from Artashat: "thou hast the sword, we the neck" (N 2143-2144). The king summons ten princes, Vardan among them, to court at Easter (N 2204-2206, 2242). *Documented.* Why: the king wants one religion in his realm (N 1199-1201, the Magi's counsel: "all tribes and nations of thy kingdom be brought under one law").
  3. **The refusal and the threat.** Before the king, the princes refuse: their church was not founded by man (Elishe, collectively: N 2332; L12 80220-80222). Lazar has Vardan speak first and alone (L12 90623-90626). The king threatens chains, armies with elephants, and the exile of wives and children (N 2372-2383). *Documented.*
  4. **The outward yielding (the turn).** The princes agree to accept the king's religion in appearance only, to get home and save their people. In Elishe a royal councillor, secretly a Christian, proposed it to some of them (N 2402-2407, 2444-2446). In Lazar the princes proposed it; Vardan refused in anger, then wept and gave way when they swore on the Gospel to follow him (L12 90796-90799, 90960-90966). Some, not all, seemed to bow in the fire-temple (L12 90969-90971). *Documented*, with Vardan's personal part in Lazar alone.
  5. **The rising, 450-451.** At home the people reproach the princes, who cannot explain (N 2598-2600). Vardan tells his household he denied Christ "in appearance" only (L12 91185-91193). Led by the priest Leont, the people stone the Magi at a church door (N 2648-2659). The league stones a prince who would not join (N 2891-2893), storms the Persian camp, burns fire-temples, and kills Magi (N 2957-2961; L12 91622-91625). Vardan's force wins in Albania, the Christian land east of Armenia (N 3191-3240). Then Vasak, the Persian-appointed governor, goes over to Persia (N 3284-3288), and the new emperor Marcian refuses help (N 3108-3117). *Documented.*
  6. **Avarayr, 451.** Vardan tells his army they once fought for a mortal king's honours and now fight for the immortal King (N 3881-3897; L12 82614-82638). He gives arms, clothes and horses from his own means (N 3950-3953). He reads them the Maccabees, Jews who died fighting a Greek king rather than break God's law (N 3956-3966). In the night the unbaptized in the army are baptized, and at dawn all take communion (N 4009-4011). By the Deghmut his charge breaks the Persian right wing (L12 83327-83338). He is killed near the elephants (N 4165-4166; L12 83368-83371). Leaderless, the survivors scatter to the strongholds (L12 83402-83411). *Documented.*
  7. **The cost.** Both historians count 1,036 Armenian dead, and 3,544 on the Persian side (N 4227-4229; L12 92386-92391). His brother Hmayeak falls soon after (L12 92519-92523). The patriarch Joseph, Leont and other priests are taken to Persia and killed (O 2273-2276). Vasak dies in disgrace, his name struck from the altar (N 4887-4889). *Documented* (the 1,036 as the historians' count).

- **What came of them** (heaviest first; about a third of the story):
  1. **His nephew's generation won what he died for.** The widows of the martyrs raised their sons (L12 95201-95205). The eldest son of Hmayeak, Vahan, led a new rising thirty years on (L12 95333-95334). The princes told him they trusted "in the death of our ancestors" who pleased Christ by martyrdom (L12 95976-95983). Vahan's first condition to Persia: no Armenian made a Magus, the fire-temples removed, Christians free to worship (L12 99485-99498). The Persian king granted religious liberty and made Vahan governor, 484-485 (O 2283-2294). **Defended: the right to keep the faith of their fathers.** *Documented* (Lazar); *Widely Accepted* (outcome, Ormanian).
  2. **The church made him its martyr and saint.** Lazar: "the title of martyr" (L12 91072-91076), and "saint Vartan" throughout (L12 92276, 92353-92354). About 550 years later Stepanos of Taron calls him "the holy Vardan", who fell "for the holy church and the Christian faith" (ST 2789-2792, 2807-2810). The Armenian calendar keeps the martyrs of Avarayr on a Thursday before Lent, and counts "Vardan and his thousand and thirty-five companions" among its saints (O 2263-2265, 8564-8565). *Documented* (Lazar, Stepanos); *Widely Accepted* (feast, Ormanian).
  3. **His war became a classic of Armenian writing.** Elishe wrote the "History of Vartan" at the request of a priest of his house (N 1035); Lazar wrote the second account; Stepanos lists both among the great Armenian historians (ST 191-198). *Documented.*

- **Quotes** (none from the Armenian original, which is not held; all rendered from Langlois's French, with Elishe checked against Neumann's English). French is given as the OCR reads, with only run-together words parted (L12 82668-82671 "nous.le", "ua", "ordresiniques", "estle"; L12 91076 "parini" for parmi):
  1. **Vardan before the king** (Lazar, L12 90623-90626). French: « Mais abandonner une religion que j'ai reçue de Dieu dès mon enfance, et la changer par crainte des hommes, cela n'est pas possible! »
     Rendering: "But I received this religion from God as a child. To abandon it, and change it out of fear of men, is not possible!"
     Job: his stand, in the one witness who gives him his own words. Credit it to Lazar.
  2. **Vardan to his army before Avarayr** (Elishe, L12 82667-82673; agrees with N 3930-3933). French: « pendant un certain temps, nous trompâmes ce prince injuste et nous le surprimes comme un faible enfant, comme si, en apparence, nous avions exécuté des ordres iniques. Le Seigneur cependant est le témoin caché de nos pensées; [il sait] que nous avons persévéré sans nous séparer de lui. »
     Rendering: "For a time we deceived that unjust prince. We caught him out like a feeble child, as if we had outwardly carried out his wicked orders. But the Lord is the hidden witness of our thoughts. He knows that we held firm and never left him."
     Job: names the outward yielding in Vardan's own reported voice and turns it into the reason for the battle. Credit it to Elishe.
  3. **Lazar on his name** (L12 91072-91076). French: « Mais Dieu leur déroba la promesse que Vartan, général des Arméniens, avait donnée de rendre son nom célèbre, puisque Dieu lui accorda le titre de martyr, qui devait durer éternellement parmi toutes les nations. »
     Rendering: "But God took from them the promise that Vardan, general of the Armenians, had given: to make his name famous. For God gave him the title of martyr, which would last forever among all nations."
     Job: someone else's words about him; pairs with Opening 1 ("them" is the king and his nobles) and carries the turn to how he was remembered.

- **Connection to carry into the story:** From Vardan to his nephew **Vahan Mamikonian**, and through him to the settlement of 484. The listener needs four things, told through what people did:
  - Vahan was the son of Vardan's brother Hmayeak, who also died in the war (L12 92519-92523, 95333-95334).
  - The martyrs' widows raised the sons of the fallen (L12 95201-95205).
  - About thirty years after Avarayr, Vahan led a new rising. His companions said they put their trust in the martyrdom of their ancestors (L12 95976-95983).
  - Vahan's first demand of Persia was exactly what Vardan's generation had died refusing: that no Armenian be made to serve the fire religion, and that Christians worship freely (L12 99485-99498). Persia granted religious liberty and made Vahan governor in 484-485 (O 2283-2294).
  Also say once, plainly, that Lazar wrote his history for Vahan (an editor's statement, L12 87282). The fullest picture of Vardan therefore comes from his nephew's circle. No "this matters because" sentence: let Vahan's demand land after Vardan's refusal.

- **Links:**
  - Mesrop Mashtots (`mesrop-mashtots`): met; travelled with him to Constantinople (L12 55966-55968).
  - Koriun (`koriun`): wrote of that journey (L12 55966-55968).
  - Elishe (`elishe`): wrote his life (N; L12 78263ff).
  - Lazar Parpetsi (`lazar-parpetsi`): wrote his life (L12 90515-92404).
  - Moses of Khoren (`moses-of-khoren`): named him in his history (L12 76302-76543).
  - Council of Chalcedon (`council-of-chalcedon-451`): same year only; Armenia took no part (O 2448-2452). Not a link to tell.

- **Who tells it:**
  - **Elishe**, an Armenian churchman who says he saw these events himself (N 1117-1118). He wrote for a priest of Vardan's own family (N 1035) and calls the fallen martyrs. The listener should hear early that the story comes from his side. His date is discussed by scholars; do not call him an eyewitness as settled fact. Say "he says he saw it".
  - **Lazar of Parpi**, writing a generation later, by his editor's account for Vardan's nephew (L12 87282). He alone gives Vardan's personal refusal, tears and flight. Place him where beat 4 splits.
  - **Ormanian**, a modern Armenian patriarch, for the feast and the 484 outcome only.

- **Do not say:**
  - "Vardan came up with the plan to pretend" → *the princes (Lazar) or a secret Christian at court (Elishe) proposed it; in Lazar he refused, then wept and gave way.*
  - "They all bowed to the fire" → *Lazar: some, not all, seemed to bow* (L12 90970).
  - "66,000 Armenians" as fact → *"some sixty thousand", by Elishe's count* (French 60,000, English 66,000).
  - "1,036 died with him" as a firm total → *"the historians count 1,036"*.
  - "the Persians lost seven times as many" → *leave out; the two renderings disagree.*
  - "on 26 May" or "on 2 June" → *"in the spring of 451"*, or "at Pentecost" with Lazar.
  - "they wrote to the Council of Chalcedon" → *Neumann's gloss; they wrote to the capital.*
  - "Avarayr was really a victory" or "a defeat that won the war" → *a defeat; the outcome is told only through Vahan's settlement, 33 years later.*
  - "Vardan the Great" → *a later name, in an editor's note; not needed.*
  - "Elishe, his secretary" → *editors' claim only; omit.*
  - "Armenia, the first Christian nation" → *not in these sources; omit.*
  - "the Persians' Zoroastrianism was false/evil" in the narrator's voice → *the witnesses' hostility stays theirs ("as Elishe tells it, the king…").*
  - Any age, look, inner feeling or family scene not in the outline (no "with a heavy heart" beyond Lazar's tears).
  - "Vardan's speech" as exact words he spoke → *"as Elishe/Lazar gives his words".*

- **Leave out:**
  - Lazar's claim that Vahan Amatuni pushed for revolt out of hatred for Vasak (L12 91402-91411): a side quarrel.
  - Leont's radiance seen by Mesrop and his pupils (L12 92172-92178): a wonder story about another man.
  - Elishe's eighth chapter on the captive priests: a different story.
  - Stepanos's "19 years" of leadership and Moses's imperial commission: single, late or disputed.
  - The 1527 colophon's "holy Vardans": unclear meaning.
  - His daughter Shushanik, a martyr of the Georgian church (PO18 5964-5966): a fine thread, but a second story.
  - Keep, do not soften: the stoning of the Magi and of a dissenting prince, the killing of Magi, and the burning of temples. They are told as plainly as the courage.
