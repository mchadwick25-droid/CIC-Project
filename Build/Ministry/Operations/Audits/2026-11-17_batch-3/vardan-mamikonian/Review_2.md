## 1. The test, from one hearing

- **Who:** Vardan Mamikonian, general of Christian Armenia when Armenia was ruled by Persia, in the mid-400s.
- **What happened:** In 450 the Persian king ordered every people to take up the fire religion of the Magi. Vardan refused at court to change his faith, the princes yielded in outward show, then rose against the order. He fell at Avarayr in spring 451. A short-lived toleration followed and broke down.
- **Why the connection matters:** His nephew Vahan, raised among the martyrs' sons and also outwardly bearing the Persian religion's name, rose again and won from the Persian king, under seal, exactly what Vardan's generation had refused to give up: no Armenian made a Magus, and Christians free to worship.

**The heart of his life** (written from the census entry and his "known for" before judging the brief): Vardan is the Armenian general who, after yielding in outward show at the Persian court, led the princes, priests and people in an armed rising to defend Christian worship against the Persian king's imposed religion, and fell at Avarayr in 451. The Armenian church keeps him and his companions as its martyrs. What his stand won came later: his nephew Vahan, a generation on, gained lasting freedom of worship under seal.

**Where a listener may get lost:**
- After the heart, the story steps back in time with no marker ("The king threatened chains..."). A listener who has just heard that Vardan fell at Avarayr is now back at court. It is followable, because "the king" ties back to the opening, but it costs a beat.

It does not read as a children's book and does not talk down.

## Findings

**MINOR 1. The captive priests, and "It did not hold".** The brief: "It did not hold: the patriarch and his priests were taken to Persia and killed." In Elishe, the priests were already in chains on the way to the king's winter residence when the official arrived and gave his gentle orders (L12 83850-83856). Ormanian dates their martyrdom to 454 (O 2273-2276). So the killing follows the toleration, but the arrest does not. Change to: "It did not hold: the patriarch and his priests, already captive, were killed in Persia." No outline change needed beyond a Do not say line, if the writer revises: "'the priests were then taken to Persia' after the order of 452 → they were already captive; they were killed in Persia (454, Ormanian)."

**MINOR 2. What Vardan refused, and what the others begged.** "In Lazar, Vardan refused in anger, and gave way in tears only when the others begged him to spare their people." It does not say he refused the pretence, and "spare their people" drops what they were to be spared from. The outline (after Outline_Check_2 MINOR 2) carries "deportation and forced worship" (L12 90692-90698, 90963-90966). Change to: "In Lazar, Vardan at first refused in anger to pretend, and gave way in tears only when the others begged him to spare countless people from exile and forced worship."

**MINOR 3. "The Maccabees" unglossed.** The outline gives "Jews who fought the Greek king Antiochus for God's law and died, but were remembered" (N 3956-3960; L12 82808-82813). The brief's "who died for God's law" also leaves out that they fought, which is the point of reading it to an army. Change to: "the Maccabees, Jews who fought a Greek king for God's law and died, but were remembered."

**MINOR 4. "Within a year".** The outline has "within about a year", and the year 452 is the editor's (L12 83858-83860, 92428-92430). Change to "Within about a year". "The Persian side" could also be "Persia's new governor" (Adrormizt in Lazar, L12 92410-92414, and the official in Elishe), which a listener can picture.

**MINOR 5. The step back after the heart.** After "the freedom to keep that faith", the next sentence returns to the court with no time marker. The outline rules out "But first". A light anchor such as "At court, the king threatened chains..." would carry the listener back without the banned frame.
