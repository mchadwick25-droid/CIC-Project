# Outline check: Moses the Black

**Verdict: FIX** (three blocking findings, eight minor).

Checked against LH 185, 197, 331–337, 361, 467, 657, 711, 713, 921; PAR2 88, 96, 164, 1028, 1464, 2766–2768, 2850, 2874; SOZ 39459–39461, 39497–39525; ESX-M 37092–37197; NIK 41885–41957. All three quotes are verbatim to LH 335 and LH 337. The renderings match my own ("Since I may not hurt anyone, what do you want me to do with these?"; "If a man so great and powerful in robbery has feared God, why should we put off our salvation?"; "I will never stop until the demons stop appearing"). Setting, spoken tradition name and shape (Monk or ascetic) are sound.

## BLOCKING

**B1. Length: the record is not thin.** The Template allows 1 minute only "when the record is thin". This record holds one full narrative chapter with a dozen scenes (LH 331–337), sayings that name him in two places in the collection, and two church calendars. The outline itself plans seven beats, three impact items and three quotes, which 150 words cannot carry, least of all with a third for impact. The short length is also the stated reason for cutting the insult sayings (B2).
*Correction:* "**Length:** 2 minutes (about 300 words). One full narrative chapter (LH 331–337), later sayings that name him, and two church calendars. Do not stretch past 2."

**B2. The "Ethiopian" insults must go in.** The Leave out line is plain: "Never the record's force, coercion or conflict: those are told as plainly as its virtues." The Standard's witness line says the same: a hostile view "is told openly and credited to its source", and the narrator "never softens the record by leaving out its force or conflict." Contempt aimed at him for his colour is conflict in the record. Unlike the "Abba Moses" sayings set aside under Leave out, these sayings name him: "this Ethiopian" (PAR2 164) and "O Ethiopian" (PAR2 1028). Later witnesses carried them on. PAR2 2874 retells the first as a lesson on holding back anger. The Ethiopian Synaxarium retells the sanctuary test (ESX-M 37139–37147: "Why have ye brought this black man here? Take him away."). A late source is a reason to credit the story, not to drop it. The outline already draws on the same collection for its impact item 2. "Cannot be told within one minute" falls with B1.
*Correction:* delete the fifth Leave out line. Insert a beat between beats 6 and 7:
"**Contempt.** The sayings remember him insulted for his colour. At a gathering at Scete the fathers said, 'Why doth this Ethiopian come and go in our midst?' He kept silent. Afterwards he said, 'Although I was afraid I uttered not a word' (PAR2 164; retold at PAR2 2874 with 'troubled'). When he was one of the clergy, the bishop wanted to test him and told the clergy to drive him out of the sanctuary. They said, 'Get outside, O Ethiopian.' He said to himself, 'They have treated thee rightly, O thou whose skin is dark and black; thou shalt not go back as if thou wert a [white] man' (PAR2 1028; the bracket is Budge's). Documented as what the sayings say; the events Inferential-Thin. Tell this plainly, in the sayings' voice."
Add to Do not say:
- "The fathers were testing him" at PAR2 164 → only PAR2 1028 says it was a test, and that one was the bishop's.
- "He forgave them", "he rose above it", or any feeling → the sayings give only his silence, his fear and his words against himself.
- "The bishop then made him a priest" or "you have become white within and without" → both are from the Ethiopian Synaxarium only (ESX-M 37147–37150). At PAR2 1028, "thou art wholly white" is the bishop's remark about his white garment.
- Do not soften "dark and black" or leave out his words against himself. Do not call the insult racism in the narrator's voice: tell what was said and credit it to the sayings.

**B3. The martyrdom: one credited sentence, not silence.** A doubtful claim may be left out of the beats, and the Template allows that. But the outline tells the feasts (impact 3), and the Ethiopian Church keeps its feast as the day "Saint Abba moses the Black became a martyr" (ESX-M 37094). To name that feast and stay silent on what it marks misleads. The Standard also says, "Say plainly what is uncertain... do not bury it." The outline's own uncertainty is the reason to tell it with its witness, not to drop it. The sheet also missed one fact. Nikodemos's own text says he "fell asleep" in old age, aged seventy (NIK 41954, "έκοιμήΟη έν ττ] άσχήσει"), and keeps him as a holy father (ὁσίου), not a martyr. Only his footnote (NIK 41935) points to a martyrdom.
*Correction:* add to the Father beat: "Palladius, the earliest witness, says only that he died at Scete at seventy-five. The Ethiopian calendar of saints remembers raiders killing him there with seven brothers (ESX-M 37094; 37163–37188). A saying tells the same of an 'Abba Moses' it does not name further (PAR2 1464). The Greek calendar says he died in old age (NIK 41954). Contested." Replace the martyrdom line under Do not say with: "'He was martyred' or 'raiders killed him' as fact or in the narrator's voice → 'a later Ethiopian tradition remembers him killed by raiders; Palladius says only that he died at Scete.'" Delete the first Leave out line. Keep it to one or two sentences. Under the Martyr shape's trap, the death must not swallow the story.

## MINOR

**M1. Beat 5 merges two advisers.** Isidore told him to endure. The vow to stand all night came on the advice of "another one of the saints" (LH 337 [7]). *Correction:* "Isidore told him to endure. He ate twelve ounces of dry bread a day, worked hard and said fifty prayers. Another elder told him to keep watch and pray, so for six years he stood all night."

**M2. Beat 6 turns reported speech into fact.** *Correction:* "About two months later, when Isidore asked him, he said that he no longer suffered anything" (LH 337 [11]).

**M3. Beat 4 turns his words into a rule.** "A monk was not allowed to hurt anyone" → "He said he was not allowed to hurt anyone" (LH 335; SOZ: "he did not consider himself authorized to punish any one").

**M4. The Connection adds a cause.** "Seventy disciples follow from the same pattern" is not in the source. Change it to "He also left seventy disciples (LH 337); Palladius does not say what drew them." The source says the robbers turned "because of his conversion", so replace "His own past, and not a sermon, is what moved them" with "What moved them was his conversion, set against the robber they knew he had been."

**M5. Impact ranking.** The use later witnesses made most of is "proof of what repentance does." Palladius states it as his purpose (LH 333). Nikodemos repeats it (NIK 41915–41919), the Ethiopian Synaxarium closes on it (ESX-M 37189–37192), and PAR2 2850 uses it. *Correction:* rank (1) proof of repentance; (2) disciples and followers; (3) a case for teaching, adding PAR2 2874's lesson from his silence under insult; (4) feasts.

**M6. Add to Do not say: Isidore.** There are two. "Isidore, priest of Scete" is not the Isidore whom Palladius met at Alexandria in 388 (LH 197; LH 711 lists three Isidores). Do not merge them. Also: "the robbers became his disciples" or "joined his monastery" → they "renounced the world" (LH 335).

**M7. Who tells it.** Once B2 is in, replace "not used in the outline beats" with: "Later; used for the insult beat and for teaching. Only sayings that name him 'the Black' or 'the Ethiopian' are his."

**M8. Library sweep.** I spot-checked it, and it holds. A grep of `cic/texts/` and `cic/corpus-map/` returns the same files. Three more hits (anf08, Ephraim's Prose Refutations, Teresa's Way of Perfection) are about other men named Moses. The Arabic Jacobite and Armenian synaxary volumes do not hold him. The sheet missed two things: the sanctuary-test story in ESX-M (37139–37150), and Nikodemos's own "fell asleep" (NIK 41954). Both are covered above.
