# Review: Moses the Black

**Verdict: REVISE** (one blocking finding, a one-phrase fix; four minor).

## The test, from one hearing

- **Who:** Moses the Black, a black slave from Egypt, called an Ethiopian, who was said to have been a robber and killer. He became a monk and then a priest among the desert fathers at Scete in the late 300s.
- **What happened:** he turned to a monastery after one of his raids. He carried four robbers who attacked him to the church instead of hurting them. He fought lust for years, until Isidore sent him to Communion. He was insulted and driven out for his colour and blamed himself. He left seventy disciples and died at Scete, though a later tradition says raiders killed him.
- **Why the connection matters:** the robbers knew who he had been, so his conversion made them give up their own lives of crime. He left seventy disciples after him.

**One-hearing test alone: PASS.** All three lines come clear on one hearing. Where I got lost: "told him his dreams had ended" (paragraph 4), because the dreams were never mentioned before. Nothing reads as a children's book or talks down.

## Scores

- **Voice 4.** Adult documentary register throughout. The sayings paragraph keeps its witness and the self-blaming words without commentary. The flat "Later sayings recall..." opening of paragraph 5 is the one place it reads like a report.
- **Craft 4.** Strong opening scene, and the time, place and tradition arrive early. The robbers' scene carries the connection where it is strongest, in their own words. It ends on the death in two balanced sentences with no moral.
- **Rigor 4.** Witnesses are named where needed: "they used to say", "was said to", "Palladius says", "later sayings", "a later Ethiopian tradition". It honours the Do not say list on murder, the robber band, origin, conversion cause, the demon, the martyrdom, Isidore and the disciples. Held down by one detail that is not in the source (B1).

## Findings

**B1 (BLOCKING). "Swam the flooded Nile at night" is not in the source.** LH 333 says the shepherd "had one night with his dogs impeded him in a project". It does not time the swim. The "night" belongs to the shepherd's foiling, and the opening line moves it onto the crossing. The outline left room for this: its Opening 1 reads "A robber swims the flooded Nile at night". *Change:* "They used to say he once swam the flooded Nile, his sword in his mouth, to kill a shepherd who had foiled him one night." Alternatively, drop "at night".
*Do not say (add to the outline, and correct Opening 1):* "He swam the Nile at night" → the shepherd foiled him "one night"; the swim itself is not timed (LH 333).

**M1 (MINOR). "His dreams had ended" lands without set-up.** The dreams are never introduced, so a listener hears a new idea at the moment of resolution. *Change:* name them once in the fight, for example "his fight with lust, and the dreams that came with it, lasted years". LH 337 has "troubled by dreams".

**M2 (MINOR). The first insult is softened.** "When the fathers asked why this Ethiopian came among them" makes a question out of what PAR2 164 calls being "treated ... with contumely". The outline's beat begins "remember him insulted for his colour". *Change:* "Later sayings recall that the fathers at Scete insulted him: 'Why does this Ethiopian come and go among us?' He kept silent." Optionally keep the sanctuary rebuke "Get outside, Ethiopian" (PAR2 1028), so the second scene's insult is heard and not only implied.

**M3 (MINOR). "Later sayings recall" reads as a report about the record.** A plainer form fits the narrator's voice: "In sayings collected later, ..." Optional, and it goes with M2.

**M4 (MINOR). Length is at the outline's ceiling.** The fixes above add a few words. Trim to keep near 300. "Palladius, the bishop who wrote the earliest account" could lose "who wrote the earliest account" here, since the death sentence already calls him the earliest witness.
