# Heart test: Moses the Black (2026-11-18)

Tester did not write the story. Read: Standard.md, census entry `moses-the-black`, Outline.md, Source_Sheet.md, `moses-the-black.brief.txt`. Not read: Review.md, Recheck*.md, other `.brief.*` files.

## 1. The heart, before reading the story

From the census (knownFor: "A house-servant driven out for robbery who became a monk at Scete in Egypt, a priest and a teacher of seventy disciples"; role "Monk and priest"; legacy: seventy disciples, sayings kept under his name):

**Heart:** a slave driven out for crime, said to be a robber and killer, turned to God and became a father of monks at Scete: a priest who left seventy disciples. His life was told from the first as proof of what repentance can do, and the robbers he caught gave up the world because of what he had become.

**Against the sources.** The outline holds all of it:
- Thread: "a slave turned robber became a father of monks at Scete ... told as proof of what repentance can do" (LH 333).
- Beat 8 and What came of them 2: priest, seventy disciples (LH 337; Sozomen's "numerous eminent disciples", SOZ 39525). Documented.
- What came of them 1: Palladius tells the crimes "to show the virtue of his repentance" (LH 333); Nikodemos and the Ethiopian Synaxarium repeat it (Source_Sheet, Impact 1).
- Connection: the four robbers "renounced the world because of his conversion" (LH 335).
- Nothing he defended; no dispute. The sayings add his silence under contempt as a teaching case (Impact 3).

## 2. The story, heard once

Length 345 words (outline: about 300).

**Words 1-150** carry: the Nile swim as report (1-27); desert fathers, late 300s, Palladius, "Ethiopian by race, and black", house-servant, said to have killed and led robbers (28-77); conscience-stricken, "gave himself to a monastery" (78-90); the four robbers carried to church, his "not allowed to hurt anyone", their confession (91-150). The robbers' line, "why should we put off our salvation?", ends at word 157, just past the minute.

**What lands:** the turn from robber to monk, and the connection (robbers who knew him followed him). Time, place-region and tradition arrive early and lightly.

**What does not land in the first minute:** what he became. In the first 150 words he only "gave himself to a monastery". Scete is first named at word 152. "Priest" and "seventy disciples" (the census's knownFor and the outline's thread, "a father of monks") come at word 277, compressed into one clause beside his death. The weight the outline gives the life, that it was told as proof of what repentance does, is carried only implicitly by the robbers' quote, and the listener has not yet heard that the robber became a teacher of others.

**Opening heard once:** plain enough. "A shepherd who had foiled him one night" is slightly opaque but survives. "He was said to have killed and led a band of robbers" can be heard as one verb pair; acceptable.

**Where time goes:** how-we-know is light and well placed ("Bishop Palladius, his first biographer"; "Palladius says" at the demon and the death). Colour: the Nile opening (27 words) does its job of drawing in. The heaviest spend outside the heart is the closing barbarian saying (about 50 words, 286-345), which takes the last stretch from the outline's own "keep it to one or two sentences" death beat.

**Aside, for the reviewer (outside this test, not checked further):** the closing saying is PAR2 1464, which Source_Sheet line 80 lists under "Sayings naming only 'Abba Moses' (not used as his)", and the outline's Leave out excludes such sayings; the brief also calls it "an early saying" where the outline dates the collection to the 600s. "They confessed and became monks" sits near the Do not say entry ("renounced the world").

## 3. Verdict: FIX

**Missing from the first minute:** that the robber became a father of monks at Scete, a priest who left seventy disciples, and that his life was told as proof of what repentance can do. Scete itself.

**What in the outline can carry it:**
- Thread and beat 8 (LH 337): move "at Scete, in the desert of Egypt" and "in time he became a priest and left seventy disciples" up into the conversion paragraph, so the turn reads robber, monk, father of monks before the robbers scene shows why others followed.
- What came of them 1 (LH 333): one clause crediting Palladius's purpose, that he told the crimes "to show the virtue of his repentance", gives the life its weight in his voice.
- Room: the closing barbarian saying (about 50 words) can shrink to the outline's one-sentence death beat, which pays for the move and brings the length back to about 300.
