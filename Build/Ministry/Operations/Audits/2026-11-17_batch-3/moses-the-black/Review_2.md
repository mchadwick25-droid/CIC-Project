# Review 2: Moses the Black

Fresh draft from the revised outline. Read against `Outline.md`, `Source_Sheet.md`, `Outline_Check_2.md`, the census entry (`cic-website/data/world-census.json`, id `moses-the-black`) and the held texts. Heard in Neo's voice, about 150 words a minute. `Heart_Test.md` was not read.

## 1. The test, from one hearing

- **Who:** Moses, a black house-slave, "an Ethiopian by race", driven out for crime and said to be a killer and robber chief, who became one of the great desert fathers of Scete in Egypt, a priest with seventy disciples.
- **What happened:** after a turn Palladius gives no cause for, he entered a monastery; he caught four robbers and would not hurt them; he fought his old lust for years until Isidore sent him to Communion; the monks' sayings remember him insulted for his colour and silent under it; he died at Scete at seventy-five.
- **Why the connection matters:** his change drew others. The robbers he caught left the world "because of his conversion", reasoning that if a robber this great had feared God they had no excuse to wait.

All three lines came clear from one hearing.

**The heart, from the census and what he is known for (written before checking the story's first minute):** a house-servant driven out for crime who became a monk and priest at Scete and left seventy disciples. His life was told from the first as proof of what repentance can do (Palladius's own stated reason, LH 333), and his contribution is the people drawn after that change, beginning with the robbers he caught, and the pattern of a hard, long repentance later teachers used.

**Does the first minute carry it?** Yes. By word 82 the listener has heard that the desert fathers counted him among their great ones, that he was a priest at Scete with seventy disciples, that he began as a slave driven out for immorality and robbery and said to have killed, and that Palladius tells the crimes "to show what his repentance could do". The robber scene starts at word 115, and the connection lands at word 198 (about 1:20), inside the outline's range. The heart gets its weight: it is the opening contrast, not a passing clause.

**Where I got lost:** paragraph 2, "one of his adventures left him conscience-stricken ... though Palladius names no cause." Heard once, the adventure sounds like the cause, so "names no cause" seems to contradict it.

**Listener read at grade 6.4.** It does not sound flat and does not talk down. The low score comes from short, common words, not from short thoughts: sentences carry chains ("Moses tied them up, carried them on his back to the church, and asked the brothers..."), and the turns are marked with "Yet", "In time", "Then", "Afterwards". Paragraph 5 runs closest to a list of single acts ("He kept silent... Once a bishop put him out..."), but it holds. Nothing reads as a children's book.

## 3. The race-related lines

- **"An Ethiopian by race and black" (P1)** is Palladius's phrase, word for word, said in the narrator's voice. Palladius is named in the next sentence as the one telling these things, so it is heard as the record's description. Acceptable; finding 4 offers a cleaner credit.
- **"Asking why this Ethiopian came among them" (P5)** is credited ("Sayings from the 600s remember"), but it drops the force. PAR2 164 says the fathers "treated him with contumely". Heard once, "asking why" sounds like a question, not an insult. Finding 1.
- **"Since his skin was dark and black, and he must not act like a white man" (P5)** is credited to Moses ("Moses told himself"), but it is not faithfully reported. Finding 2.

## Verdict: REVISE

## Findings

1. **BLOCKING: the fathers' contempt is softened (P5).** PAR2 164: "they treated him with contumely, saying, 'Why doth this Ethiopian come and go in our midst?'" The brief says only that they were "asking why this Ethiopian came among them". The Standard says never soften the record by leaving out its force, and the outline's beat 6 says he was "insulted for his colour". Change to, for example: "Sayings gathered in the 600s remember the fathers of Scete scorning him: why, they asked, did this Ethiopian come and go among them?" Keep the narrator from calling it racism (Do not say).
   *Do not say line to add:* "Asked why this Ethiopian came among them" without the contempt → "they scorned him" or "they treated him with contempt, saying..." (PAR2 164, "contumely").

2. **BLOCKING: his words against himself are reshaped (P5).** PAR2 1028: "They have treated thee rightly, O thou whose skin is dark and black; thou shalt not go back as if thou wert a [white] man." The brief says "they had treated him rightly, since his skin was dark and black, and he must not act like a white man." Three shifts: (a) "since" turns an address ("O thou whose skin...") into a stated reason, a cause the Standard says must be in the source; (b) "go back as if" (re-enter the sanctuary he was driven from) becomes "act like", a general rule of conduct, which heard aloud lands as a rule about race rather than one act; (c) "white" is Budge's bracketed supply, and the held Latin parallel (Dorotheus, Doctrina XIV, Source_Sheet line 139) has "non homo", "not a man", so the word is the translator's, not plainly the saying's. Spoken without a sign, it becomes the record's word. Change to the outline's own words in quotation, after the driven-out line, for example: "Driven out, he said to himself: 'They have treated thee rightly, O thou whose skin is dark and black; thou shalt not go back as if thou wert a white man.'" That keeps "dark and black" and his words against himself, as the Do not say list requires. If the old English is too heavy, render it as quote 4 in the outline first (Opus authors the rendering), keeping "go back" and the vocative, and let the card carry the note on Budge's bracket.
   *Do not say lines to add:* "since his skin was dark and black" → the saying addresses him ("O thou whose skin is dark and black"); it gives no reason (PAR2 1028). "He must not act like a white man" → "he must not go back in as if he were a [white] man"; "white" is Budge's supply, and a held Latin parallel reads "not a man" (Dorotheus, Doctrina XIV).

3. **MINOR: "though Palladius names no cause" (P2)** sits against "one of his adventures left him conscience-stricken" and confuses on one hearing. Change to: "In time, Palladius says only, one of his adventures left him conscience-stricken, and he gave himself to a monastery."

4. **MINOR: "an Ethiopian by race and black" (P1)** would be heard more surely as the record's with a credit in place: "Palladius calls him an Ethiopian by race, and black." This also lets the next sentence drop the repeated name.

5. **MINOR: "and Moses was said to have killed" (P1)** hangs without an object when heard. "And he was said to have killed men" goes past LH 333 ("the length of murder"); keep "he was even said to have murdered" or "said to have gone as far as murder".

6. **MINOR: "Sayings from the 600s" (P5)** suggests the sayings began then. The 600s is the compilation; the sayings are older (Source_Sheet row PAR2). "Sayings gathered in the 600s" fixes it.

7. **MINOR: "broke into his cell" (P3)** → "attacked him in his cell" (LH 335).

8. **MINOR: "a bishop put him out of the sanctuary" (P5)** → the bishop had the clergy drive him out (PAR2 1028).
