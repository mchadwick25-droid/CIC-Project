# Outline: Moses the Black

File paths are under `cic/texts/`. LH = `palladius_lausiac-history_clarke1918.txt`; PAR2 = `anan-isho_paradise-v2-sayings_budge1907.txt`; SOZ = `npnf202_socrates-sozomen-ecclesiastical-histories.xml`; ESX-M = `ethiopian-synaxarium_book-of-saints-ethiopian-church-v2-v4-microform_budge1928.txt`; NIK = `nikodemos-hagiorite_synaxaristes-vol2-mar-aug-grc_1900.txt`. Full lines and verbatim text are in `Source_Sheet.md` (the census check is rows H1–H7).

- **The heart of their life:** a slave driven out for crime became one of the great fathers of the monks at Scete, in the Egyptian desert. He was a priest there, and he left seventy disciples (LH 337, Documented). A saying remembers a brother coming to him for a rule of life (PAR2 88, Documented as the saying says). What he made was not a book or a house but a life others followed: robbers he had caught left the world "because of his conversion" (LH 335). And his life was told from the first as proof of what repentance can do. Palladius says he tells the crimes "to show the virtue of his repentance" (LH 333), and later calendars, Greek and Ethiopian, kept it for that (NIK 41915–41919; ESX-M 37189–37192). The census says "driven out for robbery"; the source says "immorality and brigandage" (LH 333). "Teacher" rests on the sayings, not on Palladius. He founded nothing and defended nothing in the record.

- **Length:** 2 minutes, about 300 words (Neo, about 150 a minute). One narrative chapter (LH 331–337), a handful of sayings that name him, and two calendars. Not a major figure; the record does not need more room. The heart must be told by word 150.

- **Setting:**
  - *Time:* no year is held. Palladius knew the Egyptian monks from 388 (LH 197, note LH 713) and wrote in 419–420 (LH 657); by then Moses was dead and "numbered among the great ones of the fathers" (LH 337). Spoken: "around the year 400" or "in the late 300s". The researcher's inference (Inferential-Thin): keep it loose.
  - *Place:* Scete, a monks' settlement in the Egyptian desert (LH 337). His robber days were by the Nile (LH 333). Spoken: "Scete, in the desert of Egypt".
  - *Tradition:* the desert monks of Egypt. Spoken: "the desert fathers". "Abba", meaning father, is the title for a proven elder, not an office (census entry desert-monasticism).

- **Shape:** Monk or ascetic.

- **Openings:**
  1. *Recommended.* The contrast, said plainly: the man the desert monks counted among their great fathers, a priest at Scete with seventy disciples, had once been a slave thrown out of his master's house for crime (LH 333; 337). It says who he is in the first breath and leads straight into the heart.
  2. The robbers' own words, once they knew who had caught them: "If a man so great and powerful in robbery has feared God, why should we put off our salvation?" (LH 335). Strong, but it needs the set-up first, so it fits better as beat 3 than as the first line.

- **Story beats:**
  1. **Who he became, and why his life was told** (heart; first 60–80 words). A monk of Scete, a priest who left seventy disciples, counted among the great fathers (LH 337). Before that, a house-servant whom Palladius calls "an Ethiopian by race and black" (credit it to him), whose master drove him out for "immorality and brigandage"; he was even said to have murdered (LH 333). Palladius, a bishop who wrote about the desert monks around 420, says he tells the crimes to show the worth of the repentance (LH 333). Documented; the killings themselves Contested ("he was said to").
  2. **Robber, and the turn.** "They used to say" he led a band of robbers. One line of colour is enough: once he swam the flooded Nile with his sword in his teeth to kill a shepherd who had foiled him (LH 333; Documented as report). Then, "conscience-stricken as a result of one of his adventures", he gave himself to a monastery. No cause is named (LH 335). Sozomen calls it "sudden" (SOZ 39502–39503).
  3. **The robbers** (the connection; should finish by about word 150–170). Four robbers attacked him in his cell, not knowing him. He tied them, carried them on his back to the church and asked the brothers what to do, since he was not allowed to hurt anyone. When they knew it was Moses, the famous robber, they confessed and left the world because of his conversion (LH 335). Palladius's tale ("Among other tales this is told of him"). Quotes 1 and 2.
  4. **The long fight.** His old lust nearly made him give up, "as he himself testified". Isidore, the priest of Scete, told him to endure. Another elder told him to keep watch and pray, so for six years he stood all night in his cell, "and he could not master the thing". By night he secretly filled the old monks' water-pots from wells up to five miles off. One night he was struck down at the well; Palladius says the demon did it. He was ill a year (LH 337). The blow stays in Palladius's voice.
  5. **Rest.** Isidore told him to stop fighting the demons; he refused, saying he would not stop until the demons stopped appearing. Isidore said his dreams had ceased and sent him to Communion, the shared bread and wine of the church. Two months later Moses said he suffered nothing more (LH 337). Documented.
  6. **The father others came to.** A saying remembers a brother coming to him for a rule of life (PAR2 88). The sayings also remember him scorned for his colour. Documented as the sayings say; the events Inferential-Thin. Tell plainly, in the sayings' voice, credited as "sayings gathered in the 600s".
     - *The fathers' scorn* (PAR2 164: "they treated him with contumely, saying, 'Why doth this Ethiopian come and go in our midst?'"). It is an insult, not a question. Use this wording or keep its force: **"Sayings gathered in the 600s remember the fathers of Scete scorning him. 'Why does this Ethiopian come and go among us?' they said."** Then: he kept silent, and afterwards said he had been afraid but had not said a word (PAR2 164).
     - *His words against himself* (PAR2 1028). The bishop, "wishing to try him", told the clergy to drive him out of the sanctuary; they did, saying, "Get outside, O Ethiopian." Outside, he said to himself the words of quote 3, used as rendered. Budge's "[white]" is his own supply, marked by his bracket (the Syriac is not held), and a held Latin parallel has "non homo", not a man (Dorotheus, Doctrina XIV, 136342–136344; Source_Sheet, Library sweep). So the rendering leaves it out, and the card carries the note. Keep the address ("you whose skin is dark and black"); do not turn it into a reason. Keep "go back in": he means going back into the sanctuary.
  7. **Death, one sentence.** Palladius says only that he died at Scete at seventy-five (LH 337). A much later Ethiopian tradition remembers raiders killing him there (ESX-M 37094; 37185). Contested.

- **What came of them** (ranked):
  1. **Proof of what repentance does.** Palladius's stated reason (LH 333). Nikodemos repeats it (NIK 41915–41919). The Ethiopian Synaxarium closes on "the power of repentance" that made him "a father and a teacher" (ESX-M 37189–37192). The Syriac book uses him as the man grace moved "suddenly" (PAR2 2850).
  2. **Followers.** The four robbers (LH 335); seventy disciples (LH 337); Sozomen's "numerous eminent disciples" (SOZ 39525).
  3. **A case for teaching.** A Syriac book of the 600s used Isidore's restraint of him to teach that effort alone does not heal a monk (PAR2 2766–2768), and his silence under insult as a lesson against anger (PAR2 2874).
  4. **Feasts.** Ethiopian, 24 Sane (ESX-M 37094); Greek, 28 August (NIK 41888–41889).
  - *What he defended:* nothing in the record.

- **Quotes** (rendered from Clarke's English; the Greek is not held):
  1. LH 335. *Job:* his own words; the robber who will not hurt anyone. "Since I am not allowed to hurt anyone, what do you bid me do with these?" → **"I am not allowed to hurt anyone. So what do you tell me to do with these?"**
  2. LH 335, the robbers. *Job:* the connection, in the words of those who followed him. "If he who was so great and powerful in brigandage has feared God, why should we defer our salvation?" → **"If a man so great and powerful in robbery has feared God, why should we put off our salvation?"**
  3. PAR2 1028, Budge's English of the Syriac; the Syriac is not held. *Job:* his own words against himself, in the sayings' voice; the record's force, not softened. "They have treated thee rightly, O thou whose skin is dark and black; thou shalt not go back as if thou wert a [white] man." → **"They have treated you rightly, you whose skin is dark and black. You shall not go back in as if you were a man."** "[white]" is left out as Budge's supply (see beat 6). "Go back" becomes "go back in", which the scene gives: he has just been driven out of the sanctuary.
  - *Dropped:* Isidore's "stop struggling with the demons" (LH 337), the optional quote, to keep three. Beat 5 tells it in the narrator's words.

- **Connection to carry into the story:** the robbers he caught became monks because of what he had become (LH 335). They knew him: they "recognized that it was Moses the erstwhile renowned and far-famed robber". Their reasoning, in their own words, is that if this robber had turned to God they had no excuse to wait. Carry it through the scene, early, so the listener hears that his change drew others before hearing of his long fight. He also left seventy disciples (LH 337); Palladius does not say what drew them.

- **Links:**
  - Isidore, priest of Scete: met; guided him (LH 337).
  - Palladius of Helenopolis: wrote his life (LH 331–337).
  - Sozomen: retold his life (SOZ 39499–39525).
  - The Ethiopian and Greek churches: kept his feast (ESX-M 37094; NIK 41888).

- **Who tells it:** one or two sentences, in beat 1. Palladius, a bishop, wrote around 420; he knew the desert monks but does not say he met Moses, and the robber stories reached him as talk ("they used to say", LH 333). The sayings are a later Syriac collection of the 600s. Sozomen copies Palladius and is not a second witness. The doubt is not the story.

- **Do not say:**
  - "He murdered people" as fact → "he was even said to have murdered" (LH 333).
  - "He led a robber gang" as fact → "they said he led a band of robbers" (LH 333).
  - "Driven out for robbery" alone → "for immorality and robbery" or "for crime" (LH 333).
  - "To show the virtue of his repentance" in a modern moral sense → "to show the worth of his repentance" or "what his repentance could do" (LH 333; the old "virtue" means power or worth).
  - "He came from Ethiopia" or "an Ethiopian prince" → "an Ethiopian by race and black" (LH 333). Sozomen calls him "an Egyptian" (SOZ 39461); a Syriac chapter list calls him "the Indian". No modern country.
  - Any cause of his conversion → none is given; "one of his adventures" left him "conscience-stricken" (LH 335).
  - "He became a monk at Scete" as his first step → he "gave himself to a monastery" (LH 335); Scete is where he lived under Isidore and died (LH 337).
  - "The demon beat him" in the narrator's voice → "Palladius says the demon struck him".
  - "He was martyred" or "raiders killed him" as fact → "a much later Ethiopian tradition remembers him killed by raiders; Palladius says only that he died at Scete."
  - The barbarians saying, or "Those who take the sword shall perish by the sword", as his → not told. Its Moses is unnamed; only the late Ethiopian Synaxarium makes it him (Source_Sheet B1–B2).
  - "Asked why this Ethiopian came among them" without the contempt → "they scorned him" or "they treated him with contempt, saying..." (PAR2 164, "contumely").
  - "Since his skin was dark and black" → the saying addresses him ("O thou whose skin is dark and black"); it gives no reason (PAR2 1028).
  - "He must not act like a white man", or "white" in any form → "he must not go back in as if he were a man"; "white" is Budge's bracketed supply, and a held Latin parallel reads "not a man" (Dorotheus, Doctrina XIV). Do not gloss "a man" either way.
  - "A bishop put him out of the sanctuary" → the bishop had the clergy drive him out (PAR2 1028).
  - "Sayings from the 600s" → "sayings gathered in the 600s"; the sayings are older than the collection (PAR2 header).
  - "Though Palladius names no cause" set against "one of his adventures" → "In time, Palladius says only, one of his adventures left him conscience-stricken, and he gave himself to a monastery" (LH 335).
  - "He was said to have killed men", or "said to have killed" with nothing after it → "he was even said to have murdered" or "said to have gone as far as murder" (LH 333).
  - "Robbers broke into his cell" → "robbers attacked him in his cell" (LH 335).
  - "The fathers were testing him" at PAR2 164 → only PAR2 1028 says it was a test, and that was the bishop's.
  - "He forgave them", "he rose above it", or any feeling → the sayings give only his silence, his fear and his words against himself.
  - "The bishop then made him a priest" or "white within and without" → Ethiopian Synaxarium only (ESX-M 37145–37148). At PAR2 1028, "thou art wholly white" is the bishop's remark on his white garment.
  - Do not soften "dark and black" or leave out his words against himself. Do not call the insult racism in the narrator's voice.
  - Merging the two Isidores → "Isidore, priest of Scete" (LH 337) is not the Isidore of Alexandria (LH 197; LH 711).
  - "The robbers became his disciples" or "joined his monastery" → they "renounced the world" (LH 335); "became monks" is close enough only if not tied to him.
  - "Isidore told him to stand all night" → "Isidore told him to endure; the all-night vigil was another elder's advice" (LH 337).
  - "He suddenly turned" in the narrator's voice → "Palladius names no cause; Sozomen calls it sudden" (LH 335; SOZ 39502).
  - "Seventy" for his age → he died at seventy-five; seventy is the number of disciples.
  - "Abbot of five hundred monks", "baptized by Macarius", "his master worshipped the sun" → late Ethiopian Synaxarium only (ESX-M 37101–37138).
  - "He founded a monastery" → not in the record.
  - "Isidore cured him" as the narrator's claim → Isidore said his dreams had ceased; two months later Moses reported no more trouble (LH 337).
  - Any year for his birth or death → none is held.
  - Merging him with the Abba Moses of Cassian's Conferences → a different monk.
  - "He swam the Nile at night" → the shepherd foiled him "one night"; the swim is not timed (LH 333).
  - "He taught seventy disciples" as Palladius's words → Palladius says "he left seventy disciples"; the teaching is from the sayings.

- **Leave out:**
  - The barbarians saying (PAR2 1464): its Moses is not named the Ethiopian, and only a witness about a thousand years late joins it to him.
  - The Ethiopian Synaxarium's conversion and abbacy details: late legend.
  - The demon "accomplice in crime" line (LH 335): even Butler could not explain it (LH 921).
  - Other sayings naming only "Abba Moses" (PAR2 1160, 178): not shown to be his.
  - The quart of wine and the fifty-mile walk (LH 333): colour.
