# Outline: Moses the Black

File paths are under `cic/texts/`. LH = `palladius_lausiac-history_clarke1918.txt`; PAR2 = `anan-isho_paradise-v2-sayings_budge1907.txt`; SOZ = `npnf202_socrates-sozomen-ecclesiastical-histories.xml`; ESX-M = `ethiopian-synaxarium_book-of-saints-ethiopian-church-v2-v4-microform_budge1928.txt`; NIK = `nikodemos-hagiorite_synaxaristes-vol2-mar-aug-grc_1900.txt`. Full lines and verbatim text are in `Source_Sheet.md`.

- **Length:** 2 minutes (about 300 words). One full narrative chapter (LH 331–337), later sayings that name him, and two church calendars. Do not stretch past 2.

- **Setting:**
  - *Time:* no year is held. Palladius knew the Egyptian monks from 388 (LH 197, note LH 713). He wrote in 419–420 (LH 657), and by then Moses was dead and counted "among the great ones of the fathers" (LH 337). Spoken: "around the year 400" or "in the late 300s". This is the researcher's inference (Inferential-Thin), so keep it loose.
  - *Place:* Scete, a monks' settlement in the Egyptian desert (LH 337, "So he died in Scete"). His robber days were by the Nile (LH 333). Spoken: "Scete, in the desert of Egypt".
  - *Tradition:* the desert monks of Egypt. Spoken: "the desert fathers". "Abba", meaning father, is the title given to a proven elder; it is not an office (census entry desert-monasticism, aliases).

- **Thread:** a slave turned robber became a father of monks at Scete. From the first telling onward, his life was told as proof of what repentance can do (LH 333).

- **Shape:** Monk or ascetic.

- **Openings:**
  1. A robber swims the flooded Nile at night, sword in his mouth, to kill a shepherd (LH 333). This is told as report: "they used to say".
  2. The same man, now a monk, carries four robbers to the church on his back and asks what he should do with them (LH 335).
  3. An old monk who is still a novice in his own fight: he spends six years standing all night in his cell, and "he could not master the thing" (LH 337).

- **Story beats:**
  1. **Slave and outcast.** He was "an Ethiopian by race and black", a house-servant (a slave) of a government official. His master drove him out "because of his immorality and brigandage". He "was said to" have murdered. LH 333. Documented as Palladius's report; the murders themselves are Contested.
  2. **Robber.** "They used to say" he led a robber band. Once he swam the flooded Nile to kill a shepherd who had foiled him. The shepherd hid in the sand, so Moses killed four rams, ate the meat and sold the skins for wine. LH 333. Documented as report.
  3. **The turn.** "Conscience-stricken as a result of one of his adventures", he gave himself to a monastery. No cause is named. LH 335. Documented. Sozomen calls it a "sudden conversion" (SOZ 39502).
  4. **The robbers.** Four robbers attacked his cell. He tied them up and carried them to the church. He said he was not allowed to hurt anyone, and asked the brothers what to do with them. Sozomen: "he did not consider himself authorized to punish any one" (SOZ 39519–39520). When they learned who he was, the robbers confessed and left the world themselves. LH 335. This is Palladius's tale ("Among other tales this is told of him").
  5. **The long fight.** His old lust drove him almost to give up, "as he himself testified". He went to Isidore, the priest of Scete. Isidore told him to endure. He ate twelve ounces of dry bread a day, worked hard and said fifty prayers. Another elder told him to keep watch and pray, so for six years he stood all night. At night he secretly filled the older monks' water-pots from wells up to five miles away. One night he was struck down at the well. Palladius says the demon did it. He was ill for a year. LH 337. The striking is Palladius's wonder and must be told in his voice.
  6. **Rest.** Isidore told him to stop fighting the demons. He refused. Isidore told him his dreams had ended and sent him to Communion, meaning the shared bread and wine of the church. About two months later, when Isidore asked him, he said that he no longer suffered anything. LH 337. Documented.
  7. **Contempt.** The sayings remember him insulted for his colour. At a gathering at Scete the fathers said, "Why doth this Ethiopian come and go in our midst?" He kept silent. Afterwards he said, "Although I was afraid I uttered not a word" (PAR2 164; retold at PAR2 2874 with "troubled"). When he was one of the clergy, the bishop wanted to test him and told the clergy to drive him out of the sanctuary. They said, "Get outside, O Ethiopian." He said to himself, "They have treated thee rightly, O thou whose skin is dark and black; thou shalt not go back as if thou wert a [white] man" (PAR2 1028; the bracket is Budge's). Documented as what the sayings say; the events Inferential-Thin. Tell this plainly, in the sayings' voice.
  8. **Father.** He became a priest and left seventy disciples. LH 337. Documented. Palladius, the earliest witness, says only that he died at Scete at seventy-five. The Ethiopian calendar of saints remembers raiders killing him there with seven brothers (ESX-M 37094; 37163–37188). A saying tells the same of an "Abba Moses" it does not name further (PAR2 1464). The Greek calendar says he fell asleep in old age (NIK 41953–41955). Contested. Keep it to one or two sentences: the death must not swallow the story.

- **What came of them** (ranked):
  1. **Proof of what repentance does.** Palladius tells his crimes "to show the virtue of his repentance" (LH 333). Nikodemos repeats the purpose (NIK 41915–41919). The Ethiopian Synaxarium closes on "the power of repentance" (ESX-M 37189–37192). The Syriac book uses him as the man grace moved "suddenly" (PAR2 2850).
  2. **Disciples and followers.** The four robbers "renounced the world because of his conversion" (LH 335), and he "left seventy disciples" (LH 337). Sozomen has "numerous eminent disciples" (SOZ 39525).
  3. **A case for teaching.** A Syriac book of the 600s used him to teach other monks. Isidore's restraint showed that effort alone does not heal a monk without the Spirit and Communion (PAR2 2766–2768). His silence under insult became a lesson on holding back anger (PAR2 2874).
  4. **Feasts.** Church calendars kept his day: the Ethiopian on 24 Sane (ESX-M 37094) and the Greek on 28 August (NIK 41888–41889).
  - *What he defended:* nothing in the record. He is not tied to any dispute.

- **Quotes** (rendered from English translations; neither the Greek nor the Syriac original is held):
  1. LH 335, Clarke's English of Palladius's Greek. *Job:* his own words, showing the robber who will not hurt anyone. "Since I am not allowed to hurt anyone, what do you bid me do with these?" Rendering: **"I am not allowed to hurt anyone. So what do you tell me to do with these?"**
  2. LH 335, Clarke's English, spoken by the robbers. *Job:* the connection, in the words of those who followed him. "If he who was so great and powerful in brigandage has feared God, why should we defer our salvation?" Rendering: **"If a man so great and powerful in robbery has feared God, why should we put off our salvation?"**
  3. LH 337, Clarke's English. *Job:* the turn, showing the man who could not stop fighting. Optional, if there is room. "Moses, stop struggling with the demons, and do not provoke them." / "I will never cease until the appearance of the demons ceases." Rendering: **"Moses, stop struggling with the demons, and do not provoke them." / "I will never stop until the demons stop appearing."**

- **Connection to carry into the story:** the robbers he caught became monks because of what he had become (LH 335). Their reasoning was that if this famous robber had turned to God, they had no excuse to wait. The listener needs to understand that they knew him. They "recognized that it was Moses the erstwhile renowned and far-famed robber". What moved them was his conversion, set against the robber they knew he had been. Carry this through the scene and the robbers' own words. He also left seventy disciples (LH 337); Palladius does not say what drew them.

- **Links:**
  - Isidore, priest of Scete: met; guided him (LH 337).
  - Palladius of Helenopolis: wrote his life (LH 331–337).
  - Sozomen: retold his life (SOZ 39499–39525).
  - The Ethiopian and Greek churches: kept his feast (ESX-M 37094; NIK 41888).

- **Who tells it:**
  - **Palladius**, a bishop, wrote in 419–420. He knew the desert monks from 388 and visited Scete (LH 361), but he does not say he met Moses. His book is a book of holy lives with demons and wonders. He tells the crimes "to show the virtue of his repentance" (LH 333). The listener should know that the robber stories reached him as talk ("they used to say").
  - **Sozomen** wrote a church history begun about 443. He tells the same story in the same order (SOZ 39499–39525) and is not a second witness.
  - **The sayings** come from a 600s Syriac collection of older material (PAR2 header). They are later; used for the insult beat and for teaching. Only sayings that name him "the Black" or "the Ethiopian" are his.

- **Do not say:**
  - "He murdered people" as fact → "he was said to have killed" (LH 333).
  - "He led a robber gang" as fact → "they said he led a band of robbers" (LH 333).
  - "He came from Ethiopia" or "an Ethiopian prince" → Palladius's words are "an Ethiopian by race and black" (LH 333). Sozomen calls him "an Egyptian" (SOZ 39461), and a Syriac chapter list calls him "the Indian". Do not name a modern country.
  - Any cause of his conversion (a vision, a monk's preaching, a near-death) → none is given; "one of his adventures" left him "conscience-stricken" (LH 335).
  - "The demon beat him" in the narrator's voice → "Palladius says the demon struck him".
  - "He was martyred" or "raiders killed him" as fact or in the narrator's voice → "a later Ethiopian tradition remembers him killed by raiders; Palladius says only that he died at Scete."
  - "The fathers were testing him" at PAR2 164 → only PAR2 1028 says it was a test, and that one was the bishop's.
  - "He forgave them", "he rose above it", or any feeling → the sayings give only his silence, his fear and his words against himself.
  - "The bishop then made him a priest" or "you have become white within and without" → both are from the Ethiopian Synaxarium only (ESX-M 37145–37148). At PAR2 1028, "thou art wholly white" is the bishop's remark about his white garment.
  - Do not soften "dark and black" or leave out his words against himself. Do not call the insult racism in the narrator's voice: tell what was said and credit it to the sayings.
  - Merging the two Isidores → "Isidore, priest of Scete" (LH 337) is not the Isidore Palladius met at Alexandria in 388 (LH 197; LH 711 lists three Isidores).
  - "The robbers became his disciples" or "joined his monastery" → they "renounced the world" (LH 335).
  - "Seventy" for his age → his age was seventy-five; seventy is the number of his disciples. A late Greek calendar gives age seventy (NIK 41955), but do not use it.
  - "He became abbot of five hundred monks", "he was baptized by Macarius", "his master worshipped the sun" → these come only from the late Ethiopian Synaxarium (ESX-M 37101–37138).
  - "He founded a monastery" → not in the record.
  - "Isidore cured him" as the narrator's claim → Isidore said, "In the name of Jesus Christ your dreams have ceased", and two months later Moses reported no more trouble (LH 337). Keep it as what Isidore said and what Moses reported.
  - Any year for his birth or death → none is held.
  - Do not merge him with the Abba Moses of Cassian's Conferences, a different monk (see the Source_Sheet, under links).

- **Leave out:**
  - The Ethiopian Synaxarium's conversion and abbacy details, which are late legend.
  - The demon "accomplice in crime" line (LH 335), because even Butler could not explain it (LH 921) and two minutes cannot carry it.
  - Sayings that name only "Abba Moses", such as the basket of sand and "your cell will teach you everything" (PAR2 1160, 178). This edition does not show that they are his.
  - The Nile crossing's quart of wine and the fifty-mile walk (LH 333), which are colour.
