Most of the outline holds against the files: the setting, the voice and the angel's charge (both Lives), the Athanasius visit, the Latopolis attack (Greek Life), the death sequence and Petronius/Horsiesius, quotes 1 and 3, the tablet finding, and the Do not say entries on the island, the ordination, the dates and the successor. ## BLOCKING

**B1. The beats do not say which Life they rest on.** The writer gets only the outline. The E-codes point to a sheet the writer never sees. So a beat that names no Life reads as both Lives agreeing. Correct each beat as follows:
- Beat 1: "for love of the God of heaven" is the Bohairic Life only (A 5145-5146). The Greek Life has no speech here. It says only that Christians were moved by pity and fed them (G 80985-80988), and that he asked about them and was told (80988-80989). Add: "The words are the Bohairic Life's. The Greek Life says only that Christians, moved by pity, brought food." The vow is in both Lives (A 5182-5183; G 81009-81013). Keep that as is.
- Beat 2: tag it "Bohairic Life" for the deserted village Schénésît (A 5195-5196), the epidemic (5283-5291) and Palamon's rule (5424-5435). Add "the Greek Life also has his baptism at Chenoboscia (G 81023-81027)". Palamon's rule is "half the night awake, meditating on the word of the Lord" (5425-5426), with manual work (5427-5428) and daily fasting until evening in summer (5433-5434). Write "half the night awake with the Scriptures", not "in prayer".
- Beat 4: add "Bohairic Life only" (A 6252-6336, 6436-6482). Write "the brothers serving at table were changed every three weeks" (6477-6479). Do not write "the work turned over".
- Beat 5: the ordination is in both Lives, but they say different things about where he hid. The Bohairic says he hid "parmi la foule nombreuse" (A 6673-6674). The Greek says he hid "inter Fratres", until the bishop of Alexandria had passed (G 82514-82520). Write "he hid", or give both. Mary, the doorkeeper, the dwelling, abba Peter and the rules in a book: add "Bohairic Life".
- Beat 7: add "Greek Life" (G 86510-86672). Add "the Arabic Life agrees on 14 Pachon and on Petronius (A 35674, 35825-35877), but says no one has known where he was buried (35733-35734)". The secret moving of the body is the Greek Life's account.

**B2. Quote 2's third "Pachomius" is the editor's, not the Life's.** Amélineau's note e (A 6260): "Le mss. ne contient que deux fois le mot [Pakhôme] : je l'ai ajouté une troisième fois". The text says the angel spoke "par trois fois" (6247). The Greek Life has no name at all (G 81973-81974). So "the Greek Life's Latin says the same" is true of the charge only. Correction: **"Pachomius, Pachomius, the will of God is that you serve the human race and unite them to Him."** Source: "Rendered from Amélineau's French, Bohairic 6247-6248; the Life says the angel spoke three times, and the manuscript writes the name twice. The Greek Life's Latin (81973-81974) gives the same charge, without the name." Add to Do not say: "'Pachomius, Pachomius, Pachomius' as the Life's words" → "the Life says the angel called him three times".

**B3. Ardo on Benedict of Aniane is misread, and his stronger evidence is missing.** Ardo 28948-28951 describes Benedict's first years as a monk (§§7-8, from 28832). In those years his abbot urged him to ease his rigour, and he would not (28945-28947). Ardo shows that phase giving way. Benedict later spread Benedict's Rule through the houses of Aquitaine (30140-30146). He then compiled the *Concordia regularum*, so that "beati Benedicti praecederet sententia" and the rules that agreed with it followed. His purpose was to show that Benedict's Rule rested on the rules of others (30903-30911). The Concordia's own text quotes "Ex Regula sancti Pachomii" under Benedict's chapters (B 66976, 68752, 70285 and later). Ménard's note (editor, 58651-58655) says this Pachomian rule is Jerome's Latin, not Palladius's angel rule. So "set it beside Basil as a harder rule than Benedict's" turns a passing youthful phase into his settled view, and it gets the later relationship backwards. Corrections:
- What came of them, item 1, and the Connection: replace the Ardo sentence with this. "In the early ninth century, Benedict of Aniane (died 821) gathered the old rules into a *Concordia of Rules*. Under each chapter of Benedict's Rule he set passages from the rule of Pachomius and others, to show that Benedict's Rule stood on theirs (Ardo 30903-30911; the Concordia, e.g. 66976)." Confidence: Documented for the Concordia's quotations, because the compilation is his own act. Ardo's account of its purpose is a disciple's account (later, contemporary with Benedict). If the youthful phase is kept, say: "as a young monk, his biographer Ardo says, he thought Benedict's Rule was for beginners and reached for Basil and Pachomius (28948-28951)". Mark it as a phase he left behind.
- Add to Do not say: "Benedict of Aniane thought Pachomius's rule better or harder than Benedict's" → "he set Pachomius's rule under Benedict's, as one of the rules it agreed with".
- Sheet I1: change "Documented that a Carolingian abbot used it" so that it rests on the Concordia, not on Ardo 28948.

**B4. The Jerome connection leaves out who Jerome's preface says the Latin was for, and it implies something about Cassian that no text supports.**
- Jerome's preface (J 4381-4392) gives a second purpose besides the Latin monks in Egypt. He translated so that Eustochium "haberet quod sororibus agendum tribueret", and so that "nostrique fratres Aegyptiorum, hoc est, Tabennensium monachorum exempla sequerentur". The preface opens with his grief at Paula's death (4326-4329), and the books came to Silvanus "Alexandria missos" (4330). Add to the Connection: "Jerome also says he made the Latin for his own brothers, and for the nuns under Paula's daughter Eustochium, so that they could follow the example of the Tabennesiot monks." This is the first named Latin community the preface says received the rule. Say "his own brothers and Eustochium's nuns". Do not place them, because the preface names no town.
- "The Latin was the copy that carried the rule west: Cassian used the Tabennesiot rule" implies that Cassian read Jerome's Latin. Cassian (Inst. IV.1, `npnf211` 18527-18531) says only that he adds "some things ... from that of the monks of Tabenna". He does not say he used Jerome. Correction: make Cassian a separate channel: "Cassian, who had been among the Egyptian monks, drew on 'the monks of Tabenna'." Keep "the Latin carried it" for Jerome's own houses and for Benedict of Aniane's Concordia (B3), whose Pachomian text the editor identifies as Jerome's (58651-58655; Codex regularum list, "cum praefatione sancti Hieronymi", 34229-34230).
- "Jerome never met Pachomius": no held line says this. It follows from the preface, where Jerome works from books sent to him. Write "Jerome worked from books sent to him, more than fifty years after Pachomius died."

## MINOR

**M1. Plague number (beat 7).** G 86511-86512: "in omnibus monasteriis centum ac plures". Write "across all the monasteries, a hundred or more died", not "more than a hundred brothers". Add the qualifier to Do not say.

**M2. Latopolis is not "Greek Life only" (beat 6; sheet E12).** The Arabic Life tells a related clash. Bishops and monks gather in the church of Esneh against the brothers, out of envy, wanting them out of their dioceses (A 32855-32870). Later he recalls how God saved him from murder there (35413). Tag beat 6: "Greek Life; the Arabic Life tells a related clash at Esneh differently (bishops and monks who wanted his monasteries out of their dioceses)". Remove "uncontradicted" from the sheet.

**M3. "Koinonia ... his own name" (beat 6) rests only on the census alias (S5).** No held line is cited. Either drop it, or write "his houses were called a fellowship (koinonia)" with a held line. It also does not belong in the Conflict beat. Move it to beat 4 or drop it.

**M4. Tablet attribution (Who tells it).** "He [Palladius] is the source of the brass tablet" states as fact the Bollandists' "videtur" (G 82214-82217). Jerome's preface also has an angel "sent to them for this very institution" (J 4345-4347), and Gennadius has "angelic dictation" (42257-42259). Correction: "The angel's charge is in both Lives, and Jerome and Gennadius also speak of an angel behind the rule. The tablet first appears in Palladius. The Greek Life has it only in bracketed passages, which its editors judged were taken from Palladius." Add to Do not say: "the Greek Life says the angel gave him a tablet" → "only in bracketed passages its editors say were not in their Greek manuscript".

**M5. The fellowship split after his death (What came of them, item 2).** Gennadius 8 (`npnf203` 42277-42286): Theodore urged "to restore to harmony and unity those who, a dissension having arisen after the death of the Abbot, had broken the unity". The template says conflict is told as plainly as virtue. Add: "it split after his death, and Theodore's letters urged the breakaways back."

**M6. Thread: "ate, dressed and worked alike".** Same food and clothes are supported (A 6335-6336). For the women, Palladius has "the same constitution and the same manner of life" (Clarke 409). "Worked alike" has no line. Write "lived under the same rule, ate and dressed alike".

**M7. Ge'ez (What came of them, item 1).** Ethiopic Part I is Palladius's chapter (Schodde 170-184). Write "The Ethiopian church kept a Ge'ez version, much of it Palladius's account of the rule", not "a version" of the rule itself.

**M8. Spoken name.** "The Desert Fathers and Mothers" or "monks of the Egyptian desert" sits awkwardly with the outline's own village-by-the-river setting (A 6013-6015). Prefer "the monks of Egypt", or say his houses stood in villages along the Nile. "The Great Persecution" (Setting) is a modern label. The Lives say only "the persecution" (A 5093; G 80977).
