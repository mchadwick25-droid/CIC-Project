## 1. The test, from one hearing

- **Who or what:** a council of more than five hundred bishops, called by the emperor Marcian to Chalcedon, across the water from Constantinople, in the autumn of 451.
- **What happened:** it undid the council of Ephesus in 449, deposed and exiled Dioscorus of Alexandria, received Leo's Tome, and, after Leo's legates threatened to leave, defined Christ as one person "in two natures" and banned any other creed. It also put monks under bishops and ranked Constantinople after Rome.
- **Why the connection matters:** the tree forks here. The Greek and Latin churches kept it as their fourth council. The Coptic, Syriac and Armenian churches refused it, confessed one nature from two, condemned Eutyches too, honoured Dioscorus as a martyr, and in 1912 were still one communion apart.

All three lines came from one hearing. None is blank or wrong.

**The heart (from the census entry and what the council is known for).** Chalcedon set the classic definition of Christ: one and the same Son, fully God and fully human, known in two natures, unmixed and undivided, in one person. It did so by receiving Leo's Tome and deposing Alexandria's bishop, Dioscorus, who stood for "from two natures". The same act forked the churches: the Greek and Latin churches made it their fourth council, while Egypt, Syria and later Armenia refused it, and still stand apart.

**Does the first minute carry it?** Yes, at the edge of the count. The Definition, with its quoted four "withouts", lands by word 75, and the nature/person gloss and the refused "from two natures" follow by word 120. The division sentence begins at word 126 and closes at about word 152, roughly 70 seconds at Daniel's 130 a minute. That is "about 130" and inside the first minute in substance, so not blocking. It gets its weight: the decision is quoted, explained and set against its rival phrase, and the last third of the story returns to the fork and ends on it. The deposition of Dioscorus is not in the first minute, but the outline's heart puts it in beat 4, and he arrives by name in paragraph 3.

**Where I got lost.** Briefly at "Leo or Dioscorus?" (finding 1): the listener has not been told the draft carried Dioscorus's own phrase. Also at "keeping Leo's letter unread" (finding 2), where the time is unclear. Nowhere does it read as a children's book or talk down.

## Findings

**1. MINOR: "a draft without 'in two natures'" hides why the judges asked "Leo or Dioscorus?"** The outline (beat 5; B14a) says the draft said "of two natures", which was Dioscorus's own phrase. Heard once, "without 'in two natures'" does not tell the listener that the draft carried his words, so the judges' question arrives unexplained. *Change:* "Most bishops wanted to sign a draft that said 'from two natures', but Leo's legates…"

**2. MINOR: "keeping Leo's letter unread" has no time attached.** The charge concerns Ephesus in 449 (beat 4, B11a), but heard after the 451 trial it could be taken for Chalcedon. *Change:* "keeping Leo's letter from being read at Ephesus".

**3. MINOR: the ban without its penalty.** "Any other creed was banned" is true. But the outline gives its force: deposition for clergy and anathema for monks and lay people (beat 5, B15). The Standard asks that force not be softened by omission. The sentence carries the act, so this is not blocking. *Optional change:* "Any other creed was banned, on pain of deposition for clergy."
