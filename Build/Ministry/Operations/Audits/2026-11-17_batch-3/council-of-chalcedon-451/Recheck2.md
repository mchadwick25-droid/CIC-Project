## The three lines, from one hearing

- **Who or what:** the council that the emperor Marcian and the empress Pulcheria called at Chalcedon, across the water from Constantinople, in the autumn of 451. Leo of Rome sent legates and his Tome but did not come.
- **What happened:** it tried and deposed Dioscorus of Alexandria. After a stand-off over a draft that said "of two natures", it defined Christ as known "in two natures" and banned every other creed. It also put monks under bishops and ranked Constantinople after Rome, which Leo voided.
- **Why the connection matters:** the Greek and Latin churches came to keep it as their fourth council. Egypt, Syria and Armenia refused it, confessed "one nature from two", condemned Eutyches as well, and kept the deposed Dioscorus as a martyr. Both sides claimed Cyril.

**One-hearing test: PASS.** All three lines can be filled, and filled correctly.

**Where I got lost.** "Of two" (the draft), "from two" (Dioscorus and the refusing churches) and "in two" (the Definition) go by without the story ever setting "from two" against "in two". "The judges ordered 'two natures' added" does not say the wording changed (BLOCKING 3). "The judges" are not tied to the "imperial officials" of the paragraph before. Anatolius and Juvenal are named but never introduced. The riot in Alexandria comes with no cause, because Proterius, the bishop it rose against, appears only in the last line. "Than sign" does not say sign what. "Person" is defined and never used again. Nothing reads as a children's book, and nothing talks down.

## New findings

### BLOCKING

**1. Length.** 614 words, about four minutes, against the outline's "about 450 … It may not stretch further". It is also about 90 words over a 3:30 ceiling of about 525. The length analysis below shows the outline's own must-tell list cannot be told in 450, so that part of the fault starts in the outline, and raising the cap is Mark's call. But the story is well past even a raised cap, and the cuts named below lose nothing the outline requires.

**2. "The churches of Egypt and Syria had condemned Eutyches too" (P8).** The pluperfect places the condemnation before or at the council. The story has just told that Dioscorus, Egypt's bishop, restored Eutyches in 449 and was deposed partly for receiving him. Every source for the refusing churches condemning Eutyches comes after 451: Dioscorus's letter from exile (Zachariah 2372–2376), the chronicle's anathema on "those two heresies" (Zachariah 2183–2184), Severus about 508 (Brooks 705–722), and Armenia's synod of 506 (Ormanian 2502–2512). As heard, the line contradicts the story and misleads.
*Change:* drop "had", and carry it through their own acts: for example, Dioscorus wrote from exile that Christ's flesh was not "foreign from our body", and their chronicle anathematised both Nestorius's and Eutyches's teaching.
*Do not say, add to the outline:* "The refusing churches had condemned Eutyches (before or at the council)" → "They condemned Eutyches too, in their own later words: Dioscorus from exile, their chronicle, Severus, Armenia's synod (E4, E5). In 449 Dioscorus restored him (B2)."

**3. "From two" against "in two" is left to inference.** The outline says the listener must understand that "the words at stake were 'from two natures' against 'in two natures'", and that the writer "has to state" it. The story uses "of two" for the draft, "from two" for Dioscorus and the refusing churches, and "in two" only inside the Definition quote. It then says "the judges ordered 'two natures' added", which does not tell the hearer that the wording changed. A first-time listener cannot tell that "of two" and "from two" are the same phrase (the Greek *ek*), or that the Definition replaced it.
*Change:* one clause after the order. For example: so the Definition said not "from two natures" but "in two". Use one English word for *ek* throughout.
*Do not say, add to the outline:* "'Of two natures' for the draft and 'from two' for Dioscorus (two English words for one Greek phrase)" → "Use 'from two natures' for *ek* throughout (B8, B14, B14a), and state the change to 'in two natures' (B15)."

### MINOR

1. **Unintroduced names.** Say once that "the judges" were the emperor's officials who ran the sessions, and that Anatolius was bishop of Constantinople. Juvenal (P10) needs a word: "bishop of Jerusalem", or drop his name.
2. **"Person" is defined and dropped again.** Either keep it ("one person" is in the Definition, B15) or cut "and his person, who he is" (saves 6 words).
3. **The hand saying lacks its object and its stake.** "Than sign" comes before the Definition is told. Both witnesses say he was asked to sign the Definition and take back his see (B20). Say so, or move the line after the Definition.
4. **The riot comes before its cause.** Proterius appears only in the last line. Tell Alexandria in one place and in order: Proterius takes Dioscorus's place, clergy, monks and people break communion, then Priscus and the chronicle.
5. **Three of five grounds.** "For receiving Eutyches, excommunicating Leo and refusing to come" drops the many written charges and the keeping of Leo's letter from being read (B11a). Add "and many charges against him" (four words), or say "among other grounds".
6. **The ending is a roll.** P9–P11 are four short reports in a row, one per church. Join them by place or by cause, and let the story end on a scene or on the connection, as the Standard allows.
7. **The Church of the East "received it in part"** is Contested in the outline. Credit it lightly ("by its own collection of synods") or leave it as the outline's one-sentence impact; it should not sound settled.
