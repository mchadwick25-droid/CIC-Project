# Recheck: the Council of Chalcedon (451)

## Verdict: FAIL

## The three lines, from one hearing

- **Who or what:** the council Marcian and Pulcheria called at Chalcedon in 451, and Dioscorus of Alexandria, whom it deposed.
- **What happened:** Dioscorus was condemned in Leo's name; the bishops were pushed from "of two natures" to "in two natures"; Alexandria rose against his successor.
- **Why the connection matters:** the council's side heard Leo and Cyril agree; the churches that refused it kept Dioscorus as a martyr and held "one nature from two". Heard once, who "those churches" are, and that the split ran through Palestine and Armenia too, does not come through.

## Findings

- **BLOCKING 1 (force and the refusers' witnesses):** mostly made; new problem. Priscus (E2, credited), the altar killings (Zachariah 2524–2550, credited to the refusers' chronicle), the Coptic History (Evetts 34868–34870, 35109–35110, verbatim) and the Church of the East (E7) trace to the outline. But Severus is misreported: the story says the council "cured" Eutyches's error with "an opposite infection". His letter says it "wished to heal the disorder in an unintelligent way by means of an opposite infection, I mean by the evil impiety of Nestorius" (Brooks 720–722). The quote is verbatim; the verb turns his charge into a success. Say "tried to cure", and keep "the teaching of Nestorius" (outline impact 3).
- **BLOCKING 2 (Cyril on both sides):** made. "Leo and Cyril taught the same thing" is verbatim (npnf214 20002), credited to the bishops' acclamation.
- **BLOCKING 3 (length):** not made. The profiler gives 471 words against "about 450 … It may not stretch further". The review asked for the additions to be paid for by cuts that lose no must-tell; the story is still about 5% over, and the cuts it did make took must-tell material (next lines). Ruling: over the ceiling; does not pass.
- **New problem (cut must-tell, connection's third point):** the revision dropped Palestine's monks choosing their own bishop (E3) and Armenia refusing by synod (E5). The outline says the writer "has to state" that the division ran between churches, through these acts. It also dropped the Syriac Orthodox and Armenians by name, so "Those churches" now points only to Alexandria's clergy and people. It dropped "for them 'two natures' divided Christ" (impact 3) and the Greek and Latin churches' later acceptance (impact 1, E1).
- **New problem (hand saying cut):** the refusers' memory that he would sooner lose his hand than sign and take back his see (beat 4, B20) is gone. It was the one place the deposition becomes, for Syria, a confessor's stand. MINOR 6 asked to strengthen it, not cut it.
- **New problem (narrator voice, paragraph 2):** "He held that Christ is one nature from two" reads after "Dioscorus had restored him", so "He" can be heard as Eutyches. Name Dioscorus. "The dispute came down to one letter" is the narrator settling the dispute; the outline supports "from two" against "in two" as the words at stake (B14, B16), not that it came down to them. Use the outline's wording.
- **MINOR 1 (Session V order):** made. Threat, then the judges' remark, Anatolius, then the question.
- **MINOR 2 (officials' absence):** made, and the answers are now credited to "the minutes of the winning side".
- **MINOR 3 (roles):** made. The judges are the emperor's, Anatolius is named bishop of Constantinople, and Alexandria is named.
- **MINOR 4 ("person"):** made (the definition was cut).
- **MINOR 5 (one-act runs):** made in paragraphs 4 and 5.
- **MINOR 6 (hand saying stake):** not made; the saying was cut (see above).
- **MINOR 7 (canon 4):** not made; optional, and there is no room.

Credits otherwise hold: "not for the faith" goes to Anatolius, the riot to Priscus, the altar killings and the robe to the refusers' chronicle, "for the orthodox faith" to the Coptic History. Profiler: grade 8.1, reading ease 70.6 (names out), longest sentence 28 words, pass.
