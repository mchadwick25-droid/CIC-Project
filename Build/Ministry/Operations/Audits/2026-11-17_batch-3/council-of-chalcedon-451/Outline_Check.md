# Outline check: the Council of Chalcedon (451)

**Verdict: FIX.** Five blocking findings. Four of them are on the witness line: as written, the outline tells Dioscorus's fall and the making of the Definition from the minutes' frame, leaving out what the same minutes record against that frame. A writer following it faithfully would let the council's case stand as the narrator's.

Checked at the cited lines: Percival (npnf214), Leo (npnf212), Mansi 6 and 7 (Latin and garbled Greek), Evagrius (Walford; Bidez–Parmentier Greek), Zachariah (Hamilton–Brooks), Michael the Syrian t. 2, Severus Letter I.1, the History of the Patriarchs, Ormanian, Ter-Minassiantz, Chabot, Wigram, Hefele v3, Perry, John of Nikiu, the Coptic Synaxarium. Most lines hold: beats 1, 3 and 6, the Serapeum and altar accounts, the Theodosius revolt, Severus, the Coptic History, Armenia's 506/554 and the Church of the East items say no more than their sources.

## BLOCKING

**1. Beat 4: the deposition's grounds are given only in part, and Dioscorus's own answer is missing.** The outline gives only the synod's letter (npnf214 20082–20090). The operative sentence is the legates' sentence (Mansi 6 99288–99441, Latin). It lists five grounds: he received Eutyches into communion irregularly before the 449 council; he would not let Leo's letter be read at Ephesus; he "presumed to dictate excommunication" against Leo; many petitions charging him with wrongs were laid before the council (four of them from Alexandrian deacons, a former priest and a layman: npnf214 20044–20048); and he refused three summonses. The minutes also record his answer. He said he would come willingly but his guards prevented him, and later that he would come if the imperial officials and the senate were present (Mansi 6 95080–95092; Hefele 16988, 16997, 17032, from the acts). Percival notes that the officials were absent from that session (npnf214 20018–20020). Michael gives the same answer from the refusers' side: "Je suis prêt à me rendre près du saint synode ; mais on ne me le permet pas" (3538–3539). "Summoned three times, Dioscorus did not come" in the narrator's voice is the winners' frame.
*Correction.* Replace beat 4's first two sentences with: "Session III met without the imperial officials (npnf214 20018–20020). Dioscorus was summoned three times. He answered that his guards kept him back, and that he would come if the officials and senate were present (Mansi 6 95080–95092; Hefele 16988–17032). Leo's legates gave sentence: he had received Eutyches into communion, kept Leo's letter from being read in 449, excommunicated Leo, faced many written charges, including petitions from Alexandrians, and refused three summonses. In Leo's name and Peter's they stripped him of his office (Mansi 6 99288–99441; npnf214 20044–20062). The synod's letter gave 13 October as the date (B11). Documented." Move the Anatolius sentence to beat 5 (finding 2).

**2. Beat 5: how the Definition was made leaves out the first draft and the bishops' resistance.** The minutes record that the draft read in Session V, which Anatolius said had pleased everyone the day before, said Christ was "of two natures", Dioscorus's own phrase. Most bishops cried, "Either let the definition stand or we go" (npnf214 20154–20157; Hefele 18100–18131). The judges then pointed out that "Dioscorus said he condemned Flavian because he said two natures; but the definition has 'from two natures'" (Mansi 7 9797–9801; Hefele 18146–18153). Anatolius's "not for the faith" line answers that point (Mansi 7 9803–9813), so it belongs here, not in beat 4. Then came "Leo or Dioscorus?", and the judges ordered: "Add then to the definition … that there are two natures" (npnf214 20175–20178). Percival's bracket says the emperor threatened to send them all home (20147–20153, editor). Without these lines, the refusers' charge of compulsion (B20) floats free of the minutes. With them, a listener can see why both sides read the same session differently.
*Correction.* Beat 5 becomes: "Session II: the bishops cried that they would make no new creed (npnf214 19340–19341). Session V, 22 October (Hefele): a draft that said Christ was 'of two natures' was read. Most bishops wanted to sign it as it stood: 'Either let the definition stand or we go.' Leo's legates said that if the bishops did not agree to Leo's letter, they would ask leave to go and hold a council in the West. The imperial judges pointed out that the draft used Dioscorus's own phrase. Anatolius answered that Dioscorus had been condemned 'not for the faith', but for excommunicating Leo and refusing three summonses (Mansi 7 9797–9813). The judges asked: 'the most holy Leo or Dioscorus?' The bishops cried, 'We believe as Leo.' The judges ordered 'two natures' added, and a committee wrote the Definition (npnf214 20142–20177). Percival's summary says the emperor threatened to send them home (editor). [Definition, ban, 350 signatures, Marcian as before.] Documented." In the Do not say row on compulsion, add: "Do not tell the change from 'of two' to 'in two' as the bishops' free choice, or as forced. Give the minutes' steps and credit 'under compulsion' to the refusers' chronicle."

**3. The Do not say row "Dioscorus was condemned as a heretic" puts the council's own account in the narrator's mouth.** Its right wording ends on Anatolius's "not for the faith". That is the council's position, given at the moment the judges tied Dioscorus's phrase to the draft (finding 2). The refusers say the opposite. Zachariah says the Nestorian party "brought about the deprivation of Dioscorus" because he confessed "from two natures" (2316–2320). The Coptic History says he suffered "for the orthodox faith" (34868–34870). Michael calls the sentence "inique" and cites its "not for the faith" as proof of that (3542–3545). The sheet's B12 says "the refusers carried the same words". That is true of the wording, but it hides that they used it against the sentence.
*Correction.* Replace the row with: "Easy wrong wording: 'He was deposed as a heretic', or 'He was not condemned for his faith' (either as the narrator's fact). Right wording: give the sentence's grounds as the minutes give them (finding 1). Credit 'not for the faith' to Anatolius, in the council. Credit the refusers' view to them: their chronicle says he was deposed for confessing 'one nature from two', and the Coptic History says he suffered 'for the orthodox faith' (Zachariah 2316–2320; History of the Patriarchs 34868–34870)." In the Connection, after "made him … a confessor and a martyr", add: "and each side's reason for the sentence must be credited to that side."

**4. Quote 1: "touches my soul" carries a modern sense that misleads.** The Greek reads τὸ ἐκ δύο δέχομαι· τὸ δύο, οὐ δέχομαι (npnf214 19256–19259). Mansi's Latin reads "Ex duabus suscipio, duas non suscipio. Cogor [mihi res est] impudenter agere: de anima … est mihi sermo" (Mansi 6 65952–65956, with the margin at 65905). The garbled Greek column shows ἀναγκάζομαι … περὶ ψυχῆς μοι ὁ λόγος (65973–65975). "It is about my soul" means his salvation is at stake. In modern English, "touches my soul" means "moves me deeply". "But" is not in either text.
*Correction.* Quote 1 becomes: "I accept 'from two'. 'Two' I do not accept. I am forced to be shameless: my soul is at stake." Its source line becomes "Rendered from the Greek in Percival (npnf214 19256–19259) and Mansi 6 65952–65956 (Latin; Greek column garbled, 65973–65975)." In Openings, change "this touches his soul" to "his soul is at stake".

**5. Impact 1: "The Definition held for the Greek and Latin churches" overstates it.** Ormanian, the sheet's own source for E1, says that Marcian's successors "disavowed" the council and that Basiliscus, Zeno and Anastasius set its confession aside, before "later" the Greek and Latin churches recognised it (2496–2499, 2515–2517). Evagrius holds the texts. Basiliscus's circular ordered the Tome "and all things said and done at Chalcedon" anathematised (Walford 5460–5466). Zeno's Henotikon anathematised any other opinion "whether at Chalcedon or in any synod whatever" (6024–6030). The Council shape puts the impact in "whether its decisions held". That is exactly the line the outline gets wrong.
*Correction.* Item 1 becomes: "In the East it did not hold at once. Later emperors set it aside: Basiliscus's circular anathematised the Tome and Chalcedon's acts, and Zeno's Henotikon condemned any other faith 'whether at Chalcedon or in any synod' (Evagrius III.4, III.14; Ormanian 2496–2499). In time the Greek and Latin churches recognised it as the fourth general council (Ormanian 2515–2517), and Evagrius copied its Definition whole (B15). Defended: Leo's Tome as 'a common pillar against misbelievers' (npnf214 20267–20269). Widely Accepted." Add a Do not say row: "It settled the matter / held from 451" → "Later emperors set it aside before the Greek and Latin churches made it their fourth council." Add Evagrius III.4 and III.14 to the sheet under E1.

## MINOR

6. **Evagrius's "single letter" is his side's view.** He says the two phrases meant the same thing, and that the quarrel was a demon's device (Walford 3450–3475). Add a Do not say row: "The two phrases meant the same / it was only a misunderstanding (as the narrator's view)" → "Evagrius, on the council's side, thought so. The refusers held that 'in two natures' divided Christ (E4)."
7. **Hand saying (beat 4).** Both refusers' witnesses say he was asked to sign the Definition and "get back his throne" (Zachariah 2341–2343; Michael 3540–3541, where it comes before the sentence). Add "asked to sign the Definition and take back his see". Make its confidence match the sheet's: "the refusers' memory; Inferential-Thin for the words".
8. **The refusers' names for themselves.** "Refusers" is the outline's working label. Add a Do not say row: "the refusers / anti-Chalcedonians (in the narrator's voice)" → "the Church of Alexandria, the Syriac Orthodox, the Armenians, by name. Their own word for their faith is 'orthodox' (History of the Patriarchs 34868; Zachariah "believers", 2548)."
9. **Claim to Cyril.** Connection point two cites B20 for the refusers' claim to Cyril, but the quoted B20 lines do not name him. Cite Zachariah 2364–2367 ("his faith was like that of Athanasius, and Cyril"). Do not call "one nature from two" Cyril's own formula in the narrator's voice: no Cyril text is cited for it.
10. **"Armenia's synod condemned Eutyches by name"** (impact 3, marked Documented) rests on Ormanian, an editor. Mark it Widely Accepted, credited to Ormanian.
11. **Priscus, same passage.** Priscus also reports that the 2,000 troops the emperor sent abused "the wives and daughters of the Alexandrians" (Walford 3388–3395). Add this after the Serapeum line, so the force on both sides comes from one witness.
12. **Library sweep.** The Coptic Synaxarium, in the Church of Alexandria bucket, keeps Dioscorus's feast on 7 Tout. It names him a confessor who "fought the good fight for the orthodox faith" (`synaxaire-arabe-jacobite_po1-…basset-guidi1907.txt` 14328–14437). The sweep does not list it. Add it to the sheet, and add the link "Dioscorus: kept his feast (Coptic Synaxarium, 7 Tout)". Its beard-and-teeth story goes under Leave out as legend. The sweep is otherwise real: the five buckets hold nothing else essential that was missed. Philoxenus and John of Nikiu (Eudocia refusing communion with Juvenal, 6825–6831) are optional.

## Quotes: my renderings compared

- **Dioscorus:** see finding 4.
- **Definition.** From Evagrius's Greek (Bidez–Parmentier 5242–5246), my rendering reads: "one and the same Jesus Christ, Son, Lord, Only-begotten, made known in two natures without confusion, without change, without division, without separation". The outline's rendering matches. "Is known" for the participle γνωριζόμενον is fair for a stand-alone quote. Mansi's Latin agrees but lacks "Jesus", and so does one manuscript family in Evagrius's apparatus. No change needed.
- **Robe.** Hamilton–Brooks reads "tore into ten thousand rents the perfect Robe of Christ, woven from the top throughout" (2181–2183). Michael's French reads "déchira en dix mille morceaux la tunique intègre du Christ tissée d'une seule pièce" (2859–2861). The outline's "whole … woven in one piece from the top" holds against both. In Michael, these are the chronicle's own words, which Zachariah drew from a Greek writer addressing Eupraxius (2850–2852). "The refusers' own chronicle" stays accurate.

## Length

The 3-minute stretch is justified. This is a major council, the Council shape needs its spine, its other business and its division, and the witness line needs both sides in their own sources. It may not stretch further. With findings 1, 2 and 5 added, 450 words cannot carry everything listed, so add to Length: "Carry beats 1, 2, 4 and 5 in full, and give beat 3 as the Tome and its acclaim only. Give beat 6 in one or two sentences (canon 28 and Leo's annulment; monks under bishops). Give impacts 1–4, then 5–7 in one sentence. Use one opening." The Session IV vote count, Marcian's acclamation, Theodoret and Juvenal's 449 vote may drop.
