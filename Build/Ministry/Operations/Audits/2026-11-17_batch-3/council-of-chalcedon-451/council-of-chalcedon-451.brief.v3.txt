In the autumn of 451, Dioscorus of Alexandria, on trial, stops the reading of the minutes. He says: "I accept 'from two'. 'Two' I do not accept. I am forced to be shameless: my soul is at stake."

The emperor Marcian and the empress Pulcheria had called this council to Chalcedon, just across the water from Constantinople. Leo of Rome sent legates and a letter, but did not come. Eutyches, a monastery head, had said Christ was "of two natures before the union" but of one after. Dioscorus had restored him at Ephesus. He held that Christ is one nature from two, God and man. The dispute came down to one letter: Greek ek, "from," against en, "in."

When Leo's letter, the Tome, was read, bishops cried, "Peter has spoken thus through Leo," and "Leo and Cyril taught the same thing."

In the third session, which the emperor's officials did not attend, Dioscorus was summoned three times. The minutes of the winning side say his guards held him back, that he would come if the officials were present, and once that he was ill. Leo's legates gave the sentence: he had received Eutyches, excommunicated Leo and refused the summonses. In Leo's name they stripped him of his office, and he went into exile.

In the fifth session most bishops wanted to sign a draft saying Christ was "of two natures." The legates threatened to leave and hold a council in the West, and the emperor's judges pointed out that the draft used Dioscorus's own phrase. Anatolius, bishop of Constantinople, answered that Dioscorus was condemned "not for the faith," but for excommunicating Leo. The judges asked, "Leo or Dioscorus?" The bishops cried, "We believe as Leo," and the judges ordered "two natures" added.

The Definition says Christ "is known in two natures: without confusion, without change, without division, without separation." Any other creed was banned. Canon 28 ranked Constantinople next after old Rome; Leo declared it void.

In Alexandria, when Proterius took Dioscorus's place, Priscus, a historian in the city, says a crowd drove soldiers into the old temple of Serapis and burned them alive. He says the troops sent next abused the women. The Syriac chronicle, from the side that refused the council, says Proterius's soldiers killed believers at the altar. Clergy, monks and people broke with him and kept Dioscorus's name. The Coptic History says he suffered "for the orthodox faith" and died with "the crown of martyrdom."

Those churches had not followed Eutyches. They held "one nature from two," the faith, they said, of Athanasius and Cyril. Severus of Antioch said the council cured Eutyches's error with "an opposite infection." Their chronicle says it "tore into ten thousand pieces the whole robe of Christ, woven in one piece from the top." The Church of the East received the council in part.
