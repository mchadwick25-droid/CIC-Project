Dioscorus, bishop of Alexandria, was on trial as the minutes of his council of 449 were read. He broke in: "I accept 'from two'. 'Two' I do not accept. I am forced to be shameless: my soul is at stake."

The quarrel was over Christ's nature, what he is as God and as man. Flavian of Constantinople had condemned Eutyches, a monastery head who said Christ was of two natures before the union but of one after. Dioscorus's council restored Eutyches and deposed Flavian, and Leo of Rome called it "not of judges but of robbers".

The emperor Marcian and the empress Pulcheria called a new council to Chalcedon, across the water from Constantinople. Leo sent legates and his letter, the Tome. In the autumn of 451 the legates demanded that Dioscorus not sit as a judge, and the bishops declared Flavian orthodox. Hearing the Tome, they cried, "Peter has spoken thus through Leo", and "Leo and Cyril taught the same thing".

The minutes, kept by the winners, say Dioscorus was summoned three times to a session without the emperor's officials, who ran the council. He answered that his guards held him back, and that he would come if the officials and senate were there. Leo's legates deposed him for receiving Eutyches, excommunicating Leo, refusing to come and many other charges. He was exiled to Gangra.

A draft then said Christ was from two natures, and most bishops cried, "Either let the definition stand or we go." Leo's legates threatened, if the bishops did not agree to Leo's letter, to leave and hold a council in the West. The officials noted that this was Dioscorus's own phrase, and Anatolius answered that Dioscorus was condemned "not for the faith". "The most holy Leo or Dioscorus?" the officials asked. The bishops cried, "We believe as Leo", and the officials ordered "two natures" added. So the Definition said not "from two natures" but "in two": "One and the same Jesus Christ, Son, Lord, Only-begotten, is known in two natures: without confusion, without change, without division, without separation." Every other creed was banned. The Syriac Orthodox chronicle remembers that Dioscorus was asked to sign it and take back his see, and answered that he would sooner see his hand cut off.

The council also put monks under their bishops and ranked Constantinople next after Rome, a ruling Leo declared void.

Later emperors set the council aside before the Greek and Latin churches made it their fourth general council.

When Proterius took Dioscorus's place, Alexandria's clergy, monks and people broke communion with him and kept Dioscorus's name as a faithful priest. Priscus, a historian then in the city, says a crowd burned soldiers alive in the old temple of Serapis. The emperor's troops, he says, abused "the wives and daughters of the Alexandrians". The Syriac chronicle says soldiers killed believers at the altar. The Coptic Church's own History says Dioscorus suffered "for the orthodox faith" and died with "the crown of martyrdom".

Armenia, absent from Chalcedon, refused it by synod early in the sixth century, by the account of Ormanian, an Armenian patriarch, while Rome and Constantinople quarrelled over its ranking and the Church of the East received it in part.

In their own later words, the churches of Egypt, Syria and Armenia condemned Eutyches too. They confessed Christ as one nature from two, and their chronicle said Dioscorus's faith was like that of Athanasius, and Cyril. Severus of Antioch said the council deserved condemnation because it tried to cure Eutyches's error with "an opposite infection", the teaching of Nestorius. Their chronicle said: "That council tore into ten thousand pieces the whole robe of Christ, woven in one piece from the top."

In Palestine, monks back from the council made Theodosius bishop of Jerusalem in place of its bishop, Juvenal.

From exile at Gangra, Dioscorus wrote that Christ's flesh was not "foreign from our body".
