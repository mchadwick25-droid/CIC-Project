Dioscorus, bishop of Alexandria, was on trial, and the minutes of his own council were being read. He broke in: "I accept 'from two'. 'Two' I do not accept. I am forced to be shameless: my soul is at stake."

At issue was Christ's nature, what he is as God and as man, and his person, who he is. Flavian of Constantinople had condemned Eutyches, a monastery head who said Christ was of two natures before the union but of one after. Then a council under Dioscorus restored Eutyches and deposed Flavian, a meeting Leo of Rome called "not of judges but of robbers".

The emperor Marcian and the empress Pulcheria called a new council to Chalcedon, across the water from Constantinople. Leo sent legates and his letter, the Tome. In the autumn of 451 the legates demanded that Dioscorus not sit as a judge, and the bishops declared Flavian, now dead, orthodox. Hearing the Tome, they cried, "Peter has spoken thus through Leo", and "Leo and Cyril taught the same thing".

The minutes, kept by the winners, say Dioscorus was summoned three times to a session the imperial officials did not attend. He answered that his guards held him back, and that he would come if the officials and senate were there. The legates deposed him for receiving Eutyches, excommunicating Leo and refusing to come. The Syriac Orthodox chronicle remembers him saying he would sooner see his hand cut off than sign. He was exiled to Gangra.

Then a draft said Christ was "of two natures", and most bishops cried, "Either let the definition stand or we go." Leo's legates said that if the bishops did not agree to Leo's letter, they would ask leave to go and hold a council in the West. The judges noted that the draft used Dioscorus's own phrase. Anatolius answered that Dioscorus was condemned "not for the faith", but for excommunicating Leo and refusing the summonses. "The most holy Leo or Dioscorus?" the judges asked. When the bishops cried, "We believe as Leo", the judges ordered "two natures" added: "One and the same Jesus Christ, Son, Lord, Only-begotten, is known in two natures: without confusion, without change, without division, without separation." Every other creed was banned.

The council also put monks under their bishops and ranked Constantinople next after Rome, a ruling Leo declared void.

Later emperors set the council aside before the Greek and Latin churches made it their fourth. Egypt refused it. Priscus, a historian then in Alexandria, says a crowd burned soldiers alive in the old temple of Serapis, and the emperor's troops abused "the wives and daughters of the Alexandrians". The Syriac chronicle says soldiers killed believers at the altar. The Coptic Church's own History says Dioscorus suffered "for the orthodox faith" and died with "the crown of martyrdom".

These churches had condemned Eutyches too: Dioscorus wrote from exile that Christ's flesh was not "foreign from our body". They confessed Christ one nature from two, and their chronicle said his faith was like that of Athanasius, and Cyril. Severus of Antioch said the council deserved condemnation because it tried to cure Eutyches's error with "an opposite infection", the teaching of Nestorius. Their chronicle said: "That council tore into ten thousand pieces the whole robe of Christ, woven in one piece from the top."

Armenia, absent from Chalcedon, condemned Eutyches by synod, by the account of Ormanian, an Armenian patriarch, and early in the sixth century refused the council. Rome and Constantinople quarrelled over its ranking, and the Church of the East received it in part.

In Palestine, monks back from Chalcedon made one of their own, Theodosius, bishop of Jerusalem in place of Juvenal.

In Alexandria, clergy, monks and people broke communion with the new bishop, Proterius, and kept Dioscorus's name as a faithful priest.
