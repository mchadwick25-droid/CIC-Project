Dioscorus, bishop of Alexandria, was on trial as the minutes of his council of 449 were read. He broke in: "I accept 'from two'. 'Two' I do not accept. I am forced to be shameless: my soul is at stake."

The quarrel was over Christ's nature, what he is as God and as man. Flavian of Constantinople had condemned Eutyches, who said Christ was of two natures before the union but of one after, and Dioscorus's council had restored Eutyches and deposed Flavian.

The emperor Marcian and the empress Pulcheria called a new council to Chalcedon, across the water from Constantinople. Leo of Rome sent legates and his letter, the Tome. In the autumn of 451 the legates demanded that Dioscorus not sit as a judge, and Flavian was declared orthodox. Hearing the Tome, the bishops cried, "Peter has spoken thus through Leo", and "Leo and Cyril taught the same thing".

The minutes, the winners' record, say that, summoned three times to a session held without the emperor's officials, Dioscorus answered that his guards held him back, and he would come if the officials and senate were there. The legates deposed him for receiving Eutyches, excommunicating Leo, refusing to come and other charges.

A draft said Christ was from two natures, and most bishops cried, "Either let the definition stand or we go." Leo's legates threatened to leave and hold a council in the West. The officials, who ran the council, noted this was Dioscorus's phrase; Anatolius answered that he was condemned "not for the faith". Asked, "The most holy Leo or Dioscorus?", the bishops cried, "We believe as Leo", and the officials ordered "two natures" added. So the Definition said not "from two" but "in two natures": "One and the same Jesus Christ, Son, Lord, Only-begotten, is known in two natures: without confusion, without change, without division, without separation." Every other creed was banned. The Syriac Orthodox chronicle says that, asked to sign it and take back his see, Dioscorus would sooner have seen his hand cut off.

The council also put monks under bishops and ranked Constantinople after Rome, a ruling Leo declared void. Later emperors set the council aside before the Greek and Latin churches made it their fourth.

When Proterius replaced Dioscorus, Alexandria's clergy, monks and people broke communion with him. Priscus, a historian then in the city, says a crowd burned soldiers alive in the old temple of Serapis, and the emperor's troops abused "the wives and daughters of the Alexandrians". The Syriac chronicle says soldiers killed believers at the altar. The Coptic Church's History says Dioscorus suffered "for the orthodox faith" and died with "the crown of martyrdom".

By the account of Ormanian, an Armenian patriarch, Armenia refused the council by synod early in the sixth century, Rome and Constantinople disputed its ranking, and the Church of the East received it in part.

In their own later words, the churches of Egypt, Syria and Armenia condemned Eutyches too. They confessed Christ as one nature from two, and their chronicle said Dioscorus's faith was like that of Athanasius, and Cyril. Severus of Antioch said the council deserved condemnation because it tried to cure Eutyches's error with "an opposite infection", the teaching of Nestorius. Their chronicle said: "That council tore into ten thousand pieces the whole robe of Christ, woven in one piece from the top."

In Palestine, monks back from the council made Theodosius bishop of Jerusalem in place of Juvenal.

From exile at Gangra, Dioscorus wrote that Christ's flesh was not "foreign from our body".
