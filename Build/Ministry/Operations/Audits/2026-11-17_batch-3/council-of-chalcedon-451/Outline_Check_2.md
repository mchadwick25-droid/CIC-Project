# Outline check, round 2: the Council of Chalcedon (451)

## The heart, written before opening Outline.md

From the census entry (`knownFor`, `decided`, `outcome`), Source_Sheet.md and what the council is known for.

The council met at Chalcedon in October 451. The emperor Marcian, with Pulcheria, called it after the death of Theodosius II (A1, A4). Its spine is one question: how Christ is God and man. Its answer was the Definition. Christ is "one and the same" Son, perfect in Godhead and in manhood, of one being with the Father and with us. He is confessed "in two natures", without confusion, change, division or separation, united in one person (B15, H1). It received Leo's Tome and Cyril's letters, and meant the Definition to shut out two errors at once: Nestorius's split into two Sons, and Eutyches's mixing of the natures (H2). The ban on any other faith came with it (B15).

On the way it undid Ephesus 449. It declared Flavian orthodox (B7). It deposed Dioscorus of Alexandria, who said "from two" and refused "two" (B8). The sentence gives canonical grounds and three refused summonses; Anatolius said "not for the faith"; the refusers say he fell for the faith (B11, B12, B20). The bishops wanted no new creed. A draft saying "from two" was pushed into "in two natures" under the threat of a Western council and the judges' question "Leo or Dioscorus?" (B14, B14a).

Other business it decided: monks under bishops (canon 4), Jerusalem's three Palestines (B21), and Constantinople ranked next after Rome (canon 28), which Leo annulled (C1–C4).

What it did to the churches. This is the weight. The Greek and Latin churches came to hold it as the fourth general council: a test of communion in 553 and, for Gregory, one of four councils received like the four Gospels (E1, H3, H4). That took time; emperors set it aside in the 470s and 480s (E1a). Egypt and the Syriac churches refused it, and Armenia later did too. Their memory is of a council that "tore into ten thousand rents the perfect Robe of Christ" and of Dioscorus as confessor (E2, E4, E5, H7, H8). Refusal turned violent in Alexandria and Palestine (E2, E3). The split lasted: the refusing churches remain a communion of their own (H6). The Church of the East received it only partly (E7).

So the heart: a council that set the classic definition of Christ as one person in two natures, against both Nestorius and Eutyches, at the price of deposing Alexandria's bishop and splitting the Eastern churches into those who received it and a lasting family of churches who would not.

## The outline's heart against mine

They agree in substance. Both centre on the Definition (one person, "in two natures", the four "withouts"). Both say it was aimed at Nestorius and Eutyches at once. Both give the Tome's reason for "two". Both say the same decision forked the churches into the Greek and Latin on one side and Egypt, Syria and Armenia on the other, a fork that lasted. The outline's heart leaves out the deposition of Dioscorus and the other business. Beats 4 to 6 carry both, inside the word budget, so no major contribution is missing. Beat 1 tells the heart inside the first 130 words, which is the first minute at Daniel's 130 a minute. One claim in the heart goes past the sources (finding 1).

## Findings

**1. BLOCKING: "The churches of Egypt and Syria refused it from the start" (heart).** No held source has Syria refusing in 451–452. Antioch's bishop was at the council and agreed the Phoenicias and Arabia with Juvenal (Session VII). The resistance told at once is in Alexandria (E2, Evagrius 3363–3398) and among Palestine's monks (E3, Evagrius 3408–3420). The Syriac refusal is voiced by Severus of Antioch, about 508 (E4), and by the chronicle drawn from Zachariah's history of 450–491 (B20, E4). The outline's own Do not say row ("Egypt and Palestine resisted at once") already says this, so the heart contradicts it. A writer gets the heart first and would repeat it.
*Correction:* in the heart, replace "The churches of Egypt and Syria refused it from the start, and Armenia by synod in the sixth century" with: "Egypt refused it at once, and monks in Palestine rose against it. In Syria the refusal is voiced within two generations, by Severus of Antioch and the Syriac chronicle. Armenia refused it by synod in the sixth century." Add a Do not say row: "Syria refused it at once / from the start" → "Egypt and Palestine resisted at once; the Syriac refusal is voiced by Severus (about 508) and the Syriac chronicle" (E3, E4, B20).

**2. MINOR: beat 3, "asked for time", cites a line that does not hold it.** The Latin at Mansi 6 92457–92465 records only that the Illyrian and Palestinian bishops doubted that passage; a chapter of Cyril was then read to them. The request for delay is Percival's bracket, npnf214 20008–20010 ("a delay of a few days was asked for"), and Hefele 16825–16832 (Atticus of Nicopolis). *Correction:* cite "B5, Latin, for the doubt; npnf214 20008–20010 (Percival, editor) for the delay asked".

**3. MINOR: beat 4, "held without the emperor's officials".** Percival writes that they "do not seem to have been present" (npnf214 20018–20020). Dioscorus's minuted answer asks for them to be there, which supports it, but the editor hedges. *Correction:* "held, it seems, without the emperor's officials", or keep it in Dioscorus's own minuted demand that the officials and senate be present (Mansi 6 95088–95091).

**4. MINOR: What came of them 2, "drove soldiers into the old temple of Serapis".** Priscus: the crowd stoned the troops and put them to flight. When they took refuge in the old temple of Serapis, the crowd stormed it and burned them alive (Evagrius 3375–3380). Evagrius also says the city was divided: some wanted Dioscorus back, others upheld Proterius (3366–3372). *Correction:* "put the soldiers to flight, and when they took refuge in the old temple of Serapis, stormed it and burned them alive".

**5. MINOR: "the Coptic Synaxarium still keeps his feast on 7 Tout".** The held text is a medieval calendar in Basset's 1907 edition (14326–14331, 14436–14437). It shows the feast kept in that calendar, not present practice. *Correction:* "the Coptic calendar of saints keeps his feast on 7 Tout". Add a Do not say row: "still keeps / keeps to this day" → "keeps his feast on 7 Tout, in its calendar of saints" (E2).

**6. MINOR: opening 1 quotes Evagrius ("the change of a single letter") beyond the three quotes.** The writer may use only the outline's quotes. Taken as a quote, it would be a fourth. And Evagrius's own point is that the two phrases really meant the same (3450–3456), which is the council side's view. *Correction:* mark it in Openings as paraphrase, not a quote: "Evagrius, a historian on the council's side, wrote that the whole quarrel turned on a single letter." Add that his view that the difference was small stays credited to him (as the existing Do not say row already says).

**7. MINOR: two Theodosiuses.** Beat 2 names the emperor Theodosius II, and What came of them 5 names the monk Theodosius made bishop of Jerusalem (Zachariah 2105, "one Theodosius, a monk"; Evagrius 3418–3420). Heard once, a listener may take them for one man. *Correction:* add a Do not say row: "Theodosius" alone in the Palestine item → "a monk named Theodosius", or leave out his name.

## Checked and holding

- **Quotes.** I made my own renderings first. Quote 1, from Evagrius's Greek (Bidez–Parmentier 5243–5246), reads: "one and the same Jesus Christ, Son, Lord, Only-begotten, known in two natures without confusion, without change, without division, without separation". That matches the outline, and "in two natures" is the Greek's ἐν δύο φύσεσιν. Quote 2, from npnf214 19256–19259 and the Mansi Latin ("Cogor … impudenter agere: de anima est mihi sermo"), reads: "I accept 'from two'; 'two' I do not accept.
