# Source sheet: the Council of Chalcedon (451)

All files are under `cic/texts/`. Line numbers are the file's own. For `npnf214_seven-ecumenical-councils.xml`, `npnf212_leo-great-gregory-great.xml` and `npnf203_…xml` the numbers are raw XML lines; quotations are given with the markup stripped. OCR slips are kept as printed and marked [sic].

Witness kinds: **own** (their own words), **acts** (the council's minutes, a contemporary record kept by the winning side), **contemporary**, **later** (with rough gap), **late legend**, **editor** (a modern editor or historian's claim, not a primary text).

Census spoken names (`cic-website/data/world-census.json`): the council is "Council of Chalcedon", informal "The Fourth Ecumenical Council". Traditions: Imperial and Juridical Christianity ("Church and Empire"); The Church of Alexandria and Miaphysite Egypt ("Church of Alexandria"); Syriac Orthodox (West Syriac) Christianity ("The Syriac Orthodox Church"); The Church of the East under Persia ("Church of the East"); Armenian Christianity after Avarayr. ## A. Setting

**A1. Place and building.** Acts, Definition heading. npnf214 20190–20194. Acts. Documented.
> "The holy, great, and ecumenical synod, assembled by the grace of God and the command of our most religious and Christian Emperors, Marcian and Valentinian, Augusti, at Chalcedon, the metropolis of the Bithynian Province, in the martyry of the holy and victorious martyr Euphemia, has decreed as follows:"

**A2. Where the church stood.** Hefele, v3, `hefele_history-of-the-councils-v3-431-451_clark1883.txt` 15136–15141. Editor (1883, citing Evagrius II.3). Widely Accepted.
> "The whole of the sessions were held in the Church of S. Euphemia the Martyr, which was situated in front of the town on the Bosporus, only two stadia or twelve hundred paces from it, on a gentle slope opposite Constantinople, and offered a magnificent view over the sea and the fields."

**A3. Session dates.** Hefele 15761, 22215. Editor. Widely Accepted (the acts themselves date only the deposition, B9).
> "Sec. 189. First Session, October 8, 451." / "Sec. 201. Sixteenth and last Session, November 1, 451."

**A4. Who called it; first set at Nicaea.** Leo, Letter XCV to Pulcheria, npnf212 8610–8618. Own (Leo, 451). Documented.
> "Your clemency's command, therefore, that a Synod should be held at Nicæa … and your gently expressed refusal of my request that it should be held in Italy … I have received in a spirit so far removed from scorn as to nominate two of my fellow-bishops and fellow-presbyters respectively to represent me"

**A5. Why moved to Chalcedon (the court's reason).** Hefele 15019–15025, quoting Marcian's letter. Editor citing an imperial letter. Widely Accepted.
> "the bishops might, if they pleased, remove to Chalcedon, because this was so near the capital that he could attend in person both to the business in Constantinople and to that of the Council."

**A6. Why moved (the refusers' reading).** Zachariah, Chronicle III.1, `zachariah-rhetor_chronicle_hamiltonbrooks1899.txt` 2267–2270. Later (Syriac compilation finished 569, from Zachariah's Greek history of 450–491, written 491–518, per editors 311, 363–364, 466). Contested.
> "for the king issued a new order that the assembly should be convened to Chalcedon, so that Nicea might not be the meeting-place of rebels."

**A7. Called at Leo's urging (refusers' account).** Zachariah III preface, 2072–2075. Later. Contested.
> "the number of the bishops was five hundred and sixty-seven, who were brought together in consequence of the exertion of Leo of Rome, and the letter that he wrote to the king and his wife Pulcheria."

**A8. Evagrius on what caused it.** Evagrius II.2, `evagrius-scholasticus_ecclesiastical-history_walford1846.txt` 2801–2827. Later (Chalcedonian side; Hefele/Percival note he wrote "somewhat more than a hundred years after", npnf214 20396). Widely Accepted for the petitions; the trampling claim is Eusebius's charge.
> "the emperor is addressed both by the legates of Leo … who alleged that Dioscorus had, during the second council of Ephesus, refused to receive the epistle of Leo … and also by those who had been contumeliously treated by Dioscorus, intreating that their case might be submitted to the decision of a synod. … He also said, that Flavian had even been brought to a miserable end by being thrust and trampled on by Dioscorus himself. These circumstances caused the synod at Chalcedon to be assembled"

**A9. Armenia was absent.** Ormanian, `ormanian_church-of-armenia_gregory1912.txt` 2445–2456. Editor (former Armenian patriarch of Constantinople, 1912; the Armenian Church's own account). Widely Accepted.
> "Armenia was not concerned with these quarrels until the beginning of the sixth century. The Councils which were summoned for and against Eutyches took place without her knowledge ; that of Chalcedon, which met on October 8th, 451, was not convened until after the great battle of Avarair (May 26th, 451). … the patriarch and the bishops were either in prison or in exile"

**A10. Almost all Eastern.** Hefele 15751–15758. Editor. Widely Accepted.
> "Yet all these many bishops who were present, with the exception of the Roman legates and two Africans (Aurelius of Adrumetum and Rusticianus), were purely Greeks and Orientals"

## B. The issue and the events

**B1. Eutyches in 448.** Syriac acts of Ephesus 449 (reading the 448 minutes), `council-of-ephesus-449_second-synod-of-ephesus-syriac-acts_perry1881.txt` 23842–23844. Acts (in a Syriac version Perry dates "about a century after the events", 1325–1327, from the Syrian monastery in the Nitrian desert, 1240–1242 — editor). Documented.
> "I confess that our LORD was of Two Natures before the Union, but after the Union I confess Him to be of One Nature."

**B2. 449: Eutyches restored; Juvenal's vote.** Perry 24017–24020. Acts. Documented.
> "Juvenal, of Jerusalem, said :— Inasmuch as he (Eutyches) has frequently made the declaration that he is Orthodox, I withdraw what I said about him. And I, also, decree and vote that he be restored to his Monastery and to his Grade."

**B3. 449: Flavian deposed, protests.** Perry 24117–24138. Acts. Documented.
> "we, confirming these things, adjudge them … (viz.) Flavian and Eusebius, alien from all the Honor of the Priesthood and of the Episcopate … Flavian said :— I repudiate you. I protest."

**B4. Leo on Ephesus 449.** Leo, Letter XCV, npnf212 8639–8641. Own. Documented.
> "all that was allowed to be perpetrated in that meeting, not of judges but of robbers, at Ephesus"

**B5. The Tome's key sentence.** Leo, Tome, as read at Chalcedon, npnf214 19797–19801. Own. Documented.
> "For each "form" does the acts which belong to it, in communion with the other; the Word, that is, performing what belongs to the Word, and the flesh carrying out what belongs to the flesh; the one of these shines out in miracles, the other succumbs to injuries."
Latin, Mansi 6, `mansi_sacrorum-conciliorum-collectio-tomus-6-441-451-lat_welter-facsimile1901.txt` 92457–92465, with the doubt of the Illyrian and Palestinian bishops at that very passage. Acts. Documented.
> "Similiter cum legeretur ex epiftola pars, quz continet; Agit enim utraque forma cum alterius communione, quod proprium eft , Verbo fcilicet operante quod Verbi eft, & carne exequente quod carnis eft, Unum horum coruícat miraculis, aliud fuccumbit injuríis.) Dubitantibus Illyricianis & Palaftinis reverendiffimis epifcopis"

The delay asked for is not in that Latin; it is Percival's bracket, npnf214 20008–20010 (editor; Hefele 16825–16832): "Some explanations were asked by the Illyrian bishops and the answers were found satisfactory, but yet a delay of a few days was asked for". Widely Accepted.

**B6. Session I: Dioscorus not to sit.** npnf214 19159–19167. Acts. Documented.
> "We received directions at the hands of the most blessed and apostolic bishop of the Roman city … which directions say that Dioscorus is not to be allowed a seat in this assembly"

**B7. Session I: Flavian vindicated.** npnf214 19239–19243 (Lucentius). Acts. Documented.
> "Since the faith of Flavian of blessed memory agrees with the Apostolic See and the tradition of the fathers it is just that the sentence by which he was condemned by the heretics should be turned back upon them by this most holy synod."

**B8. Session I: Dioscorus's stand ("from two").** npnf214 19254–19260; the Greek is printed at 19256–19259. Acts (his words as minuted by the winning side). Documented.
> "Dioscorus, the most reverend Archbishop of Alexandria said, I receive "the of two;" "the two" I do not receive (τὸ ἐκ δύο δέχομαι· τὸ δύο, οὐ δέχομαι). I am forced to be impudent, but the matter is one which touches my soul."
Mansi 6 65950–65956 (Latin; the margin note at 65905 fills the gap), Acts: "Dioícorus reveren- … diffimus epifcopus Alexandrie dixit: * Ex duabus fuícipio , duas non fufcipio . Cogor" + margin "mihi res et & impudenter agere: de anima" + "eft mihi fermo ." The Greek column (65973–65975) is garbled but legible in part: "dvay= xe opat … reel rhs Luxe pol esw ó Aón(Q-" (ἀναγκάζομαι … περὶ ψυχῆς μοί ἐστιν ὁ λόγος, "the matter concerns my soul": his salvation is at stake, not his feelings).

**B9. Session I close: the Illyrians.** npnf214 19261–19268. Percival's bracketed summary (editor), not the acts' text. Widely Accepted.
> "This met with the approval of all the bishops except those of Illyrica who said: "We all have erred, let us all be pardoned.""

**B10. Session II: the Tome acclaimed.** npnf214 19996–20007. Acts. Documented.
> "This is the faith of the fathers, this is the faith of the Apostles. … Peter has spoken thus through Leo. So taught the Apostles. Piously and truly did Leo teach, so taught Cyril. … Leo and Cyril taught the same thing … These are the things Dioscorus hid away."

**B11. Session III: the deposition.** npnf214 20082–20090. Acts (the sentence). Documented.
> "We do you to wit that on the thirteenth day of the month of October you were deposed from the episcopate and made a stranger to all ecclesiastical order by the holy and ecumenical synod, on account of your disregard of the divine canons, and of your disobedience to this holy and ecumenical synod and on account of the other crimes of which you have been found guilty, for even when called to answer your accusers three times by this holy and great synod according to the divine canons you did not come."
The legates' sentence claims Peter's authority, 20055–20062: "the most holy and blessed Leo … through us, and through this present most holy synod together with the thrice blessed and all-glorious Peter the Apostle … hath stripped him of the episcopate". Percival's note (20065–20067, editor): the bishops voted "usually on the ground of his refusal to appear when thrice summoned."

**B11a. The legates' sentence: its grounds.** Mansi 6 99302–99303, 99315–99318, 99428–99441 (Latin). Acts, Session III. Documented. Five grounds: he received Eutyches into communion irregularly before the 449 council; he would not let Leo's letter be read; he dictated excommunication against Leo; many written charges were laid against him; he refused three summonses.
> "Eutychem … irregulariter in communionem fufcepit , priufquam ! confideret in Ephefo" / "neque epiftolam legi permifit beatifsimi pape Leonis , {criptam ad fan&z memoriz Fiavianum" / "fumpfit enim & excommunicationem dictare adverfus fan&iffimum & beatiffimum archiepifcopum magnz Rome Leonem , * infuper etiam multi libelli ( multis ) ejus pleni iniquitatibus, contra eum in fando & magno concilio oblati funt , & femel, & fecundo , & tertio per P» Dei amantiffimos epifcopos regulariter vocatus, minime voluit obedire"
The petitions: npnf214 20043–20048 (Percival's bracket, editor): "four petitions … The first two by deacons of Alexandria, the third by a quondam presbyter of the diocese, and the fourth by a layman also of Alexandria."

**B11b. Session III met without the officials; Dioscorus's answers.** npnf214 20018–20020 (Percival's bracket, editor): "[The imperial representatives do not seem to have been present". Hefele 16986–16988, 16996–16998, 17011–17016, 17029–17032 (editor, from the acts, Mansi vi. 986). Documented as minuted.
> "he had answered them that "he would willingly appear, but his guards prevented him."" / "Dioscurus answered them also that he would willingly come, but that he was prevented by his guards, the Magistriani and Scholarii (imperial officers)" / "He demanded that his affair should again be brought forward in the presence of the commissioners and senators." / "Dioscurus now declared that he was ill; and when the deputies met him, he said he had just recovered, but he returned to his former excuse that he would now appear if the imperial commissioners were present"
Mansi 6 95088–95091 (Latin), his words: "Diofcorus epifcopus dixit : Semel dixi, quia poftulavi gloriofiffimos & magnificentiffimos judices & facratifimum fenatum adeffe his que nunc debent examinari." Hefele's word "excuse" is the editor's.
Michael the Syrian 3537–3539 (refusers' side, later): "Dioscorus dit : « Je suis gardé ; s'ils veulent me laisser descendre, qu'ils le disent. » Dioscorus dit de nouveau : « Je suis prêt à me rendre près du saint synode ; mais on ne me le permet pas'.»"

**B12. "Not for the faith" (Anatolius, in the acts).** Mansi 7, `mansi_sacrorum-conciliorum-collectio-tomus-7-451-492-lat_welter-facsimile1901.txt` 9803–9813 (Latin column; Greek column garbled). Acts, Session V. Documented.
> "Anatolius reverendi(fimus archiepifcopus Con- … ftantinopolis dixit: * Propter fidem non eff … damnatus Diofcorus , fed quia excommuni- … cationem fecit domino archiepifcopo Leoni, … & tertio vocatus ett, & noh venit, & ideo … damnatus eft."
Context (Hefele 18147–18160, editor, from the acts; Mansi 7 9797–9801, garbled Latin): Anatolius said it straight after the judges' remark that "Dioscurus asserts that he condemned Flavian for having maintained that there are two natures in Christ; in the new doctrinal formula, however, it stands: Christ is of two natures."
Each side used the words its own way. The council's side: Dioscorus fell for canonical offences, not doctrine. The refusers turned the same words against the sentence. Zachariah III.1, 2315–2325 (later, Contested): "the Nestorian party, with Theodoret at their head, brought about the deprivation of Dioscorus ; because he said, "It is right for us to believe that Christ became incarnate from two matures [sic]; and we should not confess two natures after the union, like Nestorius." And then Anatolius … cried out in words to this effect, "Not for the faith is Dioscorus deposed ; but he is set at nought for refusing to hold communion with the chief priest, my lord Leo."" Michael the Syrian 3542–3545 calls the sentence "inique" and cites its grounds as proof (B20). The Coptic History says he suffered "for the orthodox faith" (E2).

**B13. Session IV: one by one.** npnf214 20123–20132. Acts plus Percival's bracket. Documented.
> "let each one of the bishops here assembled declare whether the epistle of most blessed archbishop Leo is in accordance with the exposition of the 318 fathers … [To this question the bishops answered one by one, until 161 separate opinions had been given, when the rest of the bishops were asked by the imperial judges to give their votes in a body (col. 508).]"

**B14. Session II and V: no new creed wanted; the stand-off.** npnf214 19340–19344; 20142–20178. Acts plus Percival's brackets. Documented.
> "The most reverend bishops cried out, we make no new exposition in writing." (19340–19341)
> "If they do not agree to the letter of that apostolic and blessed man, Pope Leo, give directions that we be given our letters of dismission, and let a synod be held there [i.e. in the West]." (20144–20146)
> "Dioscorus acknowledged that he accepted the expression "of two natures," but not that there were two natures. But the most holy archbishop Leo says that there are two natures in Christ unchangeably, inseparably, unconfusedly united in the one only-begotten Son our Saviour. Which would you follow, the most holy Leo or Dioscorus? The most reverend bishops cried out: We believe as Leo." (20165–20173)
**B14a. Session V: the first draft and the bishops who wanted it.** Hefele 18061 (date), 18103–18106, 18127–18129 (editor, from the acts, Mansi vii. 99 ff.). Widely Accepted for the date; Documented as minuted.
> "SEC. 193. Fifth Session, October 22,451." / "Anatolius replied, asking "whether it had not yesterday given universal satisfaction," which produced the acclamation: "It is excellent, and contains the Catholic faith"" / "The majority, however, wished to retain the doctrinal formula which had been read, and demanded in many acclamations that it should be subscribed by all"
And the acts themselves, npnf214 20154–20156: "The most reverend bishops cried out: Many years to the Emperor! Either let the definition [i.e. the one presented at this session] stand or we go." Then the judges' question and order (20165–20178, above): "Add then to the definition, according to the judgment of our most holy father Leo, that there are two natures in Christ united unchangeably, inseparably, unconfusedly."
Percival's bracket, 20147–20153 (editor): the emperor threatened that "they all should be sent home and a new council called in the West."

**B15. The Definition.** npnf214 20278–20298 (English); Greek in Evagrius II.4, `evagrius-scholasticus_historia-ecclesiastica-grc_bidez-parmentier1898.txt` 5243–5246; Latin in Mansi 7 10892–10902. Acts (copied by Evagrius). Documented.
> "This one and the same Jesus Christ, the only-begotten Son [of God] must be confessed to be in two natures, unconfusedly, immutably, indivisibly, inseparably [united], and that without the distinction of natures being taken away by such union, but rather the peculiar property of each nature being preserved and being united in one Person and subsistence, not separated or divided into two persons" (English)
> "ἕνα καὶ τὸν αὐτὸν ᾿Ιησοῦν Χριστὸν υἱὸν κύριον μονογενῆ ἐν δύο φύσεσιν ἀσυγχύτως, ἀτρέπτως, ἀδιαιρέτως, ἀχωρίστως γνωριζόμενον" (Evagrius's Greek)
> "unigenitum, * fe , immutabiliter, indivife, 4 infeparabiliter agnofcendum ; … in duabus naturis inconfu" [sic, OCR splits the line] (Mansi Latin)
And the ban, npnf214 20299–20312: "no one shall be suffered to bring forward a different faith … if they be Bishops or clerics let them be deposed … but if they be monks or laics: let them be anathematized."

**B16. "In" or "of" two natures.** Hefele as Percival prints him, npnf214 20361–20370, and Evagrius's Greek (B15). Editor's argument. Dominant Modern Reconstruction.
> "The present Greek text has ἐκ δύο φύσεων while the old Latin translation has, in duabus naturis. … there can be no doubt whatever that the old Latin translator had the more accurate text before him, and that it was originally ἐν δύο φύσεσιν."
(Mansi's Greek column at 10915–10916 is garbled but reads "£x duo pú- ciu", i.e. ἐκ δύο φύσεων.)

**B17. Signatures.** npnf214 22617–22621. Percival's bracket (editor). Widely Accepted.
> "[ The canon (number XXVIII.), was then read, and the signatures, in all 192 … Only a week before 350had [sic] signed the Definition of faith."

**B18. Session VI: the emperor present.** npnf214 20470–20484. Acts. Documented.
> "[The Emperor was present in person and addressed the Council …] … all the most God-beloved bishops cried out: Many years to our Emperor and Empress, the pious, the Christian. … To the Priest, the Emperor. Thou hast straightened out the churches, victor of thine enemies, teacher of the faith. Many years to the pious Empress, the lover of Christ."

**B19. Theodoret received.** `npnf203_theodoret-jerome-gennadius-rufinus.xml` 3331–3338 (Blomfield Jackson quoting the acts, Labbe iv. 621). Acts at second hand. Documented.
> "‘He is a heretic! He is a Nestorian! Away with the heretic! …’ Theodoret, the most reverend bishop, said, ‘Anathema to Nestorius and to whoever denies that the Holy Virgin Mary is the Parent of God, and who divides the only begotten Son into two Sons. I have subscribed the definition of faith, and the epistle of the most holy Archbishop Leo."

**B20. The refusers' account of the sessions.** Zachariah III.1. Later (c. 40–70 years for Zachariah's Greek; compiled 569). Contested throughout.

Whereupon he was sent into banishment to Gangra"

Later. Inferential-Thin for the words; Documented as the refusers' memory.

**B21. Juvenal's turn (refusers' account) and the acts' record.** Zachariah III.3, 2591–2604. Later, hostile. Contested.
> "And at first when he went there, he made a great struggle, along with Dioscorus, on behalf of the faith. But because the royal pressure was brought to bear … and because the king also promised that he would give the three provinces of Palestine to the honour of the see of Jerusalem; then the eyes of his mind were darkened, and he left Dioscorus the champion alone"
The acts (Session VII) record only an agreement: npnf214 20500–20511, Documented: "the most holy Juvenal, bishop of Jerusalem, or rather the most holy Church which is under him, shall have under his own power the three Palestines". Late legend frame. Contested.

## C. Other business

**C1. Monks put under bishops (canon 4).** npnf214 20828–20836. Acts. Documented.
> "it is decreed that no one anywhere build or found a monastery or oratory contrary to the will of the bishop of the city; and that the monks in every city and district shall be subject to the bishop"

**C2. Constantinople's rank (canon 28).** npnf214 22225–22236. Acts. Documented.
> "the One Hundred and Fifty most religious Bishops, actuated by the same consideration, gave equal privileges to the most holy throne of New Rome, justly judging that the city which is honoured with the Sovereignty and the Senate, and enjoys equal privileges with the old imperial Rome, should in ecclesiastical matters also be magnified as she is, and rank next after her"

**C3. The legates' protest (Session XVI).** npnf214 22623–22634. Acts. Documented.
> "it was brought to pass by circumventing the holy bishops so that they were forced to sign the as yet unwritten canons … The most reverend bishops cried out: No one was forced."

> "But the bishops' assents, which are opposed to the regulations of the holy canons composed at Nicæa in conjunction with your faithful Grace, we do not recognize, and by the blessed Apostle Peter's authority we absolutely dis-annul in comprehensive terms"

**C5. Chalcedon made a metropolis in name.** npnf214 20486–20490. Acts. Documented. (Minor; leave out.)

## E. Impact, heaviest first

**E1. The Definition became the standard of the Greek and Latin churches.** Ormanian 2515–2522. Editor (Armenian). Widely Accepted.
> "Later, the Greek and Latin Churches revoked their opposition and recognised it as the fourth Oecumenic Council. The Armenian Church would have nothing to do with this transaction, which was prompted by designs which had no bearing on theology."
Evagrius, more than a century on, copied the Definition whole into his history (B15; npnf214 20396–20398, Hefele). Percival's own title for the council: "Fourth Ecumenical Council" (npnf214 18940). What its supporters defended: Leo's Tome as "agreeable to the confession of the great Peter, and as it were a common pillar against misbelievers" (Definition, npnf214 20267–20269, Documented).

**E1a. It did not hold at once in the East.** Evagrius III.4, the circular of Basiliscus, Walford 5411, 5457–5466. Later (Evagrius quotes the document). Documented.
> "the so-called tome of Leo, and all things said and done at Chalcedon in innovation upon the before-mentioned holy symbol of the three hundred and eighteen holy fathers, whether by way of definition of faith, or setting forth of symbols, or of interpretation, or instruction, or discourse; we ordain that these shall be anathematised both here and every where by the most holy bishops in every church"
Evagrius III.14, Zeno's Henotikon (482, Ormanian 2434–2435), Walford 5919, 6027–6030. Documented.
> "every one who has held or holds any other opinion, either at the present or another time, whether at Chalcedon or in any synod whatever, we anathematise; and specially the before-mentioned Nestorius and Eutyches"
Ormanian 2496–2499 (editor): "the Council of Marcian had been disavowed by his successors ; and by the decrees of Basiliscus, of Zeno, and of Anastasius the Chalcedonian profession of faith had been officially set aside."

**E2. Egypt refused; violence in Alexandria.**

**E4. The refusers' verdict and reasons, in their own words.**
Zachariah III.1, 2175–2183. Later; the Syriac Orthodox chronicle. Documented as its verdict.
> "the Council of Chalcedon—that Council which, ostensibly convened about the matter of Eutyches, introduced and increased the heresy of Nestorius ; and shook all the world; and added evil upon evil; and set the two heresies, one against the other; and filled the world with divisions; and confounded the faith delivered by the apostles, and the good order of the Church ; and tore into ten thousand rents the perfect Robe of Christ, woven from the top throughout"

## F. Links to other people on the tree

- **Leo of Rome** — wrote to Flavian (the Tome, read and acclaimed at Sessions II and IV, B5, B10); sent the legates (A4); wrote to Pulcheria against canon 28 (C4). Not present.
- **Dioscorus of Alexandria** — kept his feast: the Coptic Synaxarium, 7 Tout (E2). Present at Session I (B8); deposed 13 October (B11); exiled to Gangra (E2; Evagrius 3363–3364, "Gangra in Paphlagonia"); kept as confessor and martyr by the refusing churches (E2, B20).
- **Eutyches** — not present; his 448 confession (B1) is anathematized in the Definition (npnf214 20275–20277).
- **Flavian of Constantinople** — dead before 451; declared orthodox (B7); Leo held him up as model (npnf212 9246–9249).
- **Theodoret of Cyrrhus** — received after anathematizing Nestorius (B19); the refusers saw his presence as proof of the council's bias (B20; Zachariah 2429–2441).
- **Juvenal of Jerusalem** — voted to restore Eutyches in 449 (B2); gained the three Palestines (B21); driven out by monks and later restored (E3).
- **Severus of Antioch** — wrote against it (E4).
- **Timothy Aelurus** — wrote the Refutation (E4); exiled to Gangra like Dioscorus (History of the Patriarchs 35199–35204).
- **Marcian and Pulcheria** — called it (A1, A4); attended Session VI (B18).

## G. Where the sources disagree or come late

- The minutes are the winners' record; the refusers speak through Zachariah (written 491–518, compiled 569), Severus (~508–11), the Plerophories (after 512), the Coptic History (10th-century compilation), Michael (12th century). Timothy Aelurus's refutation, the earliest full case against the council, is held only in unrendered Armenian.
- Why Dioscorus fell: the sentence gives canons, disobedience, three summonses and "other crimes" (B11); Anatolius in the acts and the refusers say "not for the faith" (B12); the Session V judges frame it as Leo against Dioscorus (B14).
- Number of bishops (D).
- Session dates other than 13 October are Hefele's.
- "In" or "of" two natures (B16).
- Juvenal's change: agreement in the acts, bribe and pressure in Zachariah (B21).
- Compulsion: Zachariah says the bishops signed under compulsion (B20); the acts record the cry "No one was forced" only in the canon 28 dispute (C3). The emperor's threat in Session V is Percival's summary (B14).
- Who presided: the Latin reads that Leo "commanded our littleness to preside in his place", but the Greek differs (npnf214 20035–20037, Percival's note); in practice the imperial judges and senate ran the sessions (B6, B13, B14). Zachariah says the Nestorian party asked for Theodoret to preside (B20).
- Armenia: Ormanian has Dvin 506 rejecting the acts openly; Ter-Minassiantz has it condemning them only silently, with an open condemnation in Babken's second letter (E5).
- The Church of the East: received "in so far as it condemned Eutyches" (Chabot) against "doctrinally … accepted" (Wigram) (E7).
- Place of exile: "Gangra of Thrace" (Zachariah 2075–2076), "Gangra in Paphlagonia" (Evagrius 3364), "the island of Gangra" (History of the Patriarchs 35110).

## H. Added for the revision (2026-11-18): the heart and its lasting result

**H1. What the Definition says Christ is, in full.** npnf214 20278–20284 (English). Acts. Documented.
> "the Son [of God] and our Lord Jesus Christ is to be confessed as one and the same [Person], that he is perfect in Godhead and perfect in manhood, very God and very man, of a reasonable soul and [human] body consisting, consubstantial with the Father as touching his Godhead, and consubstantial with us as touching his manhood; made in all things like unto us, sin only excepted"
Then, 20293–20295: "the peculiar property of each nature being preserved and being united in one Person and subsistence, not separated or divided into two persons".

**H2. The Definition's own reason: two errors at once.** npnf214 20236–20243 and 20269–20273. Acts. Documented.
> "some of them daring to corrupt the mystery of the Lord's incarnation for us and refusing [to use] the name Mother of God (Θεοτόκος) in reference to the Virgin, while others, bringing in a confusion and mixture, and idly conceiving that the nature of the flesh and of the Godhead is all one"
> "For it opposes those who would rend the mystery of the dispensation into a Duad of Sons; … it resists those who imagine a mixture or confusion of the two natures of Christ"
(The first are Nestorius's side, the second Eutyches's: the council meant to shut out both. It received Cyril's letters against Nestorius and Leo's Tome against Eutyches, 20258–20266.)

**H3. The Greek church makes it a test of communion: the Fifth Council, 553.** npnf214 23648–23653, the Sentence of the Synod (Constantinople, 553; date 22849, 22878). Acts, about a century later. Documented.
> "we again confess that we receive the four holy Synods, that is, the Nicene, the Constantinopolitan, the first of Ephesus, and that of Chalcedon, and we have taught, and do teach all that they defined respecting the one faith. And we account those who do not receive these things aliens from the Catholic Church."

**H4. The Latin church: Gregory of Rome, 590/591.** Gregory the Great, Register I.25, to John of Constantinople, Eulogius of Alexandria, Gregory of Antioch, John of Jerusalem and Anastasius (Book I, "the first year of his ordination", npnf212 30955–30969). Own words, about 140 years later. Documented.
Latin, `gregory-great_epistolae-selectae_turchi1907.txt` 5633–5634 and 5638–5641:
> "sicut sancti evangelii quattuor libros, sic quattuor concilia suscipere et venerari me fateor. … Chalcedonense vero, in quo Euthychis Dioscorique pravitas reprobatur, tota devotione complector, integerrima approbatione custodio, quia in his velut in quadrato lapide, sanctae fidei structura consurgit"
English, npnf212 31907–31919 (31908–31918 for the words quoted): "I confess that I receive and revere, as the four books of the Gospel so also the four Councils … and the Chalcedonian, in which the pravity of Eutyches and Dioscorus is reprobated. These with full devotion I embrace … since on them, as on a four-square stone, rises the structure of the holy faith"
Rendering (from the Latin): "As I receive the four books of the holy Gospel, so I confess that I receive and revere four councils." Note: Gregory names Dioscorus with Eutyches; the Latin side's memory, not the narrator's.

**H5. Armenia held to its refusal.** Ormanian 2520–2525 (continues E1). Editor (former Armenian patriarch, 1912; his church's own account). Widely Accepted.
> "The Armenian Church would have nothing to do with this transaction, which was prompted by designs which had no bearing on theology. She remained firm in her original resolve, and ever maintained an attitude of ultra-conservatism."

**H6. The family of churches that refused it, still in communion in 1912.** Ormanian 8950–8967. Editor (Armenian, 1912), describing the churches of his own day. Documented as his account; Widely Accepted that these churches remain a communion apart from the Greek and Latin churches (census: the Alexandrian, Syriac Orthodox and Armenian traditions are marked living).
> "The whole of Christendom is divided into four branches, viz. the Catholic and Protestant branches in the West ; and the Dyophysite and Monophysite branches in the East. … The Armenian Church, which belongs to this branch, occupies the leading position among the various groups into which the Monophysites are divided. Communion in faith and in spiritual love continues to be a bond between the latter … Thus, the Syrian, Coptic, and Abyssinian Churches retain their autocephalic hierarchy without abandoning their communion with the Armenian Church."
("Monophysite" is Ormanian's word for his own branch in 1912; the outline keeps it out of the narrator's voice.)

**H7. The Coptic memory as lasting.** History of the Patriarchs 35103–35110 (E2, re-read): "at that time the creeds were separated, and the sees were torn asunder … He preserved the orthodox faith, which persists in the see of the evangelist Saint Mark to this day and for ever". Later (10th-century compilation). Documented as that church's memory.
