## The three lines, from one hearing

- **Who or what:** a council of bishops at Chalcedon, across the water from Constantinople, called in 451 by the emperor Marcian and the empress Pulcheria, with Leo of Rome's legates and his letter but not Leo himself.
- **What happened:** it put Dioscorus of Alexandria on trial and stripped him of his office, and, after a stand-off over "of two natures", wrote a Definition that Christ is known "in two natures"; it also ranked Constantinople next after Rome, which Leo voided.
- **Why the connection matters:** the Greek and Latin churches came to keep it as their fourth council, while Alexandria, the Syriac Orthodox and the Armenians refused it, kept Dioscorus's name and "one nature from two", and held that "two natures" divided Christ.

**Where I got lost.** The opening quote ("from two" / "two") lands before the listener knows what the two are; "nature" is defined only in paragraph two, and "one nature from two" only in the last paragraph. Paragraph two carries five people and a backstory told out of order (448, then "had led" 449). "The judges" are never said to be the emperor's officials, and "Anatolius" is never said to be the bishop of Constantinople. "Of two", "from two", "in two" and "two" all pass by, and a hearer has to work to link "of two natures" with Dioscorus's "from two". "Person" is defined and then never used again. "In Egypt … the city rose" does not name Alexandria. Nothing reads as a children's book, and nothing talks down.

**One-hearing test alone: PASS (narrowly).** All three lines can be filled, and filled correctly, from one hearing. The lost points above slow the listener but do not leave a line blank or wrong.

## Findings

### BLOCKING

**1. The record's force and the refusing churches' own witnesses are left out.** The outline names these as must-tell. "The city rose" stands in for Priscus's account: a crowd drove the soldiers into the old temple of Serapis and burned them alive, and the 2,000 troops the emperor then sent abused "the wives and daughters of the Alexandrians" (E2, Evagrius/Walford 3365–3392). It also stands in for the Syriac chronicle's report that Proterius's soldiers killed believers at the altar (Zachariah 2524–2550). Leaving these out softens the record, which the Standard forbids ("never softens the record by leaving out its force or conflict"). The Coptic History is also missing (Dioscorus suffered "for the orthodox faith" and died with "the crown of martyrdom", E2). So is Severus (the council tried to cure Eutyches's error with "an opposite infection", E4). The outline's Length line names the Coptic History, the Syriac chronicle and Severus as the witnesses the story "has to give". Without them, the connection's first point, that the same sentence made Dioscorus a confessor and martyr for Egypt and Syria, is not told. The Church of the East (impact 7, received it in part) is also missing, which leaves the two-sided picture the Do not say list warns against.
*Change:* in the Egypt paragraph, give Priscus's account credited to him ("Priscus, a historian who was in the city, says…"), and the chronicle's killings at the altar credited to it. Give the Coptic History's "for the orthodox faith" and "crown of martyrdom", credited. In the last paragraph, let Severus give the reason in his own words ("an opposite infection"). Add one clause for the Church of the East receiving it in part.
*Do not say, add to the outline:* "Alexandria rose / the city rose (alone)" → "Priscus, who was in the city, says the crowd burned soldiers alive in the old temple of Serapis, and the emperor's troops abused the women; the Syriac chronicle says believers were killed at the altar (E2). Tell both as plainly as the Definition." And: "They kept Dioscorus's name (alone)" → "The Coptic History says he suffered 'for the orthodox faith' and died with 'the crown of martyrdom' (E2)."

**2. Only one side's claim to Cyril is given.** The connection requires the listener to understand that both sides claimed Cyril. The story gives only the refusing churches' "the faith of Athanasius and Cyril". The council's own acclamation, "Leo and Cyril taught the same thing" (B10, npnf214 19996–20007), is not used. As told, Cyril sounds like the refusing churches' teacher alone.
*Change:* add the acclamation in the Tome paragraph, after "Peter has spoken thus through Leo" (for example, "…and 'Leo and Cyril taught the same thing'").
*Do not say, add to the outline:* "Cyril on one side only" → "Both sides claimed Cyril: the council's acclamation 'Leo and Cyril taught the same thing' (B10), and the chronicle's 'his faith was like that of Athanasius, and Cyril' (Zachariah 2366–2367)."

**3. Length.** 479 words against "about 450 … It may not stretch further." The additions above must be paid for with cuts. Places to cut that lose no must-tell: the clause "in the minutes of the side that won" can move to the deposition paragraph, where the minutes are first cited, and be shortened there. The Session III list of answers and grounds can be told as one joined sentence each. "The council did not hold at once" repeats the sentence after it. The last two paragraphs can merge around Severus's reason.

### MINOR

1. **Session V is out of order.** In the acts the legates' threat (npnf214 20144–20146) comes before the judges' remark about Dioscorus's phrase and Anatolius's answer (Hefele 18108–18160; Mansi 7 9797–9813). The emperor's intervention then follows (Percival's bracket, 20147–20153), and only then "Leo or Dioscorus?". The story puts the threat after Anatolius and follows it with "Then", which implies the threat answered Anatolius and led straight to the question. Restore the order: draft, most bishops want it, legates threaten, judges point out the phrase, Anatolius answers, judges ask.
2. **The officials' absence.** "He would come if the officials were present" reads as an excuse unless the listener knows they were absent from that session (B11b; the Do not say row's right wording includes it). Add "who were absent from that session".
3. **Unnamed roles.** Say once that "the judges" were the emperor's officials who ran the sessions, and that Anatolius was bishop of Constantinople. Name Alexandria in "the city rose".
4. **"Person" is defined and then dropped.** Either give the Definition's "in one person" (it is in the outline's B15) or cut "a person is who he is". As told, the definition costs words and pays nothing back.
5. **Runs of one-act sentences** in paragraphs four and five. Join them with cause, contrast and time, as the Standard asks.
6. **The hand saying loses its stake.** Both witnesses say he was asked to sign and take back his see (beat 4, B20). "Than sign" alone drops what he gave up. Add "and take back his see".
7. **Canon 4 (monks under bishops)** is in the outline's beat 6 priority. One clause would carry it, if the cuts in BLOCKING 3 leave room.
