# Outline: the Council of Chalcedon (451)

Every reference points to `Source_Sheet.md` (item number) and through it to the file and line in `cic/texts/`.

**Length:** stretch to 3 minutes (about 450 words). A major council, and the story has to give the churches that refused it their own witnesses (the Coptic History, the Syriac chronicle, Severus), not only the minutes of the side that won. Two sides told fairly need the room. It may not stretch further. **Priorities to fit 450 words:** carry beats 1, 2, 4 and 5 in full; give beat 3 as the Tome and its acclaim only; give beat 6 in one or two sentences (canon 28 and Leo's annulment; monks under bishops). Give impacts 1–4, then 5–7 in one sentence. Use one opening. The Session IV vote count, Marcian's acclamation, Theodoret and Juvenal's 449 vote may drop.

**Setting**
- Year: 451, autumn. Sessions from 8 October to 1 November by Hefele's dating (A3); the acts date only the deposition, 13 October (B11). Spoken: "in the autumn of 451".
- Place: Chalcedon, a city of Bithynia, in the church of the martyr Euphemia, on the Bosporus, facing Constantinople (A1, A2). First called to Nicaea; moved so the emperor could attend from the capital (A4, A5). Spoken: "Chalcedon, just across the water from Constantinople".
- Who called it: the emperor Marcian, with the empress Pulcheria (A1, A4, B18). Leo of Rome sent legates and his letter, the Tome; he did not come (A4). Almost everyone present was a Greek or Eastern bishop (A10). Armenia was absent: its bishops were in prison or exile after the battle of Avarayr that May (A9).
- Traditions (census spoken names): Church and Empire (gathered it); the Church of Alexandria (its bishop condemned); the Syriac Orthodox Church and Armenia of Avarayr (refused it); the Church of the East (received it in part).

**Thread:** It gave the Greek and Latin churches their lasting words for Christ, "one person in two natures", and in the same act divided them from the churches of Egypt, Syria and Armenia, which held to "one nature from two" and honoured the bishop it deposed.

**Shape:** Council.

**Openings**
- Dioscorus of Alexandria, in the first session, as the minutes of 449 were read aloud: "I accept 'from two'. 'Two' I do not accept" (B8). A man on trial stopping the reading to say his soul is at stake.
- Evagrius, a historian on the council's own side, more than a century later: the quarrel hung on "the change of a single letter", the Greek *ek* ("from") against *en* ("in") (E4, B16).
- The Syriac chronicle's verdict: the council tore Christ's seamless robe into ten thousand pieces (E4).

**Story beats**
1. **What called it.** In 448 a synod at Constantinople under its bishop Flavian condemned Eutyches, a monastery head, who said Christ was "of two natures before the union" but of one after it (B1). In 449 a council at Ephesus under Dioscorus restored Eutyches and deposed Flavian, who cried "I protest" (B2, B3). Juvenal of Jerusalem voted for Eutyches there (B2). Leo called that meeting one "not of judges but of robbers" (B4). After Theodosius II died, Marcian and Pulcheria called a new council (A1, A4). *A "nature" here means what Christ is, as God and as man; a "person", who he is.* Documented.
2. **The trial of Dioscorus (Session I, 8 October).** Leo's legates demanded that Dioscorus not sit as a judge (B6). The minutes of 449 were read out. The bishops declared that Flavian, now dead, had taught rightly (B7). Dioscorus stopped the reading: he accepted "from two", not "two" (B8). Documented.
3. **Leo's letter.** The Tome taught that each nature "does the acts which belong to it, in communion with the other" (B5). When it was read, bishops cried "Peter has spoken thus through Leo" (B10). Bishops from Illyria and Palestine doubted that very passage and asked for time (B5, Latin). In a later session the bishops gave their opinions one by one, 161 of them, before the rest voted together (B13). Documented.
4. **The deposition (Session III).** Session III met without the imperial officials (B11b). Dioscorus was summoned three times. The minutes record his answers: he would come willingly, but his guards kept him back; then, that he would come if the officials and senate were present; once, that he was ill (B11b). Michael the Syrian gives the same answer from the other side: "I am ready … but they do not allow me" (B11b). Leo's legates gave sentence. He had received Eutyches into communion before the 449 council, kept Leo's letter from being read there, excommunicated Leo, faced many written charges, including petitions from Alexandrian deacons, a former priest and a layman, and refused three summonses. In Leo's name and with Peter they stripped him of his office (B11a, B11). The synod's letter to him dates it 13 October (B11). Both the Syriac chronicle and Michael say that he was asked to sign the Definition (the chronicle puts this after the sentence, Michael before it) and take back his see, and that he answered he would sooner see his hand cut off, and his blood on the paper (B20). That is the refusers' memory, not the minutes. He was sent into exile at Gangra (E2). Documented (sentence and answers as minuted); the hand saying is the refusers' memory, Inferential-Thin for the words.
5. **The Definition (Session V, 22 October by Hefele).** In Session II the bishops had cried that they would make no new creed (B14). In Session V a draft was read that said Christ was "of two natures" (B14a, B12 context). Anatolius said it had pleased everyone the day before. Most bishops wanted to sign it as it stood: "Either let the definition stand or we go" (B14a). Leo's legates said that if the bishops did not agree to Leo's letter, they would ask leave to go and hold a council in the West (B14). The imperial judges pointed out that the draft used Dioscorus's own phrase: Dioscorus had condemned Flavian for saying two natures, and the draft said "of two natures" (B12 context). Anatolius answered that Dioscorus had been condemned "not for the faith", but for excommunicating Leo and refusing three summonses (B12). The judges asked: "the most holy Leo or Dioscorus?" The bishops cried, "We believe as Leo." The judges ordered "two natures" added (B14). Percival's summary says the emperor threatened to send them all home (B14, editor). A committee then wrote the Definition: one and the same Christ, known "in two natures", without confusion, change, division or separation, in one person (B15). It banned any other creed, on pain of deposition for clergy and anathema for monks and lay people (B15). About 350 bishops signed it, by Percival's count (B17). Marcian came in person and was hailed as "teacher of the faith" (B18). Documented.
6. **Other business.** Theodoret of Cyrrhus, deposed in 449, was received once he anathematized Nestorius (B19). Jerusalem gained the three provinces of Palestine (B21, acts). Monks were put under their bishops (C1). Canon 28 ranked Constantinople, "New Rome", next after old Rome (C2). Leo's legates protested (C3), and Leo, by Peter's authority, declared the bishops' assent to it void (C4). Documented.

**What came of them** (about a third of the story)
1. **In the East it did not hold at once.** Later emperors set it aside. The circular of Basiliscus ordered the Tome and "all things said and done at Chalcedon" anathematised. Zeno's Henotikon of 482 condemned any other opinion "whether at Chalcedon or in any synod" (E1a). In time the Greek and Latin churches recognised it as the fourth general council (E1), and Evagrius copied its Definition whole (B15). *Defended:* Leo's Tome as "a common pillar against misbelievers" (E1). Widely Accepted.
2. **Egypt refused it.** When Proterius took Dioscorus's place, Alexandria rose. Priscus, a historian who arrived in the city then, says a crowd drove soldiers into the old temple of Serapis and burned them alive. He says too that the 2,000 troops the emperor then sent abused "the wives and daughters of the Alexandrians" (E2). The Syriac chronicle says Proterius's soldiers killed believers at the altar (E2). Clergy, monks and people broke communion with him and kept Dioscorus's name as a faithful priest (E2). The Coptic Church's own History says Dioscorus suffered "for the orthodox faith" under Marcian and Pulcheria, and died at Gangra with "the crown of martyrdom" (E2). It calls the council's followers "Melkites", the emperor's party (E2). The Coptic Synaxarium keeps his feast on 7 Tout, as one who "fought the good fight for the orthodox faith" and received "the crown of the confessor" (E2). *Defended:* "one nature from two", which they held to be the faith of Athanasius and Cyril (B20; Zachariah 2366–2367). Documented as each witness's account.
3. **The refusers' reason, in their own words.** They did not take Eutyches's side: Dioscorus wrote from exile that Christ's flesh was not "foreign from our body" (E4), and, by Ormanian's account, Armenia's synod condemned Eutyches by name (E5; Widely Accepted). Severus of Antioch, some sixty years on, said the council deserved condemnation because it tried to cure Eutyches's error with "an opposite infection", the teaching of Nestorius (E4). For them, "two natures" divided Christ. Documented.
4. **Palestine and Syria.** Monks back from the council made a monk, Theodosius, bishop of Jerusalem in Juvenal's place (E3). The Syriac chronicle, finished in 569, said the council "tore into ten thousand rents the perfect Robe of Christ"; Michael the Syrian repeated it some seven hundred years later (E4). Documented.
5. **Armenia.** A synod at Dvin in 506 rejected it, by Ormanian's account, and a second at Dvin in 554 confirmed that (E5). Contested as to 506 (see Who tells it).
6. **Rome and Constantinople** disagreed from the start over canon 28 (C3, C4, E6). Documented.
7. **The Church of the East** kept the council's canons and Definition in its own collection of synods; its editor says it received the council in so far as it condemned Eutyches (E7). Contested.

**Quotes, 2 or 3 at most**
1. Dioscorus, Session I (B8). Rendered from the Greek in Percival (npnf214 19256–19259) and Mansi 6 65950–65956 (Latin, with margin 65905; Greek column garbled, 65973–65975).
   > "I accept 'from two'. 'Two' I do not accept. I am forced to be shameless: my soul is at stake."
   Job: puts the whole dispute in his own mouth, at the moment it was on trial.
2. The Definition (B15). Rendered from the Greek as Evagrius copied it (Bidez–Parmentier 5243–5246), checked against Mansi's Latin (10892–10902).
   > "One and the same Jesus Christ, Son, Lord, Only-begotten, is known in two natures: without confusion, without change, without division, without separation."
   Job: the words the Greek and Latin churches came to keep; the four "withouts" are what the narrator cannot say better.
3. The Syriac chronicle known as Zachariah's (E4). Rendered from Hamilton and Brooks's English (the Syriac is not held), checked against Michael the Syrian's French (2857–2861).
   > "That council tore into ten thousand pieces the whole robe of Christ, woven in one piece from the top."
   Job: the refusers' verdict in their own image, from their own chronicle.

**Connection to carry into the story:** The same sentence that deposed Dioscorus made him, for Egypt and Syria, a confessor and a martyr (B11, E2, B20), and each side's reason for the sentence must be credited to that side (B12). The council's supporters kept the Definition (E1); the churches that refused it kept Dioscorus's name and his formula, "one nature from two" (E2, B20). The listener must understand three things the writer has to state, not leave to inference. First, the refusers also condemned Eutyches (E4, E5): this was not a fight between Chalcedon and Eutyches's followers. Second, both sides claimed Cyril of Alexandria (the acclamation "Leo and Cyril taught the same thing", B10; the chronicle's "his faith was like that of Athanasius, and Cyril", Zachariah 2366–2367), and the words at stake were "from two natures" against "in two natures" (B14, B14a, B20, E4). Do not call "one nature from two" Cyril's own formula in the narrator's voice; no Cyril text is cited for it. Third, the division was between churches, not only between bishops: Alexandria's clergy and people broke communion (E2), Palestine's monks chose their own bishop (E3), and Armenia later refused by synod (E5). Carry it through these acts, not through a "this matters because" sentence.

**Links**
- Leo of Rome: wrote to Flavian (the Tome, read and acclaimed at the council); sent the legates; wrote to Pulcheria against canon 28 (B5, B10, A4, C4).
- Dioscorus of Alexandria: on trial at the council; deposed and exiled; kept as confessor and martyr by Egypt and Syria (B8, B11, E2); kept his feast: the Coptic Synaxarium, 7 Tout (E2).
- Eutyches: not present; his teaching condemned in the Definition (B1, E1 via npnf214 20275–20277).
- Flavian of Constantinople: dead before the council; declared orthodox (B7).
- Theodoret of Cyrrhus: received after he anathematized Nestorius (B19).
- Juvenal of Jerusalem: voted for Eutyches in 449, with the majority in 451; gained Palestine; driven out by monks (B2, B21, E3).
- Severus of Antioch: wrote against it (E4).
- Timothy Aelurus: wrote a refutation of it, kept only in Armenian; exiled to Gangra like Dioscorus (E4, F).
- Marcian and Pulcheria: called it; Marcian attended (A1, B18).

**Who tells it**
- *The acts* (Percival's English extracts, Mansi's Latin and Greek): the minutes, sentence, Definition and canons, kept by the side that won. The listener should know the minutes are the winners' record.
- *Leo's letters* (his own words, 449–451): for the council's faith, against its canon 28.
- *Evagrius* (Greek, writing over a century later, on the council's side): copied the Definition; passes on Priscus's report of Alexandria.
- *The Syriac chronicle known as Zachariah's* (Zachariah's Greek history of 450–491, written by 518, taken into a Syriac compilation finished in 569; on the side that refused). Tell the listener it is the refusers' own chronicle when quoting it or the hand saying.
- *Severus of Antioch* (his own letter, about 60 years later; leader of the refusers in Syria).
- *The History of the Patriarchs of Alexandria* (a 10th-century compilation; the Coptic Church's own memory).
- *The Coptic Synaxarium* (a medieval calendar of saints, Arabic, in Basset's French): Dioscorus's feast.
- *Michael the Syrian* (12th century; Syriac Orthodox patriarch of Antioch, 1166–1199, per the file header; repeats the chronicle).
- *Malachia Ormanian* (1912; former Armenian patriarch of Constantinople) for Armenia. Ter-Minassiantz (1904) reads the Armenian letters differently: the synod of 506 condemned Chalcedon only silently, and a letter of the Catholicos Babken soon after named it. The card can carry the disagreement; the story says "early in the sixth century" or credits Ormanian.
- *Chabot and Wigram* (modern editors) for the Church of the East.

**Do not say**
| Easy wrong wording | Right wording |
|---|---|
| The churches that refused it followed Eutyches / were "Monophysites" | They condemned Eutyches too; they confessed Christ "one nature from two" (E4, E5, B20). Avoid "Monophysite" in the narrator's voice. |
| "He was deposed as a heretic", or "He was not condemned for his faith" (either as the narrator's fact) | Give the sentence's grounds as the minutes give them (B11a, B11). Credit "not for the faith" to Anatolius, in the council (B12). Credit the other view to its holders: their chronicle says he was deposed for confessing "one nature from two", and the Coptic History says he suffered "for the orthodox faith" (B12; Zachariah 2316–2320; E2). |
| "Summoned three times, Dioscorus did not come" (alone) | Give his minuted answers too: his guards held him back; he asked for the officials and senate, absent from that session (B11b). |
| Leo presided / Leo attended | Leo sent legates and his letter; he did not come. The emperor's officials ran the sessions (A4, B6, B13). |
| 630 bishops (as fact) | "More than five hundred bishops", or "520, by their own letter to Leo" (D1–D6). |
| Dioscorus said "I would rather lose my hand…" (as narrator's fact) | "The refusers' own chronicle remembers him saying…" (B20). |
| The bishops were forced to sign (as fact) | "The refusers' chronicle says they signed under compulsion" (B20). The minutes record the legates' threat to leave and the cry "Leo or Dioscorus?" (B14). Do not tell the change from "of two" to "in two" as the bishops' free choice, or as forced. Give the minutes' steps (B14a, B12, B14) and credit "under compulsion" to the refusers' chronicle. |
| It settled the matter / held from 451 | Later emperors set it aside before the Greek and Latin churches made it their fourth council (E1a, E1). |
| The two phrases meant the same / it was only a misunderstanding (as the narrator's view) | Evagrius, on the council's side, thought so. The refusers held that "in two natures" divided Christ (E4). |
| "The refusers" / "anti-Chalcedonians" (in the narrator's voice) | Name them: the Church of Alexandria, the Syriac Orthodox, the Armenians. Their own word for their faith is "orthodox" (History of the Patriarchs 34868–34870); Zachariah calls them "believers" (2548). "Refusers" is this outline's working label only. |
| Juvenal was bribed with Palestine | The acts record an agreement giving Jerusalem the three Palestines (B21); the bribe is the refusers' charge, credited if used. |
| The council split the church in two that day | Egypt and Palestine resisted at once (E2, E3); Armenia was absent and refused in the sixth century (A9, E5); the Church of the East received it in part (E7). |
| Leo approved everything the council did | He rejected canon 28 and declared it void (C4). |
| "One word" divided them | "One letter", in Evagrius's phrase: *ek* against *en* (E4). |
| Dioscorus died on an island / in Thrace | "In exile at Gangra" (E2, Evagrius: in Paphlagonia). |
| Riot details as fact | "Priscus, who was in the city, says…" (E2). |
| The division lasts to this day (narrator's claim) | If used, credit it: the Coptic History says Dioscorus's faith "persists in the see of the evangelist Saint Mark to this day" (E2). |

**Leave out**
- The death of Nestorius on the road, his tongue eaten by worms, and Marcian's sympathy for him (Zachariah III.1): legend from a hostile witness; Evagrius disputes the recall (A8 context).
- Theodoret bribing Leo with "the gift which blinds the eyes" (Zachariah III.1): a hostile allegation with no other support.
- Aetius beating Amphilochius, Eustace of Berytus's shorthand note "under compulsion" (Zachariah III.1): single hostile witness.
- The Coptic Synaxarium's story of the emperor and empress having Dioscorus's beard pulled and teeth knocked out (E2): legend.
- Theopistus's History of Dioscorus (the slap, Dioscorus's letter calling Leo a tree to be cut down), and Pulcheria striking Dioscorus (a later tradition Nau mentions): legend.
- The visions of the Plerophories: legend, two generations later.
- Chalcedon made a metropolis in name (C5); the other canons beyond 4 and 28; the Euthymius and Leontius evidence for "in two natures" (for the card).
- The full roll of bishops and session-by-session dates beyond 8 and 13 October.
- Timothy Aelurus's own arguments: held only in Armenian OCR, not rendered; name the work, do not characterize its content.
- Not left out: the deposition, the burning at the Serapeum and the killings at the altar, the legates' threat, the ban on any other creed. These are told as plainly as the Definition.
