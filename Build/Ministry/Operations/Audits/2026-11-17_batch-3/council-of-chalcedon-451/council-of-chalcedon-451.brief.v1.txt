In the autumn of 451, at Chalcedon, just across the water from Constantinople, a bishop on trial stops the reading of the minutes. Dioscorus of Alexandria says: "I accept 'from two'. 'Two' I do not accept. I am forced to be shameless: my soul is at stake."

The emperor Marcian and the empress Pulcheria had called this council. Leo of Rome sent legates and a letter, but did not come. In 448 a synod condemned Eutyches, a monastery head who said Christ was "of two natures before the union" but of one after. At Ephesus in 449, Dioscorus had restored Eutyches and deposed Flavian, bishop of Constantinople. A nature is what Christ is, as God and as man; a person is who he is.

Leo's letter, the Tome, taught that each nature "does the acts which belong to it, in communion with the other." When it was read, bishops cried, "Peter has spoken thus through Leo."

In the third session Dioscorus was summoned three times. The minutes record his answers: his guards held him back; he would come if the officials were present; once, that he was ill. The legates gave sentence. He had received Eutyches, kept Leo's letter from being read at Ephesus, excommunicated Leo, and refused the summonses. In Leo's name, they stripped him of his office, and he went into exile at Gangra.

In the fifth session a draft said Christ was "of two natures," and most bishops wanted to sign it. The judges pointed out that it was Dioscorus's own phrase. Anatolius answered that Dioscorus was condemned "not for the faith," but for excommunicating Leo. The legates threatened to leave and hold a council in the West. Then the judges asked, "Leo or Dioscorus?" The bishops cried, "We believe as Leo." The judges ordered "two natures" added.

The Definition, in the minutes of the side that won, says Christ "is known in two natures: without confusion, without change, without division, without separation." About 350 bishops signed, and any other creed was banned. Canon 28 ranked Constantinople next after old Rome; Leo declared it void. The Syriac chronicle, written by those who refused it, remembers Dioscorus saying he would sooner see his hand cut off than sign.

The council did not hold at once. Later emperors set it aside, until the Greek and Latin churches made it their fourth council. In Egypt, when Proterius took Dioscorus's place, the city rose, and clergy, monks and people broke with him and kept Dioscorus's name. Palestine's monks made their own bishop, and Armenia later refused it by synod.

Alexandria, the Syriac Orthodox and the Armenians had not followed Eutyches. They held "one nature from two," the faith, they said, of Athanasius and Cyril, and for them "two natures" divided Christ. Their chronicle says the council "tore into ten thousand pieces the whole robe of Christ, woven in one piece from the top."
