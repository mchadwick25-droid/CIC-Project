# Outline check, round 2: Thomas à Kempis

## The heart of his life, written before reading the outline

From the census entry, the source sheet and what he is known for:

Thomas spent about seventy years in one poor house of canons, Mount St Agnes near Zwolle. He came in 1399 as a scholar formed by Florens Radewijns and the Brothers at Deventer, and was clothed in 1406. His work was books: he copied them (the house's whole Bible, others for its use and for sale), and he wrote them. He wrote the house's Chronicle and the Lives of the New Devotion's founders, "a memorial for them that come after", so the movement's own inside memory runs through his pen. He wrote little books for the young and gathered his talks to the novices, plain in style.

His major contribution, as he is known, is the Imitation of Christ: four little books calling the reader to follow Christ by copying his life, to turn inward (the kingdom of God within), to prefer holiness and humility over learning ("feel compunction rather than know its definition"), and to take the royal way of the cross. Johannes Busch, a canon of his own order, named him its author in his lifetime; early copies and printings gave it to John Gerson; scholars still split (Contested). Its reach is the heaviest thing that came of him: Ignatius read a chapter a day and set it in the Spiritual Exercises (as "the little Gerson"); Wesley found in it that true religion is seated in the heart, and the Methodist preachers read it at dawn; Lutheran Pietists and Quakers across confessional lines took it up.

What he defended: obedience to the pope's interdict, at the cost of three years' exile in Frisia (1429–1432).

The heart, then: a humble copyist in one small house who wrote down the New Devotion's memory, and who is credited, on disputed ground, with the book that carried that movement's inward, Christ-copying devotion to Ignatius, Wesley and readers on every side. The book's teaching and the contested credit must both be in the first minute.

## The outline's heart, compared

The outline's heart is the Imitation's teaching, its contested credit, and the house life he wrote down (copying, the Chronicle, the Lives, books for the young and the novices). This agrees with mine in substance and leaves out no major contribution. The reach (Ignatius, Wesley, Woolman, Spener) is ranked heaviest first, and what he defended (the interdict, the exile) is marked. Beats 1–3 tell the teaching and the credit in about the first 150 words. The documented work as copyist and chronicler arrives only in beats 4–5 (see MINOR 1).

## Findings

**BLOCKING 1. The cross teaching is glossed against its own chapter.** Beat 2 says "'Dying to self' means giving up one's own will, not physical death", and Do not say forbids "suffering". The chapter does not say "giving up one's own will". It opens with "deny yourself and take up your cross" (Pohl II 3720–3722, "abnega temet ipsum"; Benham 4604–4608). It then says that whatever you arrange, you will always find something to suffer, "pain of body, or tribulation of spirit", and must bear it willingly (Pohl II 3764–3769; Benham 4664–4670, 4679). To bar "suffering" softens the book's severity, which Wesley himself called "too strict". It also steers the writer away from what the chapter says.
- Correction to beat 2: replace the gloss sentence with: "'Dying to self' here means denying oneself, and willingly bearing the pain of body or trouble of spirit that comes, rather than escaping it (Pohl II 3720–3722, 3764–3769; Benham 4604–4608, 4664–4670)."
- Correction to Do not say: replace the "Dying to self" line with: "'Dying to self' must not become 'punishing the body' as a practice the book prescribes, and must not lose its suffering either. Say: denying oneself, and willingly bearing the suffering that comes, each day."

**BLOCKING 2. "The first English" is wrong.** Impact item 4 heads Atkynson's version "The first English". Ingram's edition, the cited source, prints three versions. The first is "the Old Version, by an unknown author" (Ingram 167–169), under the heading "I. OF THE OLDEST ENGLISH VERSION" (Ingram 182). Atkynson's version is the second.
- Correction to item 4: "**An early English version,** made by William Atkynson for Margaret, mother of King Henry VII, and printed by Wynkyn de Worde in 1504, again under Gerson's name (Ingram 168–178, 1176–1182). Documented."
- Add to Do not say: "'The first English translation' for the 1504 printing. An older English version by an unknown author survives in manuscript (Ingram 167–169, 182). Say 'an early English version'."

**MINOR 1. His certain work comes after the first minute.** The outline's own heart includes "the life of the house that he wrote down". Yet before beat 4 the listener hears only that the book is "credited" to him, so they do not yet know what he certainly did. Correction to beat 3, first sentence: "It is credited to Thomas, a canon who spent almost all his life in one small house, Mount St Agnes near Zwolle, copying books and writing down the house's history (Chronicle 3426–3430, 292–300). "Almost": the exile years were spent away (Chronicle 1908–1915)."

**MINOR 2. The order of Books III and IV.** Beat 2 cites Pohl II 574–579 for "its third part is a conversation ... its fourth is on Holy Communion". Pohl's list, following the 1441 autograph, puts De sacramento third and the conversation fourth (Pohl II 576–579; Ingram 173–175). The usual printed order is Benham's (255–258, 425–426). Correction: in beat 2 and "Its shape", cite Benham 255–258 and 425–426 for the order, not Pohl II 574–579. Also add: "in the usual printed order; the 1441 copy puts the Communion book third."

**MINOR 3. "Think little of" for contemnere is weak.** The Latin is "to scorn", and the first chapter's title names "contempt of the world" (Pohl II 671–673). "Think little of" is milder than the source. Correction to quote 3 and the rendering note: "Learn to scorn outward things and to give yourself to inward things. And you will see the kingdom of God come within you." Keep the Do not say line that the inward turn must stand beside it.

**MINOR 4. Advice, not orders.** The Exercises say reading these books "mucho aprouecha", "helps greatly" (Exercises 24997–25000). The Methodist minutes "advise" the preachers (Methodist minutes 2647). The outline has the Exercises "tell" those making them to read, and has the preachers "set" to read. Correction: in What came of them item 1 and the Connection, write "advise those making them, from the second week on, that it helps greatly to read at times in the Imitation of Christ, the Gospels or the lives of saints". In item 2 and the Connection, write "advised its preachers to read". Also take the quotation marks off "the Imitation of Christ, or the Gospels, or the lives of saints": it is a paraphrase.
