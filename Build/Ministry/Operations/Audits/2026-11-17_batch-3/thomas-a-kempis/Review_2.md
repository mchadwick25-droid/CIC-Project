# Review 2: Thomas à Kempis

Brief reviewed: `thomas-a-kempis.brief.txt` (fresh draft from the revised outline). Judged against `Standard.md`, `Shapes.md` (Teacher or thinker) and `Process.md`, with `Outline.md`, `Source_Sheet.md` and `Outline_Check_2.md`. Heard in Neo's voice, about 150 words a minute.

## The test, from one hearing

- **Who:** Thomas à Kempis, a fifteenth-century canon of Mount St Agnes near Zwolle, schooled at Deventer in the New Devotion, to whom the Imitation of Christ is credited, though this is disputed.
- **What happened:** The book calls the reader to follow Christ by copying his life, to put a changed heart above learning, to turn inward and to take the cross. Thomas lived and wrote in one small house, shared its exile, and died in 1471.
- **Why the connection matters:** The same inward teaching reached Ignatius of Loyola (as "the little Gerson"), John Wesley and the Methodist preachers, and the Quaker John Woolman, across the lines that divided them.

All three lines can be written. The connection lands.

## The heart of his life, written from the census entry and what he is known for

A canon who spent almost his whole life in one small house, Mount St Agnes near Zwolle, copying books and writing down the New Devotion's memory: the house's Chronicle and the Lives of the movement's founders. He is credited, on disputed ground (Busch names him; early copies named Gerson), with the Imitation of Christ, whose call to follow Christ inwardly reached Ignatius, Wesley and readers on every side. The census `knownFor` leads with the canon and copyist, then the Chronicle and Lives, then the contested Imitation.

## Does the first minute carry it?

No. The first 150 words are the book's teaching only. Thomas is first named at word 151, the disputed credit is told at words 151–190, and his own documented life (Mount St Agnes, word 230; the Chronicle, word 265) arrives at about 1 minute 30 to 1 minute 45. In the first minute the listener does not know whose story this is, or that the book is only credited to him. His own work also lacks weight: the brief never says that Thomas himself copied books (only the Brothers "copied books"), and the Chronicle and Lives get one clause. See BLOCKING 1.

Where I got lost: the second sentence, "a little book of the fifteenth century, which says they urge us", where "which" and "they" are hard to place on one hearing. Nothing reads as a children's book or talks down.

## Verdict: REVISE

## Findings

**BLOCKING 1. The heart is not told in the first minute, and his own work lacks its weight.** The first 150 words tell only what the book teaches. Thomas, the disputed credit and his life in the house all come after the minute. The outline's own heart is "the Imitation of Christ, credited to him, and the life of the house that he wrote down", and its beat 3 gives the sentence for it: "a canon who spent almost all his life in one small house, Mount St Agnes near Zwolle, copying books and writing down the house's history" (Chronicle 3426–3430, 292–300). The brief dropped that clause, and nowhere says Thomas copied books (the house's Bible, books for its use; Chronicle 3426–3430).
- Change: tighten paragraph 2 (for example, fold "Later parts let Christ speak to the soul, and turn to Holy Communion" into the cross sentence, or move it later) so that, by about word 150, the listener has heard that the book is credited to Thomas, a canon who spent almost all his life in one small house near Zwolle copying books and writing down its history, and that the credit is disputed. Keep the Busch/Gerson sentences short. Later, in the life paragraph, let him copy the house's Bible, as the notice says.
- No Do not say line: the outline already marks beats 1–3 for the first 150 words and gives the beat 3 sentence. The writer did not use it.

**BLOCKING 2. "At school in Deventer he was taught by the Brothers of the Common Life" is not held.** Thomas's own account says Florens Radewijns kept him in his house, "placed me in the School", gave him books and found him free lodging with a devout matron; he then lived "associated with this man ... and with the Brothers of his Order", seeing their lives daily (Founders 9005–9017). The source does not say the Brothers taught him in the school. The outline's "schooled and lodged him" left room for the error.
- Change: "At Deventer, where he went to school, Florens Radewijns, who led the Brothers of the Common Life, took him in, gave him books and found him lodging. He lived among the Brothers, of the movement called the New Devotion, who shared all they had and copied books."
- Do not say line to add to the outline: "Do not say the Brothers of the Common Life taught him at school, or ran the school at Deventer. Florens placed him in the school, gave him books and found him lodging; he lived among the Brothers and saw their lives daily (Founders 9005–9017)."

**MINOR 1. "Told" should be "advised".** The 1746 minutes say "We would advise them ... From four to five in the morning ... partly some close, practical book of divinity. In particular, ... Kempis" (Methodist minutes 2647–2653). The outline, after Outline_Check_2 MINOR 4, says "advised". Change: "In 1746 his Methodist Conference advised its preachers to read it, among other books, between four and five in the morning."

**MINOR 2. "The lives of its founders" mislocates the Lives.** "Its" refers to the small house; the Lives are of the New Devotion's founders, Geert Grote, Florens Radewijns and their followers (Founders 2063–2072; outline "His documented work"). Change: "and the lives of the New Devotion's founders".

**MINOR 3. The exile's date compresses the ban.** "In 1429, when the canons stopped their services to keep a pope's ban" can be heard as the services stopping in 1429. They stopped singing under the interdict from the 1425 dispute and were driven out in 1429 (Chronicle 1819–1829, 1892–1896). Change, for example: "When a dispute over the bishop of Utrecht brought a pope's ban on church services, the canons kept it. In 1429 they were insulted and driven out, and the community spent three years in Frisia."

**MINOR 4. The second sentence is hard to follow once.** "a little book of the fifteenth century, which says they urge us to imitate" puts "which" and "they" too far from what they name. Change, for example: "These are the first words of the Imitation of Christ, a little book of the fifteenth century. It says these words call us to imitate the life and ways of Jesus."
