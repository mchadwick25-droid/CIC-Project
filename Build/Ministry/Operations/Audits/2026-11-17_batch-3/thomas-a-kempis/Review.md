# Review: Thomas à Kempis

**Verdict: REVISE**

## The test, from one hearing

- **Who:** Thomas, a scholar from Deventer, born about 1380 near Cologne, who joined the poor house of canons at Mount St Agnes near Zwolle and lived there until 1471. The Imitation of Christ is credited to him, but its authorship is disputed.
- **What happened:** He came to the house in 1399, was clothed in 1406, went into exile with his house in 1429 rather than break a papal ban, gathered talks for the novices, wrote the house's Chronicle, and died there in his ninety-second year.
- **Why the connection matters:** The book credited to him reached Ignatius of Loyola, who read it as "the little Gerson" and wrote it into his Spiritual Exercises. Later it reached Wesley and the Methodists, and the Quaker Woolman, so it was read on both sides of the church's divides.

**One-hearing test alone:** I would pass it. All three lines came clear on one hearing.

Where I got lost: only briefly, at "the pope's pardons" in the first sentence, which is clear but vague until the house comes into view. The turn from Woolman back to Mount St Agnes is marked ("Back at Mount St Agnes") and holds. Nothing reads as a children's book or talks down.

## Scores

- **Voice 4.** An adult narrator throughout, in plain documentary register. The Ignatius paragraph lets his naming of the book make the authorship point without commentary.
- **Craft 4.** A good opening scene. The authorship dispute is placed exactly where the book enters, and the connection gets room through what Ignatius, Wesley and the Methodists did with it. The 1406–1429 paragraph is a little list-like, but it is not a finding.
- **Rigor 3.** All three quotes and the Busch and Wesley claims hold. But the exile leaves out the record's force, and the gloss on "canon" makes him a priest in 1406 (see B1, B2).
- **Accessibility 4.** The terms (canon, Spiritual Exercises, novices) are worked into the sentences. The score is grade 7.6 (names out 6.6), reading ease 77.5, longest sentence 29 words. That is under 8, but on the listener read it is not flat.

## Findings

**B1. BLOCKING: the exile is softened.** The story says only "The canons kept it, and chose exile in Frisia over breaking it." The record has more. "We ceased from our singing ... the nobles of the land and many of the vulgar had indignation against us ... we suffered many insults, and at last we were driven to go forth" (Chronicle 1823–1829). "Thus were the Brothers driven forth" (1903), and they "abode three years" (1913–1914). The outline's beat 4 and "What he defended" carry the insults and the price of three years. Told as a free choice alone, the record loses its force and conflict.
- Change: tell it as the record has it, kept short. For example: "The canons kept it and stopped singing. They were insulted and at last driven out, and chose exile in Frisia over breaking the ban. The community stayed three years." Keep "the community", not Thomas, as the one that stayed three years.
- **Do not say** (add to the outline): "Do not tell the 1429 exile as a free choice alone. Say the canons stopped singing, were insulted and were driven out for keeping the ban, and that the community stayed three years in Frisia."

**B2. BLOCKING: "clothed in 1406 as a canon, a priest living in community".** This makes him a priest in 1406. The Chronicle records him invested as one of "two brothers that were Clerks" (Chronicle 1189–1192). At his death in 1471 he had been "a Priest for above fifty-seven years" (3417), so he was ordained some years after his clothing. The outline's Setting gloss left room for this.
- Change: "Thomas was clothed in 1406 as a canon, one of a community living under the Rule of St Augustine." Or drop "a priest".
- **Do not say** (add to the outline): "Do not gloss 'canon' as 'a priest' at his 1406 clothing. He was invested as a clerk and was a priest only later (Chronicle 1189–1192, 3417). Say 'a canon, living in community under the Rule of St Augustine'."

**M1. MINOR: "he walked to Zwolle".** The source says "came to Zwolle" (Chronicle 1103–1104). "Walked" is a detail added for effect. Change it to "came".

**M2. MINOR: length.** The story runs about 10% past the outline's cap, and B1 adds words. Net back to about 375. Two places can give words without losing a must-tell: the Deventer paragraph (fold Florens and the copying into one sentence), and the Woolman sentence.
