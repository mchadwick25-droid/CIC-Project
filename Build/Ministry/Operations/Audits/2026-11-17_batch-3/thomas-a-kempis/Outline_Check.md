## MINOR

**M1. Beat 1, "His brother John ... sent him to Florens".** Founders 8992–8994 says "I was led by his advice to approach". Correction: "On the advice of his brother John, a canon at Windesheim, he went to Florens Radewijns".

**M2. Beat 1, "copied books for a living".** Founders 9031–9033 says only "laboured carefully in copying books". Earning by copying is said of Mount St Agnes (Chronicle 1094–1095), not of the Deventer Brothers in these lines. Correction: "and spent their days copying books".

**M3. Beat 4 lets a writer put Thomas in Frisia for all three years.** Chronicle 2024–2026: he was sent by the Visitors to his brother at Bethany and "abode with him for a year and two months" before John's death on 4 November 1432 (2010). Correction: after "stayed three years", add "Thomas was later sent to nurse his brother John at Bethany, near Arnhem, and stayed fourteen months, until he closed his eyes in November 1432." Add to Do not say: "Thomas spent three years in Frisia." Say "the community spent three years in Frisia; Thomas spent the last part of the exile with his dying brother."

**M4. Opening 1, "a half-built house on a sandy hill".** This is not in Chronicle 1103–1106. It is supported by Chronicle 1052–1055 (the church walls were built under John's priorate, from 1399) and 1062–1077 (a hill levelled, "this sandy soil"). Correction: add "Chronicle 1052–1055, 1068–1077" to the citation.

**M5. Opening 2, "The book's most-quoted line, set against the scholars' arguments of its age".** No source says it is the most quoted, and "the scholars of its age" is the researcher's gloss. Correction: "A line from the book's first chapter, set against arguing about the Trinity without humility" (Pohl II 701–706; Benham 1141–1148).

**M6. Beat 5, "the sermons he had given 'to our novices'".** The Latin, Pohl VI 255–257, has "pro noviciis nostris ... in unum collegi" ("for our novices ... I gathered into one"). Scully's "given to" is looser than the Latin. Correction: "He gathered into one book the talks made 'for our novices'". In Do not say, change "he preached to the novices" to "he gathered sermons for the novices".

**M7. Impact 1, "pressed it on everyone".** Scripta 9405 has "encomendaua á todos los que trataua". Correction: "commended it to everyone he dealt with", as quote 3 already has it.

**M8. Who reports Manresa.** The passage is Câmara's record, in Spanish, inside his Portuguese Memoriale (1555), of what Ignatius said ("Item dixo mas", Scripta 9403). Ignatius did not write it. "Who tells it" has this, but Impact 1 and the Connection read as if Ignatius said it directly. Add to Do not say: "Ignatius wrote that he first read it at Manresa." Say "Ignatius told Luis Gonçalves da Câmara, who wrote it down in 1555".

**M9. Connection, the authorship clause.** "A canon of Thomas's own order ... named Thomas as its author (Busch 5413–5415)" stands without the dispute beside it, and a writer could close the question with it. Correction: append "; early copies and printings gave it to Gerson, and scholars still disagree (Ingram 1176–1186; Neale 4716–4722)."

**M10. Connection, "which shaped the Jesuits".** No held line is cited for the Exercises shaping the order. Correction: cite a held line, or reduce it to "the retreat method Ignatius wrote" and "Ignatius, who founded the Jesuits".

**M11. The Wesley line.** It is held and says what the outline claims (Wesley 960–968, Wesley's own 1738 account quoted in Curnock's introduction). Two gaps a writer could fall into:
- "Kempis's Christian Pattern" is not explained. The same passage gives "though I read him only in Dean Stanhope's translation". Add to the outline: "'The Christian's Pattern' was the title of Stanhope's English translation of the Imitation."
- Wesley tells it in 1738 as a stage before his conversion. The passage ends with "I doubted not but I was a good Christian", and it also credits "a religious friend". Add to Do not say: "Kempis converted Wesley" or "Kempis changed Wesley's life." Say "reading it, Wesley began to see that true religion was seated in the heart".

**M12. Impact 3, Woolman "knowing Thomas lived & died...".** Woolman 12189 has "I have been informed". He also speaks of "his writings", not the Imitation by name. Correction: "The Quaker John Woolman, told that Thomas 'lived & died in the profession of the Roman Catholick Religion', judged from his writings that he was 'a man of a true Christian spirit'."

**M13. Link, John of Kempen, "received him (Chronicle 1189–1193)".** Those lines record Thomas's investiture and their kinship. "Received his investiture" from his brother is in the death notice. Correction: cite Chronicle 1189–1193 and 3421–3422.
