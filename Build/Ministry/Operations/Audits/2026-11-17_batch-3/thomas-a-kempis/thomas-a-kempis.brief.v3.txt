Thomas was a young scholar at Deventer when he came to Zwolle, in what is now the Netherlands, to gain the pope's pardons at St Michael's church. The year was 1399, and he went on, glad at heart, to a poor, half-built house of canons called Mount St Agnes, and asked to stay.

He was born about 1380 in Kempen, near Cologne. At Deventer Florens Radewijns had schooled and lodged him among the Brothers of the Common Life, men of the New Devotion who shared everything and spent their days copying books.

After six years of testing, Thomas was clothed in 1406 as a canon, living in community under the Rule of St Augustine. In 1429 a disputed election for bishop of Utrecht brought a papal ban on church services. The canons kept it and stopped singing until, insulted, they were at last driven to go forth. The community spent three years in Frisia.

The Imitation of Christ, a little book credited to him, travelled much further. It says, "I would rather feel sorrow for sin than know how to define it." Johannes Busch, a canon of Thomas's own order writing in his lifetime, names him as its author. But early copies gave it to John Gerson, a Paris theologian, and scholars still disagree.

Ignatius of Loyola, founder of the Jesuits, knew it by that other name. A fellow Jesuit, Luis Gonçalves da Câmara, wrote down in 1555 what Ignatius told him: "At Manresa he had first seen the little Gerson. After that he never wanted to read any other book of devotion. He commended it to everyone he dealt with. He read one chapter a day, in order." He set it as reading in his Spiritual Exercises, the retreat he wrote.

Reading it centuries later, the young John Wesley began to see that true religion was seated in the heart. His Methodists set it for their preachers' early mornings. The Quaker John Woolman, told Thomas was a Roman Catholic, judged him a man of a true Christian spirit.

Back at Mount St Agnes, Thomas gathered into one book the talks made for the novices, the men in their trial year, in plain speech for simple minds. He wrote his house's Chronicle as a memorial for those who came after.

He died there after the last prayer of the day. It was 1471, and he was in his ninety-second year.
