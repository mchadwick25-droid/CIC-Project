## The MEANING item

### arius Q2

- Source: Epiphanius, Panarion, Oehler, line 45607: "ὥςτε ἐκδιῶξαι ἡμᾶς ἐκ τῆς πόλεως ὡς ἀνθρώπους ἀθέους", "so as to drive us out of the city as godless men".
- Brief: Arius told him his bishop had driven him out "as atheists".
- What differs: ἄθεος means "godless", without God or denying the true God. It does not mean a person who denies that any god exists. Today "atheists" means exactly that. A listener hears that Arius's party was charged with unbelief in God, when the charge was impiety. The brief's wording rests on the NPNF English ("as atheists"), but the modern sense of that word misleads.
- Wording that holds to the source, at the brief's length: "as godless men" (the blind reader's). In the brief: Arius told him his bishop had driven him out "as godless men".

## Words whose modern sense may mislead

- "Atheists" (arius Q2): misleads. Repair above.
- "Ethiopian" (moses-the-black Q3): the quote stands, since the brief glosses the word twice. But the brief's last paragraph uses the same word in its modern sense: "a later Ethiopian tradition remembers raiders killing him there". A listener has just learned that "Ethiopian" means a black African, and then hears it name a church and a country. The quote is not at fault; that sentence is the brief's own prose. Flagged for the reviewer, not changed.
- "Compunction" (thomas-a-kempis Q1): not used by the brief, which says "sorrow for sin". No problem.
