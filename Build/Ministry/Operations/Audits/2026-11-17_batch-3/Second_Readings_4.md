## Notes on the reader's flags

**S4-01, "the lines do not name Ariminum".** The requested lines (32396-32415) do not name it, but the text just before them does. Jerome's section 18 tells the story of the synod by name: the bishops gathered "in the Church at Ariminum" (32349-32350), and the account ends "in the acts of the Synod of Ariminum, the source from which I have myself drawn them" (32394-32396). Section 19 then opens "After these proceedings the Council was dissolved" (32397). That is the same council. Valens and Ursacius then said they had denied only that the Son was like other creatures; "at that moment the term Usia was abolished", and "the whole world groaned" (32408-32413). So Jerome places the groan at the outcome of Ariminum, just after it broke up, and the brief's "at Ariminum" holds. It is a light compression: strictly, the world groaned over what Ariminum had done, once its leaders' gloss came out. "After Ariminum" would be slightly more exact; no change is required. "The first" is our own framing, not Jerome's. It holds against the brief's "In 359 and 360 ... councils": Ariminum (359) came before Constantinople (360). Seleucia also met in 359 (endnote 4113, 32284), but the brief names neither Seleucia nor Constantinople, so "the first" only orders Ariminum before the 360 council.

**S4-02, OCR and logos.** The reader is right that "οἷ" (line 1231) is OCR damage for "οὗ"; our "in whom" follows "οὗ", as the second reading of this clause already held (Second_Readings_3.md, S3-23). The missing breathing on "Αβραὰμ" (line 1234) is outside our quote. On logos: the edition prints "λόγον" and "λόγου" in lower case, but our brief says "the Word" both times, the link to Christ the reader wants kept, and the brief elsewhere explains that logos means both "word" and "reason" (S3-22). Nothing to change.

**S4-04, Budge's "[white]".** The bracket is printed in the source (PAR2 line 1028) and is Budge's own supply, not a word he read in the Syriac (Source_Sheet Y3 says the same). Dropping it, as ours does, gives the text as transmitted: "as if you were a man". That is a harsher line than the reader's, but it is the source's own self-reproach, not something we added. The reader is right that "[white]" picks up the bishop's earlier "thou art wholly white"; ours loses that echo, which is Budge's interpretation, not the text. If the brief ever wanted the echo, it would have to mark "white" as the translator's word. "Go back in": the setting (driven out of the sacrarium, "having gone forth") supports "in", so it adds no new sense. "Shall not" keeps Budge's "shalt not", which can be heard as fact or as a resolve; the reader's "will not" picks one. A listener would not take it differently.
