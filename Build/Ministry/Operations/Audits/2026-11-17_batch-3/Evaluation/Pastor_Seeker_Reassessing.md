# Batch 3 briefs: pastor, seeker and re-assessing listener

Independent listener read, 2026-11-18. I heard each of the twelve `<slug>.brief.txt` files once, straight through. I opened no other evaluation file, card, review, outline or earlier version.

## Overall judgements

**Pastor (mainline and evangelical).** I would trust these and use most of them. None of them preaches. Faith is shown through what people did and said, and each source is named and weighed. Both kinds of pastor could use Justin, Moses, Pachomius, Thomas a Kempis, Wyclif, Arius and Baudonivia as they stand. An evangelical group will warm to Wyclif and to the Wesley thread in Thomas. A mainline group will value the honesty about sources in Arius and Chalcedon. No piece mocks a tradition. Chalcedon gives the Oriental Orthodox side its full voice. A Catholic or Orthodox pastor might feel the council's own case comes over thinner than its opponents' case, but nothing in it is unfair. The one line a pastor would need to handle with care is Justin telling Trypho that the Jews' sufferings were just.

**General seeker.** Nine of the twelve land on one hearing: I can say who it was, what happened, and roughly why it matters. Two fail on one hearing. Chalcedon has too many names and never says plainly what was at stake. Alopen is ordered for the page, not the ear, and has no ending. Narsai and Toledo pass only just, because each rests on a quarrel I am never told about (Nestorius in one, "Arian" and "from the Son" in the other). The pieces most often get lost by jumping forward and back in time, and by closing on a stray detail instead of an ending.

**Someone re-assessing their faith.** This is the most trustworthy batch I could ask for. Christian violence and coercion are told as plainly as courage. In Vardan the princes stone a prince and kill Magi. In Toledo come forced baptism, confiscation and exile. In Chalcedon a crowd burns soldiers alive. Moses faces racial contempt, and in Arius books are burned under pain of death. The pieces keep saying whose record we hear: "the winners' record", "the men who defeated it", and Athanasius "who was not there". One place reads like airbrushing. Wyclif ends on the council mocking Hus and never says that the same council burned him weeks later.

## Per piece

**Justin Martyr — PASS (all three).**
- Best: the conversion quote and the trial exchange ("I do not suppose it. I know it"). The seeker gets Logos and Socrates in one clean line.
- Pastor and re-assessor, SHOULD FIX: "the Jews' sufferings had come to them justly, yet the two parted as friends." Coming straight after, "yet... friends" softens the harsh line. Let the harsh line stand on its own, then give the friendship as a separate fact, so it does not sound like an excuse.
- Seeker: the Tatian paragraph is a list of who said what. It is the weakest stretch for the ear, but it does no harm.

**Vardan Mamikonian — PASS (pastor, re-assessor); PASS, just (seeker).**
- Re-assessor, best: both witnesses' accounts of the faked conversion are given, and the princes' own violence is told plainly. "Armenians on both sides" is honest.
- Seeker, SHOULD FIX: the Vahan paragraph jumps thirty years ahead, then "But first came Avarayr" jumps back. On one hearing I lost the thread. The piece then ends on a defeat, and its meaning sits in the flash-forward I had already half-forgotten. Put the battle first and Vahan after it, so the piece ends on why it mattered.

**Moses the Black — PASS (all three).**
- Best: the four robbers carried to church, and the contempt passage. The re-assessor hears the church's own racism told without excuse ("The monks' sayings remember contempt as well").
- Pastor, note only: "as if he were a white man" is his own humility in the sayings. The sentence before it frames it well enough. A study group will want to talk about it, which is a good thing.

**Alopen — FAIL on one hearing (seeker); PASS (pastor, re-assessor).**
- Seeker, MUST FIX: the first paragraph runs 781, then 845, then 1625, before I am told he arrived in 635. The 845 edict sits there with no link to the story. "Sent back to ordinary life" does not tell me the faith was forced out. The piece then stops on Lohan and Kie-leih "holding the community together", and I never learn what became of it. Tell it in order, put the 845 edict where it belongs as the end of the story, and say plainly that the stone is almost all we know of him.
- Re-assessor, best: "His own church set up that stone" names the source's interest honestly.

**Council of Chalcedon, 451 — FAIL on one hearing (seeker); PASS (pastor, re-assessor).**
- Re-assessor, best of the batch: "the minutes, the winners' record". Violence on both sides is told. The losing churches speak in their own words ("tore into ten thousand pieces the whole robe of Christ").
- Seeker, MUST FIX:
  - The stake is never put in plain words. The piece opens on "I accept 'from two'. 'Two' I do not accept" and I cannot hear what the difference means for what Christ is. One plain sentence on what each side feared the other was saying needs to come early.
  - There are about fifteen named people. Flavian, Anatolius, Proterius, Theodosius and Juvenal could go or be described by role.
  - "Their chronicle" in the late paragraphs has no clear owner by ear.
  - The piece ends on two stray lines: the Palestine bishop swap, then Gangra.
- Seeker and pastor, SHOULD FIX: "Every other creed was banned" sounds as if the Nicene Creed were banned too. "Made it their fourth" assumes I know the councils are counted.

**Third Council of Toledo, 589 — PASS (pastor, re-assessor); PASS, just (seeker).**
- Re-assessor, best: coercion is told in full and by name: Sunna exiled, the children of Jews baptized, property taken, relics tested by fire. The source problem is stated ("we hear it only through the men who defeated it").
- Seeker, SHOULD FIX:
  - "Remember exiles and forced rebaptism" does not say who did it to whom. By ear it can sound like the Catholics rebaptizing Goths.
  - I am never told what the Goths' church believed, or why "from the Father and the Son" matters. One clause each would carry it.
  - The canon details in the middle (wives, Seville, Saragossa) pile up for the ear.

**John Wyclif — PASS (pastor, seeker); PASS with MUST FIX (re-assessor).**
- Best: the plain account of the bread teaching, the "pearl trampled by pigs" complaint, and the closing words of Hus.
- Re-assessor, MUST FIX: the piece ends on the council "mocking" Hus. It never says that the same council burned him alive about a month later. That death is the hard fact of the scene the piece chose to end on. Leaving it out reads as softening the church's wrong at the climax. One clause fixes it.
- Re-assessor, SHOULD FIX: Thorpe "told Archbishop Arundel". Say that he was on trial for heresy, so the cost of following Wyclif is heard.

**Thomas a Kempis — PASS (all three).**
- Pastor, best: the Imitation moving from Ignatius to Wesley to Woolman across traditions. It suits both kinds of pastor. The authorship doubt is told plainly.
- Seeker, SHOULD FIX: "The canons kept it and stopped singing until, insulted, in 1429 they were at last driven to go forth" is garbled on one hearing. Say who insulted and expelled them, and why. "The pope's pardons" might be named as indulgences for an evangelical listener.

**Narsai — PASS (pastor, re-assessor); PASS, just (seeker).**
- Best: "Edessa grew dark; Nisibis grew bright" is clearly the school's own boast, and the furnace-and-gold closing line is beautiful.
- Seeker, SHOULD FIX: "A follower of Theodore and Nestorius" is the whole charge against him, and I am never told what those names stood for. There are also many names (Barhadbeshabba four times, Barsauma, Simeon, Cyrus, Zeno, Abraham).
- Re-assessor, note: Barsauma appears only as a kindly host. A listener who knows his harder record may wonder whether he is airbrushed. Check whether the record supports a word on this.

**Pachomius — PASS (all three).**
- Best: the opening in the prison and the blanket on the sickbed. His refusal to see his sister and the split after his death are told plainly.
- Seeker, SHOULD FIX: I am never told in plain words what was new: monks living together under one rule, instead of alone. "The monks of Egypt already had a way of life" muddies it. The tail has two Benedicts in one sentence (of Aniane, and of the Rule), which loses the ear right at the close.

**Arius — PASS (all three).**
- Re-assessor, best: "nearly every word of his that survives was copied by men who wanted him condemned". The death is told plainly, along with the fact that Athanasius was not there. The bishops refusing the "Arian" label is a fine touch.
- Seeker, SHOULD FIX: the piece never says that the creed made at Nicaea is still said in churches today. Without that, I don't know why this quarrel matters to me. The jump to Aquileia and back ("did not live to see any of that") is signposted but heavy.

**Baudonivia — PASS (all three).**
- Best: the opening phrase about touching the sky with a finger, and the close ("Her eyes were closed, and ours were darkened"). The re-assessor notes that Radegund burning a pagan shrine is told without excuse.
- Seeker, SHOULD FIX: "the enemy of mankind" means the devil, and the piece should say so once. The relic of the Cross is named before I know why it matters to the story.

## MUST FIX summary

1. **Alopen**: put it in time order, place the 845 edict as the end of the story and say what it did, and give the piece an ending (seeker).
2. **Council of Chalcedon, 451**: state the "from two" / "in two" stake in plain words early, cut the names down, give "their chronicle" a clear owner, and end on an ending rather than the Palestine and Gangra lines (seeker).
3. **John Wyclif**: say that the council burned Hus (re-assessor).
