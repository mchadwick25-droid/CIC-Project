"Watching the blue clouds, he carried the true sacred books. Watching the way the winds blew, he faced hardships and dangers." So an old stone pictures Alopen, who brought the books of the Church of the East to China from the far west. His own church set up that stone in 781, almost a century and a half after he came, and it lay lost until workmen digging near Xi'an found it in 1625.

He reached Chang-an, the Tang capital, in the year 635, and the emperor Taizong had him met outside the city and brought to the palace. The books were translated in the imperial library, and the emperor studied the teaching and ordered it spread. "Every region has the teaching that suits it, and its quiet influence does the people good," his decree of 638 said. Officials then built a monastery in the capital for twenty-one priests. The next emperor gave Alopen a high title, and that is the last the stone tells of him.

That community outlived him, and when its priests came to carve their story, they began it with his arrival.
