Alopen brought the books of the Church of the East to China from the far west. A stone pictures his journey: "Watching the blue clouds, he carried the true sacred books. Watching the way the winds blew, he faced hardships and dangers." His story rests almost entirely on that stone, which his own church set up in 781, about 146 years after he came.

The stone dates his arrival to 635. The emperor Taizong had him met outside Chang-an, the Tang capital, and brought to the palace. The books were translated in the imperial library, and the emperor, the stone says, studied the teaching and ordered it spread. Officials built a monastery in Chang-an for twenty-one priests, and the next emperor gave Alopen a high title. There the stone leaves him.

Around 699 Buddhists spoke out against his community, and about 712 others mocked and slandered it, but the priests Lohan and Kie-leih held it together.

In 845 an edict aimed chiefly at Buddhism forced more than three thousand foreign teachers, Christians and others, back to ordinary life. After that, the church that grew from Alopen's books fades from view in China.
