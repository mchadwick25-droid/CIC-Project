In 1625, workmen digging outside Xi'an, in China, came upon a great stone with a cross carved at its top and strange writing beside the Chinese. It told of Christians who had taught in that city a thousand years before, with the favour of the Tang emperors.

The stone named the man who first brought the books of their church, the Church of the East. We know him as Alopen, and his is the earliest Christian mission to China that the record describes. In the year 635 he reached Chang-an, the Tang capital — today's Xi'an — coming from the far west. The emperor Taizong sent his chief minister to meet him outside the capital and bring him to the palace.

The books were translated in the imperial library, and the emperor studied the teaching and ordered it spread. In 638 his decree, quoted on the stone, said of this faith: "It helps every creature, and it does people good. Let it spread freely through the empire." Officials built a monastery in the capital for twenty-one priests, and a foreign faith now had the emperor's open licence.

His church outlived him, and under the next emperor, the stone says, its monasteries filled a hundred cities. In the years after, Buddhists spoke out against it and others mocked it, but its priests held it together. In 781 they set up the stone, about 146 years after he came, and told his story: his church looking back, in words that also praise the emperors. It pictures his journey: "Watching the blue clouds, he carried the true sacred books. Watching the way the winds blew, he faced hardships and dangers." That is the stone's picture of a long, hard road, which he found by the sky and the weather, keeping the books with him.

After 781, Chinese history keeps only slight traces of the church. One is an edict of 845 that sent more than three thousand foreign teachers, Christians and others, back to ordinary life. The stone lay in the ground until 1625. Then a Chinese Christian official sent a copy to the Jesuit missionaries, and they circulated it through the empire as proof that Christianity had come to China early. So a church the world had forgotten was known again.
