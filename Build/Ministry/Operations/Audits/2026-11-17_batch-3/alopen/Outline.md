# Alopen — Outline

Line references are to files in `cic/texts/`. MAL = `malech_history-of-the-syrian-nation-and-old-evangelical-apostolic-church-of-the-east_1910.txt`; YULE = `marco-polo_book-of-ser-marco-polo-v2_yule-cordier1903.txt`; BUDGE = `yahballaha-sauma_monks-of-kublai-khan_budge1928.txt`. Full lines and verbatim text: `Source_Sheet.md`.

- **Length:** about 1 minute (about 150 words). The record is thin. One witness holds everything: a stone his own church carved about 146 years after he came. No words of his own survive, and he drops out of the record after the next emperor honours him. Do not fill.

- **Setting:**
  - Time: 635, his arrival; 638, the emperor's decree (MAL 13690, 13696). Spoken: "in the year 635".
  - Place: Chang-an, capital of the Tang emperors; today's Xi'an (YULE 2908–2910). Spoken: "Chang-an, the Tang capital". He came from what the stone calls Ta-ts'in, the Chinese name for the Roman lands in the west; Wylie renders it Syria (MAL 13687–13688; YULE 2933–2935, 2968). Spoken: "from Syria, far to the west".
  - Tradition: the Church of the East (census name: the Church of the East on the Silk Road). The stone's own name for the faith, in Wylie's English: "the Illustrious Religion" (MAL 13565, 13643); Saeki, via Budge, "the Luminous Religion" (BUDGE 5163–5164).
  - Emperor: Taitsung, founder of the Tang line (MAL 13680–13681).

- **Thread:** He carried the Church of the East's sacred books to the Tang court, where they were translated and a church was licensed in the capital — the beginning his own church carved in stone a century and a half later.

- **Shape:** Missionary or translator. Whose record: entirely the receiving church's own stone, set up by his successors in Chang-an in 781 — not the sender's, and not the Chinese court's.

- **Openings:**
  1. The stone's own image of his journey: watching the clouds and the winds, carrying the books (MAL 13688–13689). Quote 1.
  2. A stone found by workmen digging outside Xi'an in 1625, which told a forgotten story (YULE 2929–2931, 2914–2916).

- **Story beats:**
  1. **He came east with books.** In the reign of Taitsung, a man the stone calls "Most-virtuous Olopun" came from Syria carrying "the true sacred books", through hardship and danger. Why he came, or who sent him, is not recorded. MAL 13680–13689. Documented (stele's words).
  2. **Met at the border, 635.** He reached Chang-an in 635. The emperor sent his chief minister, Fang Hiuen-ling, out to the western border to bring him in as a guest — an honour for a visitor. MAL 13690–13692. Documented (stele); year Contested 635/636, tell 635.
  3. **The books translated at court.** The books were translated in the imperial library. The emperor studied the teaching in his private rooms, was "deeply impressed" with its rightness, and ordered it spread. The stone does not say who translated, or that the emperor became a Christian. MAL 13692–13695. Documented (stele).
  4. **The decree of 638.** The emperor's proclamation, quoted on the stone: right teaching has no single name; this one helps all people; let it be made known through the empire, and let a church be built in the capital, served by twenty-one priests. It stood in the I-ning ward of the capital (YULE 2967). MAL 13696–13711. Documented as the stele's quotation; the court's own record is not held.
  5. **Honoured by the next emperor.** Under Kautsung, the stone says, churches rose in every province, Alopen was given a high title, and "every city was full of churches". This is the last the stone says of him. MAL 13738–13744. Documented as the stele's claim; scale Inferential-Thin.

- **What came of them** (about a third of the piece):
  1. **The stone itself, heaviest.** In 781 his church in the capital set up a carved stone telling how the faith came, naming him first. Its Chinese text was written by the priest King-Tsing (MAL 13566–13567); the Syriac at its foot names the priest Jazedbuzid, who set it up, and lists many priests (MAL 14162–14166; YULE 2996–2997). Lost, it was found by chance in 1625 and revealed churches the world had forgotten (YULE 2914–2916, 2929–2931). Everything we know of him comes from it. Widely Accepted that it is genuine (MAL 14212–14217).
  2. **A church that lasted.** For nearly 150 years after him the stone traces the community: attacks by Buddhists in 699 and slanders in 713, which its priests withstood — defended, by Lohan and Kie-leih, not by Alopen (MAL 13744–13751); a new priest from Syria in 744 (MAL 13766–13769); care for the naked, the sick and the dead (MAL 13997–13999). Documented as the stele's account.
  3. **The end in China.** In 845 an edict of the emperor Wu-tsung sent the foreign priests of Ta-ts'in, by S. Wells Williams's report "no less than three thousand", back to ordinary life (MAL 14249–14252; YULE 3289–3292). Widely Accepted; the edict is not held.

- **Quotes** (rendered from A. Wylie's English of the stele; the Chinese is not held in a legible text):
  1. MAL 13688–13689. Original: "Observing the azure clouds, he bore the true sacred books; beholding the direction of the winds, he braved difficulties and dangers." **Rendering:** "Watching the blue clouds, he carried the true sacred books. Watching the way the winds blew, he faced hardships and dangers." Job: the opening — the stone's own picture of the journey, the only one it gives.
  2. MAL 13699–13701 (the 638 decree). Original: "Right principles have no invariable name, holy men have no invariable station; instruction is established in accordance with the locality, with the object of benefiting the people at large." **Rendering:** "Right teaching has no one fixed name. Holy men have no one fixed place. Teaching is set up to suit each place, so that it may benefit all the people." Job: why an emperor could welcome a foreign faith, in the court's own terms.
  3. MAL 13708–13709 (same decree). Original: "it is beneficial to all creatures; it is advantageous to mankind. Let it be published throughout the Empire," **Rendering:** "It does good to every creature. It is a help to humankind. Let it be made known throughout the empire." Job: the licence itself; use if Quote 2 is not used, or with it if room allows.

- **Connection to carry into the story:** What passed from Alopen was the books and their welcome. He brought sacred books to the court; they were translated in the imperial library; the emperor's decree let the faith be taught and built a church in the capital for twenty-one priests (MAL 13692–13711). That church outlived him, and in 781 its priests carved the whole story on stone, starting with his arrival (MAL 14030–14035, 14141–14166). The listener must understand that Alopen is known *only* because his successors remembered him this way: the stone is his church looking back on its own beginning, not a report from his own time. Carry this by telling who set the stone up and when, not by a "this matters because" line.

- **Links:**
  - Adam (King-Tsing) — wrote his story (the stele's Chinese text, which tells his coming). MAL 13566–13567; identification of Adam with King-Tsing is Cordier's, YULE 3000.
  - No other tie on the tree is supported. Timothy I and Thomas of Marga (same tradition) have no recorded tie to him.

- **Who tells it:**
  - **The Xi'an stele, 781** — set up in Chang-an by his own church, about 146 years after he came; Chinese text by the priest King-Tsing. The listener must know it is the church's own retrospect, and praise of emperors is part of how it is written. Read here in A. Wylie's 19th-century English (reprinted in Malech, 1910).
  - **Henry Yule and Henri Cordier (1903), S. Wells Williams (reprinted 1910), E. A. Wallis Budge (1928)** — modern scholars, for the stone's finding, the edict of 845 and the readings of the name and title. Not needed by name in the audio unless a disputed point is voiced.

- **Do not say:**
  - "the first Christian in China" → "the first missionary the stone names" or simply "came to China". Earlier claims exist (MAL 14202–14207); none is secure.
  - "Nestorian" for his church → "the Church of the East". The stone's English heading says "the Syrian Church".
  - "monk", "bishop" or "priest" → no rank: "a man from Syria", or the stone's own "Most-virtuous". His rank is not recorded.
  - "sent by the patriarch" / "sent by his church" → nothing; who sent him is not recorded.
  - "his name means Rabban, 'monk'" → leave out; it is one scholar's guess among several.
  - "Alopen translated the Bible / the scriptures" → "the books were translated in the imperial library". Who translated is not stated.
  - "the emperor became a Christian" → "the emperor studied the teaching and ordered it spread".
  - "he led the twenty-one priests" / "he built the church" → "the decree ordered a church built in the capital, served by twenty-one priests".
  - "churches in every province" / "every city was full of churches" as fact → "the stone says". It is the stele's own boast with no second witness.
  - A title in quotes ("Great Patron", "great conservator") → "a high title"; translators word it differently.
  - Alopen's portrait in the church → leave out; the portrait was the emperor's (MAL 13716–13718).
  - "he died in China", or any date or place of death → nothing is known.
  - "persecuted", "killed", "martyred" for 845 → "sent back to ordinary life".
  - "the stone was hidden / buried in 845 to save it" → leave out; it is Pauthier's guess (YULE 3288–3289).
  - "he travelled the Silk Road", "across deserts", "by caravan" → leave out; the stone gives only clouds, winds, hardship and danger.
  - 636 or 630 → 635, the stone's year as Wylie gives it.

- **Leave out:**
  - The year dispute (635/636/630), the name etymologies (Rabban, Ruben, Yahballaha) and the title wordings: card material.
  - The emperor's portrait, the Ta-ts'in geography passage (MAL 13721–13735), and the later emperors' gifts: side detail with no room at one minute.
  - The Syriac name lists and Adam's titles ("Pope of China"): Chavannes and Pelliot doubt the "pope" title (chavannes-pelliot 1911, 29500–29502); not needed.
  - Wigram's account (empress's library, "about 630"): not in the stele; unreliable.
  - The claimed meeting with the Buddhist teacher Zendo (IRM 1916, 38830): speculation.
  - The authenticity dispute beyond one clause: settled in Williams's view, and the card carries it.
