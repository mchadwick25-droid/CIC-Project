## BLOCKING

**B1. Taitsung was not the founder of the Tang dynasty.** Setting says "Emperor: Taitsung, founder of the Tang line" (sheet S4). That comes from Wylie's wording at MAL 13680–13681. Legge corrects it: "T'ai Tsung was not the founder of the T'ang dynasty, as Wylie's and other translations unnecessarily say. His father Kao Tsu was the first emperor" (LEGGE 1033–1034; text at 1008–1010, "commenced his glorious reign over the (recently) established dynasty", reign dates 627–649).
*Correction:* "Emperor: Taizong (Wylie 'Taitsung', Legge 'T'ai Tsung'), second emperor of the Tang, reigned 627–649 (LEGGE 1008–1010, 1033–1034). Spoken: 'the emperor Taizong'." Add to **Do not say**: "the founder of the Tang dynasty" → "the Tang emperor Taizong".

**B2. Western suburb, not the western border.** Beat 2 says the minister went "out to the western border to bring him in". Wylie has "to the west border, conducted his guest into the interior" (MAL 13691–13692). Legge has "to the western suburb, there to receive the visitor, and conduct him to the palace" (LEGGE 1234–1236). Budge (Saeki) has "with a guard of honour to bring him to the palace" (BUDGE 5175–5177). Two of the three put the welcome at the capital and end it at the palace. Wylie alone sends the minister to the frontier.
*Correction:* Beat 2: "The emperor sent his chief minister, Fang Xuanling (Wylie 'Fang Hiuen-ling', Legge 'Fang Hsüan-ling'), carrying his staff of office, to the western edge of the capital to receive the visitor and bring him to the palace — an honour for a guest. MAL 13690–13692; LEGGE 1231–1236; BUDGE 5175–5177." Add to **Do not say**: "met him at the frontier / the border" → "met him outside the capital and brought him to the palace".

**B3. A monastery for twenty-one priests, built by officials after the decree.** Beat 4, Thread, Connection and Do not say all carry Wylie's "let the proper authority build a Syrian church ... which shall be governed by twenty-one priests" (MAL 13709–13711) as part of the decree. Legge differs on three points:
- The decree ends at "let it have free course throughout the empire". Legge: "Here I think the words of the proclamation should terminate" (LEGGE 1254, 1279).
- The building is then told as an act: "The proper officers forthwith, in the capital in the Ward of Righteousness and Repose, built a Ta Ts'in monastery, sufficient to accommodate twenty-one priests" (LEGGE 1256–1257, 1477).
- "Not 'a church;' one of its halls would serve the part of one" (LEGGE 1285).

Budge (Saeki) agrees with Legge: "A Ta-ch'in monastery was built in the capital and 21 priests were appointed to it" (BUDGE 5209–5211). Wylie's "governed by" also suggests the twenty-one ran it, which no other rendering says.
*Ruling:* call it a monastery, built by the officials, with twenty-one priests.
*Corrections:*
- Beat 4: "... let it have free course through the empire. The officials then built a monastery in the I-ning ward of the capital (Legge: 'the Ward of Righteousness and Repose'), with twenty-one priests. MAL 13696–13711; LEGGE 1240–1257, 1477; BUDGE 5206–5211."
- Thread: replace "a church was licensed in the capital" with "the faith was allowed to spread and a monastery was built for it in the capital".
- Connection: replace "built a church in the capital for twenty-one priests" with "and officials built a monastery in the capital for twenty-one priests"; replace "That church outlived him" with "That community outlived him".
- Do not say: replace the line on the priests with: "he led the twenty-one priests" / "he built the church" / "a church governed by twenty-one priests" → "officials built a monastery in the capital, with twenty-one priests".

**B4. "Every city was full of churches" is Wylie's alone.** Beat 5 quotes it, and Do not say repeats it. Legge has "Monasteries filled a hundred cities", and before that "in every one of the Prefectures" (LEGGE 1636–1642). Budge (Saeki) has "monasteries to be built in every prefecture" (BUDGE 5211–5212). The quoted phrase overstates even the stone's own boast.
*Correction:* Beat 5: "Under Kao Tsung (Wylie 'Kautsung', reigned 650–683), the stone says, monasteries were built in every prefecture and filled 'a hundred cities', and Alopen was given a high title. MAL 13738–13744; LEGGE 1633–1643; BUDGE 5211–5214." Do not say: replace the line with: "churches in every province" / "every city was full of churches" → "the stone says monasteries were built in every prefecture, in a hundred cities", always as the stone's claim. Add Legge's "Lord of the Great Law" (LEGGE 1639) to the Do not say line on the title as a third wording, and keep "a high title".

**B5. The 845 edict: more than 3,000 covers two foreign religions.** Item 3 says the edict sent "the foreign priests of Ta-ts'in, by S. Wells Williams's report 'no less than three thousand'". Legge quotes the edict from Du Halde's translation: "as well those of Ta Ts'in as of Mu-hu-pi, amounting to more than 3,000 persons, be required to resume the ways of ordinary life" (LEGGE 4346–4350). Yule also names both groups, "Ta-T'sin and Mubupa (Christian and Mobed or Magian?)" (YULE 3291–3292). Williams's wording gives all 3,000 to the Christians. That is a misleading number.
*Correction:* Item 3: "In 845 an edict of the emperor Wu-tsung sent the foreign teachers of Ta-ts'in and of the Mu-hu-pi (probably Zoroastrian priests), more than three thousand in all, back to ordinary life (LEGGE 4339–4350; YULE 3289–3292). Widely Accepted; the edict is not held." Add to **Do not say**: "three thousand Christian priests" → "more than three thousand foreign teachers, Christians and others".

**B6. Quote 2's rendering turns a point the translators disagree on into a claim about place.** My rendering of Wylie: "Right teaching has no single fixed name. Holy men have no single fixed station. Teaching is set up to fit each place, to do good to all the people." The outline's rendering is faithful to Wylie. But Legge gives the second clause a different meaning: "sages have not always the same personality" (LEGGE 1242), meaning the person or form, not the place. The two part exactly at the clause this quote leans on, and the legible Chinese is not held to settle it.
*Correction:* use Legge, the closer translation, which has a note on almost every clause. Quote 2 → LEGGE 1241–1244. Original: "Systems have not always the same name; sages have not always the same personality. Every region has its appropriate doctrines, which by their imperceptible influence benefit the inhabitants." Rendering: "Teachings do not always have the same name. Sages are not always the same person. Every region has the teaching that suits it, and its quiet influence does the people good." The job is unchanged. Under the Quotes heading, replace "rendered from A. Wylie's English" with "from Wylie's or Legge's English, as marked".

**B7. "From Syria" spoken as fact flattens a Contested point.** Setting gives "Spoken: 'from Syria, far to the west'". The stone's word is Ta-ts'in. Wylie renders it "Syria" (MAL 13687–13688). Legge keeps "the kingdom of Ta Ts'in" (LEGGE 1011, 1245) and places Alopen's start only generally, "the Far West from China" (LEGGE 4117–4118). Yule says "here apparently implying Syria" (YULE 2968–2969). Budge says "(Judea, Syria)" (BUDGE 5173–5174). The sheet itself marks it Contested (S2).
*Correction:* "Spoken: 'from the far west — the land the Chinese called Ta-ts'in, which his translators read as Syria'." Beat 1: "came from Ta-ts'in (Wylie: 'Syria')". Item 2's "a new priest from Syria in 744" → "from Ta-ts'in" (Legge has "the monk Chi-ho", LEGGE 2145–2146). Add to **Do not say**: "from Syria" as plain fact → "from the far west, the land the Chinese called Ta-ts'in"; and "from Persia" → leave out (sheet S2 sets it aside, and the Church of the East census entry uses Persia only for where the church's bishops sat).

## MINOR

**M1. Clouds and winds.** Legge has "Guiding himself by the azure clouds ... Watching the laws of the winds" (LEGGE 1012, 1227–1229), against Wylie's "Observing" (MAL 13688). Quote 1 stays as written; its rendering ("Watching") fits both. Add to Do not say: "the clouds guided him" / "led by a sign in the sky" → keep the stone's image, "watching the clouds and the winds" (Assemani, after Renaudot, ASS 35917–35920).

**M2. Beat 3.** "Deeply impressed" is Wylie's phrase alone. Legge has "deeply convinced of its correctness and truth" (LEGGE 1238–1239), and Budge has "became convinced of its truth" (BUDGE 5180). Drop the quotation marks: "the emperor looked into the teaching in his palace rooms, was satisfied it was right, the stone says, and ordered it spread." Legge alone has the emperor questioning Alopen in person ("questioned him about his system in his own forbidden apartments", LEGGE 1237–1238), so do not state a private audience as fact. Add to the translator line in Do not say: "the whole Bible / all the scriptures were translated" → leave out. Legge: "This can only mean that portions of the Scriptures were translated" (LEGGE 1268).

**M3. Legge's lecture guesses** (LEGGE 4146–4169, 4240–4246, 4380–4385). He thinks Alopen left Mesopotamia about 630, led "a considerable band of other monks", had "some previous communication" with the emperor, translated or oversaw the translation of "short and important passages", and that Christians buried the stone in 845. Wylie's "among the enlightened and holy men who arrived was ... Olopun" (MAL 13681–13687) also hints at companions; Legge's text does not. Add to Do not say: "he came alone" / "he led a band of monks" / "the emperor had sent for him" → none of these is in the stone. Extend the "hidden / buried in 845" line to name Legge's guess along with Pauthier's (LEGGE 4382–4384).

**M4. "Most-virtuous" is a courtesy title.** Legge: "'The Greatly Virtuous' is perhaps not more than the equivalent of our 'Reverend,' or 'Father'" (LEGGE 1259–1260). Do not say line on rank: if the stone's word is used, frame it as a title of respect ("whom the stone calls by the honorific 'Most-virtuous'"), not as praise of his character.

**M5. Item 2's opposition.** "Attacks by Buddhists" goes further than the stone. Wylie has "raised their voices"; Legge has "made their voices heard (against the Religion)" (MAL 13745–13746; LEGGE 1645–1647). Legge's dates are 698–699 and 712 (LEGGE 1645, 1863). Legge's defenders are "restoring the mysterious net, and ... rebinding its meshes which had been broken" (LEGGE 1870–1871), where Wylie has "maintained ... and preserved" (MAL 13750–13751).
*Correction:* "around 699 Buddhists spoke out against it in the eastern capital, and about 712–713 others mocked and slandered it in the western; the priests Lohan and Kie-leih (Legge: Lo-han, Chi-lieh) held it together." The care for the hungry, naked, sick and dead was the yearly work of the priest-official I-sz' (Legge "I-sze"; MAL 13997–13999; LEGGE 2512, 2924–2930), not of the whole church. Say so.

**M6. Item 1's names and heading.** "Lists many priests" → "names about seventy clergy, from bishop to deacon" (LEGGE 3549–3552; YULE 2996–2997: sixty-seven, most of them priests). The Do not say line "The stone's English heading says 'the Syrian Church'" → "Wylie's English heading says 'the Syrian Church'; Legge's says 'a priest of the Ta Ts'in Monastery' (LEGGE 364–365)". Keep "the Church of the East".

**M7. Link to Adam.** The stone's own heading pairs the Chinese author with the Syriac "Adam, Presbyter and Chorepiscopos, and Papas of China" (LEGGE 364–368). Cite LEGGE 364–368 beside YULE 3000. The identification then rests on the stone's heading, with Cordier agreeing, not on Cordier alone.

**M8. Who tells it.** Replace "Read here in A. Wylie's 19th-century English" with "Read in two 19th-century English translations: A. Wylie's (reprinted in Malech, 1910) and James Legge's (1888, with notes). Where they differ, the outline says which it follows." Add Legge to the scholars line for the 845 edict.

**M9. Quote 3.** Wylie has "Let it be published throughout the Empire" (MAL 13709). Legge has "let it have free course throughout the empire" (LEGGE 1253–1254), and Budge "let it have free course" (BUDGE 5208). Wylie reads as a proclamation; the other two give the faith leave to spread, which is the job this quote does. Use LEGGE 1252–1254. Original: "(the system) is helpful to (all) creatures, and profitable for men: — let it have free course throughout the empire". Rendering: "It helps every creature, and it does people good. Let it spread freely through the empire."

**M10. Opening 2.** Add to Do not say: "digging a grave" → leave out; Legge says the accounts differ (LEGGE 3664–3666).
