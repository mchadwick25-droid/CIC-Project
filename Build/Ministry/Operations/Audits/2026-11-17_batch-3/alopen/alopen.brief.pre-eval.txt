"Watching the blue clouds, he carried the true sacred books. Watching the way the winds blew, he faced hardships and dangers." So an old stone pictures Alopen, who brought the books of the Church of the East from the far west. His own church set up that stone in 781, about 150 years after he came, and named him first. In 845 an emperor's edict sent more than three thousand foreign teachers, Christians and others, back to ordinary life. The stone itself lay lost until 1625.

He reached Chang-an, the Tang capital, in the year 635, and the emperor Taizong had him met outside the city and brought to the palace. The books were translated in the imperial library, and the emperor studied the teaching and ordered it spread. Officials built a monastery in the capital for twenty-one priests, and the next emperor gave Alopen a high title.

Around 699 Buddhists spoke out against the faith, and about 712 others mocked and slandered it, but the priests Lohan and Kie-leih held the community together.
