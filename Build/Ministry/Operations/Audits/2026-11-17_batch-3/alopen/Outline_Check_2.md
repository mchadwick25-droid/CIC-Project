# Alopen — Outline Check, round 2

## The heart of his life (written before reading Outline.md)

From the census entry (`alopen`, knownFor and longDescription) and Source_Sheet.md (E1–E6, I1–I3, H1–H2):

Alopen carried the Church of the East's sacred books from the far west (Ta-ts'in, which Wylie renders Syria) to Chang-an, the Tang capital, in 635. It is the first Christian mission to China that the record describes. His work was a bridge into Chinese and into the court: the books were translated in the imperial library, the emperor Taizong questioned him and studied the teaching, and in 638 a decree let the faith "have free course throughout the empire" and had officials build a monastery in the capital for twenty-one priests. Under the next emperor, Gaozong, monasteries were built in every prefecture and Alopen received a high title. What he planted lived as an officially sanctioned church for about a century and a half, held together under Buddhist and other pressure by later priests, and was ended by the edict of 845. Everything known of him comes from one witness: the stone his own church set up in 781, found again in 1625. No words of his own survive. Shape: Missionary or translator.

## The outline's heart, compared

The outline's heart: the earliest Christian mission to China the record describes; the books brought to Chang-an in 635, translated in the imperial library; the emperor's study and order to spread it; the 638 decree and the monastery for twenty-one priests; a church that lasted and faded; known only from the stone of 781, whose finding in 1625 gave back a forgotten church.

It agrees with mine in substance. It gives the 1625 finding more weight than I did, as half of the heart rather than as how-we-know and legacy. The sources hold that weighting: Yule says the finding "disclosed their forgotten existence" (YULE 2914–2916), and Legge tells the finding and the Jesuits' use of it at length (LEGGE 3662–3710). Gaozong's expansion and Alopen's title sit in beat 5, not the heart. That is the stone's claim about the next reign, and leaving it out of the heart is acceptable. No major contribution is missing. Beats 1–4 tell the heart inside the first minute.

## Verdict: FIX

One blocking finding: a quote that presents one translator's reading as the decree's words where the two translations differ in meaning. The rest are minor.

## Findings

### BLOCKING

**B1. Quote 1 carries a clause the two translations read differently.** LEGGE 1241–1242 has "sages have not always the same personality"; Wylie, MAL 13699–13700 (sheet E4), has "holy men have no invariable station". One means person, the other means place. The Chinese is not held to settle it (Source_Sheet E4). The outline's own rule (line 3) is to tell only what both support. Spoken as the emperor's words, "Sages are not always the same person" presents Legge's reading as settled.
*My rendering, before comparing:* "Teachings do not always go by one name, and sages are not always the same person. Every region has the teaching that suits it, and its quiet influence does the people good." The outline's rendering matches mine and Legge's English. The problem is the source, not the rendering.
*Correction:* Cut the middle sentence. Original: "Systems have not always the same name; … Every region has its appropriate doctrines, which by their imperceptible influence benefit the inhabitants." (LEGGE 1241–1244). Rendering: "Teachings do not always have the same name. Every region has the teaching that suits it, and its quiet influence does the people good." Wylie agrees on name, locality and benefit (MAL 13699–13701). Add to Do not say: "sages are not always the same person" / "holy men have no fixed place" → leave out; the translations differ.

### MINOR

**M1. Heart: "The emperor Taizong had the books translated".** The stone uses the passive: "the sacred books were translated in the imperial library" (MAL 13692–13693; LEGGE 1236–1237). It names no one who ordered or did the translating. The outline's own Do not say (line 71) already gives the passive. *Correction:* "The books were translated in the imperial library; the emperor Taizong studied the teaching and ordered it spread." Add to Do not say: "the emperor had the books translated" → "the books were translated in the imperial library".

**M2. "Very slight traces" is anchored to the wrong date.** Legge's "Very slight indications of its presence in China are found subsequently" (LEGGE 4252) comes straight after the stone's erection in 781 (LEGGE 4249–4251), not after 845. Beat 7 says "After that [845], Chinese history keeps only 'very slight' traces", and the heart cites the same line. The edict of 845 is itself one of those slight traces. *Correction:* Beat 7: "After 781 Chinese history keeps only very slight traces of the church (LEGGE 4251–4253); the edict of 845 is one of them." In the heart, "lasted about two centuries, then faded from view" holds (the 845 edict still names teachers of Ta Ts'in, LEGGE 4347–4348), but cite it as LEGGE 4346–4350 with 4251–4253. Correct Do not say line 61 to match.

**M3. "About seventy clergy named on it, from bishop to deacon" (beat 6, What came of them 2).** Legge says "about seventy names in Syriac of individuals … of various ranks, from bishop down to deacon" (LEGGE 3549–3551). Yule gives sixty-seven names, "most of whom are characterised as priests" (YULE 2996–2997). Not every name is shown to be a cleric. *Correction:* "about seventy names, most of them clergy, from bishop to deacon".

**M4. Length is tight for what the outline asks.** The census marks him `major: true`. At about 225 words the outline asks for: the finding, the arrival, the translation, the decree, Gaozong, the opposition of 699 and 712–713 (now required by Do not say line 84), the stone of 781, the 845 edict, the silence, and the Jesuits. Impact should be about a third, so beats 5–7 and impact get about 75 words. That pushes a writer toward the run of one-act sentences that Standard.md forbids. *Correction:* set the length at the template default of 2 minutes, about 300 words: "a major figure whose heart has two halves; the thin record keeps it from stretching further". Or, if 1½ minutes stays, mark beat 5 and the Jesuits' circulation as the first things to shorten.

**M5. Do not say: two additions.** (a) "Alopen built churches across China" / "he founded monasteries in every province" → the stone credits Gaozong's reign, and always as the stone's claim (MAL 13738–13740; LEGGE 1633–1636). (b) "the stone was carved by Alopen's own disciples" → "set up by his church, about 146 years after he came" (MAL 14141–14166). "His successors" in the heart could be heard as personal disciples.
