## The test, from one hearing

- **Who or what:** Alopen, a man from the far west who brought the Church of the East's sacred books to Tang China.
- **What happened:** He reached the capital, Chang-an, in 635. The emperor Taizong had him received and brought to the palace. The books were translated, a decree of 638 let the faith spread, and officials built a monastery for twenty-one priests. The next emperor gave him a high title.
- **Why the connection matters:** Everything about him comes from a stone his own church set up in 781, about 146 years later. The community he started remembered its beginning as his arrival.

Where I got lost: the stone is "found near Xi'an" in paragraph one, and he "reached Chang-an" in paragraph two. A first-time listener is not told these are the same city. Nothing reads as a children's book or talks down.

**One-hearing test alone: PASS.** All three lines come through on one hearing. The REVISE below is for the source check, not for understanding.

## Verdict: REVISE

## Findings

**B1 (BLOCKING). The record's conflict and coercion are left out.** "That community outlived him" leaves the impression of an unbroken, favoured success. The stone itself records that Buddhists spoke out against the faith around 699 and others mocked and slandered it about 712–713, and that the priests Lohan and Kie-leih held it together (MAL 13744–13751; LEGGE 1645–1647, 1863–1871). Its end in China came by edict in 845 (LEGGE 4339–4350; YULE 3289–3292). *Change:* in the closing paragraph, tell in two short linked sentences that the community was spoken against and mocked within a lifetime of him and was held together by Lohan and Kie-leih — told as theirs, not Alopen's — and that in 845 an emperor's edict sent more than three thousand foreign teachers, Christians and others, back to ordinary life. Keep the Do not say wordings ("spoke out against", "mocked and slandered"; no "persecuted", "killed", "three thousand Christian priests"). The ending on the priests carving his arrival can stay, after this.

*Do not say line to add to the outline:* "That community outlived him" (or any after-life that is all favour) with no opposition and no end → "Buddhists spoke out against it around 699, and others mocked and slandered it about 712–713; the priests Lohan and Kie-leih held it together. In 845 an edict sent its foreign teachers, Christians and others, more than three thousand, back to ordinary life." Both are must-tell even at one minute.

**M1 (MINOR). Over length.** 184 words against about 150. Fixing B1 adds roughly 40 words, so cut elsewhere to land near 160–170 at most. Candidates: the outline lists Quote 2 as optional in its job; the first paragraph's "who brought the books of the Church of the East to China from the far west" can tighten; "and that is the last the stone tells of him" can be shorter.

**M2 (MINOR). Chang-an and Xi'an not tied.** The stone is "found near Xi'an", then he "reached Chang-an, the Tang capital". *Change:* "Chang-an, the Tang capital, today's Xi'an" (YULE 2908–2910), or name the find place as the old capital.
