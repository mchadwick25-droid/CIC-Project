# Review: Justin Martyr

Brief reviewed: `justin-martyr.brief.txt` (470 words). Reviewer did not write it and read no earlier draft, brief, or card text.

## The test, from one hearing

- **Who:** Justin, a second-century philosopher from a Roman town in Samaria who became a Christian, taught in Rome and wrote a defence of the Christians to the emperor.
- **What happened:** He tried the philosophy schools, turned to the prophets and Christ after meeting an old man, argued with Trypho, asked Antoninus Pius to judge Christians by deeds not the name, taught Christ as the Word every race shares; about 165 he refused to sacrifice before Rusticus and was scourged and beheaded with six others.
- **Why the connection matters:** His books lived on: Irenaeus used them against Marcion for the one God who made the world. His hearer Tatian praised him and repeated his teaching, though Irenaeus, hostile, says Tatian left the Church after Justin died.

Where I got lost: only briefly in the Tatian paragraph ("his hearer left the Church" comes after two other witnesses, so "his hearer" takes a beat to resolve). Nothing reads as a children's book or talks down.

**One-hearing test alone: PASS.** All three lines came clear from a single hearing.

- **Craft: 4.** Strong opening that sets time, place and the search at once; the stakes (the name as the charge) are told; the connection is carried through acts (quoting, repeating, the shared danger from Crescens). The ending on Tertullian's name is clean. Weight is off the outline's budget: the life runs long and "what came of him" is about a fifth of the story, not the outline's third.
- **Rigor: 5.** Every sentence traces to the outline; all Do-not-say lines honoured (Crescens not made the killer; trial credited to "the account... written later by an unknown hand"; "rooms above a bath-house"; Trypho's hard edge told with the friendly parting; two reasons for the conversion; "godless" avoided; no reply from the emperor claimed; "Irenaeus and Hippolytus say" for Tatian's hearing; Irenaeus's hostility flagged).
- **Accessibility: 4.** Grade 7.2, reading ease 77.5, 16.2 words a sentence, longest 30. Under grade 8, but on the listening read it is not flat: the thought is kept whole and *logos* is worked in naturally. The Tatian paragraph is the densest stretch.
