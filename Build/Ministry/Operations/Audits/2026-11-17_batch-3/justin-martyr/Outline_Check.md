# Outline check: Justin Martyr

**Verdict: FIX** (three blocking findings; the rest minor).

Checked against R1, R2, AR, GEB, TAT, ANF1, ANF2, ANF3, ANF5, EUS, Photius and the Jerome letter at their cited lines. I also checked `justin-martyr_opera-addubitata-grc-lat_otto1879.txt` (Otto), which the sheet set aside.

Quote renderings, done independently first:
- **Q1 (AR 428–436).** Mine: "At once a fire was kindled in my soul, and a longing for the prophets took hold of me, and for those men who are friends of Christ. Turning his words over with myself, I found this alone to be philosophy, sure and profitable. So, and for these reasons, I am a philosopher." The outline's version holds: every clause is there and nothing is added. "Sure" for ἀσφαλῆ is right. "His words" are the old man's (αὐτοῦ).
- **Q2 (R1 1230–1233).** Mine: "We have been taught that Christ is the firstborn of God, and we have declared earlier that he is the Word, of whom every race of men partook. And those who lived with the Word are Christians, even if they were thought godless." The outline's version holds.
- **Q3 (GEB 1410–1411, 1420–1421).** Mine: "So you suppose that you will go up into the heavens to receive some rewards?" "I do not suppose; I know, and am fully assured." The outline's version holds. The line numbers are right.

## BLOCKING

**B1. The Acts: wrong witness kind, and no account of which text the beats rest on.**
- The sheet's witness-kind key files the trial record as **contemporary**. McGiffert (EUS 23767–23771) says it belongs "to a later age (probably the third century)". Dods (ANF1 29216) says nothing is known of its date or authorship.
- The held Greek is a single text. Gebhardt reprints Otto (GEB 209).
- Otto's prolegomena (Otto 1829–1925) trace that text to one Grottaferrata manuscript, from the tenth-century collection of saints' lives of Symeon Metaphrastes. He says that collection is "partly collected … partly composed or interpolated". Papebroch first printed it in 1695, and Vatican 655 is a later copy. Otto also says part of the title is "of a later age".
- Beats 6–7, Opening 2 and Q3 all rest on this one transmission. The census's "three recensions" rests on Musurillo, who is not held. So no beat can be placed in a particular recension, and none may be called the oldest or shortest form.

*Correction:*
- In the sheet's witness kinds, move the trial record from "contemporary" to "later: author and date unknown (McGiffert: probably third century); held in one text, Otto's, reprinted by Gebhardt, which came down through a tenth-century collection of saints' lives (Otto 1829–1925)".
- In beat 6, replace "near-court record" with "a record set out as question and answer, by an unknown later hand".
- In Who tells it, replace "It reads like a court transcript" with "It is set out like a court record, though written down later by an unknown hand, and the held text comes through one medieval collection".
- Add to Do not say: "the court transcript", or "his exact words at the trial". Say instead: "the account of his trial has him say …".
- Keep Widely Accepted for the core facts: Rusticus, the refusal, the beheading. Attribute the dialogue wording to the record.
- In the Library sweep, move Otto 1879 from "Set aside" to "Used", for the Acta prolegomena and apparatus. That file holds more than the doubtful works.

**B2. Opening 2 misreads the address.**
- The outline says "a room above the baths of one Martinus". The Greek is "ἐπάνω μένω τινὸς Μαρτίνου τοῦ Τιμοθίνου βαλανείου" (GEB 1347–1348). That reads: above one Martinus, at the bath of Timothinus. The manuscripts give "Timiotinus" (GEB 1364), and ANF1 29309 reads "above one Martinus, at the Timiotinian Bath".
- The baths are not said to be Martinus's.
- Otto (25440–25500) judges "τινὸς Μαρτίνου" a copyist's marginal gloss. He reads "I lodge on the upper floor of the bath of Timothinus".

*Correction:* Opening 2 should read "…then gives his address: he lodged upstairs at a bath-house in Rome. The copies differ over the names, and 'one Martinus' may be a copyist's addition (Otto)." Add to Do not say: "the baths of Martinus" or "Martinus's house". Say instead: "rooms above a bath-house".

**B3. Tatian: a claim pinned to his own words that he does not make, and a Connection that does not say what passed.**
- What came of them 3 says "Tatian heard him at Rome and called him 'the most admirable Justin' (TAT 7956; ANF2 6526)". Tatian's text says neither that he heard Justin nor where.
- That Tatian was Justin's hearer rests on Irenaeus (ANF1 33664), who is later and hostile to Tatian ("excited and puffed up"). It also rests on Hippolytus (ANF5 13005), who is later still.
- "At Rome" is an inference from Crescens "made his nest in the great city" (TAT 8105).
- The Connection says "Hippolytus says the same". Hippolytus says only that Tatian "did not entertain similar opinions with his master". He does not mention Justin's death or a break with the Church.
- The Connection never names the concrete thing that passed. Tatian's own book repeats a teaching of Justin's: the demons are like robbers who seize people and then release them for ransom. Tatian says Justin "rightly denounced" them as such (TAT 7956–7960; ANF2 6526–6533).

*Correction:*
- Impact 3 should read: "Irenaeus and Hippolytus, both later, say Tatian was his hearer or disciple (ANF1 33664; ANF5 13005). Tatian himself calls him 'the most admirable Justin' and repeats his teaching that demons act like kidnappers holding people for ransom (TAT 7956–7960; ANF2 6526–6533)."
- In the Connection, replace "Tatian was one of those who came to hear him" with "Irenaeus and Hippolytus say Tatian was one of his hearers".
- State what passed: Justin's teaching, of which Tatian repeats the demons-as-robbers point by name, and the quarrel with Crescens, which Tatian says endangered them both.
- Replace "(Hippolytus says the same…)" with "(Hippolytus, also later, says only that Tatian did not hold his master's opinions)".
- Add to Do not say: "Tatian says he studied under Justin". Say instead: "Irenaeus and Hippolytus say Tatian was his hearer".

## MINOR

- **M1.** Q1 cite: AR 429–436, not 429–435 (the last sentence is on line 436). The sheet's E3 should read 428–436.
- **M2.** Q2 ANF cross-check: ANF1 15685–15692, not 15692–15693.
- **M3.** Beat 4, "were in a sense Christians". Justin says flatly that they "are Christians" (R1 1232). Cut "in a sense".
- **M4.** Beat 4, "judge … by what they did, not by the name". The name argument is First Apology 4 (R1 124 ff.). Add that cite.
- **M5.** Setting, "born at Flavia Neapolis in Samaria (R1 72–75)". R1 says his family were "of Flavia Neapolis in Syria Palaestina". "Born" and "in Samaria" are McGiffert's words, as an editor (EUS 22420–22422). Attribute them.
- **M6.** Beat 5, "clashed in public". Justin says he questioned Crescens and proved he knew nothing (R2 283–285). "In public discussions" is Eusebius (EUS 23797). Rewrite as: "He questioned Crescens and, he says, proved he knew nothing".
- **M7.** Impact 1, Tertullian named him "first among" these writers. Rewrite as "first in a list of" (ANF3 43162), so a writer does not read it as a ranking.
- **M8.** Impact 5. Delete the licence to say "one of the fullest early descriptions": no held source says it. Describe the gathering only.
- **M9.** The Thread leaves out the heaviest impact. It also presents the Word idea as a legacy, though Impact 4 says no later carrier is traced. Rewrite: "A philosopher who found his philosophy in Christ, argued to the emperor that Christians were condemned for a name alone, and left books that later teachers used to defend the one God who made the world."
- **M10.** Length and shape. The 3-minute stretch is justified. But three quotes (about 115 words), a trial opening, two trial beats and a death-led Impact 2 risk letting the end swallow the story. Correction:
  - Use Opening 2 or Q3 at the trial, not both.
  - Cut the companions' answers to Euelpistus's, which serves Impact 3.
  - Give Trypho and Crescens two or three sentences each.
  - Budget roughly 130 words for the search and turn, 110 for the debate, petition and Word, 70 for the trial and death, and 140 for what came of him.
- **M11.** Beat 6, "an offering to the gods for the emperor's order". Rewrite as "to sacrifice to the gods, as the emperor commanded" (GEB 1433–1434; ANF1 29396).
- **M12.** Links, Irenaeus "first to mention his martyrdom". That is McGiffert's judgment (EUS 23765). Attribute it.
- **M13.** Do not say should add a line on the editor's preface: ANF1 29209–29211 gives Crescens "the ill-renown of stirring up the persecution". That is a modern editor's view, and the Acts do not name Crescens.

Everything else holds. The Crescens Do-not-say lines match R2 273–276, TAT 8108–8111 and McGiffert (EUS 23782–23795). The hard edge with Trypho is supported (AR 788–806, 6589–6607). The Tertullian, Irenaeus, Eusebius, Photius and Jerome lines are verbatim. The Ptolemaeus and Lucius case is supported (R2 136–158). The impact ranking is sound. The census buckets and the Acta Sanctorum volumes show no other held text of the Acts.
