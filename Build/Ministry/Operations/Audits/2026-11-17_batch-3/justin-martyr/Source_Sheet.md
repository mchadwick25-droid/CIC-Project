## 1. Setting

**S1. Who he was and where from.** Own words, First Apology 1. Documented.
R1 72–75: "ὑπὲρ τῶν ἐκ παντὸς γένους ἀνθρώπων ἀδίκως μισουμένων καὶ ἐπηρεαζομένων, Ἰουστῖνος Πρίσκου τοῦ Βακχείου, τῶν ἀπὸ Φλαουΐας Νέας πόλεως τῆς Συρίας Παλαιστίνης, εἷς αὐτῶν ὤν, τὴν προσφώνησιν καὶ ἔντευξιν πεποίημαι."
ANF1 13965–13969: "I, Justin, the son of Priscus and grandson of Bacchius, natives of Flavia Neapolis in Palestine, present this address and petition in behalf of those of all nations who are unjustly hated and wantonly abused, myself being one of them."
Plain word: a town in Samaria. McGiffert (editor) at EUS 22420–22422: "He was born in Flavia Neapolis, a Roman town built close by the ruins of the ancient Sychem, in Samaria." (Editor's note.)

**S2. Addressees of the First Apology.** Own words. Documented.
R1 68–71: "Αὐτοκράτορι Τίτῳ Αἰλίῳ Ἀδριανῷ Ἀντωνίνῳ Εὐσεβεῖ Καίσαρι Σεβαστῷ καὶ Οὐηρισσίμῳ υἱῷ Φιλισόφῳ, καὶ Λουκίῳ … ἱερᾷ τε Συγκλήτῳ καὶ Δήμῳ παντὶ Ῥωμαίων"
ANF1 13961–13965: "To the Emperor Titus Ælius Adrianus Antoninus Pius Augustus Cæsar, and to his son Verissimus the Philosopher, and to Lucius the Philosopher … and to the sacred Senate, with the whole People of the Romans".
Plain words: the emperor Antoninus Pius, his adopted sons (Verissimus is Marcus Aurelius, per McGiffert, EUS 22615–22620, editor), the Senate and the Roman people.

**S3. Rough date of the First Apology, from his own words.** Own words, First Apology 46. Documented that he writes "a hundred and fifty years" after Christ's birth under Quirinius; the year c. 150–155 is Widely Accepted.
R1 1227–1228: "εἴπωσι πρὸ ἐτῶν ἑκατὸν πεκτήκοντα γεγεννῆσθαι τὸν Χριστὸν λέγειν ἡμᾶς ἐπὶ Κυρηνίου"
The corpus-map row gives "Rome, c. 155" (`cic/corpus-map/greek-apologists-second-century.yaml` line 354, a bucket note, not a source).

**S4. His people: Samaritans.** Own words, Dialogue 120. Documented that he calls them "my people"; his own ancestry is Contested (see D5).
AR 5691 (Dialogue 120.6): "Οὐδὲ γὰρ ἀπὸ τοῦ γένους τοῦ ἐμοῦ, λέγω δὲ τῶν Σαμαρέων, τινὸς"
ANF1 24249–24252: "For I gave no thought to any of my people, that is, the Samaritans, when I had a communication in writing with Cæsar, but stated that they were wrong in trusting to the magician Simon of their own nation".

**S5. The danger: the name alone was the charge.** Own words, Second Apology 2 (the case under Urbicus, prefect at Rome). Documented.
R2 136–145: Ptolemaeus, the woman's teacher, is asked "εἰ, αὐτὸ τοῦτο μόνον, Χριστιανός ἐστι" (138–139) and is jailed; before Urbicus "ὁμοίως αὐτὸ τοῦτο μόνον ἐξητάσθη, εἰ εἴη Χριστιανός" (143).
R2 148–153: "Λούκιός τις, καὶ αὐτὸς ὢν Χριστιανός, ὁρῶν τὴν ἀλόγως οὕτω γενομένην κρίσιν, πρὸς τὸν Οὔρβικον ἔφη· Τίς ἡ αἰτία, τοῦ μήτε μοιχὸν μήτε πόρνον μήτε ἀνδροφόνον μήτε λωποδύτην μήτε ἅρπαγα μήτε ἁπλῶς ἀδίκημά τι πράξαντα ἐλεγχόμενον, ὀνόματος δὲ Χριστιανοῦ προσωνυμίαν ὁμολογοῦντα τὸν ἄνθρωπον τοῦτον ἐκολάσω;"
R2 155–158: Lucius too is led away; "Καὶ ἄλλος δὲ τρίτος ἐπελθὼν κολασθῆναι προσετιμήθη."
ANF1 16810–16836 (English of the same).

**S6. The charge of "atheism".** Own words. Documented. Christians were called godless (ἄθεοι) for not worshipping the gods; R1 186 opens chapter 6: "Ἔνθεν δὲ καὶ ἄθεοι κεκλήμεθα· καὶ ὁμολογοῦμεν τῶν …" (ANF1 chapter 6). Used in Q2 below.

**S7. Where he taught in Rome.** Trial record (later, unknown hand; see E11 and D7). That he lodged and taught at Rome: Widely Accepted. The names in the address are uncertain: the Greek reads "above one Martinus, of the bath of Timothinus"; the baths are not said to be Martinus's. Otto (editor), `justin-martyr_opera-addubitata-grc-lat_otto1879.txt` 25472–25489: "Verba τινὸς Μαρτίνου sunt offensioni"; he judges them a gloss written "in marg. codicis vel supra lineam" and reads "Ego deversor in caenaculo (sive in superiore contignatione) balnei Timothini" (I lodge in the upper storey of the bath of Timothinus).
GEB 1347–1354: "Ἐγὼ ἐπάνω μένω τινὸς Μαρτίνου τοῦ Τιμοϑίνου βαλανείου καὶ παρὰ πάντα τὸν χρόνον τοῦτον (ἐπεδήμησα δὲ τῇ Ῥωμαίων πόλεν τοῦτο δεύτερον) [καὶ] οὐ γινώσχων ἄλλην τινὰ συνέλευσιν εἰ μὴ τὴν ἐχείνου. καὶ εἴ τις ἐβούλετο ἀφικνεῖσθαν παρ᾽ ἐμοί, ἐκοινώνουν αὐτῷ τῶν τῆς ἀληϑείας λόγων." (OCR; GEB apparatus at line 1364 notes the manuscripts read "τιμιοτίνου".)
ANF1 29309–29312: "I live above one Martinus, at the Timiotinian Bath; and during the whole time (and I am now living in Rome for the second time) I am unaware of any other meeting than his. And if any one wished to come to me, I communicated to him the doctrines of truth."

**S8. He wore the philosopher's cloak.** Own words (Dialogue 1) and later (Eusebius, Jerome). Documented.
AR 72–74: "Περιπατοῦντί μοι ἕωθεν ἐν τοῖς τοῦ ξυστοῦ περιπάτοις συναντήσας τις μετὰ καὶ ἄλλων· Φιλόσοφε, χαῖρε, ἔφη." AR 77–81: Trypho says he greets anyone "ὲν τοιούτῳ σχήματι" (in such a dress).
EUS 22442 and 22455–22456 (Eusebius, Church History IV.11.8, c. 150 years later): "In the guise of a philosopher … he preached the divine word, and contended for the faith in his writings." McGiffert's note (EUS 22443–22446, editor): "ἐν σχήματι φιλοσόφου. The reference here is to the distinctive cloak or mantle of the Greek philosophers".
JER 39972 (Jerome, c. 225 years later): "a philosopher, and wearing the garb of philosopher".

## 2. Events, in order

**E1. The search through the schools.** Own words, Dialogue 2. Documented (as his own account; whether shaped as a literary set piece is Contested in modern scholarship, not argued in held files).
AR 139–145: "ἐπέδωκα ἐμαυτὸν Στωϊκῷ τινι· καὶ διατρίψας ἱκανὸν μετ᾿ αὐτοῦ χρόνον, ἐπεὶ οὐδὲν πλέον ἐγίνετό μοι περὶ θεοῦ (οὐδὲ γὰρ αὐτὸς ἠπίστατο, οὐδὲ ἀναγκαίαν ἔλεγε ταύτην εἶναι τὴν μάθησιν), τούτου μὲν ἀπηλλάγην, ἐπ᾿ ἄλλον δὲ ἦκα, Περιπατητικὸν καλούμενον … ἠξίου με ἔπειτα μισθὸν ὁρίσαι … Καὶ αὐτὸν ἐγὼ διὰ ταύτην τὴν αἰτίαν κατέλιπον, μηδὲ φιλόσοφον οἰηθεὶς ὅλως."
AR 149–158: the Pythagorean asks "Ὡμίλησας … μουσικῆ καὶ ἀστρονομίᾳ καὶ γεωμετρίᾳ;" and sends him away.
AR 163–169: with a Platonist "Καί με ᾕρει σφόδρα ἡ τῶν ἀσωμάτων νόησις … ὀλίγου τε ἐντὸς χρόνου ᾤμην σοφὸς γεγονέναι, καὶ ὑπὸ βλακείας ἤλπιζον αὐτίκα κατόψεσθαι τὸν θεόν· τοῦτο γὰρ τέλος τῆς Πλάτωνος φιλοσοφίας."
Plain paraphrase: Stoic (taught nothing about God), Peripatetic (wanted a fee first), Pythagorean (demanded music, astronomy and geometry first), Platonist (he thought he would soon see God, "in my stupidity").

**E2. The old man by the sea.** Own words, Dialogue 3 and 7. Documented as his account.
AR 175–180: "ἐπορευόμην εἴς τι χωρίον οὐ μακρὰν θαλάσσης. … παλαιός τις πρεσβύτης, ἰδέσθαι οὐκ εὐκαταφρόνητος, πρᾷον καὶ σεμνὸν ἦθος ἐμφαίνων, ὀλίγον ἀποδέων μου παρείπετο."
ANF1 17468–17472 (English).
AR 399–403 (the old man points him to the prophets): "Ἐγένοντό τινες πρὸ πολλοῦ χρόνου πάντων τούτων τῶν νομιζομένων φιλοσόφων παλαιότεροι, μακάριοι καὶ δίκαιοι καὶ θεοφιλεῖς, θείῳ πνεύματι λαλήσαντες … προφήτας δὲ αὐτοὺς καλοῦσιν."
AR 419–420: "Εὔχου δέ σοι πρὸ πάντων φωτὸς ἀνοιχθῆναι πύλας".
The old man is not named anywhere in the record.

**E3. The turn.** Own words, Dialogue 8.1–2. Documented. (Quote Q1.)
AR 428–435: "Ταῦτα καὶ ἔτι ἄλλα πολλὰ εἰπὼν ἐκεῖνος, ἃ νυν καιρὸς οὐκ ἔστι λέγειν, ᾤχετο, κελεύσας διώκειν αὐτά· καὶ οὐκέτι αὐτὸν εἶδον. Ἐμοὶ δὲ παραχρῆμα πῦρ ἐν τῇ ψυχῇ ἀνήφθη, καὶ ἔρως εἶχέ με τῶν προφητῶν καὶ τῶν ἀνδρῶν ἐκείνων, οἵ εἰσι Χριστοῦ φίλοι· διαλογιζόμενός τε πρὸς ἐμαυτὸν τοὺς λόγους αὐτοῦ ταύτην μόνην εὕρισκον φιλοσοφίαν ἀσφαλῆ τε καὶ σύμφορον. Οὕτως δὴ καὶ διὰ ταῦτα φιλόσοφος ἐγώ."
ANF1 17851–17857 (English cross-check).

**E4. What else moved him: Christians unafraid of death.** Own words, Second Apology 12. Documented.
R2 368–370: "Καὶ γὰρ αὐτὸς ἐγώ, τοῖς Πλάτωνος· χαίρων διδάγμασι, διαβαλλομένους ἀκούων Χριστιανούς, ὁρῶν δὲ ἀφόβους πρὸς θάνατον καὶ πάντα τὰ ἄλλα νομιζόμενα φοβερά, ἐνενόουν ἀδύνατον εἶναι ἐν κακίᾳ καὶ φιληδονίᾳ ὑπάρχειν αὐτούς."
ANF1 17172–17176 (English).

**E5. The debate with Trypho.** Own words, Dialogue 1, 8, 16, 142. Documented as his account; the place is Eusebius's (see D4).
AR 87–89: "Τρύφων, φησί, καλοῦμαι· εἰμὶ δὲ Ἑβραῖος ἐκ περιτομῆς. φυγὼν τὸν νῦν γενόμενον πόλεμον, ἐν τῇ Ἑλλάδι καὶ τῇ Κορίνθῳ τὰ πολλὰ διάγων."
AR 443–449: Trypho's companions laugh; Trypho says it would have been better to keep to Plato's philosophy "ἢ λόγοις ἐξαπατηθῆναι ψευδέσι καὶ ἀνθρώποις ἀκολουθῆσαι οὐδενὸς ἀξίοις."
AR 788–791 (Dialogue 16.2), the hard edge of Justin's argument: "Ἡ γὰρ ἀπὸ Ἀβραὰμ κατὰ σάρκα περιτομὴ εἰς σημεῖον ἐδόθη, ἵνα ἦτε ἀπὸ τῶν ἄλλων ἐθνῶν καὶ ἡμῶν ἀφωρισμένοι, καὶ ἵνα μόνοι πάθητε ἃ νῦν ἐν δίκῃ πάσχετε, καὶ ἵνα γένωνται αἱ χῶραι ὑμῶν ἔρημοι … καὶ μηδεὶς ἐξ ὑμῶν ἐπιβαίνῃ εἰς τὴν Ἱερουσαλήμ."
AR 801–806 (16.4): "Ἀπεκτείνατε γὰρ τὸν δίκαιον … καταρώμενοι ἐν ταῖς συναγωγαῖς ὑμῶν τοὺς πιστεύοντας ἐπὶ τὸν Χριστόν."
AR 6589–6594 (142.1), Trypho at the parting: "Καὶ ὅτι ἐξαιρέτως ἥσθην τῇ συνουσίᾳ, ὁμολογῶ … πλέον γὰρ εὕρομεν ἢ προσεδοκῶμεν … μὴ ὄκνει ὡς φίλων ἡμῶν μεμνῆσθαι ἐὰν ἀπαλλαγῇς."
AR 6603–6607 (142.3): "Μεθ᾿ ἃ ἀπῄεσαν λοιπὸν εὐχόμενοί τέ μοι σωτηρίαν καὶ ἀπὸ τοῦ πλοῦ καὶ ἀπὸ πάσης κακίας· ἐγώ τε ὑπὲρ αὐτῶν εὐχόμενος ἔφην …"
Plain: Trypho has fled "the war now just ended" (Bar Kokhba's revolt is the usual reading; not named in the text). The two part as friends, praying for each other, neither converted.

**E6. The petition to the emperor.** Own words, First Apology 2 and 68. Documented.
R1 78–79, 85–87: "Τοὺς κατὰ ἀλήθειαν εὐσεβεῖς καὶ φιλοσόφους μόνον τἀληθὲς τιμᾶν καὶ στέργειν ὁ λόγος ὑπαγορεύει … Ὑμεῖς μὲν οὖν ὅτι λέγεσθε εὐσεβεῖς καὶ φιλόσοφοι καὶ φύλακες δικαιοσύνης καὶ ἐρασταὶ παιδείας, ἀκούετε πανταχοῦ· εἰ δὲ καὶ ὑπάρχετε, δειχθήσεται."
R1 93–96: "Ἡμεῖς μὲν γὰρ πρὸς οὐδενὸς πείσεσθαί τι κακὸν δύνασθαι λελογίσμεθα, ἢν μὴ κακίας ἐργάται ἐλεγχώμεθα ἢ πονηροὶ διεγνώσμεθα· ὑμεῖς δ᾿ ἀποκτεῖναι μὲν δύνασθε, βλάψαι δ᾿ οὔ."
ANF1 13992–13994: "and you, you can kill, but not hurt us."
R1 1790–1791 (68): "καὶ μὴ ὡς κατ᾿ ἐχθρῶν κατὰ τῶν μηδὲν ἀδικούντων θάνατον ὁρίζετε."
No held source records any answer from the emperor.

**E7. The Word shared by every race.** Own words, First Apology 46; Second Apology 10, 13. Documented. (Quote Q2.)
R1 1230–1235: "Τὸν Χριστὸν πρωτότοκον τοῦ θεοῦ εἶναι ἐδιδάχθημεν καὶ προεμηνύσαμεν λόγον ὄντα, οἷ πᾶν γένος ἀνθρώπων μετέσχε. Καὶ οἱ μετὰ λόγου βιώσαντες Χριστιανοί εἰσι, κἂν ἄθεοι ἐνομίσθησαν, οἷον ἐν Ἕλλησι μὲν Σωκράτης καὶ Ἡράκλειτος καὶ οἱ ὅμοιοι αὐτοῖς, ἐν βαρβάροις δὲ Αβραὰμ καὶ Ἀνανίας καὶ Ἀζαρίας καὶ Μισαὴλ καὶ Ἡλίας καὶ ἄλλοι πολλοί" ("οἷ" is the file's typo for οὗ.)
ANF1 15685–15696 (English, from "first-born of God" at 15685; from "are Christians, even though they have been thought atheists").
R2 332–337 (Second Apology 10): "Σωκράτει μὲν γὰρ οὐδεὶς ἐπείσθη ὑπὲρ τούτου τοῦ δόγματος ἀποθνήσκειν· Χριστῷ δέ … οὐ φιλόσοφοι οὐδὲ φιλόλογοι μόνον ἐπείσθησαν, ἀλλὰ καὶ χειροτέχναι καὶ παντελῶς ἰδιῶται, καὶ δόξης καὶ φόβου καὶ θανάτου καταφρονήσαντες"
R2 405–408 (Second Apology 13): "Ἕκαστος γάρ τις ἀπὸ μέρους τοῦ σπερματικοῦ θείου λόγου τὸ συγγενὲς ὁρῶν καλῶς ἐφθέγξατο … Ὅσα οὖν παρὰ καλῶς εἴρηται, ἡμῶν τᾶν Χριστιανῶν ἐστι". The file drops a word here (standard text "παρὰ πᾶσι"); ANF1 17235–17236 reads "Whatever things were rightly said among all men, are the property of us Christians." Not used as a quote because the held Greek is defective at this point.

**E8. The Sunday gathering.** Own words, First Apology 67. Documented.
R1 1759–1767: "Καὶ τῇ τοῦ ἡλίου λεγομένῃ ἡμέρᾳ πάντων κατὰ πόλεις ἢ ἀγροὺς μενόντων ἐπὶ τὸ αὐτὸ συνέλευσις γίνεται, καὶ τὰ ἀπομνημονεύματα τῶν ἀποστόλων ἢ τὰ συγγράμματα τῶν προφητῶν ἀναγινώσκεται, μέχρις ἐγχωρεῖ. Εἶτα παυσαμένου τοῦ ἀναγινώσκοντος ὁ προεστὼς διὰ λόγου τὴν νουθεσίαν καὶ πρόκλησιν τῆς τῶν καλῶν τούτων μιμήσεως ποιεῖται."
R1 1767–1778: bread, wine and water; the president's prayers "ὅση δύναμις αὐτῷ"; the people's "Ἀμήν"; portions sent by deacons to the absent; gifts kept by the president for "ὀρφανοῖς τε καὶ χήραις … καὶ τοῖς ἐν δεσμοῖς οὖσι, καὶ τοῖς παρεπιδήμοις οὖσι ξένοις".
ANF1 16502–16528 (English).

**E9. Against Marcion and the heresies.** Own words, First Apology 26. Documented.
R1 729–731: "Μαρκίωνα δέ τινα Ποντικόν, ὃς καὶ νῦν ἔτι ἐστὶ διδάσκων τοὺς πειθομένους, ἄλλον τινὰ νομίζειν μείζονα τοῦ δημιουργοῦ θεόν"
R1 743–745: "Ἔστι δὲ ἡμῖν καὶ σύνταγμα κατὰ πασῶν τῶν γεγενημένων αἱρέσεων συντεταγμένον, (??) εἰ βούλεσθε ἐντυχεῖν, δώσομεν."
ANF1 14961 (English). The Syntagma is lost.

**E10. He expected to be killed; Crescens.** Own words, Second Apology (R2 ch. 8 = ANF ch. 3). Documented that he expected it.
R2 273–276: "Κἀγὼ οὖν προσδοκῶ ὑπό τινος τῶν ὠνομασμένων ἐπιβουλευθῆναι καὶ ξύλῳ ἐμπαγῆναι, ἢ κἂν ὑπὸ Κρίσκεντος τοῦ φιλοψόφου καὶ φιλοκόμπου. Οὐ γὰρ φιλόσοφον εἰπεῖν ἄξιον τὸν ἄνδρα, ὅς γε περὶ ἡμῶν ἃ μὴ ἐπίσταται δημοσίᾳ καταμαρτυρεῖ"
R2 283–285: he says he questioned Crescens and proved "ὅτι ἀληθῶς μηδὲν ἐπίσταται".
R2 292–293: "Ἀδύνατον δὲ Κυνικῷ. ἀδιάφορον τὸ τέλος προθεμένῳ, τὸ ἀγαθὸν εἰδέναι πλὴν ἀδιαφορίας."
ANF1 16849–16851: "I too, therefore, expect to be plotted against and fixed to the stake, by some of those I have named, or perhaps by Crescens, that lover of bravado and boasting".

**E11. The trial before Rusticus.** Trial record (Acts of Justin): later, unknown hand, set out as question and answer. GEB is OCR: Greek below is copied as it stands (line breaks joined, hyphens kept as in the file); the English twin is ANF1 (tr. Dods).
GEB 1300–1303 (I.1): "Ἐν τῷ καιρῷ τῶν ἀνόμων ὑπερμάχων τῆς εἰδωλο- λατρδίας προστάγματα ἀσεβῆ κατὰ τῶν εὐσεβούντων Χριστιανῶν χατὰ πόλιν χαὶ χώραν ἐξετίϑετο, ὥστε αὐτοὺς ἀναγκάζεσθαι σπένδειν τοῖς ματαίοις εἰδώλοις." ANF1 29266–29269: "wicked decrees were passed against the godly Christians in town and country, to force them to offer libations to vain idols".
GEB 1309–1311 (II.1–2): "Πρῶτον πείσϑητι τοῖς ϑεοῖς καὶ ὑπάκουσον τοῖς βασιλεῦσιν. 2. Ἰουστῖνος einer "Ausurerov xoi ἀκατάγνωστον τὸ πείϑεσθαι τοῖς προσταχ-" ANF1 29271–29274: "Obey the gods at once, and submit to the kings." … "To obey the commandments of our Saviour Jesus Christ is worthy neither of blame nor of condemnation."
GEB 1315–1317 (II.3): "Ἰουστῖνος eine‘ Πάντας μὲν λόγους ἐπειράϑην μαϑεῖν, συνεϑέμην δὲ τοῖς ἀληϑέσι λόγοις τοῖς τῶν Χριστιανῶν, x&v μ᾽ ἀρέσχωσι τοῖς ψευδοδόξοις." ANF1 29276–29278: "I have endeavoured to learn all doctrines; but I have acquiesced at last in the true doctrines, those namely of the Christians, even though they do not please those who hold false opinions."

## 3. Impact, heaviest first (what later witnesses carried on and used)

**I1. His books were kept, quoted and listed for seven centuries.** Later. Documented.
- Irenaeus (c. 180, ~15 years later), ANF1 44907–44914 (Against Heresies IV.6.2): "In his book against Marcion, Justin does well say: 'I would not have believed the Lord Himself, if He had announced any other than He who is our framer, maker, and nourisher.'" *(Defended: the one God who made the world, against Marcion.)*
- Irenaeus, ANF1 53355–53356 (Against Heresies V.26.2): "Truly has Justin remarked: That before the Lord's appearance Satan never dared to blaspheme God".
- Tertullian (early 3rd c., ~40 years), ANF3 43161–43163 (Against the Valentinians 5), naming those who wrote against the heresy-founders in their own day: "for instance Justin, philosopher and martyr; Miltiades … Irenæus". *(Defended: the church's teaching against the heresies.)*
- Eusebius (early 4th c., ~150 years), EUS 24209–24212 (Church History IV.18.1): "This writer has left us a great many monuments of a mind educated and practiced in divine things, which are replete with profitable matter of every kind." Eusebius copies the First Apology's address (IV.12) and the Crescens passage (IV.16.3–6, EUS 23815–23873).
- Eusebius, EUS 24309–24315 (IV.18.6): the Dialogue "which he held in the city of Ephesus with Trypho … In it he shows how the divine grace urged him on to the doctrine of the faith".
- Jerome (392, ~225 years), JER 39971–39997 (Lives of Illustrious Men 23): lists the works; "laboured strenuously in behalf of the religion of Christ … and did not shun the ignominy of the cross".
- Photius (9th c., ~700 years), PHO 11699–11718 (Library, codex 125): "He is thoroughly versed in our own and especially in heathen philosophy, overflowing with learning of all kinds and a wealth of historical knowledge; but he has not endeavoured to colour the natural beauty of his philosophy by rhetorical arts."

**I2. His death was kept as a record and became his name.** Later record (the trial record: author and date unknown; one text, Otto's, from a single manuscript) plus later witnesses. Documented that the record was kept; "Martyr" as name is the editor's observation.
- The Acts themselves (GEB 1285–1441; ANF1 29253–29408).
- Tertullian's "philosopher and martyr" (ANF3 43162–43163).
- Eusebius IV.16.1, EUS 23756–23799: "Justin … was crowned with divine martyrdom, in consequence of a plot laid against him by Crescens … After Justin had frequently refuted him in public discussions he won by his martyrdom the prize of victory, dying in behalf of the truth which he preached."
- McGiffert (editor), EUS 23764–23766: "That Justin died a martyr's death is the universal tradition of antiquity, which is crystallized in his name. Irenæus (Adv. Hær. I. 28. 1) is the first to mention it".

**I3. His hearers.** Contemporary and later. Documented.
- Tatian (his hearer according to Irenaeus and Hippolytus, both later; Tatian himself does not say he heard Justin, or where; writing perhaps 10–20 years after, rough), TAT 7956–7960 (Address to the Greeks 18; OCR; the robbers-and-ransom comparison continues to 7960, ANF2 6526–6533): "καὶ ὅ ϑαυμασιώτατος Ἰουστῖνος ὀρθῶς ἐξεφιρόνησεν ἐοικέναι τοὺς προειρημένους λῃσταῖς." ANF2 6526–6531: "And the most admirable Justin has rightly denounced them as robbers."
- Tatian, TAT 8105–8111 (Address 19; OCR): "Κρίσκης οὖν, 6 ἐννεοττεύσας δτῇ μεγάλῃ πόλει, © … ὡς καὶ Ἰουστῖνον, καθάπερ καὶ ἐμέ, "ὡς κακῷ τῷ ϑανάτῳ περιβαλεῖν πραγματεύσασθαι, διότε κηρύττων τὴν ἀλήϑειαν Ἰολίχνους καὶ ἀπατεῶνας τοὺς φιλοσόφους συνήλεγχεν." ANF2 6550–6556.
- Irenaeus, ANF1 33663–33667 (Against Heresies I.28.1): "A certain man named Tatian first introduced the blasphemy. He was a hearer of Justin's, and as long as he continued with him he expressed no such views; but after his martyrdom he separated from the Church, and, excited and puffed up by the thought of being a teacher, as if he were superior to others, he composed his own peculiar type of doctrine." (Hostile witness to Tatian.)
- Hippolytus (early 3rd c.), ANF5 13005–13006: Tatian, "although being himself a disciple of Justinus the Martyr, did not entertain similar opinions with his master."
- Euelpistus at the trial: "I willingly heard the words of Justin" (GEB 1386–1387; ANF1 29343–29344).

**I4. His idea of the Word shared by every race.** Own words (E7); later witnesses in held files do not carry it forward by name in what was checked. Documented as his teaching; its later reception is not traced in this sheet (gap).

**I5. His account of Sunday worship.** Own words (E8). Later use found only in a modern editor: the 1843 English Roman Missal's notes cite Justin for readings and for the Eucharist (`roman-missal_england1843.txt` 1545–1546, 3253–3255). The modern use is the editor's, about 1,700 years later.

## 5. Where sources disagree or come late

**D1. Did Crescens bring about his death?** Contested.
- Justin only expected a plot "by some of those I have named, or perhaps by Crescens" (R2 273–275).
- Tatian says Crescens worked to bring death on Justin "and on me too" (TAT 8109); Tatian survived.
- Eusebius says Justin died "in consequence of a plot laid against him by Crescens" (EUS 23782), and Jerome follows: "through the efforts and wiles of Crescens, he shed his blood for Christ" (JER 39993–39997).
- The Acts do not name Crescens. McGiffert (editor), EUS 23782–23795: "we have his portrait only from the pen of his bitterest enemies. In the Acta Crescens is not mentioned in connection with the death of Justin". McGiffert also reports (EUS 23880–23910) that Eusebius's copy of Tatian's sentence lacks "and indeed on me", and that Zahn, Harnack and others accused Eusebius of altering it; McGiffert doubts deliberate deceit.

**D2. Which emperor, which year?** Contested on the reign; c. 165 is Dominant Modern Reconstruction.
- McGiffert (editor), EUS 23775–23780: "There are two lines of tradition, one of which puts his death under Antoninus Pius, the other under Marcus Aurelius. The latter has the most in its favor".
- Epiphanius (late 4th c., ~210 years), EPI 15933–15935: "ἐπὶ Ῥουστιχοῦ ἡγεμόνος καὶ Ἀδριανοῦ βασιλέως, ἐτῶν τριάχοντα ὑπάρχων ἐν χαϑεστώσῃ ἡλικίᾳ" (under Rusticus and the emperor Hadrian, aged thirty). Holl (editor), EPI 15961–15962: "ἐπὶ Ἀδριανοῦ βασιλέως und τριάχοντα ἐτῶν ὑπάρχων ist beides unmöglich". Late legend on these two points.

**D3. How he died.** Documented (beheading) against late legend (hemlock).
- The Acts: scourged and beheaded (GEB 1433–1438).
- Dods (translator), ANF1 29216–29220: "Though nothing is known as to the date or authorship of the following narrative, it is generally reckoned among the most trustworthy of the Martyria. An absurd addition was in some copies made to it, to the effect that Justin died by means of hemlock."
- ANF editor, ANF1 29211–29213: the hemlock story "seems to have originated among the Greeks, who naturally gave this turn to the sufferings of a philosopher."

**D4. Where the Dialogue took place.** Justin names only "the colonnades of the Xystus" (AR 72). Eusebius places it "in the city of Ephesus" (EUS 24311). Later, ~150 years; Widely Accepted but not Justin's own statement.

**D5. His ancestry.** Contested.
- He calls the Samaritans "my people" (AR 5691; ANF1 24249).
- Epiphanius: "Ἰουστῖνος Σαμαρείτης ἢν τὸ γένος" (EPI 15927).
- McGiffert (editor): "He was of heathen parentage, and received a thoroughly Greek education" (EUS 22422–22423). The NPNF Jerome editor (npnf206 16595, note): "Born in Samaria of Greek parents". His father's and grandfather's names (Priscus, Bacchius) are Greek and Latin (R1 73), which is all the text itself shows.

**D6. One apology or two.** McGiffert (editor), EUS 22600–22601: "he really wrote only one Apology"; Eusebius and Jerome speak of a second addressed to Marcus Aurelius and Lucius Verus (EUS 23756–23762; JER 39977–39979). The held Greek prints two (R1, R2). Contested.

**D7. Date and nature of the Acts.** Unknown date and author (Dods, ANF1 29216–29218). McGiffert (editor), EUS 23767–23771: "although belonging to a later age (probably the third century), yet bear every evidence of containing a comparatively truthful account of Justin's death." The corpus-map/census note that the Acts survive in three recensions rests on Musurillo (1972), which is not held. Gebhardt prints one text, from Otto (GEB 209). Otto's prolegomena (`justin-martyr_opera-addubitata-grc-lat_otto1879.txt` 1836–1852) say the text came from a Grottaferrata manuscript of the Lives of the Saints of Symeon Metaphrastes ("saec. X"), a collection "partim collectas et sine ulla muta- tione.adoptatas … partim conseriptas vel interpolatas" (OCR as it stands, 1848–1852: partly gathered unchanged, partly composed or interpolated); first printed in Greek by Papebroch in the Acta Sanctorum, June vol. 1, 1695 (1868–1875); a Vatican copy, Vat. 655 (1905). Otto judges part of the title "aetatis est posterioris" (1945–1950). No beat can be placed in a particular recension.

**D8. Works under his name that are not his.** The Hortatory Address, the Discourse to the Greeks, On the Sole Government of God, and the Letter to Diognetus are held under his name but treated as not his: `cic/corpus-map/greek-apologists-second-century.yaml` 262–347 (bucket notes, citing Harnack and Otto); `justin-martyr_opera-addubitata-grc-lat_otto1879.txt`; `pseudo-justin_epistle-to-diognetus-grc_lake1917.txt`. Not used.

## 6. Added for the revised outline (the heart of his life)

**H1. Judge the deed, not the name.** Own words, First Apology 4. Documented.
R1 124–126: "Ὀνόματος μὲν οὖν προσωνυμίᾳ οὔτε ἀγαθὸν οὔτε κακὸν κρίνεται ἄνευ τῶν ὑποπιπτουσῶν τᾷ ὀνόματι πράξεων· ἐπεί, ὅσον γε ἐκ τοῦ κατηγορουμένου ἡμῶν ὀνόματος, χρηστότατοι ὑπάρχομεν."
ANF1 14037–14040: "By the mere application of a name, nothing is decided, either good or evil, apart from the actions implied in the name; and indeed, so far at least as one may judge from the name we are accused of, we are most excellent people." (The ANF note there, from 14040, explains the play on Christos / chrēstos, "good".)

**H2. Why he set out what Christians do.** Own words, First Apology 61. Documented.
R1 1577–1579: "Ὃν τρόπον δὲ καὶ ἀνεθήκαμεν ἑαυτοὺς τῷ θεῷ καινοποιηθέντες διὰ τοῦ Χριστοῦ, ἐξηγησόμεθα, ὅπως μὴ τοῦτο παραλιπόντες δόξωμεν πονηρεύειν τι ἐν τῇ ἐξηγήσει."
ANF1 16229–16232: "I will also relate the manner in which we dedicated ourselves to God when we had been made new through Christ; lest, if we omit this, we seem to be unfair in the explanation we are making."
Plain: he describes baptism and the Sunday gathering (chapters 61–67) so that he will not seem to be hiding something or dealing unfairly in his account.

**H3. Why Sunday.** Own words, First Apology 67. Documented.
R1 1779–1785: "Τὴν δὲ τοῦ ἡλίου ἡμέρα κοινῇ πάντες τὴν συνέλευσιν ποιούμεθα, ἐπειδὴ πρώτη ἐστὶν ἡμέρα, ἐν ᾗ ὁ θεὸς τὸ σκότος καὶ τὴν ὕλην τρέψας κόσμον ἐποίησε, καὶ Ἰησοῦς Χριστὸς ὁ ἡμέτερος σωτὴρ τῇ αὐτῇ ἡμέρᾳ ἐκ νεκρῶν ἀνέστη"
ANF1 16527–16530: "But Sunday is the day on which we all hold our common assembly, because it is the first day on which God, having wrought a change in the darkness and matter, made the world; and Jesus Christ our Saviour on the same day rose from the dead."
At the close of the chapter (R1 1785; ANF1 16532–16534) he says these things are set before the emperors "for your consideration" too.

**H4. Who Tatian became.** For the Connection. Witnesses as marked.
- Tatian's own words, Address to the Greeks 42 (contemporary). Documented. TAT 14227–14230 (OCR): "γεννηθεὶς μὲν ἐν τῇ τῶν ᾿46- / συρίων γῇ, παιδευϑεὶς δὲ πρῶτον μὲν Ξτὰ ὑμέτερα" (OCR for Ἀσσυρίων … τὰ ὑμέτερα). ANF2 7460–7464: "I was born in the land of the Assyrians, having been first instructed in your doctrines, and afterwards in those which I now undertake to proclaim."
- Eusebius, Church History IV.29.6 (~150 years after Justin). Widely Accepted that Tatian made it. EUS 26424–26426, 26433: "But their original founder, Tatian, formed a certain combination and collection of the Gospels, I know not how, to which he gave the title Diatessaron". McGiffert (editor), EUS 26433–26435: "Eusebius is the first one to mention this Diatessaron, and he had evidently not seen it himself."
- The Doctrine of Addai, a Syriac account of the church at Edessa (later; its date is the editor's: McGiffert, EUS 26443–26444, "which belongs to the third century"). Documented that the text says so; Inferential-Thin as a report of Addai's own day. `addai_doctrine-of-addai.txt` 65: "a large multitude of people assembled day by day and came to the prayer of the service, and to the reading of the Old and New Testament, of the Diatessaron". Phillips's note (editor), same file 219–221: "The Diatessaron was that made by Tatian … It was a volume compiled from the Four Gospels, and seems to have been publicly read at Edessa up to the fourth century."
- McGiffert (editor), EUS 26438–26442, reporting Theodoret (Theodoret's own *Haereticarum fabularum* is not held): Theodoret "found a great many copies of it in circulation in his diocese, and that, finding that it omitted the account of our Lord's birth, he replaced it by the four Gospels". Editor's report; card material, not for the audio.
- Irenaeus, Against Heresies I.28.1 (~15 years after; hostile to Tatian). ANF1 33655–33672 (fuller than I3): the Encratites "preached against marriage"; "A certain man named Tatian first introduced the blasphemy … after his martyrdom he separated from the Church … he declared that marriage was nothing else than corruption and fornication. But his denial of Adam's salvation was an opinion due entirely to himself."
- Census entry `tatian` (`cic-website/data/world-census.json`), told within `syriac-edessa-nisibis`: "A hearer of Justin Martyr who attacked Greek learning in his Address to the Greeks and wove the four Gospels into one, the Diatessaron." (Census, not a source.)

**H6. The Second Apology's address.** Own words. Documented. R2 97 (chapter 1): "Καὶ τὰ χθὲς δὲ καὶ πρώην ἐν τῇ πόλει ὑμῶν γενόμενα ἐπὶ Οὐρβίκου, ὦ Ῥωμαῖοι," ("And what happened yesterday and the day before in your city under Urbicus, O Romans"). It is not addressed to the emperor by name in the held text (see D6).

**H5. Eusebius's name for him.** Later, ~150 years. EUS 21864–21866 (Church History IV.8.3): "Justin, a genuine lover of the true philosophy, was still continuing to busy himself with Greek literature." Used on the card; not needed in the audio.
