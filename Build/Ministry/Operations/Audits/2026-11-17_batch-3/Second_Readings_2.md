## Chalcedon Q7: the rest of the sentence

The Mansi Latin breaks at "inconvertibiliter, et insepa-" (10063). Lines 10064-10065 are blank and 10066 begins the next scene, so the rest of the sentence is lost in this scan. The NPNF English of the same session gives it in full (`npnf214_seven-ecumenical-councils.xml` 20175-20178): "Add then to the definition, according to the judgment of our most holy father Leo, that there are two natures in Christ united unchangeably, inseparably, unconfusedly." So the Latin ends "insepa[rabiliter] et [inconfuse]" or close to it, and the three adverbs match the judges' summary of Leo a few lines before (10052-10054, "inconfuse, inconvertibiliter & indivisibiliter"; NPNF 20168-20170). Nothing lost changes the verdict: the missing words are the third adverb, outside the brief's quote.

## Words whose modern sense may mislead

- "Known" (Chalcedon Q8): the brief's "is known in two natures" may be heard as "is famous" or "is known about". γνωριζόμενον means "recognized" or "acknowledged". The meaning holds in place, since the Definition is a confession of faith. For the reviewer: "is acknowledged in two natures" is closer, at the same length.
- "Orthodox" (Chalcedon Q10): the History's own word for the Coptic faith, not Eastern Orthodoxy. The brief names the speaker ("The Coptic Church's History says"), so it does not mislead.
- "Catholic" (Toledo Q5, and the brief's prose): the church of the creeds in Spain, set against the Gothic church. The brief draws that line in its second paragraph, so it does not mislead.
- "Substance" (Toledo, the brief's prose before Q1, "one substance"): the creed's term for one being, not matter. It is outside the quoted span. No change needed in the quote.
- "Infection" (Chalcedon Q12): Severus's medical image for heresy. The brief keeps it as an image. No problem.
- "Human race" (Pachomius Q2): means humankind. No problem.
