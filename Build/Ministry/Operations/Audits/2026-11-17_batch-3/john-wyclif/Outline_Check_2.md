# Outline check, round 2: John Wyclif

## My account of the heart of his life (written before opening Outline.md)

From the census entry (`john-wyclif`), the source sheet and what he is known for:

John Wyclif was the leading Oxford theologian of fourteenth-century England (even the hostile Leicester chronicle calls him the most eminent doctor of his day, S5), later parish priest of Lutterworth. His work had three strands, held together by one conviction: scripture, God's law, is the test the church must answer to.

1. **Against the church's wealth and worldly power.** He measured the clergy against the poverty of Christ and the apostles, and told the pope himself to leave worldly lordship to the secular power (E17, Letter to Urban VI). This is what the 1377 papal bulls first struck at (E2).
2. **The bread of the Mass.** He denied transubstantiation: Christ's body is truly and really the sacramental bread, yet the bread remains bread (E3-E8). For this he was condemned at Oxford, silenced by Gaunt, and his conclusions condemned by the London "earthquake council" of 1382 (E9-E11).
3. **God's law in English.** He argued that Christ and the apostles taught in the people's own tongue (E13, B13), and an English Bible, the first to take in the whole of scripture, came out of his circle. How much was his own hand is unknown (E14-E16, B1-B3); a hostile chronicle charges him with it (Q2).

What it changed: followers (named Lollards in scorn) carried his teaching and his English scripture for more than a century, against Arundel's ban and the bishops' trials (I1-I3, B4-B8); in Bohemia Jan Hus built his own book on the Church from Wyclif's and would not call him a heretic (I5-I7); Constance condemned him as a heretic and ordered his bones dug up, and Martin V had them burned (I9-I11). He died in his own parish, never brought to trial in person (E18).

A complete outline must open on scripture as the test, and carry the Mass and the English Bible as major contributions, with Lollards and Hus as what came of him.

## Findings

### B1. BLOCKING: Tyndale is set down as something that came of Wyclif. No held source says so, and the outline's own Do not say forbids it.

The outline handles Tyndale's own words correctly. Its Do not say quotes his "had no man to counterfeit, neither was helped with English of any that had interpreted the same" (tyndale_doctrinal-treatises_walter1848.txt 25533-25536, checked: the epistle "To the Reader" at the end of the 1526 New Testament, heading 25477-25480). It also bars "Tyndale finished what Wyclif began". But the outline's structure says the opposite:
- The heart's *What it changed* ends: "In 1526 William Tyndale did the work again, from the Greek."
- In **What came of them**, item 4 is "**Tyndale.** A century and a half later, he took up the same work from the Greek."
- Beat 7 is titled "The verdict, Bohemia, and Tyndale". It ends the story on Tyndale making the New Testament "again".
- The Links label is "took up the same work".

"Took up" and a place in the ranked impact list both tell the writer that Tyndale's work grew out of Wyclif's. A listener will hear that Tyndale carried Wyclif's Bible forward. That is the exact claim the Do not say rules out.

The only held tie between the two men is memory. In the Prologue to Jonah (walter 29439-29443, checked), Tyndale names Wyclif as a preacher of repentance whom "our fathers" rejected. In context (29430-29450), Tyndale ranks him with Gildas, as a prophet whose hearers did not repent and were punished. He says nothing there of Wyclif's Bible. Tyndale's own stated motive is that lay people could not see the text: "This thing only moved me to translate the new Testament" (walter 670-671, quoting his Preface to the Pentateuch). Wyclif is not his motive. The causal "therefore" at walter 692-695 belongs to Walter, the 1848 editor, not to Tyndale.

**Correction.**
- Delete item 4 (Tyndale) from **What came of them**, and renumber.
- In the heart's *What it changed*, replace the last sentence with: "It stayed in handwritten copies until printed English Scripture came, beginning with Tyndale's New Testament of 1526, which Tyndale says he made with no earlier English to follow (walter 25533-25536)."
- Retitle beat 7 "The verdict, and Bohemia". Move its Tyndale sentence to the end of beat 6 as the end-point of the manuscript strand: "In 1526 William Tyndale printed a New Testament of his own, made afresh from the Greek, and wrote that he had no one's English to follow (walter 692-695, 25533-25536)."
- Change the Links entry to: "William Tyndale: named him, in his Prologue to Jonah, as a preacher of repentance whom England did not heed (walter 29439-29443). No tie of text."
- Add to **Do not say**: "'Tyndale took up / carried on Wyclif's work', or any ending that makes Tyndale the outcome of Wyclif's Bible → Tyndale made his own version and says he followed no one's English. The Jonah line is about Wyclif's preaching, not his Bible." Keep the existing Tyndale lines.

The Connection field may keep its Tyndale sentence. It is framed as a contrast and carries Tyndale's own denial.

### M1. MINOR: Tyndale's Greek is cited to the wrong text.
The heart's *What it changed* and beat 7 rest "from the Greek" on B11 (walter 30040-30043). That is the Prologue upon Matthew in his *revised* New Testament ("looked over again, now at the last ... compared it unto the Greek"). It is not the 1526 edition. The 1526 epistle does not mention Greek. **Correction:** cite walter 692-695 (Walter, editor: Tyndale knew tongues "out of Wiclif's reach" and made "a version of his own"), keep *Widely Accepted*, and note that B11 concerns the later revision.

### M2. MINOR: "Scripture as the test" is cited to a line that does not say it.
The heart line, beat 2 and *The rest of the heart* cite fasciculi 1635-1637. Shirley says only that Wyclif "dreamt of restoring, by apostolical poverty, its long-lost apostolical purity to the clergy". That supports "as poor as the apostles". It does not support Scripture as the test of the Mass. **Correction:** for wealth and power, add his own words from the letter to Urban VI. In it he counsels that the pope leave temporal lordship to the secular arm, "Sic enim Christus fecit ... per suos apostolos" (fasciculi 27415-27418). In the heart line, rephrase the Mass clause so it does not rest on Scripture: "... the test of the church's wealth and power; and he denied that the bread of the Mass stops being bread."

### M3. MINOR: a missing qualifier on Hus's words.
At palacky 15241-15249 (checked), Hus answered that it was true he had said these words *twelve years before*. At that time Wyclif's theological books were not yet in Bohemia, and Hus knew only his books on the arts and his good name. Beat 7 places the quote right after "who had built his own book on the Church from Wyclif's". A listener will hear the hope as resting on that theology. **Correction:** add to **Do not say**: "Hus's hope as a verdict on Wyclif's theology → at Constance he owned words he first said about twelve years earlier, when he knew only Wyclif's philosophy and his good name (palacky 15241-15245)." Add to Quote 3's job: "owned at Constance; first said c. 1403."

### M4. MINOR: "Defended by them" in What came of them, item 1.
The 1521 people were examined, and some were made to name others. The record does not show them defending a right. The defender of God's law in English is the tract. **Correction:** "*Defended by him:* God's law in the people's own tongue (Quote 1)".

### M5. MINOR: "circulated" drops the editors' inference.
In item 1 of What came of them, Forshall and Madden write that the versions "must have been widely circulated" (wycliffite-bible 71-72). That is an inference, not a count. **Correction:** "were, its 1850 editors judge, widely circulated ...".
