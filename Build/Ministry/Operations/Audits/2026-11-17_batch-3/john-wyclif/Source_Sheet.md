# Source sheet: John Wyclif

Subject: John Wyclif (census id `john-wyclif`, told within `lollardy`; census spoken names for the tradition: "Lollardy", "the Lollards"). All files are under `cic/texts/`. Line numbers are the file's own. Quotes are copied from the file as it stands, OCR errors included; where I read an OCR form as a letter, I say so.

**OCR note for the Middle English.** The batch pointer said `REGISTRY.yaml` records a thorn (þ) pattern for Arnold's and Matthew's editions. I checked: their entries (REGISTRY.yaml lines 2898-2960) carry no `apparatus` key, and no `kind: letterform` entry exists anywhere in the file (the only "thorn as `])` or `]?`" text is the schema comment at line 77). In Matthew's De Officio Pastorali, þ comes through as `]?`, `]7`, `\>`, `]>`, `J^`, `j^`, `f` or `p`, and yogh as `j` or `^`. Readings in [square brackets] below are mine.

Witness kinds: **own** (his own words), **contemporary**, **later** (with gap), **late legend**, **editor** (a modern editor's note or introduction, not a primary witness).

## 4. Links to other people

- **Jan Hus**: read his books, quoted his book (I5), defended his memory (I6, I7).
- **William Thorpe**: heard him teach, 'especially of Wyclif himself' (pollard 7238-7239); carried his teaching before Arundel (I1).
- **Thomas Arundel**: examined his follower; conceded "a great Clerk" (I2).
- **Nicholas Hereford**: follower; an early note gives him the first Old Testament translation (E14); recanted (Shirley 2703-2706).
- **John Purvey**: follower; F&M say he lived with Wyclif in his last years (wycliffite-bible 3435); the later revision's attribution to him is contested (E15).
- **John of Gaunt**: patron at St Paul's (E1); silenced him on the sacrament in 1381 (E7).
- **John Ball**: Knighton's "forerunner" charge (E20), contested.
- **Council of Constance; Martin V**: condemned him, ordered the bones dug up (I9, I10).
- **John Foxe**: told his story, the burning of the bones (I11).

## 5. Where sources disagree or come late

- **All narrative witnesses of his life are hostile** (Shirley, S6): the Carmelite collection, the Leicester chronicle, Walsingham. His own words survive in his Latin works; Thorpe is the one follower who describes him.
- **The Bible**: who translated what (E14-E16). Only Knighton (hostile) says Wyclif himself translated; the 1850 editors guess; Pollard denies the evidence.
- **The English tracts**: authorship rests on 19th-century editors (E13).
- **The stroke**: Shirley's Mass scene vs Walsingham's judgment and despair (E18, E19).
- **The synod of 1382**: whether he appeared (E9).
- **Bulls' date** (E2); **Lutterworth date** (S3); **birth year** (S7); **earthquake date** 17 vs 21 May (E10, E12).
- **The bones**: ordered dug up 1415, ordered burned 1427; the burning itself is held only in Foxe (I11). No source in the Library names who carried it out or the year it was done.
- **The 1381 rising**: Knighton's Ball charge vs Vaughan (E20).
