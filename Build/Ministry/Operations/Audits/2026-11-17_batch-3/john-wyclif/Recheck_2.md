## Findings

### BLOCKING

**B1. The pearl quote can be heard as Wyclif's own words.** "A hostile Leicester chronicler of his own day says Wyclif put the Gospel into English himself ... In his words, 'And so the Gospel's pearl is scattered and trampled by pigs.'" Heard once, "his" can attach to Wyclif, the nearest man named and the speaker of the story's previous quote. That puts the enemy's insult in Wyclif's mouth. The earlier draft had "In its words" (the chronicle).
*Correction:* "In the chronicler's words, "And so the Gospel's pearl is scattered and trampled by pigs.""

### MINOR

1. **The time order jumps back without a signal.** "Long after, in 1526, William Tyndale ..." is followed at once by "In 1415 the Council of Constance ...". A listener moves from 1526 back to 1415 with no cue. *Correction:* open the last paragraph "Earlier, in 1415, the Council of Constance declared Wyclif a heretic and ordered his bones dug up."
2. **"never printed" is absolute.** Walter says the version "had never been printed" by Tyndale's day (walter 673-674); it has been printed since, including the 1850 edition the story cites. *Correction:* "The Bible was copied by hand, not yet printed, and scarce, so a family might own a single gospel."
