## Quotes: my own renderings compared

- **Q1, letter to Urban VI** (fasciculi 27423-27428). Mine: "But if I have erred in these things, I am willing to be humbly corrected, even by death if need be. And if I could make the effort in person as I wished, I would humbly visit the presence of the Roman pontiff. But God has constrained me to the contrary, and has generally taught me to obey God rather than men." The outline's rendering matches; every clause is present and nothing is added. Arnold v3 30710-30719 confirms that the Latin is the original and the English version is a heightened one. Holds.
- **Q2, Knighton** (16948-16969). Mine: "This Master John Wyclif translated the Gospel, which Christ gave to the clergy and doctors of the church to minister to lay people and weaker persons, from Latin into the English, not the angelic, tongue. So through him it is made common, and more open to lay people and to women who can read than it usually is to well-lettered clergy of good understanding. And so the Gospel pearl is scattered and trodden by swine." The outline's rendering matches, its ellipsis is declared, and the pun is explained correctly. It is credited as the hostile chronicle's claim in beat 5, Quote 2 and Do not say. Holds.
- **Q3, Hus at Constance** (palacky 15243-15250). Mine: "I do not know where the soul of John W. is. I hope that he is saved, and I fear that he may be damned. Yet in hope I would wish my soul to be where the soul of John W. is." The outline's rendering matches, and so does the twelve-years-earlier framing. Holds.
- **Bible authorship.** F&M 2407-2420 and 2319-2320, and Pollard 840-845 and 968-973, are accurately represented as Contested. Nowhere does the outline say he translated it himself except as Knighton's charge. Holds.
- **Bones.** Mansi 65527-65560 (dig up, if they can be told apart), Raynaldus 7971-8025 (Martin V: dig up, burn, leave no trace; V id. Dec., year 11) and Foxe 8018-8045 (burned, ashes into the river) are all correctly split. Holds.
- **þ spot-checks.** Matthew 35750-35752 ("and here the friars with their supporters say that it is heresy to write thus God's law in English, and make it known to lay men") and 35791-35793 ("thus Christ and his apostles taught the people in that tongue that was most known to the people; why should not men do so now?") are both sound readings.

## Findings

1. **BLOCKING. Beat 2, "John of Gaunt, duke of Lancaster, the king's uncle."** No held source says this, and it is wrong for February 1377: Edward III was still alive (Shirley, fasciculi 1730-1731: "On the 21st of June the king died"), so Gaunt was the king's son. **Correction:** delete ", the king's uncle". Add to Do not say: "the king's uncle" → do not give Gaunt's family tie. Shirley calls him only "the duke of Lancaster" (fasciculi 1686-1687).

2. **MINOR. What came of them 3: "before the council that then condemned them both".** This reverses the order. Constance condemned Wyclif on 4 May 1415 (mansi 132797-132800), before Hus's hearing of 7 June. **Correction:** "at Constance, before the council that had condemned Wyclif a month earlier (Quote 3)".

3. **MINOR. "Hus never met him" (Connection; Do not say).** No held text states this. What the record does say (palacky 15243-15245) is that Hus first spoke these words when Wyclif's theological books had not yet reached Bohemia, and that he knew only of Wyclif's good life. **Correction:** in both places, replace it with "Hus knew him only through his books and his reputation; the books travelled from England to Bohemia (palacky 15243-15245)". In Do not say: "Hus was his student" → "Hus knew him through his books".

4. **MINOR. Do not say lacks a qualifier on what Hus defended.** At the same hearing Hus said he had defended no error of Wyclif's (palacky 15252-15254). **Correction:** add "Hus defended Wyclif's teachings" → "Hus defended Wyclif's memory and hoped he was saved. He said he had defended none of Wyclif's errors (palacky 15252-15254)".

5. **MINOR. Beat 3, "only their look and taste remain".** The Latin is "solum species eorundem" (fasciculi 11456-11457), meaning their appearance. "Taste" is added. **Correction:** "only their appearance remains".

6. **MINOR. Beat 3, the Confession.** The outline drops the verb that carries his caution. **Correction:** "...but he did not dare to say that it is 'essentially, substantially, corporeally' that bread" (fasciculi 11681-11683, "Non tamen audeo dicere").

7. **MINOR. Beat 6, "the pope should leave worldly power".** He offers this "tanquam consilium", as counsel (fasciculi 27415). **Correction:** "he advised that the pope should leave worldly power to secular rulers".

8. **MINOR. Quote 1 Job and Do not say cite "fasciculi 2744-2748" for Shirley's reading of failing health.** That reading is at 2737-2741, where Shirley takes it from Gascoigne. **Correction:** cite fasciculi 2737-2741.

9. **MINOR. Opening 1, "His enemies' chronicle".** The Fasciculi is a collection of documents, not a chronicle (as Who tells it says). **Correction:** "His enemies' collection of documents".

10. **MINOR. Do not say on the bones.** Foxe's "forty-one years after he was buried" and his heading's "A.D. 1425" (foxe 7914-7922) clash with Martin V's December 1427 letter. **Correction:** add "Do not say 'forty-one years later' or give any year for the burning. Only the orders are dated (1415, 1427)."

11. **MINOR. Leave out, Prague thunder tale: "Hus called the article untrue".** His answer is "Non est verus iste articulus in multis punctis" (palacky 12910-12911). **Correction:** "Hus called the article untrue in many points".

12. **MINOR. Library sweep.** `wright_political-poems-and-songs-v2_1861.txt` (contemporary verse on Wyclif and the Wycliffites, about 26 mentions) and `hus_tractatus-responsivus-lat-eng_thomson1927.txt` (about 41 mentions) are held and not accounted for. Nothing in the outline depends on them. **Correction:** list both under Set aside, with reasons.
