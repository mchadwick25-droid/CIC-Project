# Outline check: Narsai of Nisibis

**Verdict: FIX**

Checked against `Source_Sheet.md` and the cited lines in `cic/texts/` (History, Cause, Simeon in Assemani BO I and Bar Hebraeus's editors, Homilies, Junillus, Guidi, Mari, Chronicle of Edessa). Most lines hold. The Simeon quote and its rendering are accurate ("Narses verò Leprosus Nisibi scholam instituit", BO I 75018-75020; Cyrus and Zeno, 3743-3747). The flight, the books and the threat of fire are correctly the History's. Quote 2 matches Connolly 6857-6858. Four blocking faults remain.

## BLOCKING

**B1. Quote 1 is not fixed to its speaker.** "Edessa grew dark; Nisibis grew bright…" (Cause 20743-20746) is the school's own boast, about ninety years later, in a speech credited to Barhadbeshabba. Its line "the empire of the Romans filled with error" is a partisan verdict. The Job line ("as a partisan witness") leaves room for a writer to use it as the narrator's summary.
- Replace the Job with: "Job: the school's own boast, made about ninety years later in the Cause, a speech to the school credited to Barhadbeshabba. Bring it in with its speaker named ('Barhadbeshabba, speaking to the school about ninety years later, put it this way'). Never use it as the narrator's summary of what happened."
- Add to **Do not say**: "'Edessa grew dark; Nisibis grew bright', or 'the Roman Empire filled with error', in the narrator's voice. Say it only as the Cause's words, with Barhadbeshabba named."
- In **Who tells it**, extend the History line to: "the listener should hear his name for the flight, the books, the speeches at Edessa and Nisibis, the school's verdict (Quote 1), and any wonder."

**B2. Beat 6 tells the Jacob of Serugh story as fact.** Only the History (37258-37271), which calls Jacob a heretic, says Narsai answered Jacob. Mari 14272 says the opposite: Jacob imitated Narsai (D5, Contested). The beat carries only "The verse homilies: Documented".
- Replace the sentence with: "The History says he answered a rival preacher, Jacob of Serugh, whose verse was drawing people away, with *mimre* of his own, one for every day of the year (History 37258-37271). Mari, much later, says Jacob imitated Narsai (Mari 14272). Who answered whom: Contested (D5). That he wrote many verse homilies: Documented."
- Add to **Do not say**: "'He wrote his homilies to answer Jacob of Serugh' as fact. Say the History tells it so, and Mari says the reverse."

**B3. The death date quietly settles the departure date.** The 502-504 range rests on a departure in 457 (Connolly 299-302; Nau 37427-37428). The outline keeps "457, or after 471" open in beat 3, but beat 7 and the Setting give "about 502-504". Nau's own note (History 36700-36705) says that if the History's bishop Cyrus stands, Narsai did not leave before 472 and "ne serait pas mort avant 512". The outline's "fifty years at Nisibis" option does not fit 502-504 either.
- Beat 7: "He died in the early 500s, after forty, forty-five or fifty years at Nisibis, as the sources variously count (E15, D2). Modern editors put it about 502-504, counting from a departure in 457 (Connolly; Nau). Nau notes that a departure after 471 would put his death no earlier than 512 (History 36700-36705). Contested."
- Setting, century line: "the 400s, into the early 500s. Head of the Edessa school twenty years, from about 437 or after 452 (Nau, History 36651-36655, 36700-36705), leaving in 457 or after 471; death in the early 500s (S6, D1-D2). Spoken: 'in the 400s'; 'in the early 500s'."
- Change the **Do not say** line "'He died in 502' as fact. Say: about 502-504" to: "Any single year for his death, or '502-504' beside 'after 471'. Say: in the early 500s (D1-D2)."

**B4. Beats 1, 2 and 5 do not carry their witness kind.** All three rest on the Cause and the History. These are Barhadbeshabba's two works, possibly by one author, about ninety years later, and written in praise. The outline tags beats 3 and 4 this way but not these.
- Beat 1: "He came with Barsauma" goes further than the sources, which disagree (the Cause has them go to Edessa together, 20482-20491; the History has them meet there, 36538-36539). Say: "He studied there alongside Barsauma." Tag: "Widely Accepted (Cause; History; Bar Hebraeus 4264-4267)."
- Beat 2: tag: "Twenty years: Widely Accepted (Cause, History, Mari, Bar Hebraeus). His condition and the daily chanted commentary: the Cause's and the History's telling; Inferential-Thin as details."
- Beat 5: "Students came from Persia and Syria, and most of those at the Edessa school" becomes: "The Cause says students came from Persia and Syria, and most of those at the Edessa school (Cause 20739-20743; the school's own later account, Inferential-Thin). The History says only that 'brothers' gathered from every side (History 37080-37081)."

## MINOR

**M1. Quote 3 rendering.** The French (History 36694-36695) is a single question that the accusers "had said to one another". "Subjugué" here means mastered or won over, not conquered in war. Correction: "How has a Persian, a man who is a stranger even to the order of logic, won over the Romans?" In Opening 2, change "His accusers' question" to "what his accusers said among themselves, as the History reports it".

**M2. Beat 3 drops Nestorius from the charge.** History 36684-36686 says "Theodore and Nestorius". Say: "His enemies said he was a heretic, following Theodore and Nestorius." The narrator still does not use "Nestorian".

**M3. Connection.** "as he says in the Cause" becomes "as the Cause has him say". The Simeon line in quotation marks counts as a fourth quote. Either drop the quotation marks (paraphrase: "Simeon, his enemy, says Narsai the Leper set up the school at Nisibis") or count it against the limit of three.

**M4. What came of them 1.** The Cause says "institutions" issued from the school exist "en plusieurs endroits" (17464-17465). Say: "institutions that came from it now exist in several places", not "schools … in many places".

**M5. What came of them 3.** Guidi (9990-9995) says that in 602 the school searched out the old rules, which were no longer to be found, and joined them with later additions. Say: "In 602 the school searched out the old rules and, with later additions, brought them back into force (Guidi's summary)."

**M6. Library sweep misses a held primary witness.** `badger_nestorians-and-their-rituals-v2_1852.txt` 17649-17656 gives Abdisho's (Ebedjesu's) 1200s catalogue in English: "Narsai, the Harp of the Soul … three hundred and sixty poems". Add it to the sheet. In I5 and What came of them 4, cite it, not Connolly alone, for the 360. In What came of them 6, say: "'Harp': the 1200s catalogue of Abdisho (Badger's English, 'the Harp of the Soul') and editors ('the Harp of the Holy Spirit'); Widely Accepted as a name."

**M7. Links, Simeon.** "wrote against him" becomes: "wrote against his party, and listed him among its men by the nickname 'the Leper' (BO I 74556-74557)".
