# Outline check 2: Narsai of Nisibis

## The checker's own account of the heart of his life (written before reading `Outline.md`)

Narsai was a Persian-born teacher of Scripture and a poet, the Church of the East's great teacher in the century it took its own shape. His work had three parts that held together:

1. **He taught the Bible, in Theodore of Mopsuestia's way.** At Edessa, the Roman frontier city, he studied in the school of the Persians when Theodore's commentaries had just been put into Syriac, and then led that school for some twenty years, explaining Scripture every day (Cause 20547-20560; History 36627-36634). His accusers and his friends agree that he followed Theodore (History 36684-36686; Mari 14256-14257). That way of reading Scripture is what he defended: he would not agree even outwardly, and left under threat of burning (History 36740-36799).
2. **He founded, with Bishop Barsauma, the school of Nisibis, just inside Persia.** Students came from Persia, Syria and most of Edessa (Cause 20739-20743); a hostile contemporary also says he founded it (Simeon). It took written rules in 496 while he led it, and the school later swore to keep "the canons of Narsai" (Guidi, editor). It outlived him by more than a century under his kinsman Abraham and others, taught the Bible by rule as seen from Constantinople c. 550 (Junillus), and in its own boast made schools multiply in Persia: "Edessa grew dark; Nisibis grew bright" (Cause 20743-20746). This is the heaviest thing that came of him.
3. **He taught in verse.** He wrote verse homilies (mimre), "more than three hundred" by the Cause's count (20746-20747), 360 by Abdisho; some survive in his name and were used in worship. His friends called him the Harp of the Holy Spirit; his enemies, the Leper.

The essence: a Persian teacher who carried Theodore's reading of the Bible across the frontier and gave the Church of the East a school, its rules and a body of sung teaching. The flight from Edessa is the turn of the story, not its heart. The quarrel with Barsauma, the healing and the Persian king's suspicion are secondary.

## Findings

**B1. BLOCKING. "Forty-seven survive" (Story beats, beat 2) is unsupported, and a held text contradicts it.** Connolly 253-260 says only that Mingana's two printed volumes of 1905 "comprise 47 of the 360 Homilies with which Narsai is credited by Ebedjesu". He does not say how many survive. Scher's catalogue of the Chaldean patriarchate's manuscripts at Mosul (`scher_notice-manuscrits-syriaques-patriarcat-chaldeen-mossoul-fra_1907.txt` 1109-1131) lists a two-volume copy with forty-two and thirty-nine homilies, "la plupart" printed by Mingana, and another manuscript (1138-1141) with two homilies Mingana does not have. The heart section gets it right ("Forty-seven survive in print"); beat 2 drops "in print". A writer will repeat it.
Correction, beat 2: replace "Forty-seven survive, on Scripture and the church's rites (I5, H3)." with "Forty-seven were printed in 1905, on Scripture and the church's rites (I5, H3)." Add to **Do not say**: "'Forty-seven survive' or 'only forty-seven survive.' Say: forty-seven were printed in 1905; manuscripts hold more (Scher's Mosul catalogue)."

**M1. MINOR. John of Beth Rabban is not on the source sheet, and his kinship is an editor's.** "What it changed" says the school "went on after him, under his kinsmen Abraham and John of Beth Rabban (I4)". I4 names Abraham only. The Cause (20873-20876) says John of Beth Rabban was also once called Abraham and that "Abraham et Jean ... purent diriger l'assemblée"; that both were Narsai's kin is Scher's footnote (20887-20888, editor), not the text. The Connection's "under heads from his own household" rests on the same plural.
Correction: in "What it changed", read "under his kinsman Abraham, who had shared his cell, and others (I4)". In the Connection, read "under a head from his own household, his kinsman Abraham". If John is wanted, add him to the sheet as Cause 20873-20880 with kinship marked as Scher's note.

**M2. MINOR. "About ninety years later" (Quote 1) needs its anchor.** The Cause dates to the end of the 500s (Scher 20827; Connolly "after 585"). That is about ninety years after Narsai's death, but 115 to 130 years after the founding. Set beside the founding, "ninety years later" is wrong.
Correction, Quote 1 job line: "made about ninety years after his death, a century and more after the founding". Add to **Do not say**: "'Ninety years after the school was founded.' Say: about ninety years after his death."

**M3. MINOR. The Library sweep missed Scher's Mosul manuscript catalogue,** which holds what the outline needs for "What came of them" 4. Besides B1, it lists a book of homilies "pour les Rogations des Ninivites, choisies parmi les homélies de Mar Aprem et Mar Narsaï" (829-830). That is a held witness to his homilies used in the church's worship next to Ephrem's. The census longDescription does not need it, and nothing in the outline rests on an editor where this would carry it, so this is not blocking.
Correction: add the file to the sheet's **Used** list (lines 1109-1151, 829-830; later manuscripts, editor's catalogue, Documented that the homilies were copied and gathered for a church office). "What came of them" 4 may then read "kept, copied, and read in the church's office of the Rogations of the Ninevites beside Ephrem's".

**M4. MINOR. "The Harp of the Holy Spirit" as his friends' name rests on editors.** Connolly's note (7091-7092) and Bar Hebraeus's editors (4531) give it; the only primary witness held, Abdisho's catalogue of the 1200s, says "the Harp of the Soul" (Badger 17649). The outline's "Widely Accepted as a name" is fair. Add to **Do not say**: "'His friends in his lifetime called him...' Say: he came to be called the Harp of the Holy Spirit."
