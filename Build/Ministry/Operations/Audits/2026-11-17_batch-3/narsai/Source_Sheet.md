# Source sheet: Narsai of Nisibis

Census id `narsai`, told within `the-church-of-the-east-under-persia` (census name "The Church of the East under Persia", short name "Church of the East"). All paths are under `cic/texts/`. Line numbers are the file's own. French and Latin text is quoted as the OCR gives it, errors included; a bracketed gloss follows where the OCR is garbled.

Witness kinds: **own** (his own words), **contemporary**, **later** (with rough gap), **late legend**. Confidence uses the project's five levels.

## Setting

S1. **Edessa had a school of the Persians, closed in 489.** Chron. Edessa 189: "73. An. 800, the school of the Persians was eradicated from Edessa." (Year 800 of the Greeks = 489; Brooks's note in John of Ephesus 8393-8394 gives "489 (Chron. Edess., A.S. 100)".) Kind: later chronicle. **Documented.**

S2. **The school's ruin at Edessa told by its enemies.** John of Ephesus 8384-8390: "the holy bishop Cyrus discovered it, and tore it out from its roots, and did not allow the school (σχολή) of the Persians to be mentioned there again, as had been the case before; and this school was from that time established in the city of Nisibis, from which all that country drink dregs of gall, so that even in this our country, the country of the Romans, some men taste of it". Hostile. **Documented** (that the school moved to Nisibis); the verdict is John's.

S3. **Nisibis lay on the frontier between the two empires.** Cause 20690-20693 (Barsauma speaking): "Vous ne pourrez trouver dans toute la Perse une ville qui vous soit plus convenable que celle-ci; c'est une ville importante; et, comme elle se trouve située entre les deux empires, on y vient de toutes parts". Also History 36983-36984: the school would bring "un grand avantage aux deux partis, aux Romains et aux Perses en même temps". Kind: later. **Widely Accepted.** Spoken words: "Edessa, in the Roman Empire"; "Nisibis, just inside Persia". Modern places (census): Edessa and Nisibis are in today's Turkey (Edessa is Şanlıurfa; Nisibis is Nusaybin, census `experienceToday`).

S4. **Born in Persian territory, in Ma'alta.** History 36179-36180: "Cet homme de Dieu etait du pays de Ma'altd, son village se nommait 'Ain Doulbd." Mari 14346: "Mar Narses Ma'allaia oriundus". **Widely Accepted** (both from the Church of the East).

S5. **A Persian school under threat in his boyhood.** History 36427-36432 (garbled "nisme" = paganism): "le paganisme se souleva contre la verite par l'operation de Satan, et les Mages, les sages de la Perse, entendirent parler de cette ecole et vinrent pour faire renier la verite aux enfants ... Le maitre d'alors ... emmena l'ecole et alla se cacher dans une montagne". Sole witness, hagiographic. **Inferential-Thin.**

S6. **Dates.** Census: "left Edessa 457 or after 471; died 502-504". Nau's note, History 37427-37428: "II a vecu de 400 (?) a 503 ou 504, s'il a quitte Edesse sous Nonnus, comme cela nous parait le plus probable". Connolly 299-302: 45 years after 457 gives death in 502. Nau's note to History 37337 (on a poem of Narsai's set at the siege of Amida): "En 503. On ne doit donc pas mettre la mort de Narsès avant cette date." **Contested** (see Disagreements D1-D2). Spoken: "the late 400s"; "about 500".

S7. **The labels in the theological quarrel.** History 36684-36686, his Edessa accusers: "Cet interprete est heretique parce qu'il adhere a la pensee de Theodore et de Nestorius". Simeon tells the spread of "Nestorianism" in Persia (Bar Hebraeus editors 3661-3691, after Assemani). The census note: "'Nestorian' was a name its opponents gave it." The narrator should say "the Church of the East" and, for the quarrel, "followers of Theodore of Mopsuestia" rather than "Nestorian".

## Events

E1. **Schooling and a first teaching post near his uncle.** History 36447-36448: "Le saint demeura dans cette ecole pendant neuf ans, et, en discipline, il surpassa son maitre." Then, orphaned, he went to his uncle Emmanuel, head of the monastery of Kefar-Mari, who "avait ete instruit dans les ecoles d'Edesse" (36482-36486); he stayed one winter, then went to Edessa and "y demeura dix ans" (36494-36497); then back to Kefar-Mari ("trois cents freres se reunirent bientot pres de lui", 36524), back to Edessa, and took over the monastery for one year before returning to Edessa (36526-36536). Sole witness. **Inferential-Thin** for the details; the uncle-monastery-Edessa pattern is the History's alone.

E2. **Studied at Edessa with Barsauma and others under Qiyore.** Cause 20482-20491: "Lorsque Mar Narsaï, Barsauma et Ma'né — qui furent ensuite consacrés évêques, le second pour Nisibe et le dernier pour Réwardaschir — entendirent la renommée de cette école ... ils y allèrent aussitôt ... Le directeur et l'interprète de l'école était alors Cyoré". Cause 20547-20551: the commentaries of Theodore "ayant été traduits en syriaque et ayant passé à l'assemblée d'Édesse ... ils étudièrent les écrits de l'Interprète." Also History 36538-36539 (met Barsauma at Edessa). Bar Hebraeus 4264-4267 names Acacius as "socius ... ipsius Barsumae necnon Magnetis et Narsetis in schola Edessae". **Widely Accepted.** Word for a newcomer: "the Interpreter" was the Church of the East's title for Theodore of Mopsuestia (Scher's note, Cause 20400-20401).

E3. **Head of the Edessa school for twenty years.** Cause 20553-20560: "Après la mort de Cyoré, interprète de l'école, toute l'assemblée demanda Narsaï pour chef et directeur; car il n'avait point d'égal dans l'école. Narsaï, n'ayant pu résister, leur dit : « Moi, je ne peux pas me charger de tout le travail de l'école ... Mais si vous établissez un maître de lecture et un autre d'épellation, je pourrai peut-être me charger de l'interprétation. » L'assemblée exauça sa demande. Narsaï dirigea l'école pendant vingt ans, en faisant tous les jours des commentaires accompagnés de chant." History 36627-36634 agrees (twenty years; reading and spelling master added). Bar Hebraeus 4557 and Mari 14255 also give twenty years. **Widely Accepted.** (The History names the predecessor "Rabboula"; Nau's note 36651-36655 says Qiyore is meant.) Word: the "interpreter" is the teacher who explains Scripture.

E4. **Accused and forced out of Edessa.** History 36643-36698: the bishop "Qourd" [Qiyore/Cyrus; Nau reads Nonnus, note 36700-36705] and "ses clercs" raised trouble: "« Cet interprete est heretique parce qu'il adhere a la pensee de Theodore et de Nestorius ... »" (36684-36686). "La cause de tout cela etait l'envie. Ils s'etaient dit : Gomment [Comment] un homme perse a-t-il subjugue les Bomaius [Romains], lui qui est etranger meme a l'ordre (τάξις) de la logique? A cette premiere cause ils en joignirent une autre qui etait plus dangereuse : ils dirent que sa pensee etait avec les Perses, qu'il cherchait a jeter du trouble aux frontieres et a troubler les empires et meme qu'il voulait livrer la ville d'Edesse." (36693-36698). They asked him to agree outwardly only; he refused (36740-36747): "Si je fais cela, quelle parole sera saine et ornee de la doctrine et de la purete de la piete et quelle incorruptibilite aura-t-elle pour que nous paraissions sinceres devant les adversaires?" (36746-36785). "ils le condamnerent a etre brule" (36799). Sole witness for the scene: **Inferential-Thin** for speeches; that he left Edessa under a heresy charge: **Widely Accepted** (Cause 20610-20612; Mari 14260-14265; Bar Hebraeus through Simeon 3741-3743).

E5. **Night flight; his books sent ahead.** History 36830-36839: a friend warned him "que s'il ne s'enfuyait pas cette nuit meme ... on le jetterait au feu ... il se leva en hate, des qu'il fit nuit ... Quand il arriva a l'eglise de la ville, il y trouva des Perses, et il leur demanda, si c'etait possible, d'emporter ses livres avec eux, car c'etait la tout son tresor. Lorsque ceux-ci apprirent la cause de cette affaire, ils se rejouirent beaucoup et prirent soin de porter ses livres jusqu'a Nisibe." Sole witness. **Inferential-Thin.** Mari 14264-14265 has him flee "ad insulam quamdam" (to an island) instead: see D4.

E6. **At Nisibis he meant to go further into Persia; Barsauma kept him.** History 36931-36935: "il alla au monastere des Perses, qui est situe a l'est de la ville; car il avait forme le projet d'aller a l'est, c'est-a-dire au milieu de la Perse, pour y instruire et y semer l'enseignement de la piete". Cause 20611-20617: "Mar Narsaï quitta donc l'école, et, étant venu à Nisibe, il s'installa dans le couvent des Perses. Il avait formé le projet de se rendre en Perse. Et Barsauma, ayant appris cela, lui envoya son archidiacre et le fit introduire dans la ville avec grand honneur ... Barsauma pria Narsaï de vouloir bien rester chez lui et de fonder une école dans la ville et lui promit son secours." **Widely Accepted** (two tellings, likely related). Word: Barsauma was bishop (metropolitan) of Nisibis.

E7. **Barsauma's argument: the move is providence.** Cause 20617-20619: "« Ne pensez pas, mon frère, lui dit-il, que votre départ d'Édesse et la dispersion de l'assemblée soient accidentels; au contraire ils sont providentiels. »" History 37025-37027: "l'assemblee (l'ecole) a passe autrefois d'Antioche a Daphne et, de la, a Edesse; de la meme maniere, a mon avis, elle passe maintenant d'Edesse ici". Speeches in a later writer's mouth: **Inferential-Thin** as words; the request itself **Widely Accepted**.

E8. **The school founded in a camel stable.** History 37075-37081: "il lui acheta, pour l'ecole, une ecurie de chameaux (Beit-gamle) qui etait pres de l'eglise. Comme auparavant il y avait eu des ecoles en cet endroit avec un interprete de Cosme [?], nomme Simeon ... en peu de temps, des freres commencerent a se reunir de tous cotes a cause du saint." Cause 20739-20743: Narsai "voulut bien fonder une école à Nisibe ... non seulement les frères persans et syriens venaient à lui, mais encore la plupart des frères qui étaient dans l'école d'Édesse." Simeon (hostile), BO I 75018-75020: "Narses verò Leprosus Nisibi scholam instituit". The founding: **Documented** (friendly and hostile witnesses agree). The camel stable: **Inferential-Thin** (History alone). Words: students are called "brothers"; the school is "the assembly".

E9. **The rules of 496.** Statutes, Guidi's introduction 9884-9888 (editor): "I primi statuti della scuola, promulgati nel 496 dell'e. v. quando ne era maestro il celebre Narsai, e Osea era metropolita di Nisibi". And 9937-9940: "alcuni zelanti si presentarono al successore di Barsaumà, Osea, per chiedergli nuovi statuti, ma Osea li invitò a compilare essi stessi i propri statuti; giovandosi specialmente dell'opera di Narsai; ciò che essi fecero, e stabilirono 21 canone". 9948-9952: under Khosrau I the school "dichiararono solennemente di accettare e osservare i canoni di Narsai". **Documented** that rules were made in 496 under Narsai (Guidi reads the dated preface); **the wording is Guidi's**, since the Library holds no readable text or translation of the canons. Guidi 9875-9877: the school's fame "giunse perfino in Africa ed in Italia".

E10. **Quarrel with Barsauma.** History 37118-37175: crowds at his cell near Barsauma's house; "la femme de l'eveque, nommee Mamai ... vit une grande foule en cet endroit, et des chars et des grands, elle fut saisie d'une grande envie" (37123-37126); "« Tu n'es pas un eveque, mais un sujet »" (37130); he withdrew to Kefar-Mari and wrote two poems, one on Barsauma's wife; when it was read at Nisibis "l'eveque se repentit et il le fit revenir" (37210); he moved his cell further off "et (tous deux) eurent de l'affection l'un pour l'autre jusqu'a la fin" (37211-37215). Mari 14268-14272 tells the same envy of Mamaea. Bar Hebraeus 4410-4420 (hostile): "Barsumas autem et Narses inter se occasione pellicis rixati sunt; Barsumas vero Nisibi expulsit Narsetem, qui dum in regionem Carduaeorum se recepisset ibi minime tranquillus fuit. Quocirca carmen confecit cujus initium est : « pauper temporis, » in quo suam erga Barsumam amicitiam significat; tum vero hic eum iterum in officio redintegravit." **Contested** for the cause; the break and the return: **Widely Accepted** (all three).

E11. **Way of life.** History 37215-37227: "Il se nourrissait toujours d'un seul plat cuit qu'il se faisait lui-meme vers le soir ou meme une fois pour deux jours; son lit etait une natte de jonc et de palmier ... il se mettait un livre sur le visage et il dormait ainsi sur son siege; combien de fois ce livre etait cause de son reveil parce qu'il echappait a ses mains". Sole witness, praise genre. **Inferential-Thin.**

E12. **Verse as teaching, against a rival poet.** History 37258-37271: Jacob of Serugh "se mit a exposer ... son heresie et son erreur sous forme de mimre ... afin que, par une composition agreable de paroles sonores, il arrachat le peuple a l'illustre (Narses)". In reply Narsai "plaça la pensee veritable de l'orthodoxie sous la forme appropriee de mimre sur des tons melodieux ... il fit un mimra pour chaque jour de l'annee et il les partagea en douze volumes". Mari 14272 reverses it: "Narsetem imitatus est in carminibus condendis Jacobus sarugensis." Word: a *mimra* (plural *mimre*) is a verse homily, a sermon in metre. That he wrote many verse homilies: **Documented** (the Homilies; Cause 20746-20747 "plus de trois cents homélies"). Who imitated whom: **Contested** (D5).

E13. **Accused before the Persian king; answered with a poem.** History 37307-37335: after a poem on King Peroz's pride, two of his students denounced him to King Qawad at the siege of Amida: "« Celui qui a ecrit cela est votre ennemi et hait votre empire. »" He wrote another poem "dont le commencement est : « L'Orient a repondu a l'ordre (τάξις) du pouvoir », et les Houzaie le traduisirent en langue perse, et, quand on le lut devant le roi ... le roi abandonna son idee (premiere) et les accusateurs cesserent". Nau dates the siege to 503 (37337). Sole witness. **Inferential-Thin.** It mirrors E4: suspected of Persia at Edessa, of Rome in Persia (also 37361-37368, accused before a royal messenger "d'espionnage pour les Romains").

E14. **A healing, in the History's voice.** History 37371-37406: a demon threw a boy to the ground in the square; at the mother's plea Narsai and the students prayed and recited a psalm, "il plaça la main sur lui et il lui fit le signe de la croix sur le front et aussitot le demon s'echappa de lui". Mari 14273-14276 adds a plague lifted by his prayers and angels seen round his chair. Hagiography. **Inferential-Thin**; a wonder, keep in the witness's voice or leave out.

E15. **Death and length of office.** History 37409-37412: "Il dirigea la communaute de Nisibe durant quarante ans, celle d'Edesse durant vingt, et le monastere durant six ans, et il fut enseveli dans une bonne vieillesse". Cause 20746: "Narsaï dirigea cette école pendant quarante-cinq ans". Bar Hebraeus 4554-4557: "annis quinquaginta". Mari 14259-14260 and 14276-14278: fifty years at Nisibis, "munus doctoris gesserat annis sexaginta", buried "in ecclesia, quae sub eius nomine est". **Contested** (D2).

## Impact, heaviest first

I1. **The school at Nisibis and its line of teachers.** Cause 17457-17465 (the speech's preface to the students): "les causes qui la firent supprimer d'Édesse et la firent fonder dans cette ville par Mar Bar-Sauma, évêque, et par Rabban Mar Narsaï, prêtre ... Des avantages appréciables en découlèrent sur le royaume des Perses comme en témoignent les institutions issues d'elle et qui existent maintenant en plusieurs endroits". Cause 20743-20746: "Les écoles se multiplièrent dans l'empire des Perses; Édesse s'obscurcit; Nisibe s'illumina: l'empire des Romains s'emplit d'erreur, celui des Perses de la connaissance de la crainte de Dieu." Successors: Cause 20806-20815 (Elisha bar Qozbaye, then Abraham, "parent de Mar Narsaï"). Rabban Surin's verse, quoted by Scher, Cause 21686-21699: "Ce furent l'admirable Narsaï et Mar Bar Sauma qui l'établirent et qui l'affermirent." Kind: later, the school's own voice. **Documented** that the school lasted and kept his name as founder; the light-and-dark verdict is the Cause's.

I2. **The school's way of teaching the Bible by rule, seen from outside.** Junillus 22735-22738 (preface, c. 550): "vidisse me quendam Paulum nomine, Persam genere, qui Syrorum schola in Nisibi urbe est edoctus, ubi divina lex per magistros publicos, sicut apud nos in mundanis studiis grammatica et rhetorica, ordine ac regulariter traditur." Does not name Narsai. **Documented** for the school's method and fame c. 550; the tie to Narsai is **Inferential-Thin** (he was its first head; nothing here names him). Hostile counterpart: John of Ephesus 8384-8390 (S2).

I3. **The rules he left were renewed for a century.** Statutes, Guidi 9948-9952 and 9982-9995 (editor): the school swore to keep "i canoni di Narsai" under Khosrau I; new canons in 590; in 602 the old rules were sought out and brought back into force. **Documented** (Guidi's reading of the dated prefaces; editor's words).

I4. **Students trained in his own house carried the school on.** History 37453-37462 (Abraham): "il etait parent de Mar Narses ... Son pere repondit : « Il s'appelle Narses comme toi. » Aussitot il changea son nom et l'appela Abraham, et il dit : « Il n'y aura pas deux Narses dans une cellule »". Cause 20814-20815 and 20869-20871 agree. History 37657-37659: under Abraham "plus de mille freres se reunirent". Bar Hebraeus 4558-4563: his disciple Joseph Huzaya succeeded him and changed the way Syriac was read. Cause 21597 (Scher's note): a treatise "du vénérable Rabban Mar Joseph Iouzaya, lecteur de l'école de Rabban Mar Narsai, sur la grammaire". **Widely Accepted** (Abraham); Joseph's role **Contested** (Bar Hebraeus vs the Cause's list, which does not name Joseph as head).

I5. **His verse homilies, kept and used in worship.** Abdisho, Badger 17649-17656: "Narsai, the Harp of the Soul, wrote an exposition of Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, Joshua, Judges, Ecclesiastes, Isaiah, the twelve minor Prophets, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, and Daniel. He wrote also twelve other books, three hundred and sixty poems, a Liturgy, an exposition of the Sacraments, and a treatise on Baptism. He wrote, moreover. Consolations, Antiphonse [Antiphonae], hymns, litanies, homilies, and a treatise on an Evil Life." Homilies 253-262 (Connolly, editor): 47 homilies printed by Mingana (1905) "of the 360 Homilies with which Narsai is credited by Ebedjesu ... the thirteenth century bibliographer". Connolly 627-628: "That Narsai was their author there can be no reasonable doubt." Scher's note on the Chronicle of Seert, chronicle-of-seert_histoire-nestorienne-seconde-partie-po13-ara-fra_scher1919.txt 28409-28417 (editor, citing a Seert manuscript): two Sunday morning hymns of the office "sont attribues a Narsai", and Henana's party cut them "par mepris pour Narsai". **Documented** that homilies survive in his name; the hymn detail is an editor's note, **Inferential-Thin**.

I6. **What he defended: Theodore's way of reading Scripture.** Mari 14256-14257: "sacras literas exponebat praelegebatque, ac scientias docebat, Theodori sententiam numquam non presse secutus". Mari 14276: "effatis Mar Theodori sedulo instabat". History 36684-36686 (his accusers say the same). Homily B, Homilies 7058-7070, lists the heretics the baptized renounce, among them "Eutyches, the madman, who went mad in the matter of the passibility of the Impassible" (7066-7068; OCR "EKutyches"). **Widely Accepted.** Marked: **defended**.

I7. **Remembered by name and nickname.** Mari 14347-14348: Theodulus, "Mar Diodori discipulo ... a quo benedictus fuit appellatusque orientis lingua ac christianae religionis poeta" (late legend: Theodulus is a fourth-century master's disciple; Scher's note Cause 20392-20393 dates Theodulus's death to 492). Simeon, BO I 74556-74557: "Narses Leprosus" (hostile). Connolly's note, Homilies 7091-7092: "Narsai himself was dubbed by his enemies 'The Leper,' though his friends called him 'The Harp of the Holy Spirit.'" Bar Hebraeus editors' note 4531: "Narses, a Nestorianis Sancti Spiritus cithara appellatus" (editors). Abdisho, Badger 17649: "Narsai, the Harp of the Soul". **Documented** for "Leper" (Simeon); "Harp": Abdisho's 1200s catalogue ("the Harp of the Soul") and editors ("the Harp of the Holy Spirit"): **Widely Accepted** as a name.

## Narsai's own words (Homilies, Connolly's English)

W1. Homilies 6853-6858 (Hom. XXII, B): "Too little is the tongue of height and depth to give thanks with us to the power of the Creator who has renewed our image and blotted out our iniquity. As in a furnace He re-cast our image in Baptism; and instead of our clay He has made us spiritual gold." **Documented** (own words, in translation).

W2. Homilies 6950-6953 (B): "By the chief Rabbi (or Master) is written the lesson (or alphabet) of the redemption of our life; and unless the learner imitate he cannot understand". Connolly's own uncertainty is in the text ("or"). **Documented.**

W3. Homilies 5163-5166 (Hom. XVII, A; authorship argued, not settled): "Upon their glory my mind gazed narrowly; but dread seized upon me and caused me to desist". Use only with the authorship caveat.

## Links to other people

- **Barsauma of Nisibis** (census `barsauma-of-nisibis`): fellow student at Edessa; bishop who kept him at Nisibis and helped found the school; quarrel and reconciliation (E2, E6-E8, E10).
- **Abraham of Beth Rabban**: kinsman, renamed by him, lived in his cell, later head of the school (I4).
- **Barhadbeshabba 'Arbaia** (census `barhadbeshabba-arbaya`): wrote his life (History) and the school's history (Cause).
- **Theodore of Mopsuestia**: the master whose commentaries Narsai studied and taught (E2, I6). Read his books; never met.
- **Jacob of Serugh**: rival verse preacher (E12).
- **Simeon of Beth Arsham**: hostile contemporary who named him "the Leper" (I7).
- **Henana of Adiabene** (census `henana-of-adiabene`): later head whose party cut hymns credited to Narsai (I5, editor's note).
- **Ephrem**: the Cause makes the Edessa school Ephrem's foundation (20436-20439); a claimed line, not a meeting.

## Disagreements and late material

D1. **When he left Edessa.** Connolly 258-260: "after the expulsion of the followers of Ibas from Edessa, A.D. 457". The History names bishop "Qourd" (Cyrus, 471-498); Nau's note 36700-36705: read Nonnus; if the History's name stands, "Narses n'a pas quitte Edesse avant 472 ... Il ne serait pas mort avant 512." Scher's note Cause 20622-20625: "Il est donc inexact que Narsai et ses compagnons aient été tous ensemble expulsés d'Édesse en 457, comme le racontait Siméon de Beth-Arscham". Simeon (BO I 74817-74825) runs the 457 expulsion and the 489 closure together. Mari 14261 and Statutes (Guidi 9931-9933 with note 2 at 9958: "S'intende certamente Ciro, che fu vescovo di Edessa dal 471 al 498") point to Cyrus. **Contested.**

D2. **Years at Nisibis and death.** History 37410: forty; Cause 20746: forty-five; Bar Hebraeus 4556 and Mari 14260: fifty. Connolly: 502; Nau: 503 or 504 (37427-37428). **Contested.**

D3. **Who founded the school.** History and Cause: Narsai, urged and helped by Barsauma. Simeon: Narsai founded it. Labourt (labourt_le-christianisme-dans-l-empire-perse-sassanide-fra_1904.txt 8267-8274, modern, 1904): Barsauma founded it and named Narsai head. Cause 17459: founded by both. **Widely Accepted** that both did it; **Contested** whose initiative.

D4. **Where he fled.** History: to Nisibis by night, books sent with Persians. Mari 14264-14265: "ad insulam quamdam profugit", composing hymns as he went. **Contested**; Mari is c. 650 years later.

D5. **Jacob of Serugh.** History: Narsai answered Jacob's verse. Mari: Jacob imitated Narsai. **Contested.**

D6. **The quarrel's cause.** History and Mari: Barsauma's wife's envy. Bar Hebraeus: a concubine ("pellex"), within a hostile account of the Church of the East's bishops marrying. **Contested.**

D7. **Successor.** History 37645 with Nau's dates (37660-37662): Abraham at once (503-523), then Elisha four years, then Abraham again. Cause 20806-20815: Elisha seven years, then Abraham. Bar Hebraeus 4558: Joseph Huzaya. **Contested.**

D8. **Hom. XVII (A).** Connolly 169-172 and 423-489: late copyists gave it to 'Abdisho' of Elam (1200s); Connolly and Mingana argue it is Narsai's, perhaps interpolated "surtout à la fin" (Mingana, quoted at 501-502). **Contested.**

D9. **The Chronicle of Seert's Narsai.** PO 5 (chronicle-of-seert_histoire-nestorienne-premiere-partie-fasc2-patrologia-orientalis-5-ara-fra_scher-dib1910.txt 16010-16053) is a priest Narsai of Seleucia under Yazdgard, another man. The Chronicle's chapter on our Narsai (PO 7, 114-115, cited by Nau at History 36102-36103) is **not held**.

## Added for the heart of his life (2026-11-18)

Lines checked or added when the outline was reset to the heart of his life. Each re-read in its file.

H1. **A sixth-century count of his verse homilies.** Cause 20746-20747, straight after the founding: "Narsaï dirigea cette école pendant quarante-cinq ans; 1l [il] composa aussi plus de trois cents homélies avec d'autres ouvrages". Kind: later, c. 80-100 years; the school's own voice. **Documented** that he wrote many verse homilies (with the Homilies themselves and Abdisho, I5); "more than three hundred" is the Cause's count.

H2. **What "commentaries with chant" means.** Cause 20560: "en faisant tous les jours des commentaires accompagnés de chant". Scher's note, 20562-20566 (editor): the Syriac word "signifie ici « à chœur ». L'auteur fait allusion aux Cantiques de Narsaï qui accompagnent certaines de ses homélies poétiques et qui étaient chantés alternativement par deux chœurs". The daily chanted commentary rests on the Cause alone: the History's account of the same twenty years (36627-36634) names the reading and spelling master but not the chant. **Inferential-Thin** as a daily practice; the reading of "chant" as choir hymns is the **editor's**.

H3. **The form of his homilies.** Connolly 712-716 (editor, on Mingana's 47): "all except five are written in couplets, of which each line contains twelve syllables. The exceptions ... are in the seven-syllable metre." **Documented** (the printed texts). Spoken: "sermons in verse", "in lines of twelve syllables".

H4. **The school as the start of a church's learning: a modern editor's view.** Connolly 4482-4496 (editor, 1909): "The expulsion of the followers of Ibas from Edessa in 457 was followed immediately by the foundation of the new School at Nisibis by Narsai and his friend Barsaumâ, bishop of the city. With the foundation of this School began, undoubtedly, the definite formation of what is now known as the Nestorian Church, severed from the communion of the Churches of the Roman Empire. ... the commentaries of Theodore were taken as the standard and foundation of their exegesis of Scripture. From Nisibis other Schools were founded, and the writings of Theodore, now finally banished from Edessa, began to be propagated throughout the Persian Empire". **Dominant Modern Reconstruction** (one editor's synthesis; the 457 date in it is Contested, D1). Behind it in the primary texts: the Cause's "les institutions issues d'elle et qui existent maintenant en plusieurs endroits" (17463-17464) and "Les écoles se multiplièrent dans l'empire des Perses" (20743-20744), both the school's own boast.

H5. **A later student of the school.** Chronicle of Seert (anonymous Arabic compilation, dated by scholars to the 800s-1000s, file header), ch. XLV, `chronicle-of-seert_histoire-nestorienne-seconde-partie-po13-ara-fra_scher1919.txt` 25736-25738, on Rabban Bar 'Edta: "Quand il eut grandi, il alla a l'Ecole de Nisibe, ou il rencontra Mar Abraham, dont il se fit le disciple." (OCR "TEcole" read as "l'Ecole".) Kind: late, c. 400 years or more after. **Inferential-Thin** as a single late example; with Junillus's Paul (I2, c. 550), it shows students of the school going on to teach and lead elsewhere. No held text gives a count or general statement of the church's teachers or bishops trained there.

H6. **The school still in being a century after him.** Statutes, Guidi 9982-9995 (editor): new statutes in 590 under Henana; in 602 "alcuni zelanti ... ricercarono le antiche regole ... e di esse e di quelle aggiunte sotto Hannànà fecero tutto un corpo di statuti". **Documented** (Guidi's reading of the dated prefaces). With the Cause (c. 600), addressed to the living school: the school lasted more than a century after its founding.

H7. **Line correction.** The accusers' question (outline v2, Quote 3) stands at History 36694-36696, not 36693-36695; 36693 holds "La cause de tout cela etait l'envie. Ils s'etaient dit :". The quote is no longer used.
