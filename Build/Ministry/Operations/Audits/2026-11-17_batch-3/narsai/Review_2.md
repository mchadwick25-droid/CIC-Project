## The test, from one hearing

- **Who:** Narsai, a Persian-born teacher of the Bible and verse poet, who led Edessa's school for Persian students for twenty years.
- **What happened:** Accused at Edessa of heresy and of siding with Persia, and warned he would be burned, he fled across the border to Nisibis. There, with Bishop Barsauma, he founded a school that taught the Bible in Theodore of Mopsuestia's way. It took written rules under him in 496, and he led it until he died in the early 500s.
- **Why the connection matters:** The teaching condemned in the Roman Empire went on in Persia, in the Church of the East, for more than a century, under his kinsman Abraham and with rules kept in his name, and was known as far as Constantinople.

**The heart of his life** (from the census entry and what he is known for): a Persian Christian teacher of Scripture and poet, who carried Theodore's way of reading the Bible out of Roman Edessa and, with Barsauma, founded at Nisibis the school of the Church of the East; he taught in verse, in hundreds of metrical sermons.

**First minute (about 150 words):** carried. By word 145 the listener has the flight with his books, Edessa in Rome and Nisibis in Persia, the founding with Barsauma, students from three places, Theodore's way of reading, the enemies' condemnation, the Church of the East, and more than a century of life. The verse begins at word 146 ("Narsai taught in verse too"), right at the edge, and the baptism quote lands it a few seconds later. The school gets the weight it should: the rest of the story returns to it (rules, Abraham, Junillus, the school's own verdict).

**Where a listener may get lost:** after the opening flight and the founding, the story steps back to his birth and Edessa ("he had studied ... led it for twenty years") and tells the flight a second time. The past perfect signals the step back, and the second telling explains the first, so this is followable; it is the one place a listener has to work. Nothing reads as a children's book or talks down.

## Findings

No BLOCKING findings.

Both are in today's southeastern Turkey (outline Setting). Change to: "Edessa lay in the Roman Empire, and Nisibis just across the border in Persia. Both are in what is now southeastern Turkey."

**M2. MINOR. The flashback retells the flight.** The step back to his birth and Edessa, and the second telling of the flight, is the one place the listener has to work. It is followable and the second telling explains the first; no change required. If touched, let the paragraph's first sentence signal the step back plainly (for example, "Narsai was born in Persian territory, and had studied ...").
