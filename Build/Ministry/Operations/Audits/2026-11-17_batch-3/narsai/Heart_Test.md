# Heart test: Narsai of Nisibis

Tested 2026-11-18 against the Brief Kit Standard as changed that day (the heart of the life within the first minute, about 150 words). Read: census entry `narsai`, `Outline.md`, `Source_Sheet.md`, `narsai.brief.txt`. Not read: Review.md, Recheck*.md, other `.brief.*` files.

## 1. The heart, from the census, before reading the story

Census `knownFor`: "A Persian Christian poet and teacher of Scripture who led the school of Edessa for twenty years, fled to Nisibis when accused of heresy and of siding with Persia, and founded there, with Barsauma of Nisibis, the school of the Church of the East."

The heart of his life, then: **a Persian-born poet-teacher who explained Scripture (in Theodore's way) to students, and who carried that teaching across the Roman-Persian frontier to found, with Barsauma, the school at Nisibis that trained the Church of the East's teachers long after him.** The essence of his work is twofold: the school (his main contribution) and the teaching itself, set to chant and verse (the "Harp of the Holy Spirit", hundreds of verse homilies).

**What the sources hold.** The outline's own Thread says the same: "He carried the teaching of Scripture across the Roman-Persian frontier and, with Bishop Barsauma, set up at Nisibis the school that trained the Church of the East's teachers." The sources support every part:
- Twenty years heading the Edessa school, with daily chanted commentary: Widely Accepted (Cause 20553-20560, History, Mari, Bar Hebraeus; E3).
- Leaving Edessa under a heresy charge: Widely Accepted (E4); the year Contested (D1).
- Founding the school at Nisibis, with Barsauma's request and help: Documented, friendly and hostile witnesses agree (E6, E8; Simeon BO I 75018-75020).
- The school lasting under his line, rules kept in his name: Documented (I1, I3, I4; Guidi).
- Many verse homilies: Documented (I5, Abdisho; 47 printed 1905).

## 2. The story, heard once

411 words. The first 150 words carry: the night flight and the books (words 1-40, with about 20 words crediting Barhadbeshabba), the two empires and the Church of the East (40-75), Persian birth, study at Edessa with Barsauma, Theodore's commentaries (75-105), twenty years leading the school with chanted commentary (105-130), and the start of the accusation (130-150).

**Does the heart land in the first 150?** Half of it. The listener learns he was a Persian teacher of Scripture who led Edessa's school and fled to Nisibis. They do not yet learn why he matters: the school he founded at Nisibis. "Asked him to found a school" arrives at word 203; "went on training the Church of the East's teachers" at word 311, past two minutes. The poet half ("verse homilies", "Harp of the Holy Spirit") comes only in the last 35 words. So the first minute tells a flight and a career, not a contribution. A listener stopping at one minute could not say what Narsai left the church.

**Weight.** When the founding does come (para. 4), it is one clause in a sentence about Barsauma, then a list of facts (students, 489, 496). The outline's heaviest legacy, the school and its line of teachers, gets two sentences near the end. It is present but not given its weight.

**Plain sense heard once.** Yes. The opening scene is clear and draws the listener in; time, place and tradition reach the listener early. One small snag: "he says" after "Barhadbeshabba ... tells it" makes the listener hold a speaker they have just met; fine on one hearing.

**Where the time goes.** How-we-know: about 20 words in the opening (Barhadbeshabba's credit), the year dispute (8 words), "the school's own account says", "says his enemy Simeon, who called Narsai the Leper", "Barhadbeshabba tells", the three counts of years. Most of these are short and needed for the outline's Do not say list. The cost is in placement: the first minute spends its time on the scene and its witness, and on schooling, before the contribution.

## 3. Verdict: FIX

**Missing from the first minute:** what he built and why it matters: that at Nisibis, just inside Persia, he and Bishop Barsauma founded the school that went on training the Church of the East's teachers; and, briefly, that he taught in chant and verse (poet as well as teacher).

**What in the outline can carry it**, without new claims:
- The Thread line itself, carried as acts: after the books reach Nisibis, say early that there, with Barsauma, he founded the school that trained the Church of the East's teachers for generations (Thread; E6, E8 Documented; I1, I4).
- Opening 1 already points to it: the books going to Nisibis are the school's seed. Let the opening paragraph close on the school (founded with Barsauma) rather than on the two empires alone, then go back to Edessa.
- The chanted daily commentaries (E3) and the verse homilies (I5) can be named together in the Edessa sentence, so "poet" is in the first minute; the Harp name can stay at the end.
- To make room: trim the opening witness credit to one short clause ("a writer of his own church tells, a century later") and let the founding paragraph carry fewer list items (489 and 496 can stay later).
