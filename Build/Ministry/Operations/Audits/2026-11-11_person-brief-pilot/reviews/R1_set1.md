## Origen — REVISE

The boyhood material holds up well. Each detail checks against Eusebius (npnf201 32533-32565, 32605-32615, 32668-32676, 32730-32734), and the quote is exact. The Decius, death-at-sixty-nine, Ambrose, Hexapla, Celsus, threefold-sense, eternal-generation, end-like-beginning, Rufinus and Epiphanius claims are all supported at the cited lines.

1. BLOCKING. The Philocalia attribution is told as settled, but no ancient witness in the Library supports it. "Basil and Gregory of Nazianzus later gathered passages from his commentaries into the Philocalia." The only support comes from editors. One is the npnf207 prolegomena (22750-22754). The other is the editor's heading on Gregory's Letter 115 (npnf207 47440): "edited by himself and S. Basil". Basil's own editor hedges it: "Probably ... in conjunction with his friend Gregory, Basil compiled ... the Philocalia" (npnf208 1465-1466). Gregory's letter itself says only "we send you the volume of the Philocalia of Origen, containing a selection of passages useful to students of literature" (npnf207 47450-47452). Fix: tell it through Gregory's own letter, and keep the compilation as the usual attribution, not as fact. For example: "Years later Gregory of Nazianzus sent a friend a book of passages from Origen, the Philocalia, which he and Basil are thought to have gathered."
2. STANDARD and fidelity. The phrase "His book On First Principles carried this into doctrine" gets the facts wrong in a small way. The body, soul and spirit teaching is not earlier material that the book then applied. It is in the same book, Book IV (anf04 32370ff). Fix: "In his book On First Principles he went further, ..." or just "In On First Principles he taught ...".
3. STANDARD. The theology paragraph reads as a list: "It said ... It said ... Epiphanius charged ... Jerome accused ... Origen also answered Celsus ... in eight books." The Celsus sentence is also a bare listing of a work, which the card already shows (BRIEF: don't recite works). Fix: drop the Celsus sentence. Rebuild the paragraph so that one idea leads into the next. For example, the Son's eternal generation, then souls before birth and the end like the beginning, then the charge his enemies drew from that, then the caveat that we mostly read him through Rufinus.
4. STANDARD. Something the listener will expect is missing, and Eusebius fully supports it. The move to Caesarea comes with no reason, and the story's central conflict has been cut. Eusebius tells the self-castration (npnf201 33168-33205). He says Demetrius first encouraged Origen and then, jealous of his fame, wrote against "this deed of his boyhood" to the bishops everywhere, after the bishops of Caesarea and Jerusalem ordained him (33208-33234). That is the personal turning point of his life. Fix: put it back in one or two sentences, as Eusebius's account and with Eusebius named. Pay for the words by cutting Celsus (finding 3) and tightening the Hexapla and secretaries lines. This also brings the brief down from 308 words toward 300.
5. STANDARD, for the ear. "Under Decius he was chained" gives no time anchor, so the listener cannot place Decius. "In 553 the Fifth Council" is a bare number and an ordinal the ear can't decode. Fix: "About twenty years later, when the emperor Decius ..." and "Three centuries after his death, in 553, a council at Constantinople named him ...".

## Athanasius of Alexandria — REVISE

All claims check against the cited lines. That includes Antony (npnf204 31195-31200), Nicaea (npnf202 2792-2797, 4000-4018), June 328 and about thirty (npnf204 1510-1520), the Thalia (41455-41497), Discourse II (49583-49593), the Tyre charges, the Arsenius scene and Sozomen's "as it is said" (npnf202 5084-5122, 30009-30018, 30047-30053), and the Trier grain charge (5358-5372). The return in 366 and the death in 373 check too (npnf204 7102-7110, 7260-7270). "Plain sympathy" is a fair reading of "The special providence of God drove Arsenius also to Tyre" (npnf202 5084-5086).

1. STANDARD. A modern editor is used as a story witness: "Archibald Robertson, who edited his works, says Athanasius hid in the desert during one and wrote much of what survives." This is exactly the "his editor" problem, with a name attached. Fix: tell it directly and keep Robertson in the notes. For example: "During the third, he hid among the monks and villages of Egypt, and from his hiding places he wrote more than half of what survives from his pen" (npnf204 5900-5922, 5966-5967). Or use the ancient witness Socrates for a concrete moment: when a new edict came, Athanasius "concealed himself for four entire months in an ancestral tomb" (npnf202 13055-13064).
2. STANDARD. The ending is flat and runs as a list: "Four more exiles followed ... He came home for good in 366 and died in Alexandria in the spring of 373." The last paragraph is dates. Fix: give the four exiles one concrete image, such as the hiding in finding 1, or the search Robertson describes, "Towns and villages, deserts and monasteries, the very tombs were scoured ... but all in vain" (npnf204 5918-5922), told directly. Then end on the return to his people or on his death, said as a moment rather than a date. Robertson's account of his last days is at npnf204 7262-7268: he named Peter as his successor, following the example of Alexander.

## John Chrysostom — REVISE

Most claims check: Libanius and the law courts (npnf202 17130-17156), stern and irritable (17238-17246), the Eutropius ultimatum (17095-17110), the Palladius finance reform (Moore 3740-3778), offense to men in office (17317-17323), the Origen pretext (17834-17847), the Oak (18280-18303), the recall (18338-18346), Eudoxia, the second council and the June 404 exile (18425-18503), and the journey and death (Moore 6388-6412, 6436-6448, 6483-6502; npnf202 18605-18612).

1. BLOCKING. "Theophilus held a grudge because John had kept his favorite, Isidore, out of the see." The source does not say John kept Isidore out. Socrates says Theophilus's grudge came from "his own failure to place his favorite presbyter Isidore in the episcopal chair" (npnf202 17378-17381), and that "the court however gave the preference to John" (17097). The brief turns the court's choice into an act of John's. Fix: "Theophilus held a grudge because his own favorite, Isidore, had lost the see to John."
2. BLOCKING (minor). "In February of 398 the court chose him as bishop." February 398 is the date he was consecrated: "invested with the episcopal dignity on the 26th of February ... 398" (npnf202 17108-17110). Socrates does not date the court's choice. Fix: "In February of 398 he was made bishop of Constantinople, the court's own choice."
3. STANDARD. A modern editor is used as a witness: "Philip Schaff counts twenty-one sermons from those weeks." The paragraph also never says what John did in the crisis, or that he was a priest and preacher at all. That is his defining trait, and it is supported. Socrates says he was "ordained a presbyter" (npnf202 17236-17238). Schaff says that through those months he "delivered series of extempore orations, in which he comforted the people and exhorted them to correct their vices", and that Bishop Flavian's journey to the emperor saved the city (npnf109 1250-1262). Fix: tell it directly. For example: "John, by then a priest of the city, preached to the frightened crowds through those weeks, comforting them and urging them to change their lives, until old Bishop Flavian won the emperor's pardon." Drop the name Schaff and the sermon count.
4. STANDARD, for the ear. "Socrates, the cooler witness, adds ..." is a comparison the listener cannot follow, because Palladius is not introduced until the next paragraph. Together with "Socrates says" (four times) and "Palladius says" (three times), the brief starts to sound like a citation list. Fix: introduce the two witnesses once, as a pair, where they first disagree in tone. For example: "Palladius, who stood by him to the end, and the more distant Socrates ..." After that, attribute only where the witnesses diverge.
5. STANDARD. One sentence runs to 33 words: "The people rose, and the emperor called him back, but after a clash ... sent him away." Split it at "but".

## Methodius of Olympus — REVISE

The claims check. Jerome (npnf203 41093-41107) does say Olympus and then Tyre, martyrdom at Chalcis, and "as others affirm" Decius and Valerian. Clark (anf06 29754-29776) says Olympus and Patara, about 312, "incorrect" for Decius, and that the Banquet is the only complete work. The Banquet frame (29898-29933), Photius (35862-35870) and the fig tree (35380-35403) all check. The source block is a fragment from the Discourse on the Resurrection, so "he writes" is fair.

1. STANDARD. A modern translator is used as a story witness three times: "William Clark, who translated his works, says ...", "which Clark dates to about the year 312", and "Clark answers that this cannot be so". This is the "the translator" problem with a name attached. It also makes the whole first paragraph a list of who-says-what about sees and dates. Fix: keep Jerome as the ancient witness. Tell the rest directly and move Clark into the notes. For example: "He was bishop of Olympus in Lycia, and Jerome adds that he later held Tyre. Jerome says he died a martyr at Chalcis at the end of the last great persecution, around 312. He knew others who put it fifty years earlier, under Decius, but that cannot be right, because Methodius wrote against Origen after Origen's death." The Olympus-and-Patara point can be dropped, since even Clark's support for it is Jerome, who says only Olympus (anf06 29757-29759).
2. STANDARD. Something the listener will expect is missing, and an ancient witness supports it. The brief's main theological thread is Methodius against Origen, but it leaves out the best story about that rivalry. Socrates names "Methodius, bishop of a city in Lycia named Olympus" first among Origen's "revilers". He then says that Methodius, "when he had in various places railed against Origen, afterwards as if retracting all he had previously said, expresses his admiration of the man, in a dialogue which he entitled Xenon" (npnf202 18141-18156). Jerome also lists On the Resurrection as "against Origen" (npnf203 41098-41100), which is a better anchor for "argued with Origen" than the inference from Photius alone. Fix: add both, told as Socrates's account, since Socrates was a partisan of Origen (he calls the critics "worthless characters", 18138-18140). Say so in story terms. There is room: the brief is at 226 words.
3. STANDARD. The last sentence runs to 38 words because of the quote. Fix: introduce the quote with its own short sentence, or trim the quote at the clause boundary after "uninjured and immortal" and note the trim. Ending on "while sin is utterly and entirely destroyed" is a strong close, so the first option is better.

## Ephrem the Syrian — REVISE

The claims check. Gwynn's facts (npnf213 10605-10630) cover Nisibis, Jacob, the three sieges, Jovian in 363, the cell on the Mount, and 373 or 378. Sozomen and Theodoret on Harmonius (npnf202 32083-32105; npnf203 15055-15068) check, as do Ephrem's own attribution to Bardaisan (npnf213 11234-11246), Jerome on public reading (npnf203 41535-41545), and Palladius on the famine (palladius_lausiac-history_clarke1918.txt 471).

1. STANDARD. A modern editor is used as a story witness three times: "Gwynn, who wrote the long introductory essay ... says early and trustworthy evidence shows ...", "Gwynn adds that Ephrem himself blames Bardaisan", and "Gwynn warns that they hold things hard to believe". Fix: tell all three directly and keep Gwynn in the notes. "He lived through three Persian sieges of the city." "In one of his own hymns Ephrem lays the blame on Bardaisan himself." For the late Lives, either drop the point or say directly that the later Lives add wonders the early witnesses do not tell.
2. BLOCKING (misleading). "There he taught and wrote against rival teachers, above all Bardaisan," followed by "of that rival he wrote". The listener will take Bardaisan for a living opponent in Edessa. In fact his teaching "had been originated about the year 200 by a Syrian, Bardesan" (npnf213 11184-11186), a century and a half before Ephrem reached Edessa. Theodoret's note gives Bardaisan's birth as 155 (npnf203 15058-15060). Fix: "... against the teachings that rivaled the church's in Edessa, above all those of Bardaisan, a Syrian teacher who had lived some hundred and fifty years before," and "of Bardaisan he wrote".
3. BLOCKING (minor). "Sozomen tells it the same way" overstates the agreement. Palladius says he "died after a month" (Palladius 471). Sozomen says "after the lapse of a few days, he expired" (npnf202 32160-32162). Fix: "Sozomen tells much the same story," or keep the month and note that Sozomen says a few days.
4. STANDARD. The brief has almost no theology. Mark asked for what each person fought for and defended. The brief says only "the church's teaching" against Bardaisan. The Library supports more. Gwynn names the fronts: "Manichean and Marcionite, as well as Arian" (npnf213 11182-11184). Sozomen says Harmonius's songs carried Greek philosophical ideas "concerning the soul, the generation and destruction of the body, and the regeneration", and that Ephrem answered with hymns "in accordance with the doctrines of the Church". He adds: "From that period the Syrians sang the odes of Ephraim" (npnf202 32090-32101). Theodoret says the songs gave hearers "at once great delight and a healing medicine" and were "still used to enliven the festivals of our victorious martyrs" (npnf203 15063-15066). Fix: say plainly what the contest was about, using Sozomen's terms, and let Sozomen's or Theodoret's line on the hymns' lasting use carry "why it mattered".
5. STANDARD. The ending is flat: "Most writers date his death to 373, though some think 378." It closes on a dating dispute. Fix: end on the famine and his death in his cell, which is already the emotional peak, and move the 373-or-378 point earlier or drop it, since the card shows dates. Palladius gives Ephrem a line in his own voice that would land well aloud: "What do you think of me?" ... "Then trust me ... See, on your behalf I appoint myself hospitaller" (Palladius 471). That could also replace the Gwynn-translated hymn line. The hymn line, "He blended for the simple poison with sweetness," has an inverted word order that is hard to follow by ear.
6. STANDARD. Two sentences run to 35 words: "Gwynn adds ... sweetness." and "Ephrem left his cell ... 300 beds." Split each. Also say "some three hundred beds" in words for the narrator.
