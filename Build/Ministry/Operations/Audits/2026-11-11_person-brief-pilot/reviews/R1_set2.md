## Gregory of Nyssa — REVISE

Score: words 264, FK 8.9, FRE 64.3, in band. Length fine.
Quote: "the advance has no check" — MATCH (NY:44061, one occurrence).

1. BLOCKING — overstatement of the Eunomius answer. Brief: "no one can ever know his name." NY:199-200: "God is really incomprehensible, and that here we can never know His name." The word "here" (in this life) is dropped, turning a claim about our present knowledge into an absolute one. Fix: "that in this life we can never know his name."

2. BLOCKING — the restoration point is tilted. Brief: "though they cite sermons on eternal fire that hint at no such ending." NY:1525-1538: the passages "have been adduced" (by others, with a footnote), and the editors at once discount them: "the hortatory character of these treatises makes them less important as witnesses." The brief turns the editors' rebuttal into their own counter-evidence. Fix together with finding 4: tell the doctrine through Gregory's own dialogue, then say plainly that some of his sermons speak of eternal fire with no hint of it (NY:1529-1535, De Pauperibus Amandis), so readers have argued over what he meant.

3. BLOCKING (minor) — the quote's subject is shifted. Brief: "the soul can move toward the good, and there 'the advance has no check.'" NY:44050-44061: the subject is the changeableness of man as an image of God ("alteration is necessarily observable in man ... alteration is a kind of movement ever advancing"). Fix: "because we were made changeable, he writes, we can keep moving toward the good, and there 'the advance has no check.'"

4. STANDARD — modern editors used as story witnesses, three times: "Clarke, who translated his Life of Macrina, writes that he apparently showed no special promise"; "Moore and Wilson say his sister Macrina won him back"; "Moore and Wilson read him as teaching..." This is the "his editor" problem with names attached. Ancient witnesses exist for each:
   - No special promise: drop Clarke and use Macrina's own words to him on her deathbed, as Gregory records them (CL:218): "you that have little or no equipment within yourself for such success" — she says his fame comes from their parents' prayers. A strong, personal, sourced moment.
   - Rhetoric and the turn back: Gregory of Nazianzus's own letter of rebuke (NZ:46240-46262): he asks why Gregory "preferred to be called a Professor of Rhetoric rather than of Christianity." Use Nazianzus as the witness instead of "which disgusted his friend" via Clarke. Macrina's persuasion can then be told directly (source stays in notes, NY:651-655).
   - Restoration: tell it through Gregory's dialogue On the Soul and the Resurrection, where Macrina argues that "those who have parted with evil will be united with Him; and so ... God will be 'all in all'" (NY:39688-39690), and then name the disagreement (finding 2).

5. STANDARD — the Council of 381 is missing and the last paragraph is muddled. The brief jumps from the incomprehensibility point to "In 381 the emperor named him a bishop whose communion marked the Catholic church" — confusing to the ear, and it skips what the listener would expect: he was one of the 150 bishops at the Council of Constantinople, brought his treatise against Eunomius, preached the funeral oration for Meletius (NY:864-879). The same sentence then joins an unrelated quote with "and in the Great Catechism." Fix: give the council its own sentence or two; say the emperor's decree named him one of the bishops "regarded as the central authorities of Catholic Communion" (NY:880-883) in plain words, e.g. "one of the bishops by whose communion the emperor would judge who was orthodox"; give the quote its own landing.

6. STANDARD — the theology is thin on what he defended. The Eunomius quarrel is told only as "unbegotten is the essence" against "God is incomprehensible." The editors' summary (NY:185-195) supports the core point the listener needs: Eunomius "dug a gulf for ever ... between the Father and the Son," and Gregory defended the Son's "eternal generation." One sentence on this, said directly.

## Gregory of Nazianzus — REVISE

Score: words 266, FK 8.7, FRE 60.3, in band (FRE at the floor). Length fine.
Quote: "For that which He has not assumed He has not healed; but that which is united to His Godhead is also saved." — MATCH (NZ:44690-44692; the file has an NBSP before "For", whitespace only).

1. BLOCKING (minor) — "Gregory met Basil as a student in Athens." NZ:22612-22615: Basil joined him there "when the early acquaintance which was now renewed soon deepened into an intimacy." They already knew each other. Fix: "Gregory and Basil, who had known each other as boys, met again as students in Athens."

2. STANDARD — translators used as story witnesses: "Browne and Swallow, who translated his orations and letters, tell how Gregory spared the newcomer the rough hazing" and "Browne and Swallow think he probably never governed the place."
   - The hazing story is Gregory's own. The Prolegomena says it is a summary of the funeral oration (NZ:22617-22621), and the oration itself is in the file: "I not only refused to put to shame my friend the great Basil ... but also persuaded all the rest of the students to treat him likewise" (NZ:41422-41427), followed by "This was the prelude of our friendship. This was the kindling spark of our union" (NZ:41432-41433). Fix: tell it in Gregory's voice from the funeral speech; this also ties in the "one soul in two bodies" line (NZ:41521-41522) as one telling, not two.
   - Sasima: tell it directly with the reason the file gives: Anthimus held the village with troops, and his father begged him to stay at Nazianzus, so he likely never served there (NZ:22992-22997). The hedge "probably" can stay; the translators' names should go.

3. STANDARD — the Constantinople story leaves out what the listener expects and makes the resignation unclear. The brief never says he became archbishop of the capital: Theodosius put him in possession of the cathedral of Saint Sophia, and the council confirmed him and enthroned him there (NZ:23163-23168). "So he resigned" then has no clear object. Fix: one clause, "the council enthroned him as archbishop of the city," then "he gave up the see and went home." Optionally the reason the Egyptians gave, a canon against moving bishops between sees (NZ:23177-23179), in plain words.

Otherwise sound: father's ordination against his will, flight to Pontus, Anastasia, Five Orations, Meletius, Cledonius, Ephesus and Chalcedon all check out against the cited lines. The ending lands well on his own voice.

## Bede of Jarrow — REVISE

Score: words 228, FK 9.0, FRE 63.9, in band. Length fine for the sources.
Quote: "I always took delight in learning, teaching, and writing." — MATCH (BH:15428-15429).

1. BLOCKING (minor) — "He wrote his History of the English church for King Ceolwulph." BH:117-121: "I formerly, at your request, most readily transmitted to you the Ecclesiastical History ... which I had newly published ... and I now send it again." The king asked to read it; the file does not say Bede wrote it for him. Fix: "When King Ceolwulph asked to read his History of the English church, Bede sent it to him, and he opens it by naming his helpers."

2. STANDARD — "He is no neutral guide to the quarrel over Easter" is the narrator's verdict, and it leaves out what Bede says about himself in the same passage. BH:6985-6991: he detests Aidan's Easter "but, like an impartial historian, relating what was done." Fix (more faithful and better story): "He calls himself an impartial historian, and of Bishop Aidan, who kept the old Easter, he writes with love, yet he says plainly that he detests that error."

3. STANDARD — the Easter quarrel is never explained, so a listener cannot follow it. "Kept the Easter his elders had given him" and "the old Easter" leave the ear with nothing. The file gives the two appeals in story terms: Colman said his Easter came from his elders and was the one St. John the Evangelist kept (BH:7985-7993); Oswy opened by saying those who serve one God should keep one rule (BH:7975-7983); the king's reason for choosing Peter is vivid and in Bede's own pages: "lest, when I come to the gates of the kingdom of heaven, there should be none to open them" (BH:8200-8206). Fix: name John against Peter, and give Oswy his own reason in his own words. Abbess Hilda presided and sided with the Scots (BH:7958-7966); one clause would add a real named person.

4. STANDARD — flat ending. The last paragraph is a one-sentence death notice tacked on, and the date and Gospel of John rest on the unsigned editor's notice (BH:15610-15617), not on anything Cuthbert says in the file. "Cuthbert ... described Bede's death on Ascension Day" fuses the two. Fix: either say directly "He died on Ascension Day in 735, just after finishing a translation of the Gospel of John" and "his pupil Cuthbert wrote an account of his last hours" as two plain facts (sources in notes), and fold it into a closing paragraph that also carries his years of work (deacon at nineteen, priest at thirty, writing until his fifty-ninth year, BH:15429-15434, already sourced in the notes) so the ending lands on the life, not a bare notice.

## Constantine the Great — REVISE

Score: words 262, FK 9.6, FRE 62.5, in band. Length fine.
Quotes: "The time is arrived which I have long hoped for, with an earnest desire and prayer that I might obtain the salvation of God." — MATCH (E:67792-67794). "Conquer by this", "fellow-servant" — MATCH.

1. BLOCKING — the opening states the shields as shared fact. Brief: "Before the battle at the Milvian Bridge, Constantine had a sign put on his soldiers' shields, and our two witnesses tell it differently." Only Lactantius has shields (L:10620). In Eusebius the dream tells him to make a likeness of the sign for use in war, and at dawn he has goldsmiths make it as a standard (E:61274-61290); and Eusebius sets the vision on the march, "on this expedition" (E:61206-61207), not at the bridge. Fix: open on the coming battle, then: Lactantius says a dream told him to mark the sign of Christ on his soldiers' shields; Eusebius says Constantine himself told him, years later and under oath, of a cross of light at midday, then a dream, and a standard of gold and jewels made to that pattern.

2. STANDARD — typo: "the cipher of Christ,." (stray comma before the period). Also a mismatch with the notes, which say Eusebius's "hard to believe" was cut; it is in the brief (it checks out at E:61193-61195; fix the notes, keep the line).

3. STANDARD — what the listener most expects is missing. The brief never says he won the battle, and never mentions the end of persecution. Lactantius carries both: the battle and its outcome (L:10616-10630 onward), and the edict Constantine and Licinius issued for freedom of worship (L:10684 onward, "When we, Constantine and Licinius, emperors, had an interview..."). One or two sentences. Without it, the story of the vision has no consequence.

4. STANDARD — Nicaea has no Arius in it. The brief tells of the letter calling the quarrel trifling and of homoousios, but never says what Arius taught or what was decided, so "no division in God" floats. Fix: one sentence on the question at stake, said directly (sources stay in notes), before the homoousios clause. Also: "Eusebius says ..." in paragraph one and "Eusebius of Caesarea told his own church" in paragraph two are the same man, and the ear may hear two; say "Eusebius, the bishop of Caesarea," once at first mention, then "Eusebius."

5. STANDARD — the Crispus story is misplaced. Sozomen's chapter (S:26625-26651) is about why Constantine became a Christian at all, not about his deathbed baptism, but the brief sets it between the baptism request and his last words, so the ear takes it as the reason he sought baptism. Fix: move it to the conversion part, after the vision accounts ("Pagans later told another story ... Sozomen calls it an invention"), and let the last paragraph run straight from Helenopolis to his own words. It would also help to say that he had waited until he was dying to be baptized and had hoped to be baptized in the Jordan (E:67794-67796), which makes his words land.

## Hugo Grotius — REVISE

Score: words 242, FK 9.3, FRE 61.6, in band. Length fine for the sources.
Quote: "that sin should not be thought of less importance, if so many great sins should be remitted without an example." — MATCH (G:6853-6855; front-trimmed at a clause boundary, as the notes say).

1. BLOCKING — witnesses misnamed. (a) The King James remark is not Calder's report; Calder is quoting Le Vassor, who tells it (C:6220-6238, "writes Le Vassor ... said the king to Grotius"). (b) The arrest on "the nineteenth of August" by "the Lieutenant of the Princes guard" is Calder quoting Cross, whom Calder calls "prejudiced in favour of the Calvinists" (C:15464-15481); Calder's own sentence (C:15556-15557) says only that Grotius was arrested "by a similar stratagem." Fix: name Le Vassor for the king, and either name Cross or tell the arrest directly.

2. BLOCKING (minor, ear-confusing) — "Calder ... reports that King James told him he would think better..." The "him" reads as Calder. The file says the king said it to Grotius during audiences in England (C:6232-6238). Fix: "King James told Grotius..."

3. STANDARD — the brief is carried almost entirely by modern writers as story witnesses: Calder (1838) three times, Mosheim, and Foster ("Foster, who translated the treatise, says..."), and it ends on the translator. This is the "the translator" problem with a name attached.
   - Opening: replace the flat "Hugo Grotius was a jurist, and his writing on the law of nations was prized in England" (Foster, G:1270-1274) with the sourced England scene from Le Vassor: Grotius in England on East India Company business, "in great esteem with all the learned men in that island," defending the Remonstrants before the bishops, and the king telling him in an audience that Calvin and Beza's views were "very harsh," that on their view "God would be the author of sin," and yet he wanted proof the Remonstrants had no leaning to Socinianism (C:6215-6238). This is personal, theological, and sets up the 1617 treatise.
   - Imprisonment and escape: tell directly (sources in notes). Calder himself calls the chest story "too well known to be repeated" (C:16625-16628); he is no witness to it. Mosheim, writing a century later, adds that he does not know on what pretext Grotius was condemned (D:4581-4583). Either say "he was condemned to prison for life" directly, or keep Mosheim and include his admission, which is the more honest telling.
   - Reception: drop "Foster, who translated the treatise, says..." and say directly that the leading Dutch Remonstrants rejected it while English Arminians largely took it up, and that it came to be called the governmental theory of the atonement and reached New England through Samuel Hopkins (G:913-914, 1281-1282, 2017-2021). Then end on Grotius's own voice or on that fact, not on a translator.

4. STANDARD — "on the nineteenth of August" with no year sounds odd aloud, and the date is a hostile witness's. Drop the day, or give a year only if a file states it.

The atonement paragraph itself is accurate and well told (G:4590-4606, 4764-4770, 2517-2526, 6848-6858 all check out).
