Around the year two hundred, in Alexandria, Origen's father was arrested for his faith. Origen was a boy, and Eusebius says he was seized by such a longing for martyrdom that he rushed toward danger. His mother hid his clothes to keep him home, so he wrote to his father in prison. Eusebius kept one sentence of that letter: "Take heed not to change your mind on our account."

His father died a martyr, and the state took the family's property. Origen was not yet seventeen. In his eighteenth year he took charge of teaching the faith in Alexandria, because everyone else had fled.

He taught that Scripture, like a person, has body, soul and spirit. In On First Principles he says the plain story builds up simple believers, its soul those who have advanced, and its spiritual law the perfect. The book also taught that the Son's generation is as eternal as brightness from the sun, and that souls existed before birth. We read it mostly in Rufinus's Latin, and Jerome said Rufinus changed it.

But on a journey through Palestine, the bishops of Caesarea and Jerusalem ordained Origen a priest. Jerome says this so enraged his own bishop, Demetrius, that he wrote everywhere to ruin Origen's name. In the year 231 Origen left Alexandria for Caesarea. About twenty years later, under the emperor Decius, he was stretched in the stocks. Jerome says he died at Tyre, aged sixty-nine.

Gregory of Nazianzus later sent a friend a book of passages from Origen, which he and Basil are thought to have gathered. Socrates, who defended Origen, says Athanasius often cited him in support of his own faith. Three centuries after his death, a council at Constantinople named him among condemned heretics, though how far it went is still argued.
