In 379 Gregory came to Constantinople, where the Arians held the churches. He lodged with a relative, and part of the house became a chapel that he named Anastasia, for the faith that was to rise again. There he preached the five orations that earned him the name of the Theologian. They defend the Trinity against those who blurred the three Persons together and those who split the one substance. The emperor Theodosius gave him the cathedral, and the council of 381 made him archbishop of the city. When its president Meletius died, Gregory took the chair. Egyptian bishops objected, so he gave up the see and went home.

His closest friend was Basil, whom he had known as a boy and met again as a student in Athens. In his funeral speech for Basil, Gregory says he refused to haze the newcomer, and that this was the kindling spark of their friendship. Gregory's father, the bishop of Nazianzus, ordained him a priest against his will, and Gregory fled to Basil in Pontus before returning. Years later Basil made him bishop of Sasima, a miserable little village, almost by force. Gregory was angry. Another bishop held the village with troops, and his father begged him to stay home, so he likely never served there.

He wrote two letters to the priest Cledonius against Apollinarius, who denied that Christ had a human mind. The councils of Ephesus and Chalcedon later adopted them, and Jerome, who heard him preach, called him his teacher in the Scriptures. Gregory wrote: "For that which He has not assumed He has not healed; but that which is united to His Godhead is also saved."
