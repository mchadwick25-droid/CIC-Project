## The quote
File AN, Methodius, From the Discourse on the Resurrection, Part I, section V, lines 35399-35402 (line breaks joined with spaces).
Printed: "in order that the flesh, after sin is withered and dead, may, like a restored temple, be raised up again with the same parts, uninjured and immortal, while sin is utterly and entirely destroyed."
Used: "the flesh, after sin is withered and dead, may, like a restored temple, be raised up again with the same parts, uninjured and immortal, while sin is utterly and entirely destroyed." — drops the leading "in order that" only; ends at the printed full stop.

## Contested points and how marked
- Sees: Jerome (Olympus, then Tyre) vs Clark (Olympus and Patara together). Told as a plain disagreement.
- Martyrdom: Jerome gives the end of the last persecution or, as "others affirm", under Decius and Valerian; Clark rejects the second and also notes some place Chalcis in Syria (AN:29764-29766), not mentioned in the brief for length.
- On Free Will: not mentioned (Clark: authorship doubtful).
