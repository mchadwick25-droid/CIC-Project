In 399, the emperor's chief eunuch, Eutropius, fled into the cathedral of Constantinople, though he had just stripped refugees of the right to shelter there. As he lay trembling under the altar table, Bishop John climbed into the pulpit and preached against him. Socrates says some thought John added insult to cruelty. John himself said he spoke to soften the people's hearts toward him, for the church, like a mother, had hidden her enemy under her cloak.

Years before, a tax riot in Antioch tore down the emperor's statues. John, by then a priest, preached to the frightened crowds, comforting them and urging them to change their lives, until old Bishop Flavian won the emperor's pardon.

In February of 398 John had been made bishop, the court's own choice, and Eutropius had made Theophilus of Alexandria ordain him. Palladius, who stood by John to the end, says he found the bishop's expenses extravagant and moved that money to the hospital. John told the rich: "Wouldest thou do honor to Christ’s body? Neglect Him not when naked."

But Theophilus held a grudge, because his own favorite, Isidore, had lost the see to John. Socrates says Theophilus used a quarrel over Origen's books against him. In 403 the bishops at the Oak cited John four times. He refused them as his enemies, and they deposed him for refusing. The people rose, and he was recalled. After a clash with the empress Eudoxia, a second council silenced him, and in 404 an imperial decree sent him away. He died on the road to the Black Sea in 407, Palladius says, hurried along by soldiers who hoped he would not survive.

In 438 Bishop Proclus carried John's body back to Constantinople in procession, and John's followers returned to the church.
