## The quote
File GW, quoting Ephrem's metrical Homily LIII, Against Heretics, in Gwynn's translation, lines 11241-11244 (the run starts at 11241, the used sentence is at 11243-11244) (line breaks joined with spaces).
Printed: "He fashioned hymns, and joined them with tunes; and composed psalms, and brought in moods. By weights and measures, he portioned language. He blended for the simple poison with sweetness."
Used: "He blended for the simple poison with sweetness." — the last full sentence of that run, nothing altered. Source note: the words are about Bardaisan (Gwynn at GW:11237-11239 attributes the hymn-making to Bardesan), and the brief says "of that rival he wrote".

## Contested points and how marked
- Who set the false doctrine to tunes: Sozomen and Theodoret say Bardaisan's son Harmonius; Ephrem himself, per Gwynn, says Bardaisan. Told as the disagreement it is.
- Famine story: Palladius is the earliest witness the brief names; Sozomen agrees; the later Syriac and Greek Lives expand and Gwynn doubts them. The brief says so and tells only the Palladius core.
- Date of death 373 vs 378, told as "most writers ... some".

## Dropped (coordinator checklist)
- Where he was ordained and by whom: dropped. Gwynn (GW:10622-10625) says Basil gave him the diaconate, but this is tangled with the later Lives; Palladius (PA:471) calls him "the deacon of the Church of Edessa". Not told.
- School at Edessa after 363 (Barhadbeshabba): not in the files I read; not claimed.
- The siege hymns' content: the Nisibene Hymns are in GW:13699 onward and Gwynn assigns the first twenty-one to the third siege (GW:11229-11232); not used in the brief beyond the sieges themselves.
- Sozomen's story of Ephrem feigning madness to avoid being made a bishop (SZ:32127-32136): left out for length.
- The gnats-and-Persians miracle (GW:10795-10820) is in the late Life; dropped.
