# Dossier: the Colloquy of Regensburg (Ratisbon), 1541

Research dossier for the era 7 council profile. The subject is the religious colloquy held inside the imperial Diet of Regensburg, spring and summer 1541: the six theologians, the Book they worked from, the articles agreed and not agreed, the recess. All paths are under `cic/texts/`. Line numbers are file lines. Translations from Latin and Italian are mine and marked "(my tr.)". OCR is quoted as it stands in the file, with a normalized reading in brackets where the OCR is broken.

**The one-line verdict.** No act of the colloquy is held: not the Regensburg Book, not the agreed article on justification, not the recess. What is held is strong all the same: Calvin's own letters written in the city (participant, Protestant), Peter Faber's letter from the city (participant at the diet, Catholic, Jesuit), Pope Paul III's own account in the 1542 bull calling Trent (the Curia's voice), and two long rival histories, Sarpi (anti-curial) and Pallavicino (written to answer him). That carries a full profile, provided the brief says early that the colloquy's own papers are not held.

## 2. Candidate opening scenes

**A. The legate rides in, 12 March 1541 (Faber, quoted by editors; Calvin)**
- IGN22 l.7027-7029 (editors quoting Faber to Ignatius, "12 Martii 1541"): «Esta tarde ha hecho su entrada en esta ciudad el Legado Reverendísimo Contareno.» (my tr.: "This afternoon the Most Reverend Legate Contarini made his entry into this city.")
- CLET1 l.10975-10978 (Calvin, 28 March): "The Cardinal Contarini is legate from the Pope, who has distributed so many crosses for us at his first entrance, that for two days afterwards his arm, I think, must have felt the fatigue of it."
- CLET1 l.10979-10983: "Contarini is desirous of bringing us under the yoke of subjection without bloodshed ; on that account he tries all methods of settling the business on the ground of expediency, without having recourse to arms."
- Strength: two sides on one moment, each in its own voice. Weakness: the Faber line reaches us only through the MHSI editors' note.

**B. The emperor gives the six his hand (Calvin, 24 April)**
- CLET1 l.11619-11622: "On the 20th of this month he appointed, on the part of our adversaries, Julius Pflug, John Gropper of Cologne, and John Eck; on our side, Philip Melanchthon, Martin Bucer, and John Pistorius."
- CLET1 l.11629-11636: "they were kindly received by the Emperor, who gave them his hand on their first meeting, and when they took their leave. By an interpreter the Emperor addressed to them a very solemn exhortation ... That, therefore, they should have the glory of God, the public peace, the salvation of all nations as their aim".
- CLET1 l.11681-11684: "when I begin to hope, then I remember what took place at Worms."

**C. The Protestants vote on the Supper, and Calvin speaks in Latin (Calvin, 11 May)**
- CLET1 l.11880-11883: "There stood the impassable rock which barred the way to farther progress. Transubstantiation, reposition, circumgestation, and other superstitious forms of worship, were utterly rejected."
- CLET1 l.11896-11900: "I had also to explain in Latin what were my sentiments. Although I had not understood any one of the others, deliberately, without fear of offence, I condemned that peculiar local presence ; the act of adoration I declared to be altogether insufferable."
- Note: this was a consultation of the Protestant side ("Our friends having consulted, they called us together", l.11889-11890), not a session of the colloquy.

**D. Granvelle hands the legate the Book (Pallavicino)**
- PALL l.27495-27497: "fè portar dal Granuela vn libro al Legato, compollo [composto], com'egli affermò, da perfone pie e dotte" (my tr.: "he had Granvelle carry to the Legate a book, composed, as he affirmed, by pious and learned persons"). A phrase after it (l.27498, "E fl, spaio ili Fiandra") is OCR-broken; it may mention Flanders, but I could not read it with confidence.
- PALL l.27516-27522: the legate read it with the nuncio and Gropper; the first two guessed from various signs that Gropper was the author; Contarini found "piu di venti luoghi" (more than twenty places) needing correction, and Gropper corrected them without a word of contradiction.

## 3. Issue

- The emperor's stated aim (Sarpi, later narrative): SARPI l.15654-15658: "That began in March 1541. The Emperour was personally present, with great hope to end all Discords, and to Unite [the] Religion". The emperor's proposition: a "small number of godly and learned men might be chosen on either side, to confer upon the Controversies, in friendly manner" (SARPI l.15749-15752). PALL l.27353-27360 gives the same proposition in Italian, with the reservation "salua sempre la conclusione e la costituzione d'Augusta" (always saving the Augsburg recess).
- The emperor's own pressures (Calvin, participant): CLET1 l.11143-11147: "the mind of the Emperor ... is altogether turned to peace, if he can obtain it, so that, putting off the consideration of religion to another time, he may direct all his energies to this object". He feared the Turk and France (CLET1 l.11031-11034).
- The Protestant aim (Calvin): CLET1 l.11100-11106: "as they do not expect there will be any secure or lasting peace, unless there is a settled agreement in religion, and the churches restored to order and right discipline, they will strongly urge that the states of the Empire may seriously set about this undertaking."
- The Curia's aim, in its own words afterwards (Paul III's bull, WAT l.11935-11941): the scheme "of procuring and treating of a reunion in those diets seemed only adapted to excite greater discord, we were led to revert to our former remedy, a general council ; and ... we proposed this to the emperor himself ; and this we did especially and finally in the diet of Ratisbon".
- The legate's instructions, told by both historians, in conflict (see 11, point 1): SARPI l.15665-15677; PALL l.27098-27120.
- Background: the colloquy continued the conference of Worms. SARPI l.15649-15651: "Ecchius and Melanthon having spoken three days, the Conference was interrupted, For Letters came from the Emperour, which recalled Granvel, and referred the rest to the Diet in Ratisbon." Bonnet's note CLET1 l.10457: "The Conferences at Worms had been transferred to Ratisbon by a decree of the Emperor." Faber's own letter places the three-day disputation on original sin at Worms (FAB l.13104-13120; "Wormatia profecti sumus Spiram", l.13121-13122); Boero puts it at Spires (BOER l.2690-2697). Small conflict; Faber, the witness, wins on this one.

## 4. Called by

- The diet: the emperor Charles V, present in person (SARPI l.15654; CLET1 l.10908-10909, "The Emperor has been waiting hitherto for the arrival of the princes").
- The colloquy: the emperor proposed it; the estates left him the choice of men. CLET1 l.11614-11618: "The Emperor was allowed to choose a few pious and experienced men ... who ... might deliberate upon the means of agreement, and afterwards submit to the approval of the states the heads of agreement". PALL l.27449-27458: the choice was given to the emperor first by the Protestants and the free cities, then by the princes; "Cefare commife la fcelta alla Dieta ... la rimife all'Imperadore" (the emperor committed the choice to the diet ... it handed it back to the emperor); he named the men "con partecipazion del Legato" (with the legate's participation).
- The pope sent the legate at the emperor's request (SARPI l.15656-15666; PALL l.26975-26986).

## 5. Attended

- **The six collocutors.** Three and three, the same names in three witnesses: CLET1 l.11620-11622 (Calvin); PALL l.27459-27468 ("Giouanni Echio e Giulio Flugio ... e Giouanni Gruppero Arcidiacono di Colonia"; "Filippo Melantone, Martino Bucero ... e Giouanni Pistorio" [OCR "Pilioriok"]); BOER l.2942-2945. Sarpi's list is lost in an OCR gap after "For the Catholicks he Elected John [OCR 'Fohy Fethi-']" (SARPI l.15767), and his text resumes in the middle of the next topic (l.15778).
- **Presidents.** PALL l.27468-27470: two presidents, "il Granuela" (the emperor's minister) and "Federigo Palatino fratello dell'Elettore". Calvin, earlier: the emperor "would fix upon one of the princes, who might preside" (CLET1 l.11623-11624). Faber's 12 May letter? Not stated.
- **Witnesses (auditors).** PALL l.27476-27477: "Sei furono assegnati per Vditori, i più di loro Vfficiali di varij Principi" (my tr.: "Six were assigned as hearers, most of them officials of various princes"). Calvin: the Protestant side asked "that a greater number of witnesses might be present" (CLET1 l.11624-11626).
- **Legate and nuncios.** Contarini, legate (CLET1 l.10975; SARPI l.15665; PALL l.26976-26977). The bishop of Modena as nuncio, "son of Jerome Morone" (CLET1 l.10978; Calvin names him only as "the Bishop of Modena" and "He of Modena"); Boero adds a second nuncio, "John Pozzi" (BOER l.2938-2940).
- **Princes and envoys at the diet** (not the colloquy), Calvin's list of 28 March: CLET1 l.10908-10921 (the two dukes of Bavaria, Henry of Brunswick, Frederick the Palatine, Otho his nephew, the young duke of Württemberg, the Landgrave, the archbishop of Mainz, Albert of Baden, the prince of Anhalt, the Saxon ambassador; the elector of Saxony absent, excused by ambassadors); bishops, CLET1 l.11004-11008; envoys of Venice, England ("the Bishop of Winchester"), Portugal, France (CLET1 l.10986-11003). FOX7 l.48615-48620: Henry VIII sent "the said bishop of Winchester, and sir Henry Knivet knight, as joint ambassadors to the emperor, being then at a diet or council at Ratisbon".
- **Calvin** came as a deputy (CLET1 l.10340-10344: "I have been chosen as a deputy to go to the Diet of Ratisbon ... I am serving your Church, as well as that of Strasbourg, inasmuch as it is a common cause"). Pallavicino notes him too: PALL l.27393-27397 "E fpecialmente fù quiui Giouanni Caluino tuttoché di picciol nome [fin] à quell'ora" (my tr.: "And especially there was there John Calvin, though of small name until that hour").
- **Others named by Calvin as present**: Brenz, Musculus, "Freithus" (CLET1 l.11994-12008).
- **Faber** at the diet, not in the colloquy: FAB l.1089-1095; BOER l.2929-2945.
- **Counts:** no count of attendees is held, and no list of names. "About 200" in Sarpi is the 1530 hall, not this one. Only the six, two presidents and six hearers have counts, from Calvin and Pallavicino.

## 6. Dates

- Contarini's entry: 12 March 1541 (Faber, as quoted by the MHSI editors, IGN22 l.7027-7028).
- The diet "began in March 1541" (SARPI l.15654). Calvin's letter of 28 March says the emperor was still waiting for the princes (CLET1 l.11040).
- "The first Action began the fifth of April" (SARPI l.15726): the emperor's proposition.
- Collocutors appointed "On the 20th of this month" (CLET1 l.11619, letter of 24 April).
- Agreement on original sin, free will and justification: reported in Calvin's letter of 11 May (CLET1 l.11844-11860). Pallavicino's margins give dates from Contarini's letters, but the OCR is unreadable (PALL l.27545-27547 margin).
- Eck's apoplexy: by 12 May (CLET1 l.11962-11963); "Eck, as they say, is getting better" (l.12008).
- Calvin left before the end; Bonnet's note: "He took the route of Strasbourg, where he no doubt arrived toward the end of June 1541" (CLET1 l.12350-12352; Bonnet's dating).
- Recess: "the 28. of July" (SARPI l.16158). Pallavicino says the pope spoke in consistory "a' [29] di Luglio ... vn [giorno] dopo il Recesso di Cesare in Ratisbona" (PALL l.28457-28466; OCR "a' ap. di Luglio ? cìcosi vn ^ordodopo"), which fits 28 July, but the OCR is too broken to quote as a second witness with confidence. Calvin's letter of 3 August, from Strasbourg, says "The Diet concluded" (CLET1 l.12676).
- Waterworth, loosely: "the colloquy begun at Hagenau, and continued, at intervals, during the years 1541-42, at Worms and Ratisbon" (WAT l.3173-3175). His "1541-42" is his.

## 7. Decided (no acts held; told from the witnesses)

1. **The Book as basis.** Granvelle brought the legate a book (PALL l.27494-27497); it was "telTuto in gran parte con [testi] della Scrittura, e di Santi Padri; e conteneua vent[i]due capi" (PALL l.27503-27505; my tr.: "woven largely from Scripture and the holy Fathers, and it contained twenty-two chapters"). Found free of error by the legate's men, it was put to the collocutors by the emperor's command (PALL l.27542-27544). Sarpi calls it "the Book of the Concord" (SARPI l.15823) and lists its topics (l.15780-15804, partly lost in OCR). Calvin: "All our articles are appended to the book" (CLET1 l.12408-12409).
2. **Original sin and free will: agreed.** CLET1 l.11851-11855: "Our friends in the commission have come to agreement on the doctrine of original sin without any difficulty ; a discussion followed on that of free-will, which was drawn together out of the writings of Augustine ; they departed in neither of these points from ourselves."
3. **Justification: a formula agreed.** CLET1 l.11855-11859: "The debate in controversy was more keen upon the doctrine of justification. At length a formula was drawn up, which, on receiving certain corrections, was accepted on both sides." PALL l.27544-27546: "efsendo conuenute le Parti negli articoli della Giuftificazione, della Fede, dell'Opere, de' Vefcoui, e del Battefimo" (my tr.: "the parties having agreed on the articles of Justification, of Faith, of Works, of Bishops and of Baptism"), the legate reserving the pope's approval (l.27547-27549). The text of the formula is not held (Calvin enclosed it, l.11858-11861; the enclosure is not in the edition).
4. **The Church: definition agreed, power not.** CLET1 l.11872-11877: "as to the definition they agreed in opinion ; on the question of the power of the Church they began to differ. When at length they could not anyhow be reconciled, it seemed best to omit that article."
5. **The Supper: not agreed.** CLET1 l.11880-11885 (above); PALL l.27563-27572: "Mà il primo foggetto della diffenfione fù il mifterio dell'Eucariftia. Cominciarono i Luterani la contefa dal rifiutare il vocabolo di Tranfuftanziazione, come non vfitato da' Padri antichi" (my tr.: "But the first subject of dissent was the mystery of the Eucharist. The Lutherans began the quarrel by refusing the word Transubstantiation, as not used by the ancient Fathers"). Melanchthon's written statement was "rejected with harsh expressions" by Granvelle (CLET1 l.11903-11906).
6. **Later articles: mass, confession, saints, the pope, the Church's authority: not agreed.** CLET1 l.12405-12408: "In the same manner the invocation of the saints, the primacy of the Pope, and the authority of the Church, were separately discoursed and treated of; but we could come to no agreement." Sarpi's list of articles not agreed: "the ninth, of the power of the Church, the fourteenth, of the Sacrament of Penance, the eighteenth, of the Hierarchy, the 19. of the Articles determined by the Church, the 21. of single Life. In these they differed, and either Party wrote [its opinion]" (SARPI l.15806-15812).
7. **Referred to the estates.** CLET1 l.12409-12416: "The Emperor returned liberal acknowledgment and thanks to the commissioners ... He then referred the consideration of the whole to the States ... the book was offered along with the articles."
8. **The recess (28 July; text not held).** SARPI l.16158-16166: "remitting the whole Action of the Colloquy unto the general Council, or National Synod of Germany, or a Diet of the Empire ... he would intimate a Diet of the Empire within 18 Months ... He commanded the Protestants to receive no opinions but those that were agreed on [OCR: 'tq receive Ro gow opinns'], and the Bishops to reform their Churches." PALL l.28275-28286: "nel Receffo impofe l'accettazione de' fopranominati articoli fin'ad vn Concilio Ecumenico da tenerfi in Germania, promeffogli (come dicea) dal Legato" (my tr.: "in the recess he imposed acceptance of the aforesaid articles until an Ecumenical Council to be held in Germany, promised him (as he said) by the Legate"), the council in Germany being a condition "always refused by the Cardinal". Calvin, 3 Aug (CLET1 l.12676-12682): "all has been referred to an Universal Council, or, at least, to a National one, if the former cannot be soon obtained ... a new Diet is to be held eighteen months hence".
9. **Reform of the clergy.** Contarini called the bishops and exhorted them (SARPI l.15867-15880; PALL l.28088-28091).

## 8. Outcome

- **Nothing took hold (Pallavicino).** PALL l.28300-28302: "Mà nulla di ciò hebbe effetto. Peròche né l'offefe ceffarono, né fi celebrò Concilio Vniuerfale ò Nazionale in Alemagna; né quegli articoli furono riceuuti da veruna delle Parti." (my tr.: "But none of this had effect. For neither did the attacks cease, nor was a universal or national council held in Germany; nor were those articles received by either party.") Then Eck, absent from the last sessions with fever, wrote "vn'acerba confutazione del libro", and his two colleagues answered with an apology (PALL l.28302-28309); "quel libro, il qual fu teffuto per vincolo di concordia, riufcì più tofto intrigo di controuerfie" (l.28309-28311; my tr.: "that book, woven as a bond of concord, turned out rather a tangle of controversies").
- **"Passed off in smoke" (Calvin).** CLET1 l.12676-12678: "The Diet concluded very much as I had always foretold that it would ; for the whole plan of pacification passed off in smoke". But also l.12687-12689: "the adverse party will feel that they have received no slight wound, and time will make this more evident."
- **The pope turned to a council, and said so in his bull (1542).** WAT l.11943-11951: "that by the advice of that diet we were called upon to declare that certain of the articles, maintained by the dissenters from the Church, were to be tolerated until they should be examined and decided upon by an oecumenical council ; and whereas neither Christian and Catholic truth, nor our own dignity and that of the Apostolic See, would suffer us to yield this, — we chose rather to command that a proposal should be openly made, that a council should be held as soon as possible." Sarpi says the same of cause and effect, hostile to the Curia: SARPI l.15712-15722 (the diet "was the principal cause, which induced the Pope not only to consent, as before, but to use all endeavours that the Council might be Celebrated, and which assured the Protestants, that neither in Council, nor in any other place, where any Minister of the Pope was present, they could hope to obtain any thing"). Pallavicino denies the second half (see 11, point 6).
- **Contarini suspected in Rome.** SARPI l.16222-16224: "The Legate Contarini was much Caluminated in the Court of Rome, where [a suspicion] arose, that he was somewhat [inclined] to Lutheranism". PALL l.28336-28341 agrees that some in Rome found him too cold against the Lutherans and others thought he had yielded "nella fostanza de' dogmi" to an error; Pole's letter consoled him (l.28342-28348). FOX7 l.48653-48656 (1837 editors): Contarini "did not proceed with so much dogmatism as was thought proper at Rome, and accordingly fell under suspicion of heresy".
- **Reach beyond the two parties:**
  - Metz: BEZ3 l.31486-31520: citizens, "ayans entendu au mefme temps les articles conclus & paffés cefte mefme année en la Diette Impériale à Ratisbonne", petitioned the magistrates "leur accorder libre exercice de la religion fuivant la refolution de la Diette ; ce que toutesfois ne leur fut accordé" (my tr.: "having heard at the same time of the articles concluded and passed that same year in the Imperial Diet at Ratisbon ... [asked] to grant them free exercise of religion according to the resolution of the Diet; which was not granted them").

## 9. The refusing parties in their own voices

There was no condemned party. Each side refused something, and each has its own voice for at least part of it:
- **Protestant refusal of transubstantiation and of a papal council.** Calvin's own words (CLET1 l.11890-11900, l.12343-12349). The Protestant estates' answer, in Sarpi only: "as they desired a free and Christian Council in Germany, so they could not [agree] to any, where the Pope and his Ministers had power to hear and judge the Causes of Religion" (SARPI l.15937-15941, OCR damaged). Melanchthon's and Bucer's own words: not held; they reach us through Calvin (l.11884-11888, l.11966-11980) and Pallavicino (hostile, l.27706-27740, OCR badly damaged).
- **Rome's refusal of toleration.** Paul III's own bull (WAT l.11943-11951, above). Contarini's own written answers are told only by Sarpi and Pallavicino (SARPI l.15840-15866, l.16012-16030; PALL l.28073-28118); his letters are cited by Pallavicino but not held.
- **Catholic princes and bishops refusing the agreed articles.** Told only by the historians (PALL l.28140-28155; SARPI l.15823-15826).
- **Eck's refusal of the Book.** Told by Pallavicino (l.28302-28306) and, as a character, by Calvin (l.11677-11680, l.11961-11966). Eck's own confutation is not held.

## 11. Contested points

1. **Contarini's opening move with the emperor.** Sarpi: he first excused the pope for not giving him full power, because authority to decide matters of faith is "Annexed to the very Bones of the Papacy" and cannot be delegated, though he had power to agree with the Protestants if they did not deny the primacy, the sacraments as taught in Rome, and the bull of Leo (SARPI l.15678-15705). Pallavicino: Sarpi "inciampa gravemente nel primo passo" (stumbles badly at the first step): the instruction gave the legate no power at all to dispense laws and rites, so Sarpi's account runs "dirittamente opposta al primo capo della sua Istruzione" (PALL l.27098-27110; catalogue item 54, l.52975-52982).
2. **Was justification really agreed, and was it orthodox?** Calvin: accepted on both sides, and "Our friends have thus retained also the substance of the true doctrine, so that nothing can be comprehended within it which is not to be found in our writings" (CLET1 l.11858-11864). Rome (per Pallavicino): the words did not satisfy because of their ambiguity; the pope told the legate to approve nothing not Catholic in sense and free of ambiguity in words (PALL l.27655-27660). The Catholic princes: the articles were in forms "diverse dal comune parlar della Chiesa, ed ambigue; in cui erasi conceduto soverchiamente al piacer degli Eretici" (PALL l.28146-28149; my tr.: "different from the Church's common speech, and ambiguous, in which too much had been granted to the heretics' liking"). Faber, on the whole business: "valde ambigue" (FAB l.13476). Luther's verdict on the article: not held. Give both sides; settle neither.
3. **Who wrote the Book.** Pallavicino: Granvelle said "pious and learned persons"; Contarini and Morone guessed Gropper; Eck suspected "il Vicelio" [Witzel], whom he hated (PALL l.27495-27497, l.27516-27518, l.27534-27536). No other held source names the author. Calvin says nothing on authorship.
4. **Which articles split them.** Calvin: after the Supper "we could no more agree together upon any other" (CLET1 l.12343-12344). Pallavicino: the first dissent was the Eucharist (l.27563). Sarpi's list of disputed articles (9, 14, 18, 19, 21) does not name the Eucharist (SARPI l.15806-15812); but the passage sits next to an OCR gap, so this may be loss, not Sarpi's view. Flag; do not build on Sarpi's silence.
5. **Who rejected the agreed articles.** Sarpi: "The Bishops rejected absolutely the Book of the Concord ... unto whom the other Electors and Catholick Princes, desirous of Peace, agreed not" (SARPI l.15823-15826). Pallavicino: the electors wanted the agreed doctrines received until a council (l.28112-28120), but in the Catholic estates' separate writing "in quefto rifiuto conuennero folo i Principi, i Baroni, ed i Vefcoui; là doue i Deputati delle Città Cattoliche ... amarono per lo più, che i prenominati articoli s'accettaffero" (PALL l.28152-28156; my tr.: "in this refusal only the Princes, Barons and Bishops agreed; whereas the deputies of the Catholic Cities ... mostly wished the said articles accepted"). They agree on the bishops and the electors and differ on the Catholic princes.
6. **What the diet proved.** Sarpi: that nothing good could be done where the pope's ministers had a part, and it moved the pope to procure a council (SARPI l.15712-15722). Pallavicino: both claims are "false con evidenza"; Contarini worked so hard for concord that Sarpi himself says Rome rebuked him for it; and the pope had sought a council long before (PALL l.27370-27412; catalogue items 55-56, l.52983-52992). Paul III's own bull supports the link between the diet and the council (WAT l.11935-11951) and is silent on Sarpi's first claim.
7. **Contarini's written answer: clear or an oracle?** Sarpi: "not more Perspicuously than the ancient Oracles" (SARPI l.15842-15843). Pallavicino: "quant'ella fofse chiara, ciafcuno il fente" (how clear it was, everyone can feel), and the emperor bent it to his own reading (PALL l.28083-28087).
8. **The recess and the legate.** Pallavicino: the emperor told the electors that the legate shared his view that the articles be accepted until the council; the legate, surprised, published another writing saying he had told the emperor the contrary (PALL l.28101-28111). Sarpi tells the same third writing (SARPI l.16021-16030). Here they agree; the dispute is between the emperor and the legate, told by both historians.
9. **How Rome received Contarini.** Pallavicino, catalogue item 57: Sarpi says the pope received him badly; in fact the pope welcomed him at Lucca and gave him the legation of Bologna (PALL l.52993-52997; l.28402-28407). The held English Sarpi, at this point, reads: "he [gave account] of his Legation, and gave absolute Satisfaction" (SARPI l.16232-16234, OCR damaged). So Pallavicino's charge does not match the held English text; it may answer the Italian original. Report both; do not settle.
10. **The Margrave who sent to Luther.** Calvin: "the Marquis of Brandenbourg, clandestinely, not, however, without the knowledge of the Emperor, despatches one of the princes of Anhalt on an embassy to Luther" (CLET1 l.12363-12366). Bonnet's notes identify them as "Albert, Margrave of Brnndenbourg" [sic OCR] and "Without doubt, George of Anhalt" (l.12389, l.12395). Calvin's own letter of 28 March lists "the Elector of Brandenbourg is expected shortly" (l.10917), so which Brandenburg prince is meant is open. Bonnet's identifications are his; no other held source checks them. Give Calvin's words only.
11. **Gropper's death.** Bonnet's note says Gropper became a cardinal and "was put to death in a strange and unusual manner, having been strangled with the strings of his Cardinal's hat. See Bezae Icones." (CLET1 l.11644-11648). Only Bonnet says this, citing a work not held. Do not use.

## 17. Length

The held record can carry the full ceiling, about 400 words (2:30), and should not need padding. Reasons: a dated eyewitness series from inside the city (Calvin, 28 March to 3 August), a second participant from the other side (Faber), the pope's own later account (the 1542 bull), and two detailed rival histories that agree on the frame and quarrel on the meaning. Weak points that must be said, not hidden: none of the colloquy's own documents is held (the Book, the article on justification, the recess), and the Protestant collocutors' own words (Melanchthon, Bucer) and Luther's verdict reach us only through Calvin and, hostilely, Pallavicino. The brief should say once, early, that we have no papers from the table itself and that Calvin's letters and the two historians stand in for them.
