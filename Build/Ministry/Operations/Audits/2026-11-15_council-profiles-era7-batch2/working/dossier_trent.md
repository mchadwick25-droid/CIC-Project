# Dossier: the Council of Trent (1545-1563)

Research dossier for the era 7 council profile. All paths are under `cic/texts/`. Line numbers are file lines, each re-found by grep in this session. Translations marked "(my tr.)" are mine. OCR spellings are kept inside quotes (long s shows as "f" or "ſ").

Scope note: this is a long council in three periods and 25 sessions. The acts are held in full (English), so the profile can be built from the acts first. The two big held histories are opposed: Sarpi (anti-curial) and Pallavicino (written to refute him). Waterworth's long essay is a third, Catholic, 19th-century reconstruction. They are kept apart below.

## 2. Candidate opening scenes

**A. The close, 4 December 1563: the acclamations (strongest).** From the acts.
- WAT l.24021-24024: "the most illustrious and most reverend Cardinal Morone, the first Legate and President, blessing the holy Synod said : After having given thanks to God, most reverend Fathers, go in peace. They answered: Amen." (OCR "M or one".)
- WAT l.24035-24037: "The Cardinal of Lorraine. To the most blessed Pius, Pope, and our lord, pontiff of the holy and universal Church, many years and eternal memory."
- WAT l.24081-24083: "The sacred and holy oecumenical Synod of Trent : let us confess the faith thereof, let us ever keep the decrees thereof. Answer. Ever let us confess, ever keep."
- WAT l.24103-24104: "Cardinal. Anathema to all heretics. Answer. Anathema, anathema."
- Eye-witnesses, as quoted by Waterworth in a footnote (WAT l.11159-11165; the diaries themselves are NOT held): Paleotto, "Ubi vero ad acclamationes ventum est tunc ingens omnium admiratio, plausus, laetitiaque exorta est" ("When they came to the acclamations, great wonder, applause and joy broke out among all", my tr.); Servanzio's diary, "Si partirono facendo una grande allegrezza, baciandosi, et abbracciandosi stretti, et molti molte lagrime gettando per gran letitia" ("They left in great rejoicing, kissing and holding each other tight, and many shedding many tears for great joy", my tr.).
- Pallavicino agrees (T3 l.57610-57613): "si videro in ogn'intorno i volti bagnati di lagrime" ("all around, faces were seen wet with tears", my tr.).
- Sarpi's hostile colour on the same moment (SARPI l.70195-70199): Lorraine took on himself "not only to compose the acclamations, but to thunder them out also; which was generally construed for a lightness and vanity not beseeming such a Prelate". Pallavicino instead: the acclamations "furono composte dal cardinal di Loreno a foggia degli antichi concilii" ("were composed by the cardinal of Lorraine in the manner of the ancient councils", T3 l.57627-57630, my tr.). Tell each as his.

**B. The opening, 13 December 1545.** Acts: WAT l.12150-12173: "Doth it please you ... for the extirpation of heresies ; for the peace and union of the Church ; for the reformation of the Clergy and Christian people ; for the depression and extinction of the enemies of the Christian name,—to decree and declare that the sacred and general council of Trent do begin, and hath begun ? They answered : It pleaseth us." Procession, Mass and sermon from the histories: SARPI l.18406-18411 (legates and "all the Prelates, in number 25", procession from Trinity church to the cathedral, Monte sang the Mass of the Holy Ghost, the Bishop of Bitonto preached); P1 l.35093-35133 (same; the cathedral "dedicato à San Vigilio"); WAT-ESSAY l.3490-3533. Gaudete Sunday chosen: P1 l.34584-34589 ("nel giorno terzodecimo di Decembre, in cui cadeva la terza Domenica dell'Avvento"; the Sunday "è dinominata dal gaudio"). Weaker scene: thin room, all words are formula.

**C. 24 January 1552: the Saxon envoy before the fathers (Sarpi only).** SARPI l.36581-36600: "Afterwards the Saxon Ambassadors were brought in; where being entred, and having made their Obeysance to the Assembly, Badehornus spake". He asked for a safe-conduct like Basel's, because of "a certain Constitution of the Council of Constance, that Faith or Safe-Conduct, given by the Emperour, Kings, or others, to Hereticks, or Persons suspected, ought not to be observed" (l.36595-36597). Strong drama, but rests on Sarpi (Waterworth l.6698-6727 gives the same audience and demands in summary).

**D. 20 April 1562: the vote on residence by divine right.** Sarpi's count and Pallavicino's correction (see §11). Good for the residence dispute; not an opening.

Recommendation: A for the scene (acts plus two eye-witnesses via Waterworth plus Pallavicino), with B's date and question told quickly after.

## 3. Issue: what brought them together

- The pope's own words, the bull of indiction (WAT-ACTS; Paul III, 22 May 1542): Christendom was "rent and well-nigh torn asunder by schisms, dissensions, heresies" (l.11694-11695); Rhodes lost, Hungary ravaged (l.11699). The council was to treat "whatsoever appertains to the integrity and truth of the Christian religion ; the restoration of good and the correction of evil manners ; the peace, unity, and concord both of Christian princes and peoples ; and whatsoever is needful for repelling those assaults of barbarians and infidels" (l.12096-12102).
- Why Trent: the Germans asked for it. "seeing, upon several places being proposed, that they (the Germans) wished for the city of Trent, we,—though of opinion that everything might be transacted more commodiously in Cisalpine Italy,—nevertheless yielded up our will" (l.11982-11985). And: "it is principally on their account, and at their instance, that the council has been indicted and convoked, and in the very city which they desired" (l.12077-12079).
- The emperor's interest and the Protestants: Paul III's bull says Charles "had promised those who dissent from Catholic unity, that he would interpose his mediation" (l.11901-11903), and that at the diet of Ratisbon (Regensburg) the pope was asked to declare some Protestant articles tolerated until a council, which he refused (l.11942-11952).
- The opening decree's own aims (WAT l.12164-12171, above): faith, the extirpation of heresies, peace, reform of clergy and people, the Turks.
- The Protestant side, in its own words: Calvin (January 1545, to an unnamed correspondent; Bonnet's dating, CAL1 l.20036-20043): "our side will begin to remonstrate, that it is disgraceful that the settlement of religion should be entrusted to the professed enemies of God. They will cite Antichrist as a criminal and defendant : certainly, they will never permit him to be the judge." (CAL1 l.20092-20096; OCR "hira"). The Saxon demand (via Sarpi, l.36586-36590): Maurice would send divines "if ever a general, free, and Christian Council were celebrated, where the Controversies of Religion might be decided according to the Holy Scripture, and all might speak securely, and a Reformation be made in the Head and the Members".
- Sarpi's frame (hostile, his own judgment, SARPI l.8788-8791): a convocation "by some, for divers ends, and by divers means procured and hastened, by some hindered and deferred for the space of 22 years, and for 18 years more, sometimes assembled, sometimes dissolved".

## 4. Called by

- Paul III, by the bull of indiction, Rome, St Peter's, "in the year MDXLII. of the Lord's Incarnation, on the eleventh of the calends of June" (WAT l.12140-12143; Waterworth's footnote "May 22nd", l.12176). The operative words: "we indict, announce, convoke, appoint, and decree a sacred, oecumenical and general council,—to be opened on the ensuing calends of November of the present year, MDXLII, ... in the city of Trent, a place commodious, free, and convenient for all nations" (l.12022-12026; OCR "MDXLIL").
- Suspended, then reopened by the bull *Laetare Jerusalem*: MANSI35 l.35612-35623 ("BULLA REVOCATORIA Suspensionis, & indictionis concilii in civitate Tridentina ad Dominicam Laetare MDXLV. ... Laetare Jerusalem, & conventum facite"), dated "MDXLIIII. tertio decimo calendas Decembris, pontificatus nostri anno XI" (l.35915-35917) = 19 Nov 1544. Waterworth's essay names it as "the Bull of November the 19th, 1544, removing the suspension" (WAT l.3513-3514). Then a papal brief moved the opening to "dominica Gaudete, tertia praesentis adventus" (MANSI35 l.36737-36738).
- Recalled by Julius III (bull, WAT l.14821-14900; session XI, 1 May 1551, l.14917-14920; the bull's year reads "MDXLVIII" in the OCR at l.14897 though it says "in the first year of our Pontificate" — OCR or print error, unsure; do not cite the year from that line).
- Recalled by Pius IV (bull for celebration, WAT l.17362-17520, "Given at ... St. Peter's, in the year MDLX ... on the third of the calends of December", l.17513-17514; the bull's first word is misprinted "Paul" at l.17365 under a "PIUS IV" heading).
- Confirmed: Pius IV's bull (§8). No held source disputes who called it.

## 5. Attended

**At the opening, 13 December 1545** (no subscription list for this day is held; three historians):
- Sarpi: "the Legates in Trent, with all the Prelates, in number 25. in Pontifical Habit" (SARPI l.18406-18407).
- Pallavicino: "fuor de' trè Legati, il Cardinal di Trento, quattro Arcivescovi, venti Vescovi, cinque Generali d'Ordini Religiosi, Bastiano Pighini Auditor di Ruota, e gli Oratori di Ferdinando" ("besides the three legates, the cardinal of Trent, four archbishops, twenty bishops, five generals of religious orders, Bastiano Pighini, auditor of the Rota, and Ferdinand's envoys", P1 l.35131-35135, OCR "Arci-uelcoui, venti Vclcoui", my tr.).
- Waterworth (citing Pallavicino): "the three Legates, four archbishops, twenty bishops, five generals of religious orders, and the ambassadors of King Ferdinand" (WAT l.3536-3538); his note: "Le Plat's list is somewhat different. According to him, there were, besides the Legates, four archbishops, twenty-two bishops, and five Generals of Orders" (l.3552-3554).
- Safe wording: "about twenty-five prelates" with Sarpi named, or "four archbishops and twenty bishops" by Pallavicino's count. These agree in substance (Pallavicino's cardinal + 4 + 20 = 25).
- The three legates: Del Monte, Cervini (Santa Croce), Pole (WAT-LISTS l.24419-24443; Pole "Recessit ante quintam sess.", left before the fifth session, l.24443). Waterworth's claim (his own) that "not one in five of the bishops ... but was completely independent" of papal territorial influence (WAT l.3566-3575) is his argument; a hostile line on the same point is Sarpi's proverb (§11).

**At the reopening, January 1562:** Waterworth: first general congregation, 15 Jan 1562, "one hundred and two prelates present" besides the presidents (WAT l.7108-7111); at session XVII "besides the cardinals, one hundred and six bishops, four mitred abbots, and four generals" (l.7183-7185). Sarpi: prelates "besides the Cardinals, were one hundred and twelve, that did wear Miter" (SARPI l.43951-43953). Giussano gives a round "two hundred and fifty Bishops" assembled for the resumed council (GIUS1 l.3650-3652), a hagiographer's round figure for the whole period, not a count for one day.

**At the close (the subscription, acts):** "all of whom subsequently subscribed, and they were in number CCLV.; to wit, four legates, two cardinals, three patriarchs, twenty-five archbishops, one hundred and sixty-eight bishops, seven abbots, thirty-nine proctors of absent (prelates) with lawful commission, seven generals." (WAT l.24111-24116), certified "It agrees with the original" by Angelus Massarelli, secretary, and two notaries (l.24119-24131). The parts add to 255 (my arithmetic). This is the acts' count; the signatures themselves are not printed, so a full list of signers is NOT held (Le Plat's lists are of those present at any session, not of signers).
- Pallavicino: 255, "centosessant'otto vescovi" (T3 l.57682-57688); he names the patriarchs and abbots (l.57683-57693) and says the French abbots accepted the faith decrees absolutely but said of the reform decrees only that they were ready to obey (l.57692-57697).
- Waterworth's essay: 255, same breakdown (WAT l.11170-11175).
- Sarpi: the same breakdown but "268 Bishops" (SARPI l.70214-70216). His other figures match the acts, so 268 is probably a misprint or OCR error for 168; unsure; a reviewer should check the page image. Do not use Sarpi's figure.
- The ambassadors did not sign with the fathers. Acts silent. Sarpi: two reasons, the French ambassador's absence and the Count of Luna's refusal to sign without reserve (l.70216-70228). Pallavicino: Luna would sign only "riservato l'assenso del re cattolico", and the other envoys' acceptances were taken two days later in separate instruments (T3 l.57718-57731). Waterworth agrees (l.11186-11195).
- Pius IV's bull (acts): "a most numerous assembly of bishops and of other distinguished prelates, and one worthy of an oecumenical Council" (WAT l.24237-24239).

Where from: Pius IV's bull says gathered "from all sides out of the nations of Christendom" (WAT l.24240-24241). Sarpi has Lorraine say he had only six prelates with him against "the consent of more than two hundred" (l.70711-70713). A national breakdown would need the Le Plat lists counted; not done.

## 7. Decided (from the acts)

1. **Scripture and tradition, Session IV (8 Apr 1546).** "this truth and discipline are contained in the written books, and the unwritten traditions which, received by the Apostles from the mouth of Christ Himself, or from the Apostles themselves, the Holy Ghost dictating, have come down even unto us, transmitted as it were from hand to hand" (WAT l.12415-12419). The Synod "receives and venerates with an equal affection of piety, and reverence, all the books both of the Old and of the New Testament ... as also the said traditions" (l.12421-12425; Latin "Pari pietatis affectu (sentiment), ac reverentia", Waterworth's footnote l.12446; Latin text MIRBT l.16557-16575). Canon list follows, then anathema on those who do not receive the books "as they are contained in the old Latin Vulgate edition" (l.12460-12464). Second decree: the Vulgate to be "held as authentic" (l.12478-12483), and no one may interpret Scripture "contrary to that sense which holy mother Church ... hath held and doth hold" (l.12486-12490).
2. **Justification, Session VI (13 Jan 1547).** Proem: "a certain erroneous doctrine disseminated touching Justification" (l.12963-12965). Ch. VIII: "we are therefore said to be justified by faith, because faith is the beginning of human salvation, the foundation, and the root of all Justification ... but we are therefore said to be justified freely because that none of those things which precede Justification—whether faith or works—merit the grace itself of Justification" (l.13243-13250). Canon IX: "If anyone saith that by faith alone the impious is justified ; in such wise as to mean that nothing else is required to co-operate ... let him be anathema" (l.13681-13686). Canon XI against justification "by the sole imputation of the justice of Christ" (l.13699-13703). Note: the canons do not name Luther; Waterworth (essay) says ch. VII "is directed mainly against the errors of Luther" and "also aims at the opinion of Seripando" (WAT l.4970-4975).
3. **Transfer to Bologna, Session VIII (11 Mar 1547).** "a disease of the kind stated is so plainly and notoriously certain, that the prelates cannot without danger of their lives remain in this city" ... "this Council be transferred for a time to the city of Bologna" (l.14686-14710).
4. **Safe-conduct for Protestants, Sessions XIII and XV.** Session XV promises not to use "any authority, power, right, or statute ... of any Councils whatsoever, especially those of Constance and Sienna ... to the prejudice in any way of this public faith" (l.17250-17258). Session XV also admits that the Protestants "have not as yet come" and defers to 19 March (l.17093-17099).
5. **Suspension, Session XVI (28 Apr 1552).** Wars broke out, and "so far was the holy Synod from remedying the evils and troubles existing amongst Christians that, contrary to its intention, It irritated rather than calmed the minds of many" (l.17319-17323); German bishops and electors "had withdrawn" (l.17326-17328).
6. **Reopening formula, Session XVII (18 Jan 1562):** matters to be treated which, "the Legates and Presidents proposing", seem suitable (l.17544-17545). Contested at once (§11).
7. **Residence, Session XXIII, reform ch. I (15 Jul 1563).** "Whereas it is, by divine precept, enjoined on all to whom the cure of souls is committed, to know their own sheep" (l.19390-19391); all set over churches, "even though they be cardinals", "are obliged to personal residence in their own church or diocese" (l.19408-19410, 19419-19420). The decree does not say residence is of divine right. Waterworth's footnote on the word "Assistunt" (are with): "this word was for a long time opposed by those who denied the residence of bishops to be of divine right" (l.19413-19414, editor).
8. **Seminaries, Session XXIII, reform ch. XVIII.** "the holy Synod ordains that all cathedral, metropolitan, and other churches greater than these shall be bound, each according to its means and the extent of the diocese, to maintain, to educate religiously, and to train in ecclesiastical discipline, a certain number of youths ... in a college to be chosen by the bishop" (l.19909-19916); at least twelve years old, able to read and write (l.19916-19926); "It wishes that the children of the poor be principally selected" (l.19928); "that so this college may be a perpetual seminary of ministers of God" (l.19937-19938).
9. **Close, Session XXV.** On receiving the decrees: "no hope remains that the heretics, after being so often invited, even with the public faith which they desired, and after being so long expected, will come hither later" (l.23966-23969). The decrees under Paul III and Julius III were read again ("They were recited", l.24002). Confirmation to be asked of the pope (l.24008-24016). Index, catechism, missal and breviary handed to the pope (MIRBT l.19391-19400, Latin).

## 8. Outcome, through events

- **Confirmation.** Consistory 26 Jan 1564: Morone and Simonetta petition; Pius IV: "We ... do confirm all and singular the things which have been decreed and defined in the said Council" (WAT l.24141-24178). Bull *Benedictus Deus* (Mirbt's title, MIRBT l.19428; English opening "Blessed be the God, and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ", WAT l.24203) forbids anyone to publish "any commentaries, glosses, annotations, scholia, or any kind of interpretation whatsoever" without papal authority (l.24341-24346), and reserves doubts to "the Apostolic See, the mistress of all the faithful" (l.24351-24354).
- **Profession of Faith.** Bull *Iniunctum nobis* (Mirbt's date 13 Nov 1564, MIRBT l.19496): all to be set over churches must make it. Key line: "Romanoque pontifici, beati Petri apostolorum principis successori ac Iesu Christi vicario, veram obedientiam spondeo ac iuro" ("to the Roman pontiff, successor of blessed Peter prince of the apostles and vicar of Jesus Christ, I promise and swear true obedience", MIRBT l.19575-19576, my tr.). English in P4E; Latin in SCH l.18263ff.
- **Roman Catechism.** McHugh and Callan (1923 introduction, modern): Pius V, elected 17 Jan 1566, had it revised; the first edition, "The Manutian (Rome, 1566)", printed by command of Pius V (RC l.1211-1215, 1260-1262). Giussano: Borromeo "presided over the compilation of the Roman Catechism" with theologians who had been at Trent (GIUS1 l.3787-3790).
- **Borromeo and Milan.** Giussano (hagiography): Borromeo, nephew of Pius IV, had "the special superintendence of the acts of the Council" in Rome (GIUS1 l.3653-3656). At Milan's first provincial council (Giussano's contents date it "October 15, 1565", l.1334-1336), "The decrees of the holy Council of Trent were read and formally accepted" (l.4839-4841); prelates came to "this, the first fruits of the Council of Trent" (l.4819-4820). His first seminary in Milan, St John Baptist, was "to accommodate a hundred and fifty students" (l.6467-6470). Milan's own acts quote Trent's seminary order (`borromeo_acta-ecclesiae-mediolanensis-lat_1599.txt` l.2786-2790, per the census edge; not re-read).
- **Pius IV on seminaries** (Pallavicino, T3 l.57979-57983): he praised "come decretata per inspirazione di Dio l'instituzione de' seminarii: dicendo ch'egli voleva essere il primo a darne spontaneamente l'esempio" ("as decreed by God's inspiration the founding of seminaries, saying he wished to be the first to give the example of his own accord", my tr.).
- **Beyond the Latin river: the Church of the East.** At session XXII (17 Sep 1562), Cardinal Amulio's letter was read: Abdisho, "Patriarch of Muzale in Assyria", had come to Rome, obeyed the pope and received confirmation; his confession of faith was read (SARPI l.51628-51660; T3 l.9548-9615). His own oath (ABD l.268-289): he could not come to the synod ("cum mihi non liceat ad Sanctam Sacram Tridentinam Synodum accedere"), but promised to teach "praecipue in ijs, quae in sancta sacra Oecumenica Tridentina Synodo terminabuntur" ("above all in what shall be settled in the holy sacred ecumenical synod of Trent", l.288-289, OCR cleaned, my tr.). The Portuguese ambassador protested that Indian sees he listed belonged under the archbishop of Goa (T3 l.9625-9630; SARPI l.51646-51650).
- **France.** Sarpi (hostile): Lorraine was taxed in France for the decrees; the Parliament's counsellors opposed the reform articles (SARPI l.70723-70728); and "it [was said] in France, in manner of a Proverb, that the modern Council had more authority than that of the Apostles, because their own pleasure only was a sufficient ground for the Decrees, without admitting the Holy Ghost" (l.71034-71037). The census edge to the ancien-régime church (1699, archbishop of Paris) says Trent's rules for church order were "not yet received everywhere there"; that edge carries no held source in the excerpt, so do not rest the profile on it.
- **Germany.** Sarpi: "the Princes of Germany held none esteem of this Council" (l.71101), while Catholics wanted "the Communion of the Cup, Marriage of Priests, and relaxation" (l.71104-71106).
- **England.** Jewel's Apology (1562): "we have not tarried for in this matter the authority or consent of the Tridentine council, wherein we saw nothing done uprightly, nor by good order; where also everybody was sworn to the maintenance of one man; where our prince's ambassadors were contemned; where not one of our divines could be heard" (JEWEL-E l.3590-3593).
- **Sarpi's last word on Pius IV's rewards** (SARPI l.71296-71300): on 12 March he made nineteen cardinals, choosing none "of those who held the Residence and Institution of Bishops, to be de jure divino".

## 9. The condemned and refusing parties in their own words

- **Lutherans (Saxony, Württemberg, 1551-52).** Their own written confessions handed in at Trent are NOT held (see §12). Their speeches reach us only through Sarpi (Catholic but anti-curial; SARPI l.36581-36670) and Waterworth (Catholic; WAT l.6669-6727). Sarpi's Badehorn: the divines would not come because "by some Decrees made in Trent, and already printed, it did appear, that they are accounted Hereticks and Schismaticks, though they never have been either called or heard" (l.36603-36606); he asked that the Council "would first repeat, approve, and ratifie the Article of the Superiority of the Council above the Pope" (l.36640-36641). Württemberg's envoys (Sarpi, l.36660-36668): "their Doctrine being repugnant to that of the Pope and the Bishops his Adherents, it was unjust that either the Plaintiff or the Defendant should be Judge". Sarpi's Protestant objection to the safe-conduct: it was "very captious", with "this Clause of Reservation, as far as belongeth to the Synod" (l.34695-34696), and "even the very Pedants did laugh at it, that the principal Verb was more than an hundred and fifty Words distant from the beginning" (l.34720-34722). Waterworth, hostile to them: "To these demands, some heretical, others schismatical, it was replied that an answer should be given in due time" (l.6725-6726). Name note: both Sarpi (l.35536) and Waterworth (l.6669) write "Duke of Wittenberg/Wittemberg"; by the context (a duke, with a confession) this is Württemberg, but the held lines spell it so; say "the duke of Württemberg's envoys" only as a gloss, or keep "Wittenberg" in quotes.
- **Calvin (Reformed), own words.** 1545 (above, §3). 10 April 1551, to Bullinger, on a papal invitation: "We are not, however, of the number of those who obtain a place, either from right or custom, or the favour of the Apostolic See. We may accordingly remain at home. Yet there is something for us to do even at home." (CAL2 l.14473-14476; date l.14421). 1547: "I have commenced work upon the Fathers of Trent" (CAL2 l.6687; the letter is headed "CCIV.— To Farel", Geneva, 21 August 1547, l.6606-6611, though the page running head at l.6683 reads "VIRET"; unsure which). The *Antidote* itself is NOT held; Bonnet's note describes it (CAL2 l.7336-7341).
- **England, own words.** Jewel (1562): "this is a conspiracy and not a council" (JEWEL-E l.2919-2920); "none of us may be suffered so much as to sit, or once to be seen in these men's meetings, much less suffered to speak freely our mind" (l.2955-2957). Jewel's Latin letter to Scipio (JEWEL-L l.5562-5590) answers Scipio's wonder that England alone "nec Legatum eo aliquem misisse" (sent no envoy there).
- **Swiss Reformed cantons.** Only Bonnet's note: an invitation went to the cantons, and the Reformed cantons refused (CAL2 l.14490-14495, editor). Their own reply is not held.

## 11. Contested points (each side with lines)

1. **The transfer to Bologna, 1547: real disease or pretext?** Acts: the disease was "plainly and notoriously certain" (WAT l.14686-14688). Sarpi: Monte "suborned some to ask the Physicians, whether those Infirmities were not Contagious", and the physicians "spake some ambiguous word, which was studiously spread abroad" (SARPI l.28736-28745); a papal messenger "concealed until then" (l.28722-28730, noisy OCR). Pallavicino: a real "infermità di petecchie con insolita mortalità" (P2 l.4933-4934), the bishop of Capaccio died three days after the session (l.4936-4937), Fracastoro's oath (above), and Sarpi asserts the papal order "senza veruna prova" (P2 l.4928-4931). Votes: Sarpi, "35 Bishops, and three Generals did assent. But the Cardinal Pacceco, and 17 other Bishops opposed" (SARPI l.28872-28876). Pallavicino: of 56 present, 38 approved absolutely, 14 opposed absolutely, "tutti dependenti da Cesare eccetto il Fiesolano" (all dependent on the emperor except the bishop of Fiesole), 2 doubtful, 2 conditional (P2 l.5572-5575). Waterworth follows Pallavicino (WAT l.5512-5516). Tell both; the acts record only "They answered : It pleaseth us" (l.14720).
2. **Residence by divine right, 20 April 1562.** Sarpi: "68 said, Placet absolutely : 33 Non placet absolutely, 13 said Placet, consulto prius Sanctissimo Domino nostro : and 17 answered, Non placet, nisi prius consulto Sanctissimo Domino nostro" (SARPI l.45897-45900); "the greater part, by one half, did approve the declaration" (l.45914-45915); he says the 13/17 split was used "only where every one doth think to do his Master the best service" (l.45906-45907). Pallavicino: Sarpi's 68 is excusable ("Presso à settanta", near seventy, P2 l.47484-47493), but the opponents were not 33 but 38 or 37 (l.47497-47499), the middle not 30 but 34 (l.47500-47506), so the majority "non voleva la definizione senza prima ricercar del suo sentimento il Pontefice" (P2 l.47530-47532, did not want the definition without first asking the pope); he quotes the council secretary's tally: affirmative sixty-six, later sixty-seven with Lérida's vote (l.47547-47555; the negative figure reads "fertanruno" in the OCR at l.47549, probably "settantuno", 71; unsure, check the image). He cites the acts of Paleotto, Servanzio's diary and the Florentine envoy's letters (l.47532-47536). The final decree (WAT l.19390) said "by divine precept" but did not define divine right (Waterworth's note l.19413-19414).
3. **"Proponentibus legatis" (1562).** Acts: the formula at WAT l.17544. Waterworth: the Archbishop of Granada and the Bishop of Orense protested against the words; Leon and Almeria agreed conditionally (WAT l.7175-7181). Sarpi: four contradicted, naming "Peter Guerrero, Archbishop of Granata, Francisco Bianco, Bishop of Orense, Andreas della Questa, Bishop of Leon, Antonio Colermero [sic], Bishop of Almeria" (SARPI l.43979-43987). The two agree in names, differ on whether all four opposed outright.
4. **The vote to close and to ask confirmation, 4 Dec 1563.** Sarpi: "they answered not one by one, but altogether, Placet" (SARPI l.70109-70110; the OCR reads "now", read as "not"). Pallavicino: the secretary took the votes by custom and "sol uno ripugnò al chiedimento della confermazione, cioè l'arcivescovo di Granata", who said "piace che si finisca il concilio, ma non chieggo la confermazione", and three Spaniards (Agostino of Lérida, Sebastiani of Patti, Mendoza of Salamanca) answered that they asked confirmation "come necessaria" (T3 l.57580-57606). Waterworth agrees with Pallavicino on Granada (WAT l.11128-11131).
5. **The papal confirmation in Rome.** Sarpi (hostile): eight cardinals were "almost all of opinion that it was fit they should be moderated before the Confirmation" (SARPI l.70324-70327); Cardinal Amulio, "in whose Memorials I have seen this negotiation", urged confirming at once, so that in time "things will insensibly, the World not observing it, return unto the same state" (l.70378-70416). Pallavicino: the cardinals approved "di comune consentimento", and the confirmation decree was named specifically "affinchè si scorgesse, come il concilio avea riconosciuto il bisogno dell'autorità pontificia" (so that it might be seen that the council had recognised its need of papal authority, T3 l.58036-58048). Acts: confirmation "with the advice and assent of our venerable brethren the cardinals" (WAT l.24171-24174).
6. **The acclamations and Lorraine.** Sarpi: vanity (l.70195-70199). Pallavicino: in the manner of ancient councils; and by calling Pius "pontiff of the holy and universal Church", Lorraine "parve che gli attribuisse quella maggioranza sopra la Chiesa universale, che da lui e da' Francesi gli era stata contesa" (seemed to grant him the supremacy over the universal Church that he and the French had disputed, T3 l.57635-57640).
7. **Musso's opening sermon.** Sarpi: the legates' exhortation "accounted Pious, Christian, Modest", but the Bishop's sermon showed "Vanity and Ostentation of [Eloqu]ence ... noted by all" (SARPI l.18575-18577). Pallavicino rebuts at length (P1 l.35141-35175). Minor; use only if room.
8. **The count of subscribing bishops** (168 acts and Pallavicino; 268 Sarpi as printed/OCR). Probably not a real dispute; see §5.
9. **Who controlled the bishops.** Waterworth: "not one in five" under papal territorial influence (WAT l.3566-3575). Sarpi's Spaniards: everything "consulted of and resolved also at Rome" (SARPI l.45938-45946). Partisan on both sides.
10. **Abdisho's profession date.** Print: Rome, "die vii Martii MDLXII" (ABD l.209); Pallavicino's note "A' 7 di marzo 1562" (T3 l.9619). Sarpi: "made in Rome the 17. of March" (SARPI l.51655). Use 7 March.

## 16. Traditions touched (census ids at the date)

**Gathered**

## 17. Length

The held record can carry the full 2:30 ceiling (about 400 words) and must be cut to fit, not padded. Reasons: the acts are held whole (bull of calling, every session, acclamations, the subscription count, confirmation), two opposed full histories give the room's arguments with numbers, and the refusing side speaks in its own words (Calvin, Jewel), though the Lutheran envoys reach us only through Sarpi and Waterworth. Suggested spine for 400 words: the acclamations scene (A); Paul III's call and "the city which they desired"; whose account (acts; Sarpi and Pallavicino named and placed); counts (about 25 at the opening; 255 signers at the close, 168 of them bishops); the Protestants and the safe-conduct (Constance; Badehorn via Sarpi; Calvin's "remain at home"); three decrees in short quotes (Scripture and tradition; justification; seminary); one dispute told both ways (residence 1562, or Bologna 1547); outcome (confirmation 1564, the Profession, the Catechism 1566, Milan, Abdisho, Jewel/France); close.
