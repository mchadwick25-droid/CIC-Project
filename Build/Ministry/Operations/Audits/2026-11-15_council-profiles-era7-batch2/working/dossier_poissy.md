# Dossier: the Colloquy of Poissy (September-October 1561)

Research dossier for the era 7 council profile. All paths are under `cic/texts/`. Line numbers are file lines; quotes were re-checked against the file. Translations marked "(my tr.)" are mine. OCR spellings are kept inside quotes (long s often reads as "f"; doubled spaces are the OCR's). In some quotes the OCR "f" for long s is silently read as "s" for readability. A reviewer should match the quoted words, not the long-s letter.

Weekdays, as a check on dates:
- Beza's printed speech calls 9 September 1561 a Tuesday ("le mardy neufiesme jour de Septembre", HE1 27897).
- Polanco calls 24 September a Wednesday ("El miércoles passado", LAI6 3607, with the editors' note "Id est 24 Septembris 1561", LAI6 3632). He calls 26 September a Friday ("El viernes siguiente, que fué ayer 26 de Setiembre", LAI6 3653).
- The Sattler "Sommaire" (quoted in HE1 33213-33222) has Sunday 28 and Monday 29 September. These all agree.

## 1. Held sources

### Who wrote the Histoire ecclésiastique (caution 1)

**The book names no author.**
- The 1580 title page reads: "HISTOIRE ECCLESIASTIQVE DES EGLISES REFORMEES AV ROYAUME de France ... A ANVERS. 1580." It names no author (HE1 110-130).
- The Library header says the same: the attribution to Beze "is the host catalogue's and the editors'" (HE1 2).

**Reuss, the 1889 editor, treats Beza's authorship as an open question.**
- His chapter heading: "THÉODORE DE BÈZE EST-IL L'AUTEUR DE L'HISTOIRE ECCLÉSIASTIQUE?" (HE3 785-786).
- He says the tradition's foundations "ne sont rien moins que solides" (are anything but solid) (HE3 798-799).
- He cites evidence from the Poissy chapter itself.
  - The narrator writes of Beza "(comme depuis je le luy aj ouï dire)" (as I have since heard him say). Reuss says this keeps narrator and minister "si nettement distinguées" (so clearly apart) (HE3 1641-1648).
  - Soldan and Heppe, whom Reuss cites, objected that Beza would not have called his own speech "fort agréable" (HE3 1629-1633).

**Reuss's own conclusion.**
- "Ce ne fut pas, à coup sûr, Bèze lui-même qui trouva le loisir de se livrer à ce travail de compilation littéraire, mais il le dirigea de près." (my tr.: "It was certainly not Beza himself who found time to do this work of literary compiling, but he directed it closely.")
- He names possible compilers: "soit par Nicolas des Gallars, soit par Simon Goulart, ou tel autre".
- He thinks Beza "y contribua sans doute ... par ses réminiscences du colloque de Poissy" (no doubt contributed his memories of the Colloquy of Poissy) (HE3 2002-2010).
- He calls the book "l'œuvre collective du protestantisme français" (HE3 1874).
- He says it "a paru à Genève", despite "l'indication d'Anvers" on the title page (HE3 429-435).

**Its main source was Pierre de la Place.**
- Much of the Poissy chapter copies La Place's *Commentaires* of 1565.
- Reuss calls La Place "président de la Cour des Aides" and a "protestant convaincu", killed at St Bartholomew (HE3 3235-3240).
- Baum-Cunitz note the copying passage by passage. For example, of L'Hospital's speech: "Le résume de ce discours donné ici est littéralement identique avec celui qui s'en trouve dans De la Place" (HE1 27739-27740). See also HE1 26203 and 27812.

**How the brief should name it (a suggestion).**
- "The *Ecclesiastical History of the Reformed Churches*, printed in 1580 with no author's name and long credited to Beza."
- Then: "Its editor Reuss thought Beza directed it and gave his own memories of Poissy, but did not compile it."

**Its side.**
- A Reformed narrative, polemical in places. For example, most prelates were "du tout incapable de traitter de la religion" (HE1 26874-26875).
- It prints Beza's speech of 9 September (HE1 27866-28703). That is the participant's own words, as this text gives them.
- Baum-Cunitz say the first printing of the speech was "faite probablement par les soins de Bèze lui-même, peu de jours après le colloque" (made probably under Beza's own care, a few days after the colloquy) (HE1 27881-27882).

### Acts (the meeting's own documents)

- **Canons of the assembly of prelates.**
  - The Histoire prints a French text (HE1 33925-35113). Its preamble: "Le Roy tres-chrestien ayant mandé les Prélats de l'Eglise Gallicane, pour se trouver à Poissy" (HE1 33925-33926).
  - The Latin closing is given in a Baum-Cunitz note, from Languet's letters: "Acta Poissiaci ... anno humanae salutis 1561. Pridie Idus Oct." (14 October) (HE1 35129-35140).
  - Witness kind: acts, though printed in a hostile setting.
- **The prelates' "Conclusion" of 9 October.** It rejects the ministers' formula and adds their "Confession catholique" (HE1 33643-33729).
  - The Sorbonne faculty's judgment on the formula is at HE1 33488-33605.
  - Witness kind: acts, as the Histoire prints them.
- **The ministers' petitions.** 17 August (HE1 27011-27139) and 8 September (HE1 27541-27619). Also their signed declaration of 26 September (HE1 32298-32595). Witness kind: acts of the Reformed party.
- **The prelates' reception of the Jesuits, 15 September.** This survives inside royal letters given in the Parlement of Paris on 14 February 1562 (LAI8 39565-39640). They cite "L'acte d'approbation et reception des dicts pbres. et escolliers faicte en l'assemblée du clergé et concile national tenu a Poissy le lundy quinziesme jour de septembre dernier" (LAI8 39599-39601). Witness kind: acts, quoted in a later royal and Parlement document.
- **The Edict of January**, St-Germain-en-Laye, dated 17 January 1561 old style (1562 new style).
  - Printed in full in HE1 36904-37241; summarized in CRES3 30506-30540.
  - Witness kind: acts, of what followed the colloquy.

## 2. Candidate opening scenes

**A. The nuns' refectory, noon, 9 September. The strongest.**
- "Le lendemain 9 de Septembre, environ midi, s'assemblerent à Poissy, au grand refectoir des nonnains, le Roy ..." (HE1 27647-27648). (my tr.: "The next day, 9 September, about noon, there gathered at Poissy, in the great refectory of the nuns, the King ...")
- The seating: three cardinals on one side, "& trois de l'autre; & au dessous d'iceux trente six Evesques qu'Arcevesques" (HE1 27653-27678). (my tr.: "and three on the other; and below them thirty-six bishops and archbishops.")
- The king: "je vous ay fait assembler de divers lieux de mon royaume ... j'ay délibéré que vous ne bougiés de ce lieu, jusqu'à ce que vous y ayés donné bon ordre" (HE1 27684-27694). (my tr.: "I have had you gathered from many parts of my kingdom ... I have resolved that you shall not stir from this place until you have set it in good order.")
- The ministers came in later, to the barrier: "sur lesquelles estans appuyés testes nues, Théodore de Beze, esleu de tous les autres pour ce faire, parla" (HE1 27863-27864). (my tr.: "Leaning on it bare-headed, Theodore Beza, chosen by all the others to do it, spoke.")
- He began with a prayer: "Seigneur Dieu Père éternel ..." (HE1 27873).
- Sarpi says he spoke "having prayed on his knee" (SARPI 42821). Vermigli, in a Baum-Cunitz note, says Lorraine knelt on 16 September to match what Beza had done "lors de sa prière" (HE1 30267-30270).

**B. The sentence that broke the room (same day, later).**
- The Histoire's narrative: Christ's body "estoit toutesfois aussi loin du pain que le haut des cieux est eslongné de la terre" (HE1 28712-28713). (my tr.: "was yet as far from the bread as the top of the heavens is from the earth.")
- "Ceste seule parole ... fut cause que les prelats commencèrent à bruire et murmurer, dont les uns disoient: Blasphemavit; les autres se levoient pour s'en aller" (HE1 28713-28718). (my tr.: "That one word made the prelates begin to stir and murmur. Some said 'He has blasphemed'; others rose to leave.")
- Tournon asked the king and queen "qu'on imposast silence à de Beze, ou qu'il luy fust permis & à sa compagnie de se retirer" (HE1 28719-28722). (my tr.: "that Beza be silenced, or that he and his company be allowed to withdraw.")
- "Le Roy ne bougea" (the King did not stir). Beza then said: "Messieurs, je vous prie d'attendre la conclusion qui vous contentera" (HE1 28723-28726). (my tr.: "Gentlemen, I beg you to wait for the conclusion, which will satisfy you.")

**C. Before the room: the night of 24 August, in the King of Navarre's chamber.**
- Lorraine and Beza met before Catherine (HE1 27164-27465).
- Lorraine: "vous trouverés que je ne suis pas si noir qu'on m'a fait" (you will find I am not as black as I have been painted) (HE1 27460-27461).
- Madame de Crussol, in Beza's letter of 25 August as Baum-Cunitz quote it: "Bon homme pour ce soir, mays demain quoy?" (Good man for tonight, but tomorrow what?) (HE1 27507-27508).
- A good human scene, but it rests on Reformed witnesses only.

**D. The Catholic side's scene, 26 September (Polanco).**
- "vino la reina madre, no obstante la lluuia" (the queen mother came, despite the rain) (LAI6 3655).
- Lainez then spoke "en italiano, y siempre enderezando su razonamiento a la reyna, por spacio me pareze de tres quartos de hora" (LAI6 3747-3749). (my tr.: "in Italian, always turning his speech to the queen, for about three quarters of an hour, I think.")
- This is a Jesuit eyewitness. It pairs with scene A if the profile wants both voices.

## 3. Issue: what brought them together

**The crown and the Parlement's July assembly (the Histoire, after La Place).**
- The prelates' assembly at Poissy was "derechef arrestée" (resolved again) in that assembly.
- The ministers were to have safe-conduct "pour s'y trouver, & y estre ouis, afin d'adviser s'il y auroit moyen de les reunir à l'Eglise Romaine" (HE1 25995-26000). (my tr.: "to be heard there, to see whether there was any way to reunite them to the Roman Church.")
- Baum-Cunitz add that the prelates' assembly was called for 20 July to choose its deputies to the Estates General. It was also to "préparer la convocation d'un concile par le pape et délibérer en attendant des réformes ecclésiastiques" (HE1 25790-25792). This is the editors' account.

**The Estates asked for a national council.**
- Bretagne, speaking for the Third Estate at the Estates (moved to Pontoise and continued at St-Germain), asked the king to "indire & assigner un Concile national libre & légitime", with safe-conduct, over which the king would preside (HE1 26113-26114, 26214, 26523-26528).
- A further list asks that the Edict of July be revoked. It also asks for abuses removed "par un Concile national libre & légitime, ainsi qu'il avoit esté jà promis" (HE1 26785-26790).
- The Histoire says the Third Estate "seul pour lors toucha au droit but" (alone, at that time, hit the mark) (HE1 26766-26767). That is partisan praise.

**The crown's aim, in L'Hospital's opening speech.** This is the Histoire's summary, copied from La Place.
- The king had granted "un saufconduit aux ministres de ceste secte, esperant qu'une conférence avec eux amiable & gratieuse pourroit grandement profiter" (HE1 27793-27795).
- The prelates should receive them "comme le père fait ses enfans" (HE1 27796).
- If it failed, at least no one could say "qu'ils ayent esté condamnés sans les ouir" (HE1 27799-27801).
- A second summary, from the original "Discours des Actes", has L'Hospital say not to wait for the general council. It quotes: "user de nos remèdes presens et domestiques ... en ce Concile national" (HE1 27745-27750, in a Baum-Cunitz note).
- Sarpi has him say that a national council once condemned what the general council of Rimini had set up (SARPI 42799-42802).

**The Reformed aim.**
- Their conditions: the bishops "ne soient point nos juges, attendu qu'ils sont nos parties" (HE1 27114-27115). All differences "jugés & décidés par la seule parole de Dieu" (HE1 27123-27124).
- Beza's speech: "Nostre desir est ... que ces troupeaux ... soient ralliés & recueillis en la bergerie de ce souverain & unique Pasteur" (HE1 28084-28089).

**The Catholic objection in principle.**
- Lainez: matters of faith belong "al summo sacerdote et al concilio genérale". With that council open, "non par' conueniente ni legitimo far' simili congregationi" (LAI8 37505-37508).
- Pallavicino: no national council was concluded.
  - The Catholics thought it "ingiurioso all'autorità del Concilio Generale, che già stava in atto".
  - The Reformed knew they would not get judging power "da' Prelati di Francia" (PAL2 41992-41999).

## 4. Called by

**The king.**
- His own words: "je vous ay fait assembler" (HE1 27684).
- The canons' preamble: "Le Roy tres-chrestien ayant mandé les Prélats de l'Eglise Gallicane" (HE1 33925-33926).

**Who fetched the ministers.**
- Martyr and Beza were sent for "par letres expresses du Roy de Navarre" (HE1 26000-26001).
- Beza was "expressement mandé par les Roy de Navarre et Prince de Condé" (HE1 27159-27161).
- Vermigli, quoted by Robinson, says the safe-conduct came "in the name of the king and of the queen mother, both subscribed and sealed" (zurich-letters 5726-5728).

**Catherine claims the colloquy as her own decision.**
- "je trouvé bon que nosdits Prelatz et Evêques entrassent en quelque Colloque gracieux avec lesdits Ministres sur les Articles de leur dite Confession de Foi" (AYM 25542-25545). (my tr.: "I thought it good that our prelates and bishops should enter a gracious colloquy with the said ministers on the articles of their confession of faith.")

**Two earlier assemblies.**
- L'Hospital: the king acted "suivant ce qui jà avoit esté arresté par les deux assemblées" (HE1 27792-27793). Which two is not said. Probably the Estates and the July Parlement assembly, but that is unsure.
- The Histoire says the colloquy was already "arresté" when Lorraine urged the Paris assembly in May (HE1 25454-25455).

**No held source disputes that the crown called it.** The Estates asked for a national council (section 3). No held line says they called Poissy.

**The Reformed names (the Histoire).**
- Marlorat, François de Saint-Paul, Jean-Raymond Merlin, Jean Malot, François de Morel, Nicolas Folion, Claude de la Boissière, Jean Virel, Nicolas des Gallars and Jean Bouquin. Jean de l'Espine joined them. Then came Peter Martyr and Beza, "Et finalement Jean de la Tour" (HE1 26918-27006).
- That is fourteen names, matching Sarpi's fourteen.
- Baum-Cunitz call La Tour "Le douzième des ministres députés qui assistèrent au colloque au nom des Eglises de France (les députés laïques étaient au nombre de 22)" (HE1 27017-27018). On their count, Martyr and Beza come on top of the twelve.

**Where the ministers came from.**
- Martyr from Zurich; Beza from Geneva (HE1 26001-26004).
- Des Gallars was "depuis deux ans pasteur de l'Eglise de Londres" (editors, HE1 26972-26973).
- Saint-Paul came from Dieppe (HE1 26939); Folion from Orléans (HE1 26962-26963).

**The court on 9 September.**
- The king; the Duke of Orléans; Antoine, King of Navarre; Catherine; Jeanne d'Albret, Queen of Navarre; and many princes and princesses (HE1 27648-27652).
- Marguerite and Condé were there too, per the editors citing La Place (HE1 27672-27673).

**The legate and his party.**
- Ippolito d'Este, Cardinal of Ferrara, came to court on 19 September: "Die 19 Sept. ad aulam venit legatus pontificius" (Vermigli's Relatio, in a Baum-Cunitz note, HE1 30472-30475).
- He brought Lainez, "Fra Justinian" and others (HE1 30480-30536).
- The Histoire's text calls Lainez "Lieva". The editors write: "Le nom de Lieva doit être une faute, c'était Jacques Lainez" (HE1 30501, 30526).
- Lainez's own editors give his arrivals: Paris on 17 September and St-Germain on 19 September (LAI6 185-187). Coudret also gives the 19th (LAI6 3896).

**The later Lutheran theologians.**
- Beuerlin, Andreae and Bidembach came from Württemberg; Diller and Boquin came from Heidelberg.
- They "arrivèrent à Paris le 19 octobre", by the editors' account (HE1 33797-33799, 33806, 33865-33867). That was after the prelates had dispersed.

## 6. Dates

**The prelates' assembly opened in late July or early August.** The date is contested. It is told in a Baum-Cunitz note (HE1 26025-26037, 26890-26897):
- Languet's letter of 1 August: "Ante triduum habuit initium conventus Episcoporum ... Indictus erat ad 20 Julii" (HE1 26894-26895).
- Brulart: convoked "le premier jour d'Aoust" (HE1 26032-26035).
- De Thou: 10 August. The editors reject it: "Cette date ne peut pas être exacte" (HE1 26025-26027).
- Chantonnay, 26 July: "l'assemblée des Evesques sera Dimanche", which the editors read as 3 August (HE1 26035-26036).
- Sarpi: "In August the Prelates did assemble in Poissi" (SARPI 42729).

**The ministers' first petition, 17 August (the Histoire, HE1 27011).**
- Goulart gives 10 August (editors, HE1 27036).
- Beza's letter of 22 August says the eight ministers went to court on the 12th and gave a "supplicem libellum". The editors weigh the two dates (HE1 27036-27047).
- Reuss lists the gap among the points against Beza's authorship (HE3 1609-1617).

**Beza's arrival.**
- He reached St-Germain on 23 August and preached the next day (HE1 27161-27162).
- From his letter, the editors say he left Geneva on 16 August and reached Paris on the 22nd (HE1 26045-26046).
- The meeting with Catherine and Lorraine was "Ce jour mesme", that night (HE1 27164).

**Vermigli's arrival.**
- He reached Paris on 9 September and St-Germain on 10 September (his journal, editors' notes HE1 26991-26994, 30659-30660).
- The Histoire's text places him earlier; the editors call it an error (HE1 26995-27002, 30657).

**The second petition, 8 September (HE1 27504-27505).** Beza's letter says the 7th, which the editors call "l'erreur" (HE1 27560).

**The opening session, 9 September, "environ midi" (HE1 27647).**
- Also: Beza's printed speech (HE1 27897).
- Lainez's editors: "nona scilicet die et sexta decima" (LAI6 188).
- Bonnet: "the Conferences of Poissy opened on the 9th September" (CLET4 10179-10180).
- Crespin's heading: "tenu au mois de Septembre & Octobre 1561" (CRES3 27486).

**10 September.**
- Beza wrote to the queen (HE1 28794).
- The prelates met, and Lorraine made his remark (section 10, line 7). The editors date it from Vermigli's letter of 12 September (HE1 29024-29027).

**15 September.**
- The prelates' assembly received the Jesuits, "le lundy quinziesme jour de septembre" (LAI8 39600-39601).
- Languet, in a Baum-Cunitz note: "in hac sua Synodo approbarunt ordinem Jesuitarum" (HE1 30405-30409).

**16 September, the second session.**
- Lorraine replied (HE1 29166-30330; Polanco, LAI8 37166-37168).
- The ministers asked to answer at once and were told a day would be set (HE1 30373-30386).

**24 September, the third meeting, in the "chambre priorale" (HE1 30589-30591).** Polanco: Wednesday the 24th (LAI6 3607, 3632). Coudret: "alli 24 et 26" (LAI6 3950).

**25 September.** Montluc and Despence met Beza and Des Gallars privately (HE1 33050-33150). The Sattler "Sommaire", in a Baum-Cunitz note, gives the 25th (HE1 33175-33183).

**26 September, the last session of the colloquy.**
- The Histoire: "Ceste troisiesme conférence ... fut la dernière" (HE1 33010-33013).
- The editors: "Le 26 septembre ... fut le dernier jour du Colloque; les conférences qui suivirent n'eurent que le caractère de conférences particulières" (HE1 33188-33190).
- Polanco: it "se acabó de noche, auiendo durado más de 5 horas" (LAI8 37425).

**29 and 30 September and 1 October: the conference of five and five at St-Germain (HE1 33195-33410).** The text's "26" for the first day is corrected to 29 by the editors (HE1 33187-33188).

**4 October.** The formula went to the prelates at Poissy (HE1 33434).

**9 October.** The prelates rejected it (HE1 33439-33440). Sattler's "Sommaire", in a note, confirms the vote on 9 October (HE1 33474-33479, 33665-33669).

**13-15 October.**
- The prelates, "ayans le 13 d'Octobre remercié & renvoyé leurs docteurs", went home (HE1 33870-33871).
- The canons are dated "Pridie Idus Oct." (14 October) (HE1 35140).
- Languet, 17 October: "Tandem solutus est conventus nostrorum Episcoporum nudiustertius" (HE1 33950-33951). (my tr.: "at last our bishops' assembly was dissolved the day before yesterday.")

**21 November.** Martyr was back in Zurich (Robinson, zurich-letters 5734; Bonnet, CLET4 9768).

## 7. Decided

The colloquy itself decided nothing jointly. The prelates' assembly acted on its own.

1. **It resolved to answer only two points, the Church and the Supper.**
   - It would do so "non pas toutesfois pour entrer en dispute" (not, though, to enter into debate).
   - It would also "dresser une confession de foy opposée à celle des ministres, laquelle s'ils refusoient d'approuver, sentence de condamnation seroit solennellement prononcée" (HE1 29001-29013; the editors trace it to La Place, HE1 29033-29035).
   - (my tr.: "draw up a confession of faith against the ministers'; if they refused to approve it, sentence of condemnation would be solemnly pronounced.")
2. **It received the Jesuits** "par forme de collége et non de religion nouuellement institutée". The condition was that they take "aultre tiltre que de société de Jésus ou de Jésuites" (LAI8 39602-39605). (my tr.: "as a college and not as a newly founded religious order ... another name than Society of Jesus or Jesuits.")
3. **It rejected the ministers' formula, 9 October.**
   - It found the formula "non seulement insuffisant, mais aussi captieux".
   - If Beza and his company "ne veulent presentement confesser & souscrire" the Catholic confession, the assembly resolved "de ne les plus ouïr en façon que ce soit".
   - It asked that they be barred from the kingdom, "auquel ... n'y a eu qu'un Dieu, un Roy, une foy, & une loy" (HE1 33676-33720).
   - The confession they had to sign: "le vray corps & sang de Jesus Christ est réellement & transubstantiellement sous les especes du pain & du vin" (HE1 33722-33729).
4. **Its canons on discipline, dated 14 October.**
   - The first canon covers the naming of bishops. A bishop named by the king is to be posted publicly and examined; he must make profession of faith before the chapter and preach (HE1 33965-34000).
   - The assembly submits "tous leurs décrets au vouloir & pouvoir de sa saincteté" (HE1 33939-33960).
   - The last canon tells the faithful to abhor "Luther, Zvingle & Calvin, herefiarques" (HE1 35108-35110).
   - The Histoire's judgment: "Voilà toute la reformation imaginaire couchée par escrit en ce colloque, & non jamais pratiquée" (HE1 35114-35115). That is the hostile narrator's view.
5. **The formula of 1 October** was worked out by the moderates of both sides: "Nous confessons que Jesus Christ en sa saincte Cène nous presente, donne & exhibe véritablement la substance de son corps & de son sang ..." (HE1 33383-33395). It was never adopted; see item 3.

## 8. Outcome

**The colloquy broke off.**
- The prelates refused any further hearing (HE1 33703-33711).
- The Histoire: "depuis ce temps là il ne leur fut possible d'obtenir aucune dispute sur leur confession de foy" (HE1 33741-33742).
- Beza on 12 April 1562: "Colloquium Possiacenum Lotharingii Cardinalis non tam astu quam impudenti audacia dissipatum est" (editors' note, HE1 33214-33215). (my tr.: "The Colloquy of Poissy was broken up by the Cardinal of Lorraine, not so much by cunning as by shameless daring.")

**Catherine's own account, October 1561.**
- The two meetings had brought only "Confusion de Disputes sur Disputes nourries de Dissencions et Discordes beaucoup plus que d'Union" (AYM 25545-25548). (my tr.: "confusion of disputes upon disputes, fed by dissension and discord far more than by union.")
- She then tried five or six of the most learned, but "ce Moien la ne plaisoit pas au Surplus de nosdits Prelatz et Docteurs" (that way did not please the rest of our prelates and doctors) (AYM 25550-25557).
- Of their canons: "il n'y a riens qui puisse pourvoir aux Troubles" (there is nothing in them that can remedy the troubles) (AYM 25572-25574).
- She still awaited "le prochain Concilie General", though "je ne sçai que m'en promettre assurement" (I do not know what to expect of it for certain) (AYM 25600-25602).

**Rome's view.**
- Sarpi: the Pope, "understanding that the Colloquy was dissolved without doing any thing, was very glad". He said Lainez "might be compared to the ancient Saints" (SARPI 42969-42974).
- Pallavicino: "La riuscita del Colloquio fù qual suol'essere in questi combattimenti, che niuna delle Parti s'arrenda e ciascuna poi attribuisca à sè una specie di vittoria" (PAL2 42041-42043). (my tr.: "The colloquy ended as such contests usually do: neither side surrendered, and each then claimed some kind of victory.")

**The Reformed gained open preaching.**
- Calvin, 24 September: "That was an auspicious day in which liberty was secured for the churches" (CLET4 10669-10671).
- Bonnet: "The Colloquy of Poissy, though without any results, everywhere increased the audacity of the Reformed" (CLET4 11032-11033).
- Pallavicino: French Catholics blamed the colloquy for "la libertà conseguita dagli Ugonotti à professar la lor Fede in aperto" (the freedom the Huguenots won to profess their faith openly) (PAL2 42068-42070).

**The Edict of January, 17 January 1562.**
- It allowed Reformed gatherings "de jour hors desdites villes". It did so "par provision, & jusques à la détermination dudit Concile general" (HE1 37078-37082).
- It gives its reason as "en attendant que Dieu nous face la grâce de les pouvoir reunir & remettre en une mesme bergerie, qui est tout nostre desir & principale intention" (HE1 37070-37073). (my tr.: "until God grants us the grace to reunite them and bring them back into one fold, which is all our desire and chief intention.")

**The crown kept trying.**
- A royal letter, which Baum-Cunitz date to 20 January 1562 (HE1 35302-35313), plans for Sorbonne doctors to meet the legate.
- They were to seek "une si bonne modération & pacification de tous nos differens, que cela fust cause de ramener ceux de ladite nouvelle religion à l'obeissance de nostre Eglise Catholique & Romaine" (HE1 35420-35427).

**Reach beyond France.**
- **The Lutherans.**
  - Lorraine read part of a German confession and pressed the ministers to sign (HE1 32088-32095). It proved to be a copy of a Württemberg confession of 1559 (HE1 32150-32156; editors, HE1 32175-32178).
  - The Histoire says Lorraine sent for Augsburg-confession theologians "afin de les diviser" (HE1 29115-29118).
  - They came after the end and did nothing: "Eos nihil effecisse non est mirum, cum non fuerint inter se concordes" (Languet, in an editors' note, HE1 33905-33908).
  - Beza's view of the Lutherans, from his Apologia, in an editors' note: "pro fratribus charissimis nos habere quos Protestantes appellas: nec nisi in pauculis ab Augustana Confessione dissidere" (HE1 32128-32130).
- **Trent.**
  - Waterworth: "the articles proposed at the Conference of Poissy" were secretly examined at Trent in 1562 (waterworth 9100-9103).
  - In September 1562 the legates wrote that the French would not rest until points from "l'assemblea di Poissì" were taken up (PAL3 9900-9906).
  - Lorraine at Trent, in Pallavicino's report, November 1563: "a grandissimo stento eransi ritenuti il clero e gli stati di quel reame raunati in Poissì dal pigliare altra provvisione finchè il concilio si terminasse" (PAL3 53712-53715). (my tr.: "with the greatest difficulty the clergy and Estates of that realm, gathered at Poissy, had been held back from taking another course until the council ended.")
- **The Jesuits.** Their order "era stata riceuuta in Franza in questa assemblea delli vescoui" (Coudret, LAI6 3938-3939).
- **Scotland and England.** See section 13 (Melville) and section 5 (Des Gallars of London).

## 9. The refused party's own voice

The Reformed were the party refused a further hearing. The Histoire holds them in their own words.
- Their mandate: "nous sommes icy de la part d'un million de personnes qui sont en ce Royaume, en Suisse, en Polongne, en Alemagne, en Angleterre, & en Escosse" (HE1 32498-32501). This is from their written declaration of 26 September, read by Beza. (my tr.: "we are here on behalf of a million people in this kingdom, in Switzerland, Poland, Germany, England and Scotland.")
- Against signing a clipped article: "on nous aura baillé la dixiesme partie d'un article, & dit: Signés cela, sinon nous ne passerons point outre" (HE1 32503-32505). (my tr.: "they will have given us a tenth of an article and said: Sign that, or we go no further.")
- Polanco gives the formula of 26 September as: "Credimus in vsu coenae dominicae veré, reipsa, substantialiter seu in ipsa substantia verum corpus et sanguinem X.i spirituali et inefabili modo esse, exhiberi et sumi a fidelibus communicantibus" (LAI8 37339-37342). He calls it "diuersíssima de la que les fué propuesta" (LAI8 37344).

The Reformed are also described by opponents:
- Lainez: "la loro chiesa, ministri et senso ... non sonó catholici, ma simie delli catholici" (LAI8 37487-37489). (my tr.: "their church, ministers and meaning ... are not Catholic, but apes of the Catholics.")
- Polanco: "los ministros (como ellos se llaman, y lo son de Sathanás)" (LAI6 3605-3606). (my tr.: "the ministers, as they call themselves, and they are Satan's.")

## 11. Contested points

1. **Who wrote the Histoire.**
   - It is attributed to Beza, but its title page names no author (HE1 110-130).
   - Reuss: Beza directed it and gave his Poissy memories, but did not compile it (HE3 2002-2010).
   - Soldan and Heppe deny that he wrote it (HE3 1629-1648).
2. **Beza's sentence: the bread, or the bread and wine?**
   - The speech text: "du pain & du vin, autant que le plus haut ciel" (HE1 28516-28517).
   - The narrative: "aussi loin du pain que le haut des cieux" (HE1 28712-28713).
   - Vermigli, quoting Lorraine: "plus distare a pane et vino, quam distet coelum a terra" (HE1 32170-32171).
   - Bonnet: "as far removed from the bread as heaven is distant from the earth" (CLET4 10689-10690).
   **Was he stopped?**
   - Sarpi: he spoke "with such heat ... that he was commanded to conclude" (SARPI 42836-42838).
   - Against Sarpi:
     - The Histoire: "Le Roy ne bougea", and Beza finished (HE1 28723-28727).
     - Languet, in a note: "lussi tamen quiescere, passi sunt eum perorare" (HE1 28772-28773).
     - Catherine, 14 September, says she was close to silencing him, "peu s'en fallut, que je ne luy imposasse silence", but held back (HE1 28785-28812).
   - Brulart, in a note, says someone cried "Blasphème" and Beza "perdist son premier propos" (HE1 28736-28741).
3. **The prelates' numbers.**
   - Bishops: 36 (HE1 27678), 40 (PAL2 42006), 25 on 26 September (LAI6 3662).
   - Cardinals: six (HE1 27653-27678; SARPI 42788) against "cinque ... oltre il Legato" (PAL2 42004-42005).
4. **The ministers' numbers.** Twelve with 22 deputies (HE1 27840-27841) against fourteen (SARPI 42733).
5. **The order on 9 September.** Were the ministers brought in after Tournon spoke (HE1 27840-27842)? Or were they present from the start (La Place and De Serres, as the editors cite them, HE1 27845-27850)?
6. **What Beza gave in writing on 26 September.**
   - Polanco: the Latin formula "Credimus in vsu coenae ... substantialiter" (LAI8 37326-37342).
   - The Histoire prints a French statement without "substantialiter" (HE1 32566-32572). It says the Latin formula came from the private meeting of 25 September (HE1 33132-33148). The ministers then agreed "qu'on ne s'y arresteroit" (that they would not rely on it) (HE1 33150-33155).
   - The Sattler "Sommaire", in an editors' note, says Beza and Des Gallars agreed to the formula but could not bring Martyr round (HE1 33175-33183).
7. **Were the ministers divided?**
   - Despence and others made much of Martyr's "pro parte mea" (HE1 33256-33257).
   - The Histoire denies a split: "il n'y eut jamais dispute ne différent quelconque entre eux sur la doctrine" (HE1 33150-33152).
   - Vermigli to Bullinger: "Collegae mei videntur aliquid remittere" (my colleagues seem to give something up) (HE1 33238-33239).
8. **Was the legate present?**
   - Polanco and Coudret say Ferrara would not attend the disputations (LAI6 3701-3702, 3957).
   - Pallavicino reports Ferrara's own claim that his face ("volto") gave the Catholic doctors strength at the colloquy (PAL2 46462-46468).
9. **Lainez's tone.**
   - The Histoire: "un amas d'injures & de mesdisance", calling the ministers "singes & regnards" (apes and foxes) (HE1 32807-32811). His own text does use "simie" and "uolpi" (LAI8 37469-37489).
   - Polanco: the doctors near him were "quasi jubilar" (almost rejoicing) (LAI6 3809).
   - Pallavicino defends him against Sarpi (PAL2 42044-42062).
   - The editors: no Protestant source names him (HE1 32822-32823).
10. **Did Lorraine approve the formula of 1 October?**
    - The Histoire: "il est certain qu'il prononça ces mots: que jamais il n'avoit creu autrement" (HE1 33404-33405).
    - Des Gallars, in a note, confirms it (HE1 33445-33449).
    - Beza's letter of 4 October "ne dit rien de la parole attribuée au cardinal" (says nothing of the words ascribed to the cardinal) (HE1 33444).
11. **When the prelates' assembly opened:** see section 6.
12. **"The hope of a common French church ended": whose judgment?** No held text says it.
    - For "ended":
      - Calvin, beforehand: "the boasted results of the conference would come to nothing" (CLET4 10204-10205).
      - Coudret: "poca speranza" (LAI6 3991).
      - Pius IV, in Pallavicino's report: he saw France "in tal precipizio che già ne disperava" (in such a fall that he already despaired of it) (PAL2 42310-42311).
      - Bonnet: "the absolute incompatibility existing between the Church of Rome and that of Christ" (CLET4 10692-10693).
    - Against:
      - The legate Ferrara refused to judge France's religion "si ruinoso che se ne dovesse disperare" (PAL2 42337-42338).
      - The crown, in January 1562, still hoped for "une mesme bergerie" (HE1 37072) and planned more talks (HE1 35420-35427).
      - Catherine still looked to the general council (AYM 25600).
    - The scan row's "the hope ... ended" is the project's summary. Tell it as one view among these, or drop it.

## 16. Traditions touched (census ids at the date)

**Gathered**
- **the-huguenots (1559-1685).** Its ministers and 22 lay deputies of the French churches were heard. They presented their Confession (HE1 27840-27841, 28727-28744).
- **the-tridentine-church (1517-1650).** The French cardinals and bishops met as the prelates' assembly, and the legate came from Rome (HE1 27653-27680, 30472-30475).
- **the-reformed-cities-zurich-and-geneva (1519-1650).** Beza came from Geneva and Martyr from Zurich (HE1 26001-26004).
- **the-society-of-jesus (1540-1650).** Lainez, its general, spoke (LAI8 37434). The prelates' assembly received the Society in France on 15 September (LAI8 39599-39605).

**Refused**
- **the-huguenots.** The prelates refused to hear them further unless they signed the Catholic confession (HE1 33703-33711).

**Affected**
- **lutheran-wittenberg-and-its-congregations.** A Württemberg confession was used against the ministers (HE1 32150-32156). Lutheran theologians were sent for "afin de les diviser" (HE1 29115-29118).
- **the-scottish-reformation-and-kirk.** Melville cites Poissy in 1607 as a warning (melville 33968).
- **the-english-reformation-and-elizabethan-settleme** (weakly).
  - Des Gallars was pastor of the London church (HE1 26972-26973) and wrote to the Bishop of London about the conferences (HE1 33229).
  - The ministers claimed to speak for people "en Angleterre, & en Escosse" (HE1 32500-32501).

## 17. Length

**The held record can carry the full ceiling: about 400 words, 2 minutes 30 seconds.**
- There is a named room, an hour and a seating plan (HE1 27647-27682).
- The king's and the chancellor's speeches are held.
- Participants on both sides speak in their own words:
  - Beza, in the Histoire.
  - Lainez and Polanco, in the Monumenta.
  - Catherine, in Aymon.
- There is a clear break-point: the "heaven" sentence and the cry "Blasphemavit".
- The prelates' own refusal and canons are held, and so is the sequel, the Edict of January.

**Three limits must be said once:**
1. The fullest account is a Reformed history whose authorship is contested.
2. There are no joint signed acts of the colloquy.
3. The counts differ by witness.

**Do not assert the closing judgment** ("the hope ... ended"). The crown's own edict still hoped for "une mesme bergerie" (HE1 37072).
