## 1. Held sources

### Who wrote the Histoire ecclésiastique (caution 1)
- **The 1580 title page names no author.** It reads "HISTOIRE ECCLESIASTIQVE DES EGLISES REFORMEES AV ROYAUME de France ... A ANVERS. 1580" (HE1 112-130). The Library header says so too (HE1 2).
- **Reuss, the editor, puts the attribution as a question.** His heading is "THÉODORE DE BÈZE EST-IL L'AUTEUR DE L'HISTOIRE ECCLÉSIASTIQUE?" (HE3 785-786). He says the tradition's foundations "ne sont rien moins que solides" (are anything but solid) (HE3 797-798).
- **Reuss cites evidence from the Poissy chapter itself.**
  - The narrator writes of Beza "comme depuis je le luy ay ouï dire" (as I have since heard him say). Reuss says this sets narrator and minister "si nettement distinguées" (so clearly apart) (HE3 1641-1651).
  - Soldan and Heppe, cited by Reuss, said Beza would not have praised his own speech as "fort agréable" (HE3 1628-1636).
- **Reuss's conclusion:** "Ce ne fut pas, à coup sûr, Bèze lui-même qui trouva le loisir de se livrer à ce travail de compilation littéraire, mais il le dirigea de près." He says the compiling was done by "Nicolas des Gallars, soit par Simon Goulart, ou tel autre". Beza, he says, "y contribua sans doute ... par ses réminiscences du colloque de Poissy" (HE3 2002-2010). He calls the book "l'œuvre collective du protestantisme français" (HE3 1873-1874).
- **Printing place, by Reuss:** the work "a paru à Genève", despite "l'indication d'Anvers" on the title (HE3 428-435).
- **Its main source.** Much of the Poissy chapter copies Pierre de la Place's *Commentaires* (1565). Reuss calls La Place "président de la Cour des Aides", "protestant convaincu", killed at St Bartholomew (HE3 3232-3243). Baum-Cunitz's notes say so passage by passage: "Le résume de ce discours donné ici est littéralement identique avec celui qui s'en trouve dans De la Place" (HE1 27736-27737); also HE1 27841-27848 and 28756-28758.
- **How the brief should name it.** Suggested: "the *Ecclesiastical History of the Reformed Churches*, printed in 1580 with no author's name and long credited to Beza". Then: "Its editor Reuss thought Beza directed it and gave his own memories of Poissy, but did not compile it himself."
- **Its side.** It is a Reformed narrative and polemical in places. The prelates, for example, are "du tout incapable de traitter de la religion" (HE1 26874-26875).
- **What it prints.** It prints Beza's speech of 9 September (HE1 27866 ff.), as the participant's own words in that text. Baum-Cunitz say a first printing was "faite probablement par les soins de Bèze lui-même, peu de jours après le colloque" (HE1 27882-27883).

### Acts (the meeting's own documents)
- **The canons of the assembly of prelates.** The Histoire prints a French text (HE1 33915-35111). Its preamble reads "Le Roy tres-chrestien ayant mandé les Prélats de l'Eglise Gallicane, pour se trouver à Poissy" (HE1 33915-33916). The Latin closing comes from Languet's letters, through Baum-Cunitz's note: "Acta Poissiaci ... anno humanae salutis 1561. Pridie Idus Oct." (14 October) (HE1 35130-35140). Witness kind: acts, though in a hostile setting.
- **The prelates' resolution of 9 October** rejecting the ministers' formula, with their "Confession catholique" (HE1 33650-33730). The Sorbonne faculty's judgment is at HE1 33488-33600. Kind: acts, as the Histoire prints them.
- **The ministers' petitions** of 17 August (HE1 27050-27126) and 8 September (HE1 27555-27610), and their signed declaration of 26 September (HE1 32354-32583). Kind: acts of the Reformed party.
- **The canons' acceptance of the Jesuits.** The Parlement's letters of 14 February 1562 cite "l'acte d'approbation et reception des dicts pbres. et escolliers faicte en l'assemblée du clergé et concile national tenu a Poissy le lundy quinziesme jour de septembre dernier" (LAI8 39598-39601). Kind: acts, quoted in a royal and Parlement document.
- **The Edict of January**, St-Germain-en-Laye, 17 January 1561 old style (1562), printed in full (HE1 36904-37241). It is also summarized in Crespin (CRES3 30506-30540). Kind: acts, of what followed.

## 2. Candidate opening scenes

**A. The nuns' refectory, noon, 9 September (strongest).**
- The setting: "Le lendemain 9 de Septembre, environ midi, s'assemblerent à Poissy, au grand refectoir des nonnains, le Roy ..." (HE1 27647-27648). (my tr.: "The next day, 9 September, about noon, there gathered at Poissy, in the great refectory of the nuns, the King ...")
- The seating: three cardinals on each side, and below them "trente six Evesques qu'Arcevesques" (HE1 27677-27678).
- The ministers stood at the barrier: "estans appuyés testes nues" (leaning there bare-headed) (HE1 27900).
- The king's words: "je vous ay fait assembler de divers lieux de mon royaume ... j'ay délibéré que vous ne bougiés de ce lieu, jusqu'à ce que vous y ayés donné bon ordre" (HE1 27683-27694). (my tr.: "I have had you gathered from many parts of my kingdom ... I have resolved that you shall not stir from this place until you have set it in good order.")
- Then Beza, "esleu de tous les autres pour ce faire" (chosen by all the others to do it), knelt and prayed (HE1 27901-27912). Sarpi: "having prayed on his knee" (SARPI 42821).

**B. The one sentence that broke the room (same day, later).**
- The Histoire's narrative: Beza said Christ's body "estoit toutesfois aussi loin du pain que le haut des cieux est eslongné de la terre" (HE1 28712-28713).
- "Cette seule parole ... fut cause que les prelats commencèrent à bruire et murmurer, dont les uns disoient: Blasphemavit; les autres se levoient pour s'en aller" (HE1 28714-28719).
- Tournon asked the king and queen "qu'on imposast silence à de Beze, ou qu'il luy fust permis et à sa compagnie de se retirer" (HE1 28720-28722).
- Beza: "Messieurs, je vous prie d'attendre la conclusion qui vous contentera" (HE1 28724-28725).
- (my tr.: "aussi loin du pain ...": "as far from the bread as the top of the heavens is from the earth"; "Blasphemavit": "He has blasphemed.")

**C. Before the room: the night of 24 August, in the King of Navarre's chamber.**
- Lorraine and Beza met before Catherine (HE1 27410-27461). Lorraine: "je ne suis pas si noir qu'on m'a fait" (I am not as black as I have been painted) (HE1 27461).
- Madame de Crussol, in Beza's letter of 25 August as Baum-Cunitz quote it: "Bon homme pour ce soir, mays demain quoy?" (Good man for tonight, but tomorrow what?) (HE1 27508).
- A good human scene, but it rests on Reformed witnesses only.

**D. The Catholic side's scene, 26 September (Polanco).**
- The rain and the queen: "vino la reina madre, no obstante la lluuia" (LAI6 3682).
- Lainez spoke "en italiano, y siempre enderezando su razonamiento a la reyna, por spacio me pareze de tres quartos de hora" (LAI6 3735-3737). (my tr.: "in Italian, always turning his speech to the queen, for about three quarters of an hour, I think.")
- A Jesuit eyewitness. It pairs well with scene A if the profile wants both voices.

## 3. Issue: what brought them together

- **The prelates' own reform.** The Histoire says the prelates' assembly was "derechef arrestée" (resolved again) in the July 1561 assembly at the Parlement of Paris. The ministers would have safe-conduct "pour s'y trouver, et y estre ouis, afin d'adviser s'il y auroit moyen de les reunir à l'Eglise Romaine" (HE1 25995-26000).
  - (my tr.: "to be heard there, to see whether there were any means to reunite them to the Roman Church.")
  - Baum-Cunitz's note: the prelates' assembly was called for 20 July to choose its deputies to the Estates General. It was also to "préparer la convocation d'un concile par le pape et délibérer en attendant des réformes ecclésiastiques" (HE1 25790-25792). This is the editors' account.
- **The Estates asked for a national council.** Bretagne, speaking for the Third Estate at the Estates (moved to Pontoise and continued at St-Germain), asked the king to "indire et assigner un Concile national libre et légitime" with safe-conduct. He asked that the king preside over it (HE1 26213-26528).
  - A further list of requests asks for the Edict of July revoked and abuses removed "par un Concile national libre et légitime, ainsi qu'il avoit esté jà promis" (HE1 26785-26790).
  - The Histoire says the Third Estate "seul pour lors toucha au droit but" (alone hit the mark) (HE1 26766-26767). That is partisan praise.
- **The crown's aim, in L'Hospital's opening speech** (the Histoire's summary, copied from La Place). The king had granted "un saufconduit aux ministres de ceste secte, esperant qu'une conférence avec eux amiable et gratieuse pourroit grandement profiter" (HE1 27782-27784).
  - The prelates were to receive them "comme le père fait ses enfans". If that failed, at least "on ne pourroit dire ci après ... qu'ils ayent esté condamnés sans les ouir" (HE1 27785-27790).
  - A second summary, from the original "Discours des Actes", has L'Hospital say not to wait for the general council: "user de nos remèdes presens et domestiques ... en ce Concile national" (HE1 27745-27750, Baum-Cunitz's note quoting it).
  - Sarpi's version: a national council had corrected the general council of Rimini (SARPI 42790-42793).
- **The Reformed aim.** The ministers' conditions: the bishops "ne soient point nos juges, attendu qu'ils sont nos parties". All differences are to be "jugés et décidés par la seule parole de Dieu" (HE1 27108-27119). Beza's speech: "Nostre desir est ... que ces troupeaux ... soient ralliés et recueillis en la bergerie de ce souverain et unique Pasteur" (HE1 28084-28089).
- **The Catholic objection in principle.**
  - Lainez: matters of faith belong "al summo sacerdote et al concilio genérale"; with the council open, "non par' conueniente ni legitimo far' simili congregationi" (LAI8 37505-37508).
  - Pallavicino: no national council was concluded. The Catholics thought one "ingiurioso all'autorità del Concilio Generale, che già stava in atto". The Reformed knew they would not get judging power "da' Prelati di Francia" (PAL2 41991-41999).

## 4. Called by

- **The king (the Histoire and the acts).**
  - The king's own words: "je vous ay fait assembler" (HE1 27683).
  - The canons' preamble: "Le Roy tres-chrestien ayant mandé les Prélats de l'Eglise Gallicane" (HE1 33915).
  - Martyr and Beza were sent for "par letres expresses du Roy de Navarre" (HE1 26000-26002). The Histoire adds that Beza was "expressement mandé par les Roy de Navarre et Prince de Condé" (HE1 27389-27390).
  - Vermigli, in Robinson's quotation, says the safe-conduct came "in the name of the king and of the queen mother" (zurich-letters 5726-5729).
- **Catherine claims the colloquy as her own decision:** "je trouvé bon que nosdits Prelatz et Evêques entrassent en quelque Colloque gracieux avec lesdits Ministres" (AYM 25543-25544).
- **Two earlier assemblies.** L'Hospital speaks of the safe-conduct "suivant ce qui jà avoit esté arresté par les deux assemblées" (HE1 27781-27782). Which two is not said (likely the Estates and the Parlement assembly; unsure).
- **The Cardinal of Lorraine's part.** The Histoire says the colloquy had been "arresté" before the Paris assembly that Lorraine urged (HE1 25454-25455).
- **No held source disputes that the crown called it.** The Estates at Pontoise asked for a national council (section 3), but no held line says they called Poissy.

## 6. Dates

- **The prelates' assembly opened in late July or early August. It is contested and told in a Baum-Cunitz note.**
  - Languet's letter of 1 August: "Ante triduum habuit initium conventus Episcoporum ... Indictus erat ad 20 Julii" (HE1 26894-26895, 26028-26030).
  - Brulart: convoked "le premier jour d'Aoust" (HE1 26034-26035).
  - De Thou: 10 August, "Cette date ne peut pas être exacte" (editors, HE1 26026-26027).
  - Chantonnay, 26 July: "l'assemblée des Evesques sera Dimanche", which the editors read as 3 August (HE1 26036-26037).
  - Sarpi: "In August the Prelates did assemble in Poissi" (SARPI 42729).
- **The ministers' first petition, 17 August (Histoire, HE1 27047-27048).** Goulart gives 10 August. Beza's letter of 22 August says the eight left for the court on the 12th and gave a "supplicem libellum". The editors weigh it (HE1 27030-27047). Reuss lists the five-day gap among the points against Beza's authorship (HE3 1609-1617).
- **Beza reached St-Germain on 23 August (HE1 27390-27391)** and preached the next day (HE1 27391-27392). The editors, from his letter, give Paris on 22 August; he left Geneva on 16 August (HE1 26043-26044). The night meeting with Catherine and Lorraine was "ce jour mesme" (HE1 27393).
- **Vermigli reached Paris on 9 September and St-Germain on 10 September** (his journal, editors' note HE1 26940-26941; also HE1 30647-30650). The Histoire's text puts him earlier; the editors call it an error.
- **The second petition, 8 September (HE1 27551-27552).** Beza's letter says the 7th, which the editors call his error (HE1 27556-27562).
- **The opening session, 9 September, about noon** (HE1 27647). Also: Beza's printed speech (HE1 27897); Lainez's editors, "nona scilicet die et sexta decima" (LAI6 188); Bonnet, "opened on the 9th September" (CLET4 10179). The Histoire's chapter heading reads "Septembre et Octobre 1561" (CRES3 27486).
- **10 September:** Beza's letter to the queen (HE1 28858). The prelates met, and Lorraine made his remark; the editors date it from Vermigli's letter of 12 September (HE1 29016-29019).
- **15 September:** the prelates' assembly approved the Jesuits, Monday 15 September (LAI8 39600-39601). Languet, in an editors' note, says the same (HE1 30422-30427).
- **16 September, the second session:** Lorraine's reply (HE1 29164; PAL2 and Polanco, LAI8 37163-37165). The ministers were refused an immediate answer (HE1 30410-30418).
- **24 September, the third meeting, in the "chambre priorale"** (HE1 30589-30591). Polanco: Wednesday the 24th (LAI6 3609; note, LAI6 3632). Coudret: "alli 24 et 26" (LAI6 3946).
- **25 September:** Montluc and Despence met Beza and Des Gallars privately (HE1 33058-33076). The Sattler "Sommaire", in an editors' note, gives the 25th (HE1 33175-33183).
- **26 September, the last session of the colloquy.** The Histoire: "Cefte troifiefme conférence ... fut la dernière" (HE1 33010-33013). The editors: "Le 26 septembre ... fut le dernier jour du Colloque; les conférences qui suivirent n'eurent que le caractère de conférences particulières" (HE1 33188-33190). Polanco: Friday the 26th, lasting "más de 5 horas" (LAI8 37425).
- **29 and 30 September and 1 October:** the conference of five and five at St-Germain (HE1 33195-33380). The text's "26" is corrected to 29 by the editors (HE1 33186-33188).
- **4 October:** the formula went to the prelates at Poissy (HE1 33435). **9 October:** they rejected it (HE1 33439-33440; the Sattler "Sommaire", in a note, confirms the 9th, HE1 33470-33474).
- **13 October:** the prelates "ayans ... remercié et renvoyé leurs docteurs" (HE1 33870). The canons are dated "Pridie Idus Oct." (14 October) (HE1 35140). Languet on 17 October says the assembly was dissolved "nudiustertius" (HE1 33945-33946).
- **Martyr was back in Zurich on 21 November** (Robinson, zurich-letters 5734; Bonnet, CLET4 9768).

## 7. Decided

The colloquy itself decided nothing jointly. The prelates' assembly acted on its own:

1. **It resolved to answer only two points, the Church and the Supper.** "Non pas toutesfois pour entrer en dispute". It also agreed "de dresser une confession de foy opposée à celle des ministres, laquelle s'ils refusoient d'approuver, sentence de condamnation seroit solennellement prononcée" (HE1 28993-29011, after La Place).
2. **It accepted the Jesuits** "par forme de collége et non de religion nouvellement institutée", on condition that they take "aultre tiltre que de société de Jésus" (LAI8 39602-39605).
3. **It rejected the ministers' formula, 9 October.** It found the formula "non seulement insuffisant, mais aussi captieux". If Beza "ne veulent presentement confesser et souscrire" the Catholic confession, the assembly resolved "de ne les plus ouïr en façon que ce soit". They should be barred from the kingdom, "auquel ... n'y a eu qu'un Dieu, un Roy, une foy, et une loy" (HE1 33680-33722).
   - Their confession: "le vray corps et sang de Jesus Christ est réellement et transubstantiellement sous les especes du pain et du vin" (HE1 33725-33727).
4. **Its canons on discipline,** dated 14 October. The opening article says a bishop named by the king is to be posted publicly, examined, profess his faith and preach (HE1 33935-33990). The assembly submits all its decrees "au vouloir et pouvoir de sa saincteté" (HE1 33933-33936).
   - The closing article names "Luther, Zvingle et Calvin, herefiarques" (HE1 35108-35111).
   - The Histoire's judgment: "Voilà toute la reformation imaginaire couchée par escrit en ce colloque, et non jamais pratiquée" (HE1 35113-35115). This is the hostile narrator's view.
5. **The formula worked out by the two sides' moderates** ("Nous confessons que Jesus Christ en sa saincte Cène nous presente, donne et exhibe véritablement la substance de son corps et de son sang ...", HE1 33395-33407) was never adopted (item 3).

## 8. Outcome

- **The colloquy broke off.** The prelates refused any further hearing (HE1 33722-33752). The Histoire: "depuis ce temps là il ne leur fut possible d'obtenir aucune dispute sur leur confession de foy" (HE1 33751-33753).
  - Beza on 12 April 1562: "Colloquium Possiacenum Lotharingii Cardinalis non tam astu quam impudenti audacia dissipatum est" (HE1 33213-33215, editors' note). (my tr.: "The Colloquy of Poissy was scattered by the Cardinal of Lorraine, not so much by cunning as by shameless daring.")
- **Catherine's own account, October 1561:**
  - The two public meetings brought "Confusion de Disputes sur Disputes nourries de Dissencions et Discordes beaucoup plus que d'Union" (AYM 25545-25548).
  - She then tried five or six of the most learned, but "ce Moien la ne plaisoit pas au Surplus de nosdits Prelatz et Docteurs" (AYM 25551-25558).
  - Of their canons: "il n'y a riens qui puisse pourvoir aux Troubles" (AYM 25572-25574).
  - She still awaited "le prochain Concilie General", though "je ne sçai que m'en promettre assurement" (AYM 25600-25602).
- **Rome's view.**
  - Sarpi: the Pope, "understanding that the Colloquy was dissolved without doing any thing, was very glad", and compared the Jesuit to "the ancient Saints" (SARPI 42969-42972).
  - Pallavicino: "La riuscita del Colloquio fù qual suol'essere in questi combattimenti, che niuna delle Parti s'arrenda e ciascuna poi attribuisca à sè una specie di vittoria" (PAL2 42041-42043). (my tr.: "The colloquy ended as such contests usually do: neither side surrendered, and each then claimed some kind of victory.")
- **The Reformed gained open preaching.**
  - Calvin, 24 September: "That was an auspicious day in which liberty was secured for the churches" (CLET4 10669-10671).
  - Bonnet's note: "The Colloquy of Poissy, though without any results, everywhere increased the audacity of the Reformed" (CLET4 11032).
  - Pallavicino: French Catholics blamed the colloquy for "la libertà conseguita dagli Ugonotti à professar la lor Fede in aperto" (PAL2 42068-42070).
- **The Edict of January, 17 January 1562.**
  - It allowed Reformed gatherings "de jour hors desdites villes" "par provision, et jusques à la détermination dudit Concile general" (HE1 37078-37082).
  - It says this is "en attendant que Dieu nous face la grâce de les pouvoir reunir et remettre en une mesme bergerie, qui est tout nostre desir et principale intention" (HE1 37070-37073). (my tr.: "until God grants us the grace to reunite them and bring them back into one fold, which is all our desire and chief intention.")
- **The crown kept trying.** A royal letter that Baum-Cunitz date to 20 January 1562 plans to have Sorbonne doctors meet the legate. They were to seek "une si bonne modération et pacification de tous nos differens, que cela fust cause de ramener ceux de ladite nouvelle religion à l'obeissance de nostre Eglise Catholique et Romaine" (HE1 35408-35422; the editors' dating is at HE1 35304-35317).
- **Reach beyond France.**
  - The Lutherans. Lorraine read part of a German confession, which turned out to be Württemberg's of 1559, and pressed the ministers to sign (HE1 32064-32076, 32150-32156; editors identify it, HE1 32183-32188).
  - The Histoire says Lorraine sent for Augsburg-confession theologians "afin de les diviser" (HE1 29107-29112). They came after the end and did nothing, "cum non fuerint inter se concordes" (Languet via the editors, HE1 33905-33908).
  - Beza's declaration on the Lutherans: "pour freres charissimos nos habere quos Protestantes appellas: nec nisi in pauculis ab Augustana Confessione dissidere" (Beza's Apologia, in a Baum-Cunitz note, HE1 32116-32118).
  - Trent. Waterworth says "the articles proposed at the Conference of Poissy" were secretly examined at Trent in 1562 (waterworth 9100-9103). The legates in September 1562 wrote that the French would not rest until points from "l'assemblea di Poissì" were taken up (PAL3 9900-9906).
  - Lorraine at Trent, in Pallavicino's report: "a grandissimo stento eransi ritenuti il clero e gli stati di quel reame raunati in Poissì dal pigliare altra provvisione finchè il concilio si terminasse" (PAL3 53712-53715).
  - The Jesuits: their admission in France was "receuuta in Franza in questa assemblea delli vescoui" (Coudret, LAI6 3938-3939).
  - Scotland and England: section 13 (Melville), and Des Gallars of London (section 5).

## 9. The refusing party's own voice

The Reformed were the party refused a further hearing, and the Histoire holds them in their own words.
- On their mandate: "nous sommes icy de la part d'un million de personnes qui sont en ce Royaume, en Suisse, en Polongne, en Alemagne, en Angleterre, et en Escosse" (HE1 32498-32501). This is their written declaration of 26 September, read by Beza.
- Against signing a clipped article: "on nous aura baillé la dixiesme partie d'un article, et dit: Signés cela, sinon nous ne passerons point outre" (HE1 32503-32505).
- In Polanco's commentarium, the 26 September formula is "Credimus in vsu coenae dominicae veré, reipsa, substantialiter ... spirituali et inefabili modo" (LAI8 37332-37335). Polanco calls it "diuersíssima de la que les fué propuesta" (LAI8 37336-37337).

The condemned side's views also reach us through their opponents:
- Lainez: "la loro chiesa, ministri et senso ... non sonó catholici, ma simie delli catholici" (LAI8 37487-37489).
- Polanco: "los ministros (como ellos se llaman, y lo son de Sathanás)" (LAI6 3605-3606).

## 11. Contested points

1. **Who wrote the Histoire.** It is attributed to Beza; the 1580 title page names no author (HE1 112-130). Reuss: Beza directed it and gave his Poissy memories, but did not compile it (HE3 2002-2010). Soldan and Heppe deny his authorship (HE3 1636-1650).
2. **What Beza's sentence said: the bread, or the bread and wine.**
   - The speech text: "son corps est esloigné du pain et du vin, autant que le plus haut ciel" (HE1 28516-28517).
   - The narrative: "aussi loin du pain que le haut des cieux" (HE1 28712-28713).
   - Vermigli, quoting Lorraine: "plus distare a pane et vino, quam distet coelum a terra" (HE1 32128-32129).
   - Bonnet: "as far removed from the bread as heaven is distant from the earth" (CLET4 10688-10689).
   - Brulart, in a note, says someone cried "Blasphème" (HE1 28736-28737). Catherine calls it "une comparaison si absurde et tant offensive" (HE1 28788-28789). Sarpi says he "spake with such heat ... that he was commanded to conclude" (SARPI 42825-42827). But the Histoire says the king "ne bougea" and Beza finished (HE1 28723-28725). Languet: "lussi tamen quiescere, passi sunt eum perorare" (HE1 28750-28751). Sarpi's "commanded to conclude" stands against the rest.
3. **The prelates' numbers:** 36 (HE1 27678), 40 (PAL2 42006), 25 on 26 September (LAI6 3662). The cardinals: 6 (HE1, SARPI) against "cinque ... oltre il Legato" (PAL2 42004-42005).
4. **The ministers' numbers:** twelve with 22 deputies (HE1 27840-27841) against fourteen (SARPI 42733).
5. **The order on 9 September.** Were the ministers brought in after Tournon spoke (HE1 27840-27845), or present from the start (La Place, De Serres, cited by the editors, HE1 27847-27850)?
6. **What Beza gave in writing on 26 September.**
   - Polanco: the Latin formula "Credimus in vsu coenae ... substantialiter" (LAI8 37322-37335).
   - The Histoire: a French statement without "substantialiter" (HE1 32563-32570). It says the Latin formula came from the private meeting of 25 September and was set aside: "il fut arresté seulement qu'on ne s'y arresteroit" (HE1 33141-33143).
   - Sattler's Catholic "Sommaire", in an editors' note, says Beza and Des Gallars agreed to it but could not bring Martyr round (HE1 33175-33183).
7. **Were the ministers divided?** Despence and others made much of Martyr's "pro mea parte" (HE1 33255-33259). The Histoire denies any split: "il n'y eut jamais dispute ne différent quelconque entre eux sur la doctrine" (HE1 33137-33139). Vermigli wrote: "Collegae mei videntur aliquid remittere" (HE1 33221-33223).
8. **The legate's presence.** Polanco and Coudret say Ferrara would not attend the disputations (LAI6 3701-3702, 3952-3953). Pallavicino reports Ferrara's own claim that his face ("volto") strengthened the Catholic doctors at the colloquy (PAL2 46462-46468).
9. **Lainez's tone.**
   - The Histoire: "un amas d'injures et de mesdisance", calling the ministers "singes et regnards" (HE1 32807-32811). His own text does call them "simie" and "uolpi" (LAI8 37469-37489).
   - Polanco: those near him "quasi jubilar" (LAI6 3769-3771). Pallavicino defends him against Sarpi (PAL2 42044-42062).
   - The Histoire's editors: no Protestant source names him (HE1 32832-32834).
10. **Did Lorraine approve the 1 October formula?** The Histoire: "il est certain qu'il prononça ces mots: que jamais il n'avoit creu autrement" (HE1 33419-33422). Des Gallars confirms (HE1 33452-33455). Beza's letter of 4 October "ne dit rien de la parole attribuée au cardinal" (HE1 33450-33451).
11. **When the prelates' assembly opened:** see section 6 (Languet, Brulart, De Thou, Chantonnay).
12. **"The hope of a common French church ended": whose judgment?** No held text says it.
    - For ending: Calvin expected nothing in advance (CLET4 10205); Coudret saw "poca speranza" (LAI6 3991); Pius IV "già ne disperava", in Pallavicino's report (PAL2 42311); Bonnet speaks of "absolute incompatibility" (CLET4 10692).
    - Against: the legate Ferrara refused to judge France "si ruinoso che se ne dovesse disperare" (PAL2 42337-42338). The crown in January 1562 still wanted them in "une mesme bergerie" (HE1 37072) and planned further talks (HE1 35416-35422). Catherine still looked to the general council (AYM 25600).
    - The scan row's "the hope ... ended" is the project's summary. It should be told as one view, or dropped.

## 13. Close candidates (later memory)

- **James Melville, Scotland, 1607.** In a letter to the brethren he warns against colloquies "leiveing thame in worse estait ... as witnesseth Colloquium Poissiaconum in Gallia, anno 1561, quhairin Beza and Martyr were colloquitoris for the one pairt" (melville 33964-33969). This is the strongest close: later Reformed memory of Poissy as a warning.
- **The Reformed synod of 1872.** "Le président ouvre la séance en récitant la belle confession des péchés qui avait été lue pour la première fois par Théodore de Bèze au Colloque de Poissy" (bersier 3640-3643). Beza's opening prayer was still in use three centuries on.

## 17. Length

**The held record can carry the full ceiling, about 400 words (2:30).**
- There is a named room, hour and seating (HE1 27647-27681).
- The king's and the chancellor's own speeches are held.
- The participants' own words are held from both sides: Beza in the Histoire; Lainez and Polanco in the Monumenta; Catherine in Aymon.
- There are a clear break-point (the "ciel" sentence and "Blasphemavit"), the prelates' own refusal and canons, and a documented sequel (the Edict of January).

Three limits must be said once:
1. The fullest account is a Reformed history whose authorship is contested.
2. There are no joint signed acts of the colloquy.
3. Counts differ by witness.

The closing judgment ("hope ended") should not be asserted. The crown's own edict still hoped for "une mesme bergerie" (HE1 37072).
