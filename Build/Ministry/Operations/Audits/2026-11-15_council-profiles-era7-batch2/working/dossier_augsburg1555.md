# Dossier: the Diet of Augsburg of 1555 and the Religious Peace it made

Research dossier, era 7 council profiles, batch 2. All line numbers are lines of the file in `cic/texts/`. Translations are mine unless marked otherwise. OCR spellings are kept in quotes exactly as in the file (long-s as "ſ" or "f", "u" for "v").

**Not the 1530 Diet.** Batch 1 profiled `diet-of-augsburg-1530` ("Diet of Augsburg and the Augsburg Confession"). This one is a different meeting, 25 years later, in the same city.

- Proposed id: `religious-peace-of-augsburg-1555`
- Proposed name: "The Religious Peace of Augsburg (Diet of 1555)"; shortName "Peace of Augsburg"; informalName "Religionsfriede" (Mirbt's heading, MIRBT 1135, 16238).
- Why: no reader can take "Peace" or "1555" for the 1530 confession diet. Sarpi's English calls it "a peace of Religion" (SARPI 81517, 83509, margin index); the 1648 treaty calls it "pax religionis" (MIRBT 21625-21626).

**How the Peace names the 1530 Confession.** The recess never gives a year or an edition. It protects "keinen stand des reichs von wegen der Augspurgischen confession und derselbigen ler, religion und glaubens halb" ("no estate of the empire on account of the Augsburg Confession and its teaching, religion and faith"), MIRBT 16272-16274. It calls that side "die stende, so der Augspurgischen confession verwant" ("the estates related to the Augsburg Confession"), MIRBT 16288. The other side is "der alten religion anhengig" ("adherents of the old religion"), MIRBT 16289. Which text of the confession counted was soon fought over (see Contested, item 6).

## 2. Candidate opening scenes

**A. The legate arrives, and the pope dies (March 1555).** This is the strongest scene. It is told by Polanco, and Nadal wrote from the city.
- POL5 15369-15376: "Pervenerunt deinde ipsa vigilia Annunciationis Augustam, ubi magnifice a Rege Romanorum, Cardinali augustano et aliis Episcopis et Principibus excepti fuerunt; qui fere leucam unam obviam Legato processerunt. Vigesima septima die ejusdem mensis perlatum est Augustam quod in extremis Julius II laboraret; 29.ª de morte ejusdem nuntius advenit. Ultima die mensis Martii Legatus ipse Moronus simul cum Cardinali Augustano Romam versus discessit." The file reads "Julius II" (sic). Polanco means Julius III, who died on 23 March (PALL2 26392-26395; WAT 6882-6884).
  - Translation: "They reached Augsburg on the very eve of the Annunciation. The King of the Romans, the Cardinal of Augsburg and other bishops and princes received them splendidly, coming out almost a league to meet the legate. On the 27th of that month word reached Augsburg that Julius was dying; on the 29th came news of his death. On the last day of March the legate Morone himself left for Rome with the Cardinal of Augsburg."
- POL5 15376-15381: the Jesuits had been preparing "ad colloquium aut disputationem cum primis praedicatoribus haereticorum" ("for a colloquy or disputation with the chief preachers of the heretics"), but "Omnia tamen interrupit Legati discessus" ("the legate's departure cut everything off").
- Sarpi's version: Julius had sent Morone "above all, to divert all Colloquies, and Treaties of Religion. The Cardinal was no sooner arrived in Awsburg, but Pope Julius died, whereof he received advice eight days after. He therefore departed the last of March" (SARPI 38144-38151).
- Why Morone went at all: PALL2 26376-26380. Julius sent him only "con ripugnanza estrema, per doversi trattar materie di Religione in vn Conuento misto d'Ecclesiastici, e di Secolari, di Cattolici, e d'Eretici: alla qual mostruosità non pareua conueneuole spettatore vn Legato." ("with extreme reluctance, since matters of religion were to be handled in an assembly mixed of churchmen and laymen, Catholics and heretics; at such a monstrosity a legate did not seem a fitting spectator"). The OCR spells it "mido", "Ecclefiadici", "moftruofità".

**B. Ferdinand opens the diet, 5 February.** Told only by Sarpi.
- SARPI 38065-38072: "Ferdinand began the Diet, in the Emperours name, the fifth of February; where he shewed at large, the lamentable spectacle of Germany, in which men of the same Baptism [OCR: 'Bap-'], Language, and Empire, were distracted by so various a profession of Faith, there arising new Sects every day".
- SARPI 38107-38109: the proposal was meant "to invite men to the Diet, to which very few were come."

**C. "The diet will not last fifteen or twenty days" (1 September).** This scene carries dramatic irony.
- SAL 8456-8461: Ferdinand told Salmerón he would sooner "perder antes sus stados y vida propria y hijos, que en ninguna cosa fauoreçer á los herejes; y que en esta dieta no permittirá cosa, que sea en detrimento y desfauor de la fe ó de los cathólicos". Translation: he would "lose his states, his own life and his children rather than favour the heretics in anything; and in this diet he will permit nothing to the detriment and disfavour of the faith or of the Catholics".
- The same sentence goes on: "aunque por ellos [the editors' note: "Scilicet ex haereticorum parte"] no a^ faltado de metter toda diligencia en traer al rey á su voluntad, y prometido, como suelen, y aun amenazado; pero en fin, no ay que temer en pensar que S. M. consienta en cosa que no deua" ("although on their side no effort has been spared to bring the king round to their will, with promises, as they do, and even threats; but in the end there is no fear that His Majesty will consent to anything he should not") (SAL 8460-8461, 8483-8485; the letter runs over a page break).
- On arriving on 20 August Salmerón had heard "las buenas nueuas de lo que passaua en la dieta en fauor de la religión" ("the good news of what was happening in the diet in favour of religion") (SAL 8450-8451).
- SAL 8485-8486: "es fama pública que la dieta no durará quinze ó veynte días, y que se dissoluerá presto" ("it is common talk that the diet will not last fifteen or twenty days, and will soon break up").
- The recess followed on 25 September (MIRBT 16248).

**D. Canisius: "no one to stand as a wall" (3 August).**
- Canisius to Ignatius, quoted by the Monumenta editors from Braunsberger (POL5 14943-14946): "Non habeo aliud de praesentibus comitijs scribendum quam quod plus inde damni quam vtilitatis rei Catholicae sit metuendum, et nonest qui se opponat murum pro domo. Israél." Translation: "I have nothing else to write about the present diet except that more harm than good is to be feared from it for the Catholic cause, and there is no one to set himself as a wall for the house of Israel."
- Polanco's own report of it: POL5 14914-14918.

## 3. Issue: what brought them together

- **The emperor's purpose, in Sarpi.** It was "a Diet in Ausburg, which the Emperour had intimated, principally to compose the Controversies of Religion, in regard this was the Fountain of all the troubles, and calamities of Germany" (SARPI 38057-38065).
- **Ferdinand's opening (Sarpi).** A general, free and pious council "was formerly thought the only remedy"; its failure was known to all. A national council was out of use; colloquies "did no good, because both parties aimed more at private gain". Even so, he urged them to try a colloquy once more "if the Diet did not propose a better way" (SARPI 38084-38104). The Turk was on the agenda too: the proposal also concerned "the Peace and War with the Turks" (SARPI 38106-38107).
- **The recess's own preamble.** The peace was made "der spaltigen religion halben" ("because of the divided religion"), as "die hohe notturft des heil. reichs teutscher nation erfordert" ("as the high need of the Holy Empire of the German nation requires") (MIRBT 16266-16268). §25 says it was granted "diese lobliche nation vor endlichem vorsteendem undergang zu verhütten" ("to save this praiseworthy nation from final ruin standing before it") (MIRBT 16323-16324).
- **The Catholic side (Rome).**
  - Julius III would send a legate only on two conditions. Great lords had to attend in person, not by proxies. And nothing was to be settled "contra l'autorità della Chiesa, de' Padri, e de' Concilij, e segnatamente contra le determinazioni del Tridentino" ("against the authority of the Church, the Fathers and the councils, above all against the decisions of Trent") (PALL2 26381-26388).
  - Sarpi says Julius cursed "the Colloquies, and Inventors of them", and complained he would always have "a Council, a Colloquy, or a Diet ... on his back" (SARPI 38114-38118).
- **The Catholic side (in Germany).** Canisius, by Polanco's report: "omnibus catholicis videbatur multo consultius fuisse eam nunquam inchoatam esse" ("all Catholics thought it would have been far wiser never to have begun it") (POL5 14914-14916).
- **The Lutheran side.** No Lutheran participant's own words from the diet are held. Their aims reach us through:
  - Sarpi: "those of the Augustan Confession did pretend, that it was lawful for all to accept their Doctrine, retaining the Honours, States, and [Offi]ces which they possessed" (SARPI 38426-38429).
  - Pallavicino: while Ferdinand was at Augsburg, the Protestant princes of Saxony, Brandenburg and Hesse met at Naumburg "quasi in una Contradieta" ("as if in a counter-diet"). They wrote to him that they meant to confirm their hereditary league (PALL2 27427-27433).
  - Nadal, hostile: "guadagnano ogni giorno d' grosso li lutherani" ("the Lutherans gain heavily every day") (NAD1 16972-16973).
- **The background in Passau (1552).** The recess refers to an earlier settlement: Pallavicino's article says church goods seized were left "alla Concordia di Passauia" (PALL2 27462-27464). The 1648 treaty links them: "Transactio a. 1552 Passavii inita et hanc a. 1555 secuta pax religionis" ("the agreement made at Passau in 1552 and the peace of religion that followed it in 1555") (MIRBT 21625-21626). Sarpi tells the Protestant war and Maurice's demand for "peace of Religion" (SARPI 37402-37405). WAT 6866-6869 dates the Convention of Passau to August 1552.

## 4. Called by

- **Charles V called it; Ferdinand opened and ran it.**
  - Sarpi: the diet "the Emperour had intimated"; "Ferdinand began the Diet, in the Emperours name" (SARPI 38057-38067).
  - Pallavicino: Ferdinand had asked Charles for specific orders. Charles answered "in secca forma" ("curtly") that sickness and war kept him from German affairs, "Onde à esso Ferdinando ne rimettea la dispositione" ("so he left the arrangement to Ferdinand") (PALL2 27415-27423).
  - The Monumenta editors say Ferdinand announced the next diet in the recess: "Ferdinandus I 25 Septembris 1555 in comitiis imperii augustanis nova comitia indixerat" (POL5 2509, 2541-2542, quoting Braunsberger).
- **Who speaks as "we" in the recess.** The recess speaks of "der Röm. Kai. Mt, uns" ("His Roman Imperial Majesty, us") (MIRBT 16270) and says "So haben wir ... disen fridstand ... bewilligt" (MIRBT 16323-16326). Mirbt's extract does not name the speaker. Sarpi and Pallavicino make Ferdinand the one who granted it (PALL2 27413-27415, 27441-27442).
- No held source names anyone else as convener. No held source disputes this.

## 5. Attended

- **No list of members is held, and no count.** I searched the files above for "Fürsten", "Stände", "electors" and "princes" lists near the diet; there are no subscription lists.
- **Few came at first.** Sarpi says the opening proposal was spread "to invite men to the Diet, to which very few were come" (SARPI 38107-38109).
- **The papal side.**
  - The legate Cardinal Giovanni Morone came with Lainez and Nadal. Morone is "Joanne Moron" in NAD1 296 and "il Cardinale Morone" in POL5 292. The three arrived on 24 March and Morone left on 31 March (POL5 15369-15376).
  - Morone had served long as nuncio to Ferdinand, and had been at the Regensburg diet under Contarini (PALL2 26303-26308).
  - The nuncio Zaccaria Delfino stayed on. When Paul IV was elected, he asked to be recalled and handed his business to Luigi Lippomano, bishop of Verona (PALL2 27379-27391). Lippomano is "Aloysius Lipomanus, Veronensis Episcopus" in POL5 2024-2025.
  - Lippomano gave Ferdinand "una ponderosa scrittura" ("a weighty paper") arguing that questions of faith have no court but the Apostolic See. He then went on to Poland rather than stay as "spettatore poco onorato" ("an ill-honoured spectator") (PALL2 27392-27405).
  - Salmerón came with Lippomano and arrived on 20 August (SAL 8448-8449). He left on 7 September (SAL 8528).
- **The Jesuits.**
  - Peter Canisius was at Augsburg "toto fere mense Junio (post diem 9)" ("almost all June, after the 9th") and from the last days of July "ad 5.am usque Augusti" ("to 5 August"). This is the editors' dating from Braunsberger (POL5 14815-14817).
  - He preached twice there and blocked a public disputation he thought dangerous (POL5 14856-14861).
- **Catholic princes and churchmen.**
  - Cardinal Otto Truchsess, the "Cardinalis Augustanus", is named in POL5 15370-15376. His name is given in an editors' note at POL5 1457.
  - Nadal met the bishop of Laibach ("labacense") and Pedro de Soto, "olim Imperatoris Caroli confessario" ("once confessor of the Emperor Charles"), at POL5 15385-15386. Polanco says Soto then left the diet, called away by Philip of England (POL5 15387-15388, 15423-15424).
  - The duke of Bavaria is named at POL5 15440-15441.
- **Ferdinand himself** was there; Polanco: "Romanorum Regi, qui tunc Augustae agebat" ("the King of the Romans, who was then at Augsburg") (POL5 13601-13602).
- **The Protestant princes.** Pallavicino says the great Protestant houses (Saxony, Brandenburg, Hesse) were at Naumburg, not Augsburg (PALL2 27427-27430). This is his account alone. No held line names a Protestant prince or envoy present at Augsburg.

## 7. Decided (from the recess extract in MIRBT first; then the histories for what Mirbt omits)

1. **No force against the Augsburg Confession estates (§15).** The emperor, "we", and the estates "keinen stand des reichs von wegen der Augspurgischen confession ... mit der tat gewaltiger weis überziehen, beschedigen, vergewaltigen oder in andere wege wider sein conscienz, gewissen und willen von dieser Augspurgischen confession ... tringen" (MIRBT 16272-16279). They are to leave such estates "bei solcher religion, glauben, kirchengebreuchen, ordnungen und ceremonien, auch iren hab und gütern ... ruiglich und friedlich bleiben lassen" (MIRBT 16280-16284).
   - Translation: they shall not "attack, harm or overpower any estate of the empire on account of the Augsburg Confession, nor in any other way force it against its conscience and will away from this Augsburg Confession", but let it remain "in peace and quiet with its religion, faith, church customs, ordinances and ceremonies, and its goods".
   - The scope is the estates' own lands: what "sie aufgericht oder nochmals aufrichten möchten in iren fürstentumben, landen und herschaften" ("they have set up or may yet set up in their principalities, lands and lordships") (MIRBT 16277-16279).
   - And: "sol die streitig religion nicht änderst, dan durch christliche, freuntliche, friedliche mittel und wege zu einhelligem, christlichem verstant und vergleichung gepracht werden" ("the disputed religion shall be brought to agreement only by Christian, friendly, peaceful means") (MIRBT 16284-16286).
2. **The same protection for the old religion (§16).** "Dargegen sollen die stende, so der Augspurgischen confession verwant ... des hl. reichs stende, der alten religion anhengig, geistlich oder weltlich ... gleichergestalt bei irer religion ... unbeschwert pleiben ... lassen" (MIRBT 16286-16290). Translation: "In turn, the estates related to the Augsburg Confession shall likewise leave the estates of the empire that hold to the old religion, spiritual or secular, unburdened in their religion."
3. **All others excluded (§17).** "Doch sollen alle andere, so obgemelten beden religionen nit anhengig, in diesem frieden nit gemeint sondern genzlich ausgeschlossen sein" (MIRBT 16290-16292; the OCR runs the words together: "So obgemelten bedenreligionennit anhengig", and spells "ssin"). Translation: "But all others who do not adhere to the two religions named above are not meant in this peace, but wholly excluded." The clause names no group.
4. **The ecclesiastical reservation (§18), headed by Mirbt "(Reservatum ecclesiasticum)".** "Wo ein erzbischof, bischof, prelat oder ein anderer geistlichs stants von unser alten religion abtretten wurd, das derselbig sein erzbistum, bistum, prelatur und andere beneficia, auch damit alle frucht und einkommen ... alsbalt on einiche verwiderung und verzug, jedoch seinen eren onnachteilig, verlassen" (MIRBT 16294-16298). The chapter may then elect "eine person der alten religion verwant" (MIRBT 16298-16301).
   - Translation: "Where an archbishop, bishop, prelate or other churchman leaves our old religion, he shall at once and without resistance or delay give up his archbishopric, bishopric, prelacy and other benefices, with all their fruits and income, though without harm to his honour". The chapter may then choose "a person of the old religion".
   - Note the recess's own voice: "unser alten religion" ("our old religion").
5. **Church lands already taken (§19).** Mirbt gives only a paraphrase: "Die von den evgl. Ständen eingezogenen reichsmittelbaren Kirchengüter verbleiben ihnen" ("the church goods held mediately of the empire that the Protestant estates have seized stay with them") (MIRBT 16301-16302). Pallavicino's version: such seizures were left to the Concord of Passau and suspended until a future diet (PALL2 27462-27464).
6. **Bishops' jurisdiction suspended over the Augsburg Confession (§20).** The "geistlich jurisdiction" shall not be exercised against their religion, faith, ministries, customs and ceremonies "bis zu entlicher vergleichung der religion" ("until the final settlement of religion"). It "ruhen, eingestelt und suspendirt sein und pleiben" ("shall rest, be stopped and suspended") (MIRBT 16302-16316).
7. **The right to leave (§24).** Subjects "der alten religion oder Augspurgischen confession anhengig" who wish to move away "von solcher irer religion wegen ... mit iren weib und kindern" may do so. They may also sell their goods, against a fair release from serfdom and departure tax, "unverhindert menniklichs" ("unhindered by anyone"), and "an iren eren und pflichten allerding unentgolten" ("without loss of honour") (MIRBT 16316-16323). The clause works both ways.
8. **Until agreement, and for ever (§25).** If agreement through "generalconciliums, nationalversamblung, colloquien oder reichshandlungen nit erfolgen wurde" ("general council, national assembly, colloquies or imperial negotiations does not follow"), the peace shall still stand "bis zu entlicher vergleichung" (MIRBT 16326-16330). It is to be "ein bestendiger beharrlicher und unbedingter, für und für, ewig werender friet" ("a lasting, steadfast and unconditional peace, for ever and ever") (MIRBT 16331-16332).
9. **The imperial court (§106).** The judge and assessors of the Kammergericht may be presented "von beyden der alten Religion und der Augspurgischen Confession" ("from both the old religion and the Augsburg Confession") (MIRBT 16332-16336).

**What Mirbt's extract leaves out, held only in the histories:**

10. **Free imperial cities.** This is Pallavicino's article: "Hauesse luogo la Concordia presente e fra la Nobiltà immediatamente soggetta à Cesare, e nelle Città imperiali doue fin' a quel tempo l'una, e l'altra Religione era stata in vigore: sicche i seguaci d'amendue vi douessero conuersar Pacificamente" (PALL2 27483-27486; the OCR spells "Hducffc", "figgetta", "dotte", "ò-uerftr").
    - Translation: "The present concord shall hold also among the nobility directly subject to the emperor, and in the imperial cities where until then both religions had been in force, so that the followers of both should live together there in peace."
    - Sarpi tells the bargain behind it. The Catholics would not let cities that had received the Interim "seven years since" go back to the Augustan Confession. At last "The Catholicks were content that the Cities should do as they pleased, and the Protestants gave over their pretence concerning the Ecclesiasticks" (SARPI 38429-38443).
11. **The Declaratio Ferdinandea.**
    - Pallavicino: "Fù anche dichiarato da Ferdinando, che i sudditi in temporale de' Principi ecclesiastici non soggiacessero come i sudditi de' secolari all'obligatione d'osseruar la Religione del Principe, ò di mutar paese" (PALL2 27505-27507; the OCR spells "[additi", "foggiacejfero", "obligajione"). Translation: "Ferdinand also declared that the temporal subjects of church princes should not be bound, as the subjects of lay princes were, to keep the prince's religion or to change country."
    - Sarpi, OCR damaged: "The Recess being made, another difficulty arose; for the removing of which, Ferdinand using the absolute Imperial Authority of his Brother, did declare with consent of the Ecclesiastical Order, that the Titulars, Cities, and Communities, subject to Ecclesiastical Princes, who have [...] many years to the Augustan Confession, and received since the Rites and Ceremonies thereof, and do observe them still, [...]" (SARPI 38467-38476). After that the page is lost.
    - **Flag (unsure):** "with consent of the Ecclesiastical Order" is how the damaged OCR reads. I would not quote it without checking a clean copy. Pallavicino does not mention consent.
12. **Oaths.** Pallavicino's article: oaths may be sworn "per Dio, e per l'Euangelio" ("by God and the Gospel"), so Protestants are not forced "à giurar pe' Santi" ("to swear by the saints") (PALL2 27491-27503).
13. **No poaching of subjects.** Pallavicino's article says no estate may draw another's lord or subject to its religion, nor protect vassals against their lord (PALL2 27452-27455). The 1648 treaty recalls the same rule "dudum in pace religionis ... cautum" (MIRBT 21652-21657).
14. **What was left undecided.**
    - Pallavicino: Ferdinand, seeing tempers not well disposed and lacking any apostolic minister, "giudicò buono di non deliberare l'articolo riserbato in quale de' quattro proposti modi si potesse ciò conseguire, mà di rimetterlo ad vn'altra futura Dieta" ("judged it best not to decide the reserved article, by which of the four proposed ways [concord] might be reached, but to put it off to another future diet") (PALL2 27409-27412).
    - Do not confuse this "articolo riserbato" with the ecclesiastical reservation. The new diet was called for Regensburg (POL5 2509, 2541-2544).

**Sarpi's summary of the whole,** a paraphrase and not the recess's words: "every one having Power to establish [OCR: 'liſh'] in his own State what Religion he will, and to forbid the other" (SARPI 38458-38459).

## 8. Outcome

- **Rome refused it.**
  - Paul IV wrote to Ferdinand on 18 Dec 1555 (MIRBT 16346-16349): "Vidit serenitas tua, quanta nos et nostri proximi praedecessores cura et sollicitudine quibusque apud omnes officiis procuravimus, ut conventus Augustanus potius religionis rebus intactis dissolveretur, quam ad recessum veniretur tam perniciosum sicuti et nobis et tuae serenitati et catholicis omnibus invitis tandem ventum est." The OCR spells "ciira", "solhcitudine", "perniciosura", "catholieis", "in\ätis tandeni".
    - Translation: "Your Serenity has seen with what care and anxiety we and our nearest predecessors worked, by every means with everyone, that the Augsburg assembly should rather break up with the matters of religion untouched than reach a recess as ruinous as the one it has at last reached, against our will, yours and that of all Catholics."
  - To Bishop Wolfgang of Passau, the same day (MIRBT 16355-16357): "Nam quid alienius ab ea potuit deliberari, quam quae in Augustae proximi conventus recessu decretum fuisse accepimus!" Translation: "For what could have been resolved more foreign to [the Catholic faith] than what we hear was decreed in the recess of the recent assembly at Augsburg!"
  - Pallavicino: on hearing of it the pope "fè perciò asprissime querele col Rè Ferdinando" ("made very bitter complaints to King Ferdinand"), conveyed by the nuncio Delfino early in 1556 (PALL2 27564-27568).
  - Ferdinand answered "con maniere più risentite di quel che portaua l'vso della sua temperata natura" ("more sharply than his mild nature usually allowed"). It seemed to him "che nelle concessioni dannose a' cattolici la manifesta necessità l'hauesse tenuto lungi da ogni nota, e che nelle cose arbitrarie à sé, si fosse per lui dimostrato ogni maggior zelo" ("that in the concessions harmful to Catholics plain necessity had kept him free of any blame, and that in matters left to his own choice he had shown the greatest zeal") (PALL2 27561, 27569-27576; the OCR prints "ogni nota" out of place at 27561 and spells "concezioni", "manifefia", "telo").
  - Sarpi: "The Fame was, that the Pope was moved to say so, not only by his own inclination, but by the perswasion of the Cardinal of Ausburg, who was displeased with the liberty granted to the Confessionists" (SARPI 38524-38526). What "so" refers to is on the lost lines. The surviving text has the pope telling ambassadors he "freed and absolved them" from their oaths, "yea commanded not to observe them" (SARPI 38513-38514).
- **Ferdinand's defence (in Pallavicino).** "ch'egli niente hauea fatto, saluo approuar ciò in che erano conuenuti vnitamente Cattolici ed Eretici, Ecclesiastici e Secolari" ("that he had done nothing but approve what Catholics and heretics, churchmen and laymen, had agreed together") (PALL2 27441-27442).
- **Pallavicino's verdict.** "L'effetto d'vn tal Recesso tanto riuscì più nociuo in perpetuo quanto sembrò più gioueuole allora" ("the effect of such a recess proved the more harmful in the long run the more helpful it seemed then"). The worn-out Germans fell asleep on this opium, but the "repose" cost Germany in another age "vn lunghissimo trauaglio, con tante stragi e desolazioni che ne prende orrore il pensiero" ("a very long agony, with so much slaughter and desolation that the mind shrinks from it") (PALL2 27509-27522).
- **Rulers' religion binds subjects.** Ferdinand's and Bavaria's subjects asked for the same freedom as their neighbours. Pallavicino gives the answer: "la comun condizione, secondo il tenore de' Recessi, non essere di libertà, mà d'obligazion a' vassalli di seguir la Religion de' Padroni, ò à partirsi" ("the common condition, by the tenor of the recesses, was not freedom but the vassals' duty to follow their lords' religion, or to leave") (PALL2 27523-27529).
- **Lutheran princes used the exclusion clause.** Duke Christoph of Württemberg's mandate (drafted 1556, still debated early 1557; Bossert's note BOSS 9320-9324) says Anabaptists, Schwenckfelders and other sects are to be punished in body, honour and goods, "dann sie auch in jung-stem zuo Augspurg ufgeriebtem religionsfridenstand und Freistellung mit nichten begriffen, sonder darvon usgesohlossen" (BOSS 9307-9310; exactly as the file reads, "ufgeriebtem" for "ufgerichtem").
  - Translation: "for they are in no way included in the recent religious peace and freedom set up at Augsburg, but excluded from it."
  - The mandate's list includes "Zwinglischer", added above the line in Duke Christoph's own hand (BOSS 9244, 9280 with note a).
  - Bossert's index: "Augsburger Religionsfriede schließt die Täufer aus 141, 11" ("the Augsburg Religious Peace excludes the Anabaptists") (BOSS 76706-76707).
- **The Lutherans quarrelled over who counted.** At Worms in 1557 "wollte jede Parthen die rechten Augsburgischen Confessions-Verwandten seyn". They wanted to condemn the others "und mithin vom Religions-Frieden ausschließen". The Catholics answered: "so müßt ihr zuerst ausmachen, wer dieselbe sind" ("then you must first settle who they are") (SCHULTZ 12315-12350, OCR-normalized spelling).
- **The Reformed.** Schaff says Frederick III "was threatened with exclusion from the benefits of the Augsburg Treaty of Peace (concluded in 1555), since Zwinglianism and Calvinism were not yet tolerated on German soil". At Augsburg in 1566 Frederick confessed his faith before the emperor (SCHAFF 1819-1823). Westphalia in 1648 extended the peace's rights to those "qui inter illos reformati vocantur" ("who among them are called Reformed") (MIRBT 21737-21742). It allowed no other religion: "Praeter religiones super nominatas nulla alia in sacro imperio Romano recipiatur vel toleretur" (MIRBT 21747-21748).
- **The Jesuits in 1566.** Nadal, Canisius and Ledesma, asked whether reconfirming the 1555 peace harmed Trent's dogmas, said no. The conditions held "nihil ... praeterquam permissionem, dissimulationem ecclesiasticae iurisdictionis, ac iuris etiam imperialis debiti suspensionem" ("nothing but a permission, a tacit setting-aside of church jurisdiction, and a suspension of imperial law that was owed"). They were accepted "ut durae et ineuitabili necessitati temporum ... ad tempus cedatur" ("to yield for a time to the hard and unavoidable necessity of the times") (NAD3 5668-5684). Rome "ferre quidem dolenter" ("bears it with grief"), "nec ... pacem hanc publice constitutam laudari" ("and does not praise this publicly made peace") (NAD3 5712-5722).
- **Bohemia.** Lützow says it "contained no reference to Bohemia, but it greatly encouraged the Lutheran party in that country" (LUTZ 11500-11502). In 1609 the Letter of Majesty, quoted by the estates in 1618, places them "při tom pokoji v svaté říši o náboženství, jenž Religionsfried slove, učiněném" ("in that peace made in the Holy Empire about religion, which is called the Religionsfried") (BOH 18497-18498, cf. 1102).
- **At Trent, 1563.** The emperor's envoy, the archbishop of Prague, warned that a draft decree seemed to overturn "tutti i recessi delle diete alemanne in materie ecclesiastiche, e perciò rimetter le spade in mano alle parti pacificate" ("all the recesses of the German diets in church matters, and so put the swords back into the hands of the pacified parties") (PALL3T 43390-43396; speaker at 43384-43385).
- **Westphalia, 1648.** It ordered that the 1555 peace "rata habeatur sancteque et inviolabiliter servetur" ("be held valid and kept holy and inviolable") (MIRBT 21625-21628). Innocent X protested that the "Haereticis Augustanae, ut vocant, confessionis liberum suae haereseos exercitium ... permittitur" ("the heretics of the so-called Augsburg Confession are allowed free exercise of their heresy") (MIRBT 21813-21815).

## 9. The refusing party's own voice

**The refusing party is the papacy.** Its own words are held. They are Paul IV's two sentences of 18 Dec 1555 (MIRBT 16346-16357, quoted above). Julius III's terms reach us through Pallavicino, who used the consistorial acts and letters (PALL2 26376-26388; margins "negli Atti Concistoriali", OCR damaged, at 26338-26341 and 26411-26413).

**The excluded parties** (§17: "alle andere"):
- **The Reformed (Zwinglians, Calvinists).** No 1555 word of theirs about the peace is held. Calvin's 1555 letters are not held: Bonnet vols 1, 2 and 4 cover 1541-1554 and 1559-1561. Their exclusion reaches us through a Lutheran prince's mandate (BOSS 9244, 9280, 9310-9312), through Schaff (SCHAFF 1819-1821), and through Westphalia (MIRBT 21737-21742).
- **Anabaptists and Hutterites.** No reaction to the peace in their own words. The Hutterite chronicle gives a count of Oettingen in 1557 telling arrested brethren: "Du mußt gefangen sein und mußt mit mir; denn die Konfession zu Augsburg hat es also beschlossen" ("You must be a prisoner and come with me; for the Confession at Augsburg has so decided") (HUT 21383-21385).
  - **Flag (unsure):** this is the persecutor's word as the victims remembered it. It is not clear he meant the 1555 peace and not the 1530 confession; it should not be told as the chronicle's comment on the peace.
- **Schwenckfelders.** Their own later voice: SCHULTZ 12315-12350 (written 1771). Modern descendants: SEIPT 2241-2251; KRIEB 1087-1090.

## 11. Contested points

1. **"Rulers choose."**
   - The recess (MIRBT) protects the estates' religion "in iren fürstentumben, landen und herschaften" and gives subjects of both religions a right to leave (§15, §24).
   - Sarpi, on the debate before the recess: it was resolved "to establish a Peace, that for cause of Religion there might be no more War, and that it might be lawful for all the Princes and States of the Empire to follow and cause to be observed in their Dominions what pleased them best" (SARPI 38408-38425, across a page break; OCR "eſta-|bliſh", "-o|the", "to-follow", "Domynions").
   - Sarpi paraphrases the recess as power "to establish in his own State what Religion he will, and to forbid the other" (SARPI 38458-38459).
   - Pallavicino reports the answer to Ferdinand's and Bavaria's subjects: they must "seguir la Religion de' Padroni, ò à partirsi" (PALL2 27527-27529).
   - The 1648 treaty calls it "ius reformandi" and says the "beneficium emigrandi" was granted "dudum in pace religionis" (MIRBT 21652-21657).
   - Seipt (1909) puts it more harshly: "granted to every ruling prince the right to enforce his religion upon his subjects, and to banish all who refused to conform" (SEIPT 2240-2243). Lützow gives a similar 1620s reading (LUTZ 15879-15884).
   - **The exceptions are held.** The free imperial cities with both religions (PALL2 27483-27486; SARPI 38441-38442). Church princes' subjects under Ferdinand's declaration (PALL2 27505-27507; SARPI 38467-38476). The ban on church princes changing religion and keeping their sees (MIRBT 16294-16298).
   - Tell it as "rulers chose for their lands, with stated exceptions". Pallavicino calls the rule a duty of vassals; Seipt speaks of banishment. The recess speaks of a right of departure.
2. **"First legal toleration."**
   - No held text says "first" or uses the word toleration for 1555. The recess says "frieden" and "fridstand".
   - Held texts put Passau (1552) before it as the agreement it built on (MIRBT 21625-21626; PALL2 27462-27464; LUTZ 15879-15880). Kriebel also names "the Truce of Nuremberg" (1532) before it (KRIEB 1088-1089).
   - **Permanent or temporary?** The recess says "bis zu entlicher vergleichung", and that it remains "ewig werender" even if agreement fails (MIRBT 16326-16332). Pallavicino says Ferdinand consented "non però à perpetuo, mà fin' alla concordia finale" ("not for ever, but until the final concord") (PALL2 27413-27414). Arndt's editor says "temporary external repose" (ARNDT 534-535). The 1566 Jesuits treat it as a mere "permissio" (NAD3 5676-5678).
   - Both readings are held; tell both.
3. **"Calvinists left out."**
   - The recess names no one; it excludes "alle andere" (MIRBT 16290-16292).
   - That the Reformed were outside is held only in later texts: Duke Christoph's mandate adding "Zwinglischer" (BOSS 9244, 9280, 9310-9312); Schaff on Frederick III (SCHAFF 1819-1821); Westphalia's admission of the "reformati" (MIRBT 21737-21742).
   - Calvinism as such is named only by Schaff. No held 1555 text names Calvinists.
4. **Whether Ferdinand gave way or held firm.**
   - Salmerón, 1 Sept, has him swear to permit nothing against the faith (SAL 8456-8461). Pallavicino says he "consentì à grauissimi detrimenti della Religion Cattolica" ("consented to very grave losses for the Catholic religion") and pleaded necessity (PALL2 27413, 27568-27574).
   - Pallavicino puts the cause on Charles's curt answer leaving it all to him (PALL2 27419-27423). Sarpi puts it on the debate, where "all inclined to the second proposition", to leave everyone to his liberty (SARPI 38401-38410).
5. **The Declaratio.** Pallavicino gives it without comment (PALL2 27505-27507). Sarpi's damaged line reads "with consent of the Ecclesiastical Order" (SARPI 38469-38470). Unsure; recheck. No held text gives the date or form of the declaration.
6. **Who counted as "Augsburg Confession".** The recess names no edition. At Worms in 1557 the Lutheran parties each claimed to be the true "Augsburgischen Confessions-Verwandten", and the Catholics asked them first to say who they were (SCHULTZ 12324-12350). Calvin in 1560 attacks those who shelter "under the shade of the Augsburg confession" (calvin_letters-vol4_bonnet1858.txt 5611-5612). That letter is about the Lord's Supper, not the peace; use it only if needed, with that caution.
7. **What moved Paul IV.**
   - Sarpi reports "The Fame" that Cardinal Truchsess of Augsburg pushed him (SARPI 38524-38526).
   - Pallavicino puts Paul's anger alongside his ill will toward the House of Austria (PALL2 27576-27582). He also says Sarpi tells the later wars "senza dirne i veri principij" ("without their true beginnings") (PALL2 27583-27590).

## 17. Length

The held record can carry a full profile of about 2:00, up to the 2:30 ceiling. It has a strong scene (Morone's arrival and the pope's death), the recess's own words on every main clause, three Jesuit participants writing from the city, the pope's own condemnation, and two rival histories that tell the bargain (cities against bishops).

The limits:
- the free-cities clause and Ferdinand's declaration are held only through Pallavicino and Sarpi;
- no Lutheran or Reformed participant's own words from 1555 are held (the Lutheran side speaks in the joint recess and a year later in Württemberg);
- no list of members or count is held.

The brief must say early that the account of the room comes from the Catholic side (the Jesuits and Pallavicino) and from Sarpi, with the recess's articles as the one joint record.
