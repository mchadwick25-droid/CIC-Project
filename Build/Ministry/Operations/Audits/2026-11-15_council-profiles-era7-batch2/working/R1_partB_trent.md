## Verified

- **Acts kept apart from Waterworth's essay.** The acclamations, the 255 and 168, "no hope remains", the Bologna decree, the confirmation and the four decrees are from the acts (W l.11650-24402). Waterworth's essay (before l.11650) is cited only for the Servanzio footnote and as "the Catholic editor". "Catholic" rests on his preface: the prelates "secured from error, in matters of faith" and "the chief points which still unfortunately separate so many from our communion" (W l.80, 95-96). "J. Waterworth" is on the title page ("REV. J. WATERWORTH", W l.46). His verdicts, Sarpi "skilful, but unfair" and Pallavicino "laborious and authentic", are at W l.342-347.
- **Opening scene.** "Begun on the third and terminated on the fourth day of December ; MDLXIII" (W l.21862-21863). "The Cardinal of Lorraine" leads (l.24032 onward). "Ever let us confess, ever keep" (l.24083). "Anathema to all heretics" / "Anathema, anathema" (l.24103-24104), with two acclamations between them, which "Then came" allows. 4 December 1563 (Julian) was a Saturday, and Sarpi has the subscriptions on "The next day, being Sunday" (S l.70212), so the dates fit.
- **Servanzio via Waterworth's footnote.** "molti molte lagrime gettando per gran letitia" ("many shedding many tears for great joy"), in "Servanzio, in his Diary" (W l.11161-11163). "Many wept for joy, wrote Servanzio, as Waterworth quotes his diary" is faithful. Pallavicino does not quote either diary on the close: his only Servanzio citation near it is for the ambassadors' instruments (P3 l.57747-57749). He has his own tears, "i volti bagnati di lagrime" (P3 l.57610-57612), so the entry's "words on the close reach us only in Waterworth's footnote" holds.
- **Sarpi and Pallavicino labels.** Sarpi: "Father Paul the Venetian, of the Order of the Servi" (S l.677-678), and Waterworth's "Fra Paolo" (W l.342). "Friar" and "Venetian" hold. Sarpi's own frame against Rome's "exorbitant power" (S l.8820-8836) supports "wrote against the Roman court". Pallavicino: "SCRITTA DAL PADRE SFORZA PALLAVICINO DELLA COMPAGNIA DI GESV" (P1 l.25-29), so "Jesuit" holds. His history refutes "un'istoria falsa ... sotto nome di Pietro Soave Polano" (P1 l.31-33). The held Sarpi is "Written in Italian by Pietro Soave Polano" (S l.153), and its contents name Father Paul as "Author of the Council of Trent" (S l.678). So "wrote to refute him" rests on held lines. The labels match batch 2's Augsburg 1530 brief ("The Venetian friar Paolo Sarpi", "The Jesuit Sforza Pallavicino").
- **The calling.** The bull was given at Rome "in the year MDXLII ... on the eleventh of the calends of June" (W l.12139-12141). The Germans "wished for the city of Trent" (l.11982-11983, verbatim). Opening 13 December 1545 (l.12158-12160). Sarpi: "the Legates in Trent, with all the Prelates, in number 25" (S l.18406), on "The thirteenth of December" (l.18386).
- **Calvin's letter and its dating.** Headed "January 1545" by Bonnet, with his note "without address and without date" (C1 l.20043, 20050). "excluding Antichrist" with "their Romish idol" and "authority of the Pope" (l.20079-20090). Then "our side will begin to remonstrate ... They will cite Antichrist as a criminal and defendant : certainly, they will never permit him to be the judge" (l.20090-20096; OCR "hira"). "His side, he wrote" keeps Calvin's "our side". The future tense is kept, and the quote is verbatim.
- **The Saxon envoys' demand.** Sarpi: "Therefore prayeth the Assembly, That they would first repeat, approve, and ratifie the Article of the Superiority of the Council above the Pope" (S l.36639-36641). Pallavicino agrees independently: the envoys urged "che innanzi à tutto si dichiarasse questo punto da' Padri Tridentini", that before all else the pope's subjection to the council be declared (P2 l.22532-22538). So "Before anything else, Sarpi says" is faithful, though the demand comes fourth in the list (Waterworth l.6697-6723; P2 l.22424-22536). The Saxons arrived "the 7th of January" 1552 (S l.36136), so "In 1552" holds.
- **Jewel.** "As though all the world may not now see that this is a conspiracy and not a council" (J l.2919-2920, Lady Bacon's English of 1564). The Latin of 1562 agrees: "conspirationem istam esse, non Concilium" (JL l.4426). "First published in 1562" (J l.82). "Bishop of Salisbury" (J l.2).
- **The decrees' wording.** Scripture and tradition: "the written books, and the unwritten traditions ... transmitted as it were from hand to hand ... receives and venerates with an equal affection of piety, and reverence" (W l.12409-12419). Justification canon IX: "If anyone saith that by faith alone the impious is justified ; in such wise as to mean that nothing else is required to co-operate ..." (l.13681-13686). "with nothing else required" keeps the canon's limiting clause. Residence: "by divine precept, enjoined" (l.19390), "obliged to personal residence in their own church or diocese" (l.19406-19418). Seminaries: "a perpetual seminary of ministers of God" (l.19931-19932). The decree of 1563 does not call residence divine right. No "divine right" stands in session XXIII's reform chapters (search below), and Waterworth notes "Assistunt" was opposed "by those who denied the residence of bishops to be of divine right" (l.19413-19414).
- **Bologna, each historian as his own.** Acts: "so plainly and notoriously certain" and "transferred for a time to the city of Bologna" (W l.14686-14706). Sarpi: "Monte suborned some to ask the Physicians, whether those Infirmities were not Contagious" (S l.28736-28738). Pallavicino: Fracastoro, "medico deputato del Concilio" (P2 l.4992-4993), swore before the notary "Io giuro ... non istarei più un mese in Trento" (P2 l.5371-5372). "Del Monte" is Waterworth's form (W l.3296, 3501).
- **Residence, 1562, each historian as his own.** Sarpi: 68 placet, 33 non placet, 13 and 17 deferring to the pope (S l.45897-45900). Pallavicino: "presso à settanta" for; 38 or 37 against; 34 between; so "la maggior parte volesse puramente la diffinizione: e di vero fù la minore" (P2 l.47484-47510). The secretary's tally is 66, then 67 (l.47547-47555).
- **The counts.** Opening 25: Sarpi, as above. Close: "they were in number CCLV.; to wit, four legates, two cardinals, three patriarchs, twenty-five archbishops, one hundred and sixty-eight bishops, seven abbots, thirty-nine proctors ..., seven generals" (W l.24111-24116). The parts sum to 255, certified by Massarelli and two notaries (l.24119-24131). Pallavicino gives the same (P3 l.57680-57688), and so does Waterworth's essay (W l.11172-11175).
