## Substantial

**A-S1. Substantial (pattern rule: partisan main witness unmarked; the Catholic side's own eyewitness unused).** Brief, paragraphs 2, 4 and 6.
- The brief marks its two historians, Sarpi "hostile to the papal court" and "the Jesuit Sforza Pallavicino". It does not mark Calvin, who carries six of its quotations and stands in for the lost papers ("Calvin's letters stand in for them"). Calvin counts himself with the Protestants: "on our side, Philip Melanchthon, Martin Bucer, and John Pistorius" (CLET1 l.11621-11622). The pattern: "A participant ... is a partisan witness ... placed once and said to be partisan" (Council pattern, "How a council differs", item 3; Who checklist item 6).
- The legate is drawn only through Calvin: the mocking crosses (CLET1 l.10975-10977) and "the yoke of subjection" (l.10979-10981). The Catholic side of the talks is then told only by a history of 1656-1664 and by the bull. Yet the Library holds a Catholic eyewitness at the Diet. Peter Faber, a Jesuit, went to Ratisbon "ubi facta diæta imperialis" (FAB l.1055-1056). He wrote from the city on 12 May ("Ratisbonæ, 12 Maii t541", FAB l.13514): "Negotium fidei valde ambigue procedit" (FAB l.13476; my tr.: "The business of the faith goes forward very doubtfully"). The dossier marks him as "a second participant from the other side" (dossier, "Brief length" section) and the entry lists him in `record` as a participant, but the brief never uses him. Faber does not name the colloquy; the fix keeps his own general words.
- A second Catholic counter to Calvin's "yoke" is also held: "Certo è, che'l Contarino si diede con tanta voglia all'impresa della concordia" (PALL l.27398-27399; my tr.: "It is certain that Contarini gave himself so eagerly to the work of concord"). I tested it in place of the hand sentence. It costs the brief its readability floor (Flesch 57.9), so I recommend Faber, who carries the Catholic side from inside the city at the moment of the agreement.
- Fix, brief (all in the scratch copy; net 0 words):
  - Paragraph 2: `Calvin, then at Strasbourg, had come as a deputy.` → `Calvin, a Protestant at Strasbourg, had come as a deputy.`
  - Paragraph 4: delete `He gave them his hand, Calvin says, and told them to aim at "the glory of God, the public peace."` (see "Length test": it repeats paragraph 2's peace and cuts Calvin's list mid-way, CLET1 l.11634-11635 "the glory of God, the public peace, the salvation of all nations as their aim").
  - Paragraph 6, after the Farel quotation: insert `The Jesuit Peter Faber was at the Diet. On 12 May he wrote that the faith's business went on in great doubt.` (8 and 13 words).
  - Paragraph 3: `None of the colloquy's own papers` → `None of the colloquy's papers` ("own" adds nothing; this pays for the inserted words).
- Fix, records: add claim `{"claim": "Faber, writing from Ratisbon on 12 May 1541: 'Negotium fidei valde ambigue procedit' (the business of the faith goes forward very doubtfully)", "witness": "faber_memoriale-lat_1873.txt l.13476; faber_memoriale-lat_1873.txt l.13514-13516", "witness_kind": "participant", "formation_confidence": "Documented"}`.
- Fix, entry `uncertain[7]` (with A-M8): append `Faber, a Catholic writing from the city on 12 May, says the business of the faith went forward 'valde ambigue', very doubtfully.`
- Fix, README line: `... so its story rests on Calvin's dated letters from the city, with Faber's from the Catholic side, set against two hostile histories.`

**A-S2. Substantial (a stale line left by the brief's revision, and a wrong claim about the witness's word).** Records `contested_claims[7]` (X7) ends: "The brief's 'blessings' renders Calvin's 'crosses'." Entry `uncertain[3]` ends: "Calvin's own word is 'crosses'."

## Length test

The brief sits exactly at 400 words. Its stages each need their own witness: the legate's arrival, the record caveat, the six named, the Book, justification and Rome's doubt, the Supper, the recess, and the bull. The README's reason ("Each stage ... needs its own witness, and Rome's doubt on the agreed words needs a sentence") holds. Read sentence by sentence:
- One sentence does not earn its place. "He gave them his hand, Calvin says, and told them to aim at 'the glory of God, the public peace.'" (20 words). It repeats paragraph 2's point that the emperor wanted peace and cuts Calvin's list mid-way. It is deleted in A-S1.
- Cutting it alone gives 380 words, but Flesch falls to 58.53, below the floor (`grade_text` on the cut copy). The brief's plain short sentences are carrying its readability, which sits only 0.18 above 60. So the honest outcome is a swap, not a shorter brief: the 20 words go to the Catholic eyewitness the brief lacks (A-S1). The fixed copy is 400 words at Flesch 60.56.
- No other sentence repeats or idles. "Which English writers called Ratisbon" is needed for the bull's "diet of Ratisbon". "The Protestants rejected transubstantiation" and Pallavicino's sentence after it tell the same break from the two sides, which the pattern asks for.
- So this is not a Substantial length finding: no cut leaves a shorter brief that both keeps every sourced point and passes the readability floor.
