## Blocking

**D-B1. Blocking (a source sentence's concession dropped, so the claim misleads).** Brief, paragraph 4: `Beza said that his side did not make Christ absent from the Supper. But in distance of place, Christ's body was as far from the bread and wine as the highest heaven from the earth.`
- The History's narrative tells the sentence that caused the uproar with its concessive clause: "le corps de Jefus Ghrift, combien qu'il nous fuft véritablement offert & communiqué en icelle, eftoit toutesfois aulîi loin du pain que le haut des cieux eft elloigné de la terre" (HE1 l.28709-28713: "Christ's body, though it was truly offered and communicated to us in the Supper, was yet as far from the bread ...").
- The printed speech frames the same sentence the same way. It limits the distance to "fa prefence corporelle" (HE1 l.28513-28516). It then says "nous fommes faits participans de fon corps & de fon fang par une maniere fpirituelle, & moyennant la foy, auffi véritablement que nous voyons les Sacremens à l'œil" (HE1 l.28526-28529: "we are made partakers of his body and blood in a spiritual way and through faith, as truly as we see the sacraments with the eye").
- The brief keeps only the negative ("did not make Christ absent") and the distance. What Beza affirmed, and what both the speech and the narrative put around the sentence, is gone. A listener hears a Reformed denial of the Supper with a formal disclaimer. This is batch 1's lesson: read the whole source sentence, including its concessive clause, before rewording.
- Fix, brief (replace the first sentence; 15 words): `Beza said believers truly shared in Christ's body and blood in the Supper, by faith.` Then, in the next sentence, `Christ's body` becomes `his body`: `But in distance of place, his body was as far from the bread and wine as the highest heaven from the earth.` (Both sentences rest on the printed speech in the History, as before; the narrative's concessive agrees.)

## Length test

The README's reason ("its standing and the Catholic witnesses must be placed. The scene, Beza's sentence and the uproar, the Catholic replies in their own words, and the prelates' refusal each need a sentence") holds. I read each sentence for whether it carries a sourced point the listener needs:
- Paragraph 1 (scene and count): needed, except the clause "and the King and Queen of Navarre" (7 words). The brief never uses Navarre again; Catherine is the one figure the later paragraphs need.
- Paragraph 2 (whose account): needed; the authorship dispute and the side of each witness are the pattern's first duty for this council. After D-S1 it is slightly longer.
- Paragraph 3 (the July aim): needed; it is the stake, and the edict at the close answers it.
- Paragraphs 4-6 (sentence, uproar, replies, formula, refusal): each sentence carries a dated, sourced event. Nothing repeats.
- Paragraphs 7-8 (outcome): the crown's own voice, the Catholic historian's verdict, and the edict's terms. No sentence repeats another; "It still hoped to bring them back into one fold" is the edict's own words and keeps the close from asserting that hope ended (dossier, section 11, item 12).

So the brief could lose 7 words without loss, and no more without dropping a sourced point. That is not the drift Mark warned of, and I do not raise it as a Substantial finding. The 7 words are better spent on D-B1 and D-S1. With all brief fixes applied, these cuts keep it at the ceiling:
- `The king sat with his mother, Queen Catherine, and the King and Queen of Navarre.` → `The king sat with his mother, Queen Catherine.` (−7)
- `The aim, the History says, was to see whether there was any way to reunite them with the Roman Church.` → `The aim, the History says, was to see if they could be reunited with the Roman Church.` (−3; "afin d'advifer fil y auroit moyen de les reunir", HE1 l.25998-25999)
- `At the queen's order, five from each side then reached a formula on the Supper.` → drop `then` (−1)
