# Source sheet: Justin Martyr (writer's copy)

## 1. Setting

S1. **His name and family.** "I, Justin, the son of Priscus and grandson of Bacchius, natives of Flavia Neapolis in Palestine" (First Apology 1, ANF 13965–13967). Greek: Ἰουστῖνος Πρίσκου τοῦ Βακχείου, τῶν ἀπὸ Φλαουΐας Νέας πόλεως τῆς Συρίας Παλαιστίνης (AP1 73–74), "Justin, son of Priscus, son of Bacchius, of those from Flavia Neapolis in Syria Palestine". Eusebius quotes the same address (IV.12, EUS 22645–22649). OWN. Documented. Jerome gives "the son of Priscus Bacchius" (NP3 39973–39974; see D6).

S2. **His city and people.** (a) The city: Flavia Neapolis, "in Palestine" (ANF 13966–13967) or "in Syria Palestine" (AP1 74). Jerome: "a citizen of Neapolis, a city of Palestine" (NP3 39973). (b) His people: in the *Dialogue* he speaks of "my people, that is, the Samaritans" (Dial 120, ANF 24249; Greek τοῦ γένους τοῦ ἐμοῦ, λέγω δὲ τῶν Σαμαρέων, DIA 5691). He calls Simon the magician one "of my own nation" (Second Apology 15, ANF 17271–17275; AP2 431, where the editor puts the sentence in brackets, see D17). (c) He calls himself uncircumcised: "do not delay or hesitate to put faith in me, although I am an uncircumcised man" (Dial 28, ANF 18870–18871; τῷ ἀπεριτμήτῳ ἐμοί, DIA 1289). OWN. Documented as his words. What "my people" means for his own descent is not stated (D5). Epiphanius, much later: "this Justin was a Samaritan by race" (HOL 15927; LATER).

S3. **The century, by his own count.** He dates his own writing: some might say "that Christ was born one hundred and fifty years ago under Cyrenius" (First Apology 46, ANF 15680–15681; πρὸ ἐτῶν ἑκατὸν πε[ν]τήκοντα, AP1 1227). He writes to the emperor Antoninus Pius and his sons (S5). He calls the revolt of Barchochebas (Bar Kokhba) "the Jewish war which lately raged" (First Apology 31, ANF 15107; ἐν τῷ νῦν γεγενημένῳ ἰουδαϊκῷ πολέμῳ, AP1 843). He says Antinous "lived in our day" (First Apology 29, as Eusebius quotes it, EUS 21875–21878). He says Marcion "is even at this day alive" (First Apology 26, ANF 14943–14944). OWN. Documented. Plain spoken form the record supports: "in the second century, about a hundred and fifty years after Jesus was born, by his own count." The label "second century" is modern reckoning (C10); the "hundred and fifty years" is his.

S4. **Where he taught.** Rome. Before the prefect he says: "I live above one Martinus, at the Timiotinian Bath; and during the whole time (and I am now living in Rome for the second time) I am unaware of any other meeting than his. And if any one wished to come to me, I communicated to him the doctrines of truth" (Martyrdom 2, ANF 29309–29313; GEB 1347–1353; see Q3). His *Second Apology* opens on "the things which have recently happened in your city under Urbicus", addressed to "Romans" (ANF 16736–16739; ὦ Ῥωμαῖοι, AP2 97). Eusebius: "For he lived at Rome" (IV.11.11, EUS 22601). Jerome: "when he had held διατριβάς [schools, discussions] in the city of Rome" (NP3 39991–39993). OWN; ACTS; LATER. Documented.

S5. **Whom he addressed.** The First Apology is addressed "To the Emperor Titus Ælius Adrianus Antoninus Pius Augustus Cæsar, and to his son Verissimus the Philosopher, and to Lucius the Philosopher, the natural son of Cæsar, and the adopted son of Pius, a lover of learning, and to the sacred Senate, with the whole People of the Romans" (ANF 13961–13965; AP1 68–71). He writes "in behalf of those of all nations who are unjustly hated and wantonly abused, myself being one of them" (ANF 13967–13969). He says that he once wrote to "Cæsar" about his own Samaritans (Dial 120, ANF 24249–24252). His conversation partner in the *Dialogue* is Trypho, "a Hebrew of the circumcision", who fled "the war lately carried on there" and lives "in Greece, and chiefly at Corinth" (Dial 1, ANF 17346–17351; DIA 87–89). OWN. Documented.

S6. **His church as he describes it.** Converts pray and fast, and are washed "in the name of God, the Father and Lord of the universe, and of our Saviour Jesus Christ, and of the Holy Spirit" (First Apology 61, ANF 16233–16240). "This washing is called illumination" (ANF 16265). They are brought "to the place where those who are called brethren are assembled" (65, ANF 16408–16409). There are a "president of the brethren", "those who are called by us deacons", bread and a cup of wine mixed with water, and the people's "Amen" (65, ANF 16421–16436). On "the day called Sunday" all gather; the "memoirs of the apostles or the writings of the prophets are read"; the president teaches; gifts go to "the orphans and widows ... those who are in bonds and the strangers sojourning among us" (67, ANF 16502–16526; Q19). He names no bishop and no city for these customs; he speaks of what "we" do. OWN. Documented as his account.

S7. **The danger Christians faced, in his words.** (a) Punished for the name alone: "in our case you receive the name as proof against us" (First Apology 4, ANF 14061–14062; Q11). (b) Called atheists: Socrates was killed "as an atheist and a profane person ... and in our case they display a similar activity" (First Apology 5, ANF 14104–14109). Crescens says in public "that the Christians are atheists and impious" (Second Apology 3, ANF 16857–16858). (c) Put to death on confession: the case of Ptolemæus and Lucius under Urbicus (Second Apology 2, ANF 16809–16840; Q22). (d) In the Jewish revolt, Christians alone were punished unless they would deny Jesus (Q38). (e) He appended Hadrian's letter forbidding condemnation by mere outcry (First Apology 68, ANF 16547–16553; the letter at 16557–16580; Eusebius IV.8.6–IV.9, EUS 21915–22112). (f) The *Martyrdom* opens: "wicked decrees were passed against the godly Christians in town and country, to force them to offer libations to vain idols" (ANF 29266–29269; Q2). OWN; ACTS. Documented.

S8. **The philosopher's cloak.** Trypho greets him in the street, "Hail, O philosopher!", because his teacher had told him not to "despise or treat with indifference those who array themselves in this dress" (Dial 1, ANF 17321–17331; τῶν περικειμένων τόδε τὸ σχῆμα, DIA 78; Q12). Eusebius: "In the guise of a philosopher he preached the divine word" (IV.11.8, EUS 22442–22456; Q30). Jerome: "a philosopher, and wearing the garb of philosopher" (NP3 39972–39973). OWN; LATER. Documented.

S9. **Plain spoken forms for his place and church**, from his own record, not from a description of the age:
- "a teacher among the Christians of Rome" — support: Martyrdom 2 (S4); "If any one wished to come to me" (ANF 29311–29313); Euelpistus "willingly heard the words of Justin" (ANF 29343–29344).
- "born in Flavia Neapolis, in Palestine" — support: First Apology 1 (S1); Jerome (NP3 39973).
- "a man of Samaria by birth, who called the Samaritans his people" — support: Dial 120 (S2). Say only what he says; do not say "a Samaritan believer" or "a Gentile" (D5).
- "who wrote to the emperor Antoninus Pius" — support: First Apology 1 (S5).
- "the church as he knew it, meeting on Sunday" — support: First Apology 67 (S6).


## 2. Recorded moments

M1. **"Hail, O philosopher!"** One morning, walking "in the walks of the Xystus" (a covered walk), Justin was greeted by Trypho and his friends because of his philosopher's dress, and they began to talk (Dial 1, ANF 17317–17337; DIA 72–82). OWN. Documented as his own account. The city is not named in the *Dialogue*; Eusebius says Ephesus (D4).

M2. **"I surrendered myself to a certain Stoic."** He tells Trypho how he went from teacher to teacher. The Stoic taught nothing about God and "said such instruction was unnecessary"; a Peripatetic asked "to settle the fee"; a Pythagorean sent him away because he knew no "music, astronomy, and geometry". Then a Platonist "who had lately settled in our city" gave his mind "wings", and "I expected forthwith to look upon God" (Dial 2, ANF 17421–17458; DIA 139–169). OWN. Documented as his account. Which city "our city" is, is not said (N3).

M3. **"A certain old man ... followed me at a little distance."** Seeking quiet, he went to "a certain field not far from the sea". An old man met him and questioned him about philosophy and God, and pointed him to "certain men more ancient than all those who are esteemed philosophers ... They are called prophets" (Dial 3–7, ANF 17467–17842; DIA 175–420). OWN. Documented as his account. The old man is not named; he says he had come to look for "some of my household" (ANF 17487–17489).

M4. **"Straightway a flame was kindled in my soul."** The old man left, and Justin did not see him again. "Thus, and for this reason, I am a philosopher" (Dial 8, ANF 17851–17857; DIA 428–435; Q15). OWN. Documented as his account. Eusebius summarises this conversion story (IV.18.6, EUS 24310–24315).

M5. **"I ... saw them fearless of death."** His second account of what turned him: while he was "delighting in the doctrines of Plato", he heard Christians slandered but saw them unafraid of death, and judged that they could not be living in wickedness (Second Apology 12, ANF 17172–17181; AP2 368–374; Q16). OWN. Documented. Eusebius quotes it (IV.8.5, EUS 21895–21913). See D7.

M6. **"Then I rose up and was about to leave."** Trypho's friends laughed at him; Justin stood to go; Trypho "taking hold of my garment" held him to his promise, and they moved to stone seats in the middle of the Xystus (Dial 9, ANF 17918–17932; DIA 473–485; Q37). OWN. Documented as his account.

M7. **"You are on the eve of departure."** At the end of the *Dialogue* Justin is about to sail. Trypho says, "we have found more than we expected"; Justin urges them to set "a higher value on the Christ of the Almighty God than on your own teachers"; they part wishing each other well, and Justin prays for them (Dial 142, ANF 25309–25332; DIA 6589–6607; Q23). OWN. Documented as his account. Where he sailed to is not said.

M8. **"I, Justin ... present this address and petition."** He wrote to Antoninus Pius, his sons and the Senate, asking that Christians be judged by their deeds, not their name (First Apology 1–4, 68; ANF 13961–14082, 16541–16553; Q9–Q11, Q24). OWN. Documented. Jerome's *Chronicle* places the handing-over of the book in the fourth year of Antoninus (FOT 42754–42757; Q34). Whether the emperor read or answered it is not recorded (N13).

M9. **"On the day called Sunday."** He describes for the emperor how Christians baptise, give thanks over bread and wine, and meet each Sunday (First Apology 61, 65–67; ANF 16227–16533; Q19, Q20). OWN. Documented as his account.

M10. **"The things which have recently happened in your city under Urbicus."** A woman who became a Christian divorced her dissolute husband; he accused her; then he had her teacher Ptolemæus arrested. Urbicus condemned Ptolemæus for confessing the name. Lucius protested and was condemned; "a third" also (Second Apology 1–2, ANF 16736–16840; AP2 97–158; Q22). OWN. Documented as his account. Eusebius quotes the whole story (IV.17, EUS 24087–24198).

M11. **"I too, therefore, expect to be plotted against."** Justin had questioned the Cynic Crescens in public and "found most convincingly that he, in truth, knows nothing". He offers to repeat the questioning before the emperors, and expects to be plotted against by one of those he has named, "or perhaps by Crescens" (Second Apology 3 [AP2 8], ANF 16849–16881; AP2 273–293; Q21). OWN. Documented as his account.

M12. **"Obey the gods at once, and submit to the kings."** Justin and others were arrested and brought before Rusticus, prefect of Rome. Asked what teachings he held, Justin said he had tried to learn all and had settled on those of the Christians (Martyrdom 1, ANF 29266–29293; GEB 1300–1338; Q2). ACTS. Widely Accepted for the hearing before Rusticus (D1). The "kings" are the emperors (ANF note 2643, at 29272); the Greek plural is τοῖς βασιλεῦσιν (GEB 1310).

M13. **"Where do you assemble?"** Rusticus asks where Christians meet. Justin answers that God "is not circumscribed by place", then gives his own lodging, "above one Martinus", where he taught whoever came. "Yes, I am a Christian" (Martyrdom 2, ANF 29302–29314; GEB 1339–1354; Q1, Q3). ACTS.

M14. **"Did Justin make you Christians?"** The others are questioned: Chariton, the woman Charito, Euelpistus ("a servant of Cæsar"), Hierax, Pæon and Liberianus. Each says he or she is a Christian. Euelpistus says he heard Justin gladly but learned the faith from his parents; Pæon says the same; Hierax says his parents are dead and he was "driven from Iconium in Phrygia" (Martyrdom 3, ANF 29323–29353; GEB 1355–1400; Q4). ACTS.

M15. **"If you are scourged and beheaded, do you believe you will ascend into heaven?"** Rusticus mocks Justin's learning and threatens him; Justin answers, "I do not suppose it, but I know"; ordered to sacrifice, he says, "No right-thinking person falls away from piety to impiety"; the others say, "Do what you will" (Martyrdom 4, ANF 29362–29387; GEB 1402–1432; Q5–Q7). ACTS.

M16. **"Were beheaded, and perfected their testimony."** Rusticus sentenced those who refused to sacrifice to be scourged and beheaded "according to the laws". They went "to the accustomed place" and were beheaded. "Some of the faithful having secretly removed their bodies, laid them in a suitable place" (Martyrdom 5, ANF 29396–29408; GEB 1433–1444; Q8). ACTS. Widely Accepted for death by beheading at Rome (D1, D2). The place of burial is not named (N9).

M17. **"After his martyrdom."** Irenaeus, writing of Tatian: "He was a hearer of Justin's, and as long as he continued with him he expressed no such views; but after his martyrdom he separated from the Church" (Against Heresies I.28.1, ANF 33663–33667; Q26). LATER. Documented as Irenaeus's report. It is the earliest held statement that Justin died a martyr, given in passing.

M18. **"Endeavoured to inflict on Justin, and indeed on me, the punishment of death."** Tatian says Crescens tried to bring death on Justin, and on Tatian too, because Justin "by proclaiming the truth ... convicted the philosophers of being gluttons and cheats" (Address 19, ANF2 6550–6556; OTT 8105–8111; Q28). HEARER. Documented as Tatian's report. Tatian says Crescens tried; he does not say Crescens succeeded (D2).

M19. **"Crowned with divine martyrdom, in consequence of a plot laid against him by Crescens."** Eusebius sets his death after a second address to the emperors and blames Crescens, citing Justin's own expectation and Tatian (IV.16, EUS 23749–24078; Q31). LATER. Documented as Eusebius's account; the cause is Contested (D2).

M20. **"Accused of being a Christian, through the efforts and wiles of Crescens."** Jerome: after his schools at Rome and his public refutation of Crescens, Justin "shed his blood for Christ" (Lives 23, NP3 39991–39997; Q33). His *Chronicle* tells the same in one line (FOT 42856–42861; Q34). LATER.

M21. **"Under Rusticus the governor and Hadrian the emperor."** Epiphanius says Justin, "a Samaritan by race", was martyred at Rome under Rusticus and the emperor Hadrian, "being thirty years old" (Panarion 46.1, HOL 15923–15935; Q35). LATER. Documented as Epiphanius's report; the reign and the age are Inferential-Thin (D1, D15).


## 3. Quotes (final readings)

Each quote is in our modern English, settled from two independent renderings. Use the final reading exactly. Where a quote carries a warning, the warning says how it may be used.

**Q1. Martyrdom 2: "Yes, I am a Christian"**
- Source: ANF 29312–29314; GEB 1353–1354 (Οὐκοῦν λοιπὸν Χριστιανὸς εἶ; … Ναί, Χριστιανός εἰμι).
- Final reading: Rusticus said, "So then, you are a Christian?" Justin said, "Yes, I am a Christian."
- Use: USABLE.

**Q2. Martyrdom 1: the decrees, the order, and his answers**
- Source: ANF 29266–29278; GEB 1298–1317.
- Final reading: In the time of the lawless champions of idol worship, ungodly decrees were posted against the godly Christians, in town and country. They were meant to force them to pour offerings to worthless idols. So the holy men were arrested and brought before the prefect of Rome, a man named Rusticus. When they had been brought before his judgment seat, Rusticus the prefect said to Justin, "First, obey the gods, and submit to the emperors." Justin said, "To obey what our Saviour Jesus Christ commanded deserves no blame and no condemnation." Rusticus the prefect said, "What teachings do you follow?" Justin said, "I tried to learn every teaching. But I gave my assent to the true teachings, those of the Christians, even if they do not please people who hold false beliefs."
- Use: USABLE.

**Q3. Martyrdom 2: where they meet**
- Source: ANF 29302–29313; GEB 1339–1353 (apparatus 1363: τιμιοτίνου codd.).
- Final reading: Rusticus the prefect said, "Where do you meet?" Justin said, "Wherever each one chooses and is able. Do you really think we all meet in the same place? Not so. The God of the Christians is not bounded by place. He is unseen, and he fills heaven and earth. Everywhere the faithful worship him and give him glory." Rusticus the prefect said, "Tell me, where do you meet? Or in what place do you gather your disciples?" Justin said, "I live above a certain Martinus, by the bath of Timothinus. This is my second stay in the city of Rome. All this time I have known no other meeting but his. If anyone wished to come to me, I shared the words of truth with him."
- Use: USE WITH WARNING: the bath's name differs in the witnesses (D10); a heard brief should say "a bath-house".

**Q4. Martyrdom 3: "Did Justin make you Christians?"**
- Source: ANF 29338–29344; GEB 1380–1388.
- Final reading: Rusticus the prefect said, "Did Justin make you Christians?" Hierax said, "I was a Christian, and I will be one." Paeon stood and said, "I too am a Christian." Rusticus the prefect said, "Who taught you?" Paeon said, "We received this good confession from our parents." Euelpistus said, "I gladly listened to Justin's words. But I too learned to be a Christian from my parents."
- Use: USABLE.

**Q5. Martyrdom 4: "I do not suppose it, but I know"**
- Source: ANF 29362–29374; GEB 1402–1421, apparatus 1414 (δώματα scripsi] δόγματα codd., δόμαεα O.).
- Final reading: The prefect said to Justin, "Listen, you who are called learned, and think you know the true teachings. If you are flogged and beheaded, are you sure you will go up to heaven?" Justin said, "If I endure these things, I hope to have his gifts. For I know that the divine gift stays with all who have lived this way, until the whole world comes to its completion." Rusticus the prefect said, "So you suppose you will go up to heaven, to receive some reward?" Justin said, "I do not suppose it. I know it, and I am fully sure of it."
- Use: USE WITH WARNING: do not build anything on the word after "his"; the rest of the exchange is secure.

**Q6. Martyrdom 4: "No right-thinking person"**
- Source: ANF 29374–29377; GEB 1421–1425.
- Final reading: Rusticus the prefect said, "Now let us come to the matter in hand, which is pressing. Come together, then, and all of you sacrifice to the gods." Justin said, "No one in his right mind falls away from godliness into ungodliness."
- Use: USABLE.

**Q7. Martyrdom 4: "Do what you will"**
- Source: ANF 29378–29387, ANF note at 29380–29383; GEB 1425–1432.
- Final reading: Rusticus the prefect said, "If you do not obey, you will be punished without mercy." Justin said, "It is our prayer to be punished for the sake of our Lord Jesus Christ, and so to be saved. For this will be our salvation and our confidence at the more fearful, world-wide judgment seat of our Master and Saviour." The other martyrs said the same: "Do what you want. We are Christians, and we do not sacrifice to idols."
- Use: USE WITH WARNING: Justin's first sentence can also be read "Through prayer we can be saved"; the others' answer is clear.

**Q8. Martyrdom 5: sentence, death and burial**
- Source: ANF 29396–29408; GEB 1433–1444.
- Final reading: Rusticus the prefect gave sentence: "Those who would not sacrifice to the gods, or yield to the emperor's command, are to be flogged and led away. They will pay with their heads, as the laws require." The holy martyrs gave glory to God. They went out to the usual place, and their heads were cut off. So they completed their witness in confessing the Saviour. Some of the faithful took their bodies in secret and laid them in a fitting place. The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ worked with them. To him be glory for ever and ever. Amen.
- Use: USABLE.

**Q9. First Apology 1: the address**
- Source: ANF 13961–13969; AP1 68–75.
- Final reading: To the Emperor Titus Aelius Hadrian Antoninus Pius Caesar Augustus, and to his son Verissimus the Philosopher. To Lucius, son by birth of Caesar the Philosopher and son of Pius by adoption, a lover of learning. To the sacred Senate and the whole Roman people. I, Justin, son of Priscus, son of Bacchius, of a family from Flavia Neapolis in Syria Palestine, have made this address and petition. I make it on behalf of people of every race who are unjustly hated and abused. I am one of them.
- Use: USE WITH WARNING: the titles are long for a heard brief; use the tail ("I, Justin, … am one of them"); the Greek makes Lucius the son of "Caesar the Philosopher", the printed English makes Lucius "the Philosopher".

**Q10. First Apology 2: "You can kill, but not hurt us"**
- Source: ANF 13987–13994; AP1 88–96.
- Final reading: We have not come to flatter you with this writing, or to speak to win your favour. We have come to demand that you judge by careful and searching reason. Do not let prejudice hold you, or a wish to please superstitious people. Do not let blind impulse or long-held evil rumour lead you to cast a vote against yourselves. For we reckon that no one can do us any harm, unless we are shown to be doers of evil or found to be wicked. You can kill us, but you cannot harm us.
- Use: USABLE.

**Q11. First Apology 4: punished for the name**
- Source: ANF 14060–14070; AP1 134–145.
- Final reading: You do not punish any of those accused before you until they are proved guilty. But in our case you take the name itself as proof. Yet as far as the name goes, you ought rather to punish our accusers. We are accused of being Christians. But to hate what is good is unjust. Again, if one of the accused denies it in words, and says he is not one, you let him go. You have nothing to prove him a wrongdoer. But if anyone admits he is one, you punish him for admitting it. You ought to examine the life both of the one who confesses and of the one who denies. Then each one's deeds would show what kind of person he is.
- Use: USABLE.

**Q12. Dialogue 1: "Hail, O philosopher!"**
- Source: ANF 17317–17331; DIA 72–79.
- Final reading: One morning I was walking in the walks of the Xystus. A man met me, with others, and said, "Greetings, philosopher!" As he said it, he turned and walked along with me, and his friends turned with him. I greeted him in turn and said, "What is it?" He said, "I was taught at Argos by Corinthus the Socratic not to despise or ignore those who wear this garb. I should show them every kindness, and talk with them."
- Use: USABLE.

**Q13. Dialogue 2: "I expected forthwith to look upon God"**
- Source: ANF 17451–17458; DIA 166–169.
- Final reading: The understanding of things without bodies quite carried me away, and so did the study of the Forms. It gave my mind wings. In a short time I thought I had become wise. In my foolishness I hoped to see God at once. For that is the goal of Plato's philosophy.
- Use: USABLE.

**Q14. Dialogue 3: the old man by the sea**
- Source: ANF 17467–17472; DIA 175–179.
- Final reading: While I was in this frame of mind, I once wanted to be filled with deep quiet, and to keep off the path of men. So I made my way to a field not far from the sea. As I came near the place where I meant to be by myself, a very old man followed me, a little way behind. He did not look like a man to be scorned. His manner was gentle and grave.
- Use: USABLE.

**Q15. Dialogue 8: the flame**
- Source: ANF 17851–17857; DIA 428–435.
- Final reading: He said these things and many more, which there is no time to tell now. Then he went away, telling me to pursue them, and I have not seen him since. At once a fire was lit in my soul. A love took hold of me for the prophets, and for those men who are friends of Christ. As I turned his words over in my mind, I found this alone to be a philosophy that is safe and useful. That is how, and why, I am a philosopher.
- Use: USABLE.

**Q16. Second Apology 12: "fearless of death"**
- Source: ANF 17172–17176; AP2 368–370.
- Final reading: I myself, when I took delight in Plato's teachings, heard the Christians slandered. But I saw them unafraid of death, and of everything else thought fearful. And I realised it was impossible that they were living in wickedness and the love of pleasure.
- Use: USABLE.

**Q17. Second Apology 13: "to be found a Christian"**
- Source: ANF 17218–17230, 17235–17236; AP2 399–408.
- Final reading: I learned that the evil demons had thrown a wicked disguise over the Christians' divine teachings, to turn other people away. I laughed at those who spread these lies, at the disguise itself, and at what the crowd thinks. I confess that I pray, and struggle with all my strength, to be found a Christian. It is not that Plato's teachings are foreign to Christ's. It is that they are not alike in every way, and the same goes for the others: Stoics, poets and prose writers. … So whatever has been said well among all people belongs to us Christians.
- Use: USE WITH WARNING: εὐχόμενος can mean "pray" or "boast" (the printed English has "boast"); "among all people" rests on a word the file lacks.

**Q18. First Apology 46: "those who lived reasonably are Christians"**
- Source: ANF 15684–15696; AP1 1230–1236.
- Final reading: We have been taught that Christ is God's firstborn. We have already shown that he is the Word, in whom every race of people has shared. Those who lived with the Word are Christians, even if they were thought godless. Among the Greeks there were Socrates and Heraclitus, and people like them. Among the non-Greeks there were Abraham, Ananias, Azarias, Mishael and Elijah, and many others. We know it would take too long to list their deeds or names, so we pass over them for now.
- Use: USE WITH WARNING: λόγος is also "reason", and a listener may hear "the Word" as scripture; the card should say Justin means both.

**Q19. First Apology 67: Sunday**
- Source: ANF 16502–16507, 16522–16527; AP1 1759–1767, 1773–1779.
- Final reading: On the day called Sunday, all who live in towns or in the country gather in one place. The memoirs of the apostles or the writings of the prophets are read, for as long as there is time. When the reader stops, the one who presides speaks. He gives a warning, and a call to imitate these good things. … Those who have plenty, and are willing, each give what they wish, as each one chooses. What is gathered is left with the one who presides. He helps orphans and widows, and those in need through sickness or any other cause. He helps those in prison, and the strangers staying among us. ## 4. Impact as the record gives it

Ranked heaviest first. Weight is what later witnesses carried on and used, not what is easiest to tell. [DEFENDED] marks what he defended.

I1. **"Justin, philosopher and martyr."** He was remembered first for his death as a witness, and named by it. Irenaeus dates Tatian's break "after his martyrdom", as a fact everyone knew (Q26). Tatian, his hearer, says Crescens tried to have him killed (Q28). Tertullian calls him "Justin, philosopher and martyr" (Q29). Eusebius: "crowned with divine martyrdom" (Q31). Jerome, twice: "he shed his blood for Christ" (Q33, Q34). Epiphanius: "the holy Justin the martyr" (HOL 15938) and "the perfect crown" (Q35). Theodoret lists him among those who "adorn the martyr's band" (Letter to the Monks of Constantinople, NP3 35178–35181). ACTS; HEARER; LATER. — Rank 1. Every later witness held names his martyrdom; it became part of his name. Carrying quote: Q1 (Rendering 1).

I2. **"You can kill, but not hurt us."** [DEFENDED] He defended Christians before the emperor and Senate: judge us by our deeds, not our name (Q9–Q11, Q24). Later witnesses kept and used the *Apologies* as the record of that age. Eusebius quotes them for Hadrian's letter (IV.8.6–IV.9, EUS 21915–22112), the Bar Kokhba war and Antinous (IV.8.3–4, EUS 21864–21893), the address (IV.12, EUS 22610–22652), and the trial under Urbicus (IV.17, EUS 24087–24198). Jerome's *Chronicle* dates the handing-over of the book (Q34); Jerome's *Lives* lists both addresses (NP3 39975–39979). OWN; LATER. — Rank 2. The most used of his writings by the held later witnesses. Carrying quote: Q10 (Rendering 1).

I3. **"I would not have believed the Lord Himself."** [DEFENDED] He wrote against Marcion and "all the heresies", and later teachers used his words against the same errors. Justin names Marcion as "even at this day alive" and offers his treatise against the heresies (First Apology 26, ANF 14943–14963). Irenaeus quotes him twice, "Justin does well say" (IV.6.2, Q25) and "Truly has Justin remarked" (V.26.2, ANF 53355–53365). Tertullian puts him first among the writers who refuted the heresies (Q29). Eusebius quotes First Apology 26 and Irenaeus's quotations (IV.11.8–10, EUS 22495–22593; IV.18.9, EUS 24340–24354). Jerome lists the works (NP3 39985–39991). OWN; LATER. — Rank 3. Carrying quote: Q25 (Rendering 1).

I4. **"Thus, and for this reason, I am a philosopher."** He was remembered as the philosopher who found in Christ the one true philosophy, and kept a philosopher's dress to teach it. His own two accounts (Q13–Q16). Eusebius quotes the *Second Apology* account (IV.8.5) and retells the *Dialogue* account (IV.18.6, EUS 24310–24315); he says Justin preached "in the guise of a philosopher" (Q30). Jerome: "wearing the garb of philosopher" (Q33). Tatian and Tertullian call him a philosopher (Q27, Q29). OWN; LATER. — Rank 4. Carrying quote: Q15 (Rendering 1).

I5. **"If any one wished to come to me."** He taught in Rome, and his hearers carried the faith; one left it. Euelpistus heard him gladly (Q4). Tatian was "a hearer of Justin's" (Q26); Jerome: Tatian "was distinguished so long as he did not leave his master's side" (Lives 29, NP3 40088–40091); Epiphanius: Tatian "was strong in the faith, as long as he was with the holy Justin the martyr" (HOL 15937–15938, my English); the *Against All Heresies* appendix to Tertullian: Tatian "was Justin Martyr's disciple" (ANF3 55817–55819). ACTS; LATER. — Rank 5. Carrying quote: Q3 (Rendering 1).

I6. **"Remember us as friends."** [DEFENDED] He argued from the Hebrew prophets, with a Jewish teacher, that Jesus is the Christ, and parted from him in friendship (Q23). Eusebius summarises the *Dialogue* and uses it for three things: Justin's search for truth, his statement that "prophetic gifts shone in the Church" in his time, and his naming of the Apocalypse as the apostle John's (IV.18.6–8, EUS 24300–24333; Dial 81, ANF 22211–22214). Jerome lists it (NP3 39984–39985). OWN; LATER. — Rank 6. Carrying quote: Q23 (Rendering 1).

I7. **"I too, therefore, expect to be plotted against."** His open debates with Crescens became the remembered cause of his death. Justin's own words (Q21); Tatian (Q28); Eusebius (Q31); Jerome (Q33, Q34). OWN; HEARER; LATER. — Rank 7. The *Martyrdom* does not name Crescens (D2). Carrying quote: Q21 (Rendering 1).

I8. **"On the day called Sunday."** His account of baptism, the thanksgiving over bread and wine, the Sunday meeting and the collection for the poor (Q19, Q20). OWN. — Rank 8. His own, and full; no held ancient witness quotes or retells it.

I9. **"Whatever things were rightly said among all men, are the property of us Christians."** [DEFENDED] He held that the Word was in every people, so that what was well said anywhere belongs to Christians (Q17, Q18; Second Apology 10, ANF 17089–17123). OWN. — Rank 9. His own; no held ancient witness quotes it.

I10. **"A thousand years in Jerusalem."** He expected the saints to reign a thousand years in a rebuilt Jerusalem, citing John's Revelation, and said many "true Christians, think otherwise" (Dial 80–81, ANF 22107–22118, 22199–22218). OWN. — Rank 10. Eusebius takes only the naming of the Apocalypse as John's from this passage (IV.18.8).

**Card only, not for the story**

I11. **"The earliest extant."** The ANF editors say his *Apologies* "are the earliest extant" and the *Dialogue* "the first elaborate exposition" of Christ as the Messiah of the Old Testament (ANF 13876–13884). These are modern editors' claims about firstness, not held ancient witness.
I12. **Works written in his name.** Works not his were later handed down under his name (the "second" and "third" classes, ANF 13886–13918; OTJ). His name drew later writing.
I13. **The emperors' letters ANF prints after the First Apology.** The "Epistle of Antoninus to the common assembly of Asia" and the "Epistle of Marcus Aurelius to the senate" (ANF 16581–16723) are not Justin's. Eusebius gives the letter of Antoninus as a separate document, not as part of Justin's book (IV.13, EUS 22672–22810).


## 5. Disputes and unclear points

D1. **When did he die, and under which emperor?** (a) Under Marcus Aurelius and Lucius Verus: Eusebius sets his death "about this time", after a second address to the rulers of that reign (Q31; IV.18.2, EUS 24262–24265). Jerome's *Lives* has the second book go to "the successors of this Antoninus" (NP3 39977–39979). The *Martyrdom* has the prefect bid him "submit to the kings", plural (GEB 1310). (b) Under Antoninus Pius: Jerome's *Chronicle* puts the Crescens entry among the years of Antoninus Pius, before his death is entered (FOT 42856–42861, 42907–42909). (c) Under Hadrian: Epiphanius (Q35). The ANF note on the *Martyrdom* points out that Epiphanius also "connects it with the reign of Hadrian" (ANF 29227–29229). The *Martyrdom* names the prefect, Rusticus, but no emperor and no year. Modern dates (163 for Rusticus; 165 or 167 for the death) are card only (C3–C5). Contested.

D2. **Did Crescens bring about his death?** Yes: Eusebius ("in consequence of a plot laid against him by Crescens", Q31); Jerome ("through the efforts and wiles of Crescens", Q33; the *Chronicle*, Q34). Not stated: Justin only expected it (Q21). Tatian says Crescens "endeavoured" to bring death on Justin and on Tatian, not that he succeeded (Q28). The *Martyrdom* does not name Crescens; it gives "wicked decrees" and a refusal to sacrifice (Q2, Q8). McGiffert's view that Eusebius rests on Tatian alone, and that Tatian "does not prove his point", is card only (EUS note at 23782–23795). Contested.

D3. **Which apology is which, and to whom was the second addressed?** Eusebius names two: one to Antoninus Pius (IV.11.11, IV.18.2) and a second to his successor "Antoninus Verus" (IV.18.2, EUS 24262–24265). But Eusebius quotes the Ptolemæus story, which ANF and AP2 print in the *Second Apology*, as from "his first Apology" (IV.17.1, EUS 24087–24088). In that story Lucius appeals to "the Emperor Pius" as reigning (Q22). The ANF editor sets out the modern views (ANF 13920–13930, card only). Contested.

D4. **Where did he meet Trypho?** The *Dialogue* names only "the walks of the Xystus" (Q12). Eusebius says "in the city of Ephesus" (IV.18.6, EUS 24310–24311). Trypho says he lives "in Greece, and chiefly at Corinth" (ANF 17350–17351). Otto's note follows Eusebius (ANF note at 17318–17320, card only). Inferential-Thin for Ephesus (one later witness).

D5. **What were his people?** He calls the Samaritans "my people" and Simon one "of my own nation" (Q36; S2). He calls himself "an uncircumcised man" (S2). Epiphanius: "a Samaritan by race", who came to believe "from among the Samaritans" (Q35). The ANF editor calls him "a Gentile, but born in Samaria" (ANF 13758–13759, card only). Whether his family were Samaritans in religion, or Greeks or Romans living in Samaria, is not stated in his own words. Leave it at his own words.

D6. **His father's name.** His own address: "Justin, the son of Priscus and grandson of Bacchius" (ANF 13965–13966; Πρίσκου τοῦ Βακχείου, AP1 73). Eusebius quotes the same (EUS 22645–22646). Jerome: "the son of Priscus Bacchius" (NP3 39973–39974). Follow his own.

D7. **Two accounts of his turning.** The *Dialogue*: the schools, then the old man by the sea and the prophets (M2–M4). The *Second Apology*: Christians' fearlessness before death showed him the slanders were false (M5). He does not join the two, and neither says the other is wrong. Eusebius gives both (IV.8.5; IV.18.6). Record both as his own.

D8. **What did he hope to have? (Martyrdom 4).** The manuscripts read δόγματα, "teachings" (GEB 1414; ANF note at 29366–29369, "I shall have what He teaches"). The printed English has "His gifts" (ANF 29366), as Otto's δόματα (GEB 1414). Gebhardt's own conjecture, printed in GEB 1406, is δώματα, "dwellings". The writer should not lean on the word.

D9. **How many died with him, and their names.** The ANF heading names "Justin, Chariton, Charites, Pæon, and Liberianus" (ANF 29253–29254). The text of the same account questions Chariton, Charito (a woman), Euelpistus, Hierax, Pæon and Liberianus (ANF 29323–29353). Gebhardt's heading names all seven: Justin, Chariton, Charito, Euelpistus, Hierax, Pæon and Liberianus (GEB 1293–1297, OCR). Follow the text: Justin and six others. McGiffert says "with six companions" (EUS note at 23772, card only).

D10. **The bath.** ANF: "the Timiotinian Bath" (ANF 29309). GEB prints τοῦ Τιμοθίνου βαλανείου, "the bath of Timothinus", with τιμιοτίνου as the manuscripts' reading (GEB 1348, 1363). Otto's Latin notes other forms (OTJ 25389, 25489). Say "a bath-house".

D11. **"Fixed to the stake" or "put in the stocks".** Justin's ξύλῳ ἐμπαγῆναι (AP2 274), "to be fixed in wood", is "fixed to the stake" in ANF (16850) and "put in the stocks" in Eusebius (EUS 23819). The Greek allows both.

D12. **"And indeed on me."** Tatian's Greek and ANF include Tatian himself among Crescens's targets (OTT 8109; ANF2 6554). Eusebius's text of the same sentence does not (EUS 24074–24077). Otto's apparatus records Eusebius's different wording (OTT 8236–8240, card only).

D13. **Which works are his.** His own three are held: the two *Apologies* and the *Dialogue* (ANF classes them "unquestionably genuine", 13894–13898, card only). Eusebius lists, besides these, a work "against the Greeks" on demons, a *Refutation*, *On the Sovereignty of God*, *Psaltes*, *On the Soul*, and the works against Marcion and all heresies (IV.18.2–6, EUS 24217–24315). Jerome lists the same (NP3 39975–39991). ANF prints works with some of these titles (*Discourse to the Greeks*, *On the Sole Government of God*), but places them among those "regarded by some critics as Justin's, and by others as not his" (ANF 13900–13908). Whether the held works with those titles are the ones Eusebius knew is not settled in the held sources. This sheet uses none of them (see "Not his" above).

D14. **The last sentence of the Dialogue.** The Greek held (DIA 6604–6607) and the printed English (ANF 25329–25332, from an emended text, ANF note at 25332–25335) differ. See Q23.

D15. **His age.** Only Epiphanius gives one: "thirty years old" at his death (Q35). Nothing in his own works gives his age. Inferential-Thin.

D16. **The thousand years.** He holds it, and says himself that "many who belong to the pure and pious faith, and are true Christians, think otherwise" (Dial 80, ANF 22114–22116). This is his own report of a disagreement in his day. ANF's chapter heading adds "Several Catholics reject it" (ANF 22094–22095), an editor's heading.

D17. **"Simon of my own nation" (Second Apology 15).** AP2 prints the sentence in square brackets (AP2 431–432), marking it as doubtful in Rauschen's text; ANF prints it plainly (ANF 17271–17275). The same claim stands without doubt in Dial 120 (Q36).


## 6. What the record does not tell us

N1. The record does not give the year of his birth, his age except in Epiphanius (D15), any wife or children, or his trade. His father and grandfather are only names (S1).
N2. The old man by the sea is not named, and the place is not named beyond "a certain field not far from the sea" (Q14). Justin says he never saw him again (Q15).
N3. The record does not say which city was "our city", where the Platonist had "lately settled" (Dial 2, ANF 17448), or where he became a Christian.
N4. The record does not say when he first came to Rome, why he left, or where he was between his two stays (Q3).
N5. The record does not say that he held any office in the church. He describes "the president" and "deacons" (Q19) but never calls himself either.
N6. The record does not give Crescens's own side. All we have of him comes from Justin, Tatian, Eusebius and Jerome (D2).
N7. The record does not say who Martinus was, or what kind of meeting met in his house (Q3).
N8. The *Martyrdom* does not name an accuser, and does not say how Justin and the others came to be arrested beyond "wicked decrees" (Q2).
N9. The record does not name the "suitable place" where the bodies were laid, or who laid them (Q8).
N10. The record does not tell us anything of Trypho outside the *Dialogue* (S5).
N11. His work against Marcion and his treatise "against all the heresies" are lost; only Irenaeus's quotations survive in the Library (Q25; ANF note at 53355–53358). Photius's notice of his works (codex 125) is not in the held Photius volume (`photius_library-v1_freese1920.txt` lists it at line 466, but the text is not there).
N12. No held source tells any wonder of him (see "Wonders").
N13. The record does not say whether the emperor read or answered either *Apology*.
N14. A story that he died by hemlock is mentioned only by the ANF editors, as "an absurd addition ... made" in "some copies" (ANF 29211–29213, 29219–29226). No held text tells it.


## 7. Card only

C1. ANF "Introductory Note to the Writings of Justin Martyr" (ANF 13749–13948): "[a.d. 110–165.]"; "Justin was a Gentile, but born in Samaria, near Jacob's well"; birth "about a.d. 114"; father and grandfather "probably of Roman origin"; "We know that he was some time in Ephesus"; the *Chronicon Paschale* date "165 a.d."; the three classes of works; the question of the two *Apologies*.
C2. ANF "Introductory Note to the Martyrdom of Justin Martyr" (ANF 29209–29230): Crescens's "ill-renown"; the hemlock story; "generally reckoned among the most trustworthy of the Martyria"; the "two Justins" theory; Cave and Epiphanius (Oehler) on Rusticus and Hadrian.
C3. McGiffert's notes to Eusebius IV.16–IV.18 (EUS 23749–23812, 24088–24090): the *Acta* "probably the third century" yet "comparatively truthful"; Rusticus "became prefect of Rome in 163"; the two lines of tradition on the date; Holland in Smith and Wace; Crescens known "only from the pen of his bitterest enemies"; Harnack on IV.16.2; Eusebius quoting the *Second Apology* as the first.
C4. Otto, notes to the *Acta* (OTJ 25100–25112): Rusticus identified as Junius Rusticus, "praefectus urbi ... sub imperatoribus ... Marco Aurelio et Lucio Vero", citing Capitolinus, *Life of Marcus* 3, and the *Digest*.
C5. NPNF note to Jerome, Lives 23 (NP3 39971–39972): "Born about 104 (100?), Christian 133 ... wrote apology about 150, died 167" (Holland).
C6. ANF notes on the *Dialogue*: Otto on the Xystus at Ephesus (17318–17320) and "our city" as Flavia Neapolis or Ephesus (17448–17449); the note on "initiated", literally "having become perfect" (17871–17873); Kaye on Trypho and on Justin's knowledge of the Psalms (25335–25340).
C7. Otto's Tatian apparatus on Eusebius's wording of Address 19 (OTT 8205–8240).
C8. ANF note on the sentence (ANF 29398–29401): its "great indifference to the probable Roman citizenship of some of them".
C9. Gebhardt's preface: his text of Justin's *Martyrdom* follows Otto (GEB 209); his conjecture δώματα (GEB 1414).
C10. "Second century", "A.D." dates and the emperors' modern regnal years come from modern chronology. Justin's own figure is "one hundred and fifty years" after Christ's birth (S3).
C11. ANF note on Crescens as φιλόψοφος (ANF 16852–16856).
C12. The *Chronicle*'s regnal-year columns are misaligned in the OCR (FOT 42807–42843), so the exact year it gives for the Crescens entry cannot be read; only that it stands under Antoninus Pius.
C13. Witness dates. Irenaeus, Tertullian, Eusebius, Jerome, Epiphanius and Theodoret are later writers; how much later is modern dating, not stated in these files.
