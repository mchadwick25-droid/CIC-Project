## What is uncertain

- **Whether Crescens caused his death.** Justin wrote that he expected to be plotted against "by one of those I have named. Or perhaps by Crescens"; he named other enemies first. His hearer Tatian says Crescens tried to have him killed. Eusebius and Jerome say Crescens succeeded. The record of the trial names no accuser and does not name Crescens. Contested (D2).
- **When he died.** The trial record names the prefect, Rusticus, but no emperor and no year. Eusebius sets the death under Marcus Aurelius and Lucius Verus; Jerome's *Chronicle* puts the Crescens entry under Antoninus Pius; Epiphanius says Hadrian. Contested (D1). Epiphanius alone gives an age, thirty. Inferential-Thin (D15).
- **His city and people.** He says his family was from Flavia Neapolis; no ancient source held says he was born there. He calls the Samaritans "my people" and calls himself uncircumcised. Whether his family were Samaritans in religion, or Greeks or Romans living in Samaria, he does not say (D5).
- **The century.** "Second century" is modern reckoning. The "hundred and fifty years" is his own figure, and it dates his writing (S3, C10).
- **Two accounts of his turning.** The story tells the old man by the sea (*Dialogue*). In the *Second Apology* he tells a second story: he saw Christians unafraid of death and judged the slanders false. He does not join the two (D7). The old man and the place are not named (N2).
- **An answer from the emperor.** A letter in the emperor's name was later copied after the *First Apology*. It is not Justin's and is held to be a forgery; no true answer survives (I13). Contested on authenticity. The story leaves it out.
- **The words against Marcion.** The book is lost. Its words reach us second-hand, through the English of Irenaeus's Latin (Q25, N11). The sheet does not describe Marcion's teaching, so the story says only that Justin opposed it.
- **The record of the trial.** Anonymous, its date not stated; Widely Accepted for the hearing before Rusticus and the beheading at Rome (M12, M16). The text questions six companions; the printed heading names four (D9). One word in Justin's answer about heaven is unsure in the manuscripts; the story does not use it (D8).
- **The opening quotation.** Tertullian's phrase, in the sheet's final reading (Q29), has the same wording as one of the sheet's headings; it is quoted as the final reading.
