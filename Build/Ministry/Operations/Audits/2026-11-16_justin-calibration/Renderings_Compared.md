# Renderings compared: Justin Martyr, Q1–Q38

Comparer's sheet, 2026-11-16. Inputs: `Source_Sheet.md` section 3 (printed English, Greek or Latin, Rendering 1 with notes) and `Rendering2.md` (blind Rendering 2 with notes). Every Greek and Latin reading below was re-read in the file at the lines cited, not taken from the sheet. File short names are the sheet's (ANF, GEB, AP1, AP2, DIA, OTT, HOL, FOT, EUS, NP3, ANF2, ANF3).

Verdicts: AGREE = the two renderings say the same thing, and differ only in wording. MERGED-CORRECTED = one or both added, dropped or slanted something, and the final reading fixes it. MEANING DIFFERS = the two give different meanings the text cannot settle, and the card needs an "uncertain" note.

**Q1. Martyrdom 2: "Yes, I am a Christian"**
- Source: ANF 29312–29314; GEB 1353–1354 (Οὐκοῦν λοιπὸν Χριστιανὸς εἶ; … Ναί, Χριστιανός εἰμι).
- Final reading: Rusticus said, "So then, you are a Christian?" Justin said, "Yes, I am a Christian."
- Verdict: AGREE.
- Use: USABLE.
- Reason: λοιπόν here is "so then", not "in the end"; Rendering 1's wording is closer. The printed "Are you not" is not in the Greek. The Greek has no "the prefect" at this line.

**Q2. Martyrdom 1: the decrees, the order, and his answers**
- Source: ANF 29266–29278; GEB 1298–1317.
- Final reading: In the time of the lawless champions of idol worship, ungodly decrees were posted against the godly Christians, in town and country. They were meant to force them to pour offerings to worthless idols. So the holy men were arrested and brought before the prefect of Rome, a man named Rusticus. When they had been brought before his judgment seat, Rusticus the prefect said to Justin, "First, obey the gods, and submit to the emperors." Justin said, "To obey what our Saviour Jesus Christ commanded deserves no blame and no condemnation." Rusticus the prefect said, "What teachings do you follow?" Justin said, "I tried to learn every teaching. But I gave my assent to the true teachings, those of the Christians, even if they do not please people who hold false beliefs."
- Verdict: MERGED-CORRECTED. Rendering 1 dropped the clause "When they had been brought before his judgment seat" (Ὧν εἰσαχθέντων πρὸ τοῦ βήματος, GEB 1308); it is restored from Rendering 2. Rendering 2's "posted" for ἐξετίθετο is kept.
- Use: USABLE.
- Reason: πρῶτον is "first", not "at once". βασιλεῦσιν is the emperors, plural. The printed "at last" has no Greek word behind it and is dropped. Aorists ἐπειράθην and συνεθέμην are given as "I tried" and "I gave".

**Q3. Martyrdom 2: where they meet**
- Source: ANF 29302–29313; GEB 1339–1353 (apparatus 1363: τιμιοτίνου codd.).
- Final reading: Rusticus the prefect said, "Where do you meet?" Justin said, "Wherever each one chooses and is able. Do you really think we all meet in the same place? Not so. The God of the Christians is not bounded by place. He is unseen, and he fills heaven and earth. Everywhere the faithful worship him and give him glory." Rusticus the prefect said, "Tell me, where do you meet? Or in what place do you gather your disciples?" Justin said, "I live above a certain Martinus, by the bath of Timothinus. This is my second stay in the city of Rome. All this time I have known no other meeting but his. If anyone wished to come to me, I shared the words of truth with him."
- Verdict: MERGED-CORRECTED. Rendering 1 dropped the "or" (ἢ) between the prefect's two questions, and dropped "the prefect" (ἔπαρχος), which the Greek has; both are restored.
- Use: USE WITH WARNING: the bath's name differs in the witnesses (D10); a heard brief should say "a bath-house".
- Reason: The Greek has "your disciples" (τοὺς μαθητάς σου). Rendering 2's note that the phrase might mean "Martinus son of Timothinus" is possible but not the natural reading of τοῦ Τιμοθίνου βαλανείου, where the genitive agrees with "bath". The final reading does not lean on it.

- Verdict: AGREE.

The sheet's Greek cut it with "…", but Rendering 1 already carried it, so neither rendering lost it. The sheet's excerpt, not the renderings, is short. ἑστώς is "standing": "stood and said". Rendering 1 also dropped "the prefect" twice; restored.

**Q5. Martyrdom 4: "I do not suppose it, but I know"**
- Source: ANF 29362–29374; GEB 1402–1421, apparatus 1414 (δώματα scripsi] δόγματα codd., δόμαεα O.).
- Final reading: The prefect said to Justin, "Listen, you who are called learned, and think you know the true teachings. If you are flogged and beheaded, are you sure you will go up to heaven?" Justin said, "If I endure these things, I hope to have his gifts. For I know that the divine gift stays with all who have lived this way, until the whole world comes to its completion." Rusticus the prefect said, "So you suppose you will go up to heaven, to receive some reward?" Justin said, "I do not suppose it. I know it, and I am fully sure of it."
- Verdict: MEANING DIFFERS. Rendering 1: "his gifts". Rendering 2: "his dwellings". Card note: "The word after 'his' is uncertain. The manuscripts say 'his teachings'; one editor reads 'his gifts'; another 'his dwellings'."
- Use: USE WITH WARNING: do not build anything on the word after "his"; the rest of the exchange is secure.
- Reason: Copy point settled from the apparatus line itself: the text printed in the file is δώματα, "dwellings", and it is Gebhardt's own conjecture ("scripsi"). The manuscripts read δόγματα, "teachings". Otto ("O.", OCR δόμαεα) reads δόματα, "gifts", which the printed English follows. The final reading keeps "gifts" because the next sentence speaks of "the divine gift" (χάρισμα), but no reading is secure. ἐκπλήρωσις is "completion", which Rendering 2 has; Rendering 1's "end" is close but looser.

**Q6. Martyrdom 4: "No right-thinking person"**
- Source: ANF 29374–29377; GEB 1421–1425.
- Final reading: Rusticus the prefect said, "Now let us come to the matter in hand, which is pressing. Come together, then, and all of you sacrifice to the gods." Justin said, "No one in his right mind falls away from godliness into ungodliness."
- Verdict: MERGED-CORRECTED. Rendering 1 added "forward" to συνελθόντες ("having come together"); dropped. Rendering 2's "godlessness" is replaced by "ungodliness", so that "godless" stays reserved for ἄθεοι (Q18).
- Use: USABLE.
- Reason: ὁμαθυμαδόν is "all together, with one accord": "all of you".

**Q7. Martyrdom 4: "Do what you will"**
- Source: ANF 29378–29387, ANF note at 29380–29383; GEB 1425–1432.
- Final reading: Rusticus the prefect said, "If you do not obey, you will be punished without mercy." Justin said, "It is our prayer to be punished for the sake of our Lord Jesus Christ, and so to be saved. For this will be our salvation and our confidence at the more fearful, world-wide judgment seat of our Master and Saviour." The other martyrs said the same: "Do what you want. We are Christians, and we do not sacrifice to idols."
- Verdict: AGREE.
- Use: USE WITH WARNING: Justin's first sentence can also be read "Through prayer we can be saved"; the others' answer is clear.
- Reason: Both read Δι᾽ εὐχῆς ἔχομεν as "it is our prayer", the reading the ANF note allows. παγκοσμίου is one adjective, "world-wide"; both renderings had spread it into a clause, and the final keeps it as one word to stay under the sentence limit.

**Q8. Martyrdom 5: sentence, death and burial**
- Source: ANF 29396–29408; GEB 1433–1444.
- Final reading: Rusticus the prefect gave sentence: "Those who would not sacrifice to the gods, or yield to the emperor's command, are to be flogged and led away. They will pay with their heads, as the laws require." The holy martyrs gave glory to God. They went out to the usual place, and their heads were cut off. So they completed their witness in confessing the Saviour. Some of the faithful took their bodies in secret and laid them in a fitting place. The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ worked with them. To him be glory for ever and ever. Amen.
- Verdict: AGREE.
- Use: USABLE.
- Reason: Copy point settled: the file reads δοξασθέντες, clean letters with no OCR noise in the word (GEB 1437: Οἱ ἅγιοε μάρτυρες δοξασϑέντες τὸν ϑεόν). Rendering 2 is right that the sheet's δοξασ[αντες] is the sheet's correction, not the file. Whether Gebhardt printed δοξασθέντες or the scan misread it cannot be told from the file. Either way the sense is settled by the object τὸν θεόν: a passive "having been glorified" cannot take it, so only "gave glory to God" construes, as the printed English has. The sheet should quote the file's word and mark the correction. οἱ μὴ βουληθέντες is "those who would not", not "have refused". κεφαλικὴ δίκη is the death penalty by the head: "pay with their heads".

**Q9. First Apology 1: the address**
- Source: ANF 13961–13969; AP1 68–75.
- Final reading: To the Emperor Titus Aelius Hadrian Antoninus Pius Caesar Augustus, and to his son Verissimus the Philosopher. To Lucius, son by birth of Caesar the Philosopher and son of Pius by adoption, a lover of learning. To the sacred Senate and the whole Roman people. I, Justin, son of Priscus, son of Bacchius, of a family from Flavia Neapolis in Syria Palestine, have made this address and petition. I make it on behalf of people of every race who are unjustly hated and abused. I am one of them.
- Verdict: MERGED-CORRECTED. Rendering 1 tied "from Flavia Neapolis" to Justin alone; the Greek τῶν ἀπὸ … is plural, "of those from", his family, as Rendering 2 saw. Rendering 2 followed the printed English order "Augustus Caesar"; the Greek is Καίσαρι Σεβαστῷ, "Caesar Augustus".
- Use: USE WITH WARNING: the titles are long for a heard brief; use the tail ("I, Justin, … am one of them"); the Greek makes Lucius the son of "Caesar the Philosopher", the printed English makes Lucius "the Philosopher".
- Reason: Φιλοσόφου (AP1 70) is genitive and goes with Καίσαρος. The printed English drops "Syria" and adds "wantonly"; the final reading follows the Greek.

**Q10. First Apology 2: "You can kill, but not hurt us"**

Again, if one of the accused denies it in words, and says he is not one, you let him go. You have nothing to prove him a wrongdoer. But if anyone admits he is one, you punish him for admitting it. **Q18. First Apology 46: "those who lived reasonably are Christians"**

**Q22. Second Apology 2: Lucius**
- Source: ANF 16825–16839; AP2 147–157.
- Final reading: Urbicus ordered him to be led away. A man named Lucius, himself also a Christian, saw how unreasonably the judgment had been given. He said to Urbicus, "What is the charge? - Use: USABLE.
- Reason: δεσποτῶν is "masters", not "rulers". τοιοῦτος is "such a one": "one too".

**Q23. Dialogue 142: the parting**
- Source: ANF 25309–25332, note 25332–25335; DIA 6589–6607.
- Final reading: Trypho paused for a while. Then he said, "You see that it was not by design that we came to talk about these things. I admit I have been especially glad of our time together. I think these friends feel as I do. For we have found more than we expected, more than anyone could ever have expected. … But since," he said, "you are about to put out to sea, and expect to sail any day, do not hesitate to remember us as friends when you have gone." "For my part," I said, "if I were staying, I would want this to happen every day. But I expect to sail now, if God allows and helps. So I urge you to take up this greatest of contests, for your own salvation. Be eager to honour the Christ of God Almighty above your own teachers." After this they left, praying for my safety on the voyage and from every harm. I prayed for them and said, "Men, I can pray for nothing greater for you than this. May you learn that by this way it is given to every person to be happy. Then may you too, in every way, do as we do, and hold that Jesus is the Christ of God."
- Verdict: MEANING DIFFERS, in the text itself, on the last two sentences. The Greek held: "learn that by this way it is given to every person to be happy … do as we do". The printed English, from an emended text: "recognising … that intelligence is given to every man … be of the same opinion as ourselves, and believe". Card note: "The last words survive in a damaged form. One reading: 'it is given to every person to be happy'; another: 'understanding is given to every person'." Rendering 2's "come to think as we do" leans toward the emended text; Rendering 1 follows the Greek.
- Use: USE WITH WARNING: the farewell through "above your own teachers" is clear; the closing wish is uncertain and should carry the card note or be cut.
- Reason: Copy point settled: εὐδαιμονεῖν stands clean in DIA 6606, with no keying doubt, and the printed "intelligence" follows an emendation (ANF note). The editor's full stop after εὐδαιμονεῖν splits what is grammatically one ἵνα clause: "that, having learned …, you may in every way do as we do". The final keeps two sentences for the ear, joined by "Then". πάντως and καὶ αὐτοί ("in every way", "you too"), which both renderings dropped, are restored. τὸν Ἰησοῦν εἶναι is an accusative and infinitive with no verb of thinking in the Greek. "Hold" is the one word the final supplies, because the clause does not stand without it. μέγιστον is "greatest", which Rendering 2 weakened to "great". ταὐτὸ γίνεσθαι is "this to happen", not "to do the same".

**Q25. Irenaeus, Against Heresies IV.6.2: "I would not have believed the Lord Himself"**
- Source: ANF 44907–44922 (English only; Eusebius has the first sentence, EUS 24344–24346).

- Verdict: MERGED-CORRECTED. Rendering 1 added "God" after "any" and made "His own handiwork" into "God's own work"; the English "His" goes with the Son who sums up, so it is "his own handiwork". Rendering 1 dropped "begotten"; restored. Rendering 2 turned the three titles into verbs ("formed us, made us and feeds us"); the nouns are kept.

- Reason: No Greek or Latin is held; the printed English is the only base. "Summing up … in Himself" is Irenaeus's key term and is kept as "sums up".

**Q26. Irenaeus, Against Heresies I.28.1: Tatian, "a hearer of Justin's"**
