# R1 review: ten Who profiles, era 3 batch 5

Three independent Opus reviewers, each at first sight of its drafts, reviewed the batch in three parts: part A (Columba, Augustine of Canterbury, Evagrius Scholasticus, Henana of Adiabene, and the README), part B (Leander of Seville, Reccared, Gregory of Tours) and part C (Venantius Fortunatus, Baudonivia, Dorotheus of Gaza). Totals: 11 blocking and 46 minor (part A 6 and 18, part B 3 and 16, part C 2 and 12).

## Part A

Reviewer: independent, first sight of these drafts. Bar: `Who_Profile_Pattern.md` review checklist 1 to 14 and the standards it points to (Style Guide 1.1 to 1.12); `CLAUDE.md` "Trust and source fidelity" and "Accessible and rigorous"; the Who profile rulings in `Decision-Log.md`, 2026-11-12 to 2026-11-14 (majors capped at 2:30, about 405 words, a cap and not a target; non-majors 1 to 2 minutes, at most about 325 words at 162 wpm; recorders at standard length; card sentences over 25 words may stay as card text). Known for this part, by the coordinator's calls: Columba and Augustine of Canterbury are major; Evagrius Scholasticus's card tradition, "The Byzantine Imperial Church (Justinianic)", is the coordinator's correction of the scan; for Henana of Adiabene the coordinator ruled that the brief says his teaching is known only through his opponents while a pupil's praise of the man survives, that his specific charges rest on Babai alone, and that the synods' references to him are inferred by editors. I hunted hardest for the recurring faults: false absences, witness swaps, an editor's year told as the ancient writer's, contested points told as settled, dropped hedges, partisan witnesses unmarked, first mentions of common names without a place, and AI tells. I did not edit any profile file. I made no paid or model-API calls and used no web. Quote search and readability ran on local scripts only.

Profiles: `columba` (major), `augustine-of-canterbury` (major), `evagrius-scholasticus` and `henana-of-adiabene`, with their cards, audio scripts and entries in `records.json`, and the batch `README.md`. Files are in `Build/Ministry/Features/Church-Family-Tree/Who-Profiles/era3/batch5/`. Brief line numbers refer to the `.brief.txt` files, where each paragraph is one line and blank lines count (paragraphs sit on l.1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13). Card fields are named by key and list position. Other line numbers point to files under `cic/texts/`.

Method. I searched every quotation in the four briefs, cards and records entries in the vendored file cited, collapsing whitespace and reading OCR passages by line number (Huyshe's Adomnán, Giles-Jane's Bede, Stokes's Lismore, the two Ewald-Hartmann Register volumes, Turchi, Walford's and Bidez-Parmentier's Evagrius, Freese's Photius, Scher's Barhadbeshabba and Chronicle of Seert, Chabot's and Braun's Synodicon, Labourt, Assemani, Mai, Gismondi's Mari, Guidi). I read each hit in context to see who is speaking: the ancient author, a document he copies, a translator's or editor's note, or a running head. I checked every factual sentence of each brief against its passage. Before accepting each absence or "only" claim I searched the Library for the person under every spelling the sources use (Columba, Columcille, Colum Cille, Colomb Cille; Augustinus, Agustinus; Hnana, Henana, Hannana, Hananus, Khenana, Rannana). That search turned up held files the drafts do not use: the Annales Cambriae (Columba's birth, voyage and death), Wigram's history of the Assyrian church, Budge's notes to Thomas of Marga, Hoffmann's German extracts of the Life of George, and 'Amr's notice of the walk-out (Henana). I compared each `records.json` entry with its brief and card. Readability came from `engine.m1.gates.grade_text`, run from the repo root. Every suggested brief fix below was applied together to a scratch copy and re-scored; the post-fix word count and scores are given with each profile.

**Findings**
- **A-B1 BLOCKING (items 1, 7; a witness's view misreported).** Card `uncertain` 4: "Huyshe says later tradition tied his going to the battle of Cooldrevny and a penance; he calls these legends, and says the Old Irish Life tells only of a pilgrimage long resolved." Brief l.5 tells the same side: "Later Irish tradition, Huyshe says, made the battle the cause of his leaving." Huyshe calls the penance stories legends ("Such are the legends and traditions which cluster about the 'exile of Columba'", l.625-627). But he holds the battle itself to be the cause. His note on Adomnán I.7 says flatly: "The battle was fought in 561… It was the cause of Columba's exile" (l.3022-3026). In the introduction: "The tradition is a constant one that the strife and bloodshed of Cuil Dremhne had something to do with Columba's leaving Ireland" (l.1571-1574). The card makes the editor a doubter of a link he affirms. It also says Adomnán names the battle only for the date, but leaves out that Adomnán tells of a synod at Teltown that excommunicated Columba "for some venial and quite excusable causes", and that Huyshe gives Reeves's view that this was "possibly in 561, after the battle… and Columba's action in bringing about that battle may have been the reason" (III.3, l.9318-9320; note l.9362-9368). Fix, card `uncertain` 4: "Why he left Ireland. Huyshe holds that the battle of Cooldrevny was the cause of his exile, as Irish tradition says. The tale of a penance laid on him he counts among the legends, and he notes that the Old Irish Life tells only of a pilgrimage long resolved. Adomnán names the battle only to date the voyage. He does tell of a synod at Teltown that excommunicated Columba wrongly; William Reeves, as Huyshe reports him, thinks it possibly met over the battle." Fix, brief l.5: "Huyshe, with later Irish tradition, makes the battle the cause of his leaving. Adomnán names it only to date the voyage." Add the Teltown synod and Reeves's reading to the records, the first Documented, the second Inferential-Thin.
- **A-B2 BLOCKING (items 1, 7; false absence).** Card `uncertain` 5: "Huyshe says the Irish annals name Conall…; no annals are held." Records `absent_details` "His years": "No ancient held source gives his birth or death year." The Library holds the Annales Cambriae (`annales-cambriae-rolls-series-20-lat_williams-ab-ithel1860.txt`). It records his birth, his going to Britain and his death on its own count of years: "Lxxvii. Annus. Sanctus Columcille nascitur" (l.2399), "cxviii. Annus. Columcille in Brittania exiit" (l.2417), "CLI. Annus. Columcille moritur" (l.2516). John Williams ab Ithel's margin sets these at 521, 562 and 595 (l.2399, 2417; the margin column for annus CLI, l.2486-2491, reads 595). The file header calls the annals a tenth-century compilation of earlier entries (l.2-3). So a held annal gives a third year for the voyage (562, against Huyshe's 563 and Bede's 565) and a death year (595, against Huyshe's 597), and the card's "no annals are held" is false as worded. It is true that no Irish annals (Ulster, Tigernach) are held. Fix, card `uncertain` 2, add: "The Welsh annals, the Annales Cambriae, put his going to Britain in their year 118, which John Williams ab Ithel, their editor, sets at 562." Card `uncertain` 3, add: "The same annals record his birth and death in years their editor sets at 521 and 595." Card `uncertain` 5: "…no Irish annals are held." should read "…the Irish annals Huyshe cites are not among the sources used here." Records: add three claims on the Annales Cambriae (the annal's own count, Documented; the editor's years, Dominant Modern Reconstruction), add the Annales to the contested claims on the year of arrival and on his years, change the `absent_details` note to "No Irish annals are held; the Annales Cambriae give birth, voyage and death by their own count", and add the Annales to `sources.about`.
- **A-M1 MINOR (item 1).** Card `uncertain` 3: "Whitley Stokes reports that Bishop William Reeves chose 521 for his birth from among 517, 521 and 523." Stokes says the Thursday, 7 December, birthday points to 517 and 523, "But Bishop Reeves thinks a.d. 521 'most likely to be the true period'" (l.24008-24011). Reeves chose 521 against the two years the weekday gives. Fix: "Whitley Stokes reports that the birthday the Irish Lives give, a Thursday, 7 December, points to 517 or 523, but Bishop William Reeves thinks 521 most likely."
- **A-M2 MINOR (items 4, 6).** Brief l.9: "Bede has one complaint. Columba's heirs followed uncertain rules for Easter." Two short sentences, the first a beat line with no fact and a loose "one". Bede writes from the Roman side of the Easter question (records claim 15 notes it), and he gives the cause himself: "as having none to bring them the synodal decrees… by reason of their being so far away from the rest of the world" (l.5871-5874). The brief places Bede (l.5) but never marks his side on the one point where he takes one. Fix: "Bede, who held to the Roman Easter, says Columba's heirs kept uncertain rules for it."
- **A-M3 MINOR (item 1).** Brief l.11: "In the night he gave his monks his last words." Adomnán says he gave his last commands "to the Brethren, his attendant alone hearing them" (l.10806-10807). The monks did not hear them. Fix: "In the night he gave his last words for the monks, with only Diormit to hear:".
- **A-M4 MINOR (item 4).** Two narrator sentences pass 25 words. Brief l.1 (26): fix, "Then he told Diormit in secret that this day was his Sabbath, his rest. That night he would go the way of his fathers." Brief l.9 (27): fix, "Bede adds that he had founded the Field of Oaks in Ireland. Many houses in both lands grew from these two."
- **A-M5 MINOR (item 8).** Card `legacy.remembered` 3: "Wilfrid granted he might be a holy man…". Wilfrid is a common name, and this is the card's first mention. Bede introduces him as "Wilfrid a priest" in the monastery of King Alchfrid (l.7951-7954). Fix: "…; Wilfrid, the priest who spoke for the Roman side, granted…". (`legacy.remembered` 3 also says "the Irish party called him their most reverend Father Columba"; it was Colman who spoke, l.8117-8122. That wording is acceptable as is.)

Not findings. "In his forty-second year, two years after a battle" follows Adomnán, who also writes "Two years having passed after the battle" (I.7, l.3000). Bede's 565 stands in Bede's own sentence (l.5809). "Bede of Jarrow" rests on Bede's own account of himself (l.14336). Adomnán is placed as later and his Life marked as a book of wonders (l.3), as the pattern asks for a hagiographer's record.

- **A-B3 BLOCKING (items 1, 3; two meetings made one).** Brief l.9: "Bede tells of a meeting with British bishops at a place later called Augustine's Oak. The hermit's test, the chair, the refusal and the warning all belong to that second synod, whose place Bede does not give: "They that were to go to the aforesaid council, repaired first to a certain holy and discreet man… 'Do you contrive… that he may first arrive with his company at the place where the synod is to be held'" (l.3632-3660); "when they came, Augustine was sitting on a chair" (l.3670).
