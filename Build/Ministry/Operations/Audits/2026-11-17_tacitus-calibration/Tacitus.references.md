## What is uncertain

- Who Tacitus was. No held ancient text gives his office, family, birthplace, dates or career. "Roman senator" and "written c. 116 CE, describing events of 64 CE" stand only in the vendored file's header (C1, N2, N3). The brief uses neither.
- His source. He names none for Christus, Pilate or the Christians (N1). The file header says the passage's "authenticity is not seriously disputed" (C2).
- Whether the punishment of Christians was tied to the fire. Tacitus and Sulpicius say so; Tertullian, Melito, Eusebius and Augustine tell Nero's persecution without the fire; Jerome has both as separate entries; Lactantius gives the faith's growth as the reason (D2). Modern scholars debate whether a fire-linked persecution of Christians as a named group took place (Shaw against Jones, C2). Suetonius tells the punishment and the fire apart (C4).
- Whether Nero started the fire. Tacitus reports a belief; Sulpicius says all blamed Nero; Jerome and Suetonius say he did it (D3, C4).
- What the first prisoners pleaded guilty to, and how many died. The English gives no object and no number; the Latin of that sentence is not held (D4, D5).
- Which "shameful acts" Tacitus means (N6).
- Whether Sulpicius drew on Tacitus. He does not name him; the match in order and Halm's apparatus point that way. Inferential-Thin (D7, C6).
- The Latin. Most of the chapter is held in English only; the Latin held is a later scholar's quotation, which leaves out the Christus and Pilate clause without marking the gap (Q4 note, N4, G1).
- The name. A manuscript reading "chrestianos" corrected to "christianos" (D9, C2).
- Tertullian's book number. *Apology* 16 says the fifth book of the Histories, *To the Nations* I.11 the fourth; the ANF note holds the fifth correct (C5). The Histories are not held, so Tertullian's account of them cannot be checked (N4). "First put this notion" in the printed English is the translator's; the Latin has no "first" (Q9 note).
- Which "records" Tertullian means in *Apology* 5. He does not say they are Tacitus's (N8). No held early Christian text names the Annals passage (N9).
- The words flagged as misleading in the 1876 English ("mischievous", "exquisite", "circus", "car") are not used; the brief follows Rendering 1 ("deadly").
- Mark's ruling (2026-11-17): where Tacitus names the faith, the brief says "deadly superstition", because the Latin "exitiabilis" means deadly. The draft already had this phrase from Rendering 1 (Q4), so the ruling changed no wording.
