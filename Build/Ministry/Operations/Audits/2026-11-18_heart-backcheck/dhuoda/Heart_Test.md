## 1. The heart, before hearing the story

Dhuoda, a Frankish noblewoman kept apart from her sons, dictated at Uzès (841–843) a handbook of Christian faith and conduct for her elder son William. It teaches him to seek God, pray, love God and then his father, serve his king, and care for widows, orphans and pilgrims, and it holds her own verses and epitaph. She is on the tree because the book survives as one of very few voices of faith from the church of Charlemagne's heirs that is not a cleric's, a mother claiming her place as her son's teacher, and her editor called it the oldest writing by a woman born in France to reach us.

What the sources hold for each part:
- **She wrote a handbook for William, 841–843, dictated, at Uzès.** Her own book, self-dated (E8, E9, E10, S2). Documented. Completion year 843: Dominant Modern Reconstruction; "more than a year" is Documented (E11).
- **What it teaches.** Her own words: seeking God, short pure prayer, God then father, loyalty to Charles, widows, orphans, pilgrims, just judging (T3–T8). Documented. The acrostic verses and epitaph: Documented (T9, E17).
- **A mother as teacher.** Her own words, "no teacher like a mother" (T4). Documented.
- **Why she matters: a lay voice.** The census framing, "one of very few lay voices" (S7, I6). The project's own framing, not a witness; Widely Accepted at most as stated. Safe as "not one of the clergy".
- **Oldest writing by a woman born in France.** Bondurand's claim (I4). Contested / Inferential-Thin as a general claim; say it as his.
- **Copied, kept, printed, praised.** Documented (I1, I2, I4); later use in 1923 Documented as their use (I5).

Nothing central is missing from the sheet or the outline. The outline's thread names the substance ("dictated a handbook of faith", "very few voices ... not the clergy"). Its beats, though, put the book at beat 4 and its contents at beat 5, after three beats of family and politics.

## 3. Verdict

**FIX.** The material is in the outline and sources. The story does not put it in the first minute.

Missing from the first minute, and what in the outline can carry it (no new claims):
- **It is a handbook of faith for William, which she dictated.** Thread line; beat 4 (E8, E9). Can follow straight after the opening quote, in place of "a book".
- **What it asks of him, in brief.** Beat 5 (T5, T6, T7): seek God, pray in short pure words, love God and then his father, serve Charles, help widows and orphans.
- **Why she is on the tree.** Thread line (S7): one of very few voices from the church of Charlemagne's heirs that is not the clergy's. A noblewoman, a mother claiming her place as teacher (Q3, T4).
- **Optional, as his claim:** her editor called it the oldest writing by a woman born in France to reach us (I4). Now absent from the whole story.

The family and political beats (words 54–151) can move after the heart, since they explain the separation the opening quote already names.

Aside, outside the heart test: words 101–113 say Bernard "had him taken from her". The outline's do-not-say list allows "had the baby brought to him" and notes she makes no complaint (E4). "Taken from her" leans toward the forbidden "snatched from her". Worth a check by the next reviewer.
