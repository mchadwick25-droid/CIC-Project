## 1. The heart, before hearing the story

Marcion, a teacher at Rome in the middle of the second century, taught that the God of the law was not the Father of Jesus. He set the law against the gospel and kept a Scripture of his own. His churches spread from Rome to Persia and lasted for centuries. He is on the tree because he founded those churches, and because the church's writers answered him, book after book, that one God made the world and sent Jesus.

What the sources hold for each part:

- **Another god, above the Creator.** Documented. Justin says it while Marcion is alive (S2, M4, Q1). Irenaeus and Tertullian agree on the core (M4).
- **The law set against the gospel; the *Antitheses*.** Documented as Tertullian's report (M4, Q3). The book is lost (N2).
- **His own Scripture.** Documented as his opponents' report: Luke and Paul shortened (M5). His own claim, that others had added to the Gospel and he was restoring it, is Documented as Tertullian's report of it (M6).
- **His churches spread and lasted.** Documented: Justin in his lifetime, Tertullian, and Epiphanius two centuries later (I1). Their martyrs are Documented as reported, with the sources' own hedges (I2).
- **The church's answer.** Documented: Justin, Irenaeus, Tertullian's five books, and others (I3). Irenaeus's case for four Gospels against him is Documented (I4). Whether he caused the canon is Contested and rightly left out.
- **Why he taught it (the evil question).** Tertullian's hostile reconstruction. As Marcion's real reasons, Inferential-Thin (M3).

Nothing central is missing. The outline's Thread line states the heart plainly: "By splitting the law from the gospel and building churches of his own, Marcion drove the church's writers to argue, book after book, that one God made the world and sent Jesus."

## 2. Hearing the story

The story (`longDescription`) is 379 words. The first 150 words carry:

- **1–25:** the Polycarp scene. "Do you know me?" / "the firstborn of Satan."
- **26–35:** who tells it (Irenaeus, who saw Polycarp young).
- **36–62:** setting. A shipmaster from Pontus gives money to the church at Rome.
- **63–79:** how we know. No word of his own survives; only his opponents speak.
- **80–149:** Tertullian's account of the evil question: the good tree, Isaiah, the Creator as a bad tree. Ends with "his real reasons remain uncertain."
- **150:** Justin begins to speak, as the minute ends.

Does the heart land? **In part, and weakly.**

- **Two gods:** first arrives at word 136 ("found another, good God in Christ"), inside Tertullian's hostile reconstruction, and is hedged at once (141–149). Justin's plain statement, "some other god, greater than the Creator", arrives at words 171–180, after the minute.
- **Law against gospel:** word 189 (the *Antitheses*), then Q3 at 195. After the minute.
- **His own Scripture:** word 212. After the minute.
- **The church's answer:** words 268–301. After the minute.
- **His churches spread:** words 312–378, the last fifth of the story. "Marcion founded churches of his own" first comes at word 326.

The parts are given weight later. The teaching gets two quotes. The churches get about 65 words and the Tertullian wasps line. The church's answer gets about 35 words. Nothing is passed in one clause, except the four Gospels.

Where the first minute goes: about 35 words of scene and witness, 27 of setting and the gift, 17 of how-we-know, and about 70 on a reconstruction of motive that the story itself calls uncertain. A listener at word 150 knows he met Polycarp, gave money and brooded over evil. They do not yet know what he taught in plain words, that he founded churches, or why the church fought him so hard.

## 3. Verdict

**FIX.**

Missing from the first minute: the plain teaching (another god above the Creator; law set against gospel), his churches spreading widely, and the church's long answer.

What in the outline can carry it, with no new claims:

- The Thread line, said early: by splitting the law from the gospel and building churches of his own, he drove the church's writers to argue, book after book, that one God made the world and sent Jesus.
- Opening 2 and Q1: Justin, writing to the emperor in Marcion's lifetime, that he "is still teaching" the people he has won over "to believe in some other god, greater than the Creator."
- Q3: "Separating the law from the gospel is Marcion's own work, and his chief one."
- What came of him 1: Justin saw "many of every nation" follow him (I1).

The Polycarp scene can stay as the opening if it is short. The evil-question beat (M3) can move after the minute, where it still reads as Tertullian's account. Keep every hostile charge credited to its speaker, per Mark's ruling of 2026-11-17.
