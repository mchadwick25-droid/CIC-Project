## 1. The heart, before hearing the story

Hild ruled the double monastery of men and women at Whitby (after Hartlepool), and made it a house of Scripture study, shared goods and peace, so that it gave the young Northumbrian church five bishops. Her wisdom drew ordinary people and even kings and princes to seek her advice. She is on the tree as the abbess who received Caedmon and set him to turn Scripture into English song, and whose house held the synod of 664, where she stood with the Irish-trained side.

What the sources hold for each part:
- **Ruled Hartlepool, then Whitby, a house of men and women** (M6, M8, M10). Documented. Whether she founded Whitby or set an existing house in order: Contested within Bede (D1).
- **Scripture study, no one rich or poor, many fit to be priests** (M9, M11). Documented as Bede's account.
- **Five bishops from her house**, Oftfor through both houses to Theodore and Rome (I1). Documented; Bede writes "we saw".
- **Kings and princes sought her advice** (M12). Documented as Bede's praise; no king named.
- **Received Caedmon, made him a monk, had him taught sacred history** (M18, M19, I2). Documented as Bede's account; the gift itself is a wonder in Bede's voice.
- **Synod of 664 in her monastery; she held with the Scots** (M13, M14, M16). Documented (Bede, and Stephen for the place and her presence). Her words and her response to the ruling: not in the record (N5, D4).
- **Called Mother** (I4). Documented.

Nothing central is missing from the source sheet or the outline. The outline's thread ("a house ... where the Scriptures were read so closely that the young English church took five bishops from it, and where a man who could not sing was made a monk") is the heart, and aims at substance, not how-we-know.

## 3. Verdict

**FIX.** The material is all in the outline and sources. The story spends the first minute on an unresolved scene, setting, Bede as witness and her early years, and reaches what she built only after word 170.

Missing from the first minute, and what in the outline carries it (no new claims):
- **What she built at Whitby**: a house of men and women, no one rich or poor, Scripture read so closely that many became fit to be priests (beat 4; M8–M11).
- **Five bishops from her house** (What came of them 1; Quote 1; I1). Can be named early in a clause and kept as the closing quote.
- **Kings and princes sought her advice** (beat 4; Quote 2; M12). Should come back into the story at all.
- **Caedmon received and made a monk at her word**, so the opening scene resolves inside the minute (beat 6; M18).
- **The synod of 664 met in her house, and she held with the Scots** (beat 5; M13–M14), at least in one clause.

The setting, the line on Bede, and the early life (birth, faith, Gaul, Aidan) can follow the heart rather than precede it. The rules in the outline's Do not say list still hold: no "founded" as plain fact, no "trained five bishops", no words of hers at the synod, no named king.
