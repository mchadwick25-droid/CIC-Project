# Heart test: Macrina the Younger

Read: the census entry `macrina-the-younger` (`knownFor`, `card`, then `longDescription` as the story), `Source_Sheet.md` and `Outline.md` in `2026-11-17_varied-batch/macrina-the-younger/`. Not read: Review*, Recheck*, OneHearing_Test*, Fixes_R50, Card_*, Second_Readings, or any `.brief.*` file. The first minute is taken as 150 words.

## 1. The heart

Macrina, the eldest of the family of Basil and Gregory of Nyssa, turned her family's household into a community of prayer and shared work. Mistress and slave women lived there as equals, men and women lived in one community, and foundlings were taken in. She shaped her brothers, and the brother who sat with her as she died kept her teaching on death and rising again in her own voice, as "the Teacher". That is why she is on the tree: a woman who made a new kind of Christian household, and whose words were still being argued over a thousand years later.

What the sources hold for each part:

- **Eldest of the family; the household turned to prayer and work.** Documented, from Gregory's *Life* (sheet M6, LIFE 138, 158). Her mother lived with the maids "in the same food, the same kind of bed", in prayer "by night and day".
- **A community of women and men, with Peter leading the men.** Documented as fact (sheet C1, LIFE 192, 356). Clarke's "double monastery" reading, and its later weight as an early picture of such a house, is Dominant Modern Reconstruction (sheet C1, I2).
- **Foundlings, widows, the deaconess Lampadia.** Documented (sheet M9, C2).
- **Shaping her brothers.** Peter: Documented (LIFE 164). Gregory: Documented (the rebuke and the teaching, sheet M14, I1). Basil: Contested (sheet D1; Gregory of Nazianzus says Basil had already chosen that life).
- **Her teaching kept as "the Teacher".** Documented that she taught and that Gregory wrote the dialogue with her as teacher (sheet M13, I1). How much of the argument is hers: Contested (sheet D2).
- **Quoted at Ferrara, 1438.** Documented (sheet I1, PO15).
- **The year 379.** Widely Accepted, a modern date (sheet S3).

Nothing central is missing from the source sheet or the outline. The outline's own thread (outline line 9) names the substance, not how-we-know: the household made into a community of prayer and shared work, and her teaching kept in her voice.

## 2. One hearing

The story is the census `longDescription`. It runs 404 words (counted twice, by a word split and by `wc -w`; both give 404).

What the first 150 words carry:

- **Words 1-36: scene.** Gregory arrives; she lies on a sack over a board and lifts herself on one elbow.
- **Words 37-60: who she is.** Her name, "the eldest of their family" (words 41-46), and her two bishop brothers.
- **Words 61-81: place.** Fourth century, Pontus, the Black Sea, modern Turkey.
- **Words 82-101: how we know.** Gregory sat with her and wrote her life; "nearly our only witness".
- **Words 102-144: the betrothal.** The young man died; she refused every other offer, in the hope of rising again.
- **Words 145-150: the turn begins.** "After her father died, she led..." The sentence that says what she did runs on past the line, to word 161.

Where each part of the heart first arrives:

- Eldest of the family: word 41. In the first minute.
- Led her mother to live as an equal with the slave women: words 145-161. It starts in the first minute but finishes after it.
- "A community of prayer and shared work": words 177-187. After the first minute.
- Peter leading the men's side; the foundlings: words 192-226. After.
- Her teaching on death and rising, kept by Gregory as "the Teacher": words 277-330. After, in the last quarter.
- Ferrara, 1438: words 331-404. After.
- Basil drawn to a life of work, as Gregory tells it: not in the story at all. The outline had it as beat 4.

So the heart lands **in part**. The first minute says who she is and where, but not what she did. Her one act, the household made into a community, is cut in half by the line. Her teaching, the second half of the heart, waits until word 277.

The heart is given weight later, not passed in one clause. The household gets two full sentences (words 145-187), and the teaching and Ferrara together get the last 128 words. The weight is there. It just comes too late.

Where the first minute's time goes: about 36 words on the opening scene, 20 on how we know, 21 on place, and 43 on the betrothal. That is about 80 of 150 words on scene, setting and witness, before the story reaches what she did. The betrothal is real substance about her choice, but it is the turn, not the life she made.

## 3. Verdict

**FIX.** The material is in the outline and the sources, but the story does not put the heart in the first minute.

Missing from the first minute:

- That she turned the family house into a community of prayer and shared work, with mistress and slave women living as equals. The outline carries it in its thread (line 9) and beat 5 (sheet M6, LIFE 138, 158).
- That her teaching on death and rising again was kept in her voice, as "the Teacher", by the brother who sat with her as she died. The outline carries it in its thread (line 9) and "What came of them" item 1 (sheet I1, D2). It can be told in one sentence early, with the full weight kept for later.

What can make room, with no new claims: the how-we-know sentence (words 82-101) can join the teaching sentence, since Gregory as witness and Gregory as keeper of her teaching are the same fact. The place line can shorten. The opening scene is good and need not go, but it uses a quarter of the minute. The betrothal can follow the heart rather than come before it.

Nothing here is invented, and the floor, the sack and the board are strong design, not faults.
