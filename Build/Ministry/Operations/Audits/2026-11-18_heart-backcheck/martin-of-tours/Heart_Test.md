# Heart test: Martin of Tours

Read: the census entry `martin-of-tours` (card, teaser, knownFor; then `longDescription` as the story), `Source_Sheet.md`, `Outline.md`. Not read: Review, Recheck, OneHearing_Test, Fixes_R50, Card_*, Second_Readings, or any `.brief.*` file.

## 1. The heart

Martin was a Roman soldier who asked to leave the army because, as Christ's soldier, he could not fight. He became a monk and a founder of monasteries in Gaul, and then a bishop of Tours who never stopped living as a monk. Many of his monks became bishops in their turn. He is on the tree as the first figure of the monk-bishops of Gaul. He is also there for what came after him: his stand at Trier that a church's case was not for an emperor to judge, his tomb at Tours, and the Life by Sulpicius, which became a model for later lives of saints.

What the sources hold for each part:
- **Soldier who would not fight.** Forced into the army, the cloak at Amiens, the request for discharge and "I am Christ's soldier" (M1, M3, M6). Documented, as Sulpicius tells it. The peace as Martin's doing is Sulpicius's reading.
- **Monk and founder of monasteries.** A monastery near Poitiers (M9) and the house outside Tours with eighty disciples (M11). Documented, as Sulpicius tells it. The names Ligugé and Marmoutier, and "among the first monks of Gaul" (S5), are Dominant Modern Reconstruction (Gibson).
- **Bishop who stayed a monk.** Same humility and plain clothes as bishop; the cell outside the city (M10, M11). Documented, as Sulpicius tells it.
- **Others followed; his monks became bishops.** Eighty disciples, monks made bishops, "almost two thousand" monks at the funeral, as it was said (I3, M20). Documented as Sulpicius's account; the funeral number is hedged.
- **The stand at Trier.** No secular judge in a church case, no blood for heresy, and the one-day communion to save lives (M15, M16, I4). Documented as Sulpicius's account. The principle rests on Sulpicius alone; Gregory uses the story for a different point.
- **The Life as model.** Read while Martin lived (I2b), Documented. Its reach to Rome, Carthage and Egypt (I2a), Inferential-Thin, Sulpicius's own claim. Copied and continued by Gregory and Fortunatus (I2c, I2d), Widely Accepted. That it became *the* model for saints' lives, Dominant Modern Reconstruction (Brehaut).
- **The tomb at Tours.** The great church, the feast, Clovis's edict (I1). Documented, through Gregory about 180–195 years later. "The chief point of Christian pilgrimage in Gaul" is a modern judgment (Brehaut).

Nothing central is missing from the source sheet or the outline. The outline's own thread says the heart well: "A soldier who would not fight became a bishop who never stopped living as a monk, and the book his friend wrote about him made that pattern a model for later lives of saints."

## 2. Hearing the story

The story is 525 words. The first minute is about 150 words.

What words 1–150 carry:
- **1–41: scene.** The cloak at the gate of Amiens, the onlookers who laugh and groan.
- **42–50: name and setting.** "The soldier was Martin, in Roman Gaul in the fourth century."
- **51–74: how we know.** Sulpicius, one admirer, who met him late in life.
- **75–102: childhood and enlistment.** Born to pagans in Pannonia, asked for baptism at ten, forced into the army.
- **103–124: the dream and baptism.** Christ in the half-cloak; baptized; two more years in the army.
- **125–150: the donative.** Julian's payout, the request for discharge, and the quote begins ("I am Christ's", 148–150). The quote ends at 157.

Where each part of the heart first arrives:
- Soldier who would not fight: the soldier at 40, the refusal at 147–157. **In part**: it lands at the very edge of the minute.
- Monk and founder of monasteries: "founded a monastery" at 196. **Not in the first minute.**
- Bishop who stayed a monk: "As bishop he still lived as a monk" at 228–235. **Not in the first minute.**
- His monks became bishops: 411–413. **Not in the first minute**, and passed in one clause.
- Trier stand: 310–351. Given real weight, with the quote, but **not in the first minute.**
- The Life as a model for saints' lives: **never said.** Only Sulpicius's claim that it was read in Rome, Carthage and Egypt (415–428), in half a sentence.
- The tomb and Tours: 431–449, one sentence.
- The tradition he heads (the monks and monk-bishops of Gaul) is never named.

So the heart lands **in part**. The first minute carries the soldier's turn and nothing of the monk, the bishop, or why he matters. About 41 words go to scene, about 24 to how-we-know, and about 85 to childhood and army procedure. Later, the monk-bishop gets two sentences and Trier gets its weight, but the afterlife is a quick list. The Life's place as a model, which the outline names as half its thread, does not appear.

## 3. Verdict

**FIX.** The material is in the outline and the sources; the story does not put it in the first minute.

Missing from the first minute, and what in the outline can carry it (no new claims):
- **Monk and bishop who stayed a monk.** Outline thread, and beats 4–5 (M9, M10, M11): "founded a monastery near Poitiers"; "As bishop he still lived as a monk."
- **Others followed him.** What came of them 3 (I3): many of his monks became bishops.
- **Why he is on the tree.** The setting line "among the monks and monk-bishops of Gaul" (S5), and What came of them 2 (I2d): Sulpicius's Life, which a modern historian, Brehaut, calls a model for later lives of saints.
- **The Trier stand, named early.** Teaser and beat 6 (Q2, M15): he pleaded that a church's case was not for an emperor's judge.

What can give way: the cloak scene can be shorter, the how-we-know line (51–74) can move later, and the childhood (75–102) and the donative detail can be compressed. The cloak and "I am Christ's soldier" can stay as the opening image; they are design, not fault.
