## Pattern summary

1. **The round 1 fixes held.** Every round 1 blocking finding in 01-32 is fixed without new error of the same weight, and every hedge on the cards is now kept. The new blocking items are small and mechanical. One is a tradition name used as a grammatical object (08). The other two swap a named witness for a role (15, 29), which ruling 3's "prefer left out" invited. In both, the point carries the line's "what it shows" and cannot be dropped, so the name has to come back.

2. **The "so" payoff is the set's tic.** Fifteen of the 32 lines hinge S2 on "so" (01, 04, 05, 07, 09, 10, 11, 13, 15, 19, 23, 24, and the sentence-initial "So" in 18, 22, 26). Read one after another, the cadence is audible: fact, comma, "so", payoff clause. In five lines the "so" also claims a cause the sources do not give (07, 13, 18, 24, 26). Those are logged above as minor fidelity or logic findings. Where the "so" truly joins a reason to an act, it earns its place (04, 10, 22). Plainer joins already in the set work well. 20 states the point as a plain sentence. 12 joins with "and". 27 uses "but". 30 opens with "Because". Lines whose payoff leans toward a tag line: 19 ("speak in more than Leoba's one surviving letter"), 23 ("reaches us long before their books") and the draft2 wording of 05. None is bad enough to block. Recommend converting roughly half of the "so" lines to a plain statement or an "and" join, starting with those that make a false cause.

3. **Honest-limit phrasing drifts toward site talk.** "Tied only by time" (21), "tied only by place" (32), "the tie is one of time alone" (31) and "the era of" (14) all describe the website's link rather than the people. The honest limit is right in each case. It reads better as a plain fact of dates or place told about the person, as in the 32 and 21 replacements.

4. **Name identifiers are now consistent except for 24.** Hildegard is the one common name left without her place. Macarius (09) stays bare because neither card nor census gives his place, and that gap should be logged.

## Pattern summary

**Round 1 fixes held.** Every R1 blocking finding in C and D (34, 37, 48, 49, 52, 53, 56, 59) is fixed without new blocking error. Where the revisers declined R1 wording, their reasons were sound each time: 37's contrast, 48's Pacific clause, 49's "roots", 50's stray "its", 54's "Like X" opener, 56's York correction and 61's variety point. The revisers checked R1's suggested wording instead of copying it. That caught real problems, for example in 49, where R1's "roots" would have brought back the origin claim.

**What the new text got wrong.** The new errors are small, and they share one habit: a join word claims more than the source joins. In 47 a "so" makes the faith's spread follow from the reconciliation. In 52 a "because" stands where the card has "and". In 37 two links in a chain collapse into one ("his tie to the Radical Pietists"). Two further lines stretch past their source. In 55 a "which shows" payoff rests on the reviser's own inference about where the text was written, and in 56 "of that church" leans back toward the continuity R1 blocked. In 39 a blanket hedge falls on claims the caveat says are independently documented.

**Named witnesses (item 4).** 39 ("her confessor", who is Philippe Couplet) and 42 ("its minister", who is William McCulloch) let a role stand in for the person the card names. Both are fixed above at no cost in length.

**Site talk.** 60's "outside their regions" is the only line in the range where a reader hears the map's data instead of the story. Elsewhere tradition names are given in words, and no line talks about the site.

**Cadence (item 5).** Read across the 30 lines, the second sentences fall into two templates. One is the "so" payoff, which appears in 35, 40, 42, 43, 44, 45 and 47 (and 37 opens on "So"). The other is "X shows Y", which appears in 48, 49, 50, 51, 52, 53, 55, 56, 57, 59 and 61, with three lines opening flatly on "It shows" (52, 59, 61). Either template is fine on its own, but read card after card they become a refrain.

The weakest "so" lines are these:
- 44, where the "so" clause only restates the main clause (fixed above with a participle).
- 47, where the "so" makes a false cause (fixed above with "and").
- 43, "so the faith outlasted the missionaries' absence", which reads as a closing tag. A plainer join would be "..., and the faith had lasted through the missionaries' absence".

For the "shows" lines, varying a few verbs would break the refrain without changing any claim:
- 59: "Its converts were pacifists caught between both sides..."
- 61: "Its church grew from one outcaste man's initiative..."
- 53: keep "shows". Its claim is exactly a "shows" claim.

None of these is blocking. The cadence is worth a light pass across the full set of 62 before publishing, not another review round.
