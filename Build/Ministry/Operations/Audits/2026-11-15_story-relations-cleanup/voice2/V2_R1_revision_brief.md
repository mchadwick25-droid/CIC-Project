# Revision brief, round 1 fixes: "How it connects" lines

You revise drafted lines after an independent review. Read scratch/BRIEF.md (the rules) in full first, then the review file for your batch, scratch/rev/R1_<batch>.md.

For each story in your range:
- Start from scratch/draft/NN_<id>.json and its packet scratch/pk/NN_<id>.json (census entries at scratch/mov/<id>.json).
- Fix every BLOCKING finding. Fix every MINOR finding unless doing so would break a rule or cost a claim; if you decline one, say why in the note. The reviewer's suggested wording is a suggestion: check its support yourself before using it.
- Rulings that apply to everyone:
  1. A linked neighbor tradition the story does not truly belong to may simply be left out; Mark said not to restate the card's nearby list. Rule 10 applies only when the line names a link: then it must be honest about its kind.
  2. The second sentence must say what the connection shows (why it matters), supported by card or census, never a retelling of card facts. If no supported "what it shows" exists, the honest limit may stand as the second sentence, framed as what the link does and does not show.
  3. Place at first mention only for a name that could mean more than one person (Basil of Caesarea, Moses the Ethiopian, Simeon the Stylite, Pliny the Younger). Do not dress tradition names as epithets ("Basil of the Cappadocians" is wrong). A modern scholar named in a line must be given a few words of who he is, or left out (prefer left out).
  4. No quotation marks around words the Library does not hold verbatim (Masona, Abercius).
  5. Length: about 35 to 45 words. Lines over 45 need a reason in the note. Plain words; one breath per sentence; no nested clauses. No "&" in prose: write the tradition name in words.
- Write scratch/draft2/NN_<id>.json in the same format as the draft (id, line, support with verbatim quotes, note), with the note listing each finding number and what you did. Copy unchanged PASS lines across as they are.
