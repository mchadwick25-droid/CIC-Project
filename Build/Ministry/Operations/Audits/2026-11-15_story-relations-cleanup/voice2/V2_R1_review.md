### 01 silesian-childrens-prayer-revival
**BLOCKING** (42 words)

1. **fidelity.** "The revival reaches Herrnhut through Teschen". This claims the revival itself was carried on to Herrnhut. The card says only that Steinmetz's preaching formed many of Zinzendorf's refugees ("the specific link this entry draws to Herrnhut"). Nothing says the children's revival, or anyone in it, reached Herrnhut. The old line's honest limit on that point was dropped, which is right, but "reaches" now overstates in the other direction.
2. **fidelity.** "so Silesia's restored Protestant life helped shape Herrnhut". "Restored" makes Steinmetz's preaching part of what the 1707 agreement restored. The card never links Steinmetz to the Church of Grace or to the restored rights (Old_Line_Claims flags this exact tie as unsupported), and the drafter's note admits the inference.
3. **craft.** "where the 1707 agreement that coincided with it opened one of six Churches of Grace" has a relative clause nested inside a relative clause, and the ear loses "it".

Suggested replacement (44 words):
"The revival links to Herrnhut through Teschen, which won one of six Churches of Grace under the 1707 agreement the revival coincided with. Teschen pastor Johann Adam Steinmetz's preaching formed many of the refugees Zinzendorf took in, so Silesia's Protestant life helped shape Herrnhut."
Support: card text "It coincided with real diplomatic change: the 1707 Convention of Altranstädt ... leading to six new 'Churches of Grace' — among them the Jesus Church at Teschen (Cieszyn)"; card sources (Hutton) "Names Teschen pastor Johann Adam Steinmetz's preaching as the formation ground for many of the evangelical refugees Zinzendorf received — the specific link this entry draws to Herrnhut." "Links ... through Teschen" is the card's own framing. "Helped shape" is the mildest reading of "formation ground".

### 02 plinys-two-ministrae
**MINOR** (47 words)

1. **name.** "Pliny" at first mention could be the Elder or the Younger. The card says "Pliny the Younger". Use the epithet, since no place tells the two apart.

## Patterns

The drafts are faithful at the level of individual quotes: every support quote checked out verbatim, and the known traps were all handled correctly. Those traps were Anatolia not being Pliny's province, Abercius's "same faith" left unquoted, Olympias as an ordained deaconess, and Abitinae's "reportedly". The drafter also caught a real error in Old_Line_Claims, the Lesser Armenia support in the Forty Martyrs card's region field. Four kinds of failure recur.

1. **Unsupported links.** The blocking findings are mostly small causal or generalizing words the sources do not carry: "reaches" and "restored" (01), "so" (12), and "its teaching on judging others" (09). The one quote problem (16) repeats the Abercius trap for a different text.
2. **Missing identifiers on common names.** This is the most frequent finding: Pliny, Basil, Moses, Macarius, Olympias, Simeon, Severinus, Benedict and the unexplained Hodgkin. Tradition short names are also being pressed into service as identifiers ("Basil of the Cappadocians", "Clovis of Merovingian Gaul", "Gregory of Nyssa of the Cappadocians"), and a raw "Church & Empire" ampersand appears.
3. **Second sentences that restate the card.** Several second sentences copy the card's closing sentence or caveat (05, 08, 14, 15) or add facts (07, 16) instead of saying what the connection shows. That is the form rule Mark set, and 07 fails it outright.
4. **Neighbors left out without a word.** Linked traditions the story does not truly belong to are mostly dropped silently rather than named as neighbors (rule 10 says "say so"). The omissions invent nothing, so I have not counted them as findings. The coordinator may want to confirm with Mark that omission satisfies rule 10.

Lengths run 40-47 words, mostly 43-47. Several fixes above sit at 46-48 because they add the required identifiers. Where Mark's 35-45 range matters more, the spare words to cut are in the second sentences.

Line 06 changes an approved cut, for sound reasons, and needs to go back in front of Mark.

### 17 john-the-almsgiver
Verdict: MINOR

1. craft/name. "Heraclius made John..." A reader does not learn that Heraclius was the emperor until "the emperor's side" in sentence two. Fix: "The emperor Heraclius made John...". Support: card text "at the emperor Heraclius' wish".

3. craft (note). The card's story is John's relief of the Jerusalem refugees, and the card links Jerusalem. The line leaves out Jerusalem without a word. The drafter's reason (the Jerusalem tradition ends in 450) is sound. Still, this is the one omission a reader of this card will notice. Not required to fix, but see the pattern note on rule 10.

Suggested line (45 words): "The emperor Heraclius made John the Chalcedonian patriarch of Alexandria, though most of the city's Christians followed the rival Coptic patriarch of the Church of Alexandria. So his story shows the emperor's side of the city's two lines of bishops, in the decades before the Arab conquest."

## Patterns

Fidelity is strong across the batch. Every quote is verbatim, the old unsupported claims are dropped throughout, and the known traps (Lombardy, Abitinae-style hedges) are respected. The failures fall into five repeating kinds:

1. Sentence two slips back into a chain of facts with no "what it shows" (22, 26), or the named connection is held back to sentence two (30). This is the "too factual" shape Mark rejected.
2. Common names lack their place at first mention: Christina, Hildegard, Juliana, Joan, and Llull by surname alone. This is a mechanical fix.
3. A hedge on the card is lost when the drafter follows a flat teaser (31), or a phrase says a little more than its source: "tell how" (20), "met them" (24), "the two orders could get" (25), "made his cause the papal church's own" (28), "that church's bishops" (18). Two claims are merged into one (18, the hymn against the later Scripture verses).
4. A short name containing "&" misleads when read as places (32, Zurich & Geneva), and "&" is awkward in spoken prose generally (18).
5. The drafter leaves out linked neighbor traditions without a word, citing rule 5. Rule 10 asks that such neighbors be said honestly within the two sentences. I have not counted the omissions as findings, because nothing false is said. The coordinator may want one ruling on whether silent omission meets rule 10, especially where the omitted tradition is what the story is about (17, Jerusalem).

Lengths sit at about 39 to 46 words, inside Mark's range. No line has an AI-tell device.

## Summary of patterns

The most common failure is the second sentence. In four lines (48, 52, 59, and in part 56) it retells card events or states an honest limit, and never says what the connection shows.
