# Blind scoring, reader Z

Six stories, scored in order against the brief kit (Parts 1 and 2, and the bar in Part 4). Each story was read once, straight through, before its outline was opened. Every quote was rechecked in its file in `cic/texts/`. Readability comes from `python -m engine.m1.profile_readability`.

## S04

- **Who:** Evagrius Ponticus, a gifted deacon of Constantinople who became a monk in the Egyptian desert.
- **What happened:** A love for a married woman and a vision with an oath drove him from the city. Melania held him to the monk's life. In the desert he named the eight tempting thoughts and taught that prayer is the mind's own work.
- **Why it matters:** His eight thoughts and his teaching on prayer reached Gaul (Gennadius, Cassian's matching list) and the Syriac East (Thomas of Marga), even though later councils counted him among the condemned.

Scores:
- **Voice 4.** It sounds like an adult documentary narrator, and the vision and the oath land well. The last third leans toward a roll call of witnesses (Gennadius, Cassian, Thomas, Jerome, the councils, a historian).
- **Craft 4.** The hook leads straight into the essence, which is told by about 150 words. The life is told once and in order, and it ends on his death with the opening's desire quiet at last. There are many names (about twelve), and Sozomen and Socrates are named where the outline gives them by role.
- **Rigor 4.** Every quote checks verbatim (Palladius 457 §7; Praktikos 6 at dysinger 197; Jerome at npnf206 27825-27826; Gennadius's "or among the first at least"). Smaller points: "passions" is used before it is given its plain sense, which the outline asks for. Q2 uses Dysinger's raw wording rather than the outline's rendering. "Palladius says only that Evagrius imagined the husband had acted" is placed before the vision, though in Palladius that thought comes inside it.
- **Accessibility 4.** Grade 7.4, reading ease 78.8. The raw Q2 makes a 34-word sentence with semicolons, which is hard to follow by ear. The 681 words run slightly past the 675 ceiling.

BLOCKING: none.

## S07

- **Who:** Bernard of Clairvaux, a Burgundian noble who became a monk and the abbot of Clairvaux.
- **What happened:** He took his brothers and more than thirty companions into Cîteaux and founded Clairvaux. He preached on the Song of Songs and moved against Abelard. At the pope's order he preached the crusade, opposed killing the Jews while calling their scattering a just penalty, and took the blame when the crusade failed.
- **Why it matters:** His book of advice to his former monk, Pope Eugenius, was turned by Jan Hus against the popes of his own day. At Bernard's death a hundred and sixty monasteries were under his care.

Scores:
- **Voice 3.** The Nivard opening is good. From the middle on, the story is a run of paragraphs that each open on a year ("In 1140", "In 1145", "In 1146", "That year", "In 1148"), so it reads more like a chronicle than a told life.
- **Craft 3.** The essence comes early, but its "what it changed" is thin: one line, "popes and kings would come to ask Bernard's word". The Hus connection lands, but "It was a friend's counsel" barely carries the outline's point that this was loyal advice later turned into a charge. It ends on his death, Geoffrey's count and the unfinished sermon.
- **Rigor 4.** The quotes match their Latin (PL183 105303-105306; PL182 75604-75611; VP E1), and the Rhine, Abelard and the crusade keep their force and their credits. "The house was so small it had almost lost hope of lasting" is told after Nivard follows, though it describes the house when they entered. "Bernard wrote that the armies were killed by the sword or by hunger" is placed after Damascus, with nothing to say it was the march.
- **Accessibility 3.** Grade 6.5. Two passages trip a listener. The first is the credit sentence flagged below. The second: in "The king of France had asked him again and again", "him" could be Eugenius as easily as Bernard.

BLOCKING:
- "Bernard, with more than thirty companions, his friend William, who heard him preach, says, entered Cîteaux": heard once, this makes William one of the companions. The credit is garbled and the sentence misleads on one hearing. Split it, for example: "More than thirty companions went with him into Cîteaux, his friend William says."

## R1

- **Who:** Basil of Caesarea, who left teaching rhetoric for a life of prayer and shared work and became bishop of Caesarea.
- **What happened:** He wrote rules for monks living together, built a great lodging for the poor and the sick outside the city, and stood up to the emperor's official. He defended the Holy Spirit's place beside the Father and the Son, and died before the council of 381.
- **Why it matters:** Rufinus put his Rule into Latin, Benedict pointed his monks to it, and the council of 787 told the double monasteries to keep it. The council of 381 confessed the Spirit as he had argued.

Scores:
- **Voice 4.** The opening in his own words is strong, and "Who turned him away?", told as three answers, is a real scene.
- **Craft 3.** The middle is a string of "Gregory says" tags, about seven of them. The famine beat is dropped although it is not on the cut list. The ending is one thin sentence that jumps back from 787 to his deathbed.
- **Rigor 4.** The quotes check out: Letter 223 at npnf208 39133-39135 (rendered fairly), the Greek of Longer Rule 7 at PG31 72547-72549, and Letter 159 at npnf208 33483-33485. It keeps the outline's Do not say wordings on Benedict, the council of 381 and "in a letter put it this way".
- **Accessibility 3.** Grade 7.4. "Rhetoric" is used in the essence before its gloss arrives in the next paragraph. Two Gregories are named, then "Gregory says" runs on without saying which. "Gregory's speech in Basil's memory" drops the outline's "his friend's". The Sasima clause ("his friend bishop of Sasima, a hurt, Gregory said") never shows that the friend is the witness. The two forms of praise are hard to follow by ear, and nothing says why "with" mattered.

BLOCKING: none. The Gregory ambiguity is the heaviest MINOR. Say "his friend Gregory" at the official's scene, as the outline directs.

## S11

- **Who:** Priscillian, a rich and learned Spanish layman who became bishop of Abila.
- **What happened:** He called men and women to give up family, wealth and rank for God. Accused by other bishops and refused by Damasus and Ambrose, he appealed to the emperor. He was convicted of sorcery at Maximus's court at Trier and beheaded in about 385.
- **Why it matters:** His death split the church's memory. His followers kept him as a martyr and his own side's bishops quarreled for years, while Leo of Rome later praised the sword. His own words came back in 1885.

Scores:
- **Voice 4.** It is serious and even-handed. Sulpicius's "the accused displease me as much as the accusers" is used exactly for its job.
- **Craft 4.** The hook is his own call, and the essence comes by about 115 words. The life is told once, in order. Saragossa's two accounts sit side by side as a scene, and the ending returns to the opening's book.
- **Rigor 5.** Q1 matches Schepss at 4415-4417 and Q2 matches Halm at 5890-5891 ("inter nostros" supports "the other side"). Q3 is a faithful rendering of Feltoe at npnf212 3276-3278. "Beyond his lands" is in Sulpicius at npnf211 11532-11535. The court's findings are credited, and the story says plainly that no account from his side survives.
- **Accessibility 4.** Grade 8.3. "The deaths split the other side. Its bishops above all quarreled" takes a moment to place on one hearing.

BLOCKING: none.

## S12

- **Who:** Peter, bishop of Alexandria through the Great Persecution.
- **What happened:** In 306 he set rules for taking back those who had given way, case by case. One of his bishops, Melitius, broke away and started a rival church. Peter was beheaded in 311.
- **Why it matters:** His rules became Greek church law, sealed in 692. The split he left came before Nicaea in 325 and outlived it.

Scores:
- **Voice 4.** The hook in his own words, with "the marks of Jesus" explained, is moving. "Historians still weigh the two" is close to a stock line.
- **Craft 4.** The essence, including Nicaea, comes within the first minute. The break is told from both held sides, and it ends on Athanasius and the day kept for his memory. The rules paragraph comes close to a list of terms, though each one has its reason.
- **Rigor 5.** All three quotes match their Greek: Pedalion 49722-49723, Epiphanius in Oehler 43852-43853, and Eusebius in Schwartz 17556-17560, with the English confirming each. "As though" on Maximinus is kept, the charges are credited, and the condition in Peter's letter is kept.
- **Accessibility 4.** Grade 8.0. Communion, sacrifice and the fourth Easter are each glossed in the sentence where they come. At 637 words it runs well past the outline's planned 550, but stays under the ceiling.

BLOCKING: none.

## R2

- **Who:** Epiphanius, a monk from Palestine who became bishop of Salamis in Cyprus and wrote the Panarion, the "medicine chest" against eighty heresies.
- **What happened:** He catalogued the heresies with a cure for each. In old age he fought the followers of Origen at Jerusalem and Constantinople, using force (a forced ordination, a torn curtain) and refusing to pray with John Chrysostom. He died at sea on the way home.
- **Why it matters:** His list became the standard later writers used, Augustine among them. Four centuries on, both sides in the quarrel over images argued from his book and his curtain letter.

Scores:
- **Voice 4.** The medicine chest and the Euphrates water are vivid, and the narration stays adult.
- **Craft 4.** The hook leads straight into the essence, which is told by about 150 words. The Long Brother's question is a good turn, and the ending returns to the opening's image. It ends on the book rather than the man.
- **Rigor 4.** Q1 matches Holl at 12387-12391 and Q2 matches Fremantle at npnf206 10920-10922. The charges against Origen match 10813-10840 and 10896-10925, and "from the beginning" is in Holl 12398. Socrates and Sozomen are credited apart. Theophilus's earlier charge is told in the narrator's voice; Socrates and others hold it, and an editor doubts it.
- **Accessibility 4.** Grade 8.1. At 714 words it runs past the outline's 675 ceiling and the cut-first list was not applied. It also carries about ten names, including Apollinaris and Charlemagne, each heard once.

BLOCKING: none.
