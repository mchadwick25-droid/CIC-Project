# Blind scoring, reader X

## S01

- Who: John Cassian, a monk who lived among the desert monks of Egypt and then wrote at Marseilles.
- What happened: he put the desert's rules and talks into Latin, in the Institutes and the Conferences; his thirteenth Conference, on grace and free will, drew Prosper's attack.
- Why it matters: Benedict's Rule had his books read to monks, and later readers kept him, with a warning on free will.

Scores:
- Voice 4: the opening (two friends, the frozen land, the books making the journey) and the closing hope in his own words are distinctive.
- Craft 3: the back half turns into a report about the record ("The letter does not name Cassian", "It names no author, but these are Cassian's titles", "By the translator's account").
- Rigor 4: the quotes check against Gibson and Richardson, and the Do not say list is kept, but "Benedict's monks did not come from his houses" states as fact what the record only fails to show.
- Accessibility 4: grade 7.8, clear words; "his thirteenth Conference" straight after Benedict's Rule can be heard as Benedict's; "the translator" is never introduced; about fifteen names.

BLOCKING: none.

## S05

- Who: Dionysius, pupil of Origen, head of the church's school and then bishop of Alexandria through persecution and plague.
- What happened: wedding guests snatched him from soldiers; he doubted the apostle wrote Revelation; his blunt images of the Son led to an appeal to Rome and his four books in answer.
- Why it matters: his letters, kept by Eusebius, are the eyewitness record, and a century later the followers of Arius claimed his words while Athanasius took him back.

Scores:
- Voice 5: his own rescue plea, the measured Revelation judgment and Basil's woodman keep the world's own voice.
- Craft 4: tight and in order, but the hook runs to five sentences, and the plague paragraph says "dying for the faith" and then quotes the same thought.
- Rigor 5: the Greek of all three quotes checks; wonders, the hostile witness and the contested reading stay in their sources' voices.
- Accessibility 4: grade 7.6 and plain, but "Nicaea's creed" goes unglossed, and "two persecutions" is said when only one is told.

BLOCKING: none.

## R1

- Who: Basil of Caesarea, a rhetoric teacher turned monk and then bishop of Caesarea.
- What happened: he wrote rules for monks living together, built a great lodging for the poor and sick, faced down the emperor's official and defended the Holy Spirit's place.
- Why it matters: his Rule passed into Latin and Benedict pointed his monks to it, and a council after his death confessed the Spirit as he had defended.

Scores:
- Voice 4: the waking from sleep and "Whom, then, will you wash?" are strong and his own.
- Craft 3: "Gregory says" comes about eight times and is never pinned to one Gregory; "Gregory's speech in Basil's memory is praise" is a stock how-we-know sentence; the one-line ending lands thin.
- Rigor 4: the quotes check, but "Basil came back to serve the bishop" follows no told departure, the famine beat is dropped, and "they held" loses the outline's "he reports".
- Accessibility 3: two Gregories are introduced together, and then the witness is just "Gregory"; at the Nyssa and Sasima sentence the listener cannot tell whose hurt it is.

BLOCKING: none (the Gregory ambiguity is a strong MINOR: name "his friend Gregory" at the first "Gregory says" in Pontus and at the speech).

## S09

- Who: Leo, bishop of Rome from 440 to 461.
- What happened: his letter, the Tome, on Christ as one person in two natures, was refused a reading at Ephesus in 449 and taken up at Chalcedon in 451; he rejected the council's rule ranking Constantinople next to Rome, and he met Attila.
- Why it matters: the Tome's faith held in the Greek and Latin churches and split them from Egypt and Syria, and Rome's claim from Peter fought Constantinople's rank for centuries.

Scores:
- Voice 4: the Chalcedon opening and the rock letter are vivid; the Attila scene, with three witnesses, is told well.
- Craft 3: "This was the age of Church and Empire." is pasted on, and the second half is a tight but flat chain of letters.
- Rigor 4: the Latin of the quotes (Mansi), Jordanes and Hydatius check, but "Read by each side against the other" from Also told is missing, and so is the deacon's escape, which the outline said must stay.
- Accessibility 4: grade 7.2, nature and person glossed well, and clear on one hearing.

BLOCKING:
- Also told, "What came of him: each side read him against the other" (Nestorius's praise, and the Syriac chronicle's charge), is absent, though the outline marks it as not to be cut. Add one or two sentences after the Tome's faith held.

## S10

- Who: Eusebius, bishop of Nicomedia, and later of Constantinople.
- What happened: he took up Arius's cause; at Nicaea he signed the creed but not the condemnation, by his own plea; he was exiled, then recalled, and he led the removal of Arius's opponents.
- Why it matters: his party, "those around Eusebius", held the eastern churches after him, and by Jerome's account he baptized Constantine.

Scores:
- Voice 3: the Nicaea doubt-scene and Constantine's letter carry force, but much reads as a run of "X says, Y says".
- Craft 3: rigor drives the shape into a report about the record (the baptism and Ulfila paragraphs), and the ending is two verdicts by witnesses.
- Rigor 5: the Greek of all three quotes checks, and every hostile claim is credited, both Ulfila datings included, as the Do not say list asks.
- Accessibility 3: grade 9.3; "Nicaea" is never said to be a council until after the fact; "bribed the woman who charged him" comes with no charge and no clear "him"; about thirteen names.

BLOCKING: none.

## R2

- Who: Epiphanius, a monk from Palestine who became bishop of Salamis in Cyprus.
- What happened: he wrote the Panarion, a "medicine chest" naming eighty heresies with their cures; in old age he fought Origen's followers in Jerusalem and Constantinople, using force, and died at sea.
- Why it matters: Augustine followed his list, and four centuries on both sides of the quarrel over images claimed him.

Scores:
- Voice 4: the medicine chest and the Euphrates water are his own images, and well chosen.
- Craft 4: the life is in order and the force is told plainly, and the ending returns to the opening image; the "What came" paragraph is compressed.
- Rigor 5: Holl's Greek and Fremantle's English check, and Socrates and Sozomen are set side by side.
- Accessibility 4: grade 8.1, terms glossed, though "used a man of simple mind and manners" does not make plain that the man is Epiphanius; 714 words, over the outline's 675 cap with its Cut first list already spent.

BLOCKING: none.
