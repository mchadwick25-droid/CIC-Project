# Source sheet: Catherine of Siena

Researcher's sheet for this batch ([date]). The job (Mark's, [date]): a dyer's daughter who joined no convent, gathered a circle around her, and wrote to popes and cities; at Avignon in 1376 she pressed Gregory XI to return to Rome. Her Dialogue was dictated to her followers. How much she moved the pope is disputed, and the story says so. Shape: Founder or leader. New entry and card. The writer uses the outline, the shapes and the standard only; this sheet is the evidence behind the outline.

**Files and short names used below** (all in `cic/texts/` unless marked):
- **LET** = `catherine-of-siena_selected-letters_scudder1905.txt`: her letters in Vida Scudder's English (1905; the scan is the 1911 reprint). Scudder's introduction, her headnotes to each letter and her "Chief events" chronology are **LET-ed**: the editor's, not Catherine's. The Italian letters (Tommaseo) are **not held**, so every letter is rendered from Scudder's English.
- **LIB** = `catherine-of-siena_libro-della-divina-dottrina-ita_fiorilli1912.txt`: the Italian of the Dialogue (Fiorilli 1912, Project Gutenberg proofread text, clean). The Guidini memoir itself (Arch. stor. ital. 1843) is not held; it is read in Fiorilli's quotation.

- **RAY** = `acta-sanctorum_aprilis-tomus-3-lat_palme1866.txt`: the Acta Sanctorum for 30 April, holding Raymond of Capua's Latin Life of Catherine (the *Legenda*, from l.193225), the Bollandist commentary before it (**RAY-ed**), Barduccio Canigiani's letter on her death in the Bollandists' Latin (**BARD**, from l.214539) and Stefano Maconi's letter (from l.215000). **Conventions.** Line numbers are each file's own lines. OCR quotes are given as printed; where the OCR is broken, the clean reading is given in brackets after it.

**Language.** Catherine spoke and dictated in the Tuscan of Siena (RAY l.200970-200972: "ipsa enim non intelligebat Latinum sermonem"; GUID LIB l.14403). Held in her own language: the Dialogue (LIB). Not held in her language: the letters (English only, LET). Raymond's Life is held in its own Latin (RAY); Barduccio's letter only in the Bollandists' Latin translation of a printed Italian of 1517 (RAY l.214541-214547). Guidini's memoir is held only as Fiorilli quotes it (clean text).

**Witness kinds and gaps.**
- OWN = Catherine's letters (1375-1380; LET), dictated (LET-ed l.518-522; RAY l.193177-193186), and the Dialogue (dictated 1378; LIB).
- CONTEMP, her circle = **Raymond of Capua**, her confessor, who was with her at Avignon and was her interpreter there (RAY l.200969-200972, 214064-214067). He wrote her Life about ten years after her death, by the Bollandists' reckoning (RAY-ed l.192580-192582), and wrote it to show her holiness ("ut noscas quantae fuerint excellentiae dona sacrae huic virgini concessa ex alto", RAY l.201022-201024). He was not at her death (RAY-ed l.192640-192641). A partisan witness: her confessor and her order's champion. **Cristofano Guidini**, a notary and one of her secretaries (GUID, LIB-ed l.14385-14389). **Barduccio Canigiani**, a secretary who was with her at her death (RAY-ed l.192651-192654; BARD). **Stefano Maconi**, a secretary (MAC).
- LATER, about a generation = **Gobelinus**, an official of Paderborn who also reports events of 1408-1409 (MANSI l.111555-111559), cited in MANSI on the return.
- LATER, about a century = **Platina**, writer of a Life of Gregory XI, cited in MANSI and DUR (his date is not given in the held lines).
- LATE = Duranti (1628); Covel (1722); the Bollandist commentary (RAY-ed, 1866 reprint).
- MODERN = Scudder (LET-ed, 1905), Fiorilli (LIB-ed, 1912), Gardner (1910), Lea (1888), Thorold's title page (1896).
- **Not held**: the Italian of her letters; the full contemporary Lives of Gregory XI (the held Baluze volume, `baluze_vitae-paparum-avenionensium-v1-lat_mollat1921.txt`, has no line on Gregory XI despite its header; it prints older acts); a bull of canonization; Gerson's treatises on testing visions (only his tomus 2, pars 1 is held, with no line on her); Raymond's own account in any language but the damaged Latin.

## Setting

- **S1. Siena, a dyer's family.** RAY l.194254-194257 (CONTEMP, Raymond): "Porro dictus Jacobus artem exercebat componendi seu faciendi colores, quibus panni lanei seu lanae tinguntur. Unde tam ipse quam filii ejus, in patria illa tinctores vocabantur" (OCR: "comptmeudi seu faeiendi colores, qiiibus panni ianei seulana? tingiintnr. Unde tam ipse quam filii ejus, patria in illa tiiictores vocabantur"). Her father Jacopo made the colors that wool cloth is dyed with, so he and his sons were called dyers. Raymond says he had this partly from Catherine and her mother Lapa (RAY l.194261-194265). LET-ed l.1258-1261: "She is the youngest of the twenty-five children of Jacopo Benincasa, a dyer, and Lapa, his wife." **Documented** (the dyer: Raymond and Scudder agree); the twenty-five children and the birth day, 25 March 1347, are Scudder's chronology alone (**Inferential-Thin** as a fact; tell only "a dyer's daughter").
- **S2. Born 1347, died 1380.** LET-ed l.1258 (1347); LET-ed l.1394-1395 ("on April 30th she dies, in the presence of her disciples", 1380). COVEL l.51460-51461: "St. Catharine of Sienna died at three and thirty years of Age anno 1380." **Widely Accepted** (1380); birth year Scudder's, agreeing with Covel's "three and thirty": say "born in 1347", no day.
- **S3. The popes at Avignon.** CEN tradition `longDescription`: "In 1309 Clement V settled the papal court at Avignon. For nearly seventy years the popes governed the Latin church from the Rhône rather than the Tiber." MANSI l.53393-53395: the return "qui erat a translatione curiae Romanae in Gallias per Clementem facta annus septuagesimus" (the seventieth year since Clement moved the papal court to France). **Documented** (as the tree's tradition entry and the notice say it).
- **S4. Florence at war with the pope.** LET-ed l.1200-1202 (1374): "Florence, enraged, rebels against the Pope, and appoints from the ranks of the Ghibellines a new body of Magistrates, known as the Eight of War." LET-ed l.1211-1215, 1222-1227 (1376): the papal nuncio "flayed alive in the streets of Florence"; "The city is placed under an Interdict"; "War is declared against Florence by the Pope", Count Robert of Geneva's army, "the hideous massacre of Cesena". RAY l.214043-214048 (Raymond): Gregory "contra dictos Florentinos fecit processus terribiles", so that they were seized and stripped of their goods almost everywhere they traded. MANSI l.53384-53387: the pope sent the cardinal of Geneva with six thousand Breton horsemen. **Documented** (the war and the interdict: Scudder, Raymond and the notice agree); the details (nuncio flayed, Cesena) are Scudder's outline, **Widely Accepted** as hers.
- **S5. Tradition, spoken name.** CEN `name`: "The Latin Papal Church (Avignon to Fifth Lateran)". She is its second voice: "Catherine of Siena — a lay Dominican whose letters pressed Gregory XI to bring the papacy back to Rome" (CEN `voices`); `people`: "Catherine of Siena", year 1370, voice 1, held = the Scudder letters. Region "Avignon, Rome, Latin Christendom".

## Events

### E1-E3. The life she chose, and the circle

- **E1. The Mantellate, not a convent.** RAY l.196385-196392 (Raymond): she begged her parents and pressed the "Sororibus de poenitentia B. Dominici, quae vulgari sermone Mantellatae vocantur" (the Sisters of Penance of St Dominic, called the Mantellate in Siena) to receive her and give her their habit; her mother, unwilling, tried to draw her back. LET-ed l.1279-1282: "She is vested with the black and white habit of Saint Dominic, becoming one of the Mantellate, or Dominican tertiaries, devout women who lived under religious rule in their own homes." LET-ed l.1413-1415: the Mantellate "were not cloistered, nor did they take the monastic vows; they simply lived in their own homes a life of special devotion." **Documented** (she wore the habit of the Sisters of Penance: Raymond); that they lived at home and took no monastic vows is Scudder's (**Widely Accepted**, editor). Year 1363-4 is Scudder's.
- **E2. She could not write; she dictated.** LET-ed l.518-522: "Her letters were talked rather than written. She learned to write only three years before her death, and even after this time was in the habit of dictating her correspondence, sometimes two or three letters at a time, to the noble youths who served her as secretaries." RAY l.193177-193186 (Raymond, eyewitness): "Vidi ergo saepe ipsam dictantem duobus scriptoribus diversas epistolas, diversis personis mittendas, et de diversis materiis" (OCR "Vidi rgo sapeipsamdictantcinduobus scrijitoribus diversas epistolas.diversis personis mitteiidas"); others told him "aliquando tribus, aliquando quatuor scriptoribus similiter dictaverat". LIB-ed l.14603-14607 (Fiorilli): "sapeva appena leggere e meno ancora scrivere; sí che le mirabili sue lettere ... furono da lei dettate ai suoi discepoli". **Documented** (dictation: Raymond saw it); that she learned to write late is told as a wonder in Scudder's chronology (LET-ed l.1353-1354, "she learns miraculously to write") and Fiorilli's note (LIB-ed l.14630-14634, taught "in un'estasi", October 1377): **Leave out** of the story.
- **E3. The circle.** LET-ed l.1308-1310 (1370-1374): "A group of disciples gathers around her. Her correspondence gradually becomes extensive, and she becomes known as a peacemaker." RAY l.205358-205364 (Raymond, eyewitness): "Vidi ego quandoque mille vel eo amplius utriusque sexus homines" (OCR "nulle vel en amplius") come down from the hills around Siena to see and hear her, then run weeping to the confessors, "quorum unus ego fui". RAY l.205372-205377: Gregory XI, gladdened, granted Raymond and two companions papal letters to absolve all who came to her. BARD l.214581, Barduccio calls her "communis nostrae Matris" (our common mother) and "mater millenarum animarum" (mother of thousands of souls; l.214593-214595). LET-ed l.1373-1374: in Rome, "a large group of disciples, her 'Famiglia,' who live together, subsisting on alms". RAY l.210438: Stefano and Barduccio are named as her scribes ("una cum praedicto Stephano et Barduccio scriptoribus"). **Documented** (the circle and its name for her: Raymond and Barduccio); "a thousand or more" is Raymond's own count (**Inferential-Thin** as a number: credit it to him or leave it).
- **E3a. Suspicion.** LET-ed l.1311-1315 (1374): "her ecstasies and unusual mode of life excite criticism and suspicion. In May, 1374, she visits Florence, perhaps summoned thither to answer charges made against her by certain in the Order." **Inferential-Thin** (Scudder's "perhaps"). Card only.

### E4-E6. Letters to the pope, and Florence's war

- **E4. Her first letter to Gregory (1375).** LET-ed l.4615-4616: "This present letter must date from 1375, for the rebellion of the Tuscan cities was gathering when she wrote" (OCR "I375> ^or tne rebellion"). LET l.4828-4832 (OWN): "Press on, and fulfil with true zeal and holy what you have begun with a holy resolve, concerning your return, and the holy and sweet crusade. And delay no longer". LET l.4855-4857: "I have been at Pisa and at Lucca, up to now, influencing them as much as I can not to make a league with the decaying members that are rebelling against you". **Documented** (her words). Note: in her own words Gregory had already "begun" the resolve to return. This is evidence for the dispute (D1).
- **E5. Reform, the return, a crusade.** LET l.5166-5168 (OWN, to Gregory): "Do you uproot in the garden of Holy Church the malodorous flowers, full of impurity and avarice, swollen with pride: that is, the bad priests and rulers who poison and rot that garden." LET l.5210-5212: "unless you accomplished the other two things which precede the completion of the other: that is, your return to Rome and uplifting of the standard of the most holy Cross." LET-ed l.5104-5106: "Catherine in this letter presents explicitly her threefold policy: reform of the Church, return to Rome, the initiation of a Crusade." In a letter to Gregory XI (heading at LET l.4889), on the pope's governors: "rulers whom you know yourself to be incarnate demons" (LET l.5038-5039). **Documented** (her words).
- **E6. "Come, come, come."** LET l.5220-5222 (OWN, to Gregory, the letter Raymond carried: "hear and hark that which Fra Raimondo will say to you", LET l.5256-5258): "Respond to the Holy Spirit who calls you. I tell you, Come, come, come, and do not wait for time, since time does not wait for you." LET l.5232-5239: "Be manly in my sight, and not timorous. Answer God, who calls you to hold and possess the seat of the glorious Shepherd St. Peter". RAY l.214054-214056 (Raymond): Florence first arranged that he, Raymond, go to the pope on Catherine's behalf to soften the pope's anger. Date: before her own journey, in 1376 (**Inferential-Thin**: from the letter's mention of Raymond's errand and of "the circumstance which has occurred at Bologna", LET l.5252-5253, with Scudder's 1376 Bologna revolt, LET-ed l.1212-1213). **Documented** (her words).

### E7-E10. Avignon, 1376

- **E7. Florence sends her.** RAY l.214057-214064 (Raymond): Florence had her come near the city; its Priors came out to her and begged her "quod personaliter accederet Avinionem ... ut tractaret pacem inter ipsum et eos" (that she go in person to Avignon to make peace between the pope and them); she came to Avignon, "ubi me reperit". LET-ed l.1337-1339: "In May, at the request of the Florentines, she goes to Florence. Sent as their representative to Avignon, she reaches that city on June 18th." LET l.6710-6712 (heading): "TO THE EIGHT OF WAR CHOSEN BY THE COMMUNE OF FLORENCE, AT WHOSE INSTANCE THE SAINT WENT TO POPE GREGORY XI". **Documented** (Florence sent her); the day, 18 June, is Scudder's.
- **E8. The audience: the stench of the court, and the pope's silence (the opening).** RAY §152, l.200969-200993, confirmed by MIRBT l.13874-13891 (CONTEMP, Raymond as interpreter): "recolo me aliquoties fuisse interpretem inter felicis memoriae Dominum Gregorium undecimum, summum Pontificem, et hanc sacram virginem ... ipsa enim non intelligebat Latinum sermonem, et summus Pontifex non noverat Italicum idioma. Dumque me interpretante loquerentur ad invicem, sacra virgo conquesta est, quod in Romana curia, ubi deberet paradisus esse caelicarum virtutum, inveniebat foetorem infernalium vitiorum. Quae dum Pontifex percepisset, quaesivit a me, quantum tempus esset quod ad curiam pervenisset: et cum intellexisset quod essent admodum pauci dies, respondit: Quomodo infra paucos dies potuisti curiae Romanae mores investigare? Tunc illa, inclinationem corporis et abjectionem commutans in quamdam subito quodammodo majestatem, ut etiam corporeis oculis tunc percepi, et erigens in altum, in haec verba prorupit: Ad honorem omnipotentis Dei audeo dicere, quod plus percepi foetorem peccatorum qui [Mirbt: quae] in Romana curia committuntur, existens in civitate mea unde sum nata, quam percipiant ipsi qui ea commiserunt et committunt quotidie. Ad haec Pontifex subticuit: sed ego stupefactus conferebam in corde meo, et signanter notabam, quanta cum auctoritate proferebantur verba haec coram tanto Pontifice." Raymond sets the scene at the papal court ("curia"), which was then at Avignon (S3); he says "aliquoties", that he interpreted several times. **Documented** as Raymond's account (he was there); the words are his Latin of her Tuscan, given through his own interpreting.
- **E9. The pope puts the peace in her hands; Florence fails her.** LET l.6782-6791 (OWN, to the Eight of War, "Given in Avignon, the 28th day of June, 1376", l.6802): "I have talked to the Holy Father. He heard me graciously ... at the end of our talk he said that if your case were as I presented it to him, he was ready to receive you as sons, and to do what seemed best to me." LET l.6796-6797: "But you, with your taxes and frivolities, are spoiling all that is sown." RAY §420, l.214067-214072 (Raymond, interpreting): "Testisque sum coram Deo et hominibus, quod benignus ille Pontifex, me audiente et interpretante, posuit pacem in manibus virginis, dicens: Ut clare videas quod ego volo pacem, ego pono eam simpliciter in manibus tuis; tamen habeas tibi recommissum honorem Ecclesiae." RAY §421, l.214090-214100: Florence had promised to send envoys bound to do nothing except as she said; they sent them very late, and meanwhile the pope told her, "Crede mihi, Catherina, ipsi deceperunt et decipient te: ipsi non mittent; et si mittant, talis erit missio, quod nihil valebit." RAY l.214108-214111: when they came, the envoys answered "quod nullum mandatum habuerant de conferendo cum ipsa, nec de faciendo ea quae ipsa diceret eis". LET l.6859-6862 (OWN, to Buonaccorso di Lapo): "You went on in your own ways. And I never had a chance to confer with them". LET-ed l.1227: "Catherine, sent to Avignon, fails to procure peace." **Documented** (her letter and Raymond agree: the pope gave her the terms, Florence's envoys would not deal with her, the peace failed).
- **E10. The poison letter; Gregory resolved and announced.** LET-ed l.6939-6948: the pope "had received an anonymous epistle ... warning him of poison should he venture to return to Rome." LET l.7062-7065 (OWN): "men finding a lie in you, who hold the Seat of Truth. For since you have decided on your return and announced it, the scandal and bewilderment and disturbance in men's hearts would be too great if they found that it did not happen." LET l.7019-7023: "the letter seems to me a forgery. Nor does he who wrote it understand his trade very well. He ought to put himself to school". LET l.7136-7137: "I beg of you, on behalf of Christ crucified, that you be not a timorous child, but manly. Open your mouth, and swallow down the bitter for the sweet." LET l.6436-6444, 6448-6449 (OWN, the letter on arrival): counsellors "want to alarm you, so as to prevent your return, saying, 'You will die'"; "Up, father, like a man!" **Documented** (her words). Note again: "you have decided on your return and announced it" (D1).
- **E11. Gregory leaves; she goes home.** LET-ed l.7167-7170: "In September, 1376 Gregory actually started for Rome. Her mission being ended, Catherine set forth on her homeward journey on the same day as the Pope, though by a different route." LET-ed l.1345: "She leaves for home on September 13th". MANSI l.53389-53393: he had twenty-one galleys made ready on the Rhône, "alioque properare se fingens" (pretending to hurry elsewhere), went first to Genoa, then Corneto, and by land to Rome. Arrival: LET-ed l.1228-1229: "reaches Rome, after a difficult journey, on January 17th, 1377"; MANSI l.53392-53393: "Idibus Januarii Romam pervenit; anno 1376" (13 January, "1376"); DUR l.98786-98788, citing Platina: "anno Domini 1376. Idib. Ianuarij". **Documented** that he left in September 1376 and reached Rome the next January; the day and year differ (D4).

### E12-E14. Florence again, the Dialogue, the schism

- **E12. Sent to Florence; a sword drawn on her (1378).** RAY §423, l.214194-214200 (Raymond): the pope told him, "Mihi scriptum est, quod si Catharina de Senis ibit Florentiam, ego habebo pacem"; he would not send Raymond, "quia ipsi male te tractarent: sed ei, tum quia mulier est, tum etiam quia reverentiam habent ad eam, credo quod nihil mali facient". RAY §332, l.209831-209847: at Florence, then rebel, she suffered many unjust persecutions, "adeo ut cum evaginato gladio quidam satelles diaboli venerit furibundus ad occidendum eam, licet sola Dei virtute prohibitus fuerit"; she would not leave "quousque defuncto Gregorio, Urbanus VI successor ejus, pacem fecit cum Florentinis". LET-ed l.1360-1363: "She succeeds in causing the Interdict to be respected, but almost loses her life in a popular tumult, and keenly regrets not having won the crown of martyrdom." LET-ed l.1231-1235: Gregory "dies in March" 1378; "In July, peace is made with Florence". **Documented** (Raymond and Scudder agree she was in danger of death at Florence); that God alone held the man back is Raymond's (keep in his voice, or tell only that the attack was stopped). Gregory's growing coolness to her: LET-ed l.1357-1358, "perhaps regretting his return, becomes alienated from Catherine" (**Inferential-Thin**, Scudder's "perhaps"; card).
- **E13. The Dialogue, dictated in Siena, 1378.** RAY §332, l.209850-209875 (Raymond): when peace was proclaimed she went home and gave herself to a book "quem superno Spiritu afflata dictavit in suo vulgari"; she had asked her scribes, who used to write her letters, to watch, and when she was carried out of her bodily senses "tunc quod dictaret, scriberent diligenter"; "virgo sacra in illa extasi posita, totum illum librum dictavit". RAY l.210782-210785: "quemdam dialogum inter unam animam, quae quatuor petitiones petebat a Domino; et ipsum Dominum respondentem". GUID, LIB-ed l.14391-14407 (CONTEMP, a secretary): "Anco la detta serva di Cristo fece una notabile cosa, cioè uno libro, el quale è di volume d'uno messale; e questo fece tutto essendo ella in astrazione, perduti tutti e' sentimenti, salvo che la lengua. Dio Padre parlava in liei, ed ella rispondeva e dimandava ... Ella diceva e uno scriveva: quando ser Barduccio, quando el detto donno Stefano, e quando Neri di Landoccio. Questo a udire pare che sia cosa da non crédare; ma a coloro, che lo scrissero e udîro, nollo pare cosí; e io so' uno di quegli." MAC, LIB-ed l.14448-14450: "quem ego pro parte scripsi, dum ore virgineo dictabat illum mirabili modo". Date: LIB l.14324-14327 (the Sienese copy's last line): "SOCTO GLI ANNI DOMINI MCCCLXXVIII DEL MESE D'OCTOBRE"; LIB-ed l.14373-14377 (Fiorilli): written in about three months, July to October 1378; LET-ed l.1365-1366, 2184 ("dictated in trance during the year 1378"). DIA-ed title page l.19-23: "COMPLETED IN THE YEAR OF OUR LORD 1370". **Documented** (dictated to her secretaries: three of them say so, Raymond agrees); that she dictated it in ecstasy is the witnesses' own account (keep it in their voice); the date is Contested only against Thorold's title page (D5): tell 1378.
- **E13a. What the book says.** LIB l.170-180 (OWN): she asked God four things: "La prima era per se medesima; la seconda per la reformazione della sancta Chiesa; la terza generale per tucto quanto il mondo, e singularmente per la pace dei cristiani e' quali sonno ribelli con molta irreverenzia e persecuzione alla sancta Chiesa. Nella quarta dimandava la divina providenzia". LIB l.1501-1504, 1526-1530 (God speaks): "del Verbo de l'unigenito mio Figliuolo avevo facto ponte"; "E però Io, volendo rimediare a tanti vostri mali, v'ho dato il ponte del mio Figliuolo, acciò che passando el fiume non annegaste. El quale fiume è il mare tempestoso di questa tenebrosa vita." DIA l.3200-3201 (Thorold): "I have made a Bridge of My Word, of My only-begotten Son". **Documented** (her book).
- **E14. The schism; Rome; her work for Urban.** LET-ed l.1235-1241: Urban's "harsh measures", and on 20 September 1378 the cardinals declare his election invalid and choose Robert of Geneva, Clement VII. RAY §333, l.209881-209887: Urban VI, "qui viderat eam in Avinione", had Raymond write that she should come to Rome. LET-ed l.1371-1381: "She reluctantly obeys, and takes up her abode in that city on November 28th ... She writes on his behalf to the Kings of France and Hungary, to Queen Giovanna of Naples, to the magistrates of Italian cities, to the Italian cardinals who have joined the Schism". **Documented** (Raymond and her letters to Urban, the cardinals and Queen Giovanna in LET); the list of addressees is Scudder's.
- **E15. Her death, 1380.** BARD l.214951-214958 (CONTEMP, an eyewitness, in the Bollandists' Latin of a printed Italian): she asked mercy "in virtute sanguinis", "ac dein pluries exclamavit, Sanguis, sanguis. Denique Salvatoris exemplo dixit, Pater, in manus tuas commendo animam et spiritum meum: atque ita suaviter, facie prorsus angelica renidens, inclinato capite emisit spiritum. Transitus ejus accidit die Dominica sub horam sextam". BARD l.214966-214969: the tomb is visited in Rome. LET-ed l.1394-1395: 30 April 1380, "in the presence of her disciples". LET l.12922-12923 (OWN, to Raymond, early 1380): "I do not know what the Divine Goodness will do with me, whether It will have me remain here, or will call me to Itself." **Documented** (Barduccio was there); "her face shining like an angel's" is Barduccio's (keep in his voice or leave out). Damaged OCR, and the Latin is itself a translation: no other held print. Use as paraphrase only.

## What came of her (impact, heaviest first)

- **I1. The return of the popes, and the argument over her part in it.** See D1-D3. What later witnesses carried on was the claim that she brought the pope home (Raymond, Duranti, Scudder, Fiorilli), against other causes (Gobelinus, Platina). **Contested.**
- **I2. The Dialogue copied, put into Latin, printed and translated.** MAC, LIB-ed l.14467-14472: Maconi gave Caffarini "dialogum quem sancta mater Catharina composuit, licet in vulgari, sed ego latinizavi". GUID, LIB-ed l.14407-14412: Guidini made his own Latin of it "per utilitá del prossimo". RAY l.193196-193198 (Raymond): its style is so high that a Latin hardly matches it, "prout in praesentiarum experior ego ipse, qui transferre in Latinum ipsum satago" (OCR "pr.usentianira ... traitsferre in Laliniiin i|suin"). GUID, LIB-ed l.14414-14422: a French bishop who had seen her at Avignon took a copy home to preach its teaching there. LIB-ed l.14503-14511: many manuscript copies; the first printing held to be 1472, eight printings to 1496. GARD l.488-504: "in 1519, by an English rendering by Brother Dane James of the Saint's mystical treatise, the Dialogo: 'Here begynneth the Orcharde of Syon'", made "from Friar Raymond's Latin version of the latter, first printed at Brescia in 1496". **Documented** (each witness for his own act); the printing dates are Fiorilli's and Gardner's (editors).
- **I3. Her circle carried her on.** LIB-ed l.14436-14438: Stefano Maconi took the Carthusian habit on her advice and became prior of the Pavia charterhouse and general of the order. LIB-ed l.14462-14465: Tommaso Caffarini "ebbe la parte maggiore nel processo della canonizzazione fatto a Venezia". RAY-ed l.192580-192582: Raymond, made master general of his order straight after her death, wrote her Life in the tenth year after it. GARD l.481-488: Wynkyn de Worde printed an English Life from Raymond's about 1493. **Documented** (editors' notes on her circle; Raymond's office is the Bollandists').
- **I4. Canonized.** RAY-ed l.192563-192566 (the Bollandists quote the Roman Martyrology): "Romae S. Catharinae, Virginis Senensis, Ordinis S. Dominici, vita et miraculis clarae, quam Pius II in Sanctarum Virginum numerum adscripsit" (OCR "quam Pius II in Sanctaruni A'ir^iiium numeru adscripsit"). COVEL l.51460-51461: "about eighty years after was Canonized by P. Pius the second". **Widely Accepted** (two late witnesses; no bull held; no year in the held lines).
- **I5. A measure for later mystics.** TER l.686-696: Father Rodrigo Alvarez, 22 February 1582, on Teresa's manuscript: "Whosoever has read the doctrine of the Saints, such as the books of St. Gertrude, St. Catharine of Siena, or St. Bridget of Sweden, and other saints and spiritual writers, will clearly understand that the spirit of Mother Tireza (sic) of Jesus is true". **Documented** (his note). Card; a link to `teresa-of-avila` (this batch), not a sibling.
- **I6. A modern historian's verdict.** LEA l.29390-29392: "her constant theme is the corruption of every rank in the hierarchy and the immediate necessity for reform." LEA l.29419-29420: "All these warnings fell upon deaf ears". **Documented** as Lea's view (editor). Card.

**What she defended (marked for the outline):** the pope's place in Rome; peace between Florence and the pope; the reform of the clergy; and, after 1378, Urban VI as the true pope against the cardinals' second election (E4-E6, E10, E14).

## Where sources disagree or come late

- **D1. How far she moved the pope.** For her: RAY §422, l.214144-214146 (Raymond): "Demum quia Vicarius Jesu Christi deliberavit tunc, ipsa eum inducente, venire ad propriam Sedem Romanam, sicut et fecit" (OCR "ipsa eum indiicente"): the pope decided to come to his own see at Rome, "she leading him to it". LET-ed l.1228: "Gregory, swayed by her representations, returns to Italy"; LET-ed l.1343-1344: "she devotes all her efforts to persuading the Pope to return to Rome, and triumphing over all obstacles, succeeds." DUR l.98793-98796 (1628): the matter is clear from Platina "& litterae S. Catharinae Senensis ad eundem Pontificem scriptae, quibus totum negotium cum eo de reditu in Italiam peregit" (her letters to the pope, in which she carried through with him the whole business of the return). LIB-ed l.14342-14344 (Fiorilli): "avvenimento al quale santa Caterina aveva molto contribuito". Against, or beside it: her own letters say the resolve was already his before she came: "what you have begun with a holy resolve, concerning your return" (1375, LET l.4830-4831) and "since you have decided on your return and announced it" (at Avignon, LET l.7063-7064). **Contested.** The story tells Raymond's claim as his, her letters' own words, and the other causes (D2, D3).
- **D2. Bridget's revelations.** MANSI l.53395-53396: "Hujus transmigrationis causam alii aliam referunt" (some give one cause for this move, others another). MANSI l.53404-53409: Gobelinus says it happened "instinctu et revelatione Dei, quae sanctae Brigittae tunc adhuc Romae degenti aliquoties obtigerat, perque Alphonsum quemdam eremitam illi secundo insinuata erat" (by a revelation of God that had come several times to St Bridget, then still living at Rome, and was passed to him a second time through a hermit, Alphonso). DUR l.98778-98786: Duranti notes a revelation that the pope should come without delay, to be in Rome by March or April. **Contested** (Gobelinus's account, a generation later).
- **D3. A bishop's retort.** MANSI l.53397-53403: "Platina scribit, quod quidam episcopus familiariter a pontifice reprehensus ob id quod in episcopali sede non resideret, infracto et constanti animo responderit: Et tu qui universalis ecclesiae omniumque episcoporum caput es, cur ad propriam ecclesiam non redis? eaque responsione motum pontificem reditum maturasse". (A bishop the pope rebuked for not living in his see answered: "And you, head of the whole church and of all bishops, why do you not go back to your own church?" The answer moved the pope to hasten his return.) **Contested** (Platina, about a century later, as the notice reports him).
- **D4. The day and year he reached Rome.** 17 January 1377 (LET-ed l.1228-1229); the Ides of January, 13 January, "1376" (MANSI l.53392-53393; Platina in DUR l.98786-98788). The year may be counted from a different new year, but no held line says so. Tell "the following January"; the card gives both.
- **D5. When the Dialogue was finished.** October 1378 (the Sienese copy's colophon, LIB l.14324-14327; Fiorilli, July to October 1378, LIB-ed l.14373-14377; Scudder, 1378, LET-ed l.2184); 1370 (Thorold's title page, DIA-ed l.19-23, against the copies). Tell 1378; the card names Thorold's date.
- **D6. Late or single-witness details, kept off the story:** the twenty-five children and the birth day (Scudder alone); the invisible stigmata at Pisa, 1375 (LET-ed l.1323-1325; contested on the WHO card); that she learned to write by a wonder (E2); the Florence summons of 1374 (E3a); the canonization year (none held).
- **D7. The tree's documented story** (CEN, `documentedStories`, "Be a Man, Father, Arise!") was written "from reference summaries; primary text not yet read". Two of its claims are not held: "nearly four hundred" letters survive (no held line gives a count; Scudder's is a selection) and the needle pricked into her foot (not checked in RAY; not used). It is not a `longDescription` and not a source.
