## Language and the originals

- **The canons** were written in Greek. The Greek is held in one print only, PED (1886), as OCR with errors (e.g. PED 49723 "σίγματα" for στίγματα; 49719 "ἀνηχέστους" for ἀνηκέστους). The English of ANF6 (Hawkins, from Gallandi; ANF6 25730–25731) confirms every clause used. Quote 1 is rendered from PED, checked clause by clause against ANF6.
- **The letter of the four bishops, the account kept with it, and Peter's letter to Alexandria** survive only in a Latin translation first printed by Maffei (ANF6 16704–16709, 25803–25805; BELL 3144–3150). That Latin is **not held** (Routh, *Reliquiae Sacrae* vol. IV, and PG 10 and 18 are not in the Library). Held only in English (ANF6). Hefele says the Latin is translated from Greek and "in many places the text is so corrupt as to be perfectly unintelligible" (HEF 21794–21798). No quote is taken from these.
- **Epiphanius**: Greek held in two prints, EPI (Oehler 1860) and EPI-PG (PG 42), both OCR; they agree on Quote 2 (EPI 43852–43853; EPI-PG 13795–13798).
- **Eusebius, Books VII–IX**: Greek held (EUS-G), OCR with errors (EUS-G 17559 "διαξιμίνου" for Μαξιμίνου; 17557 "Blov" for βίου). McGiffert's English (EUS 47993–47998) confirms Quote 3 clause by clause. Hawkins quotes the Greek of its first words, "θεῖον ἐπισκόπων χρῆμα, βίου τε καὶ ἀρετῆς ἕνεκα καὶ τῆς τῶν ἱερῶν λόγων συνασκήσεως" (ANF6 25737–25744), a second print of that part only.
- **Athanasius, Socrates, Sozomen, Theodoret** are used in English only; their quotes used on the tree are barred, and nothing here is quoted from them.

## Setting

- **S1. Bishop from about 300, after Theonas.** Eusebius (contemporary; he wrote this in the 310s–320s): "After Theonas had held the office for nineteen years, Peter received the episcopate in Alexandria, and was very eminent among them for twelve entire years. Of these he governed the church less than three years before the persecution, and for the remainder of his life he subjected himself to a more rigid discipline and cared in no secret manner for the general interest of the churches." (EUS 43214–43215, 43261–43266; Greek EUS-G 13031–13037, "τῆς κοινῆς τῶν ἐκκλησιῶν ὠφελείας οὐχ ἀφανῶς ἐπεμέλετο"). **Documented.** McGiffert (editor) works the accession out to the spring of 300 (EUS 43224–43229). **Widely Accepted.**
- **S2. What the bishop of Alexandria governed.** Epiphanius (later, about 70 years): "it is the custom that the archbishop in Alexandria has the church government of all Egypt and the Thebaid, Mareotis, Libya, Ammoniaca and the Pentapolis" (EPI 43712–43716, Greek: "ἔϑος ἐστὶ, τὸν ἐν τῇ Ἀλεξανδρείᾳ ἀρχιεπίσκοπον πάσης τε Αἰγύπτου καὶ Θηβαΐδος ... ἔχειν τὴν ἐκκλησιαστικὴν διοίκησιν"). **Documented** as Epiphanius's statement of custom. Plain words: "the bishop of Alexandria led the churches of all Egypt and Libya".
- **S3. The Great Persecution.** McGiffert (editor): "Diocletian's edict decreeing the demolition of the churches was published in February, 303" (EUS 43273–43274). Eusebius: Book VIII begins "the persecution in our day" (EUS 43307–43312). **Widely Accepted.** Plain words: "the Great Persecution", "the last and worst persecution of the Roman emperors" only if the writer needs a gloss; "persecution" is not glossed.
- **S4. Peter was not documented as head of the catechetical school.** Corpus-map note: "He is not documented as head of the catechetical school ... The school-headship tradition descends from Philip of Side, which is not vendored here" (`cic/corpus-map/alexandria-catechetical.yaml` 7429–7436). Robertson (editor): Peter was "expressly correcting distinctive parts of the system of his spiritual ancestor", Origen (ATH 7897–7899); McGiffert (editor): "in the main an Origenist, but ... departed in some important respects", "especially on the subject of anthropology" (EUS 43255–43259). Fragment VI, "From his demonstration that the soul was not pre-existent to the body" (ANF6 28088–28089; own, via a 7th–8th c. florilegium). **Documented** (the fragment); the school headship **not held**.
- **S5. Family, birth, schooling.** No early held source gives them. Hawkins's date "a.d. 260" is marked "This first date is conjectural" (ANF6 25673–25674). **Not held** (honest limit).

## Events

### E. The canons of 306

- **E1. The date from his own words.** Canon 1 opens: "But since the fourth passover of the persecution has arrived" (ANF6 26607–26608; PED 49718, "Ἐπεὶ τοίνυν τέταρτον ἤδη Πάσχα ἐπικατείληφε τὸν διωγμόν"). **Own.** "Passover" (Πάσχα) is Easter. McGiffert (editor): "drawn up by Peter in 306 (see the opening sentence of the first canon)" (EUS 43238–43240); Hawkins (editor): "in the fourth year of the persecution, a.d. 306" (ANF6 25777–25779). Year **Widely Accepted**. Nikodemos (editor; his book held in its 1886 printing) dates them "Ἐν δὲ τῷ 304 ἔτει" (PED 49702–49703): **sources differ** (X5).
- **E2. Where they stand.** Title: "The Canons of the Blessed Peter, Archbishop of Alexandria, as They are Given in His Sermon on Penitence" (ANF6 26599–26601). **Documented.** Plain words: "rules he set out in a sermon on repentance".
- **E3. Canon 1: the tortured who gave way.** "in the case of those who have been apprehended and thrown into prison, and who have sustained torments not to be borne, and stripes intolerable, and many other dreadful afflictions, and afterwards have been betrayed by the frailty of the flesh ... yet because they contended sorely and resisted long; for they did not come to this of their own will, but were betrayed by the frailty of the flesh; for they show in their bodies the marks of Jesus, and some are now, for the third year, bewailing their fault: it is sufficient, I say, that from the time of their submissive approach, other forty days should be enjoined upon them" (ANF6 26608–26624). Greek of the core (PED 49722–49723): "οὐ γὰρ κατὰ προαίρεσιν ἐν τούτῳ ἐληλύθασιν, ἀλλὰ καταπροδοθέντες ὑπὸ τῆς ἀσθενείας τῆς σαρκὸς, ἐπειδὴ καὶ τὰ σ[τ]ίγματα τοῦ Ἰησοῦ ἐνδείκνυνται ἐν τοῖς σώμασιν αὐτῶν". **Own; Documented.** Zonaras (12th c. commentator) glosses the marks: "the scars of the wounds which, in behalf of Christ, they have endured" (ANF6 26661–26663). Balsamon (12th c.): "those who after many torments have sacrificed to the gods" (ANF6 26636–26638).
- **E4. Canon 2: prison without torture.** "a year in addition to the foregoing time will suffice" (ANF6 26678–26684). **Own; Documented.**
- **E5. Canon 3: those who fell from fear alone.** "those who have suffered none of these things ... but of their own accord have gone over to wickedness, being betrayed by fear and cowardice" are set the parable of the unfruitful fig tree (ANF6 26724–26738). **Own; Documented.** How long is read differently: Balsamon "three years' exclusion" (ANF6 26739–26741); Zonaras "after the fourth year" (ANF6 26751–26752); Johnson's summary in NPNF14 "four years are added to their former penance" (NPNF14 43178–43180). **Contested** (X6).
- **E6. Canon 4: the unrepentant.** "Let no fruit grow on thee henceforward for ever" (ANF6 26759–26766). **Own.**
- **E7. Canon 5: the tricksters.** Those who "mocked the snares of their enemies, either passing by the altars, or giving a writing, or sending heathen to do sacrifice instead of themselves ... let a penalty of six months' penance be imposed upon them" (ANF6 26807–26820). **Own; Documented.** Plain words: walked past the altar as if they had sacrificed, handed in a written statement, or sent a pagan in their place.
- **E8. Canons 6–7: slaves and masters.** Christian slaves sent by masters to sacrifice, "being threatened by them": one year (ANF6 26893–26898). "But the freemen shall be tried by penance for three years, both for their dissimulation, and for having compelled their fellow-servants to offer sacrifice" (ANF6 26928–26930); "They have drawn their fellow-servants to idolatry who would have been able to escape" (ANF6 26942–26944). **Own; Documented.**
- **E9. Canons 8–9: those who came back, and those who rushed forward.** Those who fell and then confessed again are to be received "with joy and exultation of heart" (ANF6 26969–26975). Those who "leap forth upon a contest" unbidden are blamed, yet "with them also we must communicate" (ANF6 27039–27044); Christ "would have us pass from place to place ... 'flee ye into another'" (ANF6 27066–27074). **Own; Documented.**
- **E10. Canon 10: clergy who fell do not return to office.** "it is not right either that those of the clergy who have deserted of their own accord, and have lapsed, and taken up the contest afresh, should remain any longer in their sacred office" (ANF6 27162–27165); "For communion is sufficient for them" (ANF6 27192–27193). **Own; Documented.**
- **E11. Canons 12–13: those who paid or fled are not blamed.** "Against those who have given money that they might be entirely undisturbed by evil, an accusation cannot be brought" (ANF6 27483–27485). "neither is it lawful to accuse those who have left all, and have retired for the safety of their life" (ANF6 27536–27538); "these men that have withdrawn themselves are not at all to be blamed" (ANF6 27602–27603). **Own; Documented.**
- **E12. Canon 14: those forced, hands burned.** Those who "have bravely borne the burning of their hands that against their will had been put to the profane sacrifice, as from their prison the thrice-blessed martyrs have written to me respecting those in Libya" are counted among the confessors (ANF6 27634–27646). **Own; Documented.** Shows he was writing to, and hearing from, imprisoned martyrs.
- **E13. Canon 15** (fasting on Wednesday and Friday; no kneeling on Sunday; ANF6 27695–27706). Not on the lapsed; not used. McGiffert counts fourteen canons (EUS 43238); ANF6 and PED print fifteen (PED 49708, "Οἱ ιε' Κανόνες"). **Sources differ** (X7).
- **E14. What the canons are, judged.** Hefele (editor, historian of the councils): "the penitential canons of this bishop present him in quite another light, and prove that he knew how to keep a wise middle course, and to proportion the penance to the sin" (HEF 22258–22262). Dupin, quoted by Coxe (editor): "Of all the canons of antiquity concerning the discipline of the lapsed, there are none more judicious or more equitable" (ANF6 28222–28224; card already holds it). **Editor.**

### M. The break with Melitius: the letters' side

- **M1. The four bishops' letter.** "Hesychius, Pachomius, Theodorus, and Phileas, to Meletius, our friend and fellow-minister in the Lord" (ANF6 16710–16711): they grieve at "the ordination carried through by thee in parishes having no manner of connection with thee" (ANF6 16723–16725); "it is not lawful for any bishop to celebrate ordinations in other parishes than his own" (ANF6 16732–16737); Melitius acted against "the honour of our great bishop and father, Peter, on whom we all depend" and "hast ventured on subverting all things at once" (ANF6 16750–16758); "thou oughtest to have waited for the judgment of the superior father" (ANF6 16786–16788). **Contemporary** (Latin translation, not held; English only). Eusebius names all four as martyrs with Peter but says nothing of the letter (EUS 44810–44811, 44333–44335). McGiffert (editor): "There seems to be nothing in the epistle to disprove its genuineness" (EUS 44329–44330). Bell (editor): Seeck called the documents forgeries, "but his arguments are quite unconvincing and their authenticity is now almost universally accepted" (BELL 3179–3181). **Documented** as a document; its Latin is corrupt (HEF 21796–21798).
- **M2. The account kept with it.** "After receiving and perusing this epistle, he neither wrote any reply nor repaired to them in the prison, nor went to the blessed Peter. But when all these bishops and presbyters and deacons had suffered martyrdom in the prison at Alexandria, he at once entered Alexandria" (ANF6 16822–16826). With Isidorus and "a certain Arius" he found "certain presbyters, then in hiding, to whom the blessed Peter had given power to act as parish-visitors", "suspended them for the time, and by his own authority ordained two persons in their place, namely, one in prison and another in the mines" (ANF6 16826–16844). **Contemporary or near** (anonymous; Latin not held). **Documented** as this account's claim. (The Arius named here: L4.)
- **M3. Peter's letter.** "Since I have found out that Meletius acts in no way for the common good,—for neither is he contented with the letter of the most holy bishops and martyrs,—but, invading my parish, hath assumed so much to himself as to endeavour to separate from my authority the priests, and those who had been entrusted with visiting the needy; and, giving proof of his desire for pre-eminence, has ordained in the prison several unto himself; now, take ye heed to this, and hold no communion with him, until I meet him in company with some wise and discreet men, and see what the designs are which he has thought upon. Fare ye well." (ANF6 27789–27804). **Own** (Latin translation, not held). **Documented.** "ordained in the prison several unto himself" is used on the tree (`melitius-of-lycopolis`); "hold no communion" is paraphrased by `melitian-schism`. Not quoted here.
- **M4. Where Peter was.** Bell (editor): Melitius "went down to Alexandria, where, finding that Peter was in hiding, he proceeded to excommunicate various priests and visitors. Peter thereupon wrote to his flock" (BELL 3205–3207). Hefele (editor): Peter "recommended from his retreat all the faithful not to communicate with Meletius" (HEF 21952–21954). The account itself says only that the presbyters Peter appointed were "in hiding" (ANF6 16837). **Dominant Modern Reconstruction** (that Peter himself was hiding).
- **M5. Athanasius: deposed by a council for many crimes.** "Peter was Bishop among us before the persecution, and during the course of it he suffered martyrdom. When Meletius, who held the title of bishop in Egypt, was convicted of many crimes, and among the rest of offering sacrifice to idols, Peter deposed him in a general council of the bishops. Whereupon Meletius did not appeal to another council ... but made a schism" (ATH 24776–24783). "they have been schismatics and enemies of the Church, not of a recent date, but from the times of the blessed Peter, Bishop and Martyr. They formed a conspiracy against Peter himself" (ATH 22463–22468). **Later** (Athanasius wrote decades after the split, as bishop from 328 and a lifelong opponent of the Melitians; Hefele dates one of the passages, the letter to the bishops of Egypt, to 356 or 361, HEF 22084–22096). **Documented** as Athanasius's charge; the sacrifice charge **Contested** (X1). "offering sacrifice to idols" is used on the tree; not quoted here.
- **M6. Socrates and Theodoret repeat it.** Socrates (5th c.): Melitius "in consequence of many other charges, and more especially because during the persecution he had denied the faith and sacrificed, was deposed" (SOC 2405–2409). Theodoret (5th c.): "he was convicted of certain crimes by the most holy Peter" and then "rebelled against the primacy of Alexandria" (THDT 6551–6556). Hefele: Socrates "agrees so well ... with what Athanasius says, that it might be supposed that Socrates had only copied Athanasius" (HEF 22012–22014). **Later (a century and more).**

### M. The break with Melitius: Epiphanius's side

- **M7. In prison together.** Melitius "in the time of the persecution was arrested together with Peter the holy bishop and martyr and the other martyrs" (EPI 43615–43619, Greek "Οὗτος ἐν καιρῷ τοῦ διωγμοῦ ἅμα Πέτρῳ τῷ ἁγίῳ ἐπισκόπῳ καὶ μάρτυρι ... συλληφϑεὶς"); he ranked "second to Peter" among the bishops of Egypt (EPI 43626–43629). Epiphanius says Melitius "made a schism, yet did not change the faith" (EPI 43613–43614). **Later** (Epiphanius wrote 374–377: EPI 9). **Documented** as his account.
- **M8. The quarrel over the lapsed.** Some who had sacrificed "came to the confessors and martyrs, to obtain mercy through repentance, some soldiers, some clergy" (EPI 43724–43728); some said those who had denied "ought not to be granted repentance, lest those still left ... turn aside", and these were "Melitius and Peleus and some others" (EPI 43728–43736); they said repentance might be given "after the persecution has ceased, after a sufficient time", and not so that each "be received in his own rank", but among the people, "not among the clergy" (EPI 43738–43744). Peter "being compassionate, and as father of all, begged and pleaded, saying, Let us receive them since they repent" (EPI 43747–43748, 43843–43848). **Later; Documented** as his account. Robertson (editor) calls this "The Meletian account of the matter" (ATH 1645–1649).
- **M9. The verdict line (Quote 2).** "Καὶ ἦν μὲν τοῦ Πέτρου ὁ λόγος ὑπὲρ ἐλέους καὶ φιλανθρωπίας, καὶ τοῦ Μελητίου καὶ τῶν ἅμα αὐτῷ ὑπὲρ ἀληϑείας καὶ ζήλου" (EPI 43852–43853; EPI-PG 13795–13798). Petavius's Latin: "Petri quidem oratio ad clementiam humanitatemque spectabat, Meletii vero ac fautorum eius veritatis ac divini ardoris patrocinium susceperat" (EPI 43918–43920). **Later; Documented** as his words. Not used on the tree.
- **M10. The cloak and the split.** Peter "made a curtain in the middle of the prison, spreading out a garment ... and proclaimed by a deacon, Whoever is of my mind, come over to me, and whoever holds the mind of Melitius, to Melitius" (EPI 43862–43868); "the majority went with Melitius ... very few with Peter" (EPI 43869–43872); from then each side prayed and held services apart (EPI 43873–43876). **Later; Documented** as his account. Told on the tree by `melitius-of-lycopolis` as its opening (see Sibling).
- **M11. After Peter: two churches.** Peter was martyred; Melitius was sent to the mines of Phaeno and ordained clergy and built churches on the way; "the successors of Peter, holding the old churches, wrote 'Catholic Church', and those of Melitius, 'Church of the Martyrs'" (EPI 43878–43897). **Later; Documented** as his account. "Church of the Martyrs" is used on the tree; not quoted here.

### The historians weigh the two sides

- **M12. Bell (1924, editor of the Melitian papyri).** Athanasius, "an uncompromising opponent of the Meletians, is of value chiefly for his chronological evidence; his statement ... that Meletius was deposed by Peter for many irregularities 'and for sacrificing' [to idols] need not be taken very seriously, for the accusation was one that came readily to controversialists on the morrow of a great persecution" (BELL 3153–3157). The letters "are, on the contrary, of fundamental importance, and Epiphanius, though he wrote later than Athanasius, had excellent authority, probably in part that of Egyptian Bishops banished to Palestine" (BELL 3157–3160). "it is easy to reconcile them if we suppose them to present different stages in the growth of the schism" (BELL 3161–3163). "The schism had no doctrinal origin ... probably in A.D. 305" (BELL 3167–3168). "Neither party proposed to exclude the lapsi permanently from communion, neither thought of re-admitting them unconditionally; it was merely a question of the interval to be allowed before re-admission and the status to be accorded after it" (BELL 3192–3195). **Editor.** Bell's view on the lapsed is told by `melitian-schism`.
- **M13. Hefele (1855, English 1872).** "since the discovery of original documents, this opinion is no longer tenable, and it must be acknowledged that S. Epiphanius was mistaken on the principal points" (HEF 22249–22251). "the original documents prove that, at the time of the commencement of the schism, neither Peter nor Meletius was in prison" (HEF 22253–22256). On the lapsed: "The tenth canon particularly forbids that deposed priests should be restored to their cures, and that anything but lay communion should be granted to them. Peter therefore here teaches exactly what S. Epiphanius supposes to be the opinion of Meletius, and what, according to him, Peter refused to admit" (HEF 22269–22293). On the sacrifice charge: neither the three documents, nor Sozomen, nor Epiphanius mention it, and Nicaea's mildness argues against it (HEF 22018–22036); yet Athanasius would not knowingly accuse falsely; "It is much more probable that such reports were really circulated" (HEF 22038–22046). Hefele thinks Epiphanius's Melitian countrymen at Eleutheropolis gave him a favourable account (HEF 22235–22244). **Editor.**
- **M14. Robertson (1892, editor of Athanasius).** Meletius "condemned by a synod under bishop Peter"; "The Meletian account of the matter (preserved by Epiphan.) was different from this ... taking the sterner view, in which most of the imprisoned clergy supported him"; "No doctrinal question, however, was involved. The alliance of Meletians and Arians belongs to a later date" (ATH 1637–1656). **Editor.**
- **M15. Coxe (ANF American editor).** Follows Neale: Meletius "in the time of persecution had lapsed, and sacrificed to idols"; "Of Peter's legitimate exercise of authority, and of the impurity and wickedness of Meletius before his invasion of Alexandria, there is no reason to doubt" (ANF6 28185–28208). **Editor**, openly partisan; not used.

### D. Death, 311

- **D1. Eusebius, Book VII.** "On this account he was beheaded in the ninth year of the persecution, and was adorned with the crown of martyrdom" (EUS 43266–43268; EUS-G 13037–13039, "ἐνάτῳ ἔτει τοῦ διωγμοῦ τὴν κεφαλὴν ἀποτμηϑεὶς"). **Contemporary; Documented.**
- **D2. Eusebius, Book IX (Quote 3).** "At about the same time Peter also, who presided most illustriously over the parishes in Alexandria, a divine example of a bishop on account of the excellence of his life and his study of the sacred Scriptures, being seized for no cause and quite unexpectedly, was, as if by command of Maximinus, immediately and without explanation, beheaded. With him also many other bishops of Egypt suffered the same fate." (EUS 47990–47999). Greek (EUS-G 17553–17560): "ἐξ οὐδεμιᾶς ἀνάρπαστος γεγονὼς αἰτίας, μηδεμιᾶς προλαβούσης προσδοκίας, ἀϑρόως οὕτως καὶ ἀλόγως, ὡς ἂν Μαξιμίνου προστάξαντος, τὴν κεφαλὴν ἀποτέμνεται, σὺν αὐτῷ δὲ καὶ τῶν κατ᾽ Αἴγυπτον [ἐπισκόπων πλείους]". **Contemporary; Documented.** "a divine example of a bishop" is on the card; not used on the tree; not chosen (three quotes at most).
- **D3. Eusebius, Book VIII.** "Of those who suffered death illustriously at Alexandria and throughout Egypt and Thebais, Peter, bishop of Alexandria, one of the most excellent teachers of the religion of Christ, should first be mentioned; and of the presbyters with him Faustus, Dius and Ammonius ... also Phileas, Hesychius, Pachymius and Theodorus" (EUS 44798–44811; EUS-G 15468–15473). **Contemporary; Documented.** So two of the four letter-writing bishops died in the same persecution; Eusebius's list does not say they died on the same day.
- **D4. The date.** McGiffert (editor): the ninth year runs "Feb. 311 to Feb. 312", and the death came "evidently after the publication of the toleration edict of Galerius, when the Christians were not looking for any further molestation" (EUS 43218–43224). Bell (editor): "The persecution was renewed by Maximinus very soon after Galerius's death, and one of the first victims was Bishop Peter, who was martyred on 25 Nov., 311" (BELL 3210–3212). Hawkins (editor): "his martyrdom falls in the year of our Lord 311—as the Egyptians reckon on the 29th day of the month Athyr, which answers to our 25th of November, as Lequien, after Renaudot, has observed" (ANF6 25767–25776). Year **Widely Accepted**; the day comes from the later Egyptian church's reckoning. The held synaxaria (set aside in the Library sweep) are the liturgical witnesses for the November day.
- **D5. Late legend.** *The Genuine Acts of Peter* (a Passion Latinised by Anastasius the Librarian, 9th c.; ANF6 25835–25855) give his last night, a vision of Christ in a torn garment about Arius (ANF6 26090–26116), prayer at the tomb of Mark (ANF6 26280–26333), and a soldier who "drew his sword and beheaded the pontiff" (ANF6 26416). McGiffert: "spurious and historically quite worthless" (EUS 43251–43253). **Late legend.** Nikodemos adds a heavenly voice at his beheading, "Peter the beginning of the apostles, and Peter the end of the martyrs" (PED 49700–49701). **Late legend.** Not used.

## Impact, heaviest first

- **I1. His rules became church law.** The Council in Trullo (692; NPNF14 1602) in its Canon 2: "we set our seal likewise upon all the other holy canons set forth by our holy and blessed Fathers ... Likewise too the Canons [i.e. the decretal letters] of Dionysius, formerly Archbishop of the great city of Alexandria; and of Peter, Archbishop of Alexandria and Martyr" (NPNF14 26731–26744). **Documented.** Hawkins: "They are to be met with in every collection of Canons", with "the notes of Joannes Zonaras and Theodorus Balsamon" (ANF6 25779–25784), "the 12th-c. Byzantine canonists' commentaries" (`cic/corpus-map/alexandria-catechetical.yaml` 7479–7480); the commentators' notes stand in ANF6 itself (e.g. 26634–26673). Nikodemos's Greek book of canon law (held in its 1886 printing) prints them with commentary and says that by the Trullan ratification they "take on, in a way, ecumenical force" (PED 49703–49706, "Οἰκουμενικὴν τρόπον τινὰ ἀναλαμβάνουσι δύναμιν"). **Documented.** Hawkins, after Renaudot: Oriental Christians "of every sect, make use of the testimony of these Canons" (ANF6 25787–25790). **Editor's report**; not used.
- **I2. The split outlived him and reached Nicaea.** Nicaea's letter to the church of Alexandria: Melitius should "remain in his own city but exercise no authority either to ordain or nominate for ordination ... but simply retain a nominal dignity"; those he ordained "should be admitted to communion" after "a more legitimate ordination" and rank below Alexander's clergy (SOC 3203–3220; THDT 6556–6560 for the same letter). **Documented.** "a nominal dignity" is used on the tree; not quoted. `council-of-nicaea-325` tells this in one sentence ("a leader who had ordained clergy of his own kept his title but could ordain no one"). Athanasius: "Five months however had not yet passed when ... the Meletians ... were again troubling the Churches" (ATH 24797–24806), and Eusebius of Nicomedia "buys the Meletians with large promises" against Athanasius (ATH 24808–24811). **Documented** as Athanasius's account. The Melitian church lasted: Bell, "the sect died out finally during the eighth century" (BELL 3452). **Dominant Modern Reconstruction.** Told by `melitian-schism`; a clause here.
- **I3. Remembered as bishop and martyr.** Eusebius (D2, D3). Athanasius, "the blessed Peter, Bishop and Martyr" (ATH 22465); "Meletius, when ejected by Peter the Bishop and Martyr, called his party no longer Christians, but Meletians" (ATH 41340–41341). Alexandria kept his day: Socrates, retelling Rufinus, speaks of "the day in which the memory of the martyr and bishop Peter was celebrated" (SOC 4007–4008; the boy-bishop story told there is doubted by Robertson, ATH 1582–1591). Athanasius's Life of Antony: "when at last the persecution ceased, and the blessed Bishop Peter had borne his testimony, Antony departed, and again withdrew to his cell" (ATH 32396–32399). **Documented.**
- **I4. A compiler's council.** Mansi heads an entry "Concilium Alexandrinum habitum circa annum Christi CCCVI", built from Athanasius's sentence (MANSI2 52234–52250). **Later compiler.** Not used.

## Links to other people

- **L1. `athanasius-of-alexandria`** (live text read). Athanasius, bishop of Alexandria from 328, is the main witness against Melitius (M5) and calls Peter "the blessed Peter, Bishop and Martyr" (I3). Robertson thinks Athanasius may have learned "possibly at first from the lips of Peter the bishop and martyr of 311" (ATH 1552–1554): **editor's guess**, not used. Kind: wrote of him; fought the Melitians his split left.
- **L2. `council-of-nicaea-325`** (live text read). Settled the Melitian split (I2). Kind: dealt with the split he left.
- **L3. `eusebius-of-caesarea`** (this batch, not yet researched; no outline in the folder). The contemporary witness (S1, D1–D3). Kind: wrote of him.
- **L4. `arius`** (live text read). Held sources do tie them, all late or doubted:
  - Sozomen (5th c.): Arius "upheld the innovations of Melitius. Eventually, however, he abandoned this latter opinion, and was ordained deacon by Peter, bishop of Alexandria, who afterwards cast him out of the church, because when Peter anathematized the zealots of Melitius and rejected their baptism, Arius assailed him" (SOC 27552–27563). Hartranft (editor): "No one else suggests an early connection of Arius with the Melitians" (SOC 27556–27557).
  - Yet the Maffei account (M2) does name "a certain Arius" among those who went to Melitius at Alexandria (ANF6 16828–16830). It does not say this is the later priest.
  - Epiphanius: it was Melitius who later reported Arius to Alexander (EPI 43986–43990). Bell accepts this (BELL 3217–3219); Hefele doubts it (HEF 22305–22314).
  - Robertson (editor) on the Sozomen and Epiphanius tales: "These tales deserve little credit: they are unsupported by Athanasius, and bear every trace of invention ex post facto" (ATH 1676–1685).
  - The *Genuine Acts* (late legend) give Peter a vision against Arius (ANF6 26090–26116).
  - **Contested.** Kind: ordained him deacon and cast him out, by Sozomen's account only.
- **L5. `melitius-of-lycopolis`** and **`melitian-schism`** (live texts read; see Sibling in the outline). Kind: the bishop he broke with; the church that broke away.

## Where the sources disagree

- **X1. Why Peter broke with Melitius.** The letters (M1–M3): ordaining in other bishops' churches and in Peter's own, while they were in prison or hiding. Athanasius and those who follow him (M5, M6): Peter's council deposed him for many crimes, among them sacrifice to idols. Epiphanius (M7–M10): both in prison; a quarrel over the lapsed, Peter for mercy, Melitius for strictness. Bell reads the letters and Epiphanius as stages of one quarrel and discounts the sacrifice charge (M12). Hefele holds the letters against Epiphanius, and says Peter's own canon 10 is the stricter line Epiphanius gives Melitius (M13). **Contested.** Credit each.
- **X2. Prison.** Epiphanius: both in prison together (M7). Hefele: the documents show neither was in prison when the split began (M13). Bell: they were set free soon after the quarrel began (BELL 3198–3200). **Contested.**
- **X3. Date of the split.** Robertson 301 (ATH 1638–1639); Hefele "about the year 304 or 305" (HEF 22104–22105); Bell "probably in A.D. 305" (BELL 3168); Coxe/Neale 301 or 306 (ANF6 28166–28168). **Contested.** Say "during the persecution".
- **X4. Who succeeded Peter.** Epiphanius: Alexander (EPI 43878–43881). Eusebius-based tradition, Socrates and Athanasius: Achillas, then Alexander (SOC 2067–2069; ATH 24786–24788). Hefele: Epiphanius is mistaken (HEF 22301–22303). Not needed in the story.
- **X5. Year of the canons.** 306 (McGiffert, Hawkins, from "the fourth Easter of the persecution"); 304 (Nikodemos). **Widely Accepted** for 306.
- **X6. How long for those who fell from fear.** Balsamon three years; Zonaras and Johnson four. Do not give a number.
- **X7. How many canons.** Fourteen (McGiffert) or fifteen (ANF6, Pedalion). Not needed.
- **X8. Spelling.** The census writes "Melitius" (`melitius-of-lycopolis`, `melitian-schism`); ANF6, Athanasius's translators, Hefele and the card write "Meletius"; Bell: "The correct form is undoubtedly Μελίτιος ... But the form Meletius, since it has everywhere established itself, is retained for convenience" (BELL 3230–3233). Use the census "Melitius".
