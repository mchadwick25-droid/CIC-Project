## Changed sentences

1. **"Early in the fourth century, the church in Caesarea, a city by the sea in Palestine, had a presbyter, or elder, named Pamphilus."** WITH CAUTION. Presbyter at Caesarea: SCH 24800–24801 (τῇ τοῦ κατὰ Καισάρειαν πρεσβείου τιμῇ … Πάμφιλος). Sea: EUS 46329–46331. "Early in the fourth century" is modern dating (card C12). It is safe because he died in the persecution of Diocletian's reign (S5), but no held text states it. The library is no longer placed in Caesarea, so the earlier Jerome-only caution falls away.
2. **"He gathered a library of the works of Origen and of other church writers."** SUPPORTED. SCH 5155–5156 (τῆς συναχθείσης αὐτῷ … τῶν τε Ὠριγένους καὶ τῶν ἄλλων ἐκκλησιαστικῶν συγγραφέων βιβλιοθήκης).
3. **"Eusebius was a presbyter, and then bishop of Caesarea."** SUPPORTED. This is the form the earlier check gave (NP4 19595–19598; NP2 2972–2973).
4. **"From libraries like this one, he began to write."** SUPPORTED. The library he names as his source is Aelia's (EUS 35438–35443; SCH 3959–3961). "Like this one" now covers it, and "the first" is left in his own mouth.
5. **"…he said."** (Q1 tag) SUPPORTED. EUS 6878–6879.
6. **"He meant the line of leaders in each church, from the apostles to his own day."** WITH CAUTION. Q1 (EUS 6878–6885): "those who governed and presided over the church in the most prominent local churches". Q3 (EUS 6962–6975): "if not of all, then of the most renowned of them, in the most noted churches". "In each church" claims more than he does. Fix: "in the leading churches".
7. **"He wrote, 'I am the first…'"** SUPPORTED. EUS 6935–6938. The loose "added" is gone.
8. **"In the reign of the emperor Diocletian, royal edicts ordered the churches pulled down."** SUPPORTED. EUS 43460–43489; SCH 13716–13720: in "the nineteenth year of the reign of Diocletian", royal edicts (βασιλικὰ γράμματα) ordered the churches levelled. This is the form the earlier check gave.
9. **"A later writer, Epiphanius, tells of a council at Tyre where Eusebius sat as a judge."** SUPPORTED. OEH 44476–44481 (Tyre; Eusebius named to judge); 44583–44584 (καθεζόμενον καὶ δικάζοντα).
10. **"A man named Potamon, who had lost an eye in the persecution, cried out against him."** SUPPORTED. OEH 44580–44583 (Ποτάμων; τὸν ὀφθαλμὸν … ἐν τῷ διωγμῷ … ἐτυφλώθη); 44586 (ἀπετείνατο φωνῇ μεγάλῃ Εὐσεβίῳ λέγων). "Against" is fair, because what he cried out was an accusation.
11. **"How did Eusebius get out of prison, unless he promised 'to do the forbidden thing, or did it?'"** WITH CAUTION. Words and "?" match Q39 and OEH 44596–44599. The wording is the problem. It is a free-standing direct question with no speaker, so the narrator asks Potamon's accusing question in his own voice. Fix: "He asked how Eusebius had got out of prison, unless he had promised 'to do the forbidden thing, or did it?'"
12. **"Eusebius, the story goes, broke up the court in anger."** SUPPORTED. Q39 final reading; OEH 44600 onward (he flared up, stood and broke up the court). "The story goes" is the narrator's hedge. It fits "Epiphanius names no source".
13. **"When peace came, the bishops met in a great council at Nicaea, before the emperor Constantine."** SUPPORTED. NP4 19561–19568; NP2 3046–3048.
14. **"They met chiefly over Arius."** SUPPORTED. NP2 2678–2684 (two causes, Arius and Easter); PHO 11794–11802.
15. **"He and those with him said that God existed before his Son."** SUPPORTED, from a partisan witness. NP3 6029–6032 (Theodoret I.4, Arius's own letter): "…condemned because they say that God had an existence prior to that of His Son"; NP3 6045–6046: "we say that the Son has a beginning, but that God is without beginning". It is Arius's own account of his party's view, so it is the best witness of what they said. The same sentence names "Eusebius, your brother bishop of Cæsarea" among them (D1(a)). The brief does not say so, and need not.
16. **"By his own account, the emperor approved it, but asked for one word to be added: the Son is 'of one substance' with the Father."** SUPPORTED. NP2 3046–3052; NP4 19568–19569. The attribution asked for is now in place.
17. **"Athanasius, who was hostile to him, wrote that Eusebius was 'at first an accomplice of the Arian heresy', but afterwards signed."** SUPPORTED. NP4 60746–60753 ("but afterwards, having signed at the Council of Nicæa…"). Both earlier fixes are made.

21. **"Even a writer against him admitted that 'no work of those older authors escaped his notice.'"** WITH CAUTION. MEN 20806–20826. In the source the line is followed at once by "Still, I say that he never reached any precision in doctrine". Leaving out his name is fair, and "against him" keeps the hostility. But "who, where" is not given, and the line now does the work of praise. Fix: "Antipater of Bostra, who wrote against him, admitted…".
22. **"Rufinus put the history into Latin and added two books. He began them: 'Up to here…'"** WITH CAUTION (the first sentence; the second is SUPPORTED). Fix: "put the history into Latin, shortened its last book, and added two of his own". The opening words are at SCH 26345–26355.
23. **"Theodoret wrote, 'I will begin my history where his ends.'"** Unchanged. SUPPORTED, NP3 5222–5224.

## Heard once through

- **Lost anywhere?** The earlier problems are fixed: "presbyter" and "successions" are glossed, Arius comes before "of one substance", and Potamon is named. Two places still trip a listener. The Tyre question (sentence 11) sounds like the narrator asking. "Even a writer against him" leaves the listener asking who.
- **Story or report?** It is now a story to the end: library, edict, prison, Tyre, Nicaea, his letter home, and the historians who took up his work. The Athanasius/Socrates pair is the only passage that reads as a report, and it is short.
- **What came of him?** Yes. The ending carries his reach: others built on his history, Theodoret began where he ended, and Rufinus carried it into Latin. It closes on Rufinus's "Up to here Eusebius has handed down to us…", which is a strong last line.
