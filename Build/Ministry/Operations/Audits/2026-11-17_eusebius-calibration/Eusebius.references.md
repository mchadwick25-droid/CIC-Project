## What is uncertain

- **His views on Arius's teaching.** Contested (D1). Arius counted him an ally; Athanasius, Jerome, Theodoret, Photius and the council of 787 call his views Arian; his own letter, Socrates and Gelasius say otherwise. The brief gives Athanasius and Socrates and does not settle it. Athanasius's words are a hostile description (Q38).
- **His conduct at Nicaea.** He held back, then signed (Socrates, Sozomen, his own letter). Athanasius says he "refused the day before"; what he refused is not stated (Q34). The copies of his letter differ: one paragraph is in Athanasius's copy and not in Socrates's (Q25, D2).
- **Whether he was in prison, and how he came out.** Contested (D4). The charge is Potamon's, in a story Epiphanius wrote about forty years after the council at Tyre, naming no source; the "forbidden thing" is not named. Athanasius reports a charge of sacrificing (Q35). His own held works say nothing of an imprisonment (N2).
- **His part at the council of Tyre.** Contested (D5). The brief says only "a council". His own account names no accused bishop and no verdict (N7).
- **Whether the *Life of Constantine* left things out on purpose.** Socrates and Photius judged so; his own stated plan was to write only of the emperor's religious life (D6).
- **"The first."** That he was the first to write the church's history is his own claim (Q2); "Father of Church History" is a modern editor's title (I11).
- **Pamphilus.** No held text has Eusebius call him his teacher (N3). The brief says "friend".
- **The council of 787.** Its reading of his letter came some 450 years later at a council hostile to him (Q66). The brief's "rejected his writings" follows "We reject the writings of this man".
- **Dates and labels.** "Fourth century", "the Great Persecution" and the year figures are modern (C1, C12). "About a hundred years" and "about four hundred and fifty years" rest on modern editors' dates.
- **Not told by the record.** His birth, family, age, the dates he became presbyter and bishop, and the date and manner of his death (N1); why he was in Egypt (N5).
