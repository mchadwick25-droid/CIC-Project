## What is uncertain

- **His views on Arius's teaching.** Contested (D1). Arius counted him an ally; Athanasius, Jerome, Theodoret, Photius and the council of 787 call his views Arian; his own letter, Socrates and Gelasius say otherwise. The brief gives Athanasius and Socrates and does not settle it. Athanasius's words are a hostile description (Q38). What Arius's side taught is given from Arius's own letter (Q32), written in his party's interest.
- **His conduct at Nicaea.** He held back, then signed (Socrates, Sozomen, his own letter). Athanasius says he "refused the day before"; what he refused is not stated (Q34). The copies of his letter differ: one paragraph is in Athanasius's copy and not in Socrates's (Q25, D2).
- **Whether he was in prison, and how he came out.** Contested (D4). The charge is Potamon's, in a story Epiphanius wrote about forty years after the council at Tyre, naming no source; the "forbidden thing" is not named. Athanasius reports a charge of sacrificing (Q35). His own held works say nothing of an imprisonment (N2).
- **His part at the council of Tyre.** Contested (D5). That he sat as a judge, and broke up the court, is Epiphanius's story only. His own account names no accused bishop and no verdict (N7).
- **Why Nicaea met.** The brief says "chiefly over Arius"; Socrates also names the dispute over the date of Easter.
- **Who brought in "of one substance".** That the emperor asked for it is Eusebius's own account (D2(e)); a modern editor finds it hard to square with Athanasius's (C5).
- **Whether the *Life of Constantine* left things out on purpose.** Not told in the brief. Socrates and Photius judged so; his own stated plan was to write only of the emperor's religious life (D6).
- **"The first."** That he was the first to write the church's history is his own claim (Q2), and the later historians begin from him (I1); Sozomen names earlier compilers of records. "Father of Church History" is a modern editor's title (I11).
- **Pamphilus.** No held text has Eusebius call him his teacher (N3). The brief says "friend". Eusebius does not say where the library stood; Jerome places it at Caesarea (Q41).
- **The edicts.** Eusebius dates them by Diocletian's reign and does not say who issued them; Socrates says Diocletian began the persecution (Q48).
- **"A writer against him."** Antipater of Bostra, in a book against the defence of Origen, read at the council of 787, which rejected Eusebius's writings. His concession is followed at once by "he never reached any precision in doctrine" (Q66).
- **Dates and labels.** "Fourth century" and the year figures are modern (C1, C12). "About a hundred years" rests on modern editors' dates.
- **Not told by the record.** His birth, family, age, the dates he became presbyter and bishop, and the date and manner of his death (N1); why he was in Egypt (N5).
