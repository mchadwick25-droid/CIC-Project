# R1 review: Library era 10, batch 1 (95 files as first vendored), 2026-11-15

Reviewer: one fresh Fable subagent, read and grep only. This is its report, filed by the Library thread. Line numbers
are those of the files as first vendored (checkpoint `fa868dd1`); the changes made in answer are in `R1_changes.md`.

**Verdict: not clear to proceed as it stands.** No rights row is wrong on its date, and no sign of a missed later
printing was found. Five things need fixing first: one owner notice copied short, one rule cited to a log that does
not hold it, two claims the files do not bear out, and four shelf rows whose role contradicts their own note.

## Blocking findings

1. **Cape Town Commitment: the owner's notice is copied short.** The header Rights line (and its echo in
   `REGISTRY.yaml`) ends the quotation at "...should be included." Line 14 of the file goes on: "For publishing rights
   in English or in translation, email communications@lausanne.org." The dropped sentence bears on reuse, and the
   owner-declared class rests on the owner's words as printed.
2. **"R12" is cited to a log that does not hold it.** The Search_Log and `CFT-Wants.md` cite "R1 to R12 ...
   Decision-Log.md". The rulings entry on `origin/claude/library-era10-rights-rulings` answers eleven questions, R1 to
   R11. Neither that entry, this branch's `Decision-Log.md`, nor `cic/texts/INTAKE.md` contains R12 or the no-year rule.
   Twenty files rest their date on it: Barth (English), Bultmann, Aglipay, Panama v1-3, Montevideo v1-2, Emhardt,
   Fundamentals v1-4, Niebuhr 1927, Riley, Scofield, Scopes, Tillich, Trumbull, God Hath Spoken. The rule needs
   recording where it is cited, or the citation needs to point to where it is.
3. **The Fundamentals: "first issued from 1910 to 1915" is not in the files.** It appears in all four Content notes,
   their registry notes and Search_Log caution 23. Torrey's preface (v1 lines 348-381) gives only 1909 and twelve
   volumes. The preface also says the edition leaves out "a very few" articles, which "gathers the essays" hides.
4. **Spinka: Tikhon's epistle is not printed without a gap.** The header says "printed in full"; caution 5 says
   "complete in English". The print has an elision mark ("nation. . . . Finally, the government which is"). "In full"
   is Spinka's own phrase, not what the print shows. The same epistle in Emhardt begins at line 1628 and can check it.
5. **Four shelf rows give `role: tradition` to a shelf where their own note says the text is the other side.**
   Niebuhr's Moral Man on `the-social-gospel` ("a critic's voice, written after that entry's window", and `assigned`);
   Fosdick on `fundamentalism-1910-1970s` ("the opposing side's voice"); Machen on
   `the-mainline-century-liberal-and-neo-orthodox` ("the opposing side's voice"); the Sergius letter row in Emhardt on
   `the-russian-new-martyrs-and-the-catacomb-church`, the shelf whose bishops' answers reject it. Acta v20 already
   shows the fix: Mortalium animos is split into a tradition row and a context row.

**Line numbers and wording**

## Rights rows read

95 of 95 headers read. Title page and verso read directly in about 45 files; the first 400 body lines of all 95 were
pattern-scanned for years, copyright lines, printing and edition words, ISBN and ©, and every hit was read. No Rights
line states a basis outside the rules, apart from blocking findings 1 and 2. No NC/ND license, lending-only scan or
vatican.va source appears in any header. For the eleven coordinator-call files, nothing in the scans names a later
printing.
