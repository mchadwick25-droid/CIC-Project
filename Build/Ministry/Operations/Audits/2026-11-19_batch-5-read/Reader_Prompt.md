# Does it meet the tree's needs? A reader's prompt

You are one of four fresh readers for the Church Family Tree (churchfamilytree.com). Read `cft-website/about.html` first, to learn what the tree is for. Then read `Build/Ministry/Operations/Audits/2026-11-19_batch-5-read/Pieces.md`: twelve short stories, each heard once as narration on a person's or council's page.

This is not a hunt for errors. Do not check facts against sources, and do not judge whether a piece is perfect. Your one question: **does each story successfully do what the Church Family Tree needs it to do, for your listener?** You are {PERSONA}.

## For each piece
- **One line: what you took away from one hearing.** Who this was, what they did, and why they matter.
- **A verdict:**
  - **Meets:** it does the tree's job well.
  - **Mostly:** it does the job, with one clear gap.
  - **Misses:** it doesn't do the job.
- **One sentence of why.**

## Then, across all twelve
1. What works, as a pattern. Name the two or three best pieces and say why.
2. What doesn't work, as a pattern. Name the weakest two or three and say why.
3. What your listener would want that the pieces don't give.
4. Do they sound like one collection with one voice, or like a template? Is the voice human, or does it read as generated?
5. Your top three changes that would most help the pieces serve the tree, for future batches. Each is one or two sentences.

{EXTRA}

Read nothing else in the repository: no `Build/` file other than these two, no audit or review file, and no Decision-Log. Write `Build/Ministry/Operations/Audits/2026-11-19_batch-5-read/{FILE}.md`. Plain, short sentences. Do not commit. Reply in under 150 words: your verdict counts and your top three changes.

This read is a check for drift, not a request for changes: nothing will be rewritten because of it. Say plainly whether these twelve hold to what the tree is for, as `about.html` sets it out.
