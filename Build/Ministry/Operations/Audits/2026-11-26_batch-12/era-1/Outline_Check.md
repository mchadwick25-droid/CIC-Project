# Outline check: Era 1, The Early Church Era

## My account of the heart (written before reading Outline.md)

- **Essence:** Between the fall of the Temple and Constantine's win at Rome, the churches spread through the cities of the Roman world and past it to the east, as a people with no legal standing, whom any governor or crowd could turn on and whom, twice, emperors set out to break across the whole empire (Decius 250, the edicts of 303); the age ends with an emperor's edict telling them they may be Christians again (311) and Constantine's victory (312).
- **To the church:** the Jerusalem church gone across the Jordan (Pella, Eusebius's report, wonder in his voice); local, sporadic persecution under Trajan's ruling (not hunted, punished if accused), crowds at Lyon and Vienne (177); bishops meeting in regional synods (Easter, c.190); Decius's demand that all sacrifice, the mass lapse and certificates, then the question of the fallen; Valerian's ban on assemblies and killing of clergy; Gallienus's peace and returned places; the long peace with big new churches; the Great Persecution (churches levelled, Scriptures burned, prisons full of clergy); Galerius's 311 edict.
- **In the world:** an empire under strain in the 200s: wars, Gothic raids, a captured emperor, famine and plague; Diocletian's four-ruler empire.
- **Where:** cities around the Mediterranean, thickest in Asia Minor, Syria/Antioch, Egypt/Alexandria, Rome, Roman Africa/Carthage; thin in Gaul and the Rhine; also east beyond Rome at Edessa and among the Parthians/Persians.
- **Questions left open:** who may return after giving way and who decides; what Christians owe an emperor who stops persecuting them (and what he may decide for them); how to speak of the Son beside the Father; which teachers and books carry the apostles' teaching.

Sources hold this: Josephus, Eusebius (with Dionysius and the 311 edict), Tertullian, Cyprian, *Deaths of the Persecutors*, *Book of the Laws of Countries*, Chronicle of Edessa; Harnack/Duchesne for geography and the Decian mechanism (era rule allows).

## MINOR

**M1. Essence: "after Rome burned the Temple".** This runs against its own Do not say line. Josephus says the fire was set against Caesar's wish (JW 126019–126020). Correction: "after the Temple in Jerusalem burned in 70" (or see B1.8).

**M2. Essence: "Roman law let an accused Christian be punished for the name alone".** Pliny leaves name or crimes as an open question (EUS 19468–19470). He punished those who confessed and persisted (19471–19476). Trajan's reply as held says only "when found should be punished" (E1). Correction: "Under Trajan's ruling, a Christian who was accused and would not deny the name could be punished (E1; EUS 19471–19476)." Add to Do not say: "punished for the name alone" → "accused and would not deny it."

**M3. Beat 6: "three other emperors".** The four-ruler line in DMP 10079 says "three persons to share the government". Correction: "who shared the rule of the empire with three others (E21)".

**M4. Quote 3: "pardon" for συγχώρησιν.** The edict uses συγγνώμην (pardon) just before it and συγχώρησιν (leave, indulgence) here (EUS-G 16718–16720). My rendering is "we thought we ought most gladly to extend our leave in this too, so that they may be Christians again and set up the houses where they used to meet, on condition they do nothing against good order." Correction, if the full quote stays: "extend our permission most gladly in this case too." My Quote 1 and Quote 2 renderings agree with the outline's. One optional change to Quote 1: "used all the strength they had" for "gathered the last of their strength", since "the last" adds a little.

**M5. What came of it 4 cites E4.** The synods of E4 were about the date of Easter, not teachers and books. **M6. Ending: "each told as what people were already arguing over".** No held line shows Christians arguing before 312 over what they owed an emperor (Impact 2). Correction: "the first two told as what people were already arguing over; the third as the question the order of 311 put to them, asking their prayers (E28)."

**M7. Words to explain lacks "presbyters"** (beats 4 and 6). Add: **presbyters:** "priests" or "elders of the church" (E25).

**M8. Beat 2: "the governors who judged Christians" has no line.** Add TERT-E 1448–1450 (Apology 1, "Rulers of the Roman Empire … seated for the administration of justice").

**M9. Do not say: add two lines.**
