## 1. The test for an era

- **What was happening to the church:** filled. The churches grew, letters and bishops' meetings linked them, and the story gives Lyon in 177, Decius in 250, the later emperor's ban, 303, and the order of 311.
- **What was happening in the world:** filled, though thinly. It gives the Roman war on Jerusalem, invaders on the Danube, a Persian capture of an emperor, war, famine and plague, the rule of four emperors, and a civil war in 312.
- **Where:** filled. It names the cities of the empire and lands past its eastern edge, then ranks the regions and ties the traditions to them.
- **Questions left for the traditions:** filled. They are which teachers and books carried the apostles' teaching, whether the fallen could come back and who decides, how to speak of the Son beside the Father, and what was owed to an emperor who now wanted their prayers. One small point: "how to speak of the Son beside the Father" is the least clear of the four for a listener without church background, but I could still repeat it back.

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing (all MINOR)

1. "Under an emperor's ruling, a Christian who was accused and would not deny the name could be punished." On one hearing, "the name" is unclear: what name? I worked out later that it means the name Christian. Change: "would not deny the name of Christian". I also did not know which emperor or when, but the next paragraph dates it ("in the second century").
2. "The same ruling, in the second century, also said Christians were not to be hunted out." The sentence before it ended in 311, so this felt like a jump back in time. I kept the thread because the date is given. The paragraph before it reaches ahead to 311 and then 311 is told again in full near the end. I read that as the essence reaching ahead, which the kit allows, so I do not count it as an event told twice.
3. "He made it to men who could have them killed, to show they were many and had never risen in arms." On one hearing, "made it" did not attach to "boasted", and "them" could be the governors or the Christians. Change: "He made that boast to men who could have Christians killed, to show they were many and had never risen in arms."
4. "War, famine and plague followed." Followed what? I could not place it in time or connect it to the son's letter. "Afterwards" in the next sentence then has nothing firm to come after.
5. "The next year, Constantine ended a civil war by defeating his rival at Rome, when a bridge of boats broke." ## 4. Bare statements (BLOCKING)

1. **"A book from Edessa, a city on Rome's eastern edge, speaks of Christians in Persia."** The story names a book without saying what it was, who wrote it or why it matters (Part 1, Explain it). As a listener, I heard it as a loose piece of evidence. **Change:** say in plain words what the book was, using the outline's gloss. If the outline does not hold one, cut the sentence. "Past its eastern edge" earlier in the story already carries the point.
2. **"Eusebius blames his hatred of the emperor before him, while historians since have seen a wish to bring back the old religion."** A reason is given for the persecution, but I could not understand it. Why would hating the emperor before him turn Decius against Christians? The link between that emperor and the Christians is missing, so the reason for an act of punishment is lost (Part 1, Explain it). **Change:** add, in Eusebius's voice, what the outline holds about the emperor before him and the Christians. If the outline does not hold that link, cut Eusebius's half and keep the historians' clause, which the era kit allows for a genuine disagreement.

## 5. Citations read aloud (MINOR)

The era kit tells a Dominant Modern Reconstruction plainly as the picture of the age, so this "how we know" clause is not needed. Narrator's voice: "By the early 300s, Christians were thickest in Asia Minor, today's Turkey, and at Edessa." If the trust read finds that historians genuinely disagree here, the clause should state the disagreement rather than this phrase.

### 6b. Lines that sound machine-made (MINOR, for the reviser)

1. "The age's traditions belong to these regions, and to Roman Palestine and the Danube." This reads as a note about the map, not a narrator telling the age: a catalog line in report voice. Suggested: "The traditions of this age grew up in these lands, and in Roman Palestine and along the Danube."
2. "Next came Antioch and its region, Egypt, Rome and Roman Africa, and they were fewest in northern Gaul and along the Rhine." With the sentence before it, this is a ranked table read aloud: list rhythm with no action or voice.
3. "War, famine and plague followed." This is a generic three-noun summary, a familiar generated cadence. It names no place, no time and no one.
4. The close: "...over whether..., and over how... And the order of 311 had put a new question to them: what they owed..." The three-part list followed by a colon kicker is a neat, patterned close. The kit's form asks for an ending on the questions, so this is not blocking. It is the one place where the cadence sounded built rather than told.

Lines that sounded human, and should be kept: the Perea echo and the turn "Perea was the land across the Jordan"; "others bought a paper saying they had"; "Those who gave way were called the fallen"; the bridge-of-boats detail (once its meaning is made clear).

## 8. Attention

- **The opening pulls me in.** The groans echoing back off the mountains is a strong, concrete image, and the turn through Perea to the Christians at Pella is the best move in the piece.
- **First drift:** "By the early 300s, as historians now map it, Christians were thickest in..." and the sentence after it. These are two sentences of places ranked by density, and nothing happens in them. Why it drifts: the story stops moving and reads a map aloud. Tying each region to an event or voice the story already holds would carry it.
- **A second dip** comes at "A later emperor turned on them..." through "Afterwards...". Unnamed actors and vague time words ("a later emperor", "followed", "afterwards") blur that stretch.
- **Would I listen to the end:** yes. From Decius on, the story moves in order to a clear stopping place.
