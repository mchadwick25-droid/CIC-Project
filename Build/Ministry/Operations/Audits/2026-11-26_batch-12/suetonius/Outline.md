# Outline: Suetonius

Every line cites `Source_Sheet.md` (S setting, E the two lines, R what came of them, D disagreement, X the explain-it list in §6, N not held), which gives each file and line. ROL = Rolfe's Latin and English (1914); TERT-C = Tertullian's Latin, CSEL 20 (1890).

**The heart**
- **The essence:** Suetonius, a Roman scholar who wrote the lives of Rome's rulers from Julius Caesar to Domitian, has two short lines in passing, each one item in a list of an emperor's acts: under Claudius, Jews were driven from Rome for rioting at the urging of "Chrestus"; under Nero, the Christians were punished as people of a new and harmful superstition. Only the second names the Christians, and he does not say who Chrestus was. Later, Christians complained, without naming him, that outsiders called them "Chrestians" and Christ "Chrestus", the name in his first line; and a Christian chronicle set the first line down almost in his words, without Chrestus, and joined it to Acts. (E1, E3, D1; R3: TERT-C 3904–3907, LAC-L 16813–16814; R1: BEDE 23216–23217; Documented as their words; S5: the span of the book, Documented.)
- **Also told:** What the lines say and leave out, as substance: each sits between ordinary measures (E2, E3); neither gives a year, a number or a source (E1, E3); he does not say who Chrestus was (D1); he files the punishment among Nero's acts that deserve no blame, some even praise (E4); he blames Nero for the fire in another chapter and there names no Christians (E5). Who he was, from his own words: an army officer's son, a young man under Domitian (S1), who later ran the emperor Hadrian's letters until Hadrian dismissed him (S4). What later readers did: Bede carried the Claudius line on (R1); Tertullian read him on other matters (R4); Tertullian and Lactantius, without naming him, protested that their name and Christ's were said wrong (R3); Jerome took his book on writers as a model (R5).
- **What came of him** (ranked under "What came of it" below). Nothing defended: he is an outside witness.

**Length:** about 3 minutes 20 seconds, about 500 words. The story must carry first: three quotes (about 58 words); the plain sense of the words to explain (Chrestus beside Christus, superstition, Chrestians and the Greek *chrestos*, old Troy, Acts, Tertullian's book, Bede's chronicle; about 75); the witnesses credited (Acts, Spartianus for the dismissal's reason, Tertullian, Lactantius, Bede; about 40): about 175 words before the narration. Beats as planned run to about 585; the Cut first list brings them to 500. The record is two lines of seven and nine Latin words and a thin life (E1, E3, S1–S4), so shorter than the kit's aim would drop either the lines in their lists or the readers who used them, and longer would stretch the lines past what they say.

**Setting** (facts for the writer, a clause at a time)
- Rome (S7). The expulsion "under Claudius", the punishment "under Nero" (E1, E3). No year for either: Suetonius gives none (S6, D5).
- Claudius ruled 41–54 and Nero 54–68 (S6: MIR 2500, SCH 24275; Widely Accepted). Plain, where Claudius is first named: "in the first century". These are the reigns, not a year for either line.
- He wrote after Domitian's death, a generation or more after both events (S5, S6). Never "in 120", never "born in 69 or 77" (S2, S5).
- No tradition name: he stands outside the church (S8).

**Words to explain** (plain sense where first heard)
- **Lives of the Caesars:** "his lives of Rome's rulers" (X1). Not "the Caesars" unexplained.
- **Ilium:** "old Troy, whose people, he says, founded the Roman race" (E2, X9).
- **Chrestus:** keep the name as he wrote it; give once that it is close in sound to Christus, the Latin form of Christ, and was also a real name (X3, D2). Never "a misspelling of Christ".
- **superstition:** "superstition, his word for a religion he despised" (X7). Do not reuse the sibling's or the Tacitus outline's wording for it.
- **Acts:** "the Acts of the Apostles, in the New Testament" (X10).
- **Chrestians:** the name said wrong; give beside Q3 that the Greek word *chrestos* means good or kind (X5), and before Q3 that "Christian", Tertullian says, comes from anointing (X4), with its plain sense in the same sentence: Christ means "the anointed one", for the Jews anointed their priests and kings with holy oil (Lactantius, *Divine Institutes* 4.7.6–7: LAC-L 16816–16821, 16830; LAC-E 3165, 3177; Documented as his explanation; narrator's words, no quote).
- **To the Nations:** "his appeal to the pagans" (X11).
- **Tranquillus** (only if Jerome stays): "another of Suetonius's names" (R5).
- **Bede's chronicle:** "his chronicle of the world" (X14).
- **master of the letters:** "he ran the emperor Hadrian's letters" (X16).

**Shape:** Outside witness. The record decides the order: his life in his own words, the Claudius line, the Nero line, then the readers in time order. Trap: making him a friend or a foe, or stretching two lines. He is brief and contemptuous; tell both, and say plainly how short the lines are (E1, E3).

Quote none of Tacitus's or Sulpicius's lines; do not use "Chrestianos" in Tacitus (the Tacitus outline leaves "Chrestus" and "Chrestians" to this story). Live beside: `pliny-the-younger` (link only; no quote), `josephus` (credits Suetonius for Vespasian's prediction; not told here).

**Openings**
- (a) Chosen. The line in its list (E1, E2). In his life of the emperor Claudius, a Roman writer sets down the emperor's acts one after another. Claudius freed the people of old Troy from tax for ever, as founders of the Roman race, and let German envoys sit with the senators at the theatre. Between those two comes one sentence: Q1. Then the name and the essence.
- (b) The Nero line in its list: spending limited, taverns allowed to sell only beans and greens, then the Christians, then the chariot drivers stopped from cheating people for fun (E3). Not chosen: told first, it would put Nero before Claudius and spend Q2 before its beat.

**Story beats** (the opening, the essence and beat 1 are the first minute)
1. **Opening and essence.** As above. Then: the writer was Suetonius, and in his lives of Rome's rulers this line, and one more under Nero, is all he says of the first Christians, if this one is about them at all (E1, E3, D1). Then the rest of the essence. Documented.
2. **Who he was, in his own words.** His father, in his own telling, was an army officer who fought in the emperor Otho's war (S1, *Otho* 10). No year. He remembers being a young man under Domitian (S1, *Domitian* 12, *Nero* 57). His lives run from Julius Caesar to Domitian, so he wrote after Domitian's death, a generation or more after both lines (S5, S6). He ran the emperor Hadrian's letters, until Hadrian dismissed him; a much later life of Hadrian says it was for behaving too familiarly toward the empress (S4: Documented as Spartianus's report, credited). If the dismissal stays, its credited reason stays with it. Two sentences at most.
3. **The Claudius line.** He gives no year, no number and no source (E1). The Acts of the Apostles also tells of Claudius ordering the Jews out of Rome: Paul, at Corinth, met a Jew and his wife, lately come from Italy because of that order (D6: Documented as Acts; the same event, Widely Accepted, not said by either text). Suetonius does not say who Chrestus was, or where he was. The name is close in sound to Christus, the Latin form of Christ, and was also a real name (X3, D1, D2). Contested; the narrator does not settle it.
4. **The Nero line.** In his life of Nero, among measures by which, he says, abuses were severely punished and put down, and new rules made: a limit on spending, public dinners cut to handouts, taverns barred from selling cooked food except beans and greens; then Q2; then the chariot drivers stopped from roaming the streets and cheating people for fun (E3: Documented). He gives no number, no charge and no kind of punishment (E3). He sets these among Nero's acts that deserve no blame, some even praise, before he turns to Nero's crimes (E4: Documented; paraphrase). In another chapter he says Nero himself set Rome on fire; there he names no Christians (E5: Documented as his account). Another Roman, Tacitus, tells at length of Christians put to death under Nero (sibling; one clause).
5. **The readers, in time order** (each credited):
   - About 200, at Carthage, Tertullian had read Suetonius and names him twice, once for the detail that Nero never dreamed (R4: Documented). In his appeal to the pagans he says the name "Christian" comes from anointing, with its plain sense in the same sentence (Words to explain, Chrestians), and then: Q3 (R3). Give the plain sense: *chrestos*, in Greek, means good or kind (X5). He does not name Suetonius.
   - A century later Lactantius wrote that the ignorant call Christ "Chrestus" by changing a letter (R3; narrator's words, no quote). Neither Christian names Suetonius (R3).
   - Near the end of the fourth century Jerome, writing short lives of Christian writers, was asked to follow the example of Tranquillus, another of Suetonius's names, and did for Christian writers what Suetonius had done for Rome's men of letters (R5: Documented; narrator's words). It was Suetonius's book on writers he followed, not the emperors' lives.
   - About 725, Bede, in his chronicle of the world, set down the Claudius line almost in Suetonius's words, without Chrestus. He put it in Claudius's ninth year and added that Luke also reports it (R1: Documented). The year is Bede's.
6. **Ending.** See Ending.

**Quotes** (three; none is in a live story on the tree or elsewhere in this batch; no reuse)
- **Q1.** ROL 25843–25844 (Latin, Ihm's text, clean print; confirmed by MIR 2506 and SCH 24311–24312): "Iudaeos impulsore Chresto assidue tumultuantis Roma expulit." Rendered from the Latin: **"He drove out of Rome the Jews who kept rioting at the urging of Chrestus."** Job: the whole Claudius line, which the story opens on; the narrator then gives the plain sense of the name. The Latin has no "all", "one" or "since" (E1): do not add them.
- **Q2.** ROL 28458–28460 (Latin, Ihm's text, clean print; confirmed by MIR 2570, "adflicti", and SCH 24348–24349): "afflicti suppliciis Christiani, genus hominum superstitionis novae ac maleficae". Rendered from the Latin: **"Punishments were laid on the Christians, a kind of people holding a new and harmful superstition."** Job: the whole Nero line, his contempt in his own words, heard inside its list. Rolfe's "mischievous" is not used: its modern sense, playful, would mislead (X8).
- **Q3.** TERT-C 3904–3907 (Tertullian, *To the Nations* 1.3, Latin, CSEL 20, clean print; the same point in Oehler's damaged *Apology* 3, TERT-O 12453–12456, and in the 1885 English, TERT-E 9205–9208): "etiam cum corrupte a uobis Chrestiani pronuntiamur (nam ne nominis quidem ipsius liquido certi estis), sic quoque de suauitate uel bonitate modulatum est." **Connection to carry into the story:** What passed from Suetonius was two lines, and a puzzle in a name. The listener must hear three things to follow it. First, that he was not writing about Christians at all: each line is one item in a list of an emperor's acts, and he files Nero's punishment of them among the acts deserving no blame (E2, E3, E4). Second, that "Chrestus" is close in sound to Christus, and also a real name, and that he does not say who it was (D1, D2). Third, that Christians, without naming him, complained that outsiders garbled their name and Christ's in just this way (R3: TERT-C 3904–3907, LAC-L 16813–16814), and that Bede set the first line down in his chronicle almost in Suetonius's words and joined it to Acts (R1: BEDE 23216–23217). Carried by these acts, never by a "this matters because" sentence.

**Do not say**
- Not "Claudius expelled the Jews because of Christ" or "because of the Christians". He says "Chrestus" (E1); who that was is not his to tell (D1).
- Not "Chrestus was a misspelling of Christ", "historians agree it means Christ", or "Chrestus was a Jewish agitator". Say the name is close in sound to Christus and was also a real name, and that he does not say who (D1, D2).
- Not that Chrestus was in Rome or led the riots in person. "At the urging of" is all (E1).
- Not "all the Jews", "one Chrestus" or "since" as Suetonius's words (E1, D4). "All" is in Acts.
- Not "in 49", "in 51" or any year for the expulsion in the narrator's voice. The ninth year is Bede's (D5, R1).
- Not that Acts and Suetonius say they describe the same event. Neither does; Bede joins them (D6, R1).
- Not "Nero blamed the Christians for the fire" or "punished them for the fire" as Suetonius's. He never joins the two (E5, D7).
- Not "Nero executed", "killed" or "burned" the Christians as Suetonius's words. He says punishments, and gives no kind, number or charge (E3).
- Not "Nero punished the Christians" with Nero as Suetonius's grammatical subject in a quote. The Latin is "punishments were laid", "under him" (E3). The narrator may say "under Nero".
- Not "Suetonius condemned Nero for it" or "praised Nero for it". He files it among acts deserving no blame, some even praise; say exactly that (E4).
- Not "mischievous", "impious", "witchcraft" or "magic" for *malefica*. Say "harmful" (X8).
- Not "the first", "the earliest" or "the only" outside mention of Christians or of Christ. Say "two short lines" (E1, E3).
- Not "Suetonius proves Jesus lived", or anything about Jesus beyond the name.
- Not that Tertullian or Lactantius answered, quoted or named Suetonius on the Christians. Tertullian names him on other matters (R4); neither names him on the name (R3).
- Not that Tertullian read the line on the Christians. He cites Suetonius on the games and on Nero's dreams only (R4).
- Not that Jerome modelled his book on the lives of the emperors. It was the book on writers (R5).
- Not that Bede named Suetonius or Chrestus. He names Luke, and drops Chrestus (R1).
- Not that Bede copied the line from Suetonius's book. Say he set it down almost in Suetonius's words (R1).
- Not that a law or edict against Christians existed under Nero. Suetonius names none (E3); the edict is Ruinart's reading, card (R2).
- Not "born in 69" or "77", "born in Rome", "published in 120" or "dismissed in 121" (S2, S4, S5). Not "senator", "lawyer" or "schoolmaster" (S3).
- Not the dismissal's reason as fact, or anything about an affair. Credit the much later life of Hadrian, and keep to "behaving too familiarly toward the empress" (S4, D8).
- Not Pliny in the audio at all (N1; outline check). Never that Suetonius knew of Pliny's trials of Christians.
- Not "Suetonius wrote little about great events" as fact: that is Rolfe's reading (S5). Show the lists instead.
- Not "historians still rely on them today" or "still argue today". The held readers stop at 1924 (R7).
- Not a sentence that only says what is missing. Each gap goes inside a sentence about what he does (Kit, Craft).
- Not "abuses were punished" without "severely". His Latin is "animadversa severe et coercita": "severely punished and put down" (E3).
- Not "the name 'Christian' comes from anointing" without its plain sense in the same sentence. Give the held plain sense, or leave the anointing out and keep Q3.
- Any word in Words to explain used without its plain sense the first time, "Chrestus", "Chrestians" and "superstition" among them.
