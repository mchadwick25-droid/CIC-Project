# Outline check: Suetonius

## My own account of the heart (written before reading Outline.md)

From the census entry (teaser, card), the source sheet and what he is known for:

- **Essence.** Suetonius, a Roman biographer who later ran the emperor Hadrian's letters, wrote lives of Rome's rulers that mention the Christians in only two short lines: under Claudius, Jews "constantly rioting at the urging of Chrestus" were driven from Rome; under Nero, punishments were inflicted on the Christians, "a new and harmful superstition", an item in a list of measures he counts as blameless. The lines give no year, number, charge or account of who Chrestus was, and he is on the tree as an outside witness whose few words later Christian readers took up.
- **Also told.** Who he was, in his own words (his father an officer; a young man in Rome under Domitian) and by Spartianus (master of Hadrian's letters, replaced); the book (twelve lives, Julius Caesar to Domitian, acts grouped in lists); each line in its list (Ilium and the German envoys; taverns, chariot drivers, pantomime actors); the Nero line filed among the acts "beyond criticism"; his fire chapter blaming Nero names no Christians, and his Christians line names no fire. What the lines leave open: who Chrestus was (Contested); Acts' report of the same command (Widely Accepted as the same expulsion, joined by Bede, never by Suetonius).
- **What came of them** (heaviest first, old witnesses only): Bede carried the Claudius line on in his chronicle, beside Luke, with a year that is his own; Christians protested that Romans said "Chrestianus" and "Chrestus" (Tertullian, Lactantius; the tie to Suetonius is the editors'); Tertullian read and named Suetonius on other matters; Jerome took his lost book on writers as the model for his own. Defended: Tertullian's and Lactantius's defense of the name. Ruinart goes to the card (header ruling).
