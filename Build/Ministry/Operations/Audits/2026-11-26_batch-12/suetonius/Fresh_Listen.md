## 1. The test

- **Who or what this was:** filled. "a Roman scholar who wrote the lives of Rome's rulers from Julius Caesar to Domitian", his father an army officer, later "ran the emperor Hadrian's letters".
- **What happened:** filled. Two lines. Claudius "drove out of Rome the Jews who kept rioting at the urging of Chrestus", and under Nero "Punishments were laid on the Christians".
- **What it was about:** filled. "Chrestus" is "close in sound to Christus, the Latin form of Christ, and it was also a real name". The Nero line calls the Christians "a new and harmful superstition", and "superstition" is explained.
- **Why the connection matters:** filled, though thin. Later readers used the lines: Tertullian answers the "Chrestians" mistake, and Bede ties the Claudius line to Acts. The listener comes away knowing these are the only lines and that later Christians took them up.
