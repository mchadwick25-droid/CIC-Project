## The BLOCKING finding

**1. SOFTENED OR CHANGED (who says it).** "In his appeal to the pagans, without naming Suetonius, he says the name "Christian" comes from anointing: Christ means "the anointed one", for the Jews anointed their priests and kings with holy oil."
- Tertullian says only: "Christianum uero nomen, quantum significatio est, de unctione interpretatur" (TERT-C 3902–3904). He says nothing there of priests, kings or holy oil.
- The priests, kings and oil are Lactantius's: "erat Iudaeis ante praeceptum ut sacrum conficerent unguentum, quo perungui possent ii qui uocabantur ad sacerdotium uel ad regnum" (LAC-L 16816–16821), and "nos eum Christum nuncupamus, id est unctum" (LAC-L 16830).
- The colon after "he says" makes the explanation sound like Tertullian's words. The outline gives it to the narrator only, and its Do not say list bars "that Tertullian says the Jews anointed priests and kings".
- **Exact change:** end Tertullian's clause and give the plain sense as its own narrator sentence: "In his appeal to the pagans, without naming Suetonius, he says the name "Christian" comes from anointing. Christ means "the anointed one", for the Jews anointed their priests and kings with holy oil."

## SUMMARY HOLDS (not blocking; evidence given)

- "In his life of the emperor Claudius, who ruled in the first century, the Roman writer Suetonius sets down the emperor's acts one after another." *Claudius* 25 is a run of acts (ROL 25820–25856). "First century" rests on reign dates that are Widely Accepted.
- "Claudius freed the people of old Troy from tax forever, because, he says, they founded the Roman race, and he let German envoys sit with the senators at the theater." "Iliensibus quasi Romanae gentis auctoribus tributa in perpetuum remisit" (ROL 25769–25770). "Germanorum legatis in orchestra sedere permisit", where they had seen envoys "sedentis in senatu" (ROL 25844–25848).
- "Suetonius was a Roman scholar who wrote the lives of Rome's rulers from Julius Caesar to Domitian." The span: "from Julius to Domitian" (ROL 320–326). "Scholar": Jerome followed his book on "the illustrious men of letters" (JER-E 38773–38779), and Tertullian cites him on the origin of the games (TERT-C 919–920).
- "Later, Christians complained, without naming him, that outsiders got their name wrong, calling them "Chrestians" and Christ "Chrestus", the name in his first line." "corrupte a uobis Chrestiani pronuntiamur" (TERT-C 3904–3905). "propter ignorantium errorem, qui eum inmutata littera Chrestum solent dicere" (LAC-L 16813–16814). Neither names Suetonius.
- "His lives end with that emperor, so he wrote after Domitian's death, a generation or more after both events." The book ends with Domitian's death. Claudius and Nero reigned 41–68 (Widely Accepted).
- "In his life of Nero, Suetonius lists measures by which, he says, abuses were severely punished and put down, and new rules made." "Multa sub eo et animadversa severe et coercita nec minus instituta" (ROL 28454–28455). "Abuses" is a fair rendering of "multa".
- "Superstition was his word for a religion he despised." *Superstitio* is his word in the line, and "maleficae" shows the contempt (ROL 28459–28460). A note, not a finding: elsewhere he calls the Druids' rite "religionem ... dirae immanitatis" (ROL 25849–25850). So the gloss holds for this line, not as a rule for all his usage.
- "The list runs straight on to the chariot drivers, who were stopped from roaming the streets and cheating people for fun." "vetiti quadrigariorum lusus, quibus ... passim vagantibus fallere ac furari per iocum ius erat" (ROL 28460–28462). "Furari" (stealing) is dropped. That is fair.
- "In Greek, chrestos means good or kind." Tertullian's own line carries it: the wrong name "de suauitate uel bonitate modulatum est" (TERT-C 3906–3907).
