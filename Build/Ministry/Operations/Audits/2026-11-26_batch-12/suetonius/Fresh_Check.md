## The sentence

> In his appeal to the pagans, without naming Suetonius, he says the name "Christian" comes from anointing; in plain words, Christ means "the anointed one", for the Jews anointed their priests and kings with holy oil.

## Says no more than its sources

- **Tertullian's part.** TERT-C 3902–3904: "Christianum uero nomen, quantum significatio est, de unctione interpretatur." He says only that the name, by its meaning, comes from anointing. The sentence gives him exactly that and no more. "His appeal to the pagans" is the outline's plain sense for *Ad nationes* (X11). "Without naming Suetonius" holds: the chapter does not name him (R3; Do not say, line 108).
- **The plain sense.** LAC-L 16816–16821 (*Divine Institutes* 4.7.6–7): the Jews were commanded to make a sacred ointment ("sacrum ... unguentum") to anoint those called to the priesthood or the kingship ("ad sacerdotium uel ad regnum"); for this reason "nos eum Christum nuncupamus, id est unctum". "Christ means the anointed one", "priests and kings" and "holy oil" are all in these lines. The wording is the outline's own (Words to explain), rated Documented as Lactantius's explanation, to be given in the narrator's words with no quote. Nothing is added.

It also keeps the plain sense in the same sentence, as the outline's Do not say ("Not 'the name "Christian" comes from anointing' without its plain sense in the same sentence") and Words to explain require.
