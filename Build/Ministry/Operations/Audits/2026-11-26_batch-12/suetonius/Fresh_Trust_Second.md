## Each sentence

1. "In his life of the emperor Claudius, who ruled in the first century, the Roman writer Suetonius sets down the emperor's acts one after another." **HELD.** The reign is 41–54 (MIR 2500, "Klaudius ... (41 — 54)"; SCH 24275), which is Widely Accepted. The outline asks for "in the first century" at this point.

2. "Claudius freed the people of old Troy from tax forever, because, he says, they founded the Roman race, and he let German envoys sit with the senators at the theater." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** ROL 25769: "Iliensibus quasi Romanae gentis auctoribus tributa in perpetuum remisit"; Rolfe, ROL 25826–25827: "on the ground that they were the founders of the Roman race". *Quasi* gives Claudius's ground, and "he says" keeps it as Suetonius's report. The envoys are at ROL 25844–25845.

3. "Suetonius was a Roman scholar who wrote the lives of Rome's rulers from Julius Caesar to Domitian." **HELD.** The work runs from the Deified Julius to Domitian (ROL 324: "twelve 'Caesars,' from Julius to Domitian"). The content is the same as v1; only the wording changed.

4. "Later, Christians complained, without naming him, that outsiders got their name wrong, calling them "Chrestians" and Christ "Chrestus", the name in his first line." **HELD.** TERT-C 3904–3905: "corrupte a uobis Chrestiani pronuntiamur". LAC-L 16813–16814: "propter ignorantium errorem, qui eum inmutata littera Chrestum solent dicere". Neither names Suetonius. The sentence uses the wording that the outline's Do not say list requires.

5. "His lives end with that emperor, so he wrote after Domitian's death, a generation or more after both events." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** The lives end with Domitian. Claudius ruled 41–54 and Nero 54–68 (SCH 24275), so the work came at least 28 years after either event. Changing "lines" to "events" fixes the sense. The outline holds the change ("a generation or more after both events").

6. "In his life of Nero, Suetonius lists measures by which, he says, abuses were severely punished and put down, and new rules made." **HELD.** ROL 28454–28455: "Multa sub eo et animadversa severe et coercita nec minus instituta". Rolfe, ROL 28512–28514: "many abuses were severely punished and put down, and no fewer new laws were made".

7. "About 200, at Carthage in North Africa, the Christian writer Tertullian had read Suetonius and names him on other matters." **HELD** for the added words. Jerome, *De viris illustribus* 53, JER-L 55610–55614: "Tertullianus presbyter ... provinciae Africae, civitatis Carthaginiensis" (in English, NPNF2 3, chapter LIII, `npnf203_theodoret-jerome-gennadius-rufinus.xml` 40489). The rest of the sentence did not change.

8. "In his appeal to the pagans, without naming Suetonius, he says the name "Christian" comes from anointing: Christ means "the anointed one", for the Jews anointed their priests and kings with holy oil." **SOFTENED OR CHANGED: BLOCKING.**
   - Tertullian, TERT-C 3903–3904: "Christianum uero nomen, quantum significatio est, de unctione interpretatur." He says only that the name comes from anointing.
   - The priests and kings come from Lactantius, *Divine Institutes* 4.7.6–7, LAC-L 16816–16821: "erat Iudaeis ante praeceptum ut sacrum conficerent unguentum, quo perungui possent ii qui uocabantur ad sacerdotium uel ad regnum"; and LAC-L 16830: "nos eum Christum nuncupamus, id est unctum".
   - In the sentence, the colon follows "he says", so a listener hears Lactantius's explanation as Tertullian's words. The outline rules this out twice: "Give it in the narrator's voice, never as Tertullian's words: the priests and kings are Lactantius's", and its Do not say list has "Not that Tertullian says the Jews anointed priests and kings."
   - **Exact change:** "In his appeal to the pagans, without naming Suetonius, he says the name "Christian" comes from anointing. In plain words, Christ means "the anointed one", because the Jews anointed their priests and kings with holy oil."

9. "He put it in Claudius's ninth year and added that Luke also reports it, joining it to the Acts." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** BEDE 23216–23217: "Nono regni sui anno Iudaeos tumultuantes Roma expulit, quod et Lucas refert", with the margin "act. 18,2". Bede names Luke, not Acts. Saying he joined it to Acts is a fair compression, and the outline holds it (R1, "joined it to Acts").
