# Second Readings (SR2)

Comparison of the blind second readings against our wording, batch 12. The comparer did not do the blind readings.

## Method

For each id in `Second_Reading_Key.md` (SR2-01 to SR2-12):

1. Our wording was checked word for word against the piece's brief (`<slug>.brief.txt`) and against the card entry's `modern` (`<slug>.card.json`, the `quotationOriginals` index the key names). All 12 stand word for word in the brief and equal the card's `modern`.
2. Our wording was set beside the blind rendering in `Second_Readings_Blind_A.md` (SR2-01 to 06) or `Second_Readings_Blind_B.md` (SR2-07 to 12). The `_SR1` files were not used.
3. Where the two differ, the cited lines in `cic/texts/` and the entry's `printed`, `original` and `uncertain` were opened. Where a card renders from a Greek print it does not quote, that print was opened too: Schwartz's Eusebius (`eusebius_werke-bd2-2-historia-ecclesiastica-vi-x-rufinus-x-xi-grc-lat_schwartz-mommsen1908.txt`), Lagarde's Hippolytus (`hippolytus_quae-feruntur-omnia-contra-noetum-grc_lagarde1858.txt`) and Otto's Tatian and Corpus Apologetarum (`tatian_oratio-ad-graecos-grc-lat_otto1851.txt`, `apologists-fragmentary_reliquiae-corpus-apologetarum-vol9-grc-lat_otto1872.txt`).
4. Grades: SAME (same meaning), MINOR (a difference a listener would not take differently), MEANING (a listener would understand something different).

## Hippolytus, SR2-05

Our wording: "When I say 'another,' I do not mean two Gods. I mean it as light from light, or water from a spring, or a ray from the sun. For there is one power, which comes from the whole. The whole is the Father, and from him comes the power, the Word."

Salmond (`anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian.xml` lines 23614-23626): "But when I say another, I do not mean that there are two Gods, but that it is only as light of light, or as water from a fountain, or as a ray from the sun. For there is but one power, which is from the All; and the Father is the All, from whom cometh this Power, the Word." His endnote 1661 gives the Greek ἐκ τοῦ παντός.

Lagarde's scan (lines 3705-3708) reads: "ἕτερον δὲ λέγων οὐ δύο θεοὺς λέγω, ἀλλ ὡς φῶς ix φωτὸς / [line 3706: stray marks] / Ἡ ὡς ὕδωρ ἐκ πηγῆς ἡ ὡς ἀκτῆα ἀπὸ ἡλίου. δύναμις γὰρ µία ἡ / ἐκ τοῦ παντός, τὸ δὲ πᾶν πατήρ, ἐξ οὗ δύναμις λόγος."

Checked word by word, nothing in our wording rests on a reading no held print gives.

- The damaged words are ix φωτὸς, Ἡ ὡς / ἡ ὡς and ἀκτῆα. Our "from light", "or" (twice) and "a ray" take them as ἐκ φωτός, ἢ ὡς and ἀκτῖνα. Salmond's "of light", "or as" and "a ray" confirm each one. Line 3706 carries no words. The text runs on cleanly from φωτὸς (3705) to ἢ ὡς ὕδωρ (3707), so no words are lost there.
- "one power, which comes from the whole" and "The whole is the Father" rest on line 3708, which reads cleanly (δύναμις γὰρ μία ἡ ἐκ τοῦ παντός, τὸ δὲ πᾶν πατήρ). Salmond's endnote gives ἐκ τοῦ παντός too. "Whole" and Salmond's "All" both render τὸ πᾶν.
- "from him comes the power, the Word" renders ἐξ οὗ δύναμις λόγος, also clean. The Greek has no verb in either of these clauses. Our "comes" twice follows Salmond's "is from" and "cometh". The card notes only the verb supplied by "I mean it as". The others are just as plainly supplied and need no note.
- Where ours and the blind differ, ours keeps closer to the Greek. The blind's "only" (twice) comes from Salmond's "only as" and "but one". The Greek has neither: ἀλλ' ὡς and μία. The blind's "this Power" is Salmond's "this". The Greek has none. The blind's "But" renders δέ, which ours leaves out.

Grade: MINOR. Leaving out "only" softens the stress a little, but a listener hears the same claim: one God, and the Word coming from the Father as light from light.

- **tatian, quotationOriginals[1].** The `uncertain` says the clause "puffed up as if he were better than the rest" is held only in damaged scans, with a word misread in every print that has it. Otto's Tatian of 1851 (`tatian_oratio-ad-graecos-grc-lat_otto1851.txt` lines 14653-14655) prints the second half cleanly: "ἀποστὰς τῆς ἐκκλησίας, οἰήματι διδασκάλου ἐπαρθεὶς καὶ τυφωϑείς, ὡς διαφέρων τῶν λοιπῶν, ἴδιον χαρακτῆρα διδασκαλείου συνεστήσατο". The misreading (αἷς for ὡς) is in Maran's quotation (Otto 1872 line 14150). Add Otto 1851 to the `uncertain` as a fourth print that agrees, and narrow "in every print" to the prints it holds for.

The card names Schwartz's Greek but has no `uncertain` saying the English departs from McGiffert. Add one: the modern English follows Schwartz's Greek, νήπια, infants.
