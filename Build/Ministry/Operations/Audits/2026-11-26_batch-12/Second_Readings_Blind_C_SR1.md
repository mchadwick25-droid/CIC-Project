## SR1-23
Source: `cic/texts/pamphilus_apologia-pro-origene-rufinus-lat_lommatzsch1846.txt`, lines 14153-14158 (Latin).

But for those who read Origen's books, they think that command is completely ruled out, the one that tells us to become trustworthy money-changers: people who know how to keep what is good and to hold back from every kind of evil. Instead, if anyone is merely seen to have read his books, at once these people will drench him in the disgrace of being a heretic.

Reader's notes:
- "They" (the subject of "putant", "think") is not named in the passage; it refers to people introduced earlier. The second "ab his" ("by these people") refers to those same accusers, not to the readers of Origen.
- The subject of "visus fuerit" ("is seen") is unstated; I render it "anyone".
- "Haereticorum ... infamia" is literally "the disgrace of heretics", that is, the shameful name of heretic.
- Scan damage: a footnote mark ("*))") after "scientes", and a stray period in "sed." These do not affect the sense. The word division "fue-rit" across the line is a normal hyphenation.

## SR1-25
Source: `cic/texts/suetonius_lives-of-the-caesars-loeb_rolfe1914.txt`, lines 25843-25844 (Latin).

The Jews kept stirring up trouble, urged on by Chrestus, so he drove them out of Rome.

Reader's notes:
- "He" (the subject of "expulit") is not named in the sentence; it is the emperor the surrounding Life is about.
- "Impulsore Chresto" is literally "with Chrestus as instigator". The sentence does not say who Chrestus was, or whether he was present in Rome.
- "Assidue tumultuantis" means "constantly rioting" or "causing continual disturbances".

## SR1-26
Source: `cic/texts/suetonius_lives-of-the-caesars-loeb_rolfe1914.txt`, lines 28458-28460 (Latin).

Punishments were inflicted on the Christians, a class of people given to a new and harmful superstition.

Reader's notes:
- "Afflicti suppliciis" is literally "were afflicted with punishments"; "supplicium" often means execution or severe penalty.
- "Malefica" can mean "harmful", "wicked" or "mischievous". It can also suggest magic or sorcery. "Harmful" is the plainest sense; the others are possible.
- The phrase sits in a list of measures, separated by semicolons; the actor is not named within it.

## SR1-27
Source: `cic/texts/tertullian_opera-pars1-lat_reifferscheid-wissowa-csel20.txt`, lines 3904-3907 (Latin).

Even when you mispronounce us as "Chrestians" (for you are not even clearly sure of the name itself), even then the name is formed from sweetness or kindness.

Reader's notes:
- "Pronuntiamur" is passive: "we are pronounced", that is, "you call us".
- "Modulatum est" is literally "it has been shaped" or "it is composed". I take the subject as the mispronounced name.
- Scan: vertical bars ("|") mark line breaks of the printed edition and are not text. The hyphenated "no|minis" reads "nominis". Nothing in the passage is unclear.
