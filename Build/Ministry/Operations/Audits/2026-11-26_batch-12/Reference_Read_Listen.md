## Basil of Caesarea

- **2.** After three sentences I have the name only. "Caesarea, an inland city in Asia Minor, in the fourth century" comes in sentence 4. I am not lost about who.
- **3.** (a) "When the emperor Valens, who favored the party that ranked Christ, the Son, below God the Father, moved against the churches that held them equal, Basil came back to serve the bishop." The nesting loses the thread of who is moving against whom. (b) "Basil closed prayers by giving glory to the Father in two forms: with the Son, together with the Holy Spirit, and through the Son, in the Holy Spirit." Heard once, the difference between the two forms is lost. The next sentence only half recovers it.
- **4.** "but they quarreled". A quarrel is named with no cause. I cannot tell whether the sources give one. Candidate only.
- **5.** "Basil answered by making more bishops for the province, Gregory says." This is the third credit to Gregory in the paragraph, on a plain act and not a judgment.
- **7.** "creed" (the council is glossed, the creed is not). The Spirit "beside the Father and the Son" assumes a listener who knows the Trinity.
- **Note:** it ends on Benedict, a later writer, and not on Basil. Part 1 allows that only where it is the story's point.

## Epiphanius of Salamis

- **2.** The medicine-chest opening names Epiphanius but does not say who he was, where or when. "A monk from Palestine who became bishop of Salamis" comes in sentence 5. "Heresies" goes unexplained until sentence 4.
- **3.** (a) "In 787 the council at Nicaea ruled that those writings were not his, arguing that his eighty chapters never listed the making of images." The reasoning is lost on one hearing. (b) "A book written to answer the council's argument replied with his own letter about the torn curtain." Which book, and what the reply proves, are unclear.
- **7.** "Augustine" is named twice with no role.

## John Chrysostom

- **2.** Nothing is missing: Eutropius, the cathedral of Constantinople, 399 and the bishop John Chrysostom all come within three sentences.
- **3.** (a) "They decked the altar, where the bread of Communion lies, which Christians call Christ's body, with gold cups and silk." Nested. "which" can be heard as pointing to the altar. (b) "A later bishop of Constantinople summed up the meeting's own written record, and they tell it differently: ..." "they" is unclear (the record? the bishops?).
- **4.** "but he was soon called back". No cause, but this may be told as the source gives it. Not counted.
- **7.** "chamberlain" and "Gothic" are not explained for a second-language listener.
