**Heart:** it is the right one. The sources show a teacher at Rome who held the Son to be "another" yet not a second God (NG 3704-3708), who would not give way to Zephyrinus and Callistus (RG 17721-17722, 18080-18081), who set the quarrel down in the *Refutation*, and who was deported with Pontianus in 235 and kept at Rome among its martyrs (LP 66805-66807, 67682). The essence is complete by the end of paragraph 2, at 138 words. That is within the first minute.

## Non-HELD sentences

### BLOCKING

**1. SOFTENED OR CHANGED (the taunt is moved after Zephyrinus's death).** Paragraph 5: "When Zephyrinus died, Callistus took his place and, Hippolytus says, called him and his side worshippers of two gods, because they spoke of the Father and the Son as two."
- The source sets the taunt while Zephyrinus was still bishop, as a repeated act in the imperfect tense. Callistus is the one leading Zephyrinus on, then: "ὃς εἰς ἀπόνοιαν χωρῶν διὰ τὸ πάντας αὐτοῦ τῇ ὑποκρίσει συντρέχειν, ἡμᾶς δὲ οὐ, ἀπεκάλει ἡμᾶς διθέους" (RG 18080-18082; HIP 13633-13641, "called us worshippers of two gods"). IX.12 mentions "Δίθεοί ἔστε" (RG 18216) again only as an earlier public saying that led him to devise his teaching, not as an act of his first days as bishop.
- The sentence ties the taunt to Callistus taking office. The source does not. This is a detail moved onto the wrong event.
- The "because" clause holds as the taunt's meaning: Callistus's own line is "οὐ γάρ, φησίν, ἐρῶ δύο θεούς, πατέρα καὶ υἱόν, ἀλλ᾽ ἕνα" (RG 18265). It may stay.
- **Change to:** "Even while Zephyrinus lived, Hippolytus says, Callistus called him and his side worshippers of two gods, because they spoke of the Father and the Son as two. When Zephyrinus died, Callistus took his place."
- **Do not say (for the outline):** | Callistus first called them "two gods" when he became bishop | "Even while Zephyrinus lived" (RG 18080-18082) |

**2. ASSUMPTION ADDED (a connection the texts do not make).** Paragraph 5: "Hippolytus answered that he spoke of one God, in two persons, with the grace of the Holy Spirit as a third."
- The words come from *Against Noetus* 14, where he answers an imagined questioner, "τις", rather than Callistus: "τί οὖν φήσειεν ἄν τις· Δύο λέγεις θεούς; δύο μὲν οὐκ ἐρῶ θεοὺς ἀλλ᾽ ἢ ἕνα, πρόσωπα δὲ δύο, οἰκονομίᾳ δὲ τρίτην τὴν χάριν τοῦ ἁγίου πνεύματος" (NG 3780-3783; HIP 23728-23731).
- Placed straight after Callistus's taunt, "Hippolytus answered" makes it a reply to Callistus. That is Wendland's link in his apparatus (RG 18226-18227, MODERN), which the source sheet (E3) and outline beat 3 bar: "never joined as the editor joins them".
- **Change to:** "In his sermon against Noetus, he asked himself whether he spoke of two Gods, and answered that he spoke of one God, in two persons, with the grace of the Holy Spirit as a third."
- **Do not say:** | "Hippolytus answered [Callistus]" with the *Against Noetus* 14 words | Give them as his answer in *Against Noetus* to "anyone" who asks |

**3. SOFTENED OR CHANGED (one bishop made several).** Paragraph 9: "In the East, Eusebius wrote that Hippolytus had led another church, without saying which, and later bishops there quoted him for the faith and called him bishop and martyr."
- The first clause holds: "ἑτέρας που καὶ αὐτὸς προεστὼς ἐκκλησίας" (EUSG 3964-3965).
- Among the held witnesses, only one bishop both quotes him and calls him bishop and martyr. That is Theodoret: "Testimony of the Holy Hippolytus, Bishop and Martyr" (JER 20475-20476), and "Ἱππόλυτος ὁ ἐπίσκοπος καὶ μάρτυρ" (THG 32062-32063).
- Epiphanius, the other bishop the outline means, only names him among those who refuted the Valentinians (EPI 32965-32966). He neither quotes him nor gives him those titles.
- The other old writer who does both is Jerome, at Bethlehem, who was not a bishop. He calls Hippolytus "cujusdam Ecclesiae episcopus" (JERL 56674-56675) and quotes "Hippolyti martyris uerba" (JEP 14310-14311). So "later writers there" holds where "later bishops there" does not.
- **Change to:** "... and later writers there quoted him for the faith and called him bishop and martyr."
- **Do not say:** | "later bishops in the East called him bishop and martyr" | "later writers in the East" (Theodoret, Jerome) |

### SUMMARY HOLDS (not blocking)

- **P1:** "Twice the elders called him before the church, where he asked what harm he was doing by glorifying Christ, and they put him out." The text has "προσκαλεσάμενοι ἐνώπιον τῆς ἐκκλησίας ἐξήταζον ... πάλιν προσκαλεσάμενοι ... Τί οὖν κακὸν ποιῶ δοξάζων τὸν Χριστόν; ... ἐξέωσαν τῆς ἐκκλησίας" (NG 3194-3212; HIP 23152-23172). The question was asked at the second hearing, which this wording allows.
- **P2:** "Hippolytus, who tells that story, taught in the church at Rome in the early 200s and wrote in Greek."
  - "Ὁμιλία Ἱππολύτου εἰς τὴν αἵρεσιν Νοήτου" (NG 3180).
  - "μετέχοντες ... διδασκαλίας" (RG 1349).
  - Under Zephyrinus, before Callistus, whose years begin in 218 (LP 66796-66801).
  - The texts are in Greek (RG; NG).
- **P2:** "He would not give way on this, or on how far the church could forgive, to two bishops of Rome, Zephyrinus and Callistus."
  - "ἡμῶν μηδέποτε συγχωρησάντων, ἀλλὰ πλειστάκις ἀντικαθεστώτων πρὸς αὐτούς" (RG 17721-17722).
  - "during the succession of such bishops" (HIP 13300).
  - On forgiveness: his own side's "condemnatory sentence" against those Callistus took in (RG 18282-18283; HIP 13884-13886).
- **P2:** "He set the quarrel down in a book against every teaching he held false." See RG 17702-17703 and the plan in its preface (RG 1361-1366).
- **P6:** "Hippolytus charges that Callistus forgave every sin of the body to anyone who came over to him, and held that a bishop should not be removed even for the gravest sin." The text has "τὰ πρὸς τὰς ἡδονὰς ... συγχωρεῖν ... λέγων πᾶσιν ὑπ᾽ αὐτοῦ ἀφίεσθαι ἁμαρτίας ... εἰ προσδράμοι τῇ τοῦ Καλλίστου σχολῇ ... εἰ ἐπίσκοπος ἁμάρτοι τι, εἰ καὶ πρὸς θάνατον, μὴ δεῖν κατατίθεσθαι" (RG 18278-18286).
- **P7:** "Its last part turned to the teachings of his own day, Callistus's among them." The text has "περιλείπεται νῦν ὁ μέγιστος ἀγών ... τὰς ἐφ᾽ ἡμῖν ἐπαναστάσας αἱρέσεις" (RG 17701-17703). Book X, a summary, follows it. This is the outline's wording.
- **P7:** "The high priesthood meant the church's chief priestly office." This is a gloss on "ἀρχιερατείας" (RG 1349). It is kept as his claim, and does not name the office as a bishop's (HIP 1131-1135).
- **P10:** "Damasus means Novatian, whose party split from the church at Rome in 251, refusing to take back those who had given way in persecution." Old usage gives Novatian, the Roman presbyter, as "Novatus" (EUS 38265; EUSG 7517, "Νοουάτου"). The year is the census `novatianism` date.
