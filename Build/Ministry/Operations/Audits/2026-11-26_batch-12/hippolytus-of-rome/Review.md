## BLOCKING findings

### 1. The "two gods" taunt is moved after Zephyrinus's death and given a reason the source does not give (Trust 1; Trust second 11). Needs the outline.

- **Sentence:** "When Zephyrinus died, Callistus took his place and, Hippolytus says, called him and his side worshippers of two gods, because they spoke of the Father and the Son as two."
- **Evidence on the time (both readers):** the taunt is told in *Refutation* IX.11, while Zephyrinus was still bishop. It is a repeated act, in the imperfect tense: "ὃς εἰς ἀπόνοιαν χωρῶν διὰ τὸ πάντας αὐτοῦ τῇ ὑποκρίσει συντρέχειν, ἡμᾶς δὲ οὐ, ἀπεκάλει ἡμᾶς διθέους" (RG 18080-18082; HIP 13633-13641). IX.12, after Zephyrinus's death, only looks back to it (RG 18216). Setting it after "When Zephyrinus died" puts a detail on the wrong event, Part 1's third fault.
- **Evidence on the reason (the readers disagree):** Trust holds that the "because" clause may stay, as the meaning of the taunt, on Callistus's own line (RG 18265). The second reader marks it ASSUMPTION ADDED, because the source gives a different "because": the rest went along with Callistus's hypocrisy and Hippolytus's side did not (RG 18081-18082, "διὰ τὸ πάντας ... συντρέχειν, ἡμᾶς δὲ οὐ").
- **Judged by the bar:** the second reader is right. Part 1 says every "because" stands on a line. Here the source states the cause, and the brief swaps in a different one. The content of the label is plain from the words "two gods", heard straight after his teaching on "another" and "two persons". So it does not need a cause clause. This blocks as an added assumption, on top of the moved date.
- **Exact change:** replace the sentence with: "While Zephyrinus still lived, Hippolytus says, Callistus called him and his side worshippers of two gods, because they would not go along with Callistus." Put "When Zephyrinus died, Callistus took his place." at the head of the next paragraph, before "Hippolytus charges that Callistus forgave…". With finding 2, the taunt now comes after his teaching and its answer, never before them.
- **Why it needs the outline:** beat 3 ("Callistus becomes bishop") holds the taunt after the accession. The gloss in Words to explain ("for speaking of Father and Son as two") reads as its cause. Both are the outline's own wording.
- **Do not say (add to the outline):** | "When Zephyrinus died, Callistus … called them worshippers of two gods" | "While Zephyrinus still lived, Hippolytus says, Callistus called him and his side worshippers of two gods" (RG 18080-18082) | and | "because they spoke of the Father and the Son as two" as the taunt's cause | the cause he gives: "because they would not go along with Callistus" (RG 18081-18082) |

### 2. The *Against Noetus* 14 answer is made a reply to Callistus (Trust 2; Trust second 12). Needs the outline.

- **Sentence:** "Hippolytus answered that he spoke of one God, in two persons, with the grace of the Holy Spirit as a third."
- **Evidence (both readers):** the words hold, from NG 3780-3783 and HIP 23728-23731. In that text he answers someone he imagines asking, "τί οὖν φήσειεν ἄν τις· Δύο λέγεις θεούς;" (NG 3780), not Callistus. Set straight after the taunt, "Hippolytus answered" joins the two texts the way Wendland's apparatus joins them (RG 18226-18227, a modern link). Outline beat 3 and Source Sheet E3 bar that join.
- **The readers' fixes:** Trust keeps the sentence in place, introduced by "In his sermon against Noetus". The second reader uses "In his book against Noetus" and moves the taunt into paragraph 3. Judged: in either place it still follows the taunt, so a listener could still hear it as a reply. Moving the answer up to sit with his teaching, in the same text as Q2, removes the join completely, and it costs fewer words.
- **Exact change:** delete the sentence from paragraph 5. Add it to the end of paragraph 4, straight after Q2: "He imagined someone asking whether he spoke of two Gods, and answered that he spoke of one God, in two persons, with the grace of the Holy Spirit as a third."
- **Why it needs the outline:** beat 3 holds the answer next to the taunt. The change moves it into beat 2 ("His answer").
- **Do not say (add to the outline):** | "Hippolytus answered" (of *Against Noetus* 14) placed after Callistus's taunt | "He imagined someone asking whether he spoke of two Gods, and answered …", told with his teaching (beat 2), before the taunt (NG 3780-3783) |

### 3. One later bishop becomes several (Trust 3; Trust second 23). Needs the outline.

- **Sentence:** "… and later bishops there quoted him for the faith and called him bishop and martyr."
- **Evidence (both readers):** among the held witnesses, only Theodoret both quotes him and calls him bishop and martyr (JER 20467-20476; THG 32062-32063). Epiphanius only names him among those who refuted the Valentinians (EPI 32965-32966). Jerome both quotes him and calls him bishop (JERL 56674-56675; JEP 14310-14311), but Jerome was not a bishop.
- **The readers' fixes:** Trust gives "later writers there", the second reader "a later bishop there". Judged: both hold. "A later bishop there" changes one word, adds no name, and keeps the outline's role wording.
- **Exact change:** "later bishops there quoted him" becomes "a later bishop there quoted him".
- **Why it needs the outline:** beat 6, the Connection, Names ("later bishops in the East") and the Never cut list all hold the plural.
- **Do not say (add to the outline):** | "later bishops in the East quoted him … called him bishop and martyr" | "a later bishop there" (Theodoret alone does both; Epiphanius only names him) |

### 4. "Elders" is used without its plain sense the first time (Skeptic, blocking; First-time, minor).

- **Sentence:** "Twice the elders called him before the church, …"
- **Evidence:** the gloss comes only in paragraph 9, after the word has been heard twice. The first-time listener: "first heard here, glossed only in the second-last paragraph". The skeptic: "an ESL listener with no church background will not know it".
- **Judged by the bar:** this blocks. Words to explain lists presbyter, "an elder, one of the priests who led the church with its bishop". Part 2 says Do not say bars each listed word used without its plain sense the first time, and Part 1 says to honor the Do not say list. The first-time listener's MINOR is raised for that reason.
- **Exact change:** "Twice the elders called him" becomes "Twice the elders, the church's priests, called him". In paragraph 9, "whom it calls an elder, one of the priests who led the church with its bishop, were deported" becomes "whom it calls an elder, were deported". This also fixes the 35-word deportation sentence that both listeners lost (First-time §3.4, Skeptic §3.2). "The church's priests" compresses the outline's gloss and adds nothing to it. The outline holds it, so this does not need the outline.
- **Do not say (add to the outline):** | "the elders" in the opening with no gloss, and the gloss first given in beat 6 | "the elders, the church's priests," at first use; no gloss again in beat 6 |

### 5. "He wrote many other books" stands alone (First-time §4.2; Skeptic §4.1; craft check s25). Needs the outline; heart wording (report).

### 6. The Damasus verse loses the thread at the close (First-time §1, test line "why the connection matters", and §3.1; Skeptic §3.4; Trust second's live-text echo, minor, merged here). Needs the outline.

- **Sentences:** "Hippolytus, an elder, it was said, had always stayed in the split of Novatus while a tyrant's orders pressed. Damasus means Novatian, whose party split from the church at Rome in 251, refusing to take back those who had given way in persecution."
- **Evidence:** both listeners, separately, hear a contradiction. He was sent away in 235, yet he belonged to a split of 251. Neither can tell what the tie to Novatian was. The first-time listener: "how could he belong to a split that came sixteen years after he left Rome". The skeptic: "the close leaves me puzzling over the dates instead of seeing Hippolytus".
- **Judged by the bar:** this blocks, as a genuine failure to understand on one hearing, at the point where the story's link to Novatian lands. The puzzle is the record's own, since the verse is Damasus's hearsay (LP 109159-109166). Part 1 says to say plainly what is uncertain, and the outline's Connection says later writers "could not agree … whether he had been … a man who had stood in a split". Stating the gap plainly, from two dates the story already gives, adds no reason and no judgment.
- **The second reader's echo:** "Damasus means Novatian" follows the live line's frame. The outline holds the gloss but not that frame, so it is a minor finding, and it is fixed in the same sentence.
- **Exact change:** "Damasus means Novatian, whose party split from the church at Rome in 251, refusing to take back those who had given way in persecution." becomes "Novatus is his name for Novatian, whose party split from the church at Rome in 251, sixteen years after the exile, refusing to take back those who had given way in persecution."
- **Why it needs the outline:** the outline's Do not say says "Keep the gloss's 251 and add nothing to it". The researcher rewrites that line, and the outline check confirms "sixteen years after the exile" against LP 66805-66807 (235) and the census `novatianism` date (251). Ending 2 (the calendar) is not a fix: the Damasus verse is in the approved heart ("What came of him").
- **Do not say (add to the outline):** | the 251 gloss heard with nothing setting it against the exile | "… in 251, sixteen years after the exile, …"; add no reason for the gap |
