# Outline check: Hippolytus of Rome

## My own account of the heart (written before reading Outline.md)

From the census entry (`knownFor`, home `roman-church-third-century`), the source sheet and what he is known for.

- **Essence:** Hippolytus was a Greek-writing elder at Rome in the early 200s who refused to give way when the bishops Zephyrinus and Callistus taught, as he saw it, that the Father and the Son were one and the same. He answered that the Son is another beside the Father, yet God is one, and in his *Refutation of All Heresies* traced every false teaching to Greek thought, ending with Callistus, whom he also charged with forgiving too easily. In 235 he was sent into exile on Sardinia with the bishop Pontianus, and Rome kept him among its martyrs.
- **Also told:** the question (Noetus: the Father himself was born, suffered and died; the elders' answer); his own answer in *Against Noetus* (one power, "light of light"; not two Gods, but two persons); the charge "worshippers of two gods" and his reply; the *Refutation*'s plan (heresies from Greek philosophy, mysteries, astrologers), ending with Callistus and the rule on sin, all told as his enemy's account; his claim to the apostles' succession in teaching; the Easter table and many books.
- **What came of him (heaviest first):** his books kept and read, above all in the East (Eusebius at Jerusalem, Epiphanius, Theodoret's "bishop and martyr"); his teaching on Father and Son used against the teaching that they were one; Jerome's use of him, including the Easter cycle giving Eusebius the cue; kept at Rome as a martyr (calendar of 354, Damasus's verse, as hearsay). **Defended:** one God with the Son a distinct person; the faith against teachings he traced to the Greeks; a strict line on forgiveness.
- **Shape:** Teacher or thinker.
