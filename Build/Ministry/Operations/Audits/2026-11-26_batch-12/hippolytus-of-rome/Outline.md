# Outline: Hippolytus of Rome

Census id `hippolytus-of-rome`. Kind: person (`kinds/person.md`). Source codes (E3, I4 ...) are rows in `Source_Sheet.md`, which gives every file and line. Job (slate, 2026-11-27): the Greek-writing elder at Rome who attacked bishop Callistus as lax and wrong on God in his *Refutation of All Heresies*, and was exiled to Sardinia in 235 with the bishop of Rome. The quarrel is told from Hippolytus's own side; whether he led a rival church is told only as the old sources give it, crediting each (slate decision 3).

## The heart

- **The essence:** Hippolytus was a teacher in the church at Rome in the early 200s, and he wrote in Greek. (Elder or bishop: the old sources differ; beat 6 tells it by its witnesses, D2.) Against those who said that the Father himself was born and suffered, he taught that the Son was another beside the Father, and yet one God with him. He would not give way to two bishops of Rome, Zephyrinus and Callistus, on that teaching or on how far the church could forgive, and he set the quarrel down in a book against every teaching he held false. In 235 he was sent to Sardinia with Rome's bishop, and Rome kept his day among its martyrs. (E1-E5, E7, E10, E11.)
- **Also told:** what the book says: each false teaching traced to Greek philosophers, mysteries and astrologers, its last part on the teachers of his own day, Callistus among them (E7); his claim to stand among the apostles' successors (E8); his Easter table (E9); read in the East (I1).
- **What came of him:** read and quoted as a teacher of the faith, bishop and martyr, in the East and by Jerome (I1, I3); his Easter cycle the occasion of Eusebius's (I3); kept at Rome on 13 August, with a verse on his tomb by Damasus (E11, I4). Defended: one God, with the Son a distinct person (E3); the church's strictness against Callistus's forgiveness (E5).

## Openings

1. **Noetus before the elders (recommended).** A teacher from Smyrna, Noetus, said that Christ was the Father himself, so that the Father was born, suffered and died. The elders, the church's priests, called him before the church twice. He asked them what harm he was doing by glorifying Christ (narrated: the line is the Modalist Monarchianism story's quote). They answered that they too knew one God, and knew Christ, the Son who suffered and died and rose, and put him out of the church; he set up a school of his own. Then the essence, bringing in Hippolytus as the one who tells it and answered it. (E1; Hippolytus's own account, *Against Noetus* 1, Documented as his account.) It opens the question he spent his life on, in the voice of the man he opposed, and lets the other side be heard fairly.
2. **Two names on Rome's list, 235.** A list of Rome's bishops records that in 235 Pontianus, the bishop, and Hippolytus, whom it calls an elder, were deported to Sardinia, to an island it calls unhealthy (E10; narrated: the list's words were the old card's quotes). Then the essence. Weaker: it reaches ahead to the exile, and it opens on the end of the story rather than on the question he answered.

## Story beats

1. **A teacher at Rome under Zephyrinus (early 200s).** He wrote in Greek (S3). Jerome says one of his sermons shows him preaching in church with Origen present (E9; LATER, Documented as Jerome's report; undated by Jerome, so told as Jerome's report, never as "when Origen visited"). Noetus's pupils brought the teaching to Rome (E2). Hippolytus says Zephyrinus let it spread, with Callistus as his adviser (E2; his charge, in his voice). He and his side would not give way: they argued against them and stood against them for the truth (E2, S2; narrated from RG 18080-18081). Explain it: Noetus's teaching (E1). Confidence: Documented as his account.
2. **His answer.** Against Noetus he taught that the Son was another beside the Father, yet one power with him: Q2 (E3). He took his proof from Scripture, he said, and from no other source (E3, NG 3660-3661; optional clause). He imagined someone asking whether he spoke of two Gods, and answered that he spoke of one God, in two persons, with the grace of the Holy Spirit as a third (E3, NG 3780-3783; told with Q2, never as a reply to Callistus). Explain it: the Word (Words to explain). Confidence: Documented, his own words.
3. **Callistus becomes bishop.** While Zephyrinus still lived, Hippolytus says, Callistus called him and his side worshippers of two gods, because they would not go along with Callistus (E2, RG 18080-18082; narrated). When Zephyrinus died, Callistus took his place and put the teacher Sabellius out of the church's fellowship; Hippolytus says he did it out of fear of him, to clear himself of the charge he shared Sabellius's view (E4; an enemy's motive, credited). Explain it: the charge. Confidence: Documented as each is reported.
4. **The rule on sin, from his side.** Callistus, Hippolytus says, forgave every sin of the body to anyone who came over to him, and held that a bishop should not be removed even for the gravest sin (E5). Some whom Hippolytus's own side had judged and put out of the church joined him (E5, RG 18283: his own words, which show his side acting as the church). Hippolytus called Callistus's following a school, its people Callistians; they called themselves the Catholic Church (E5). Explain it: the rule, the "school", Catholic ("the church of the whole"). Confidence: Documented as his charge; what Callistus ruled, Contested (one hostile witness).
5. **His books.** The *Refutation of All Heresies* set out to show that each teaching he held false came from Greek philosophers, mysteries and astrologers, not from Scripture or from any saint's succession; its last part turned to the teachings of his own day at Rome, Callistus's among them (E7; HIP 13249, 13870 ff.). In its opening he gave his own warrant: Q3 (E8). It came down in a manuscript found on Mount Athos in 1842 and was first printed under Origen's name (I8; a modern event, told as an event; D1). He also drew up a table for finding the day of Easter on a cycle of sixteen years, and Jerome says it gave Eusebius the cue for his own cycle of nineteen (E9, I3). Eusebius's word on his many books goes to the card. Explain it: the book's plan, high priesthood, the Easter table. Confidence: Documented (his words; Eusebius); the attribution Widely Accepted (D1).
6. **Exile, 235, and his day at Rome.** In 235, Rome's list of bishops records, Pontianus, the bishop, and Hippolytus, whom it calls an elder, were deported to Sardinia (E10; the list gives no reason; narrated, never quoted). Rome's calendar of martyrs kept both on 13 August, Hippolytus on the Tiburtine Road and Pontianus in the cemetery of Callistus (E11). In the East, Eusebius wrote that he had led another church, without naming it, and Jerome that he was bishop of a city he could not learn (I6); a later bishop in the East quoted him for the faith and called him bishop and martyr (I1); Rome's own list calls him an elder (E10). All narrated. Explain it: the deportation, told as the list gives it. Confidence: Documented, each as its witness says; the bishop or elder question Contested, told by its witnesses (D2).

## What came of it

1. **Read and quoted as a teacher of the faith.** Epiphanius names him with Clement and Irenaeus among those who refuted the Valentinians; Theodoret quotes him as a holy bishop and martyr, a witness of the faith, and ranks him with Ignatius, Polycarp, Irenaeus and Justin; Jerome sends Damasus the words of Hippolytus the martyr, calls him eloquent, and says Ambrose followed him (I1, I3). Narrated, no quotation. Defended: one God, with the Son a distinct person, against Noetus (E3, I2). For the story: one clause in the story (beat 5 or 6); the rest for the card.
2. **His Easter cycle.** Jerome says it gave Eusebius the occasion for his nineteen-year cycle (I3). One clause in the story (beat 5 or 6); the rest for the card.
3. **Kept at Rome as a martyr.** The calendar (beat 6); Damasus's verse at his tomb (Ending); Prudentius's hymn with its wild horses (I5, legend: card, in the poet's voice); the cemetery restored in the 700s (I7: card).
4. Who keeps him today: no held source says. Leave it out.

## Connection to carry into the story

What passed from him was a teaching and a record. The teaching: against Noetus and those at Rome who followed him, that the Son is another beside the Father yet one power with him (E3); a later bishop in the East quoted him for the faith and called him bishop and martyr (I1). The record: the only account of Rome's quarrel under Zephyrinus and Callistus that survives is his own, told by a man who would not give way and who claimed the apostles' office for his side (E2, E5, E8). The listener needs to understand that he was not an outsider attacking the church: he claimed for his side, as heirs of the apostles' gift, a share in its high priesthood and teaching (Q3), his side put people out of the church (E5), Rome's list calls him an elder (E10), and Rome kept him among its martyrs beside the very bishop he was exiled with (E10, E11), while later writers could not agree whether he had been a bishop, an elder, or a man who had stood in a split (I4, I6). This must come through what he did and what each witness wrote, never through a "this matters because" sentence. ## Ending

1. **Recommended: Damasus at the tomb.** More than a century later, Damasus, bishop of Rome, set a verse on his tomb on the Tiburtine Road. It is said, the verse runs, that Hippolytus, an elder, had stayed in the split of Novatus while a tyrant's orders pressed; that as he went to the kingdom of the faithful, when the people asked where they should go, he told them all to follow the catholic faith; and so he earned his place as a martyr. Damasus closes by saying he tells what he has heard (I4; LP 109159-109166; narrated, never quoted, since the verse's lines were the old live story's quotes). The listener needs the gloss on Novatus. It ends on the subject, at his grave, in the voice of a later bishop who was himself unsure. Confidence: Documented as Damasus's report; its content Contested.
2. **Alternative: the calendar.** End beat 6 on the two names kept on 13 August, Hippolytus on the Tiburtine Road and Pontianus in the cemetery of Callistus (E11), and send Damasus to the card. Shorter by about 60 words; it loses the record's own uncertainty about the split.
