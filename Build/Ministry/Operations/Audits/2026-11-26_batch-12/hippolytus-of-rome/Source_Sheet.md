## Setting

**S1. Rome under three bishops.** The list of 354 gives the bishops and their years by consuls: "Calixtus ann. v m. ii d. x. fuit temporibus Macrini et Eliogabali, a cons. Antonini et Adventi (218) usque Antonino iii et Alexandro (222)" (CHR 13070-13071, OCR "11" for "ii"); Urbanus to 230; "Pontianus ann. v m. ii d. vii. fuit temporibus Alexandri" (CHR 13072-13076; the same list in LP 66800-66807). Zephyrinus came before Callistus (HIP 13802-13803, 13816; RG 18204, 18210). LATER (compiled by 354; Documented for the order). Plain words: "Rome, in the early 200s"; "when Zephyrinus died", not a year, since the list's years run by consuls and differ by a year from other reckonings.

**S2. He lived through what he told.** Of Callistus: "ἐπεὶ κατὰ τὸν αὐτὸν χρόνον ἡμῖν ἐγεγόνει" (RG 18084-18085); "inasmuch as he was born about the same time with ourselves" (HIP 13644). Of Zephyrinus and Callistus: "καίτοι ἡμῶν μηδέποτε συγχωρησάντων, ἀλλὰ πλειστάκις ἀντικαθεστώτων πρὸς αὐτοὺς" (RG 17721-17722; OCR "ovy-"); "Never at any time, however, have we been guilty of collusion with them; but we have frequently offered them opposition" (HIP 13302-13304). OWN. Documented.

**S3. A Greek writer.** The *Refutation* and *Against Noetus* survive in Greek (RG; NG). Documented. Eusebius knew his works in a church library at Aelia, that is Jerusalem: "They have been kept until our time in the library at Ælia ... Likewise also Hippolytus, who presided over another church, has left writings" (EUS 35434-35449). LATER (about 80-90 years). Documented. Duchesne's claim that Rome's clergy turned to Latin in the next generation, so his books dropped out of sight there (DUH 11728-11731), is MODERN and stays out of the story.

**S4. Tradition.** Census `roman-church-third-century`, spoken "Third-Century Rome". The sibling's approved outline (`2026-11-25_batch-11/roman-church-third-century/Outline.md`) tells Zephyrinus's two public sentences, Callistus's formula (Q1 there, barred here), the rule on forgiveness and marriage, and the exile of 235 in a clause.

## Events

**E1. Noetus and the elders (the question).** *Against Noetus* 1: Noetus of Smyrna "ἔφη τὸν χριστὸν αὐτὸν εἶναι τὸν πατέρα καὶ αὐτὸν τὸν πατέρα γεγεννῆσθαι καὶ πεπονθέναι καὶ ἀποτεθνηκέναι" (NG 3184-3185); "He alleged that Christ was the Father Himself, and that the Father Himself was born, and suffered, and died" (HIP 23131-23133). The elders summoned him "before the Church" twice; he answered, "Τί οὖν κακὸν ποιῶ δυζάζων [δοξάζων] τὸν Χριστόν;" (NG 3196); "What evil, then, am I doing in glorifying Christ?" (HIP 23161). They replied: "Καὶ ἡμεῖς ἕνα θεὸν οἴδαμεν ἀληθῶς, οἴδαμεν Χριστόν, οἴδαμεν τὸν υἱὸν παθόντα καθὼς ἔπαθεν, ἀποθανόντα καθὼς ἀπέθανεν ... καὶ ταῦτα λέγομεν ἃ ἐμάθομεν" (NG 3197-3201, OCR rough); "We too know in truth one God; we know Christ; we know that the Son suffered even as He suffered, and died even as He died, and rose again on the third day, and is at the right hand of the Father, and cometh to judge the living and the dead. And these things which we have learned we allege." (HIP 23162-23170). "Then, after examining him, they expelled him from the Church. And he was carried to such a pitch of pride, that he established a school." (HIP 23170-23173; NG 3212-3213, "ἐξέωσαν τῆς ἐκκλησίας ... διδασκαλεῖον συστῆσαι"). OWN-REPORT (Hippolytus's account of a teacher he opposed; where the elders met is not said). Documented as his account. Noetus's own side, in Hippolytus's report: he cited "I am the God of your fathers ... you shall have no other gods beside me" and "I am the first and the last" (NG 3214-3220; HIP 23177 ff.), and reasoned that if Christ is God and suffered, then the Father suffered (NG 3220-3224).

**E2. The teaching reaches Rome; he does not give way.** *Refutation* IX.7: Noetus's deacon and pupil Epigonus "τῇ Ῥώμῃ ἐπιδημήσας ἐπέσπειρε τὴν ἄθεον γνώμην", and Cleomenes strengthened it, "κατ᾽ ἐκεῖνο καιροῦ Ζεφυρίνου διέπειν νομίζοντος τὴν ἐκκλησίαν, ἀνδρὸς ἰδιώτου καὶ αἰσχροκερδοῦς" (RG 17709-17715); "At that time, Zephyrinus imagines that he administers the affairs of the Church—an uninformed and shamefully corrupt man" (HIP 13280-13289). Callistus was his adviser (RG 17717-17718; HIP 13294-13295). IX.11: Zephyrinus said in public that he knew one God, Christ Jesus, and that the Father did not die but the Son (RG 18076-18079; HIP 13619-13625; told by the sibling, not here). Then: "οὗ τὰ νοήματα γνόντες ἡμεῖς οὐ συνεχωροῦμεν, ἐλέγχοντες καὶ ἀντικαθιστάμενοι ὑπὲρ τῆς ἀληθείας" (RG 18080-18081); "And we, becoming aware of his sentiments, did not give place to him, but reproved and withstood him for the truth's sake" (HIP 13627-13633). Because all ran with him "but we did not", Callistus "ἀπεκάλει ἡμᾶς διθέους" (RG 18082), "called us worshippers of two gods" (HIP 13639-13641). OWN; hostile to Zephyrinus and Callistus. Documented as his account; his judgment of Zephyrinus is an enemy's.

**E3. His answer: another, yet one power.** *Against Noetus* 11: "ἕτερον δὲ λέγων οὐ δύο θεοὺς λέγω, ἀλλ᾽ ὡς φῶς ἐκ φωτὸς ἢ ὡς ὕδωρ ἐκ πηγῆς ἢ ὡς ἀκτῖνα ἀπὸ ἡλίου. δύναμις γὰρ μία ἡ ἐκ τοῦ παντός, τὸ δὲ πᾶν πατήρ, ἐξ οὗ δύναμις λόγος." (NG 3705-3708; OCR "ix", "Ἡ", "ἀκτῆα" for ἐκ, ἢ, ἀκτῖνα); "I do not mean that there are two Gods, but that it is only as light of light, or as water from a fountain, or as a ray from the sun. For there is but one power, which is from the All; and the Father is the All, from whom cometh this Power, the Word." (HIP 23620-23626). Ch. 8: "as far as regards the power, God is one; as regards the economy, threefold" (NG 3649-3650, "ὅσον μὲν κατὰ τὴν δύναμιν, εἷς ἐστι θεός, ὅσον δὲ κατὰ τὴν οἰκονομίαν, τριχὴς ἡ ἐπίδειξις"; HIP 23508-23514). Ch. 9, his method: "εἷς θεός, ὃν οὐκ ἄλλοθεν ἐπιγινώσκομεν, ἀδελφοί, ἢ ἐκ τῶν ἁγίων γραφῶν" (NG 3660-3661). Ch. 14, answering the charge: "τί οὖν φήσειεν ἄν τις· Δύο λέγεις θεούς; δύο μὲν οὐκ ἐρῶ θεοὺς ἀλλ᾽ ἢ ἕνα, πρόσωπα δὲ δύο, οἰκονομίᾳ δὲ τρίτην τὴν χάριν τοῦ ἁγίου πνεύματος" (NG 3781-3783); "I shall not indeed speak of two Gods, but of one; of two Persons however, and of a third economy (disposition), viz., the grace of the Holy Ghost" (HIP 23728-23731). Ch. 7, on "I and the Father are one": "are", not "am" (NG 3554-3571; HIP 23439-23450; the old live story's quote: not used). OWN. Documented.

Wendland's apparatus ties Callistus's taunt in IX.12, "Δίθεοί ἔστε", to *Against Noetus* 14, "Δύο λέγεις θεούς" (RG 18226-18227, MODERN, clarifies). The story may set the two side by side only as the texts give them, never as Wendland's link.

**E4. Callistus bishop; Sabellius cut off "from fear of me".** IX.12: after Zephyrinus's death, "νομίζων τετυχηκέναι οὗ ἐθηρᾶτο, τὸν Σαβέλλιον ἀπέωσεν ὡς μὴ φρονοῦντα ὀρθῶς, δεδοικὼς ἐμὲ καὶ νομίζων οὕτω δύνασθαι ἀποτρίψασθαι τὴν πρὸς τὰς ἐκκλησίας κατηγορίαν, ὡς μὴ ἀλλοτρίως φρονῶν" (RG 18210-18213); "he excommunicated Sabellius, as not entertaining orthodox opinions. He acted thus from apprehension of me, and imagining that he could in this manner obliterate the charge against him among the churches" (HIP 13816-13822). Callistus said to them in public, "Δίθεοί ἔστε" (RG 18216); "Ye are Ditheists" (HIP 13838-13839). OWN, hostile. Documented as his account; the motive is an enemy's. Explain it, Callistus's side as the record gives it: he held that Father and Son are one Spirit, "οὐ γάρ, φησίν, ἐρῶ δύο θεούς, πατέρα καὶ υἱόν, ἀλλ᾽ ἕνα" (RG 18267; HIP 13855-13857: the sibling's Q1, **barred** here, never quoted, at most one clause of narration); "the Father suffered along with the Son" (RG 18271; HIP 13862). Hippolytus's side: Callistus fell "now into the teaching of Sabellius, now into that of Theodotus" (RG 18274-18275; HIP 13867-13869).

**E5. The "school" and the rule on sin.** IX.12: "συνεστήσατο διδασκαλεῖον κατὰ τῆς ἐκκλησίας ... καὶ πρῶτος τὰ πρὸς τὰς ἡδονὰς τοῖς ἀνθρώποις συγχωρεῖν ἐπενόησε, λέγων πᾶσιν ὑπ᾽ αὐτοῦ ἀφίεσθαι ἁμαρτίας" (RG 18276-18278); "established a school of theology in antagonism to the Church ... saying that all had their sins forgiven by himself" (HIP 13870-13875). Those who joined him included some "ἐπὶ καταγνώσει ἔκβλητοι τῆς ἐκκλησίας ὑφ᾽ ἡμῶν γενόμενοι" (RG 18283); "some of them, in accordance with our condemnatory sentence, had been by us forcibly ejected from the Church" (HIP 13884-13886). A bishop not to be deposed "εἰ καὶ πρὸς θάνατον" (RG 18285-18286; HIP 13889-13891). His reasons, as reported: "Who art thou that judgest another man's servant?", the tares with the wheat, and the ark of Noah with "dogs, and wolves, and ravens" (RG 18329-18336; HIP 13899-13912; the ark is the old live story's quote: narrate or leave out). They "attempt to call themselves a Catholic Church" (RG 18351-18352, "καθολικὴν ἐκκλησίαν ἀποκαλεῖν ἐπιχειροῦσι"; HIP 13950-13952); called "Callistians" (RG 18393; HIP 13957-13963). OWN, hostile. Documented as his charge; what Callistus ruled is Contested (one hostile witness). McGiffert: Callistus "took the liberal view" (EUS 35521-35524) and the portrait is "greatly overdrawn" (EUS 35520-35521): MODERN, card only. The marriage rule and the "adultery and murder" charge (RG 18342-18350; HIP 13923-13952): the sibling's job; Leave out here.

**E6. Another teacher withstood.** IX.13: Alcibiades of Apamea brought a book of Elchasai to Rome promising a new forgiveness; "καὶ τούτῳ δὲ ἡμεῖς ἀντιστάντες οὐκ εἰάσαμεν ἐπὶ πολὺ πλανηθῆναι πολλούς" (RG 18456-18457); "we offered resistance to this, and did not permit many to be deceived for any length of time" (HIP 14008-14009). OWN. Documented as his account. Leave out (side story).

**E7. The *Refutation*: its plan.** Preface: the heretics' teachings come not from Scripture or from "the succession of any saint" but "ἀρχὴν μὲν ἐκ τῆς Ἑλλήνων σοφίας λαβόντα, ἐκ δογμάτων φιλοσοφουμένων καὶ μυστηρίων ἐπικεχειρημένων" (RG 1362-1366); "from the wisdom of the Greeks, from the conclusions of those who have formed systems of philosophy, and from would-be mysteries, and the vagaries of astrologers" (HIP 1166-1170); he will "compare each heresy with the system of each speculator" (HIP 1174-1176). He had once set them out briefly: "ὧν καὶ πάλαι μετρίως τὰ δόγματα ἐξεθέμεθα" (RG 1244, 1274; HIP 1074). Book IX opens on "τὰς ἐφ᾽ ἡμῖν ἐπαναστάσας αἱρέσεις" (RG 17702-17703), "in our own day" (HIP 13249). The work ends with "The Doctrine of the Truth" (HIP 15787-15789) and a call to the reader (RG 21197-21211; HIP 16150-16194). OWN. Documented. Book VI on Valentinus (`valentinus`, a link; that story's quotes are its own, inside this batch).

**E8. His claim to office.** Preface: "τὸ ἐν ἐκκλησίᾳ παραδοθὲν ἅγιον πνεῦμα, οὗ τυχόντες πρότεροι οἱ ἀπόστολοι μετέδοσαν τοῖς ὀρθῶς πεπιστευκόσιν· ὧν ἡμεῖς διάδοχοι τυγχάνοντες τῆς τε αὐτῆς χάριτος μετέχοντες ἀρχιερατείας τε καὶ διδασκαλίας καὶ φρουροὶ τῆς ἐκκλησίας λελογισμένοι οὐκ ὀφθαλμῷ νυστάζομεν οὐδὲ λόγον ὀρθὸν σιωπῶμεν" (RG 1344-1351; OCR "zal", "χαὶ" for καί, "7" for ἢ); "the Holy Spirit bequeathed unto the Church, which the Apostles, having in the first instance received, have transmitted to those who have rightly believed. But we, as being their successors, and as participators in this grace, high-priesthood, and office of teaching, as well as being reputed guardians of the Church, must not be found deficient in vigilance, or disposed to suppress correct doctrine" (HIP 1127-1140; MacMahon's note: "The common reading is in the future, but the present tense is adopted by Richter", HIP 1137-1139; Wendland prints the present, νυστάζομεν ... σιωπῶμεν, RG 1350-1351). The translator: "It is not, however, quite certain that the words refer to the episcopal office exclusively" (HIP 1131-1135, MODERN, clarifies). OWN. Documented as his words; what office he held, see D2.

**E9. Easter tables, and many books.** Eusebius VI.22: "Τότε δῆτα καὶ Ἱππόλυτος συντάττων μετὰ πλείστων ἄλλων ὑπομνημάτων καὶ τὸ Περὶ τοῦ πάσχα πεποίηται σύγγραμμα, ἐν ᾧ τῶν χρόνων ἀναγραφὴν ἐκθέμενος καί τινα κανόνα ἑκκαιδεκαετηρίδος περὶ τοῦ πάσχα προθείς, ἐπὶ τὸ πρῶτον ἔτος αὐτοκράτορος Ἀλεξάνδρου τοὺς χρόνους περιγράφει" (EUSG 4098-4102); "At that time Hippolytus, besides many other treatises, wrote a work on the passover. He gives in this a chronological table, and presents a certain paschal canon of sixteen years, bringing the time down to the first year of the Emperor Alexander." (EUS 35650-35651, 35778-35780). He lists more, "and many others which you may find preserved by many" ("πλεῖστά τε ἄλλα καὶ παρὰ πολλοῖς εὕροις ἂν σῳζόμενα", EUSG 4103-4106). LATER (about 90 years). Documented. Explain it: a table to find the date of Easter year by year from a cycle of sixteen years. That it proved wrong (HAR 12010-12012; EUS 35770-35773) is MODERN: stays out. Jerome lists nineteen works and adds a homily "On the praise of our Lord and Saviour, in which he indicates that he is speaking in the church in the presence of Origen" (JER 40696-40703; JERL 56683-56692, "in qua presente Origene, se loqui in ecclesia significat"). LATER (about 155 years). Documented as Jerome's report. Eusebius sets Origen's visit to Rome "when Zephyrinus" was bishop (EUS 34379-34392). Documented.

**E10. Exile, 235.** The list of 354, under Pontianus: "Eo tempore Pontianus episcopus et Yppolitus presbyter exules sunt deportati in Sardinia in insula nociva Severo et Quintiano cons. [235]. In eadem insula discinctus est IIII kl. octobr. et loco eius ordinatus est Antheros" (LP 66804-66808; CHR 13078 with 13107, OCR "uocina"; Duchesne: one manuscript "uocina", the other "nociua", the second right, LP 109189-109192). LATER (compiled by 354; MODERN dating of its source to the mid-third century, HAR 11924-11925, card only). Documented. Plain sense: "At that time bishop Pontianus and the elder Hippolytus were deported as exiles to Sardinia, the unhealthy island ... On that island [Pontianus] stepped down on 28 September, and Antheros was ordained in his place." The list gives no reason and no word on mines. Eusebius VI.28 says Maximinus, from hatred of Alexander's household, ordered "that only the rulers of the churches should be put to death" (EUS 36438-36440) but names no one at Rome; joining it to this exile is MODERN (LP 109186-109187; DUH 11680-11685): stays out. Duchesne writes "condamnés aux mines" (DUH 11685-11686): MODERN, stays out.

**E11. Burial and the calendar.** The book of 354, calendar of martyrs (*depositio martyrum*), under 13 August: "id. aug. Ypoliti in Tiburtina et Pontiani in Calisti" (LP 67682; CHR 12883, 12911-12913, OCR rough, the date column printed apart from the entries). "Of Hippolytus, on the Tiburtine Road, and of Pontianus, in [the cemetery] of Callistus." LATER (by 354). Documented: both kept the same day, among Rome's martyrs. How and where he died is not recorded. MODERN views: died in the Sardinian mines (EUS 35698-35700); in Sardinia or soon after at Rome, his exile being his "martyrdom" (HAR 11977-11981); bodies brought back together (DUH 11714-11720; LP 109033-109039). Inferential-Thin: card at most, as uncertain. The later *Liber Pontificalis* says Fabian brought Pontianus back by ship and buried him in Callistus's cemetery (LP 108838-108839): LATER (sixth century or after), about Pontianus only.

## Impact, heaviest first (what later witnesses carried on and used)

**I1. His books read and kept, above all in the East.** Eusebius found his writings in the Jerusalem library and says many kept them (S3, E9). Epiphanius (about 375) names him with Clement and Irenaeus among those who "wonderfully" refuted the Valentinians: "φημὶ δὲ Κλήμης καὶ Εἰρηναῖος καὶ Ἱππόλυτος καὶ ἄλλοι πλείους, οἱ καὶ θαυμαστῶς τὴν κατ᾽ αὐτῶν πεποίηνται ἀνατροπήν" (EPI 32965-32966). Theodoret (about 447) quotes "the Holy Hippolytus, Bishop and Martyr" (JER 20467-20479, 23356-23357, 27188) among the witnesses of the faith, and names him among the heresy-fighters: "καὶ Ἱππόλυτος ὁ ἐπίσκοπος καὶ μάρτυρ" (THG 32062-32063), and in a letter: "Ignatius, Polycarp, Irenæus, Justin, and Hippolytus, of whom the more part not only shine at the head of the company of bishops, but also adorn the martyr's band" (JER 35178-35181; THG 107709-107714). LATER (140-210 years). Documented. Defended: one God, with the Son a distinct person (E3), and the faith against teachings he traced to the Greeks (E7).

**I2. His teaching on Father and Son, against the teaching that they were one and the same.** *Against Noetus* is the census's named source for Modalist Monarchianism (corpus map `modalist-monarchianism.yaml`, rows at lines 103-134). Theodoret's account of Noetus draws on him (RG 20579-20580, Wendland's apparatus: "Theodoret III 3 ὁ δὲ Νοητὸς Σμυρναῖος", MODERN, clarifies). Defended: E3.

**I3. Jerome's use of him.** Writing to Damasus, bishop of Rome, Jerome gives "the words of Hippolytus the martyr" on Isaac and Rebecca: "Hippolyti martyris uerba ponemus" (JEP 14310-14311, Ep. 36.16). He names "the eloquent Hippolytus" on fasting and daily communion (JPW 16969-16970, Ep. 71.6, English only), and says Ambrose's work on the six days followed "the views of Hippolytus and Basil" (JPW 19266-19268, Ep. 84, English only). He credits Hippolytus's sixteen-year cycle with giving Eusebius "the cue" for his nineteen-year cycle: "et Eusebio, qui super eodem Pascha Canonem, decem et novem annorum circulum ... composuit, occasionem dedit" (JERL 56680-56683; JER 40693-40696). LATER (about 150 years). Documented as Jerome's reports.

**I4. Kept at Rome as a martyr, and Damasus's verse.** The calendar (E11). Damasus, bishop of Rome (census `damasus-of-rome`), set a verse at his tomb, as restored from a broken stone and printed by Duchesne (LP 109159-109166; restored letters in lower case; OCR "dùrisse" for *dixisse*):

> Hippolytus fertur PREMERENT CuM IVSSA Tyranni / presbyter in scismk SEMPER MANSISSE XOvali. / Tempore quo gladiVS SEGVIT PIA VISGERA MAfrû, / dévolus Christo, peteret cuM REGNA PlOrum, / quaesisset populus ubinam />roceDERE posset, / catholicam dùrisse fidem sequerentur ut omnes : / sic noster meruit confessus martyr ut esset. / Haec audita rEFERT DAMasus, probat omnia Christus.

Plain sense: it is said that Hippolytus, an elder, while a tyrant's orders pressed, had stayed in the split of Novatus; at the time the sword cut the mother's holy flesh, as he went to the kingdom of the faithful, the people asked where they could go, and he said all should follow the catholic faith; so he earned to be our martyr; Damasus tells what he heard; Christ tests all. LATER (about 140 years), hearsay by its own words ("fertur", "audita"). Documented as Damasus's report; its content Contested. "Novatus": the census's Novatian (`novatian`), whose party split Rome in 251 (census `novatianism`). That Damasus confused two splits (LP 109168-109174; DUH 11721-11727; CHR 13738-13740, Mommsen) is MODERN: card only. The verse's old phrasings "the split of Novatus" and "Damasus tells what he has heard" were the old live story's quotes; narrate.

**I5. Prudentius's hymn: the legend.** Prudentius (about 400), *Peristephanon* XI, held in Ruinart (PRU 23413-23417): he finds among the tombs "Invenio Hippolytum, qui quondam schisma Novati / Presbyter attigerat" (PRU 23444-23445); he tells his people "Fugite, o miseri, execranda Novati / Schismata; catholicis reddite vos populis" (PRU 23520-23521); the judge, told his name, says "Ergo sit Hippolytus, quatiat turbetque jugales, / Intereatque feris dilaceratus equis" (PRU 23673-23681), "Then let him be Hippolytus: let him shake the team, and die torn apart by wild horses", the death of Hippolytus son of Theseus in the old myth. LEGEND (about 165 years). MacMahon: the details "are not historic" (HIP 863-868); Harnack: "pure Fabel" (HAR 11982-11983): MODERN. Leave out of the story; card, in the poet's voice.

**I6. Remembered as bishop in the East, elder at Rome.** Eusebius: "ἑτέρας που καὶ αὐτὸς προεστὼς ἐκκλησίας" (EUSG 3964-3965), "who presided over another church" (EUS 35447-35449). Jerome: "Hippolytus, cujusdam Ecclesiae episcopus, nomen quippe urbis scire non potui" (JERL 56675-56676); "bishop of some church (the name of the city I have not been able to learn)" (JER 40690-40691). Theodoret: "bishop and martyr" (I1). The list of 354: "presbyter" (E10). LATER. Documented, each as its witness says. See D2.

**I7. The tomb's cemetery, centuries on.** The *Liber Pontificalis* says Pope Hadrian (772-795) restored "cymiterium beati Yppoliti martyris iuxta sanctum Laurentium, quae a priscis marcuerant temporibus" (LP 204191-204192). LATE (about 540 years). Documented as the entry says. Card at most.

**I8. The *Refutation* lost and found.** Coxe: "the discovery, at Mount Athos, in 1842, of the long-lost Philosophumena of this author ... Emmanuel Miller, published it at Oxford under the name of Origen, which was inscribed on the ms." (HIP 637-642); MacMahon: "printed at the Clarendon Press in 1851" (HIP 748). MODERN, telling a modern event: may be told as an event. The attribution: D1.

**I9. Photius.** "Read the tractate of Hippolytus, the pupil of Irenaeus, entitled Against the Thirty-two Heresies. It begins with the Dositheans, and goes down to the heresies of Noetus and the Noetians, which he says were refuted by Irenaeus in his lectures, of which the present work is a synopsis." (PHO 11632-11637). He then gives Hippolytus Ambrose's part in Origen's work (PHO 11640-11645), which Jerome gives to Ambrose (JER 40703-40708). LATER (about 600 years), confused in part. Contested. Card at most.

## Where sources disagree or come late

**D1. Who wrote the *Refutation*.** The one manuscript bore Origen's name (HIP 641-642). MacMahon lists earlier candidates: Epiphanius, Didymus, Aetius, Caius, Tertullian (HIP 759-766); "the majority of modern scholars" chose Hippolytus (HIP 803-810). McGiffert: proved "to the complete satisfaction of almost every scholar" (EUS 35745-35747). Harnack (1904) treats it as settled but reports Batiffol's doubt (HAR 11865-11874). No old source names the *Refutation* among his works; Eusebius and Jerome name an *Against All the Heresies* (EUSG 4103-4105, "Πρὸς ἁπάσας τὰς αἱρέσεις"; JER 40701). The work itself names an earlier short treatise of its author (RG 1244, 1274) and his book *On the Substance of the Universe* (RG 20882-20883; HIP 15830). MODERN; Widely Accepted among the held editors, with named nineteenth-century doubters. The story tells the find of 1842 as an event and treats the book as his; the doubt goes to the card. Flagged for Mark (Outline, gaps).

**D2. Bishop or elder; a rival church?** The old sources: his own claim to be the apostles' successor in "high-priesthood" (E8); his side "ejected" people from the church (E5); Callistus's following "attempt to call themselves a Catholic Church" (E5); Eusebius, "presided over another church", and Jerome, bishop of a city he could not learn (I6); the list of 354, "presbyter" (E10); Damasus, a presbyter who "stayed in the split of Novatus" (I4); Theodoret, bishop and martyr (I1). MODERN readings: a rival bishop at Rome (EUS 35669-35674, McGiffert; HAR 11908-11910, Harnack; DUH 11676-11685, Duchesne, "l'antipape"); bishop of Portus (Coxe and MacMahon, HIP 852-863; Mommsen, CHR 13718-13719 and 13749-13757; Pius IV's sixteenth-century inscription on the statue, HIP 616-617); "quite worthless" (EUS 35662-35663); no split at all (Batiffol, per HAR 11881-11889). Contested. Slate decision 3: the census has no line for a rival church and none is added; the story tells only what the old sources hold, crediting each.

**D3. Whether they were reconciled.** Harnack: the shared exile reconciled him with Pontianus (HAR 11970-11975); Duchesne: in the misery of the mines "finirent par se réconcilier" (DUH 11712-11714); McGiffert: "undoubtedly returned to the Church" (EUS 35686-35688). MODERN; Inferential-Thin. Only Damasus (hearsay, I4) and Prudentius (legend, I5) tell of a return, and they tie it to Novatus.

**D4. His death.** Not recorded (E11). Torn by horses (I5, legend); in the mines (MODERN). Inferential-Thin.

**D5. The statue.** Found in 1551 on the Tiburtine Road (HIP 598-602); seated figure, Easter tables on the chair, a list of works on its back (HIP 627-632); "bears no name" yet "has been shown" his (McGiffert, EUS 35729-35734); the head modern (DUH 11649-11650); raised "par les gens de sa secte" (DUH 11636-11637). MODERN identification. Card only.

**D6. Callistus's portrait.** One witness, his enemy (E2, E4, E5). Contested; MODERN judgments (EUS 35509-35521; DUH 11450-11454) card only.
