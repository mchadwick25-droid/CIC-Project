5. **Paragraph 5**

11. "When Zephyrinus died, Callistus took his place and, Hippolytus says, called him and his side worshippers of two gods, because they spoke of the Father and the Son as two." **ASSUMPTION ADDED. BLOCKING.** Two faults.
   - The reason. The source's "because" is different: RG 18081-18082, "διὰ τὸ πάντας αὐτοῦ τῇ ὑποκρίσει συντρέχειν, ἡμᾶς δὲ οὐ, ἀπεκάλει ἡμᾶς διθέους"; HIP 13633-13641, "from the fact that all consented to his hypocrisy—we, however, did not do so—and called us worshippers of two gods". No line gives "because they spoke of the Father and the Son as two". (The outline's gloss reads the word; it is not the cause the source states.)
   - The time. The taunt is told in IX.11, while Zephyrinus lived (RG 18074-18082, directly after Zephyrinus's public sayings). IX.12, after Zephyrinus's death, only refers back to it as a cause of Callistus's later teaching: RG 18216, "διὰ τὸ δημοσίᾳ ἡμῖν ὀνειδίζοντα εἰπεῖν Δίθεοί ἔστε" (HIP 13836-13839). Setting it after "When Zephyrinus died" moves it onto the wrong event (Part 1's third fault).
   - **Change:** move the taunt into paragraph 3, after sentence 9, as the source gives it: "Because he and his side would not go along, Hippolytus says, Callistus called them worshippers of two gods." Sentence 11 then reads: "When Zephyrinus died, Callistus took his place."
12. "Hippolytus answered that he spoke of one God, in two persons, with the grace of the Holy Spirit as a third." **ASSUMPTION ADDED** (a connection). **BLOCKING.** The words hold (NG 3781-3783, "δύο μὲν οὐκ ἐρῶ θεοὺς ἀλλ᾽ ἢ ἕνα, πρόσωπα δὲ δύο, οἰκονομίᾳ δὲ τρίτην τὴν χάριν τοῦ ἁγίου πνεύματος"; HIP 23728-23731). But in *Against Noetus* 14 he answers a hypothetical questioner, "τί οὖν φήσειεν ἄν τις· Δύο λέγεις θεούς;" (NG 3780), not Callistus. "Hippolytus answered", set straight after Callistus's taunt, makes it a reply to Callistus. That joins the two texts the way Wendland's apparatus does (RG 18226-18227), which the outline (beat 3) and Source Sheet E3 forbid. **Change:** "In his book against Noetus he faced the question whether he spoke of two Gods, and answered that he spoke of one God, in two persons, with the grace of the Holy Spirit as a third." Do not say line for the outline: "'Hippolytus answered' (of *Against Noetus* 14) next to Callistus's taunt | 'In his book against Noetus he faced the question whether he spoke of two Gods...'".

## The heart

Right heart for the subject. The sources show a teacher at Rome who answered Noetus's teaching with his own, would not give way to Zephyrinus and Callistus, wrote the record of the quarrel, and was exiled with Rome's bishop and kept among its martyrs. That is the essence the outline gives, and the brief tells all of it by the end of paragraph 2 (138 words), inside the first minute.
