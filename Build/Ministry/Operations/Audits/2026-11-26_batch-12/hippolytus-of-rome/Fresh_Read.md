## BLOCKING

### 1. "Novatus is his name for Novatian" (second trust reader, BLOCKING; first trust reader, MINOR 2)

**Ruling: BLOCKING.** The bar blocks "unsupported claims (a sentence that ... adds an assumption ... is one)" and "misleading implications". The sentence says, as settled, what Damasus meant by "Novatus". No held source says so. Eusebius and the Greeks call Novatian Νοουάτος (EUSG 7517, 7618; EUS 38265), but Rome's own list, from Damasus's city and time, names Novatus as a different man, the one who split Novatian off: "supervenit Novatus ex Africa et separavit de ecclesia Novatianum" (LP 66825-66826); "Novatus extra ecclesiam ordinavit Novatianum in urbe Roma" (LP 66891-66893). The identification is a reading, told as the narrator's fact.

The first trust reader saw the same evidence and marked it MINOR because "the split is the same either way". That is true of the split, and it is why the fix costs nothing, but it does not make the sentence's own claim supported. The bar blocks the unsupported claim itself, not only one that changes the story. The kit also holds that a finding either trust read makes blocks until fixed or until a third reader shows from the source that it holds; no reader has shown that Damasus meant Novatian. The first reader's optional wording is not used: the second reader's is the exact change, it is shorter, and it keeps the outline's own gloss words.

- **Old:** "Novatus is his name for Novatian, whose party split from the church at Rome in 251, sixteen years after the exile, refusing to take back those who had given way in persecution."
- **New:** "This was the split that Novatian's party made from the church at Rome in 251, sixteen years after the exile, refusing to take back those who had given way in persecution."
