- **BLOCKING 2, the Easter table.** A reason is held in old sources. It is added to beat 5 (line 59) and the Words row (line 35) as: "a day over which Christians differed, some keeping the moon's fourteenth day". It is told beside the table, never as the reason he made it, because no held source says why he made it.
  - Eusebius, *Church History* V.23.1, EUS 31136-31140: "the parishes of all Asia, as from an older tradition, held that the fourteenth day of the moon ... should be observed as the feast of the Saviour's passover"; EUS 31217-31222: "it was not the custom of the churches in the rest of the world to end it at this time". This is McGiffert's English of 1890. The Greek of book V is not held.
  - His own *Refutation* VIII.18, RG 17504-17509 ("συνιστάνουσι δεῖν τὸ πάσχα τῇ τεσσαρεσκαιδεκάτῃ τοῦ πρώτου μηνὸς φυλάσσειν ... ἐν ᾗ ἂν ἡμέρᾳ ἐμπέσῃ", with the OCR's "τεσσαρεῦκαι-δεχάτῃ" and "ἐν ἡ" set right); HIP 13066-13069, 13073-13075 ("combine (in maintaining) that Easter should be kept on the fourteenth day ... of the first month ... on whatever day (of the week) it should occur").
  - Confidence: **Documented** (Eusebius's report, about a century on; for his own day, Hippolytus's own words). "Christians", not "churches", because Hippolytus names a party in his own day, while Eusebius names churches a generation earlier. "Moon's" is Eusebius's word.
  - Story wording: "He drew up a table, on a cycle of sixteen years, for finding the day of Easter, a day over which Christians differed, some keeping the moon's fourteenth day, and Jerome says it gave Eusebius the occasion for his own nineteen-year cycle." (**plus 11 words**, counting the added "a day"). Consider breaking it into two sentences; the writer decides.
