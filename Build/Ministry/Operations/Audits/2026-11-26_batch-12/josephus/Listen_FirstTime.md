## 1. The test (person)

- **Who was this:** filled. A Jewish priest of the Temple, a commander in Galilee in the rising of 66, who gave himself up to Rome and wrote his people's history there.
- **What happened:** filled. The cave and the surrender; life at Rome; the war history, the Antiquities, the Life; the lines on John, Jesus and James.
- **What it was about:** filled. His books tell the Jews' story to Greek readers; a few lines speak of John, James and Jesus, and the line on Jesus is in doubt because the copies differ.
- **Why the connection matters:** filled, but weakly. I can say Christians kept his lines "as the word of a Jew outside their faith", and that Origen, Eusebius and Jerome give the Jesus passage differently. I cannot say what Eusebius used the lines *for*, because the forged records are not explained (see 4).

## 2. The opening

After the first three sentences I know: where (Jotapata, a walled town in Galilee), why there (the Roman general took the town), and that Josephus is a Jew going over to the Romans. Missing:
- **Who** he is: nothing says he is a priest or the commander until paragraph 2. I was not lost (the prayer makes him a Jew facing Rome), but I did not know why his surrender mattered.
- **When:** no year or century until paragraph 2 ("in 66", "in 67").

Paragraph 2 fills both at once, inside the first minute. Not BLOCKING.

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing

1. **BLOCKING (loses the thread).** "In the cave, the men with him had held out a sword." The opening already told the prayer and the surrender, and the essence already took him to Rome and to the year 93. Now the story goes back to the cave. On one hearing I could not tell whether the sword and the lots came before or after the prayer, and the surrender is heard twice (sentence 2, and "In 67 he gave himself up"). The bar counts a jump back after the essence as a failure to understand. **Change:** tell the cave once, in order: either move the sword, the lots and the last man into the opening before the prayer (keeping it short), or end the opening at the hiding and the offer, and tell the sword, the lots, the prayer and the surrender together after the essence. Do not tell the surrender twice.
2. MINOR. "Found on the third day and offered his life, he prayed in secret..." "Offered his life" can mean he offered to die. Meaning lost: the Romans offered to spare him. Say it in the active, e.g. the Romans found him and offered to spare his life (wording to be checked against the outline).
3. MINOR. "Late in that century, Jerome's Latin reads only that Jesus was believed to be the Christ." I do not know who Jerome is, or what "Jerome's Latin" is. Meaning lost: that a Christian writer gave the passage in Latin with a weaker line. Give him by role first (a Christian scholar who wrote in Latin, as the outline holds) and say he quotes the passage.
4. MINOR. "With that reading beside the passage's own words and Origen's, how much of it Josephus wrote is uncertain, and the old sources leave it open." "The passage's own words" is unclear: which words, if the copies differ? I understood the end point (no one knows how much he wrote), but not what "own words" points to. Say "the words as Eusebius gives them" if the outline holds that.

## 4. Bare statements (Part 1, Explain it)

1. **BLOCKING.** "He set the lines on John and Jesus against forged records of Pilate's dealings with Jesus, sent out, he says, in his own day with the emperor's approval." I am not told what these records said, who they were against, or why Eusebius answered them with Josephus. This is the one place the brief shows a later writer *using* the passage, which the Outside witness shape says is where the impact sits, and I cannot follow it. **Change:** say in one plain clause what the records were and why they mattered to Eusebius, as Eusebius gives it (from the outline), or cut the sentence.
2. **BLOCKING.** "...and in his Life he answered a rival historian." A quarrel named with no word on what it was about. **Change:** say in a clause what the rival charged or wrote, as the outline holds it, or cut to "and he wrote his Life," which the close then picks up.

Not bare: "charged them with breaking the law" (the act as the source gives it, with the citizens' protest beside it); the arrest and death of John (reason given: Herod's fear of a rising).

## 5. Citations read aloud

"Nearly everything known of his life comes from his own books" is the one-witness sentence, said once, early, with a fact inside it (he wrote partly to defend himself).

## 8. Pull and drift

- **Opening:** pulls me in. A man in a cave, a secret prayer and his own words make me want to know who he is.
- **First drift:** paragraph 4, "Then came the Antiquities of the Jews... and in his Life he answered a rival historian." It turns into a list of books with no moment in it, and the last clause names a quarrel I am given nothing to hold. For the reviser: attach one reason or picture to the book list, or shorten it. A smaller dip at "between a riot under Pilate and another disaster": I did not know why the placement is told, or what the other disaster was.
- **To the end:** yes. The Jesus passage and the three Christian writers who give it differently hold me, and the close on his own words about his Life is clear.
