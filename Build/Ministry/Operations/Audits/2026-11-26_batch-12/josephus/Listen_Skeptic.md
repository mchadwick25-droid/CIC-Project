## 1. The test (person kit)

- **Who he was:** filled. He was a Temple priest and Jewish commander in Galilee who surrendered to Rome and wrote at Rome.
- **What happened:** filled. Jotapata, the cave, the surrender in 67, the histories, and the lines on John, James and Jesus.
- **What it was about:** filled. A Jew outside the faith recorded John, James and Jesus, and the Jesus passage is disputed.
- **Why the connection matters:** filled, but thinly. "Christians copied those lines for centuries as the word of a Jew outside their faith, and argued over the one on Jesus." Origen, Eusebius and Jerome show the use. But the listener is never told what the argument actually is (see the Jerome and "With that reading" sentences under item 3). I can only *guess* that the doubt is whether a Jew who, by Origen's account, did not accept Jesus as the Christ could have written "This man was the Christ." I had to infer that. Part 2 says the listener should not have to.

## 2. The opening (first three sentences)

- **Who:** I have only a name. From the prayer I can tell he is a Jew going over to the Romans. I don't learn that he is a priest and the commander until sentence 5. This is not BLOCKING, because the quote tells me which side he is on and the role follows in the next paragraph.
- **Where:** given. Jotapata, a walled town in Galilee, in the north.
- **When:** missing. No year or century until "in 66" in paragraph 2.
- **Why there:** missing. Why is he in a besieged town, and why with "forty men of standing"? Sentence 5 answers it (he commanded the forces in Galilee).

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing

1. **BLOCKING:** "Late in that century, Jerome's Latin reads only that Jesus was believed to be the Christ." Who Jerome is, and what "Jerome's Latin" is (a translation? a quotation? a copy?), are both lost. This is the hinge of the story's own question, so the thread is lost. Change: "Late in that century, Jerome, a Christian scholar, gave the passage in Latin, and his version says only that Jesus was believed to be the Christ." (The wording of Jerome's role must come from the outline. If the outline lacks it, it goes back to the researcher.)
2. **BLOCKING:** "With that reading beside the passage's own words and Origen's, how much of it Josephus wrote is uncertain, and the old sources leave it open." On one hearing I cannot hold three readings and work out the conflict between them. The story's question resolves here, and the resolution is lost. Change: "So the passage as it has come down says that Jesus was the Christ. Origen wrote that Josephus did not accept him as the Christ, and Jerome's version says only that he was believed to be. How much of the passage Josephus wrote is uncertain." (This drops the doubled "and the old sources leave it open". See item 6b.)
3. MINOR: "Found on the third day and offered his life, he prayed in secret..." The meaning of "offered his life" is lost. It could mean offered to die or offered mercy. Change: "Found on the third day, and offered his life by the Romans, he prayed in secret..."
4. MINOR: "...and in his Life he answered a rival historian." Heard aloud, "in his Life" sounds like "during his life", so the book is lost. The listener only learns at the very end that the Life is a book. See item 4 for the change.
5. MINOR: "He set the lines on John and Jesus against forged records of Pilate's dealings with Jesus..." "Set against" is lost. Did he use them to disprove the records, or just print them side by side? See item 4.

## 4. Bare statements (Part 1, Explain it)

1. **BLOCKING:** "in his Life he answered a rival historian." The book is named without saying what it is (his own account of his life). The quarrel is named without saying what the rival charged or why it mattered to him. Change: say what the Life was and what he answered, as the outline gives it. Otherwise cut the clause. The ending already names the Life, and it can carry the gloss there ("his Life, the account of his own life").
2. **BLOCKING:** "forged records of Pilate's dealings with Jesus, sent out ... with the emperor's approval." The story never says what these records claimed, whose side they served, or why Eusebius answered them with Josephus. Change: say in one clause what the records were for and how Eusebius used Josephus against them, as the outline gives it. Otherwise cut the sentence to "Early in the fourth century, Eusebius of Caesarea copied all three passages into his history of the church."
3. **BLOCKING** (overlaps item 3.1): Jerome is named without his role or what his Latin is. The change is as in item 3.1.

## 5. Citations read aloud not called for

None. Every "he writes" or "he says" stands on a judgment (John "a good man"), a wonder or providence, a motive the witness gives for himself, or Eusebius's own claim about the forgeries. All are called for.

## 6. Tells Part 1 names

1. **BLOCKING (a jump back after the essence; the bar counts it as a failure to understand on one hearing):** Paragraph 3, "In the cave, the men with him had held out a sword...". Paragraph 2 has already taken me to his surrender in 67, his years at Rome and a history finished about 93. Then the story goes back into the cave. The hook may reach ahead, but the beats may not go back. Change: tell the sword, the lots and the last man straight after the opening prayer, *before* the essence paragraph. Or make the essence paragraph stop before the surrender ("...when the Jews rose against Rome in 66") and tell the rest of the cave, the surrender in 67 and Rome in order after it.
2. **BLOCKING (a summing-up contrast turn, the cousin of "not X but Y"):** "To the forty, surrender was treason and death was honor; to Josephus, in his telling, taking his own life was wrong." This is a balanced antithesis with a semicolon. It is the narrator summing up a scene the record has just shown. Change: cut it. If the outline holds his argument against taking one's own life, tell it as an act where it happened: "He argued, in his own telling, that taking one's own life was wrong, and proposed that they kill one another by lot."

### 6b. Other lines that sound machine-made (MINOR)

- "Nearly everything known of his life comes from his own books, and he wrote them partly to defend himself." The first half is a stock how-we-know line, and it arrives in paragraph 4, not early. Part 1 asks for the one-witness note to come once, early, inside a sentence about the witness. Change: fold it into the essence paragraph: "...writing his people's history, and nearly all we know of his life is from his own books, written partly to defend himself."
- "Its key word is the Christ: the Messiah, the anointed one the Jews awaited." The colon-gloss is explainer cadence. Change: "Its key word, the Christ, means the Messiah, the anointed one the Jews awaited."
- "...how much of it Josephus wrote is uncertain, and the old sources leave it open." This is a doubled hedge. It is fixed by the change in item 3.2.
- Also noted, not a finding: paragraphs 5 to 7 open "In the Antiquities", "In the same book", "Further on". This marches through the book like a report about the record. Varying one opener with time or cause would help it sound like one person telling it.

## 7. Words, runs, tone

"Another disaster" is vague filler. Say what it was, as the outline gives it, or "another trouble under Pilate".
- No runs of short one-act sentences.
- Nothing talks down.

## 8. Attention

- **Opening:** it pulls me in. The cave, the forty men and the secret prayer in his own words are strong, and nothing in them sounds generated.
- **Heart timing:** who he was and that he wrote lines on John, James and Jesus come by about word 150. "Argued over the one on Jesus" lands near word 195, about 78 seconds in. That is just inside the first minute and a half and slightly late for "the first minute". It is MINOR, and the fix in item 6.1 (trimming the essence paragraph) brings it earlier.
- **First drift:** paragraph 3, "In the cave, the men with him had held out a sword." I lose track of time, because he was already at Rome. Attention drifts again at paragraph 4's list of books. The Jesus passage quote brings me back.
- **Would I listen to the end?** Yes. But I would come away unsure what the argument over the Jesus passage actually was.
