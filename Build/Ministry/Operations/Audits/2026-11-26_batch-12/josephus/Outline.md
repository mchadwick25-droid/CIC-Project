# Outline: Josephus

Batch 12, part A. Kind: person. Shape: Outside witness. Every reference is to `Source_Sheet.md` in this folder (item numbers S, E, I, D) and through it to the file and lines.

## The heart

- **The essence:** Josephus was a priest from Jerusalem who commanded Jewish forces in Galilee when the Jews rose against Rome. In 67 he gave himself up to the Roman general Vespasian, and he lived on at Rome under the emperors' favour, writing his people's history (E1, E2, E7, S4; Documented, almost all from his own books). In his *Antiquities of the Jews*, finished about 93, a few lines tell of John the Baptist, of James "the brother of Jesus", and of Jesus himself (E11–E14; Documented in the text). Christians copied those lines for centuries as the word of a Jew outside their faith, and argued over the one on Jesus, which the Christian writers who quote it give in different words (I1–I3, D2; how much of it he wrote is **Contested**).
- **Also told:**
  - Why he wrote: to answer those who, he says, wrote falsely of the war out of flattery to Rome or hatred of the Jews (E10), to set his people's history before Greek-speaking readers (E11), and, in his *Life*, to defend himself against a rival historian (Witness kinds; JW 4614–4632). Documented as his own words.
  - What the three passages say, each as short as it is: John, a good man in Josephus's words, killed by Herod for fear of a rising (E12); James, stoned on the high priest's charge of breaking the law, and the high priest removed for it (E13); Jesus, about a hundred words that call him wise, a doer of astonishing deeds, crucified under Pilate, and "the Christ" (E14). Documented in the text.
  - The doubt, as the old sources hold it: the passage says "This man was the Christ"; Origen, who read the *Antiquities*, says Josephus did not accept Jesus as the Christ; Jerome's Latin says only that he was believed to be the Christ (D2). Contested.
- **What came of him** (Part 2's What came of it, below), with what he defended marked.

## Setting

- **Time:** the first century. Born 37 (S2; Widely Accepted). The rising, 66 (E1); Jotapata, 67 (E2); freed, 69 (E7); Jerusalem falls, 70 (E8); the *Antiquities* finished about 93 (E11). Plain words: "in the first century", then the years as they come.
- **Place:** Jerusalem (S1), Galilee, "the north of the country" (E1), then Rome (S4).
- **Tradition:** none. He was a Jewish priest and historian, outside the church (S5). Plain words: "a Jewish historian". The church comes in only through what Christians did with his lines.

## Words to explain

The first time each is heard, worked into the sentence.

- **priest:** a priest of the Temple in Jerusalem, by birth (S1). Not a Christian minister.
- **Galilee:** "the north of the country".
- **Jotapata:** "a walled town in Galilee" (E2).
- **Vespasian, Titus:** "the Roman general Vespasian", later emperor; "his son Titus" (E3, E6).
- **Antiquities of the Jews:** "his history of the Jewish people, from the creation to his own day, in twenty books" (E11). The title alone puzzles: give the gloss once.
- **the Christ:** "the Christ, the Messiah, the anointed one the Jews awaited" (E14; JW-ed 139288–139290). Needed before Quote 2: the whole argument turns on it.
- **Greeks** (in Quote 2): non-Jews of the Greek-speaking world; Whiston's English says "Gentiles" (E14). Give the plain sense alongside the quote.
- **sanhedrin:** say "a council of judges" (E13; Whiston's "sanhedrim of judges").
- **Sadducees:** one of the Jewish schools of his day (S3), harsher judges than the rest, Josephus says (E13).
- **high priest:** "the chief priest of the Temple".
- **baptism** (John's): Do not give it the Christian sense of a washing away of sins. Josephus says it was for the cleansing of the body, the soul already made clean by right living.
- **minister** (Whiston's word in Quote 1): rendered 'servant sent by you'. Its church sense misleads.
- **forged acts** (Eusebius): "forged records of Pilate's dealings with Jesus, sent across the empire with the emperor's approval for schoolboys to learn by heart" (I1; EH 47945–47951).
- **Church History:** "his history of the church" (I1).

## Shape

**Outside witness.** Who he was: a priest who fought Rome and went over to it. Why he wrote: to set the record straight, to explain his people to Greek readers, and to defend himself. Exactly what he wrote of the Christians: three short passages, inside twenty books about his own people. What they can and cannot tell us: the John and James lines Origen already knew; the one on Jesus comes down through old writers who word its key clause differently. The trap: making him a friend of the church because of that clause, or a foe because both sides called him a traitor. He was neither. Say plainly how short the passages are.

## Openings

1. **(Recommended) At the walls of Jerusalem, 70.** The Roman commander Titus sent Josephus to speak to the defenders in their own language, hoping a countryman might persuade them (JW 121492–121495). He went round the wall, out of range of their weapons but within hearing, and begged them to spare themselves, their country and their Temple (JW 121497–121502). The Jews inside wanted him punished; the Romans, whenever they lost a fight, blamed his treachery and called for his death as a traitor (JW 5336–5345). Brings the name in at once, and sets the story's question: traitor, or something else. The hook reaches ahead; the beats do not tell the walls again.
2. **In the cave at Jotapata, 67.** Forty men in a hidden den, the Romans at the mouth, and his secret prayer (E3, E4; Quote 1). Use this only if the writer moves Quote 1 into the opening; then beat 2 starts after the prayer.

## Story beats

After the opening and the essence (about 130 words, the first minute), in time order.

1. **The priest and the rising (66).** Born in 37 to a family of priests in Jerusalem, of the first of the priestly families on his father's side and of the old royal line on his mother's (S1, S2; Documented as his claim). When the Jews rose against Rome, the leaders in Jerusalem sent him to Galilee. His own two books differ on why: the *Life* says to persuade the rebels to lay down their arms and wait on Rome; the *War* names him general of both Galilees (E1, D1). Credit each book. Confidence: Documented (the command); Contested (his purpose).
2. **The fall of Jotapata and the cave (67).** Jotapata fell to Vespasian; Josephus hid in a den with forty men of standing (E2, E3). Found on the third day, he was offered his life through a Roman officer he knew (E3). He says he remembered dreams in which God had shown him the Jews' coming disasters and the fate of the Roman emperors, and prayed in secret (E3, E4; his claim, in his voice). **Quote 1.** The men with him held out a sword: die as general of the Jews, or die as a traitor to them; they rushed at him and called him a coward (E5). He proposed that they kill one another by lot. He was left last with one other man, by chance or by God's providence, he writes, and persuaded him to live (E5). Explain it: to the forty, surrender was treason and death was honour; to Josephus, suicide was wrong and he went over as God's servant (E4, E5). Confidence: Documented as his account; the only account.
3. **The prediction, and freedom (67–69).** Brought before Vespasian, he asked to speak alone and told him that he, and his son with him, would be emperor (E6). Vespasian thought it a trick to save his life and kept him in chains (E6). Suetonius, a Roman writer of the next generation, also records the prediction (E6; Documented, two witnesses). When Vespasian was made emperor in 69 he remembered it and had the chain cut through (E7). Confidence: Documented (the prediction, two witnesses; the release, his account).
4. **Rome and the books.** After Jerusalem fell he went to Rome, where Vespasian gave him rooms in his old house, citizenship and a yearly pension (S4). Nearly everything known of his life comes from his own books, and he wrote them partly to defend himself (Witness kinds; JW 4614–4632): say so here, once, inside this sentence about his books. He wrote the *War* first in his own language, then in Greek, because others, he says, wrote falsely of it out of flattery to Rome or hatred of the Jews (E10). Then the *Antiquities*, his people's history for Greek readers, finished in Domitian's thirteenth year, when he was fifty-six: about 93 (E11; Widely Accepted). Confidence: Documented.
5. **John.** In it, Josephus tells that some Jews saw a defeat of Herod's army as God's punishment for killing John, called the Baptist, a good man, he writes, who urged the Jews to live justly and to come to his washing (E12). Herod, he says, feared that John's hold on the crowds might lead to a rising, so he had him imprisoned and killed (E12). Explain it: John's death, Herod's fear of a rising (E12). Confidence: Documented in the text.
6. **The passage on Jesus.** In the same eighteenth book, between a riot under Pilate and another disaster, stands a passage on Jesus: about a hundred words (E14). **Quote 2.** It goes on: when Pilate, at the urging of the leading men, had him crucified, those who loved him did not leave him; it says he appeared to them alive on the third day, as the prophets had foretold; and the Christians, named after him, have not died out (E14; the wonder stays in the passage's voice). Explain "the Christ" before the quote (Words to explain). Confidence: the text as printed, Documented; how much is his, Contested (D2).
7. **James.** In the twentieth book, the high priest, a Sadducee, with one Roman governor dead and the next still on the road, called a council of judges and brought before them **Quote 3** and some others, charged them with breaking the law, and had them stoned (E13). The fairest-minded citizens protested that he had no right to call the court, and the king removed him after three months (E13). Explain it: James's death, the high priest's charge of law-breaking against the citizens' charge that the high priest broke it (E13). Confidence: Documented in the text.

## What came of it

Ranked heaviest first; told in time order (Origen, Eusebius, Jerome). The slate makes Origen and Eusebius a clause each.

1. **Eusebius of Caesarea copied all three passages into his history of the church** (I1). He set the passages on John and Jesus, as the word of a Hebrew historian, against forged records of Pilate's dealings with Jesus that, he says, had been sent out in his own day with the emperor's approval for schoolboys to learn by heart (I1; EH 9242–9246, 47945–47951). **Defended:** Jesus, whom the forged records were 'full of every kind of blasphemy against' (EH 47945–47946), against those forgeries. One clause, credited to Eusebius. Documented.
2. **Origen, a Christian scholar of the third century, knew the lines on John and the phrase on James** ("the brother of Jesus called Christ", OMG 3729–3730), and wrote that Josephus did not accept Jesus as the Christ (I2; CC 37933–37934; OMG 3731–3733). In the passages where he cites Josephus, he quotes nothing of a passage on Jesus (I2, D2). One clause. Documented.
3. **The old writers who quote it word the key clause differently.** In Jerome's Latin, near the end of the fourth century, the line reads that Jesus was believed to be the Christ (I3; told in plain words, not as a quote). A twelfth-century Syriac chronicle, by Michael, patriarch of Antioch, reads that people thought he was the Messiah (I7; by role, Cut first). So how much of the passage Josephus wrote is uncertain, and the old sources leave it open (D2; Contested). This is the scene that tells the doubt: the three readings side by side, each credited.
4. Sozomen in the fifth century read it as Josephus all but proclaiming Christ to be God (I4). Card.
5. A martyr's legend has a priest quote it to the emperor Diocletian as proof that Christ was God (I5). Leave out.

**What he defended:** his own name, in his *Life*, against a rival's falsehoods (JW 4614–4632); his people, set before Greek readers (E11). Both told in beat 4.

## Quotes (3)

1. **The prayer in the cave.** Rendering: "I give them my hands willingly, and I am content to live. And I declare openly that I do not go over to the Romans as a deserter from the Jews, but as a servant sent by you." Rendered from Whiston's English (E4; JW 110852–110856); the Greek is not held. Stands on: English printed before 1931 (Whiston's translation, this printing 1859). "Them" is the Romans: the narrator sets up the sentence so this is plain. "Minister" rendered "servant sent by you". Job: his own answer to the charge of treason that both sides made, in the moment he made the choice. This is the story's question in his words.
2. **The passage on Jesus, its first four sentences.** Rendering: "About this time there lived Jesus, a wise man, if one ought to call him a man. For he did astonishing deeds, and taught people who gladly receive the truth. He won over many Jews, and many of the Greeks as well. This man was the Christ." Rendered from the Greek as two later Christian books quote it (E14; JVL 50993–51005; ADON 80255–80262; "Ὁ Χριστὸς οὗτος ἦν" also clean at JVL-ed 50948–50949). Stands on: an original in two damaged held prints that confirm each other on these sentences, and English printed before 1931 (Whiston, JW 81405–81411; McGiffert, EH 9228–9232) that confirms them. No edition of Josephus's Greek is held. Plain sense of "Greeks" alongside: non-Jews. Job: the disputed words themselves, so the listener hears exactly what the argument is about.
3. **James.** Rendering: "the brother of Jesus who was called Christ, James by name". Rendered from the Greek of Eusebius's quotation, given in his editor's note (E13; EH-ed 13596–13602), with Origen's Greek for the same phrase (OMG 3729–3730). Stands on: an original in a clean held print, which a second held print confirms; Whiston's English, "the brother of Jesus, who was called Christ, whose name was James" (JW 91005–91009), agrees. Job: the plain way a Jewish writer names Jesus here, "who was called Christ", heard against Quote 2's "This man was the Christ".

None of these is used in another story on the tree (checked against every census entry's text, 2026-11-27). Reuse: none.

## Connection to carry into the story

What passed from Josephus to the church was three short passages in a Jewish history, which Christians copied and quoted as testimony from a Jew outside their faith (I1, I2). The listener needs three things. First, why Christians wanted it: here a Jewish priest, no Christian, wrote of John, James and Jesus, so Eusebius could set his words against forged records of Jesus sent out with an emperor's approval (I1). Second, what was argued, from the old sources alone: the passage says "This man was the Christ", yet Origen, who read the book, says Josephus did not accept Jesus as the Christ, and Jerome's Latin reads that he was believed to be the Christ (I2, I3, D2). Third, that Origen already knew the lines on John and James, while he quotes nothing on Jesus (I2). Tell it through what Origen, Eusebius and Jerome did with the text. No "this matters because" sentence.

## Ending

1. **(Recommended) His own last word on himself.** The *Life* ends with the emperors' favour, and Domitian's punishment of his Jewish accusers (S4), and then: "this is the account of the actions of my whole life; and let others judge of my character by them as they please" (JW 5484–5487). Tell it in the narrator's words, that he left others to judge his character from his life as they pleased (not a fourth quote). It closes on him, and answers the opening without a moral.
2. Close on the *Antiquities*' own last lines: twenty books, finished in the thirteenth year of Domitian and the fifty-sixth of his life (E11). Use only if beat 4 does not already carry them.

## Do not say

- Any phrase in quotation marks in the story other than Quotes 1 to 3 → narrator's words, no quotation marks. Wording in quotation marks elsewhere in this outline is a suggested gloss or plain word, not a quote.
- "He was a traitor" or "he betrayed his people" in the narrator's voice → "both sides called him a traitor" (E5, E8), and his own "servant" (Quote 1).
- "He foresaw" or "he prophesied" Vespasian's rise → "he told Vespasian he would be emperor"; the dreams are "he says" (E3, E6).
- "his son after him" → "he, and his son with him" (JW 111188–111190).
- "The forty killed themselves" → they killed one another by lot (E5).
- "He was the last survivor" or "the only survivor" → he was left last with one other man, and persuaded him to live (E5).
- "Governor of Galilee" → "commander in Galilee" (E1); his purpose there is told from both his books or not at all.
- "Josephus believed Jesus was the Christ" or "confessed Christ" → "the passage says, 'This man was the Christ'" (Quote 2), with Origen's word beside it (I2).
- "A Christian added the passage", "it is a forgery", "scholars agree" or "most historians" → none of these is in an old source; say only that the old writers who quote it word it differently and how much he wrote is uncertain (D2).
- "The emperor ordered the schoolboys" → "sent out with the emperor's approval" (EH 47945–47951).
- "Eusebius was the first to quote it" → "Eusebius copied it into his history"; Origen's held works quote nothing of it (I1, I2).
- "Christ rose from the dead, Josephus says" → "it says he appeared to them alive on the third day" (E14).
- "Herod killed John for condemning his marriage" → Josephus gives fear of a rising (E12).
- "John baptized for the forgiveness of sins" → "Josephus says the washing was for the cleansing of the body, the soul already made clean by right living (E12)".
- "In 62" (James's death) → "with one Roman governor dead and the next on the road" (E13).
- "Josephus praised James's justice" → leave out; Origen says it of a line not in our text (D3).
- "Josephus says Jerusalem fell because of James" → the held text does not say it (D3). Leave out.
- "The Pharisees killed Christ" (Jerome's addition) → leave out (D4).
- "Testimonium Flavianum", "interpolation", "manuscripts" → "the passage on Jesus", "the old writers who quote it".
- "Jerome's copy" or "Jerome had a different text" → "Jerome's Latin reads" (JVL 50907).
- "A statue at Rome" → leave out (D6).
- "He died in Rome about 100" → no old source gives it; leave out (D7).
