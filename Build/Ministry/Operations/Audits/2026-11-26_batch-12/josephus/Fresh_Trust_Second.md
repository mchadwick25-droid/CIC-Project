## Sentence by sentence

1. "...Josephus hid in a cave with forty leading men." HELD. "here he met with forty persons of eminence that had concealed themselves" (JW 110740–110742).
2. "When the Romans found him on the third day and promised him his life, he prayed in secret, telling God he would surrender." HELD on the facts: "on the third day ... he was discovered" (JW 110754–110756); the tribunes "gave him assurances that his life should be preserved" (JW 110763–110765); "he put up a secret prayer to God" (JW 110845–110846). Echo finding below.
3. "In 67 he gave himself up to Vespasian, and lived on at Rome under the emperors' favor." SUMMARY HOLDS. Jotapata taken in Nero's thirteenth year (JW 110707–110710); at Rome, Vespasian's house, citizenship, pension, and the favour of Titus and Domitian (JW 5403–5482). The outline's essence holds the wording.
4. "In his people's history, finished there about 93, ..." SUMMARY HOLDS. "the 13th year of the reign of Caesar Domitian" (JW 91662–91666); his *Life*, which closes the *Antiquities*, has him at Rome under Domitian (JW 5468–5482). "There" (Rome) is a fair reading of the two, not stated in one line.
5. "Back in the cave, after his prayer, the men with him held out a sword." HELD. After the prayer, "those Jews who had fled with him ... came about him in a body" (JW 110857–110860): "We will lend thee our right hand and a sword" (JW 110897–110898).
6. "He proposed that they draw lots, each to be killed in turn by the next." HELD. "He whom the lot falls to first, let him be killed by him that hath the second lot" (JW 111107–111110).
7. "At Rome he wrote the books that tell nearly everything known of his life." SUMMARY HOLDS. "Afterward, I got leisure at Rome; ... I composed the history of those transactions" (JW 131974–131979); the *Life* written under Domitian at Rome (JW 5468–5482). Outline beat 4 and its Do not say line hold "nearly everything known of his life".
8. "The first was a history of the war in Greek, because others, he says, had written falsely of it, out of flattery to Rome or hatred of the Jews." **SOFTENED OR CHANGED. BLOCKING.** The reason is HELD (JW 91740–91743). But Josephus says the Greek *War* was not his first version: "to translate those books into the Greek tongue, which I formerly composed in the language of our country" (JW 91748–91751). Outline beat 4: "He wrote the *War* first in his own language, then in Greek" (Cut first 17 leaves out "first in his own language", but gives no warrant for calling the Greek book the first). v1 said "At Rome he wrote a history of the war in Greek". **Change:** "The first was a history of the war in Greek, because" → "He wrote a history of the war in Greek, because".
9. "Then came the Antiquities of the Jews, ... before Greek-speaking readers." HELD. "it will appear to all the Greeks worthy of their study" (JW 5546–5548); "20 books" (JW 91628–91630).
10. "In his Life, a book on his own life, he defended himself against a rival historian who, he says, had written falsely of him." HELD. Justus "falsified in what he related about me ... I am under a necessity to make my defence" (JW 4614–4632).
11. "...who urged the Jews to deal justly with one another and to honor God." SUMMARY HOLDS. "commanded the Jews to exercise virtue, both as to righteousness toward one another, and piety toward God" (JW 82090–82093). The outline holds the wording.
12. "...between a riot under Pilate, the Roman governor of Judea, and another disaster, stands a passage of about a hundred words on Jesus." HELD. The riot (JW 81390–81403); "another sad calamity" (JW 81423–81424); Pilate as governor of Judea (EH 8924–8925).
13. "Its key word, the Christ, means the Messiah, the anointed one the Jews awaited." SUMMARY HOLDS. A plain gloss of a word, as the outline's Words to explain give it ("the Christ of God, or the true Messias of the Jews", JW-ed 139288–139290).
14. "He set the lines on John and Jesus, a Jew's own word, against forged records of Pilate's dealings with Jesus, written against him and sent out, he says, in his own day with the emperor's approval." HELD. "an historian, who is one of the Hebrews themselves ... who have forged the acts against them" (EH 9242–9246); "recently given currency to acts against our Saviour" (EH 8928–8932); "forged Acts of Pilate and our Saviour full of every kind of blasphemy against Christ, they sent them with the emperor's approval" (EH 47920, 47945–47951).
15. "Late in that century, another Christian writer, Jerome, quoted the passage in Latin, and his Latin says only that Jesus was believed to be the Christ." HELD. "He wrote also concerning the Lord after this fashion: 'In this same time was Jesus ...' ... and was believed to be Christ" (JV 39724–39729); Latin "et credebatur esse Christus" (JVL 50907).
16. "The passage itself says he was the Christ, and Origen wrote that Josephus did not accept him as the Christ, so how much of it Josephus wrote is uncertain." **SOFTENED OR CHANGED. BLOCKING.** No copy of Josephus's own text is held; "He was the Christ" is the reading of the later Christian books that quote it, Eusebius first among the held (EH 9232; the Greek in JVL 50993–51005 and ADON 80255–80262). Jerome's Latin is a copy of the same passage, so "the passage itself" sets one copy's wording above another's as Josephus's own. The sheet: "the old copies themselves differ at the very clause" (D2); the outline: "the three readings side by side, each credited". v1 credited the readings ("the passage's own words"). The Origen clause is HELD (OMG 3731–3733; CC 37933–37934). **Change:** "The passage itself says he was the Christ" → "The copies in Greek, Eusebius's among them, say he was the Christ".
