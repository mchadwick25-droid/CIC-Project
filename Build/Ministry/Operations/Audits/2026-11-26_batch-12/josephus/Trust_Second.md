## Blocking

**1. Para 4, s1.** "Nearly everything known of his life comes from his own books, and he wrote them partly to defend himself." **SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** The first half holds: Suetonius is the only outside witness to any event of his life (SUET 36617–36619), and the rest is his own *Life* and *War*. But the old sources give self-defence as the purpose of one book only, the *Life*: Justus "falsified in what he related about me ... being belied by him, I am under a necessity to make my defence" (JW 4627–4630). For the *War* he gives a different reason, the false accounts of others (JW 91740–91743), and for the *Antiquities* a different one again, the Greek readers (JW 5546–5551). "He wrote them partly to defend himself" gives all his books a motive the source gives for one. The outline holds the wording (beat 4), but the source does not. **Change:** "Nearly everything known of his life comes from his own books." Then in para 4, s3, change "and in his Life he answered a rival historian" to "and in his Life he defended himself against a rival historian." This keeps the one-witness sentence and the self-defence, and puts each where the source puts it. **Do not say (for the outline):** "He wrote his books to defend himself" → only the *Life* is a defence, against a rival historian (JW 4614–4632).

**2. Para 5, s1.** "...John, called the Baptist, a good man, he writes, who urged the Jews to live justly." **SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** Source: Herod "slew him, who was a good man, and commanded the Jews to exercise virtue, both as to righteousness toward one another, and piety toward God, and so to come to baptism" (JW 82089–82094; Eusebius's English: "a good man and one who exhorted the Jews to come and receive baptism", EH 9149–9150). Leaving out the washing is the outline's Cut first 13. But "live justly" keeps only half of what John urged: the duty to God is gone, and John becomes a teacher of ethics alone. The meaning is not intact. **Change:** "...a good man, he writes, who urged the Jews to deal justly with one another and to honor God." (+7 words.) **Do not say (for the outline):** "urged the Jews to live justly" alone → "to deal justly with one another and to honor God" (JW 82091–82093).

## Summary holds (not blocking)

- **Para 1, s2.** "Found on the third day and offered his life, he prayed in secret, telling God he would surrender to the Romans." "on the third day ... he was discovered" (JW 110754–110756); the tribunes promised him his life (JW 110757 onward); "he put up a secret prayer to God ... I willingly give them my hands" (JW 110844–110851). A fair compression.
- **Para 2, s2.** "In 67 he gave himself up to Vespasian, and lived on at Rome under the emperors' favor, writing his people's history." Jotapata fell "in the thirteenth year of the reign of Nero" (JW 110707–110709; 67, Widely Accepted); "he complied with Nicanor's invitation" (JW 110858); Vespasian "gave me an apartment in his own house ... and gave me an annual pension", Titus "kept up the same respect", Domitian "still augmented his respects" (JW 5405–5474).
- **Para 2, s4.** "Christians copied those lines for centuries as the word of a Jew outside their faith, and argued over the one on Jesus, which the Christian writers who quote it give in different words." Eusebius: "an historian, who is one of the Hebrews themselves" (EH 9242–9243). The readings differ: "He was the Christ" (EH 9232), "credebatur esse Christus" (JVL 50907), "On pense que c'était le Messie" (MS 11233). The argument is in the old sources: Origen says Josephus did "not accept our Jesus as Christ" (OMG 3731–3732), while Sozomen says he "all but proclaims" Christ to be God (SOZ 26287–26304). The later open dispute is an event in the record: an old editor's note in JVL says "learned men" hold that "ὁ Χριστὸς οὗτος ἦν" is "non esse intelligenda ut sonant" (JVL-ed 50946–50949), and a 1688 English tract argues with Cardinal Bellarmine over the passage (`bellarmine_notes-of-the-church_1688.txt` 17921–17935). The sentence holds. It does not say what the argument was about. Para 9 tells that.
- **Para 3, s2.** "He could die as general of the Jews, they said, or as a traitor to them, and they rushed at him and called him a coward." Source: "if thou wilt die willingly, thou wilt die as general of the Jews; but if unwillingly, thou wilt die as a traitor to them" (JW 110898–110901); after his speech "they ran upon him with their swords ... and called him a coward" (JW 111066–111069). The sentence drops "willingly / unwillingly", but para 3, s5 makes the choice plain. Optional, not blocking: "He could die by his own will as general of the Jews, they said, or by theirs as a traitor to them..."
- **Para 3, s5.** "To the forty, surrender was treason and death was honor; to Josephus, in his telling, taking his own life was wrong." "the glory of our forefathers may not be tarnished" (JW 110894–110896); his speech: "why are we so earnest to kill ourselves" (JW 110913–110914, onward).
- **Para 4, s2.** "At Rome he wrote a history of the war in Greek, because others, he says, had written falsely of it, out of flattery to Rome or hatred of the Jews." "Afterward, I got leisure at Rome ... I made use of some persons to assist me in learning the Greek tongue, and by these means I composed the history" (JW 131974–131978); "false accounts of things, and this either out of a humour of flattery to the Romans, or of hatred toward the Jews" (JW 91740–91743). The Greek was a translation of a first version "in the language of our country" (JW 91750–91751); the outline cut that (Cut first 17). The compression is fair.
- **Para 6, s5.** "The passage goes on: Pilate, at the urging of the leading men, had him crucified; he appeared alive on the third day; and the Christians, named after him, have not died out." "when Pilate, at the suggestion of the principal men among us, had condemned him to the cross ... he appeared to them alive again the third day ... the tribe of Christians, so named from him, are not extinct at this day" (JW 81411–81421; EH 9234–9240). The wonder stays in the passage's voice.
- **Para 7, s3.** "The fairest-minded citizens protested that he had no right to call the court." "those who seemed the most equitable of the citizens ... disliked what was done"; "it was not lawful for Ananus to assemble a sanhedrim without his consent" (JW 91013–91041).
- **Para 9, s2.** "With that reading beside the passage's own words and Origen's, how much of it Josephus wrote is uncertain, and the old sources leave it open." The judgment rests on the old witnesses side by side: "Ὁ Χριστὸς οὗτος ἦν" (ADON 80262), "οὐ καταδεξάμενος εἶναι Χριστὸν" (OMG 3732), "credebatur esse Christus" (JVL 50907). The outline gives Contested (D2), and no modern view is told.
- **Para 10.** "At the close of his Life, Josephus called it the account of his whole life, and left others to judge his character by it, as they pleased." Source: "this is the account of the actions of my whole life; and let others judge of my character by them as they please" (JW 5482–5485). The source has "them", the actions; the story has "it", the account. The meaning is close. Optional, not blocking: "called it the account of the actions of his whole life, and left others to judge his character by them, as they pleased."

## The heart

It is the right heart. This is an outside witness: three short passages in a Jewish history, and their long use and dispute among Christians (E11–E14, I1–I3, D2). The cave scene carries the trap the kind kit names. He is neither friend nor foe: the men call him a traitor, and he calls himself God's servant. That rests on the only account, his own (JW 110845–111133).

Timing: the essence runs from word 88 to word 190. Who he was and what he did are told by word 135, and his lines on John, James and Jesus by word 159. The clause on what Christians did with them ends at word 190, about 75 seconds in at this brief's pace. That is at the edge of "about 150 words", and I count it as on time. I do not block for it. The opening (87 words, about 3 sentences, ending on Quote 1, which is never cut) is why the essence comes later than the outline's 130.
