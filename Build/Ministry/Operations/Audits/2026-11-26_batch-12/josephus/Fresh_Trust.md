## SUMMARY HOLDS (with evidence; no change needed)

**(2)** "When the Romans found him on the third day and promised him his life, he prayed in secret, telling God he would surrender."
- JW 110754–110758: "on the third day ... he was discovered. Whereupon Vespasian sent ... two tribunes" to promise his life.
- JW 110844–110845: "he put up a secret prayer to God".
- The sentence leaves out the third tribune, Nicanor, and the dreams. Its force is intact.

**(4)** "Josephus was a priest of the Temple in Jerusalem, from a priestly family, who commanded Jewish forces in Galilee when the Jews rose against Rome in 66."
- JW 1385–1387: "descended all along from the priests".
- JW 106926–106927: general "of both the Galilees".
- JW 92010: "the war broke out in the twelfth year of Nero". The year 66 is the Widely Accepted reckoning of that year, so it may be told plainly under Part 1.

**(5)** "In 67 he gave himself up to Vespasian, and lived on at Rome under the emperors' favor."
- JW 110708–110710: Jotapata fell in "the thirteenth year of the reign of Nero". The year 67 is Widely Accepted.
- JW 5404–5413 and 5468–5482: Vespasian, then Titus, then Domitian favoured him at Rome.

**(6)** "In his people's history, finished there about 93, a few lines tell of John the Baptist, of James, the brother of Jesus, and of Jesus himself."
- JW 91662–91666: "the 13th year of the reign of Caesar Domitian". Its date is Widely Accepted, "about 93".
- "There" (Rome): the *Life*, printed as the close of the *Antiquities* (JW 5487–5489, "do I dedicate all this treatise of our Antiquities"), places him at Rome under Domitian (JW 5404–5482).
- The three passages: JW 82087 onward (John), 81405 onward (Jesus) and 91003 onward (James).

**(7)** "Christians copied those lines for centuries as the word of a Jew outside their faith, and argued over the one on Jesus, which the Christian writers who quote it give in different words."
- Copied as a Jew's word: EH 9242–9243, "an historian, who is one of the Hebrews themselves".
- Read in different ways: SOZ 26287–26304 says he "all but proclaims that Christ ... is God". A later Christian answer to Cardinal Bellarmine's use of the passage, in `bellarmine_notes-of-the-church_1688.txt` 17920–17936, argues over what "the Christ or Messias" in it means.
- Different words: EH 9232 ("He was the Christ") against JVL 50907 ("credebatur esse Christus").

**(9)** "He could die as general of the Jews, they said, or as a traitor to them, and they rushed at him and called him a coward."
- JW 110898–110901: "if thou wilt die willingly, thou wilt die as general of the Jews; but if unwillingly, thou wilt die as a traitor to them".
- JW 111066–111069: they "ran upon him with their swords ... and called him a coward".
- His speech between the two is skipped. The "and" compresses time but adds no cause.

**(12)** "To the forty, surrender was treason and death was honor; to Josephus, in his telling, taking his own life was wrong."
- JW 110896–110901: "the glory of our forefathers may not be tarnished", and "traitor".
- JW 111060–111062: "these ... motives did Josephus use to these men, to prevent their murdering themselves".
- "In his telling" credits him.

**(13)** "At Rome he wrote the books that tell nearly everything known of his life."
- JW 131974–131978 (*Against Apion*): "Afterward, I got leisure at Rome ... and by these means I composed the history".
- "Nearly everything known": the only outside witness to any event of his life is Suetonius on the prediction (`suetonius_lives-of-the-caesars-loeb_rolfe1914.txt` 36617–36619). Every other line of his life here is his own.

**(14)** "The first was a history of the war in Greek, because others, he says, had written falsely of it, out of flattery to Rome or hatred of the Jews."
- JW 91739–91751: "given false accounts of things, and this either out of a humour of flattery to the Romans, or of hatred toward the Jews ... to translate those books into the Greek tongue, which I formerly composed in the language of our country".
- The reason he gives is the reason for the Greek, so the "because" is tied correctly.
- The earlier version in his own language is left unsaid. That was cut by the outline (Cut first 17). "The first" still holds for the books the sentence before names, the ones he wrote at Rome (JW 131974–131978).

**(20)** "Its key word, the Christ, means the Messiah, the anointed one the Jews awaited."
- This is a word gloss, the use a modern tool is allowed. Whiston's note: "the Christ of God, or the true Messias of the Jews" (JW-ed 139288–139290).

**(23)** "The passage goes on: Pilate, at the urging of the leading men, had him crucified; he appeared alive on the third day; and the Christians, named after him, have not died out."
- JW 81401–81409: "when Pilate, at the suggestion of the principal men among us, had condemned him to the cross ... he appeared to them alive again the third day ... the tribe of Christians, so named from him, are not extinct at this day".
- The wonder stays in the passage's voice.
- "Among us" (the Jews' own leaders) and "to them" are dropped. Neither changes the sense heard.

**(26)** "The fairest-minded citizens protested that he had no right to call the court."
- JW 91013–91016: "those who seemed the most equitable of the citizens ... disliked what was done".
- JW 91024–91038: "some of them" told Albinus "it was not lawful for Ananus to assemble a sanhedrim without his consent".
- The protest to Albinus came from some of the same group. This is a fair compression.

**(28)** "Early in the fourth century, Eusebius of Caesarea copied all three passages into his history of the church."
- John and Jesus: EH 9145–9164 and 9228–9240 (I.11).
- James: EH 13566–13614 (II.23).
- "Early in the fourth century": Book IX.5 tells of forged Acts sent out in his own day (EH 47942–47951). The period is Widely Accepted.

**(31)** "The passage itself says he was the Christ, and Origen wrote that Josephus did not accept him as the Christ, so how much of it Josephus wrote is uncertain."

## The heart

The heart is the right one. The shape is Outside witness, and what the sources show of him is:
- a priest and commander who went over to Rome, his own account of that (JW 110852–110856, 111125–111133);
- three short passages on John, James and Jesus inside twenty books of his people's history;
- what Christians did with them (EH 9242–9246; OMG 3731–3732; JVL 50907).

The brief makes him neither friend nor foe. It says how short the passage on Jesus is, and leaves its authorship open as the old sources do.
