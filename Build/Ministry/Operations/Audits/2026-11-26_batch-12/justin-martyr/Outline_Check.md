# Outline check: Justin Martyr

## My account of the heart (written before reading Outline.md)

From the census entry (`justin-martyr`, told within `greek-apologists-second-century`), the slate job, the existing card and `Source_Sheet.md`:

- **Essence.** Justin, a man from Flavia Neapolis in Roman Palestine, went through the philosophers' schools looking for God, found in the prophets and Christ "the only sure and useful philosophy", and went on as a Christian teacher in a philosopher's cloak, teaching at Rome. He wrote to Antoninus Pius that Christians were hated and punished for their name alone and should be judged by their deeds, and he argued that Christ is the Word in whom every people has had a share. Before the prefect Rusticus he and six companions refused to sacrifice to the gods and were beheaded. (Documented for his own words; Widely Accepted for the trial's core facts.)
- **Also told.** The search through the schools and the old man by the sea (Dialogue 2-8, own account); the petition and its argument (First Apology 1-4: the name alone is the charge; judge deeds); the one idea through his own words, the Word shared by every people, with Socrates (First Apology 46, with 5; Second Apology 10); the Dialogue with Trypho as a written argument with a Jew that the prophets spoke of Jesus, with its hard edge and friendly parting; Crescens and the death he expected (Second Apology 8), Crescens's role Contested; the trial and death (account of later, unknown author).
- **What came of him, heaviest first.** (1) His books were kept, read and used by later teachers against rival teachings: Irenaeus quotes his book against Marcion, Tertullian, Eusebius, Theodoret; *defended: the one God who made the world, against Marcion; the church's teaching against the heresies.* (2) He was remembered as "philosopher and martyr", and the trial account was kept. (3) His hearers: Euelpistus at the trial; Tatian, his hearer by Irenaeus's and Hippolytus's word (one clause; sibling `tatian`).
- **Shape.** Teacher or thinker, with the death told in its beats.

## Verdict: FIX

The heart matches mine in substance: the essence, the Also told and the ranking of what came of him (books kept and used against rival teachers, with what he defended marked; the name and the trial account kept; Tatian in one clause) agree with the sheet and the census. The shape fits. All three quotes are verbatim to the Greek and stand on it (my renderings below), none is used elsewhere on the tree, and neither barred span appears. The setting, the petition, the Word, the Urbicus case, Crescens and Tatian's side, the trial and death all check at their cited lines with the right confidence. Four BLOCKING findings: the length, one arrest given without the reason its source gives, an ending spent early, and one Cut first item that drops what a named book was about.

### My renderings of the quotes, made before comparing

1. Dialogue 8.1-2 (AR 429-435): "At once a fire was lit in my soul, and a love of the prophets took hold of me, and of those men who are friends of Christ. As I reasoned over his words with myself, I found this alone to be a philosophy sure and profitable. So, then, and for these reasons, I am a philosopher." The outline's rendering matches: no span is skipped between 8.1 and 8.2, and "sure" is right for ἀσφαλῆ.
2. First Apology 46.2-3 (R1 1230-1233, οἷ read οὗ): "We were taught that Christ is the firstborn of God, and we pointed out before that he is the Word, of whom every race of men partook. And those who lived with the Word are Christians, even if they were thought godless." The outline's rendering matches. Splitting off "Every people of humankind has had a share in him" keeps the sense and stays clear of the barred span.
3. Trial account II.3 (GEB 1315-1317; KK 1631-1632 agrees): "I tried to learn all teachings, but I gave my assent to the true teachings, those of the Christians, even if they do not please those of false opinion." The outline's rendering matches.

**B2. BLOCKING: beat 7, the arrest given without its source's reason.** "Brought before Rusticus" names an arrest. The trial account does give a reason: it says Christians were arrested under decrees "to force them to offer libations to vain idols", and "accordingly the holy men, having been apprehended, were brought before the prefect" (GEB 1300-1306, συλληφθέντες οὖν; ANF1 29266-29269). Correction: in beat 7, replace "By the account of his trial, by a writer we do not know, Justin and six others were brought before Rusticus, the prefect of Rome (GEB 1304-1306)." with "By the account of his trial, by a writer we do not know, it was a time when orders had gone out in town and country to force Christians to pour offerings to idols, and so Justin and six others were arrested and brought before Rusticus, the prefect of Rome (GEB 1300-1306, the six at 1355-1400; ANF1 29266-29269). The reason is told as the account's words; the account does not name Crescens." Add "the reason for the arrest (GEB 1300-1306)" to the Never cut line.

**M7. MINOR: Do not say additions.** Add:
