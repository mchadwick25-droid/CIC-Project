## 1. The test (person)

- **Who:** filled. "Peter, the bishop of Alexandria", who led "the churches of all Egypt and Libya through the persecution that began in 303."
- **What happened:** filled. He set rules for taking back the fallen; Melitius, one of his bishops, broke from him; he was beheaded in 311.
- **What it was about:** filled. How the church should receive those who gave way under torture, and who had the right to ordain in Peter's church.
- **Why the connection matters:** filled. "The split outlived him", Nicaea's ruling on Melitius, and his rules "copied into the collections of church law", sealed by a council in 692.

## 2. The opening

After the first three sentences I know who (Peter, bishop of Alexandria), where (Alexandria) and why there (Christians tortured into sacrifice, now asking to come back). Missing: **when**. "At the fourth Easter of the persecution" names no persecution and no century; the year 303 arrives only in sentence five. I am not lost, so MINOR.

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing (MINOR)

1. "He was seized for no cause at all, without warning, and suddenly and without reason, as if Maximinus had ordered it, he was beheaded." Meaning lost: heard once, "as if Maximinus had ordered it" leaves me unsure whether Maximinus ordered it or not. If the quote must stand as it is, a plain-sense clause beside it would settle what Eusebius means (Part 1 allows a quote's plain sense alongside).
2. "There he set aside the priests Peter had appointed to visit the churches, who were then in hiding, and put men of his own in their place." Meaning lost: "who were then in hiding" can attach to Peter or to the priests. Not story-breaking.

Understood though long: "By the law of their fathers, they wrote, no bishop could ordain outside his own church, so that those ordained were known and confusion avoided." The clipped ending ("confusion avoided") takes an extra beat but lands.
