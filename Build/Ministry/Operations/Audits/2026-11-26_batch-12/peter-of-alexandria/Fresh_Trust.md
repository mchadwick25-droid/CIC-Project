## SUMMARY HOLDS (15): evidence, no change

- S1 "… jailed and tortured until they gave way and offered sacrifice to the gods." ANF6 canon 1: "apprehended and thrown into prison … torments not to be borne … afterwards have been betrayed by the frailty of the flesh"; Balsamon, 26637: "those who after many torments have sacrificed to the gods". A fair compression.
- S4 "The marks were the scars of their torture." Zonaras, ANF6 26661–26663: "the marks of Jesus, that is to say, the scars of the wounds which … they have endured". A gloss from a later reader, given as plain sense.
- S5 "Peter led the churches of all Egypt and Libya through the persecution that began in 303." NPNF14 3405–3407 (Nicaea canon 6, "ancient customs … Egypt, Libya and Pentapolis"); EPI 43712–43714 ("πάσης τε Αἰγύπτου … ἔχειν τὴν ἐκκλησιαστικὴν διοίκησιν"); EUS 43264–43265 ("cared … for the general interest of the churches"). The year 303 is Widely Accepted.
- S6 "… weighing what each had suffered and chosen." The canons go case by case (canons 1–3, 12–13), and canon 1 turns on "κατὰ προαίρεσιν" (by choice).
- S7 "In those years one of his own bishops, Melitius, broke from him, and in 311 Peter was beheaded." ATH 24776–24783 ("during the course of it … made a schism"); EPI 43612–43614 ("ἐν καιρῷ τοῦ διωγμοῦ"); ANF6 16750–16756 (Peter is the bishop "on whom we all depend").
- S9 "Those who had given way asked to share communion again …" ANF6 canon 1: "from the time of their submissive approach" (PED "ἀπὸ τῆς προσελεύσεως"), and "were not at the first received". The gloss of communion comes from canon 8, ANF6 26972–26975: "both in prayer, and in partaking of the body and blood of Christ".
- S14 Clergy. PED 50115–50116: "τοὺς ἀπὸ Κλήρου αὐτομολήσαντας, ἐκπεπτωχότας, καὶ ἀναπαλαίσαντας, ἔτι ἐν τῇ λειτουργίᾳ εἶναι"; ANF6 27192–27193: "communion is sufficient for them". "Gave themselves up … unbidden" is a fair reading of αὐτομολήσαντας: canon 10 opens with "Whence" (Ὄθεν), following canon 9, where the same verb is used (PED 50045, "οὐ γὰρ θέλει αὐτομολεῖν ἡμᾶς"). The canon itself also speaks of those "who, contending in prison, have fallen from their ministry", and of men who "seek for that which they have left". So "not return to their office" holds against "remain any longer".
- S15 "Four bishops … rebuked Melitius, a bishop of Upper Egypt, in the south, for making priests and deacons in churches that were not his." ANF6 16723–16725: "the ordination carried through by thee in parishes having no manner of connection with thee"; 16756 ("our imprisonments"); EPI 43599–43601 ("ἐπισκόπου ἐν τῇ Θηβαΐδι"). "Priests and deacons" glosses ordination: the account names "presbyters and deacons" (16825), and Peter names "the priests" (27796–27797).
- S16 "By the law of their fathers … so that those ordained were known and confusion avoided." ANF6 16727–16742: "not lawful for any bishop to celebrate ordinations in other parishes than his own … the conversation and life of those who are ordained should be examined with great care … that all confusion … should be done away with"; such men are chosen from "those with whom he has passed his whole life".
- S17 "He should have waited for the judgment of Peter, whom they called their great bishop and father." ANF6 16786–16787: "thou oughtest to have waited for the judgment of the superior father"; 16750–16752: "our great bishop and father, Peter".
- S20 "The priests Peter had appointed … put men of his own in their place." ANF6 16836–16841: "presbyters, then in hiding, to whom the blessed Peter had given power to act as parish-visitors … suspended them for the time, and by his own authority ordained two persons in their place".
- S23 "Later writers tell the break otherwise." A fair frame for S24–S26, which differ from the letters.
- S25 "Epiphanius of Salamis, writing some sixty years later, says the two were in prison together and split over the fallen." EPI 43614: "ἅμα Πέτρῳ … μάρτυρι" (arrested with Peter); 43722–43730 (the fallen came to the confessors and martyrs, and a quarrel arose among them). EPI 1581–1582 dates Epiphanius's writing to the reign of Valentinian, Valens and Gratian. Against a break during the persecution, "some sixty" is fair.
- S27 "His end came after an order from the emperors … which the eastern emperor Maximinus would not accept." EUS 47567–47580: "The imperial edict of recantation … was posted … Maximinus, the tyrant in the East … being by no means satisfied with its contents … keeping the law … secret"; the order, EUS 45641–45643: "that they may again be Christians, and may rebuild the conventicles"; IX.6.2, "At about the same time Peter also".
- S32 "His rules were copied into the collections of church law, and in 692 a council set its seal … martyr." NPNF14 26739–26744: "Likewise too the Canons … of Peter, Archbishop of Alexandria and Martyr" (in the year 692, NPNF14 26383–26385). The commentaries of Balsamon and Zonaras, later witnesses who keep the canons, stand with them (ANF6 26634–26664).
