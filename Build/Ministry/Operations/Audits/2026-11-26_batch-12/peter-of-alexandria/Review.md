## BLOCKING

**B1. "Clergy who fell could share communion again, but never return to their office." (first trust read: SOFTENED OR CHANGED; the second read held it).**
- Evidence (first read): canon 10, ANF6 27162–27165, rules on "those of the clergy who have deserted of their own accord, and have lapsed, and taken up the contest afresh"; Greek PED 50115–50116 "τοὺς ἀπὸ Κλήρου αὐτομολήσαντας, ἐκπεπτωκότας, καὶ ἀναπαλαίσαντας"; ANF6 27185–27186 "those who, contending in prison, have fallen from their ministry, and have again taken up the struggle"; ANF6 27192 "For communion is sufficient for them". Evidence (second read): the same lines, read as the general rule.
- Judged by the bar: it blocks. The canon (ANF6 27155–27195, read for this verdict) speaks throughout of one group, clergy who went to the contest of their own accord, fell, and took up the struggle again; it says nothing of every cleric who fell. Told as a rule for all fallen clergy, the brief widens the canon, and the first-time listener shows the cost: beside Epiphanius's line that Melitius "would not restore fallen clergy to their rank", the listener could not tell whether the story contradicts itself or the witnesses differ. That is a softened source and a misleading implication.
- **Needs the outline.** Beat B2 holds the over-broad wording itself ("Clergy who fell: communion, but not their office again"). Back to the researcher to correct B2's clergy line to canon 10's scope from E11, and to settle from the Greek and canon 9 beside it what "deserted of their own accord" (αὐτομολήσαντας) means for the listener: gave themselves up to the contest unbidden, or left their flock (the canon gives "they have left destitute the flock of the Lord" as the reason, ANF6 27165–27166). The clergy line is Also told in the approved heart, so it is corrected, not cut.
- Change, once the outline holds it (the first trust read's wording, to be checked against the researcher's corrected beat): "Clergy who fell could share communion again, but never return to their office." → "Clergy who fell and then stood firm again could share communion, but not return to their office."
- Do not say (add to outline): Not "Clergy who fell could share communion but never return to their office" as a rule for every fallen cleric. Canon 10 rules only on clergy who [the researcher's wording for αὐτομολήσαντας], fell, and took up the struggle again: communion, not their office (E11, ANF6 27162–27193).

**B2. "Meanwhile four bishops of Egypt, writing from prison, rebuked Melitius..." (first trust read: ASSUMPTION ADDED; the second read held it as SUMMARY HOLDS).**
- Evidence (first read): the letter (ANF6 16710–16800) and the account kept with it (ANF6 16822–16826) give no date and do not tie the letter to the fourth Easter or the canons; the date of the split rests only on modern reckoning (Source_Sheet X3). The second read checked the letter's content, which holds; it did not weigh the time word.
- Change: "Meanwhile four bishops of Egypt, writing from prison, rebuked Melitius," → "Four bishops of Egypt, writing from prison, rebuked Melitius," (beat B3's own words; s8's "In those years" already places the break in the persecution).
- Do not say (add to outline): Not "Meanwhile", "at the same time" or any time word tying the four bishops' letter to the fourth Easter or the canons. The letter and the account give no date; say "Four bishops of Egypt, writing from prison, rebuked Melitius" (M1, X3).
