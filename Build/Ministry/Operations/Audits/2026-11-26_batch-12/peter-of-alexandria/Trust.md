## Non-HELD sentences

### 17. SOFTENED OR CHANGED (BLOCKING)

Brief: "Clergy who fell could share communion again, but never return to their office."

Source, canon 10, ANF6 27162–27165: "it is not right either that those of the clergy who have deserted of their own accord, and have lapsed, and taken up the contest afresh, should remain any longer in their sacred office"; ANF6 27192: "For communion is sufficient for them". Greek, PED 50115–50116: "τοὺς ἀπὸ Κλήρου αὐτομολήσαντας, ἐκπεπτωκότας, καὶ ἀναπαλαίσαντας, ἔτι ἐν τῇ λειτουργίᾳ εἶναι". See also ANF6 27185–27186: "those who, contending in prison, have fallen from their ministry, and have again taken up the struggle".

The canon rules on clergy who fell and then took up the struggle again. The brief makes it a rule for every cleric who fell. That changes its scope. It also bears on sentence 30, where Epiphanius gives Melitius the same refusal to restore fallen clergy. Told this broadly, the listener hears a clear contrast with Melitius that the canon does not draw.

Exact change: "Clergy who fell and then stood firm again could share communion, but not return to their office."

### 18. ASSUMPTION ADDED (BLOCKING)

Brief: "Meanwhile four bishops of Egypt, writing from prison, rebuked Melitius..."

Source: the letter (ANF6 16710–16800) and the account (ANF6 16822–16826) give no date. They do not tie the letter to the fourth Easter or to the canons. "Meanwhile" sets the letter at the same time as the Easter letter in the paragraph before it. That is a time link the sources do not give, and the outline's beat B3 does not hold it ("Four bishops of Egypt, writing from prison, rebuked Melitius"). The date of the split rests only on modern reckoning (Source_Sheet X3).

Exact change: delete "Meanwhile". Read "During the persecution, four bishops of Egypt, writing from prison, rebuked Melitius..." or start at "Four bishops of Egypt...".

### Note on 29 (not blocking)

"some sixty years later": Epiphanius dates his writing to the twelfth year of Valentinian, Valens and Gratian (EPI 1581–1582), 375/6. That is about 64 years after Peter's death and about 70 after the canons. The outline holds "about sixty years", so this stands. "more than sixty years later" would be exact against either point.

## The heart

The heart is right for this subject. In the sources, Peter is the bishop whose own canons judge each kind of fall by what it cost (canons 1–3, 5, 10, 12–13). The letters of the time show the break with Melitius, and the contemporary witness gives his beheading with no cause (EUS IX.6). The essence is told in full by the end of paragraph two, at word 130, inside the first minute: who he was and where, the persecution of 303, the rules for the fallen, the break, and his death in 311. The story keeps to the Martyr trap: the death takes one paragraph and the canons get the most room.
