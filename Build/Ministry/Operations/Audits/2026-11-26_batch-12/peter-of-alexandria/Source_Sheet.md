## Setting

- **S1. Bishop of Alexandria from about 300.** Eusebius (contemporary): "After Theonas had held the office for nineteen years, Peter received the episcopate in Alexandria, and was very eminent among them for twelve entire years. Of these he governed the church less than three years before the persecution, and for the remainder of his life he subjected himself to a more rigid discipline and cared in no secret manner for the general interest of the churches." (EUS 43214–43215, 43261–43266; Greek EUS-G 13031–13037, "τῆς κοινῆς τῶν ἐκκλησιῶν ὠφελείας οὐχ ἀφανῶς ἐπεμέλετο"). **Documented.** McGiffert (modern; clarifies the old text's dates) works the accession out to the spring of 300 (EUS 43224–43229). "About 300": **Widely Accepted.**
- **S2. What the bishop of Alexandria led.** Council of Nicaea, canon 6 (325, fourteen years after Peter's death): "Let the ancient customs in Egypt, Libya and Pentapolis prevail, that the Bishop of Alexandria have jurisdiction in all these" (NPNF14 3405–3407). Epiphanius (later, 370s): "it is the custom that the archbishop in Alexandria has the church government of all Egypt and the Thebaid, Mareotis, Libya, Ammoniaca and the Pentapolis" (EPI 43712–43714, "τὸν ἐν τῇ Ἀλεξανδρείᾳ ἀρχιεπίσκοπον πάσης τε Αἰγύπτου καὶ Θηβαΐδος ... ἔχειν τὴν ἐκκλησιαστικὴν διοίκησιν"). The four bishops call him "our great bishop and father, Peter, on whom we all depend in the hope which we have in the Lord Jesus Christ" (ANF6 16750–16756). **Documented.** Plain words: "he led the churches of all Egypt and Libya".
- **S3. The persecution begins, 303.** Eusebius: "It was in the nineteenth year of the reign of Diocletian, in the month Dystrus, called March by the Romans ... that royal edicts were published everywhere, commanding that the churches be leveled to the ground and the Scriptures be destroyed by fire" (EUS 43460, 43484–43488). McGiffert (modern; clarifies the date): the nineteenth year ran Sept. 302 to Sept. 303 (EUS 43462–43463); "Diocletian's edict decreeing the demolition of the churches was published in February, 303" (EUS 43273–43274). Year **Widely Accepted.**
- **S4. What the Christians in Alexandria were made to choose.** Phileas, bishop of Thmuis, writing from Alexandria during the persecution, quoted by Eusebius VIII.10 (contemporary; own words of a fellow prisoner): "When therefore they were ordered to choose whether they would be released from molestation by touching the polluted sacrifice, and would receive from them the accursed freedom, or refusing to sacrifice, should be condemned to death, they did not hesitate, but went to death cheerfully." (EUS 44468–44472). Tortures listed: "some beat them with clubs, others with rods, others with scourges" (EUS 44399–44401). **Documented.** Peter's own canons show the act asked: "to touch the fire with their own hands, and to offer incense to the impure demons" (ANF6 26817–26818). Plain words: "to offer incense at the altar of the gods".
- **S5. Bishops in prison.** The four bishops write of "our imprisonments and trials, and daily and multiplied reproach" (ANF6 16756–16757); the account says Melitius "neither ... repaired to them in the prison" (ANF6 16822–16823). Eusebius names Phileas, Hesychius, Pachymius and Theodorus, bishops of Egypt, among those who died with Peter's presbyters (EUS 44798–44811). **Documented.**
- **S6. Not held: birth, family, schooling, and headship of the catechetical school.** Hawkins's "a.d. 260" is marked "This first date is conjectural" (ANF6 25673–25674). The corpus map: "He is not documented as head of the catechetical school ... The school-headship tradition descends from Philip of Side, which is not vendored here" (`cic/corpus-map/alexandria-catechetical.yaml` 7444–7448). Eusebius's tie to teaching is a judgment, not an office: "one of the most excellent teachers of the religion of Christ" (EUS 44800–44801; EUS-G 15468–15470, "θεῖόν τι χρῆμα διδασκάλων τῆς ἐν Χριστῷ θεοσεβείας"). **honest_limit** (absent detail).

## Events

### E. The canons: how to take back those who gave way

- **E1. When, in his own words.** Canon 1 opens: "But since the fourth passover of the persecution has arrived" (ANF6 26607–26608; PED 49718, "Ἐπεὶ τοίνυν τέταρτον ἤδη Πάσχα ἐπικατείληφε τὸν διωγμόν"). **Own; Documented.** Πάσχα is Easter. Year: Hawkins (modern) "in the fourth year of the persecution, a.d. 306" (ANF6 25777–25778); McGiffert (modern) "in 306 (see the opening sentence of the first canon)" (EUS 43238–43239); Nikodemos (modern, 1800) "Ἐν δὲ τῷ 304 ἔτει" (PED 49702–49703). The year rests on editors' reckoning and they differ: **Contested** (X5). Tell "at the fourth Easter of the persecution".
- **E2. What the text is.** "The Canons of the Blessed Peter, Archbishop of Alexandria, as They are Given in His Sermon on Penitence" (ANF6 26599–26601); Greek title "Οἱ ιε' Κανόνες ... εἰς οὓς, παρ᾽ ἕνα, ὁ περὶ μετανοίας αὐτοῦ λόγος διήρηται" (PED 49710–49712). **Documented.** Explain it: rules, set out in a sermon on repentance, for how long each kind of fallen Christian must fast and pray before sharing communion again.
- **E3. Canon 1: the tortured.** "it is sufficient, in the case of those who have been apprehended and thrown into prison, and who have sustained torments not to be borne, and stripes intolerable, and many other dreadful afflictions, and afterwards have been betrayed by the frailty of the flesh ... yet because they contended sorely and resisted long; for they did not come to this of their own will, but were betrayed by the frailty of the flesh; for they show in their bodies the marks of Jesus, and some are now, for the third year, bewailing their fault: it is sufficient, I say, that from the time of their submissive approach, other forty days should be enjoined upon them ... those forty days during which, though our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ had fasted, He was yet, after He had been baptized, tempted of the devil" (ANF6 26608–26626). Greek of the core (PED 49722–49723): "οὐ γὰρ κατὰ προαίρεσιν ἐν τούτῳ ἐληλύθασιν, ἀλλὰ καταπροδοθέντες ὑπὸ τῆς ἀσθενείας τῆς σαρκὸς, ἐπειδὴ καὶ τὰ σ[τ]ίγματα τοῦ Ἰησοῦ ἐνδείκνυνται ἐν τοῖς σώμασιν αὐτῶν". **Own; Documented.** Hawkins's note: "The marks of Jesus, στίγματα. Cf. Gal. vi. 17" (ANF6 26618–26620). Zonaras (later, 12th c.; a reader who carried the canon on): "the marks of Jesus, that is to say, the scars of the wounds which, in behalf of Christ, they have endured" (ANF6 26661–26663). Balsamon (12th c.): "those who after many torments have sacrificed to the gods" (ANF6 26637). Explain it: "gave way" = offered sacrifice to the gods under torture; "the marks of Jesus" = the scars of their torture, in Paul's words.
- **E4. Canon 2: prison without torture.** Those who in prison "endured afflictions and nauseous odours, but afterwards, without the conflict of torments, were led captive, being broken in spirit ... a year in addition to the foregoing time will suffice" (ANF6 26679–26684). **Own; Documented.**
- **E5. Canon 3: fear alone.** "those who have suffered none of these things ... but of their own accord have gone over to wickedness, being betrayed by fear and cowardice, and now come to repentance" are set "the parable of the unfruitful fig-tree", the tree given one more year to bear fruit (ANF6 26724–26738). **Own; Documented.** The length is read two ways by later commentators: Balsamon "three years' exclusion" (ANF6 26739–26741); Zonaras "after the fourth year" (ANF6 26751–26752); Johnson's summary in NPNF14 "four years are added" (NPNF14 43178–43180). **Contested** (X6): give no number.
- **E6. Canon 4: the unrepentant.** "To those who are altogether reprobate, and unrepentant ... 'Let no fruit grow on thee henceforward for ever'" (ANF6 26759–26766). **Own.** Not used.
- **E7. Canon 5: those who tricked the officials.** Those who "have mocked the snares of their enemies, either passing by the altars, or giving a writing, or sending heathen to do sacrifice instead of themselves ... since with the greatest caution they have avoided to touch the fire with their own hands, and to offer incense to the impure demons; yet inasmuch as they escaped the notice of their persecutors by doing this, let a penalty of six months' penance be imposed upon them" (ANF6 26812–26820). **Own; Documented.** Explain it: walked past the altar as if they had sacrificed, handed in a written statement, or sent a pagan to sacrifice in their place.
- **E8. Canons 6–7: slaves and masters.** Christian slaves sent to sacrifice by their masters, "being threatened by them": "during the year show forth the works of penitence" (ANF6 26893–26897). "But the freemen shall be tried by penance for three years, both for their dissimulation, and for having compelled their fellow-servants to offer sacrifice" (ANF6 26928–26930); "They have drawn their fellow-servants to idolatry who would have been able to escape" (ANF6 26942–26943). **Own; Documented.**
- **E9. Canon 8: those who fell and then confessed again.** "it is right with joy and exultation of heart to add strength, and to communicate to them in all things, both in prayer, and in partaking of the body and blood of Christ, and in hortatory discourse" (ANF6 26969–26975). **Own; Documented.** This line gives communion its plain sense in Peter's own terms: sharing prayer and the body and blood of Christ.
- **E10. Canon 9: those who rushed forward unbidden.** Those who "themselves leap forth upon a contest" are faulted, yet "with them also we must communicate, inasmuch as they come to this in the name of Christ, even though they take no heed unto His words" (ANF6 27039–27045); Christ "would have us pass from place to place ... 'flee ye into another'" (ANF6 27070–27073). **Own; Documented.**
- **E11. Canon 10: fallen clergy.** "it is not right either that those of the clergy who have deserted of their own accord, and have lapsed, and taken up the contest afresh, should remain any longer in their sacred office" (ANF6 27162–27165); "For communion is sufficient for them" (ANF6 27192–27193). **Own; Documented.**
- **E12. Canons 12–13: those who paid or fled.** "Against those who have given money that they might be entirely undisturbed by evil, an accusation cannot be brought" (ANF6 27484–27485). "neither is it lawful to accuse those who have left all, and have retired for the safety of their life" (ANF6 27536–27537); "these men that have withdrawn themselves are not at all to be blamed" (ANF6 27602–27603). **Own; Documented.**
- **E13. Canon 14: those forced, hands burned.** Those who "have bravely borne the burning of their hands that against their will had been put to the profane sacrifice, as from their prison the thrice-blessed martyrs have written to me respecting those in Libya ... can be placed in the ministry amongst the confessors" (ANF6 27634–27641); Greek PED 50361–50364 ("ὥσπερ ἔγραψάν μοι οἱ ἀπὸ τῆς φυλακῆς τρισμακάριοι μάρτυρες"). **Own; Documented.** Shows him writing to and hearing from imprisoned martyrs. Explain it: "confessors" = those who held to the faith under trial and lived.
- **E14. Canon 15** (fasting days; ANF6 27695–27706). Not on the lapsed; not used. Count of canons: fourteen (McGiffert, EUS 43238), fifteen (PED 49710 "Οἱ ιε' Κανόνες"; ANF6). **Sources differ** (X7); not needed.
- **E15. Modern judgments of the canons (not for the story).** Hefele (modern; makes a claim of its own): the canons "prove that he knew how to keep a wise middle course, and to proportion the penance to the sin" (HEF 22259–22262). Dupin, quoted by Coxe (ANF6 28222–28224). Modern works list only.

### M. The break with Melitius

Census spelling "Melitius" (`melitius-of-lycopolis`). The English prints write "Meletius"; Bell: "The correct form is undoubtedly Μελίτιος ... the form Meletius, since it has everywhere established itself, is retained for convenience" (BELL 3230–3233).

- **M1. The four bishops' letter (contemporary; Latin not held, English ANF6).** "Hesychius, Pachomius, Theodorus, and Phileas, to Meletius, our friend and fellow-minister in the Lord" (ANF6 16710–16711). Their grief at "the ordination carried through by thee in parishes having no manner of connection with thee" (ANF6 16723–16725). "There is the law of our fathers and forefathers ... that it is not lawful for any bishop to celebrate ordinations in other parishes than his own" (ANF6 16727–16736). He acted without regard to "the honour of our great bishop and father, Peter" and, "nor softened by our imprisonments and trials", "hast ventured on subverting all things at once" (ANF6 16750–16758). His likely excuse, that "the flocks were in need and forsaken, there being no pastor with them", is answered: "there are many going about them and in a position to act as visitors" (ANF6 16760–16766). "thou oughtest to have waited for the judgment of the superior father" (ANF6 16786–16787). **Documented** as a document of the time. McGiffert (modern; on genuineness): "There seems to be nothing in the epistle to disprove its genuineness" (EUS 44329–44330). Bell (modern): Seeck's case for forgery "quite unconvincing and their authenticity is now almost universally accepted" (BELL 3179–3181). Explain it: "ordain" = make someone a priest or deacon by laying on hands; the rule was that each bishop ordains only in his own church.
- **M2. The account kept with it (anonymous; near the time; Latin not held).** "After receiving and perusing this epistle, he neither wrote any reply nor repaired to them in the prison, nor went to the blessed Peter. But when all these bishops and presbyters and deacons had suffered martyrdom in the prison at Alexandria, he at once entered Alexandria" (ANF6 16822–16826). With "a certain person, by name Isidorus" and "a certain Arius" (ANF6 16826–16830), "they discovered to Meletius certain presbyters, then in hiding, to whom the blessed Peter had given power to act as parish-visitors. And Meletius ... suspended them for the time, and by his own authority ordained two persons in their place, namely, one in prison and another in the mines. On learning these things the blessed Peter, with much endurance, wrote to the people of Alexandria" (ANF6 16836–16845). **Documented** as this account's claim. The "Arius" here is not said to be the later priest (L4).
- **M3. Peter's letter to the church at Alexandria (own; Latin not held; English ANF6).** "Since I have found out that Meletius acts in no way for the common good,—for neither is he contented with the letter of the most holy bishops and martyrs,—but, invading my parish, hath assumed so much to himself as to endeavour to separate from my authority the priests, and those who had been entrusted with visiting the needy; and, giving proof of his desire for pre-eminence, has ordained in the prison several unto himself; now, take ye heed to this, and hold no communion with him, until I meet him in company with some wise and discreet men, and see what the designs are which he has thought upon. Fare ye well." (ANF6 27789–27804). **Own; Documented.** Barred: "ordained in the prison several unto himself" (`melitius-of-lycopolis`). Free for this story: "take ye heed ... until I meet him in company with some wise and discreet men, and see what the designs are which he has thought upon".
- **M4. Where Peter was when he wrote.** The account says only that the presbyters Peter appointed were "then in hiding" (ANF6 16837). Bell (modern; makes a claim of its own): "finding that Peter was in hiding" (BELL 3205–3206). **Dominant Modern Reconstruction**; stays out.
- **M5. Athanasius: deposed by a council (later; bishop of Alexandria after Alexander, SOC 4002, and the Melitians' foe, who says they "formed a conspiracy against Peter himself").** "Peter was Bishop among us before the persecution, and during the course of it he suffered martyrdom. When Meletius, who held the title of bishop in Egypt, was convicted of many crimes, and among the rest of offering sacrifice to idols, Peter deposed him in a general council of the bishops. Whereupon Meletius did not appeal to another council ... but made a schism" (ATH 24776–24783). "They formed a conspiracy against Peter himself" (ATH 22467–22468). **Documented** as Athanasius's charge; the charge itself **Contested** (X1). Barred quote: "offering sacrifice to idols". An enemy's charge: told in its own content, credited.
- **M6. Socrates and Theodoret (later, 5th c.).** Socrates: Melitius, "in consequence of many other charges, and more especially because during the persecution he had denied the faith and sacrificed, was deposed" (SOC 2405–2409). Theodoret: "he was convicted of certain crimes by the most holy Peter" and "rebelled against the primacy of Alexandria" (THDT 6550–6555). **Later (a century and more)**, after Athanasius.
- **M7. Epiphanius: a quarrel in prison over the fallen (later; he wrote in the reign of Valentinian, Valens and Gratian, EPI 1581–1582, the 370s).** Melitius was arrested "together with Peter the holy bishop and martyr" (EPI 43614–43616, "ἅμα Πέτρῳ τῷ ἁγίῳ ἐπισκόπῳ καὶ μάρτυρι ... συλληφθεὶς"); "made a schism, yet did not change the faith" (EPI 43612, "Σχίσμα ἐποίησεν, οὐ μὴν ... μετηλλαγμένος τὴν πίστιν"). The fallen "προςῆλθον τοῖς ὁμολογηταῖς τε καὶ μάρτυσιν, ὅπως τύχωσιν ἐλέους διὰ μετανοίας" (came to the confessors and martyrs, to find mercy through repentance; EPI 43724–43725); those who said otherwise were "Μελήτιος καὶ Πηλεὺς, καί τινες ἄλλοι" (EPI 43735), granting repentance only "μετὰ τὸ παύσασθαι τὸν διωγμὸν" (after the persecution had stopped) and "οὐ μὴν ἵνα ἕκαστος δεξιωθῇ ἐν τῷ ἰδίῳ κλήρῳ" (not so that each be received in his own rank of clergy) (EPI 43737–43744); "Ὁ δὲ ἁγιώτατος Πέτρος εὔσπλαγχνος ὤν, καὶ ὡς πατὴρ πάντων ὑπάρχων, ἐδέετο καὶ καθικέτευε λέγων, Δεξώμεθα αὐτοὺς μετανοοῦντας" (the most holy Peter, being compassionate and as father of all, begged and pleaded, saying, Let us receive them as they repent; EPI 43747–43750, 43840; the OCR prints "Ζεξωμεϑα"). Greek normalized from the OCR (ϑ as θ); glosses in brackets are mine. Verdict line: "Καὶ ἦν μὲν τοῦ Πέτρου ὁ λόγος ὑπὲρ ἐλέους καὶ φιλανθρωπίας, καὶ τοῦ Μελητίου καὶ τῶν ἅμα αὐτῷ ὑπὲρ ἀληθείας καὶ ζήλου" (EPI 43852–43853; EPI-PG 13796–13798). The cloak across the prison and most going with Melitius (EPI 43858–43868). **Documented** as his account. The cloak scene is the opening of `melitius-of-lycopolis`; not told here.
- **M8. Modern views of the split (not for the story).** Bell: Athanasius's charge "need not be taken very seriously"; Epiphanius "had excellent authority"; the two accounts "different stages in the growth of the schism"; "probably in A.D. 305" (BELL 3153–3168); neither side would exclude the lapsed for ever (BELL 3192–3195). Hefele: "the original documents prove that, at the time of the commencement of the schism, neither Peter nor Meletius was in prison" (HEF 22253–22256); Peter's canon 10 "teaches exactly what S. Epiphanius supposes to be the opinion of Meletius" (HEF 22268–22293). Robertson: "The Meletian account of the matter ... taking the sterner view"; "No doctrinal question, however, was involved" (ATH 1646–1656). All **modern, making claims of their own**; told by `melitius-of-lycopolis` and `melitian-schism`; card only.

### D. Death

- **D1. Eusebius, Book VII (contemporary).** "On this account he was beheaded in the ninth year of the persecution, and was adorned with the crown of martyrdom." (EUS 43266–43268); Greek "ταύτῃ δ᾽ οὖν ἐνάτῳ ἔτει τοῦ διωγμοῦ τὴν κεφαλὴν ἀποτμηθεὶς τῷ τοῦ μαρτυρίου κατεκοσμήθη στεφάνῳ" (EUS-G 13037–13039). **Documented.** "ταύτῃ δ᾽ οὖν" can mean "so, in this way"; it does not plainly give his care for the churches as the cause. Do not tell it as cause.
- **D2. Eusebius, Book IX (contemporary).** The setting: after the edict of toleration was posted, "Maximinus, the tyrant in the East" would not accept it (EUS 47567–47572). Then: "At about the same time Peter also, who presided most illustriously over the parishes in Alexandria, a divine example of a bishop on account of the excellence of his life and his study of the sacred Scriptures, being seized for no cause and quite unexpectedly, was, as if by command of Maximinus, immediately and without explanation, beheaded. With him also many other bishops of Egypt suffered the same fate." (EUS 47990–47998). Greek (EUS-G 17553–17561): "ἐξ οὐδεμιᾶς ἀνάρπαστος γεγονὼς αἰτίας, μηδεμιᾶς προλαβούσης προσδοκίας, ἀθρόως οὕτως καὶ ἀλόγως, ὡς ἂν Μαξιμίνου προστάξαντος, τὴν κεφαλὴν ἀποτέμνεται, σὺν αὐτῷ δὲ καὶ τῶν κατ᾽ Αἴγυπτον ἐπισκόπων ἄλλοι πλείους ταὐτὸν ὑπομένουσιν". **Documented.** The act is given with no reason; the source says so itself ("for no cause"). Explain it: the old source gives no reason; it is told as given.
- **D3. Eusebius, Book VIII (contemporary).** "Of those who suffered death illustriously at Alexandria and throughout Egypt and Thebais, Peter, bishop of Alexandria, one of the most excellent teachers of the religion of Christ, should first be mentioned; and of the presbyters with him Faustus, Dius and Ammonius, perfect martyrs of Christ; also Phileas, Hesychius, Pachymius and Theodorus" (EUS 44798–44811). **Documented.**
- **D4. The year and day.** Ninth year of the persecution (D1). McGiffert (modern): "Feb. 311 to Feb. 312" and "evidently after the publication of the toleration edict of Galerius" (EUS 43218–43224). Hawkins (modern): "his martyrdom falls in the year of our Lord 311—as the Egyptians reckon on the 29th day of the month Athyr, which answers to our 25th of November" (ANF6 25769–25771). Bell: "martyred on 25 Nov., 311" (BELL 3210–3212). Robertson's note to the Life of Antony: "Martyred on Nov. 25, 311" (ATH 32397). Year 311: **Widely Accepted.** The day comes from the later Egyptian church's reckoning: card only.
- **D5. Late legend.** *The Genuine Acts of Peter* (ANF6 25834 on): his last night, a vision about Arius, prayer at Mark's tomb, a soldier who "drew his sword and beheaded the pontiff" (ANF6 26416). McGiffert: "spurious and historically quite worthless" (EUS 43252–43253). Nikodemos repeats a heavenly voice at his beheading, "Πέτρος ἀρχὴ Ἀποστόλων, καὶ Πέτρος τέλος Μαρτύρων" (PED 49700–49701), as does the Coptic *History of the Patriarchs*, "the last of the martyrs" (`cic/texts/severus-al-muqaffa_history-patriarchs-coptic-church-v1_evetts1904.txt` 27412). **Late legend.** Leave out.

## Impact, heaviest first

- **I1. His rules became church law (defended: the measured return of the fallen).** Council in Trullo, 692 (NPNF14 26383–26385, "the year 692"), Canon 2: "we set our seal likewise upon all the other holy canons set forth by our holy and blessed Fathers ... Likewise too the Canons [i.e. the decretal letters] of Dionysius, formerly Archbishop of the great city of Alexandria; and of Peter, Archbishop of Alexandria and Martyr" (NPNF14 26730–26744). Hawkins: "They are to be met with in every collection of Canons", with "the notes of Joannes Zonaras and Theodorus Balsamon" (ANF6 25779–25784); those 12th-century notes stand in ANF6 itself (26634–26673). Nikodemos's Pedalion prints them and says that by this ratification they "take on, in a way, ecumenical force" (PED 49705–49706, "Οἰκουμενικὴν τρόπον τινὰ ἀναλαμβάνουσι δύναμιν"). **Documented.**
- **I2. The split outlived him and came before Nicaea (defended: one bishop's order in each church, and Alexandria's place over Egypt).** Nicaea's letter to Alexandria (kept by Socrates I.9): "the Synod being moved to great clemency towards Melitius" decreed that "he remain in his own city but exercise no authority either to ordain or nominate for ordination" (SOC 3206–3211); those he ordained to be admitted "after having been confirmed by a more legitimate ordination" (SOC 3212–3214). Canon 6 kept the bishop of Alexandria's authority over Egypt, Libya and Pentapolis (S2). Athanasius: the Melitians "calumniated his successor Achillas; they accused Alexander even before the Emperor" and then turned on Athanasius (ATH 22468–22470). **Documented.** Barred: "a nominal dignity". Told fully by `melitian-schism`; a clause here.
- **I3. Remembered as bishop and martyr.** Eusebius (D2, D3). Athanasius: "the blessed Peter, Bishop and Martyr" (ATH 22465); "Meletius, when ejected by Peter the Bishop and Martyr" (ATH 41340); Life of Antony 47, "when at last the persecution ceased, and the blessed Bishop Peter had borne his testimony, Antony departed, and again withdrew to his cell" (ATH 32396–32399). Socrates, retelling Rufinus: "the day in which the memory of the martyr and bishop Peter was celebrated" at Alexandria (SOC 4002–4008). **Documented.**
- **I4. His teaching read at Ephesus.** Vincent of Lérins (later, about 434): at the Council of Ephesus "the men whose writings ... were recited" included "St. Peter, bishop of Alexandria, a most excellent Doctor and most blessed martyr" (VINC 14435–14438). ANF6 heads Fragment II, On the Godhead, "A fragment from his book, from the Acts of the Council of Ephesus" (ANF6 27809–27810); Cyril of Alexandria cites the same passage under "Petri martyris episcopi Alexandriae" (MANSI5 3126–3137, in Cyril's *Apologeticus*, heading at 2403–2406). **Documented** as these writers' reports. Card; not in the story (the fragments are not this story's job, and explaining them would need room).

## Where the sources disagree or come late

- **X1. Why Peter broke with Melitius.** The letters of the time (M1–M3): ordaining in other bishops' churches and then in Alexandria itself. Athanasius, Socrates, Theodoret (M5, M6): Peter, by a council, deposed him for many crimes, among them sacrifice. Epiphanius (M7): both in prison; a quarrel over taking back the fallen, Peter for mercy, Melitius for strictness. **Contested.** Credit each; the letters told in the narrator's voice as what the letters say.
- **X2. Prison.** Epiphanius: both in prison together. The letters: the four bishops in prison; nothing says Peter was. Hefele: neither in prison when the split began (M8). **Contested.** Do not put Peter in prison.
- **X3. Date of the split.** 301 (Robertson, ATH 1638–1639), "about the year 304 or 305" (Hefele, HEF 22104–22105), "probably in A.D. 305" (Bell, BELL 3168). Modern only. Say "during the persecution".
- **X4. Peter's successor.** Epiphanius: Alexander (EPI 43879–43881); Athanasius: Achillas, then Alexander (ATH 24786–24788). Not needed.
- **X5. Year of the canons.** 306 (Hawkins, McGiffert) or 304 (Nikodemos), each from reckoning. Tell "the fourth Easter of the persecution".
- **X6. Length for those who fell from fear.** Three years (Balsamon) or four (Zonaras; Johnson). Give no number.
- **X7. Number of canons.** Fourteen or fifteen. Not needed.
- **X8. The day of death.** Later Egyptian reckoning (D4). Card only.
