## BLOCKING findings

**1. SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** "Eusebius of Caesarea, writing two generations after Origen's death from his letters and from people who knew him, tells most of his life."
- Source: EUS 32515-32517 (VI.2.1), "gathering them from certain letters, and from the statement of persons still living who were acquainted with him"; EUSG 882, "ἔκ τινων ἐπιστολῶν" ("from certain letters"). Eusebius does not say whose letters. The outline (Opening 1) gives "writing from letters and from people who had known him".
- The outline's "about two generations" (Who tells it) has also lost its "about". This is a rough gap, not a figure the old sources fix.
- Exact change: "Eusebius of Caesarea, writing about two generations after Origen's death from letters and from people who knew him, tells most of his life."

**2. SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** "He begins with a father at Alexandria, in Egypt, teaching his son Scripture every day."
- Source: Eusebius does not begin there. His account opens with the persecution under Severus and the father's beheading (EUS 32397-32460, VI.1), then the boy's longing for martyrdom, his mother hiding his clothes and his letter to his father (EUS 32536-32562, VI.2.3-6). Only after that comes the training in Scripture: "requiring him to learn and recite every day" (EUS 32584-32586, VI.2.8). The daily lessons are held; "he begins with" is not.
- Exact change: "His account shows a father at Alexandria, in Egypt, teaching his son Scripture every day."

**3. ASSUMPTION ADDED.** "At Caesarea two brothers came to him for Greek and Roman learning."
- Source: EUS 36568-36571, 36680-36681 (VI.30): "many pupils came to him … Among these Theodorus … and his brother Athenodorus … Finding them deeply interested in Greek and Roman learning, he infused into them a love of philosophy". Eusebius says what Origen found in them when they came. He gives no reason for their coming, and does not say they came for that learning. Outline Beat 6 holds the same wording, so the outline left room for this.
- Exact change: "At Caesarea two brothers came to study with him, and he found them deeply interested in Greek and Roman learning."
- Do not say line to add: "Two brothers came to him for Greek and Roman learning" → "two brothers came to study with him, and he found them deeply interested in Greek and Roman learning" (E22).

**4. Live-text echo.** "Scripture, he taught, has a body, a soul and a spirit, as a person does, and its plain sense helps many readers."
- The live text has: "Origen also taught that Scripture, like a person, has body, soul and spirit." The outline holds "body, soul and spirit" but not the "like a person / as a person does" turn. The source runs the other way round: "As man consists of body, soul, and spirit, so too does Scripture" (PHIL 76, I.11). The content holds (SUMMARY HOLDS, with PHIL 78 for "the multitudes of true and simple-minded believers" who profit by the plain sense). Only the wording echoes.
- Exact change: "As a human being is body, soul and spirit, he taught, so is Scripture, and its plain sense helps many readers."

**5. Live-text echo.** "Pamphilus of Caesarea and Eusebius, the same Eusebius who tells his life, wrote a book in his defense."
- The live text has: "Pamphilus of Caesarea and Eusebius wrote in his defense." The outline holds "Pamphilus and Eusebius wrote a Defense of him" (What came of it 2), not "in his defense". The content holds: EUS 35962-35963, "the Defense which we have composed in his behalf"; Photius gives the pair as authors (I4).
- Exact change: "Pamphilus of Caesarea and Eusebius, the same Eusebius who tells his life, wrote a Defense of him."

No other sentence echoes the live wording where the outline does not already hold it. The shared phrases are "set the Hebrew text beside the Greek translations", "teaching the faith", "a book of passages chosen from his writings", "both became bishops in Pontus", "his feet … in the stocks for many days" and "three centuries after his death, a council at Constantinople". All of them are already in the outline.
