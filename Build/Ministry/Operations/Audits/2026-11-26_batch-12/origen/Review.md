## BLOCKING findings

**B1. Sentence 1: Eusebius's sources (Trust 1; Trust_Second 1).** "from his letters" is not in the source. EUS 32515-32517 has "gathering them from certain letters, and from the statement of persons still living who were acquainted with him", and EUSG 882 has "ἔκ τινων ἐπιστολῶν". The outline's Who tells it also says "about two generations", and the brief has lost the "about". Trust_Second adds that point. **Change:** the change is in B2, which rewrites sentences 1 to 3 together.

**B2. Sentence 2: "He begins with a father at Alexandria" (Trust 2; Trust_Second 2; Skeptic tell).** Both trust readers mark it SOFTENED. Eusebius begins with the persecution under Severus and the father's beheading (EUS 32397-32460). The daily lessons come only at VI.2.7-8 (EUS 32565-32586). The skeptic counts it as the tell Part 1 names, "a report about the record". The first-time listener counts sentence 1 as a how-we-know citation said before the moment (MINOR). The outline says Eusebius is credited "once, early, inside the sentence that brings in the boyhood scene". **Change (sentences 1 to 3 together, with B1 and B3):**

> At Alexandria, in Egypt, Origen's father taught him Scripture every day. Eusebius of Caesarea, who tells most of Origen's life about two generations after his death, from letters and from people who knew him, says the boy was not content with the plain, obvious meaning. He kept asking what the inspired words really meant, until his father scolded him to his face, for appearance's sake, not to search past his age or past the plain meaning.

This change also settles the first-time listener's citation finding (5a). The middle sentence runs 35 words. That is over the 30-word target, but the target is not a cut.

**B3. Sentence 3: the second half of the rebuke is missing (Trust 1; Trust_Second marks it SUMMARY HOLDS).**
- **Source:** EUS 32596-32598 has "not to search beyond his age, or further than the manifest meaning". EUSG 1081-1082 has "μηδὲ τῆς προφανοῦς διανοίας περαιτέρω τι ζητεῖν".
- **The disagreement, judged by the bar:** this is BLOCKING. The bar blocks a sentence that softens its source, and the four faults name "the record's force … softened by leaving it out". The missing half is the one that ties the scene to the heart. The outline's Opening 1 holds it ("or past the plain sense"). Under the batch rule, a finding either trust read makes blocks until it is fixed.
- **Change:** in B2, "or past the plain meaning" is added at the end of the sentence.

**B4. "At Caesarea two brothers came to him for Greek and Roman learning" (Trust 4; Trust_Second 3). Needs the outline.**
- **The error:** an added assumption. EUS 36676-36684 says that, finding them already "deeply interested in Greek and Roman learning", Origen turned them to the study of God. Eusebius gives no reason for their coming.
- **Outline:** Beat 6 holds the same wording.
- **Change:** "At Caesarea two brothers came to study with him, and he found them keen on Greek and Roman learning."

**B5. "Scripture, he taught, has a body, a soul and a spirit, as a person does, and its plain sense helps many readers" (Trust_Second 4, live-text echo; FirstTime 4a and Skeptic 4.1, bare statement). Needs the outline.**
- **Why it blocks:** the listener learns that the body is the plain sense, but the soul and the spirit are only named. That is the slate's "Scripture read on more than one level", left half told. The "as a person does" turn also echoes the live text, and the outline does not hold that wording.
- **Source:** PHIL 76 (I.11) says "the simple may be edified by … the flesh of Scripture (for thus we designate the primary sense), the more advanced by its soul, and the perfect by the spiritual law … As man consists of body, soul, and spirit, so too does Scripture". The Source Sheet's row T4 holds this.
- **Outline:** Beat 4 cites T4 but states only "the three levels in one clause at most". It does not hold what the soul and spirit are.
- **Change (once T4's wording is added to Beat 4):** "As a human being is body, soul and spirit, he taught, so is Scripture: its body, the plain sense, helps simple believers, its soul those further on, and its spirit the perfect."
- **If the researcher declines:** "As a human being is body, soul and spirit, he taught, so is Scripture: its plain sense helps many readers, and deeper senses lie beneath it."

**B6. "Pamphilus of Caesarea and Eusebius, the same Eusebius who tells his life, wrote a book in his defense" (Trust_Second 5, live-text echo; FirstTime 4c and Skeptic 4.2, bare statement). Needs the outline.**
- **Why it blocks:** the book is named, but nothing says what it answered (Kit, Explain it). "In his defense" echoes the live text, and the outline does not hold that wording.
- **Outline:** it holds only "wrote a Defense of him" (I4). The Library does hold Rufinus's translation of Pamphilus's Defence, with its preface (`npnf203_theodoret-jerome-gennadius-rufinus.xml` 45080 ff.). The researcher should check there what the Defense answered.
- **Change:** "Pamphilus of Caesarea and Eusebius, the same Eusebius who tells his life, wrote a Defense of him, [what it answered, in one clause, once the outline holds it]."
- **If the Library gives no answer:** cut the sentence, and the Defense goes to the card.
- **Not taken:** the first-time listener's 5b, which would drop "the same Eusebius who tells his life". The outline's Who tells it asks that the listener hear Eusebius is a friend of his subject.

**B7. The close jumps back in time (FirstTime, Order; Skeptic 3.1). Needs the outline.**
- **The order now:** Basil and Gregory (the next century), then the Defense (earlier), then the council (about three centuries on), then Gregory and the Philocalia (back to the next century). The bar counts a jump back after the essence as a failure to understand on one hearing.
- **Why the outline must change:** Ending 1, the preferred ending, closes on the Philocalia. That ending cannot come last without a jump back, because the council is later.
- **Not taken:** the skeptic's second option, a marked return ("Long before that, Gregory …"). It is still a jump back.
- **Change:** tell what came of him in time order and close on the council. The order is the Defense, Socrates on Basil, Gregory and the Arians, then Gregory and the Philocalia with its first chapter, then the council:

> Pamphilus of Caesarea and Eusebius, the same Eusebius who tells his life, wrote a Defense of him[, B6 clause]. In the next century, the church historian Socrates says, Basil of Caesarea and Gregory of Nazianzus got his books and drew from them the right reading of Scripture. When the Arians, who taught that the Son of God had a beginning, quoted Origen, he adds, the two showed that the Arians misunderstood him.
>
> Gregory also sent a friend the Philocalia of Origen, a book of passages chosen from his writings for students. Its first chapter is on how Scripture is to be read and understood, and why, taken literally, it sometimes seems impossible.
>
> About three centuries after his death, a council of bishops at Constantinople named him, with his writings, among the heretics.

- **The new close still ends on the subject.** It is the council's own act on him and his writings, as the record gives it, with no narrator line after it.
- **For the outline:** Ending 1 becomes "the council (I7), told last, after the Philocalia". Ending 2, his death, would send the impact to the card, so it is not recommended.
