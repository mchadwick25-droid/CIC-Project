## BLOCKING 1. The Defense: the cut rests on a "not held" that is held, and the slate's Pamphilus clause is lost

- **The error.** `Fixes_Review.md` (Worded by me, B6) cut the Defense because "the held copy (NP3 45080-45203) is not the Defense itself" and nothing held says what Pamphilus answered. NP3 is only Rufinus's preface, true. But the Library holds Book I of the Defense itself, in Rufinus's Latin: `pamphilus_apologia-pro-origene-rufinus-lat_lommatzsch1846.txt` (also `origen_opera-omnia-t7-pamphilus-apologia-pg17-grc-lat_migne1857.txt`). The Source Sheet names both under Set aside (line 166) and under Not held (line 168, "Rufinus's Latin of Pamphilus's Book I"). Its preface says what the book answered (Lommatzsch 14630-14706): "omnium, qui ei obtrectant, confutare calumnias … non nostris verbis … sed ex suis propriis vocibus, quibus ipse aliena sibi haec esse, quae isti objiciunt, proprio sermone testatur, id est, omne quicquid praeter fidem Catholicam praedicatur"; and the books "quos accusatores ejus quam maxime criminantur … hos enim asserunt maxime a praedicatione ecclesiastica discrepare: ex his praecipue libris, quos περὶ ἀρχῶν attitulavit". My rendering: "to refute the slanders of all who disparage him … not in our own words, but from his own, in which he himself testifies that what they charge is foreign to him, that is, whatever is preached outside the Catholic faith"; "the books his accusers most attack … for they claim these most differ from the church's preaching: above all the books he titled On First Principles". So the Review's first branch of B6 is open, and Explain it is met from an old source.
- **Ruling on the sibling job.** It still needs the clause. The batch header (Siblings) gives Pamphilus "a clause here", and the Defense is the one thing in this story that is what others defended for him (Kit Part 2, What came of it). The cut leaves the story with no defender of Origen's books in his own century. Methodius's clause went to the card at an earlier, checked step; that ruling stands and is not reopened here.
- **The cheapest exact line for the story** (23 words), first in What came of it, before Socrates: "Pamphilus of Caesarea wrote a Defense of him, answering from his own words those who said his books differed from the church's teaching." Pamphilus alone, since the held Book I is his (Photius: five of the six books by Pamphilus, I4); Eusebius's share stays on the card. Documented as Pamphilus's Book I in Rufinus's Latin (old; Pamphilus about two generations after Origen's death; Rufinus's translation 397). No quote: narrated.
- **Length.** The fixed outline stands at about 950 words; this line adds 23, so about **973 words**. That is inside the Review's own accepted range for "about 950" (950 to 976), and needs no offset.
