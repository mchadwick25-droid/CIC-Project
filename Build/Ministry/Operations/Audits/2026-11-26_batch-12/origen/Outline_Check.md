# Outline check: Origen (`origen`)

## My own account of the heart (written before reading `Outline.md`)

From the census entry (`knownFor`, `keyWorks`, `legacy`), the source sheet and what Origen is known for:

- **The essence:** Origen, a martyr's son who took over the Christian teaching at Alexandria while still a teenager, became the early church's great reader of Scripture. His question was how to read the Bible rightly: he held that its errors of reading (literalist Jews, heretics who made a second god, simple believers who thought unworthy things of God) came from taking it only by the bare letter, and taught that it has a body, soul and spirit, a meaning "worthy of God" beneath the letter. He is on the tree because that way of reading, his textual work and his books carried on through his pupils and later readers, and because he was argued over for centuries.
- **Also told:** the Hexapla (the Hebrew set beside the Greek translations, made so as not to bring a forgery on the churches and to argue with Jews from their own text); philosophy and the liberal studies taught as a help to Scripture; the break with Demetrius over his ordination at Caesarea (with the self-castration as Demetrius's charge, credited to Eusebius, and the order of events contested); the move to Caesarea and its pupils (Gregory and Athenodorus); the books (On First Principles, the commentaries, Against Celsus); the tortures under Decius, survived, and death in Gallus's time at sixty-nine.
- **What came of him:** his reading of Scripture carried on by Basil and Gregory of Nazianzus (the Philocalia; Socrates IV.26, defended against the Arians' use of him); the Hexapla used by Jerome (defended: Jerome's translation); his library at Caesarea, kept by Pamphilus and Eusebius, who wrote his Defense (defended); his pupils (Gregory, Athenodorus, Dionysius); Athanasius quoting him for the Son (defended); attacks by Methodius, Epiphanius and later, and the 553 anathema (Contested in its text).

Shape: Teacher or thinker. Trap: a list of books.

## Heart comparison

The outline's heart matches mine in substance: the question of what Scripture means beneath its plain words, his own account of why readers go wrong, the columns, the break with Demetrius, Decius, and what came of it through Basil, Gregory, Jerome, the Defense, Methodius and 553. On First Principles and Against Celsus as books go to the card (Against Celsus to Leave out, marked for the card), which suits the shape's trap ("a list of books"). There is no blocking difference. The beats give the essence in the first minute (Opening 1, then the essence), follow the story once in order (Alexandria, the deed, the columns, the teaching, the break and move, Caesarea, Decius and death), and do not tell the ending early.

## Findings

### BLOCKING

1. **The length runs past 675 even with the Cut first list taken out.** The independent `Length_Count.md` gives 782 words with every Cut first item removed (942 with them kept). My recount agrees: about 76 words of quotes plus about 20 of other quoted spans, 9 glosses (72), about 9 witness credits (54), and about 52 facts (about 570), for 770 to 790 words. The outline's own "about 650", and its claim that the Cut first list brings "about 840" down to 650, do not hold. Finding 2 below adds about 15 words back. **Correction:** move at least about 125 words out of the beats and into Leave out or the card, without touching the heart, the three quotes, the break's two accounts, Porphyry's charge, the tortures or the judge. For example:
   - Beat 1: the letter to his father (E2), about 11 words, to the card. Also "believers from all Egypt were brought to Alexandria" (S3), about 11 words, to Leave out.
   - Beat 3: Jerome's "against the habit of his time and people" with its credit (E11), about 17 words, to the card. Also the jar at Jericho (E10), about 11 words, to the card.
   - Beat 4: Porphyry's first passage, "riddles full of hidden mysteries" (T10), about 22 words, to the card. Keep T11 and Quote 2 with its setup (see MINOR 3).
   - Beat 7: "Jerome says he was buried at Tyre" (S7), about 17 words, to the card. Keep "in the time of Gallus, at sixty-nine". Also "letters full of comfort" (E28), about 11 words, to the card.
   - What came of it 2: Methodius and his charge (I5, T16), about 28 words, to the card; the `methodius-of-olympus` sibling tells it. Keep the Defense and the council. Then the Connection field's "Methodius first" becomes "the same books drew defenders, Pamphilus and Eusebius, and a council named him among heretics three centuries on".

   Then restate **Length** as the recounted figure, at or under 675. The recheck recounts it.

2. **Cut first item 5 drops why the columns were made.** It cuts T13, Origen's own reason for setting the Hebrew beside the Greek, and keeps only what the columns were. The Kit (Explain it) requires both what a named thing was and why it mattered to the people in it. **Correction:** delete Cut first item 5. In Beat 3, make the reason one clause: "His reason, in his letter to a fellow scholar: to keep the churches' Greek, so as not to bring a forgery on the churches, and still know the Jews' readings, so as not to quote to them what their copies lacked (T13, narrate)." Change the Cut first total line to match.

3. **Beat 4 misstates the heretics' view.** The outline says they "invented a second, lesser God", and T1 says "made a second, imperfect God". The source says the reverse: they took the Creator (the Demiurge) for imperfect and said Jesus proclaimed "a more perfect God" above him (PHIL 73; A4 33350-33352). **Correction:** in Beat 4 and T1, write "heretics, reading of God's anger and jealousy, took the God of the Jewish Scriptures for an imperfect, lesser maker of the world, and said Jesus had proclaimed a more perfect God above him." Add to Do not say: "heretics invented a lesser God" → "they took the Creator for a lesser God and set a more perfect one above him" (PHIL 73).

4. **Beat 5 moves Demetrius's first letter onto the wrong event in Eusebius's account.** In EUS 33217-33233 (VI.8.4-5), Demetrius, seeing Origen prosper, wrote of the deed as most foolish to bishops everywhere *before* the Palestinian bishops ordained him. Only after the ordination did he accuse Origen bitterly, along with those who ordained him. The beat tells the ordination first and then "Eusebius says Demetrius … wrote to bishops everywhere that the deed … was most foolish". That places the deed-letter after the ordination. **Correction:** after "Then the sources part", write: "Eusebius says Demetrius, seeing him grow famous, had already written to bishops everywhere that the deed of his youth was most foolish; after the ordination, with nothing else to say against him, he accused him bitterly, and those who had ordained him too (E17)." Add to Do not say: "after the ordination Demetrius wrote that the deed was foolish" (as Eusebius's account) → Eusebius puts that letter before the ordination and the accusation after it (E17).

### MINOR

1. **Opening 1 puts "they say" over the whole scene.** In EUS 32599-32605 and EUSG (φασίν), "they say" covers only the father uncovering and kissing the sleeping boy's chest. The questions and the rebuke are Eusebius's own telling. **Correction:** in Opening 1 and E3, change "tells it as 'they say'" to "tells the kiss as 'they say'". Add to Do not say: the kiss told in the narrator's voice → "they say he often bent over the sleeping boy and kissed his chest" (E3).
2. **"Searched out every Greek translation" (Beat 3) says more than its source.** EUS 34427-34448 says he investigated "other translators … besides the Seventy" and brought some to light. **Correction:** replace "every Greek translation" with "other Greek translations besides the Seventy". Add to Do not say: "every translation" → "other translations".
3. **Quote 2's "these writers" means Chaeremon and Cornutus,** the Stoics whose books he used (EUSG 3594-3598; EUS 35109-35124), and not Plato. The beat's setup ("always reading Plato and the philosophers") would point it at the wrong writers. **Correction:** make the setup "…he was always with Plato, Porphyry wrote, and used the books of two Stoic writers (T11)", then Quote 2. Add to Beat 4: "Porphyry met him when Porphyry himself was young". Add to Do not say: "Porphyry met him as a young man" (ambiguous) → "Porphyry met him when Porphyry was young" (EUS 34984-34986).
4. **Simple believers (T3) rests on Crombie, and the two translators differ.** Crombie (A4 33355-33358) has "the more simple among those who profess to belong to the Church". Lewis (PHIL 73) has "the less sophisticated of those who in their self-confidence have left the Church". The beat's "unworthy of a just man" softens both. **Correction:** in Beat 4, write "simple believers in the church imagined things of God they would not believe of the most savage and unjust man (T3, Crombie)". Record Lewis's different reading in T3.
5. **Beat 7 omits why he suffered.** Eusebius says he endured it all "for the word of Christ" (EUS 37489-37490). **Correction:** add "for the word of Christ" after "Origen endured". Add to Do not say: "tortured to make him deny Christ" or "the judge kept him alive to break him" → no motive is given; "his judge strove with all his might not to end his life" (E28).
6. **Beat 5 leaves out the final move.** S4 (EUS 36330-36334) gives it: he removed to Caesarea and left the teaching to Heraclas. **Correction:** before Quote 3, add "He left Alexandria for Caesarea (S4)", with no year.
7. **Beat 3 and the place of the columns.** Eusebius gives no time or place for making them, only that one translation was found in the time of Antoninus, son of Severus (EUS 34705-34718). **Correction:** add to Do not say: "at Alexandria he made the Hexapla" or "at Caesarea he made…" → no place or time; "he set them side by side in columns" (E10).
8. **The Connection field says more than its source.** "Two brothers from Pontus … went home bishops" goes beyond E22, which says only that they became bishops "in the churches of Pontus". Jerome has them come from Cappadocia (NP3 40775-40787). **Correction:** "Two brothers studied with him at Caesarea for five years and then became bishops in the churches of Pontus (E22)."
9. **The beats carry extra quoted spans beyond the three quotes.** Beat 5 has "all the winds of wickedness in Egypt" (E20). **Correction:** narrate it ("his enemy, he wrote, stirred up all Egypt against him", unnamed), or drop it under BLOCKING 1. Keep the eunuch saying as the gloss already gives it.
10. **On First Principles is named without saying what it was.** **Correction:** in Beat 4, write "a book, On First Principles, in the part that tells how Scripture is to be read (T14; PHIL 44)". Do not let it stand as a book only about reading.
11. **The Library sweep missed a held own-words passage on the eunuch saying.** Commentary on Matthew XV.1, Greek, Lommatzsch tomus 3 (`origen_commentarii-in-evangelium-matthaei-tomus3-grc-lat_lommatzsch1834.txt` 19508-19520), says that some, taking the saying bodily, dared to do it "from fear of God, but without understanding", and brought reproach on themselves. It names no one, Origen included. **Correction:** add it to the sheet as a row (own; Documented) for the card. Add to Do not say: "Origen later regretted it" or "confessed it" → never. He writes only of "some" who took the saying bodily (Comm. Matt. XV.1).
12. **The opening overlaps the home story.** `alexandria-catechetical` already tells, in narration, the teachers driven out and the pagans who came to him in his eighteenth year (E6). **Correction:** in Beat 1, keep E6 to one clause, and add it to the Sibling line for `alexandria-catechetical`.
13. **Ending 1 closes on a later writer.** **Correction:** close on the Philocalia's first chapter heading, Origen's own subject (PHIL 44), without a narrator line tying it back to the boy. The Kit bars a summing-up close.
