## 1. The test

- **Who this was:** filled. Origen, a Christian teacher and Bible scholar of Alexandria in Egypt, later of Caesarea in Palestine.
- **What happened:** filled. A boy who asked what Scripture really meant; a teacher for his bishop; castrated himself; set Hebrew and Greek texts in columns; wrote on reading Scripture; ordained at Caesarea, broke with his bishop, left Egypt; tortured under Decius; died at sixty-nine.
- **What it was about:** mostly filled. I can say: Scripture has a meaning beneath the letter, and the impossible things in it push the reader to look for it. The body, soul and spirit idea I cannot explain (see 4a).
- **Why the connection matters:** part-filled, BLOCKING. I hear that later bishops used his books, that two friends defended him, and that a council later called him a heretic. But I cannot say what people "fought over for centuries", what his defenders defended, or why the council condemned him. I also cannot say who carried his teaching on: the two brothers are unnamed and I don't know why their becoming bishops in Pontus matters. Change: the beats on what came of him must each say what was at stake, as 4c and 4d give.

## 2. The opening

After three sentences I have **who** (Origen, a boy taught Scripture by his father), **where** (Alexandria, in Egypt) and **why there** (his father taught him). **When** is missing, and it stays missing all through: no century is ever said, and "Decius", "Gallus", "two generations after" and "the next century" give me nothing to place him by. MINOR. Change: add the century in a clause where the outline holds it, for example in sentence 2: "He begins with a father at Alexandria, in Egypt, about two hundred years after Jesus..." (only as the outline rates it).

Sentence 1 also names two people I don't know (Eusebius, Origen) before any moment, and "his letters" could be Eusebius's or Origen's. See 5a.

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing

a. "He kept the churches' Greek, he explained in a letter to a fellow scholar, so as not to bring a forgery on them." Lost: what forgery? I don't hear that changing the churches' text would look like bringing in a fake Bible. MINOR.

b. "The other side comes from Porphyry, a pagan philosopher who later wrote against the Christians..." Lost: the other side of what? No argument was set up before it, and "His charge turns on the secret rites of Greek religion" does not tell me what the charge is, or why borrowing a way of reading is an accusation. This is near the line: it is where I nearly lost the thread. MINOR.

c. "His judge did all he could not to let him die." Lost: on one hearing the judge sounds merciful. I don't hear that he was kept alive so the torture could go on. MINOR; the trust read should check this does not mislead.

d. "In the next century, the historian Socrates says..." Lost: I hear the famous Greek philosopher Socrates. "the historian" goes by too fast. MINOR. Change: "a church historian of the next century, Socrates, says..." or give him by role only.

e. "Here the old sources part." Lost: "part" as "disagree" is not a sense I know at once; the next sentences rescue it. MINOR. Change: "Here the old sources disagree."

Also heard with effort: "the deed of his youth was most foolish". It took me a moment to link it back to the castration three paragraphs earlier. Not counted.

## 4. Bare statements (BLOCKING)

a. "Scripture, he taught, has a body, a soul and a spirit, as a person does, and its plain sense helps many readers." I learn the body is the plain sense. Soul and spirit are named and never said. This is the story's one idea, and it is half-told. Change: say what the soul and spirit levels are, from the outline; or keep only the body and the "stumbling blocks" line and cut "a soul and a spirit".

b. "Jerome... says the ordination itself so enraged Demetrius that he wrote everywhere against him." And: "After the ordination... Demetrius accused him bitterly." I can't tell why being ordained at Caesarea was wrong in Demetrius's eyes. Was it the castration, or another bishop ordaining his teacher? Change: give the reason where the old sources give it; if they give only the act, the trust read confirms that and this falls away.

c. "Pamphilus of Caesarea and Eusebius... wrote a book in his defense." Defense against what? Change: say what charge the book answered, from the outline.

d. "...a council of bishops at Constantinople named him, with his writings, among the heretics, those it judged false teachers." I hear the label and its gloss, but not what he was condemned for. Change: say what the council condemned, where the outline holds it.

## 5. Citations read aloud not called for (MINOR)

a. The opening sentence, "Eusebius of Caesarea, writing two generations after Origen's death from his letters and from people who knew him, tells most of his life. He begins with a father at Alexandria..." This is a how-we-know sentence before the moment, and "He begins with" makes the story a report about Eusebius's book. Narrator's voice: "At Alexandria, in Egypt, a father taught his son Origen the Scriptures every day. Eusebius of Caesarea, who wrote two generations later from Origen's letters and from people who knew him, says the boy was not content with the plain, obvious meaning..." (the credit then sits once, early, inside the moment).

b. "...and Eusebius, the same Eusebius who tells his life, wrote a book in his defense." This reminder is a citation. Narrator's voice: "Pamphilus of Caesarea and Eusebius wrote a book in his defense."

The other "Eusebius says" credits (the too-literal reading, the sources differing on Demetrius, Decius's motive), Jerome, and Socrates read to me as needed: each credits a judgment, a disagreement or a later witness.

## 7. Words, runs, tone

- "impious" (in the quote): give the plain sense alongside, or "without reverence for God".
- "Stoic philosopher": "a philosopher of the Stoic school" still says nothing to me. Give it by role ("a Greek philosopher") or gloss it.
- "figurative" (Porphyry quote): give the plain sense alongside, e.g. "reading them as pictures of something else".
- "Pontus": where? Add "in the north of what is now Turkey", if the outline holds it.
- "four spaces in the stocks": what is a space? Add the plain sense if the outline has it.
- "Cornutus": a name that does nothing. Give him by role or cut him.

"eunuchs" works because "castrated himself" follows. "presbyter, an elder, a priest" works. No runs of short one-act sentences. Nothing talks down.

## 8. Pull, drift, finish

- **Pull:** yes, from sentence 2. The father who scolds the boy in public, then kisses his sleeping chest, holds me. Sentence 1 delays it.
- **First drift:** paragraph 4, at "He kept the churches' Greek... so as not to bring a forgery on them." The columns were already told in paragraph 2, so it felt like a repeat. The reason given there is abstract, and the "forgery" sentence loses me (3a). Second drift: the Porphyry paragraph (3b), because I don't know what side or charge it is.
- **Finish:** yes, I would listen to the end. The break with Demetrius, the "out of the storm" quote and the torture pull me back. The last two paragraphs feel like a list of later books and judgments.

## Order heard (BLOCKING)

The close goes back in time. I hear Basil and Gregory "in the next century", then the council "about three centuries after his death", and then Gregory of Nazianzus sending the Philocalia. That is back to the next century. It ends on a later book, not on Origen. The bar counts a jump back after the essence as a failure to understand. Change: move the Philocalia paragraph up, next to the Socrates sentences on Basil and Gregory, so that what came of him runs forward in time to the council. Then close on the subject as the outline's Ending gives it.
