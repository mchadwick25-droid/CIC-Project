# Fixes from the review: Origen (`origen`)

The four findings `Review.md` marks "needs the outline" (B4, B5, B6, B7), applied to `Outline.md` only. Line numbers are of `Outline.md` as it now stands. Files are under `cic/texts/`.

## Applied verbatim

- **B4, the brothers.** Beat 6 (line 45): "Two brothers came to study with him, and he found them keen on Greek and Roman learning; he gave them a love of philosophy …" The review's wording, checked at EUS 36568-36569 ("many pupils came to him") and 36679-36684 ("Finding them deeply interested in Greek and Roman learning"; "Remaining with him five years"). Eusebius gives no reason for their coming.
- **B5, the soul and the spirit.** Beat 4 (line 43): T4's sense added in the review's wording: "as a human being is body, soul and spirit, so is Scripture; its body, the plain sense, helps simple believers, its soul those further on, and its spirit the perfect." Checked at PHIL 76 (I.11): "that the simple may be edified by, as it were, the flesh of Scripture (for thus we designate the primary sense), the more advanced by its soul, and the perfect by the spiritual law … As man consists of body, soul, and spirit, so too does Scripture".
- **Do not say lines, word for word from the review** (lines 104-107): the brothers (E22); body, soul and spirit (T4); the Defense (I4); What came of it in time order (I4, I1, I2, I7). The review's three other Do not say lines belong to B1-B3, which are fixes to the brief, not the outline, and are not added here.

## Worded by me

- **B5, framing in Beat 4** (line 43): "The three levels in one clause at most, with whom each level serves (T4): …" The old "one clause at most" rule is kept; "with whom each level serves" is the review's Do not say wording.
- **B6, the Defense cut; it goes to the card.** The held copy (NP3 45080-45203) is not the Defense itself but Rufinus's translation preface (397). It gives Rufinus's own creed and his fear of "those who consider themselves aggrieved whenever any one does not think evil of Origen" (45143-45144), and says only that "The opinions of Origen in the various parts of Scripture are clearly set forth in the present work" (45164-45165). It does not say what Pamphilus answered. Eusebius says only that they wrote it "on account of faultfinders" (EUS 36974-36976, VI.33.4), which names no charge, so a clause from it would name the book without explaining it. The length ruling is about 950, so the sentence is cut. The changes that follow from the cut:
  - What came of it 2 (line 49): "Pamphilus and Eusebius wrote a Defense of him (I4; **defended**)." removed; the line now begins "**The fight.** Athanasius …".
  - Card (line 52): "the Defense by Pamphilus and Eusebius, written, Eusebius says, "on account of faultfinders" (EUS 36974-36976), with its five books from prison (I4),".
  - Sibling (line 34): "Pamphilus: his Defense of Origen and his library (I4, I10) go to the card; his own story tells them." and "Eusebius: the main witness, credited once (S1); his share in the Defense goes to the card (I4)."
  - Names (line 58): "Pamphilus of Caesarea (card). Athanasius of Alexandria (link; Cut first)."
  - Connection (line 59): "A council named him among heretics three centuries on (I7)." in place of the sentence naming Pamphilus and Eusebius as defenders.
  - Who tells it, Eusebius (line 66): "He wrote a Defense of Origen with Pamphilus (I4): card." The old ask that the listener hear Eusebius is a friend of his subject rested on the Defense, so it falls with the cut.
  - Cut first 3 (line 127): "keep the council" in place of "keep the Defense and the council".
- **B7, the ending** (line 61): Ending 1 now reads: "The council (I7), told last, after the Philocalia. What came of it is told in time order: Basil and Gregory and the Arians (I1); then Gregory of Nazianzus sending a friend "the Philocalia of Origen", passages chosen from him for students, whose first chapter is how Scripture is to be read and understood, and why, taken literally, it sometimes seems impossible (I2, PHIL 44); then, about three centuries after his death, a council at Constantinople naming him, with his writings, among the heretics (I7). Close on the council's own act on him and his writings, as the record gives it, with no narrator line after it and none tying it back to the boy. Preferred." The council's wording is the outline's own (I7, N14 23852-23860); the review's "of bishops" is not added. The "Ending 1" marker moved from What came of it 1 (line 48, now "(I2).") to the council in What came of it 2 (line 49, "(I7; Ending 1)").
