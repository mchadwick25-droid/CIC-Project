## BLOCKING

**1. Paragraph 6, sentence 2. SOFTENED OR CHANGED.**
- Brief: "Yet God himself had set stumbling blocks in it, things impossible if read by the letter, so that readers would look for a meaning worthy of God."
- Source, PHIL 81 (I.16, Lewis from the Greek): "The Word of God therefore arranged for certain stumbling-blocks and offences and impossibilities to be embedded in the Law and the historical portion". Crombie, from the Greek (A4 33655-33668), agrees: "the word of God has arranged that certain stumbling-blocks, as it were, and offences, and impossibilities, should be introduced". Later in the same section Origen also credits the Spirit (PHIL 81: "not only did the Spirit thus deal with the Scriptures").
- Why it blocks: Origen names the Word (the Logos) as the one who arranged this. "God himself" puts in a different agent and adds an emphasis that is not in the source. Origen draws this line between the Word and God carefully. The outline's Beat 4 holds "God himself", but the check is against the source, not the outline.
- Exact change: "Yet the Word of God, he wrote, had set stumbling blocks in it, things impossible if read by the letter, so that readers would look for a meaning worthy of God."
- Do not say, for the outline: "God himself set stumbling blocks" → "the Word of God arranged stumbling blocks" (PHIL 81; A4 33655-33668).

## SUMMARY HOLDS (not blocking; listed with their evidence)

- **P1 s2**, "Eusebius ... tells most of Origen's life about two generations after his death, from letters and from people who knew him". EUS 32511-32517: "gathering them from certain letters, and from the statement of persons still living who were acquainted with him." The rough gap is as loose as the old sources allow: Eusebius writes of Porphyry as living "in our own times" (EUS 34940-34973).
- **P1 s3**, "He kept asking ... until his father scolded him ... for appearance's sake". EUS 32584-32605: "puzzled his father with inquiries ... his father rebuked him seemingly to his face". EUSG τῷ μὲν δοκεῖν. The word "until" is a fair link between the two acts.
- **P1 s4**, the kiss in "they say". EUS 32601-32605: "they say that often, standing by the boy when asleep, he uncovered his breast as if the Divine Spirit were enshrined within it, and kissed it reverently". The brief leaves out "as if the Divine Spirit were enshrined within it". The outline leaves it out too. Its force is not changed. Optional, noted only: the kiss would carry its meaning with "as if God's Spirit lived within him" added after "uncovered his chest".
- **P2 s1**, "That question ... became Origen's life's work." This is the essence the outline approved. It compresses fairly what Eusebius says, "So earnest and assiduous was Origen's research into the divine words" (EUS 34427), together with PHIL 73-74.
- **P2 s2**, "on the coast of Palestine". EUS 35303-35317: "he went to Palestine and abode in Cæsarea". Caesarea's place on the coast is settled geography (Widely Accepted). It is not an editor's claim.
- **P2 s3**, "bishops and scholars after him read Scripture through his books and fought over them for centuries". N2 13913-13922 (Basil and Gregory), PAM 14630-14706 (the Defense), N14 23852-23860 (the council). This is a fair compression of the later sections.
- **P4 s3**, "He kept the churches' Greek ... so that he would not be found passing a forgery to the churches". A4 (Letter to Africanus): "I paid particular attention to the interpretation of the Seventy, lest I might to be found to accredit any forgery to the Churches". It also rejects the idea that we should "reject as spurious the copies in use in our Churches" (A4 35246-35262).
- **P5 s2**, "The unbelieving Jews ... followed the bare letter ... and did not receive Jesus". PHIL 73: "Hardhearted and unlearned readers belonging to the Circumcision have not believed on our Saviour ... follow the bare letter ... they did not receive Jesus".
- **P5 s3**, the heretics. PHIL 73: "'I am a jealous God' ... believing them to have come from the Demiurge, Whom the Jews worship ... imperfect and lacking in goodness, they suppose our Saviour ... to have proclaimed a more perfect God". A4 33340-33361 agrees.
- **P5 s4**, "Simple believers imagined things of God ...". A4 33355-33361 (Crombie, from the Greek): "the more simple among those who profess to belong to the Church ... imagine regarding Him such things as would not be believed of the most savage and unjust of mankind." Lewis (PHIL 73) reads "those who in their self-confidence have left the Church". The Greek is not held. The brief follows Crombie's sense and does not join the two readings, so it holds. Note for the card: say that the translators differ here.
- **P7 s1**, "Porphyry, a pagan philosopher who had met Origen when Porphyry was young and later wrote against the Christians, attacked this way of reading." EUS 34940-34991: "wrote books against us ... attempting especially to slander Origen, whom he says he knew in his youth"; "a man whom I met when I was young". "Philosopher" is the standard old title for him (for example, Augustine, City of God, `npnf102_augustine-city-of-god-christian-doctrine.xml`).
- **P7 s2**, "He tied it to the secret rites of Greek religion." This is the content of Quote 2: EUSG 3598-3602 τῶν παρ᾽ Ἕλλησιν μυστηρίων; EUS 35109-35124.
- **P8 s4**, "After the ordination, with nothing else to say against him, Demetrius accused him bitterly". EUS 33217-33233: "having nothing else that he could say against him, save this deed of his boyhood, accused him bitterly". The sentence before it makes the deed the charge, so "else" carries "save this deed".
- **P14 s1**, "a council of bishops at Constantinople named him, with his writings, among the heretics." N14 23852-23860: "If anyone does not anathematize ... Eutyches and Origen, as well as their impious writings, as also all other heretics ...". "About three centuries" is the rough gap.
