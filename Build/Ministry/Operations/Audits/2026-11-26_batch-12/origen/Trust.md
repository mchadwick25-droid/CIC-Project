## BLOCKING findings

**1. Sentence 1. SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** "Eusebius of Caesarea, writing two generations after Origen's death from his letters and from people who knew him, tells most of his life."
- Source, EUS 32511-32517 (VI.2.1): "gathering them from certain letters, and from the statement of persons still living who were acquainted with him."
- Eusebius says "certain letters", not Origen's own. The outline (Opening 1, Who tells it) says "from letters".
- Change: "...writing two generations after Origen's death from letters and from people who knew him, tells most of his life."

**2. Sentence 2. SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** "He begins with a father at Alexandria, in Egypt, teaching his son Scripture every day."
- Source: Eusebius begins with the persecution under Severus and the father's beheading (EUS 32397-32460, VI.1), then the boy's longing for martyrdom, the hidden clothes and the letter to his father (EUS 32536-32562, VI.2.3-6). The teaching comes only at VI.2.7-8 (EUS 32565-32586: "First of all, before inducting him into the Greek sciences, he drilled him in sacred studies, requiring him to learn and recite every day").
- "Begins with" moves the scene to the wrong place in the record.
- Change: "He tells of a father at Alexandria, in Egypt, teaching his son Scripture every day."

**3. Sentence 3. SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** "...until his father scolded him to his face, for appearance's sake, not to search past his age."
- Source, EUS 32596-32598 (VI.2.10): "his father rebuked him seemingly to his face, telling him not to search beyond his age, or further than the manifest meaning." EUSG 1081-1082: "μηδὲν ὑπὲρ ἡλικίαν μηδὲ τῆς προφανοῦς διανοίας περαιτέρω τι ζητεῖν".
- The rebuke's second half, not past the plain meaning, is cut. It is the half that ties the scene to the heart. The outline's Opening 1 holds it ("not to search beyond his age or past the plain sense").
- Change: "...until his father scolded him to his face, for appearance's sake, not to search past his age or past the plain meaning."

**4. Sentence 36. ASSUMPTION ADDED.** "At Caesarea two brothers came to him for Greek and Roman learning."
- Source, EUS 36568-36571, 36676-36684 (VI.30): "many pupils came to him ... Among these Theodorus ... and his brother Athenodorus ... Finding them deeply interested in Greek and Roman learning, he infused into them a love of philosophy, and led them to exchange their old zeal for the study of divinity."
- Eusebius gives no purpose for their coming. He says Origen found them already keen on Greek and Roman learning, and turned that zeal elsewhere. "Came to him for" adds a reason. The outline's Beat 6 carries the same wording, so this is an error the outline left room for.
- Change: "At Caesarea two brothers came to study with him, keen on Greek and Roman learning."
- Do not say, for the outline: "Two brothers came to him for Greek and Roman learning" → "came to him as pupils; he found them keen on Greek and Roman learning" (E22).
