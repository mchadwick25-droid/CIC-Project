## Each sentence

1. "At Alexandria, in Egypt, Origen's father taught him Scripture every day." SUMMARY HOLDS. EUS VI.1 (32433) places Leonides, "the father of Origen", at Alexandria. VI.2.8 (EUS 32590ff.): "he drilled him in sacred studies, requiring him to learn and recite every day."
2. "Eusebius of Caesarea, who tells most of Origen's life ... from letters and from people who knew him, says the boy was not content with the plain, obvious meaning." HELD. EUS 32511-32517: "gathering them from certain letters, and from the statement of persons still living who were acquainted with him"; VI.2.9: "not satisfied with learning what was simple and obvious in the sacred words." "About two generations" is the outline's witness date (S1).
3. "He kept asking ... not to search past his age or past the plain meaning." HELD. VI.2.9-10: "puzzled his father with inquiries for the true meaning of the inspired Scriptures ... rebuked him seemingly to his face, telling him not to search beyond his age, or further than the manifest meaning."
4. "Learning of it, Demetrius admired his daring and urged him to keep teaching." HELD. EUS 33196-33215 (VI.8.3): "admired greatly the daring nature of the act ... urged him the more to continue his work of catechetical instruction."
5. "He kept the churches' Greek ... so that he would not be found passing a forgery to the churches." HELD. A4 35276-35285 (Letter to Africanus 5): "lest I might to be found to accredit any forgery to the Churches."
6. "Yet he learned the Jews' readings, so that in arguments with Jews he would not quote what their copies lacked." HELD. Same: "lest in my controversies with the Jews I should quote to them what is not found in their copies."
7. "Heretics, teachers he judged false, reading of God's anger and jealousy, took the God of the Jewish Scriptures for an imperfect, lesser maker of the world ..." SUMMARY HOLDS. PHIL 73 (I.8): "heretics when they read the words, 'A fire is kindled in mine anger' ... holding that the Demiurge is imperfect." The added words are a gloss of "heretics", Origen's own judgment of them. Fine as it stands.
8. "As a human being is body, soul and spirit, he taught, so is Scripture: its body, the plain sense, helps simple believers, its soul those further on, and its spirit the perfect." HELD. PHIL 76 (I.11): "the simple may be edified by, as it were, the flesh of Scripture (for thus we designate the primary sense), the more advanced by its soul, and the perfect by the spiritual law ... As man consists of body, soul, and spirit, so too does Scripture." This is the outline's own wording (Beat 4).
9. "Porphyry, a pagan philosopher who had met Origen when Porphyry was young and later wrote against the Christians, attacked this way of reading." HELD. EUS VI.19.2 (34980-34995): "wrote books against us ... attempting especially to slander Origen, whom he says he knew in his youth." VI.19.4 (34975): "Some persons ... have had recourse to explanations inconsistent and incongruous with the words written ... they boast that the plain words of Moses are enigmas."
10. "He tied it to the secret rites of Greek religion." SUMMARY HOLDS. EUS 35109-35124: "Becoming acquainted through them with the figurative interpretation of the Grecian mysteries, he applied it to the Jewish Scriptures."
11. "Here the old sources disagree." SUMMARY HOLDS. Eusebius (E17) and Jerome (NP3, D1/D2) give different orders, as the next sentences show.
12. "Eusebius says Demetrius ... had already written to bishops everywhere that his castration as a youth had been most foolish." HELD. EUS 33217-33233 (VI.8.4): "wrote of his deed as most foolish to the bishops throughout the world ... save this deed of his boyhood."
13. "At Caesarea two brothers came to study with him, and he found them keen on Greek and Roman learning." HELD. EUS 36569: "pupils came to him not only from the vicinity, but also from other countries"; 36679: "Finding them deeply interested in Greek and Roman learning." This is the outline's own wording (Beat 6, Do not say).
14. "The emperor Decius, Eusebius says, persecuted the churches out of hatred for Philip, the emperor before him." HELD. EUS 37383, 37413 (VI.39.1): "Philip was succeeded by Decius ... On account of his hatred of Philip, he commenced a persecution of the churches."
15. "Pamphilus of Caesarea wrote a Defense of him, answering from Origen's own words those who said his books differed from the church's teaching." HELD. `pamphilus_apologia-pro-origene-rufinus-lat_lommatzsch1846.txt` 14630-14706: "omnium, qui ei obtrectant, confutare calumnias ... non nostris verbis ... sed ex suis propriis vocibus ... hos enim asserunt maxime a praedicatione ecclesiastica discrepare."
16. "In the next century, the church historian Socrates says, Basil of Caesarea and Gregory of Nazianzus got his books and drew from them the right reading of Scripture." **SOFTENED OR CHANGED. BLOCKING.** Socrates's account holds (N2 13913-13922: "they procured Origen's works, and drew from them the right interpretation of the sacred Scriptures"). The time phrase does not. In v1, "In the next century" came after Origen's death and meant the century after his. The revision now puts it straight after Pamphilus's Defense, written early in the 300s. Heard in that order, "the next century" means the century after Pamphilus, the 400s. But Basil and Gregory did this in the same century as Pamphilus. **Change:** "In the century after Origen's death, the church historian Socrates says, Basil of Caesarea and Gregory of Nazianzus got his books and drew from them the right reading of Scripture." (Or "Later in that century, ...".)
17. "Gregory also sent a friend the Philocalia of Origen, a book of passages chosen from his writings for students." HELD. N7 47445-47451 (Ep. 115): "we send you the volume of the Philocalia of Origen, containing a selection of passages useful to students of literature."
18. "About three centuries after his death, a council of bishops at Constantinople named him, with his writings, among the heretics." HELD. N14 23852-23860 (Anathema XI): "If anyone does not anathematize Arius ... and Origen, as well as their impious writings ... let him be anathema." The word "heretics" is now explained at sentence 7, so dropping the gloss here loses nothing. The time order follows the outline (Ending 1: Defense, Basil and Gregory, Philocalia, council last).
