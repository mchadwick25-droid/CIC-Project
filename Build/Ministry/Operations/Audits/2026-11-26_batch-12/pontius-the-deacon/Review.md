## Where the readers disagree

- **Sentence 8, "On their first day at Curubis, Cyprian told them his dream."** Trust: HELD (VE 28055, "on that day whereon we first abode"). Trust_Second: SOFTENED OR CHANGED, the date moved from the vision to the telling. Judged BLOCKING. In VL 41012–41016 the day stands in Pontius's frame around "apparuit mihi, inquit", and Pontius's own year-to-the-day reckoning fixes it to the day the sentence was *shown* ("quo hoc illi ante annum fuerat ostensum", VL 41082–41083). No line says when Cyprian told it. The brief puts the date on the telling, which is the third of Part 1's four faults, and it leaves the story's later "the very day ... on which his sentence had been shown him" resting on a different day from the one the listener heard. It is also the one live-text echo the outline does not hold. Under the reviewer prompt, a finding either trust read makes stays BLOCKING until fixed or a third reader shows from the source that it holds; the fix costs three words.
- **Sentence 25, the list of works "made about a hundred years after his death".** Trust: MODERN ONLY. Trust_Second: SUMMARY HOLDS, while saying "a reader who requires the list itself would call it MODERN ONLY". Judged BLOCKING. The list's own text is not held in `cic/texts/` (Trust searched for "Cheltenh", "indiculum" and "Vita Cypriani"; the VSB reprint's table is lost in the OCR). Both what the list holds and its date rest only on Harnack and von Soden (HAR 259–262; VSB 4159–4163), and the date 359 is a modern reckoning (VSB 3319–3320, "wie Mommsen, Sanday und Turner gezeigt haben"). Part 1 lets a modern work clarify an edition, not stand as the witness for a claim about the record; a later witness may show what came of a subject only where that witness is held. The outline's "Widely Accepted" does not change that, and it notes itself that "the list's own text is not held".

## BLOCKING findings, each with its exact change

### B1. Sentence 8: the day is put on the telling, not the vision (Trust_Second; judged above).

Evidence. Trust_Second: "The first day dates the vision ('apparuit'), not the telling ... The source does not say when Cyprian told it." It also finds the wording echoes the live text ("On their first day there, Cyprian told them a dream"), where the outline has "On the day they settled at Curubis, Cyprian told them, before he fell asleep ...".

This also eases the First-time listener's MINOR 3 ("I heard 'dream' and then 'before he fell asleep' and was confused"): the dream is now placed on the day, so "before he fell asleep" reads as that night. Cost: +3 words.

### B2. Sentences 16–17: the last withdrawal (Trust and Trust_Second, SOFTENED OR CHANGED; First-time and Skeptic, BLOCKING failing sentence). Needs the outline.

Evidence, merged:
- Trust and Trust_Second, the same three faults, from EP 17370–17375 ("Cum perlatum ad nos fuisset ... commentarios esse missos qui me Vticam perducerent et consilio carissimorum persuasum esset ut de hortis nostris interim secederem ... eo quod congruat episcopum in ea ciuitate in qua ecclesiae dominicae praeest illic Dominum confiteri"): (1) the letter says news came that officers *had been sent*, not that they came; (2) "on their advice" is heard as the officers'; the letter has his dearest friends advise him; (3) "to be tried" drops his own reason, that a bishop should confess the Lord in his own city.
- First-time: "the last place I heard was Curubis; now he has 'gardens' ... I was never told he came back from exile"; "'their' sounds like the officers'"; "he refused places to go, then he left. I am not told these are two witnesses giving different sides."
- Skeptic: "Nothing says Cyprian came back from exile, so 'his gardens' and 'another city' have no setting"; "'On their advice' reads as the officers' advice, which makes no sense"; "no signal that two witnesses tell it differently."

Change. Replace the two sentences "Pontius says Cyprian later turned down friends' offers of places to go. Cyprian's own last letter says when officers came to take him to another city, he left his gardens for a while, on their advice, to be tried in the city where he led the church." with:

"Back from Curubis, Cyprian stayed at his gardens. Pontius says he turned down friends' offers of places to go. But Cyprian's own last letter says that when he heard officers had been sent to take him to another city, his dearest friends persuaded him to leave for a while. He agreed, he wrote, because a bishop should confess his faith in the city where he leads the church."

- The return rests on the court record, "back at his gardens after Curubis" (outline Beat 3; KNOPF 4855–4857, "de ciuitate Curubitana ... regressus esset ... in suis hortis manebat"). It is told in the narrator's voice because the old sources agree: Pontius too has the officer find him "at his gardens" (VE 28163–28166).
- "But" makes the two witnesses audible, as the outline's Beat 4 asks ("Tell both, side by side, credited, and no verdict"), without judging between them.
- "Confess his faith" is Trust's plain sense of *Dominum confiteri*, so a listener in a second language does not hear "confess" as admitting guilt.

Why it needs the outline. The faulty wording is the outline's own: Cut first 21 holds "on their advice ... so that he would be tried in his own city". The return is held only in Beat 3, inside Cut first 20, which sends "the return to his gardens" to the card; Mark's ruling for this batch sends every Cut first item where it is marked. So Cut first 21 is corrected to the wording above, and Cut first 20 is split to keep "Back from Curubis, Cyprian stayed at his gardens" in Beat 4, at the outline, checked, and reported to Mark. Without the return the listener is lost on where Cyprian is, which the bar blocks. Cost: +22 words (46 → 68). Longest new sentence: 30 words.

Cost: +1 word.
