## Each non-HELD sentence

1. **"In 257 the governor at Carthage sent the city's bishop, Cyprian, into exile at Curubis, a small town on the coast, because he would not take part in the ceremonies of Rome's gods."** SUMMARY HOLDS. The emperors ordered "eos, qui Romanam religionem non colunt, debere Romanas caeremonias recognoscere"; Cyprian answers "nullos alios deos noui"; then "exul ad urbem Curubitanam proficisci? ... Proficiscor" (KNOPF 4823–4836, 4829). The year comes from the consuls, "Valeriano quartum et Gallieno tertium" (KNOPF 4823), Widely Accepted. The cause is the hearing's own order of events. "On the coast" is a modern note that only places the town (Kit Part 1 allows that use). No change.

2. **"Beyond that, almost all we know of him is his own book, the work of a man who loved and praised his bishop."** SUMMARY HOLDS. No other old source on him is held beyond Jerome; the late calendars come only through AASS (Source_Sheet E12). Praise runs through the whole book, and the love shows in its close: "plus tamen doleo quod remansi" (VL 41409–41410). A fair judgment of the witness. No change.

3. **"Pontius went into exile by choice, heard Cyprian's dream there, and was with him on his last night."** SUMMARY HOLDS. "exulem uoluntarium" (VL 41013–41015); "'eo enim die quo iu [in] exilii ioco [loco] mansimus' ... 'apparuit mihi' iuquit [inquit]" (VL 41012–41016), so Cyprian told it in the author's presence; "conuiuae eius et cari in coutubernio ex more fuerimus" (VL 41246–41248). That the author is Pontius is Jerome's claim, credited in the sentence before. No change.

4. **"Cyprian told them of a dream he had on their first day at Curubis."** SUMMARY HOLDS. "eo enim die quo in exilii loco mansimus" (VL 41012); VE "on that day whereon we first abode in the place of banishment" (VE 28055). "Told them" compresses "inquit". Note, not a finding: the text calls it a "uisio" (VL 41081, "post uisionem") seen "nondum somni quiete sopito", before sleep. The next sentence says so ("Before he fell asleep"), so the meaning holds. No change.

5. **"Before he fell asleep, he said, a young man of more than human size seemed to lead him to the governor's court, where the governor wrote his judgment on his tablet."** SUMMARY HOLDS. "nondum somni quiete sopito iuuenis ultra modum hominis enormis: qui cum me quasi ad praetorium duceret, uidebar mihi tribunali sedentis proconsulis admoueri ... adnotare statim coepit in tabula sententiam" (VL 41016–41020). "Seemed" stands for "quasi" and "uidebar mihi". No change.

6. **"But Cyprian's own last letter says that when he heard officers had been sent to take him to another city, his dearest friends persuaded him to leave for a while."** SUMMARY HOLDS. "commentarios esse missos qui me Vticam perducerent et consilio carissimorum persuasum esset ut de hortis nostris interim secederem" (EP 17370–17372). The letter waits in hiding for the governor's return to Carthage, "expectamus ergo hic in secessu abdito" (EP 17384), so it is the last letter we have; it is the last in Hartel's series. Each witness is credited; the narrator does not decide between them. No change.

7. **"The church's forebears, Pontius writes, had honored ordinary believers who died as martyrs and written down their sufferings."** SUMMARY HOLDS. "maiores nostri plebeiis et catecumenis martyrium consecutis tantum honoris ... tribuerint, ut de passionibus eorum multa aut ... paene cuncta conscripserint" (VL 40271–40275). "Ordinary believers" covers both lay members and learners; "many, or nearly all" is compressed. The force (they wrote these down, so a bishop's should not be lost) is intact. No change.

8. **"It would be hard if the death of so great a bishop were passed over, and his deeds in life stayed hidden."** SUMMARY HOLDS. "certe durum erat ... Cypriani tanti sacerdotis et tanti martyris passio praeteriretur, qui et sine martyrio habuit quae doceret, et quae dum uixit gesserit non paterent" (VL 40271–40310). "Sacerdos" here is the bishop. "Passio" given as "death" fits, since the next quote and paragraph 2 tell it as suffering and death. No change.

9. **"The book was copied with Cyprian's own writings."** SUMMARY HOLDS. The evidence is the manuscripts and the list of 359, reported by the editors: "Die ‚Vita Cypriani' des Pontius findet sich bereits in dem Cheltenhamer Verzeichnis vom J. 359 unter den Werken Cyprians" (HAR 260–261); "die auch in vielen Hss. der Sammlung von Werken Cyprians beigegebene Vita" (VSB 4159–4161, as the source sheet gives it). It is a fact about how the text came down, which a modern work may clarify (Part 1, "its edition"). It is not a modern opinion, and the sheet rates it Widely Accepted. No change.

## HELD, in brief

- P1: "a willing exile" (VL 41013–41015); the wish to share the martyrdom, "quod utinam et in passione licuisset" (VL 41015).
- P2: no name in the book (VE 27662–28303 has "Pontius" only in the editors' heading and note); Jerome: "Pontius, diaconus Cypriani ... egregium volumen vite et passionis Cypriani reliquit" (JL 57618–57622), about 392 (JE 38785). "So that later readers would have his example": "ut ad posteros quoque nostros incomparabile et grande documentum" (VL 40268–40269).
- P3: the young man behind the governor reads it and shows it by a sign (VL 41022–41025). The quote matches the Latin clause for clause: "manu enim expansa et complanata ad spatae modum ictum solitae animaduersionis imitatus, quod uolebat intellegi, ad instar liquidi sermonis expressit. intellexi sententiam passionis futuram" (VL 41025–41028; HAR 1310–1313, same).
- P4: delay of one day granted (VL 41029–41040; VE 28075–28084); "hic dies unus significabat annum" (VL 41080).
- P5: the gardens, "in suis hortis manebat" (KNOPF 4856); the friends' places refused (VE 28142–28149); "congruat episcopum in ea ciuitate in qua ecclesiae dominicae praeest illic Dominum confiteri" (EP 17373–17374); the night "in domo principis" with "conuiuae eius" (VL 41246–41248); "altera die ... inimicum te diis Romanis ... gladio animadverti placet" (KNOPF 4882, 4902–4910); 14 September, "die octaua decima kalendarum Octobrium" (KNOPF 4955–4956), with 258 from "Tusco et Basso consulibus" (KNOPF 4857); "eo die post exactum annum coronatus est, quo hoc illi ante annum fuerat ostensum", credited to Pontius (VL 41082–41083).
- P6 quote: "placuit summatim pauca conscribere, non quo aliquem uel gentilium lateat tanti uiri uita, sed ut ad posteros quoque nostros incomparabile et grande documentum in immortalem memoriam porrigatur" (VL 40266–40270).
- P7: "homiuis [hominis] Dei facta non debent aliunde numerari, nisi ex quo Deo natus est ... tamen illa praetereo" (VL 40328–40331); "quid sacerdos Dei proconsule interrogante responderit, sunt acta quae referant" (VL 40900–40901), at the exile hearing (VE 28000–28004).
- P8: "egregium volumen" (JL 57621–57622).
- P9: the book's last lines (VE 28294–28303; VL 41400–41410; HAR 1806–1813, same words). "In hac me fuisse sententia" is given as "this was how I felt", which keeps both VE's reading and Harnack's colon.

## The heart

The heart is right for the subject. On the sources, Pontius is the deacon who went into exile with Cyprian by choice, heard the dream and shared the last night, and then wrote the *Life* so a bishop's whole life would be kept for later readers. Jerome names him; the book does not. It is told by the end of paragraph 2 (word 166). The who, the exile, the dream, the last night and the book all come within about 150 words, and the reason lands in the closing clause. That is within the first minute. The impact (copied with Cyprian's writings, Jerome's praise) comes later, as "what came of him". Not late.
