## 1. Setting

**S1. Time.** The first hearing and the sentence of exile: "Imperatore Valeriano quartum et Gallieno tertium consulibus tertio kalendarum Septembrium Carthagine in secretario Paternus proconsul Cypriano episcopo dixit" (KNOPF 4823–4825), 30 August, the year from the consuls: 257, Widely Accepted. The death: "Passus est autem beatissimus Cyprianus martyr die octaua decima kalendarum Octobrium sub Valeriano et Gallieno imperatoribus" (KNOPF 4955–4956), 14 September; the arrest is dated "idibus Septembris Tusco et Basso consulibus" (KNOPF 4857), the year 258, Widely Accepted. The book: after the death, for hearers who knew Cyprian's voice: "accedit ad cumulum, quod et uos de eo multum aut si fieri potest totum desideratis audire, concupiscentes ardore flagranti uel facta eius [cognoscere], etsi interim uiua uerba tacuerunt" (VL 40315–40319; HAR 511–514); VE "you also are anxious to hear very much, or if it be possible every thing, about him, longing with eager warmth at least to become acquainted with his deeds, although now his living words are silent" (VE 27692–27695). OWN. Documented that it was written after the death for such hearers. The year 259 is Harnack's "herrschende Ansicht" (HAR 379–381): Dominant Modern Reconstruction; Martin argued for a later date (SHK 31633–31634). Plain words: "after Cyprian's death, for people who had heard him preach". No year for the book.

**S2. Place.** Carthage, in today's Tunisia (CEN `modernCountries`). Curubis, the place of exile: "exul ad urbem Curubitanam proficisci? Cyprianus episcopus dixit: Proficiscor" (KNOPF 4835–4836); Pontius: "non exulem tantummodo Curubis sed et martyrem possideret" (VL 41011–41012). CONTEMP and OWN. Documented. "sie lag in Africa procons. (Zeugitana)" (HAR 1319–1320, modern, clarifies). Benson calls it a "little coast town, about fifty miles from Carthage" (BEN 27288–27290; modern, clarifies the place). Plain words: "Curubis, a small town on the coast, away from Carthage". No mileage in the story.

**S3. Tradition.** CEN `toldWithin`: `latin-pastoral-congregational-christianity`; its `informalName` is "The Latin Pastoral Congregations"; its `shortDescription`: "In Carthage and Hippo, in Roman Africa, Latin-speaking Christians gathered around their city's bishop." Plain words: "the Latin-speaking church of Carthage, in Roman Africa".

## 2. Events

**E1. Who he was, and who says so.** The *Life* never gives its author's name or office. Harnack: "In keiner der uns erhaltenen Handschriften ist m. W. der Name des Pontius als Verfasser verzeichnet und auch im Cheltenh. fehlt er" (HAR 331–333); "Aus der Schrift selbst ... ließ sich der Name des Pontius so wenig ermitteln wie sein Diakonenamt" (HAR 336–340). Hartel's heading: "Pontio diacono uulgo adscripta" (VL 40260). Jerome, chapter 68: "Pontius, diaconus Cypriani, usque ad diem passionis ejus cum ipso exsilium sustinens, egregium volumen vite et passionis Cypriani reliquit" (JL 57618–57622); English: "Pontius, deacon of Cyprian, sharing his exile until the day of his death, left a notable volume On the life and death of Cyprian" (JE 40833–40835; "Died about 260" in the same line is Richardson's note, JE-ed, no source given). Jerome's list runs "until the fourteenth year of the Emperor Theodosius" (JE 38785; JE-ed's "a.d. 492" in the note is a misprint, since Theodosius reigned from 379), so about 392. LATER, about 130 years after. Documented as Jerome's statement; that the book is Pontius's, Widely Accepted (one later witness, uncontradicted by any old source). Modern: Harnack, Jerome "folgte hier also einer Tradition, deren Quelle wir nicht kennen, deren Zuverlässigkeit wir aber zu bezweifeln nicht den geringsten Grund haben" (HAR 340–358, claim); Delehaye "Rien ne s'oppose à ce que nous adoptions le nom que lui donne S. Jérôme" (DEL 3908–3909, claim); SHK 31626–31628, most scholars accept Jerome (report). Dessau's identification with a magistrate surnamed Pontius at Curubis, and Corssen's answer (DEL 3936–3943; SHK 31628–31630): modern claims, card only. Plain words: "Pontius, as Jerome names him, one of Cyprian's deacons; the book itself never gives his name".

**E2. A willing exile.** "nam et me inter domesticos comites dignatio caritatis eius delegerat exulem uoluntarium, quod utinam et iu passione licuisset" (VL 41013–41015; HAR 1298–1300, same; AASS 196751–196754, same, with "quo primum"); VE "for the condescension of his love had chosen me among his household companions to a voluntary exile: would that he could also have chosen me to share his passion!" (VE 28056–28059). OWN. Documented as his own claim. Plain sense: Cyprian, out of love, chose him among the members of his household to go into exile with him by choice, and Pontius wishes it had been allowed him to share the martyrdom too. Jerome confirms the shared exile "until the day of his passion" (JL 57618–57621). Monceaux reads the same passage as proof the author was there (MON 10987–10990; modern, agrees). Reitzenstein, as SHK reports him, "hält ... die Augenzeugenschaft für fingiert" (SHK 31630–31631); Delehaye answers "Un faussaire n'aurait pas joué son rôle avec cette discrétion" (DEL 4268–4269). Both modern claims; card only. **Explain it (the exile), each side:** the Roman side, in the *Acts*: the emperors had written "eos, qui Romanam religionem non colunt, debere Romanas caeremonias recognoscere" (KNOPF 4826–4827), those who do not keep the Roman religion must take part in the Roman rites; Cyprian would not, and the governor asked "Poteris ergo secundum praeceptum Valeriani et Gallieni exul ad urbem Curubitanam proficisci?" and he said "Proficiscor" (KNOPF 4835–4836), "I go." CONTEMP. Documented. Pontius's side: "His tam bonis et tam piis actibus superuenit exilium. hanc enim uicem semper repraesentat impietas, ut melioribus peiora restituat" (VL 40897–40900); VE "Banishment followed these actions, so good and so benevolent. For impiety always makes this return, that it repays the better with the worse" (VE 28000–28002). OWN; a judgment, in his voice.

**E3. The place.** He first imagines the worst place, "filthy in situation, squalid in appearance, having no wholesome water ... vast wooded rocks ... a totally deserted solitude" (VE 28032–28035), then says God gave Cyprian "a sunny and suitable spot, a dwelling, secret as he wished ... the number of the brethren who visited him, and then the kindness of the citizens themselves, which supplied to him everything whereof he appeared to be deprived" (VE 28046–28051; VL 40972–40976 "aprifcum et conpetentem locum, hospitium pro uoluntate secretum ... frequentiam uisitantium fratrum et ipsorum inde ciuium caritatem"). OWN. Documented as his account. His thought on exile: "illis extra ciuitatem suam uiuere grauis poeua est: Christiano totus hic mundus una domus est" (VL 40911–40913; HAR 1195, same); VE "To them, it is a severe punishment to live outside their own city; to the Christian, the whole of this world is one home" (VE 28014–28016). OWN. Documented. Benson thinks such lines "fragments of his conversation" (BEN 27397–27402; modern claim, kept out). Benson's "devoted grandiloquent deacon ... many affected lines" (BEN 27384–27385): modern judgment, card only.

**E4. The dream at Curubis.** "'eo enim die quo iu exilii ioco mansimus' ... 'apparuit mihi' iuquit 'nondum somni quiete sopito iuueuis ultra modum hominis inormis: qui cum me quasi ad praetorium duceret, uidebar mihi tribunali sedentis proconsulis admoueri. is ut in me respexit, aduotare statim coepit iu tabula seutentiam quam non sciebam, nihil enim de me solita iuterrogatione quaesierat. sed enim iuuenis qui a tergo eius stabat admodum curiosus legit quidquid fuerat adnotatum. et quia inde uerbis proferre non poterat, nutu declaraute moustrauit quid iu litteris tabulae illius ageretur. mauu enim expansa et complanata ad spatae modum ictum solitae auimaduersiouis imitatus, quod uolebat iutellegi, ad iustar liquidi sermouis expressit. iutellexi sententiam passionis futuram. rogare coepi et petere coutiuuo, ut dilatio mihi uel unius diei prorogaretur, donec res meas legitima ordinatione dispouerem. et cum preces frequenter iterassem, rursus in tabula coeperat nescio quid adnotare. sensi tamen de uultus sereuitate iudicis mentem quasi iusta petitione commotam. sed et ille iuuenis ... chmdestino identidem nutu coucessam dilationem, quae iu crastinum petebatur, coutortis post inuicem digitis significare properauit. ego quamuis noii esset lecta sententia, et sic de gaudio dilationis acceptae laeto admodum corde resipisco : metu tamen interpretationis incertae sic tremebam, ut reliquiae formidinis cor exultans adhuc toto pauore pulsarent" (VL 41012–41073; HAR 1297–1354, same but "etsi de gaudio" at 1351, Harnack following the manuscripts, 1375). VE 28055–28089 (the English, quoted in full in VE: "a young man of unusual stature ... For, with hand expanded and flattened like a blade, he imitated the stroke of the accustomed punishment, and expressed what he wished to be understood as clearly as by speech,—I understood the future sentence of my passion ... twisting his fingers one behind the other ... yet trembled so with fear of the uncertainty of the interpretation"). OWN-reported: Cyprian's words, "inquit", as Pontius gives them, told on the first day at Curubis ("on that day whereon we first abode", VE 28055). Documented as Pontius's report of what Cyprian told them. A wonder: it stays in Cyprian's and Pontius's voice. The young man is not called an angel in the text; Harnack: "hier ist's ein Engel" (HAR 1327; modern reading, kept out). Harnack also sees no reason to doubt the dream was dreamt (HAR 2867–2868; modern claim, kept out). **Explain it:** *sententia* is the governor's written sentence; *solita animaduersio* is the usual punishment, here the sword stroke of execution; *passio* is the martyr's suffering and death, so "passion" must not stand bare. Plain sense of the delay: Cyprian asked for one more day to put his affairs in order, and the young man signalled that it was granted till the next day.

**E5. How Pontius read it.** "hic dies unus significabat annum, quo ille post uisionem acturus in saeculo fuerat. nam, ut manifestius dicam, eo die post exactum annum coronatus est, quo hoc illi ante annum fuerat ostensum" (VL 41080–41083; HAR 1362–1365); VE "This one day signified a year, which he was about to pass in the world after his vision. For, to speak more plainly, after the year was expired, he was crowned, on that day on which, at the commencement of the year, the fact had been announced to him" (VE 28098–28102). "nemo cognouit quare hoc ostensum fuisset, nisi post eodem die quo id uiderat coronatus est" (VL 41090–41092); VE "no one knew why this had been shown to him, until afterwards, when, on the very day on which he had seen it, he was crowned" (VE 28112–28114). On the reason for the delay: "quae uero res illi aut quae uoluntas ordinanda nisi ecclesiastici status? summa accepta dilatio est, ut quidquid circa pauperum fouendorum suprema iudicia disponendum fuerat ordinaretur" (HAR 1408–1411); VE "Yet what affairs or what will had he to arrange, except ecclesiastical concerns? And thus that last delay was received, in order that whatever had to be disposed of by his final decision concerning the care of cherishing the poor might be arranged" (VE 28125–28129). OWN, Pontius's interpretation. Documented as his reading. **Check:** the *Acts* give the hearing as 30 August 257 and the death as 14 September 258 (S1); the day Cyprian reached Curubis is given by no old source. "A year later, on the very day" is Pontius's claim, never the narrator's. Benson's "on September 14 reached Curubis" (BEN 27287–27288) rests on Pontius's own claim (his note, BEN 27352–27355): circular, kept out. Harnack: "Es folgt die Auslegung des Pontius, die nicht überzeugend ist. — quid manifestius? Das war nicht Cyprians Meinung" (HAR 1379–1380); "man muß wohl an seine Privatangelegenheiten denken, aber das war dem Pontius unangenehm" (HAR 1335–1337). Modern claims: card only. **A second old witness:** the *Acts* say that after Curubis Cyprian stayed at his gardens "et inde cotidie sperabat, ueniri ad se, sicut illi ostensum fuerat" (KNOPF 4856–4857; GEB 6558–6559, same), "and from there he expected every day that they would come for him, as had been shown to him." CONTEMP. Documented. It confirms that a revelation was spoken of; it gives no detail of it.

**E6. The year, and the last days.** "medio nihilo minus tempore imminens passio pro certo omnibus sciebatur" (VL 41092–41093), "in the meantime, his impending suffering was certainly known by all" (VE 28114–28115). News from Rome: "Now also a messenger came to him from the city from Xistus, the good and peace-making priest, and on that account most blessed martyr" (VE 28137–28139). Friends: "there assembled to him many eminent people, and people of most illustrious rank and family ... who, on account of ancient friendship with him, repeatedly urged his withdrawal; and ... they also offered places to which he might retire. But he had now set the world aside, having his mind suspended upon heaven, and did not consent to their tempting persuasions" (VE 28142–28149; VL 41184–41185 "nec suadelis blandientibus aduuebat"). OWN. Documented as Pontius's account. **Against it, Cyprian's own last letter:** "commentarios esse missos qui me Vticam perducerent et consilio carissimorum persuasum esset ut de hortis nostris interim secederem, iusta interueniente causa consensi, eo quod congruat episcopum in ea ciuitate in qua ecclesiae dominicae praeest illic Dominum confiteri et plebem uniuersam praepositi praesentis confessione clarificare" (EP 17370–17375), that is: officers had been sent to take him to Utica, and his dearest friends persuaded him to leave his gardens for a while; he agreed, for a just cause, since a bishop should confess the Lord in the city where he leads the church. "expectamus ergo hic in secessu abdito" (EP 17383–17384), "so we wait here in a hidden retreat" for the governor's return to Carthage. CONTEMP, his own words. Documented. Credit each; the narrator does not decide. Rigault (17th century) faulted the *Life* for leaving this hiding out (AASS 196862–196866, "Atque hoc praecipue queritur omissum ... se ... tantisper latuisse"), and the Bollandists answered him (AASS 196866–196870): modern, card only. The arrest and the last night: "receptum eum tamen et in domo principis constitutum una nocte continuit custodia delicata, ita ut conuiuae eius et cari in coutubernio ex more fuerimus" (VL 41246–41248); VE "A gentle custody, however, had him in charge when taken and placed for one night in the officer's house; so that we, his associates and friends, were as usual in his company" (VE 28181–28184). OWN. Documented. *Conuiuae* are table companions. The morning: a soldier who had once been a Christian offered dry clothes, and Cyprian answered "'medellas adhibemus querelis, quae hodie forsitan non erunt'" (VL 41306–41307); VE "We apply medicines to annoyances which probably to-day will no longer exist" (VE 28224–28226). OWN-reported. Documented. Card. Delehaye notes it is the only saying of Cyprian's, besides the dream, that the companion records (DEL 4259–4263; modern, a fair reading but kept out).

**E7. The trial and death, as Pontius tells them.** "subito proconsuli nuntiatus est. producitur admouetur interrogatur de suo nomine : se esse respondit. et hactenus uerba" (VL 41309–41310); VE "He was suddenly announced to the proconsul; he is brought forward; he is placed before him; he is interrogated as to his name. He answers who he is, and nothing more" (VE 28227–28230). The sentence as Pontius gives it: "in qua dictus est sectae signifer et inimicus deorum, et qui suis futurus esset ipse documento et quod sanguine eius inciperet disciplina sanciri" (VL 41315–41317); VE "wherein he was called a standard-bearer of the sect, and an enemy of the gods, and one who was to be an example to his people; and that with his blood discipline would begin to be established" (VE 28234–28237). He reads it as prophecy: "omnia quippe quae dicta sunt, licet a gentili dicta, diuina sunt" (VL 41318–41319). OWN. Documented as his account. **Explain it (the sentence), the Roman side:** the *Acts*: "Diu sacrilega mente uixisti et plurimos nefariae tibi conspirationis homines adgregasti et inimicum te diis Romanis et religionibus sacris constituisti ... eris ipse documento his, quos scelere tuo tecum adgregasti: sanguine tuo sancietur disciplina ... Thascium Cyprianum gladio animadverti placet" (KNOPF 4902–4910). CONTEMP. Documented. The death in a few lines (VE 28251–28266). The death date (S1). **Here a clause only:** the trial, the field and the death are the sibling's.

**E8. Why he wrote.** "tamen quia operibus eius ac meritis etiam haec praerogatiua debetur, placuit summatim pauca conscribere, non quo aliquem uel gentilium lateat tanti uiri uita, sed ut ad posteros quoque nostros incomparabile et grande documentum in immortalem memoriam porrigatur" (VL 40266–40270; HAR 458–462, "non quod [quo?]"); VE "since to his works and deserts it is justly due that his example should be recorded in writing, I have thought it well to prepare this brief and compendious narrative. Not that the life of so great a man can be unknown to any even of the heathen nations, but that to our posterity also this incomparable and lofty pattern may be prolonged into immortal remembrance" (VE 27672–27677). VL then reads "et ut ad exemplum sui litteris dirigantur" (40270–40271), words Harnack strikes because the manuscripts omit them or place them differently (HAR 447–451). OWN. Documented. Then his reason: "certe durum erat, ut cum maiores nostri plebeiis et catecumenis martyrium consecutis tantum honoris pro martyrii ipsius ueneratione tribuerint, ut de passionibus eorum multa aut ut prope dixerim paene cuncta conscripserint, utique ut ad nostram quoque notitiam qui nondum nati fuimus peruenirent, Cypriani tanti sacerdotis et tanti martyris passio praeteriretur, qui et sine martyrio habuit quae doceret, et quae dum uixit gesserit non paterent" (VL 40271–40310; HAR 462–505); VE 27677–27685 ("It would assuredly be hard that, when our fathers have given such honour even to lay-people and catechumens who have obtained martyrdom ... as to record many, or I had nearly said, well nigh all, of the circumstances of their sufferings ... the passion of such a priest and such a martyr as Cyprian should be passed over, who, independently of his martyrdom, had much to teach, and that what he did while he lived should be hidden from the world"). OWN. Documented. **Explain it:** *catecumeni* are people still learning the faith before baptism; *plebeii* are ordinary church members, not clergy (HAR 473–477, modern, clarifies). Which accounts he meant: "Gemeint sind in erster Linie die so ausführlichen Acta Perpetuae et Felicitatis" (HAR 487–489); Delehaye "il fait allusion à la passio Perpetuae" (DEL 4001–4002). Modern reading, kept out of the story; say only "accounts of martyrs". The pressure of his hearers (S1, VE 27692–27700: "I am sorely pressed on both sides, since he burdens me with his virtues, and you press me hard with your entreaties"). OWN. Documented.

**E9. What he chose to tell.** Begin at the new birth: "Vnde igitur iucipiam? unde exordium bonorum eius adgrediar, nisi a principio fidei et natiuitate caelesti? siquidem hominis Dei facta non debent aliunde numerari, nisi ex quo Deo natus est. fuerint licet studia et bouae artes deuotum pectus imbuerint, tamen illa praetereo: nondum enim ad utilitatem nisi saeculi pertinebant" (VL 40326–40332; HAR 518–557); VE "the doings of a man of God should not be reckoned from any point except from the time that he was born of God. He may have had pursuits previously, and liberal arts may have imbued his mind while engaged therein; but these things I pass over; for as yet they had nothing to do with anything but his secular advantage" (VE 27703–27708). OWN. Documented. His sources: "si quibus eius interfui, si qua de antiquioribus comperi, dicam" (VL 40333–40364; HAR 558–559; AASS 196801–196803 reads "de antiquioribus ejus operibus"); VE "if I was with him in any of his doings, if I have discerned any of his more illustrious labours, I will speak of them" (VE 27710–27712), from the AASS-type reading. The text differs; paraphrase only: "what he was there for, and what he learned of earlier things". Harnack's "Pontius war also Augenzeuge der Taten Cyprians, aber nicht von Anfang an; wir müssen ihn uns als jüngeren Mann vorstellen, den Cyprian nach seinem ersten Exil zum Diakon geweiht ... hat" (HAR 589–592): modern inference, kept out. What he tells, in order: chastity and the sale of his estates for the poor as a learner (VE 27715–27738); the people's choice of him as bishop while still new (VE 27817–27849; the sibling tells the crowd at the door); his bearing as bishop (VE 27850–27871); his withdrawal under Decius, defended as obedience to God and not fear: "Do you wish to be assured that the cause of his withdrawal was not fear? to allege nothing else, he did suffer subsequently" (VE 27905–27908; VL 40727–40733); the plague, when "All were shuddering, fleeing, shunning the contagion, impiously exposing their own friends" (VE 27943–27944) and he taught that "there was nothing wonderful in our cherishing our own people only" (VE 27961–27962), so that good was done "to all men, not to those only who are of the household of faith" (VE 27991–27992; VL 40854–40855 "quod bonum est ad omnes, non ad solos domesticos fidei"); then the exile, the dream, the last days and the death. OWN. Documented. What he says he leaves: "Multa alia et quidem magna, quae temperandi uoluminis ratio non patitur prolixo opere sermonis iterari" (VL 40844–40845); VE "I omit many other matters, and, indeed, many important ones, which the necessity of a limited space does not permit to be detailed in more lengthened discourse" (VE 27976–27978). For the first hearing: "et quid sacerdos Dei proconsule interrogante responderit, sunt acta quae referant" (VL 40900–40901); VE "And what God's priest replied to the interrogation of the proconsul, there are Acts which relate" (VE 28002–28004). OWN. Documented. **Explain it:** *acta* are the written record of the court hearing; never "the Acts" bare. Harnack's count of the book's wonders (none but the dream, HAR 2864–2868; "miraculum" only of virtue, HAR 2878–2889): checkable in the text, but Harnack's claim; kept out. That ch. 7 lists Cyprian's treatises in disguise (VSB 4279–4310): modern identification, card only.

**E10. What he does not tell.** The book has nothing on Cyprian's councils, his letters with Rome or the baptism quarrel. Delehaye: "Non moins étrange le silence qu'il garde au sujet de certains épisodes importants de la vie de l'évêque ; son action dans les conciles, ses relations avec Rome" (DEL 4168–4175); Monceaux: "l'on n'y trouve pas un mot sur la question du baptême des hérétiques, ni sur le rôle de Cyprien dans les conciles de Carthage ... ce qu'il veut peindre, c'est moins le politique, le chef d'Eglise, que le chrétien, le clerc accompli" (MON 11093–11102); Harnack: "in diesen Vorgängen lagen keine ‚opera ac merita' im Sinn des Verfassers" (HAR 2089–2093). The absence is checkable in VE 27657–28303; the reasons are modern claims. He also does not tell Cyprian's return from Curubis to his gardens (HAR 2871–2876; the *Acts* tell it, KNOPF 4850–4856). Monceaux faults him for setting the exile straight after the plague, "l'intervalle est de quatre ans au moins" (MON 11129–11131); Harnack answers (HAR 2041–2045). All card only. Leave out of the story: it would need the baptism quarrel explained, which the sibling tells, and a sentence on what is missing.

**E11. His grief at being left behind.** "quid hoc loco faciam? inter gaudium passionis et remanendi dolorem in partes diuisus animus et angustum nimis pectus affectus duplices onerant. dolebo quod non comes fuerim? sed illius uictoria triumphanda est. de uictoria triumphabo ? sed doleo quod comes non sim. uerum uobis tamen et simpliciter confitendum est, quod et uos scitis, in hac me fuisse sententia. multum ac nimis multum de gloria eius exulto, plus tamen doleo quod remansi" (VL 41381–41410; HAR 1806–1813, colon after *sententia*; AASS 196780–196790, same words). VE "What shall I do now? Between joy at his passion, and grief at still remaining, my mind is divided in different directions, and twofold affections are burdening a heart too limited for them. Shall I grieve that I was not his associate? But yet I must triumph in his victory. Shall I triumph at his victory? Still I grieve that I am not his companion. Yet still to you I must in simplicity confess, what you also are aware of, that it was my intention to be his companion. Much and excessively I exult at his glory; but still more do I grieve that I remained behind" (VE 28294–28303). OWN. Documented. Three prints agree. **The one doubtful clause:** *in hac me fuisse sententia*, "that I was of this mind". VE reads it as "it was my intention to be his companion"; Harnack's colon points it forward to the joy and the grief. Render it so that it holds both. It answers his aside at Curubis (E2). Harnack: "Der Schluß ist im Empfindungsausdruck frostig ... aber er kann deshalb doch wahrhaftig sein" (HAR 1839–1841): modern judgment, kept out.

**E12. His own end: not known.** No early source says how or when Pontius died. JE-ed "Died about 260" (JE 40833, no source). VE-ed: "Pontius is said to have followed his beloved bishop, a.d. 258, dying a martyr" (VE 28060–28061, no source; 258 cannot be right for a man who wrote after the death). The Roman Martyrology, 8 March, as AASS quotes it: "Carthagine S. Pontii, Diaconi B. Cypriani Episcopi, qui usque ad diem mortis illius cum ipso exilium sustinens, vitae et passionis ejus egregium volumen reliquit, atque in suis passionibus Dominum semper glorificans, coronam vitae promeruit" (AASS 196819–196824), after "Usuardus, Ado, Notkerus, et plurima vetera Martyrologia" (AASS 196817–196818). LATE LEGEND: its first part copies Jerome; "in his own sufferings ... earned the crown of life" has no earlier witness held. Martyrdom: Inferential-Thin. The calendars themselves are not held; only AASS's quotation.

## 3. Impact, heaviest first

**I1. The book was kept with Cyprian's own writings.** "Die ‚Vita Cypriani' des Pontius findet sich bereits in dem Cheltenhamer Verzeichnis vom J. 359 unter den Werken Cyprians; sie steht dort am Schluß" (HAR 260–263); von Soden: "Den Schluß von Ch bildet die auch in vielen Hss. der Sammlung von Werken Cyprians beigegebene Vita Cypriani per Pontium" (VSB 4159–4161), the list itself "aus Afrika und aus dem Jahre 359" (VSB 3320–3321). "Überliefert ist uns die Vita nachweislich in 23 Handschriften" (HAR 264); most break off in chapter 15, "weil hier die Acta proconsularia eintreten" (HAR 293–295). The list and the manuscripts are old evidence held only through these modern reports; the list's text is not held. Widely Accepted (two modern works and SHK 31645–31646 agree). Plain words: "copied with Cyprian's own writings; a list of them made about a hundred years after his death already ends with it". Von Soden's view that this was done at Rome (HAR 314–316) and Harnack's that it may have been earlier in Africa (HAR 344–345): card. Number of manuscripts: card.

**I2. Jerome read it and named its author** (E1), about 392. His one sentence is the only old source for the name and the office. LATER. Documented. He calls it "egregium volumen" (JL 57621–57622), "a notable volume". Jerome is on the tree (`jerome`).

**I3. Bede carried Jerome's line on.** Under Valerian: "hac persecutione Cyprianus Carthaginensis episcopus, cuius doctissima extant opuscula, martyrio coronatur: cuius uitae et passionis uolumen egregium dereliquit Pontius diaconus eius, qui usque ad diem passionis eius cum ipso exilium sustinuit" (BEDE 23904–23909; the editor's margin source note beside it is damaged in the scan; the words are Jerome's, JL 57618–57622). LATER (about 725). Documented. Card.

**I4. Kept as a saint.** The calendars keep him at Carthage on 8 March (AASS 196261–196266, 196817–196824; E12). LATE LEGEND, known only through AASS. Documented that they keep him; nothing held says he died a martyr. Card, or one clause credited to "later calendars".

**I5. Read as the record of Cyprian's end.** The *Life* joined to the *Acts* in the manuscripts (I1). A 16th-century reader, Heinrich Bullinger, cited "Pontius, the deacon" for Cyprian's dress at his death (`zurich-letters_first-series-1558-1579_robinson1842.txt` 8646–8648, 17835–17837), though the words he quotes are the *Acts*' (KNOPF 4918–4920): a sign of the joined copies, but the reading is the editor's. Leave out.

**I6. Argued over as a kind of book (modern only).** Harnack: "die älteste christliche Biographie, die wir besitzen" (HAR 1909–1910), which "eine neue Literaturgattung, die christliche Biographie, begründete" (HAR 175–176). Monceaux: "non une véritable biographie, mais un panégyrique" (MON 11030–11032). Corssen: a speech of praise and a defence, which Delehaye thinks "beaucoup exagérée" (DEL 4097–4102). Reitzenstein, as reported: with the deaths of famous men, by a "poor rhetorician" (DEL 4079–4083; SHK 31630–31633). Delehaye: Pontius wanted a book for Cyprian "qui ne fût pas inférieur en importance aux Acta Perpetuae", and fell into biography in the tone of praise (DEL 4312–4321). Rigault: "Plurima Pontius, pio erga suum Episcopum adfectu, Rhetorica libertate commentus est" (AASS 196852–196853). All modern claims: never in the story; card and Modern works list only.

## 5. Where sources disagree or come late

- **D1. The name and office.** Unsigned book (HAR 331–340); Jerome names Pontius, deacon (E1). Tell it so: the book never names him; Jerome does.
- **D2. The last withdrawal.** Pontius: he did not consent to friends' urging (VE 28142–28149). Cyprian, Ep. 81: on friends' advice he left his gardens for a while (EP 17370–17375). Credit each.
- **D3. The dream's meaning.** Pontius: the day meant a year, and the delay was for church business and the poor (E5). The *Acts*: he expected them daily "as had been shown to him" (KNOPF 4856–4857). Harnack's doubt is modern: card.
- **D4. "A year later, to the day."** Pontius's claim; no old source dates the arrival at Curubis (E5).
- **D5. His sources phrase.** "de antiquioribus" (VL, HAR) or "de antiquioribus ejus operibus" (AASS; VE "more illustrious labours"). Paraphrase only.
- **D6. His own end.** Not known (E12); the martyrology's crown is late.
- **D7. Eyewitness or not, the kind of book, whether he knew the *Acts*, the date.** Modern disputes only (I6; DEL 4215–4254; SHK 31630–31641; HAR 1829–1833). Card.
