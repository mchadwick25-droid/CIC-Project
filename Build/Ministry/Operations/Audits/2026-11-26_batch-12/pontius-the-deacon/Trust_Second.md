## Findings (BLOCKING)

**Sentence 8. SOFTENED OR CHANGED: the date is moved from the vision to the telling.**
- Brief: "On their first day at Curubis, Cyprian told them his dream."
- Source: "'eo enim die quo iu exilii ioco mansimus' (nam et me ... ) 'apparuit mihi' iuquit 'nondum somni quiete sopito iuueuis ...'" (VL 41012–41016; VE 28055–28060, "For on that day whereon we first abode in the place of banishment ... 'there appeared to me,' said he"). The first day dates the vision ("apparuit"), not the telling. Pontius's own year-to-the-day claim rests on the day it was *shown*: "eo die ... quo hoc illi ante annum fuerat ostensum" (VL 41082–41083). The source does not say when Cyprian told it. This is Part 1's "a date or detail moved onto the wrong event".
- Change: "Cyprian told them of a vision he had on their first night at Curubis." Or, keeping the outline's word: "On their first day at Curubis, Cyprian said, he had a dream."
- Outline: Beat 2's "On the day they settled at Curubis, Cyprian told them, before he fell asleep ..." left room for this reading. Do not say line: "Cyprian told the dream on the first day" → "on the first day at Curubis, Cyprian said, the young man appeared to him" (VL 41012–41016).

**Sentence 15. SOFTENED OR CHANGED: whose advice, what came, and why.**
- Brief: "Cyprian's own last letter says when officers came to take him to another city, he left his gardens for a while, on their advice, to be tried in the city where he led the church."
- Source: "Cum perlatum ad nos fuisset ... commentarios esse missos qui me Vticam perducerent et consilio carissimorum persuasum esset ut de hortis nostris interim secederem, iusta interueniente causa consensi, eo quod congruat episcopum in ea ciuitate in qua ecclesiae dominicae praeest illic Dominum confiteri" (EP 17370–17375).
- Three changes. (1) "on their advice" follows "officers", so it is heard as the officers' advice; the letter says it was his dearest friends' ("consilio carissimorum"). (2) The letter says it was reported to him that officers had been sent, not that they came. (3) "to be tried" drops the reason he gives: that a bishop should *confess the Lord* in the city where he leads the church.
- Change: "Cyprian's own last letter says that when he heard officers had been sent to take him to another city, his dearest friends persuaded him to leave his gardens for a while, and he agreed, because a bishop should confess the Lord in the city where he leads the church."
- Outline: Cut first 21 ("on their advice ... so that he would be tried in his own city") left room for it. Do not say lines: "on their advice" after the officers → "on his dearest friends' advice" (EP 17371); "to be tried" → "to confess the Lord" (EP 17374–17375).

**Sentence 22. SOFTENED OR CHANGED: "baptism" narrows where Pontius says he begins, and does not match what he then tells.**
- Brief: "He began at Cyprian's baptism, because the deeds of a man of God, he wrote, should be counted only from the day he was born of God."
- Source: "unde exordium bonorum eius adgrediar, nisi a principio fidei et natiuitate caelesti? siquidem homiuis Dei facta non debent aliunde numerari, nisi ex quo Deo natus est" (VL 40326–40329; VE 27703–27706, "except from the beginning of his faith and from his heavenly birth ... from the time that he was born of God"). The first deeds he then tells come before that birth: "While his faith was in its first rudiments ... His second birth had not yet enlightened the new man" (VE 27715–27722), and the estates sold for the poor by a man "of faith yet unskilled" (VE 27731–27741). So "He began at Cyprian's baptism" is not what the book does. "The day" is also not in the source ("ex quo", from when).
- Change: "He began where Cyprian's faith began, because the deeds of a man of God, he wrote, should be counted only from the time he was born of God."
- Outline: Beat 6 ("He began at Cyprian's baptism") and Never cut ("the start at baptism") left room for this. Do not say line: "He began at Cyprian's baptism" → "he began at the beginning of Cyprian's faith and his birth from God" (VL 40326–40329; VE 27715–27722).

## Notes, not findings

- "Dream." Cyprian says it came before he slept ("nondum somni quiete sopito", VL 41016), and Pontius calls it a vision ("post uisionem", VL 41081). The brief says "Before he fell asleep", so the listener is not misled, and "dream" is the slate's approved word.
