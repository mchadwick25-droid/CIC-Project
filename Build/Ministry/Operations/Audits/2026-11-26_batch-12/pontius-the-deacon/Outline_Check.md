# Outline check: Pontius the Deacon

## My own account of the heart (written before reading Outline.md)

From the census entry (knownFor, keyWorks, uncertain), the source sheet and what Pontius is known for.

- **The essence:** One of Cyprian's deacons, whom Cyprian chose to share his exile at Curubis, wrote the book that tells Cyprian's life and death, for people who had heard Cyprian preach, so that his example would be handed on; it was kept with Cyprian's own writings. The book never names its author; Jerome names him, Pontius, Cyprian's deacon.
- **Also told (what the book says and why):** why he wrote (the church had recorded ordinary martyrs' sufferings in detail, so a great bishop's should not be passed over; to hand the example on, VE 27672–27685); what he chose to tell (begins at Cyprian's baptism and passes over the years before; care in the plague, to all, not only the household of faith); the exile at Curubis that he shared by choice; the dream Cyprian told on the first day there (the young man, the sentence written without questions, the hand like a blade, the day's delay), kept in Cyprian's voice, and Pontius's reading of the day as a year; where Pontius's account and Cyprian's own last letter differ on the final withdrawal (credited each); the end, his grief at being left behind ("I remained behind"). The death and plague are clauses (sibling).
- **What came of him:** 1. the Life was copied with Cyprian's works (the list of 359 ends with it; joined to the Acts in manuscripts); 2. Jerome read it, named its author and called it "a notable volume"; 3. Bede carried Jerome's line on; 4. later calendars keep him as a saint on 8 March (late). Defended: Cyprian's memory as bishop and martyr, including his withdrawal under Decius as obedience, not fear (VE 27905–27908).
- **Confidence:** Life written after the death by a companion, Documented (OWN); author's name Pontius and office deacon, Widely Accepted on Jerome alone; dream Documented as Pontius's report, a wonder in his voice; his own end not known (martyrdom Inferential-Thin).

## Verdict: FIX

The outline's heart agrees with mine in substance (the willing exile, the dream and his reading of it, why he wrote, what he chose, the two accounts of the last withdrawal, the grief; kept with Cyprian's works and named by Jerome). Every quote is verbatim and well rendered. The Library sweep is real. Two BLOCKING findings: the length, and the last trial told in two beats.

## My renderings, compared

- Quote 1 (VL 40267–40270): "I have resolved to write a few things in brief, not because the life of so great a man is hidden from anyone, even among the pagans, but so that this matchless and great example may reach those after us too, into undying memory." The outline's rendering agrees; "passed on" for *porrigatur* is fair. Stops correctly before *et ut ad exemplum sui litteris dirigantur* (HAR 447–451).
- Quote 2 (VL 41025–41029; HAR 1309–1313): "For with his hand spread out and flattened like a sword, he imitated the stroke of the usual punishment, and what he wanted understood he expressed as clearly as plain speech. I understood the sentence of martyrdom to come." Agrees.
- Quote 3 (VL 41405–41410; HAR, AASS): agrees, including "this was how I felt" for *in hac me fuisse sententia*.

**2. BLOCKING: the last trial is in two beats, and Beat 6 overstates it.** Beat 4 tells the sentence and death; Beat 6 goes back to the trial ("Of the last trial he writes only that Cyprian was asked his name"). That is an event told twice and a jump back. It also says more than the source: Pontius gives the questions in one line, then tells the governor reading from his tablet "the sentence which lately in the vision he had not read" (VE 28227–28237; VL 41309–41319). Correction: in Beat 6, delete "Of the last trial he writes only that Cyprian was asked his name: 'He answers who he is, and nothing more' (VE 28229–28230; VL 41310; the English short phrase may stand)." In The heart, **Also told**, replace "and to tell the last trial in one line" with nothing (end the sentence at "for the first hearing"). In Never cut, delete "and the one-line trial". Add to Do not say: "'Pontius tells only that he was asked his name' → he gives the questions in one line, then the sentence read from the governor's tablet (VE 28227–28237)."

**3. MINOR: the Roman rites gloss adds "sacrifices".** KNOPF 4826–4827 has *Romanas caeremonias recognoscere*, "take part in the Roman ceremonies"; no sacrifice is named. In Words to explain, replace "the sacrifices to Rome's gods, which the emperors ordered" with "the ceremonies of Rome's gods, which the emperors ordered". Add to Do not say: "'refused to sacrifice' → 'would not take part in the Roman rites' (KNOPF 4826–4827)."

**4. MINOR: the news from Rome says more than Pontius.** VE 28137–28139: "a messenger came to him from the city from Xistus, the good and peace-making priest, and on that account most blessed martyr" (VL *de Xisto*). Pontius does not say the news was of his death. In Beat 4, replace "News came from Rome that its bishop had been put to death (VE 28137–28139; the bishop by role)" with "A messenger came from Rome, from (or about) its bishop, whom Pontius calls a most blessed martyr (VE 28137–28139; the bishop by role)". It is already Cut first item 4 (card); the card takes the corrected wording.

**5. MINOR: "the emperor Decius" has no cited old source.** The *Life*'s withdrawal passage (VE 27902–27910) names a "cruel persecution", not Decius. In Beat 6, replace "his withdrawal under the emperor Decius" with "his withdrawal in an earlier persecution", and in Names delete the Decius line. It is Cut first item 15 in any case.

**6. MINOR: Who tells it overstates.** "All we know of Pontius is what his book says" is wrong, since Jerome supplies his name and office. Replace with: "almost all we know of Pontius is what his own book says, with Jerome's one sentence for his name; he writes as one who loved and praised his bishop."

**7. MINOR: too many quoted spans.** Beside the three quotes the outline offers six short quoted phrases. After the cuts above, keep at most "a willing exile", "these things I pass over" and "a notable volume" in quotation marks; tell "the whole world is one home" (card) and "as had been shown to him" (card) in narration if at all. "He answers who he is, and nothing more" is Wallis's 1868 English, not a rendering of ours; it goes to the card with a modern rendering of VL 41310 (*se esse respondit. et hactenus uerba*: "He answered that he was. That was all that was said").
