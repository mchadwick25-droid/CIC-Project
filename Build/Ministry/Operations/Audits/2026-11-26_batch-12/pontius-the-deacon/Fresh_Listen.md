## 1. The test (person kit)

- **Who this was:** Pontius, one of Cyprian's deacons. He went into exile with his bishop and later wrote the bishop's Life and Passion. He is named by Jerome, because the book gives no name. Filled.
- **What happened:** He chose exile at Curubis and heard Cyprian's dream there. He was with Cyprian on his last night, and after Cyprian died he wrote the book. Filled.
- **What it was about:** Why he wrote ("so that this great and matchless example may be passed on to those after us"). What he chose to tell: he starts from Cyprian's faith, passes over Cyprian's studies, and sends readers to the court record. Filled.
- **Why the connection matters:** The book was copied with Cyprian's own writings, and Jerome called it "a notable volume". This is short, but I can say it back. Filled.

## 2. The opening

After three sentences I know where (Carthage, then Curubis on the coast), when (257), and why there (exile, because Cyprian would not take part in the ceremonies of Rome's gods). What is missing is the subject's name. He is only "one of the men who went with him" until sentence 4. That sentence explains why: the book never gives his name. So this does not feel like a held-back reveal, and I was not lost. MINOR, noted.

Also noted: "Carthage" is not placed for someone with no background. "Rome's gods" tells me it is in the Roman world, and that is enough to follow. Nothing to change.

## 3. Sentence that fails on one hearing

- "He agreed, he wrote, because a bishop should confess his faith in the city where he leads the church." (paragraph 5)
  - **Meaning lost:** On one hearing I did not see why *leaving* lets a bishop confess his faith in his own city. The link is that officers were coming to take him to another city, and hiding meant he would be tried at Carthage. I only half got it. "Confess his faith" also sounds like a church phrase.
  - **Why MINOR:** The thread comes back with the very next sentence ("Arrested, he spent his last night..."). Noted for the reviser only if the outline holds a plainer link. Nothing added here.

## 8. Pull and drift

- **Pull:** "a willing exile", and his wish to have died with Cyprian, pulled me in at once.
- **Drift:** my attention first drifted at "Back from Curubis, Cyprian stayed at his gardens." In that paragraph Pontius steps out of the story, and two accounts of whether Cyprian fled are set side by side. It is Cyprian's story for a moment, and the logic of the letter is the hard part (see section 3).
- **Interest came back:** at "The church's forebears, Pontius writes...", when the story returns to why he wrote.
- **To the end?** Yes. The ending ("I grieve more that I remained behind") is the strongest moment and closes on the subject's own words.
