## 1. The test (person kit)

- **Who was this?** Filled: Pontius, one of Cyprian's deacons, who went with him into exile and wrote his Life and Passion; his book has no name on it, Jerome names him.
- **What happened?** Filled: exile at Curubis in 257, the dream, Cyprian's arrest and last night, his death in 258, then the book.
- **What was it about?** Filled: why he wrote (so the example would pass to later readers, as earlier martyrs' sufferings had been written down), and what he chose to tell (from the baptism on, the studies passed over, the court record left to itself).
- **Why the connection matters?** Filled, thinly: I can say the book kept Cyprian's example, was copied with his writings and ended the list of his works, and Jerome praised it. I could not say what later readers did with it beyond keeping it. Not blocking; "could be stronger" only.

## 2. The opening (first three sentences)

- Who: Cyprian, bishop, and an unnamed man who went with him. When: 257. Why: he would not take part in the ceremonies of Rome's gods. Where: Carthage and Curubis.
- Missing: **the subject's name.** For three sentences I thought the story was about Cyprian. Sentence 4 ("His book never gives his name") gives the reason, so it does not feel like a held-back trick, and I was not lost. MINOR.
- Missing: **where Carthage is.** With no background I do not know Carthage is in North Africa, under Rome. "Rome's gods" tells me it is the Roman world, so I am not lost. MINOR. Change, if the outline's Setting holds it: "the governor at Carthage, in Roman North Africa, sent..."

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing

1. **BLOCKING (loses the thread).** Paragraph 5: "Pontius says Cyprian later turned down friends' offers of places to go. Cyprian's own last letter says when officers came to take him to another city, he left his gardens for a while, on their advice, to be tried in the city where he led the church."
   - Meaning lost: (a) where Cyprian is. The last place I heard was Curubis; now he has "gardens" and is in a city that is not "the city where he led the church". I was never told he came back from exile, or where the gardens are. (b) Whose advice: "their" sounds like the officers'. (c) The two sentences seem to contradict each other: he refused places to go, then he left. I am not told these are two witnesses giving different sides, so I cannot tell what he actually did.
   - Change: tell, from the outline only, where he was when the officers came (that he had come back from Curubis to his gardens, if the outline holds it); say whose advice it was by name or role; and join the two accounts so the listener hears both, for example "Pontius says Cyprian turned down friends' offers of places to go. Cyprian's own last letter adds that when officers came to take him to another city, he went away from his gardens for a while, on [whose] advice, so that he would be tried in Carthage, where he led the church." If the outline does not hold these, cut to what it holds rather than leave the jump.
2. MINOR. Paragraph 3: "The young man behind him read it and showed it by a sign." "Behind him" — behind Cyprian or behind the governor? I lost who stood where until the quote. Change: "The young man, standing behind the governor, read it..." (if that is the record's meaning).
3. MINOR. Paragraph 3: "Before he fell asleep, he said, a young man ... seemed to lead him" — I heard "dream" and then "before he fell asleep" and was confused for a moment. "Seemed" helps. Change, if the record allows: "As he was falling asleep, he said, ..."
4. MINOR. Paragraph 3 quote: "copied the stroke of the usual execution." I understood a sword, but not at once that this means cutting off the head. The plain sense could stand alongside, if the outline gives it.
5. MINOR. Paragraph 6: "It would be hard if the death of so great a bishop were passed over" — "hard" here means wrong or unfair; in my English "hard" means difficult. Change: "It would be wrong if..." only if the outline's rendering allows it.

Understood, though long or formal, and not counted: "In the same breath he wished...", "Pontius read the one day as a year more of life" (made clear later by "the very day of the year"), the sentence on the list of works.
