## Each changed sentence

1. "Cyprian told them of a dream he had on their first day at Curubis." **HELD.** VL 41012–41016: "'eo enim die quo iu [in] exilii ioco [loco] mansimus' ... 'apparuit mihi' iuquit [inquit]"; VE 28055: "For on that day whereon we first abode in the place of banishment ... 'there appeared to me,' said he". The day dates the vision, not the telling. Outline Beat 2 and Do not say (line 148) hold it word for word.

2. "The young man standing behind the governor read it and showed it by a sign." **HELD.** VL 41020–41023: "iuuenis qui a tergo eius stabat ... legit quidquid fuerat adnotatum ... nutu declaraute [declarante] moustrauit [monstrauit]"; *eius* is the seated proconsul (VL 41017). The outline holds it (Beat 2; Cut first 32).

3. "Back from Curubis, Cyprian stayed at his gardens." **HELD.** KNOPF 4854–4856: "de ciuitate Curubitana ... regressus esset, ex sacro praecepto in suis hortis manebat". The outline holds it (Beat 4; Do not say, line 150).

4. "Pontius says he turned down friends' offers of places to go." **HELD.** VE 28145–28149: "they also offered places to which he might retire. But he ... did not consent to their tempting persuasions"; VL 41184–41185: "quae sececleret [secederet] offerebant ... nec suadelis blandientibus aduuebat [adnuebat]". Credited to Pontius. The outline holds it (Cut first 21).

5. "But Cyprian's own last letter says that when he heard officers had been sent to take him to another city, his dearest friends persuaded him to leave for a while." **HELD.** EP 17370–17372: "Cum perlatum ad nos fuisset ... commentarios esse missos qui me Vticam perducerent et consilio carissimorum persuasum esset ut de hortis nostris interim secederem". "Another city" stands for Utica; "leave" is "leave his gardens", placed by sentence 3. The outline holds it verbatim (Cut first 21).

6. "He agreed, he wrote, because a bishop should confess his faith in the city where he leads the church." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** EP 17372–17375: "iusta interueniente causa consensi, eo quod congruat episcopum in ea ciuitate in qua ecclesiae dominicae praeest illic Dominum confiteri". "Confess the Lord" is rendered "confess his faith": a fair compression, its force intact. The outline holds it verbatim (Cut first 21; Do not say, line 149).

7. "Arrested, he spent his last night in an officer's house, and Pontius and others who shared Cyprian's table were with him." **HELD.** VL 41246–41248: "receptum eum tamen et in domo principis constitutum una nocte continuit custodia delicata, ita ut conuiuae eius et cari in coutubernio [contubernio] ex more fuerimus"; VE 28181–28184: "we, his associates and friends, were as usual in his company". The narrator now states Pontius's presence where v1 credited it ("Pontius writes that he"). That rests on the book's own "we" plus Jerome's name for its author, as the second paragraph already states it ("was with him on his last night"). The outline's essence states it the same way (E6, Documented, the book's own claim). Not a finding.

8. "He began where Cyprian's faith began, because the deeds of a man of God, he wrote, should be counted only from the time he was born of God." **HELD.** VL 40326–40329: "unde exordium bonorum eius adgrediar, nisi a principio fidei et natiuitate caelesti? siquidem homiuis [hominis] Dei facta non debent aliunde numerari, nisi ex quo Deo natus est"; VE 27705–27706: "from the time that he was born of God". The outline holds it (Beat 6; Do not say, line 151).

9. "The book was copied with Cyprian's own writings." **HELD**, as a fact of the record known through its editors. VSB 4159–4161: "die auch in vielen Hss. der Sammlung von Werken Cyprians beigegebene Vita Cypriani per Pontium"; HAR 260–264 agrees. This is manuscript evidence an editor reports, not an editor's opinion (Kit Part 1: a modern work may clarify an old text's edition). The outline rates it Widely Accepted (I1). The revision cut v1's clause on the list of 359, whose own text is not held, so the sentence now claims only what the held reports show. The outline holds it verbatim (essence; What came 1).

10. "His book ends in grief." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** VL 41381–41410: "intor [inter] gaudium passionis et remanendi dolorem in partes diuisus animus ... plus tamen doleo quod remansi". The passage is divided between joy and grief, but its last words weigh grief more. Quote 3 follows at once and carries both. The outline gives this frame word for word (Ending, line 109: "the frame is only 'His book ends in grief.'").
