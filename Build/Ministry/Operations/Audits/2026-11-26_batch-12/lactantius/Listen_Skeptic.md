## 1. The test (person)

- **Who:** filled. An African teacher of Latin public speaking at the emperor's city of Nicomedia who wrote for the faith.
- **What happened:** filled. The Great Persecution began where he taught. Two men wrote against the Christians in front of him. He answered with the Divine Institutes. He taught Constantine's son in old age.
- **What it was about:** filled. Argue the faith to educated pagans from their own authorities; religion cannot be forced, so it is defended by dying, not killing.
- **Why the connection matters:** filled, partly. The Lucifer paragraph shows his sentences carried on and turned against an emperor; that lands. The Constantine thread (the dream, Crispus) does not: Constantine is never said to be an emperor, or why teaching his son mattered. The "last on the tree of the Latin Apologists" line is a label, not a connection. Not counted as unfilled, since Lucifer carries it.

## 2. The opening

- **Where:** "Bithynia", unplaced. It is never tied to Nicomedia later, so the listener cannot join the two.
- **When:** "the persecution" — which one, under whom, is not said until paragraph 2.

Paragraph 2 places him at once, so the listener is not lost for long. But the hook does not make plain sense heard once: "seemed at last to give way" — give way to what? ## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing

1. "The judges, he said, knew it was a contest." — "contest" has no referent. BLOCKING, above.
2. "Religion, they hold, cannot be forced, since it lives only in the will." — "they" heard once points back to "philosophers and historians", so the heart's claim sounds like the pagans' view. Recovered in paragraph 7. MINOR. Change: "The books hold that religion cannot be forced..."
3. "He came from Africa, says Jerome, writing later that century, on whom most of his life outside his books rests." — three clauses stacked after a short main clause; "on whom ... rests" dangles. MINOR. Change: "He came from Africa. Most of what we know of his life outside his books comes from Jerome, a church scholar writing later that century." (Gloss on Jerome only as the outline gives it.)
4. "...stripped Christians of rank of their honors..." — "of rank of" trips. MINOR. Change: "stripped Christians of high rank of their honors" or "stripped high-ranking Christians of their honors".
5. "wrote two books "to" the Christians" — the quotation marks carry the point, and audio drops them. MINOR. Change: say plainly what the title's "to" meant, as the source gives it, or drop the marks.
6. "...written to settle quarrels, and his were divine, a full course on..." — "his were divine" does not land as the contrast it is meant to be. MINOR. Change: "and he called his own Institutes divine: a full course on..."
7. "Yet he asked who could forbid him to read the Institutes..." — "him" can be heard as Lactantius. MINOR. Change: "Yet he asked who could forbid him, Jerome, to read..." or "who could stop anyone reading...", as the source gives it.
8. "...recast to address the emperor, without naming him." — "him" can be heard as the emperor. MINOR. Change: "without naming Lactantius."
