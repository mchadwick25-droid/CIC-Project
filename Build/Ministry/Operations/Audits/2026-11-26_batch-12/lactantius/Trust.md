## BLOCKING

**S10. "In his own words, he was called to Nicomedia to teach, and was teaching when 'the temple of God was overthrown.'"** SOFTENED OR CHANGED. His own words name the province, not the city: "ego cum in Bithynia oratorias litteras accitus docerem contigissetque ut eodem tempore dei templum euerteretur" (`lactantius_divinae-institutiones-lat_brandt1890-csel-tei.txt` 22774–22775; Fletcher, "When I was teaching rhetorical learning in Bithynia, having been called thither", `anf07_…xml` 4487). The city is Jerome's: "summoned to Nicomedia" (`npnf203_…xml` 41035). The outline has "He was 'called there' … he says" (Beat 1).
- Change: "In his own words, he was called to Bithynia to teach, and was teaching there when 'the temple of God was overthrown.'"
- Do not say: "In his own words, he was called to Nicomedia" → "called to Bithynia", his word; Nicomedia is Jerome's.

**S30. "Jerome wished Lactantius had been as ready to build up the faith as to tear down other people's."** SOFTENED OR CHANGED. Jerome's Latin speaks of ability, not readiness: "utinam tam nostra adfirmare potuisset, quam facile aliena destruxit!" ("would that he could have affirmed ours as easily as he tore down others'", `jerome_epistulae-1-70-lat_hilberg-csel54-1910.txt` 27050–27052). The NPNF "as ready to teach" (`npnf206_…xml` 14113–14114) is the source of the outline's wording; heard today, "ready" means willing, which softens a judgment of what he could not do into one of what he did not choose to do.
- Change: "Jerome wished Lactantius had been as able to build up the faith as he was quick to tear down other people's."
- Do not say: "as ready to build up the faith" → "as able to build up the faith as he was quick to tear down" (Latin *potuisset … facile*).

## SUMMARY HOLDS (with the line)

- S13 "two men published books against the Christians 'in my presence, and to my grief'": "Hi ergo de quibus dixi cum praesente me ac dolente sacrilegas suas litteras explicassent" (DI-L 23206–23207). The next sentence keeps his "to", not "against".
- S16 "He set out to answer them and all like them": "not that I should write against these … but that I might once for all by one attack overthrow all who everywhere effect, or have effected, the same work" (AN 4567).
- S17 Tertullian "learned in every kind of letters, but rough and very obscure": "skilled in literature of every kind; but in eloquence he had little readiness, and was not sufficiently polished, and very obscure" (AN 4472). - S19 "A beginner needs milk before solid food … refuted by his own authorities": "as an infant … is supported by liquid and soft milk … so also it was befitting that this man … should be presented with human testimonies … refuted by his own authorities" (AN 4581); "formed from the beginning this man as one who was altogether ignorant" (AN 4573).
- S20 title from the civil law, "written to settle quarrels", divine, on hope, life, salvation, immortality and God: "institutiones ciuilis iuris … quibus ciuium dissidentium lites contentionesque sopirent … diuinas institutiones" (DI-L 262–266; AN 569). - S21 "The pagan gods … had been men named gods after death": "it is evident from these things that they were men" (AN 1009); "after their death … they began to reverence the memory of the deceased" (AN 1011). He also says some were called gods while they lived; a fair compression.
- S22 philosophers refute one another; no religion without wisdom: "they disagree among one another with great contention" (AN 575), "these men mutually slay one another" (AN 2300); "neither is any religion to be undertaken without wisdom, nor any wisdom to be approved of without religion" (AN 584).
- S28 the disputed book, one copy, Lucius Caecilius, the dream: "Constantine was directed in a dream" (AN 10620); Jerome's "one book On persecution" (JE 41047; TE 11193); the one manuscript's "L(ucius) Cecilius" (MO 28680, editor clarifying the text; Gibbon in Coxe, "the Colbert ms.", AN-ed 9924). ## The heart

Right for the subject. The record shows a teacher at Diocletian's city when the persecution began, a book that argues to the educated from their own authorities, and its centre, that religion is willed and defended by dying, carried on by Lucifer against a Christian emperor. The scene (Quote 1) and the claim (Quote 3) meet the Teacher-or-thinker trap. Opening and essence close at about word 149, inside the first minute. The outline's essence also names Crispus; the brief tells him in time order (S29), which Part 1 allows; not a finding.
