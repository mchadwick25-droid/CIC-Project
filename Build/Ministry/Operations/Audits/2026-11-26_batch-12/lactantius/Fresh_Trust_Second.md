## Sentence by sentence

1. "Lactantius wrote this during the persecution of the Christians, among governors who boasted that they had shed no blood." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** DI-L 24567–24569: "nam et ipse audiui aliquos gloriantes quod administratio sua in hac parte fuerit incruenta"; AN 4899 "I myself have heard some boasting that their administration has been in this respect without bloodshed". The boasters are "huiusmodi iudices" (DI-L 24563), office-holders who judged, and the one he saw is a *praeses*: "governors" is fair. "During": the passage runs in the present ("haec autem ... faciunt", DI-L 24566).

2. "The judges, he said, knew it was a contest, and tortured to win." **HELD.** DI-L 24572–24574: "in excogitandis poenarum generibus nihil aliut quam uictoriam cogitant: sciunt enim certamen esse illud et pugnam"; AN 4899 "they think of nothing else besides victory. For they know that this is a contest and a battle".

3. "Lactantius was teaching Latin public speaking at Nicomedia in Bithynia, in what is now north-west Turkey, the city where the emperor Diocletian kept his palace, when the Great Persecution began in 303." **HELD.** DI-L 22773–22774 "ego cum in Bithynia oratorias litteras accitus docerem contigissetque ut eodem tempore dei templum euerteretur"; JE 41035–41037 "summoned to Nicomedia ... taught rhetoric there ... (since it was a Greek city)"; EU 43982–43983 "the palace at Nicomedia"; EU 43460–43461 the nineteenth year of Diocletian (303, Widely Accepted per S6). "Latin" stands on Jerome's Greek city and his Chronicle's "Latinis litteris" (JC).

4. "The books hold that religion cannot be forced, since it lives only in the will." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** DI-L 26133–26135 "quia religio cogi non potest"; DI-L 26247–26250 "nihil est enim tam uoluntarium quam religio, in qua si animus sacrificantis auersus est, iam sublata, iam nulla est".

5. "He was called to Bithynia to teach, he says, and was teaching when 'the temple of God was overthrown.'" **HELD.** DI-L 22773–22774 "in Bithynia oratorias litteras accitus docerem ... dei templum euerteretur"; AN 4487 "having been called thither ... the temple of God was overthrown". The place is now his own word (Bithynia), and he is not said to have watched it.

6. "The imperial order leveled churches, burned Scriptures, stripped high-ranking Christians of their honors, and took freedom from Christian servants who would not give up the faith." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** EU 43487–43490: "commanding that the churches be leveled to the ground and the Scriptures be destroyed by fire, and ordering that those who held places of honor be degraded, and that the household servants, if they persisted in the profession of Christianity, be deprived of freedom." Dropping the credit to Eusebius is right by Part 1: the old sources do not differ on the order, and the outline's Names line says so.

7. "The other, a judge who had pressed for the persecution, wrote two books to the Christians, to prove their Scripture contradicted itself." **HELD.** AN 4505: "of the number of the judges, and who was especially the adviser of enacting persecution ... two books, not against the Christians ... but to the Christians ... to prove the falsehood of sacred Scripture, as though it were altogether contradictory to itself". The quotation marks around "to" are gone. They are not heard aloud, so nothing is lost in the audio.

8. "Of the Christian writers before him, he found Tertullian learned in every kind of literature, but rough and very obscure." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** DI-L 22707–22709: "Septimius quoque Tertullianus fuit omni genere litterarum peritus, sed in eloquendo parum facilis et minus comptus et multum obscurus fuit". The group is "ex iis qui mihi noti sunt" (DI-L 22701), meaning the learned who took up the truth's defense (DI-L 22698–22699), so "Christian writers before him" holds. "Rough" fairly gives "parum facilis et minus comptus".

9. "He took his title from the lawyers' handbooks of the civil law, called Institutes, written to settle quarrels." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** DI-L 262–265: "et si quidam prudentes et arbitri aequitatis institutiones ciuilis iuris conpositas ediderunt, quibus ciuium dissidentium lites contentionesque sopirent, quanto melius nos et rectius diuinas institutiones litteris persequemur"; AN 569. He sets his title against theirs in so many words, so "took his title from" is not an added cause.

10. "His own were divine, a full course on hope, life, salvation, immortality and God." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** AN 569: "the divine Institutions, in which ... we shall speak of hope, of life, of salvation, of immortality, and of God". "A full course" glosses *institutiones* (instruction), as the outline's Words to explain allows.

11. "He agreed religion must be defended with all one's strength: 'For religion must be defended not by killing but by dying, not by cruelty but by endurance, not by crime but by faithfulness.'" **HELD.** DI-L 26236–26242: "sentiunt enim nihil esse in rebus humanis religione praestantius eamque summa ui oportere defendi, sed ... in defensionis genere falluntur. defendenda enim religio est non occidendo sed moriendo, non saeuitia sed patientia, non scelere sed fide"; DI-L3 35546–35550 agrees. The rendering has every clause and adds nothing.

12. "A book on how the persecutors died survives in one copy under the name of a Lucius Caecilius, and whether it is Lactantius's is disputed." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** The text is held (AN 9975 ff.). The single manuscript and its name rest on editors who clarify the text's transmission: MO 28683 "[le] manuscrit attribue le De mortibus à L(ucius) Cecilius"; AN-ed 9924 "the Colbert ms. ... Cæcilius". The doubt is the outline's D1, rated Contested, and is stated plainly. The v1 sentence "Jerome lists among his books one on persecution" (JE 41047) is gone, as the outline's Leave out marks it. That does not mislead, because the doubt is still told.

13. "It tells how a dream directed the emperor Constantine to mark his soldiers' shields with the sign of Christ." **HELD.** AN 10620: "Constantine was directed in a dream to cause the heavenly sign to be delineated on the shields of his soldiers ... being the cipher of Christ". It is told as the book's. The barred "Conquer by this" is not used.

14. "In extreme old age, Lactantius taught Constantine's son Crispus in Gaul." **HELD.** JE 41051–41053: "In his extreme old age he was tutor to Crispus Cæsar a son of Constantine in Gaul"; JC "quorum Crispum Lactantius Latinis litteris erudiuit". Jerome is credited once, early ("on whom most of his life outside his books rests"), so dropping the credit here follows Part 1's one-witness rule.

15. "Jerome wished Lactantius could have built up the faith as easily as he tore down other people's." **HELD.** JLL 27050–27052: "utinam tam nostra adfirmare potuisset, quam facile aliena destruxit"; JL 14113–14114. "Could have ... as easily" now follows *potuisset ... facile*, where v1's "as ready" followed the 1893 English.

16. "Yet he asked who could forbid him, Jerome, to read the Institutes, written against the pagans with much ability." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** JL 19209–19212: "But who can forbid me to read his Institutes—in which he has written against the Gentiles with much ability—simply because this opinion of his is to be abhorred?" Note, not a finding: "Yet" joins the question in Letter 84 to the wish in Letter 58. In Letter 84 the question answers a different fault, the Holy Spirit charge, which Cut first 22 sent to the card. Both letters fault his teaching and keep reading him, so the contrast is the same and nothing is misled.

17. "Lucifer had taken over Lactantius's sentences without naming him, recast to address the emperor." **HELD.** LU 16935–16940: "dicis talia honesta uoluntate errans. sentis enim nihil esse in rebus humanis religione praestantius eamque summa ui oportere defendi; defendis gladio, at nos defendendam nouimus religionem non occidendo, sed moriendo pro deo". Compare DI-L3 35545–35550: "o quam honesta uoluntate miseri errant! sentiunt enim nihil esse ... summa ui oportere defendi ... defendenda enim religio est non oecidendo sed moriendo". Lucifer's third persons become second persons, and he names no source. The moved clause now sits beside "taken over", which reads more clearly.

18. "Jerome called Lactantius the most eloquent man of his time, yet so poor in this life that he often lacked even what he needed." **HELD.** JC (44984–44986): "uir omnium suo tempore eloquentissimus sed adeo in hac uita pauper ut plerumque etiam necessariis indiguerit". Dropping "In his history of the world" removes a description, not a qualifier.
