## Findings

**Finding 1 (BLOCKING, SOFTENED OR CHANGED): sentence 22.**
- Brief: "The pagan gods, he argued, had been men named gods after death."
- Source, AN 1011 (*Institutes* I.15): "men began to exalt the king himself, and his whole family, with the highest praises and with new honours, so that they even called them gods; whether on account of their wonderful excellence… or, as is commonly the case, in flattery of present power". Only after that does it go on: "after their death left regret of themselves… they began to reverence the memory of the deceased".
- The problem: Lactantius has kings called gods while they still ruled, and honoured further after death. "Named gods after death" narrows what he says and moves the naming onto the wrong event. AN 1009 holds the core: "it is evident from these things that they were men".
- **Exact change:** "The pagan gods, he argued, had been men named gods after death." → "The pagan gods, he argued, had once been men." (4 fewer words.)
- Do not say, to add to the outline: "named gods after death" → "had once been men" (AN 1009–1011: some were called gods in life).

**Finding 2 (BLOCKING, SOFTENED OR CHANGED, a misleading implication): sentence 27.**
- Brief: "…survives in one copy under the name of a Lucius Caecilius…"
- Source: "a Lucius Caecilius" makes the name sound like a stranger's. But the manuscripts of the *Institutes* themselves give Lactantius that name. DI-L 131: "L. Caecilii, Firmiani". DI-L 5452–5454: "CECILII FIRMIANI DE FALSA RELIGIONE… CAECILII FIRMIANI DE ORIGINE ERRORIS". DI-L 32648: "CAECILI FIRMIANI LACTANCI".
- The problem: the indefinite article tilts the dispute toward "someone else". That carries one side's view forward.
- The rest of the sentence stands. "One copy" is a fact about the text's transmission (MO 28684), which Part 1 allows a modern work to clarify. "Disputed" states the Contested rating plainly.
- **Exact change:** "under the name of a Lucius Caecilius" → "under the name Lucius Caecilius" (1 fewer word). The fuller point, that copies of the *Institutes* also call him Caecilius, goes to the card.
- Do not say, to add to the outline: "a Lucius Caecilius" → "the name Lucius Caecilius" (DI-L 131, 5452–5454, 32648).

No other sentence is BLOCKING. With both changes the brief runs 668 words, within the approved 675.
