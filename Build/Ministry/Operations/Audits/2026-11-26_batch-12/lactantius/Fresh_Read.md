- Old: "The pagan gods, he argued, had been men named gods after death."
- New: "The pagan gods, he argued, had once been men."
- Words: −3. (The reader gave −4; the count is 12 → 9.)
- **Do not say:** "named gods after death" → "had once been men" (AN 1009–1011: some were called gods in life).

**2. BLOCKING (fresh trust, Finding 2, sentence 27).** The article "a" makes the name sound like a stranger's. The copies of the *Institutes* give Lactantius that name too (DI-L 131, 5452–5454, 32648), so the article tilts the dispute toward one side.
- Old: "under the name of a Lucius Caecilius"
- New: "under the name Lucius Caecilius"
- Words: −2. (The reader gave −1; both "of" and "a" go.)
- **Do not say:** "a Lucius Caecilius" → "the name Lucius Caecilius" (DI-L 131, 5452–5454, 32648).

**3. MINOR (fresh listen, 3a and 7a, sentence 9).** The "on whom … rests" clause comes too late to follow on one hearing, and the listener never learns who Jerome was. The new sentence still says once, early, and inside the sentence that brings Jerome in, that the life rests on him (Who tells it). It keeps "from Africa, Jerome says", not "born in Africa" (Do not say). "A Christian writer later that century" matches the outline's gloss for Jerome ("a church writer later that century") in sense.
- Old: "He came from Africa, says Jerome, writing later that century, on whom most of his life outside his books rests."

- Words: +2.

**4. MINOR (fresh listen, 3b, sentence 21).** Heard aloud, the old wording sounds as if the narrator is calling the books divine. The new wording makes it the title he gave them (DI-L 262–265, "diuinas institutiones").

## The Lucius Caecilius sentence: ruling between the listener and the trust reader

The listener's cut (3c, −7 words) would drop the name: "A book on how the persecutors died survives in one copy, and whether Lactantius wrote it is disputed." The trust reader keeps the name and drops "a" (change 2). **The trust reader's rewording wins, and the cut is not applied.** There are four reasons.

- **The bar.** Finding 2 is BLOCKING: it is a misleading implication. The listener's 3c is MINOR, a dip in attention that leaves no one lost. Under the bar, a name heard once that neither acts nor links is minor, and blocks only where the listener is lost. The blocking fix takes the lead.
- **The outline.** The outline makes the name the book's only credit, in two places. Do not say has "a book … which survives in one copy under the name of a Lucius Caecilius; whether it is Lactantius's is disputed". Who tells it has "Credited only as 'a book … under the name of a Lucius Caecilius'". The trust reader's change keeps that credit and removes only the article that misleads.
- **Rigor.** The name in the one copy is the only old ground the story gives for tying the book to him. Jerome's "On persecution" is on the Leave out list. Without the name, "whether Lactantius wrote it is disputed" has nothing under it, and the book is told further than its credit goes. That fails the listener's own condition ("tells the book only as far as the old sources credit it to him").
- **The listener's own fallback.** The listener asked to keep the name if the outline holds a line for it. The outline holds the name as required wording. It holds no clause that ties the name to Lactantius, and the trust reader sends that tie to the card.
