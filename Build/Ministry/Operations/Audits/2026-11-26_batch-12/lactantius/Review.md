## Verdict: REVISE

The heart is right and told in time. The record holds what the story needs. The faults are one misplaced word in his own testimony, one judgment of Jerome's softened, one live-text echo, an opening that leaves a newcomer unsure who is suffering, and a paragraph on the disputed book that names a dream and a book without saying what either was.

## BLOCKING

**B1. S9: his own words do not name Nicomedia.** (Trust S10, Trust_Second S9: SOFTENED.) "In his own words, he was called to Nicomedia to teach, and was teaching when 'the temple of God was overthrown.'" His Latin names the province: "ego cum in Bithynia oratorias litteras accitus docerem" (DI-L 22774; AN 4487). Nicomedia is Jerome's word (JE 41035).
- Change: "He was called to Bithynia to teach, he says, and was teaching when 'the temple of God was overthrown.'" (−2 words; Bithynia is already heard in Quote 1, and B4 ties it to Nicomedia.)
- Do not say (add): "In his own words, he was called to Nicomedia" → "he was called to Bithynia to teach, he says" (*in Bithynia … accitus*, DI-L 22774). Nicomedia is Jerome's word.

**B2. S29: Jerome's wish turned from ability into willingness.** (Trust S30: SOFTENED. Trust_Second: SUMMARY HOLDS. Judged BLOCKING; see disagreements.) "Jerome wished Lactantius had been as ready to build up the faith as to tear down other people's." Latin: "utinam tam nostra adfirmare potuisset, quam facile aliena destruxit!" (JLL 27050–27052). *Potuisset* means "could have", and *facile* means "easily". The NPNF "as ready" (JL 14113–14114) used "ready" in its old sense of quick. Heard today, "ready" means willing.
- Change: "Jerome wished Lactantius could have built up the faith as easily as he tore down other people's." (−1)
- **Needs the outline:** What came 1 holds "as ready to build up the faith as to tear down others'". The researcher corrects it there first.
- Do not say (add): "as ready to build up the faith" → "could have built up the faith as easily as he tore down other people's" (*potuisset … facile*, JLL 27050–27052).

**B3. S16: the live text's frame.** (Trust_Second, live-text echo. BLOCKING under the batch's ruling on live wording and the second-read rule.) "Of the Latin writers before him, he judged Tertullian …" echoes the live "Of the Latin defenders before him, Tertullian, he judged …". The outline holds "Of those before him, he found Tertullian" (Beat 3). The second trust read proposed "Of those before him", but "those" right after "them and all like them" would be heard as the two attackers. "Christian" is held by Lactantius's own run of Minucius, Tertullian and Cyprian, "of those known to me" (DI-L 22700; AN 4470). "Found" is the outline's verb, and "judged" is the live text's.
- Change: "Of the Christian writers before him, he found Tertullian learned in every kind of literature, but rough and very obscure." (0; "literature" is M9.)
- Do not say (add): "Of the Latin writers (or defenders) before him, he judged Tertullian" → "Of the Christian writers before him, he found Tertullian".

**B4. The opening leaves a newcomer lost about who suffers and where.** (First-time: BLOCKING, who and where. Skeptic: BLOCKING, "contest" has no referent and "give way" to what.) After three sentences the listener does not know that the persecution was of Christians, what the "contest" was, or where Bithynia is. Bithynia is never tied to Nicomedia later. The Do not say line still stands: the man is not called a Christian who gave in.
- Change S2: "Lactantius wrote this during the persecution of the Christians, among governors who boasted that they had shed no blood." (+3)
- Change S3: "The judges, he said, knew it was a contest, and tortured to win." (+4) Source: "in devising modes of punishment, they think of nothing else besides victory. For they know that this is a contest and a battle" (AN 4899; "sciunt enim certamen esse illud et pugnam", DI-L 24573). Quote 1's Job in the outline already holds "torture as a contest the judges meant to win".
- Change S4: "… teaching Latin public speaking at Nicomedia in Bithynia, in what is now north-west Turkey, …" (+2). This is the outline's own spoken line under Setting.
- **Needs the outline:** Opening (a) holds S3 as "The judges, he said, knew it was a contest." and "during the persecution" without naming who was persecuted. The material is in the outline (the Quote 1 Job and the Setting line), but the outline's wording changes.
- Do not say (add): in the opening, "during the persecution" with no one named → "during the persecution of the Christians". "The judges … knew it was a contest" on its own → "knew it was a contest, and tortured to win" (AN 4899). "Nicomedia" without Bithynia → "Nicomedia in Bithynia".

**B5. Paragraph 8: a dream and a book named bare, and a "his" that cannot be placed.** (Both listeners: BLOCKING bare statements for "tells of Constantine's dream" and "Jerome lists among his books one on persecution". Both listeners name this paragraph as a drift point. First-time failing sentence 5: "his" could be Caecilius, Constantine or Lactantius. Both: Constantine is never said to be emperor.)
- The dream: AN 10620 gives what it was: "Constantine was directed in a dream to cause the heavenly sign to be delineated on the shields of his soldiers … being the cipher of CHRIST." The slate requires the clause, so it is explained in the book's own voice, as the Do not say line ("the disputed book tells of it") requires. Constantine becomes "the emperor Constantine" here, at his first mention.
- The book on persecution: Jerome gives only its title (JE 41047), and no old source ties it to the surviving book (D1). Part 1 says that where the old sources do not explain a thing, it is left out. The sentence goes to the card.
- Change, replacing "Jerome lists among his books one on persecution. A book on how the persecutors died, surviving in one copy under the name of a Lucius Caecilius, tells of Constantine's dream, but whether it is his is disputed." with: "A book on how the persecutors died survives in one copy under the name of a Lucius Caecilius, and whether it is Lactantius's is disputed. It tells how a dream directed the emperor Constantine to mark his soldiers' shields with the sign of Christ." (+7)
- If the researcher would rather keep Jerome's list as the old ground for the doubt, the cheapest form joins it to the next sentence: "Jerome lists among his books one on persecution, and a book on how the persecutors died survives …" That costs 8 more words (+15 in all), and the title still says nothing of what the book held.
- **Needs the outline:** the dream's content (AN 10620) is not in the outline. Beat 6, D1 and the Do not say line hold "tells of Constantine's dream", "Jerome lists among his books one on persecution" and "whether it is his is disputed". The card takes Jerome's "one book On persecution".
- Do not say (add): "tells of Constantine's dream" with nothing more → "tells how a dream directed the emperor Constantine to mark his soldiers' shields with the sign of Christ" (AN 10620). "whether it is his" → "whether it is Lactantius's".

## Where the readers disagree, judged by the bar

- **Jerome's "ready" (B2).** The first trust read found it SOFTENED, and the second found SUMMARY HOLDS, because "build up the faith" is the outline's wording. The bar blocks a sentence that softens its source. *Potuisset … facile* judges what Lactantius could do, and today's "ready" says what he chose to do, so it is BLOCKING. The second read checked the phrase "build up" but not the modern sense of "ready". The outline's own wording is the cause, so the fix starts there.
- **"whether it is his is disputed" as a hedge.** The skeptic filed it as a BLOCKING tell. Neither the first-time listener nor either trust read did. **Not a tell.** Part 1 asks the story to "say plainly what is uncertain, without hedging", and the sentence does that, with no softening word. The old sources do not differ: no old writer says whose the book is, so there is no witness to credit. The parties to the dispute are modern, and Part 1 bars naming them. The real fault the skeptic heard was the unplaceable "his", which B5 fixes with "Lactantius's". The outline's Do not say wording otherwise stands.
- **"no religion without wisdom, nor wisdom without religion" (S21).** The skeptic filed it as a BLOCKING bare statement, and the first-time listener as a MINOR failing sentence. **MINOR.** Explain it governs a thing that is named and left unexplained. Here the teaching itself is stated in plain words, beside the philosophers' quarrel it answers. That its point could land harder is "could be stronger", which the bar says is not a finding. No change; noted for the researcher.
- **The skeptic's machine-made lines.** "He is the last on the tree of the Latin Apologists …" is the outline's spoken tradition line (Setting), and the person kit's essence asks why he is on the tree: not a fault. "and he said how" is taken up as M4, and the Jerome sentence as M11.
