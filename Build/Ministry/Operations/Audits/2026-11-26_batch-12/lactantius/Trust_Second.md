## Non-HELD sentences

### SOFTENED OR CHANGED (BLOCKING)

**S9.** "In his own words, he was called to Nicomedia to teach, and was teaching when 'the temple of God was overthrown.'"
- His own words name the province, not the city: "ego cum in Bithynia oratorias litteras accitus docerem" (DI-L 22774); "When I was teaching rhetorical learning in Bithynia, having been called thither" (AN 4487). Nicomedia is Jerome's word: "summoned to Nicomedia" (JE 41035). The sentence credits to Lactantius a place he does not name. Outline Beat 1 has only "called there".
- **Change:** "In his own words, he was called to Bithynia to teach, and was teaching when 'the temple of God was overthrown.'" (Bithynia is already heard in the opening quote.)

### SUMMARY HOLDS

- **S2.** "Lactantius wrote this during the persecution, among governors who boasted that they had shed no blood." Source: "nam et ipse audiui aliquos gloriantes quod administratio sua in hac parte fuerit incruenta" (DI-L 24567–24569); "I myself have heard some boasting that their administration has been in this respect without bloodshed" (AN 4899). "during the persecution" stands on the passage's present tense ("faciunt", "cogitant", "sciunt", DI-L 24566–24573). "Among governors" for "some … their administration" is a fair compression, and it is the outline's Do not say wording.
- **S4.** Teaching Latin public speaking at Nicomedia, Diocletian's palace city, when the persecution began in 303. Sources: "oratorias litteras accitus docerem … dei templum euerteretur" (DI-L 22774–22775); "taught rhetoric there … since it was a Greek city" (JE 41036–41037); "the palace at Nicomedia" (EU 43982–43983); "the nineteenth year of the reign of Diocletian" (EU 43460). The year 303 is Widely Accepted, so it is told plainly under Part 1.
- **S5.** "He answered with the Divine Institutes, seven books arguing the faith to educated pagans from their own philosophers and historians." Sources: "Divine institutes against the nations, seven books" (JE 41044–41045); "human testimonies—that is, of philosophers and historians—in order that he might especially be refuted by his own authorities … I have undertaken … to do this" (AN 4581).
- **S6.** "Religion, they hold, cannot be forced, since it lives only in the will." Sources: "religio cogi non potest" (DI-L 26134); "nihil est enim tam uoluntarium quam religio, in qua si animus sacrificantis auersus est, iam sublata, iam nulla est" (DI-L 26248–26250).
- **S8.** "He came from Africa, says Jerome …" Source: "an *Itinerary* of a journey from Africa to Nicomedia" (JE 41039–41040). "Later that century" holds: Jerome writes in Theodosius's reign. "Most of his life outside his books rests" on Jerome is the outline's one-witness sentence (Who tells it), and it stands inside the sentence about that witness, as Part 1 asks.
- **S11.** "Its aim, Lactantius writes, was to make Christians worship the gods 'by force and tortures.'" Source: the persecutors "wish to recall them to a good mind … minime, sed ui atque tormentis" (DI-L 26067–26071; AN 5134–5136). He is writing of those carrying out the persecution, so linking it to the order is fair, and the outline holds it (Beat 2, *Why*).
- **S16.** Tertullian, "learned in every kind of letters, but rough and very obscure." Source: "omni genere litterarum peritus, sed in eloquendo parum facilis et minus comptus et multum obscurus" (DI-L 22707–22709). Lactantius's frame is "ex iis qui mihi noti sunt" ("of those known to me", DI-L 22700). For the wording "Of the Latin writers before him", see the echo below.
- **S17.** Cyprian, "the most renowned", answered a pagan with Scripture. Sources: "unus igitur praecipuus et clarus extitit Cyprianus" (DI-L 22712–22713); "not by the testimonies of Scripture, which he plainly considered vain, fictitious, and false" (AN 4573).
- **S18.** Milk before solid food, refuted by his own authorities. Source: AN 4581 (quoted at S5).
- **S19.** The title taken from the lawyers' Institutes. Source: "institutiones ciuilis iuris conpositas ediderunt, quibus ciuium dissidentium lites contentionesque sopirent, quanto melius nos … diuinas institutiones" (DI-L 262–265); "of hope, of life, of salvation, of immortality, and of God" (AN 569). "A full course" glosses *institutiones*, as the outline gives it.
- **S20.** "The pagan gods, he argued, had been men named gods after death." Sources: "how they who were men obtained the name of gods"; "after their death left regret … they began to reverence the memory of the deceased … honours … upon the dead" (AN 1007–1009). In the same chapter he says some were called gods in their lifetime, through flattery. The weight of the chapter falls on honours after death, so the compression holds.
- **S21.** The philosophers refute one another; no religion without wisdom. Sources: "they disagree among one another with great contention" (AN 575); "these men mutually slay one another" (AN 2300); "neither is any religion to be undertaken without wisdom, nor any wisdom to be approved of without religion" (AN 584).
- **S27.** The book on the persecutors' deaths, its one copy and its disputed authorship. Sources: "directed in a dream to cause the heavenly sign to be delineated on the shields" (AN 10620); "manuscrit attribue le De mortibus à L(ucius) Cecilius" (MO 28684); "the Colbert ms. … Cæcilius" (AN-ed 9924). These are modern works clarifying how the text survives, which Part 1 allows. The doubt is credited, and nothing is claimed for him.
- **S29.** Jerome's wish. Source: "utinam tam nostra adfirmare potuisset, quam facile aliena destruxit" (JLL 27050–27052); "would that he had been as ready to teach our doctrines as he was to pull down those of others" (JL 14113–14114). "Build up the faith" for *nostra adfirmare* is the outline's wording.
- **S31.** Lucifer, a bishop, wrote to Constantius, Constantine's son, who backed the Arian teaching. Sources: "Lucifer, bishop of Cagliari … wrote a book against the emperor Constantius" (JE 41266–41270); "Constanti imperator" (LU 15818); "defendendum est, inquis, … Arrianum dogma" (LU 16934–16935); "there was a time when the Son was not" (Socrates, `npnf202_…` 2096). Constantius as a son of Constantine: Sozomen, "the Sons of Constantine … Constantius" (`npnf202_…` 32237).
