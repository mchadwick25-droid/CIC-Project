# Outline check: Lactantius

## My account of the heart (written before reading `Outline.md`)

From the census entry (fields other than `longDescription`), the source sheet and what he is known for:

- **Essence:** Lactantius, a teacher of Latin speaking from Africa, was called to Diocletian's city of Nicomedia and was teaching there when the Great Persecution began (303). Few pupils, then the persecution, turned him to writing: in the *Divine Institutes* he set out the faith in Cicero-like Latin for educated pagans, answering the gods' worshippers, the philosophers and the persecutors, and holding that religion cannot be forced and is defended by dying, not by killing. In extreme old age he taught Constantine's son Crispus in Gaul (Jerome).
- **Also told:** what he saw (the governor's joy when one Christian gave way after two years; the two writers who mocked the fallen church) and why he took up the task; why earlier Latin defenders had not reached the learned (Tertullian unpolished and obscure, Cyprian answering a pagan from Scripture he did not accept); what the *Institutes* say (the gods were once men, the philosophers contradict each other, wisdom and religion belong together, justice, true worship, the six ages and the thousand-year reign); his poverty (Jerome). *On the Deaths of the Persecutors* only as Jerome's "book On persecution" and a disputed attribution.
- **What came of him:** read as the Latin case for the faith, praised for style and faulted for teaching (Jerome); DEFENDED: religion freely chosen, defended by dying, which Lucifer of Cagliari turned against a Christian emperor; Augustine using his Sibyl testimonies and his pagan learning; card: the edicts kept in *Persecutors*, the Gelasian list, Calvin.

## Verdict: FIX

The heart matches mine in substance: the African teacher of Latin speaking at Nicomedia, the persecution beginning there, the *Institutes* argued to the educated from their own authorities, religion that cannot be forced and is defended by dying, Crispus late in life, and Jerome and Lucifer as what came of him. No major contribution is left out. The opening and essence come within the first minute, and the beats follow the story once, in order. The ending is not told early. All three quotes are verbatim in Brandt (DI-L 207–209, 24576–24579, 26240–26242, with the second scans agreeing). My own renderings match the outline's in sense; its choices ("governor" for *praeses*, "astonishingly", "faithfulness" for *fides*) hold. The five short phrases match the Latin and Fletcher. ### B1. BLOCKING: the length runs past 675 even with the Cut first list applied

My own recount agrees and comes out at or above that. The quotes and phrases make about 116 words. Six glosses at 8 make 48 and five credits at 6 make 30. The non-cut beats, What came 1–2, the opening and the ending hold about 50 distinct facts. The outline's "about 600" is its own estimate and does not hold. The approved length is 675.

**Correction.** Add these to the end of the **Cut first** list, in order, as items 9 to 13. Then restate the closing line as "Items 1 to 13 make about 200: about 660." None of them cuts the heart, the record's force, or what a named thing was about.

### B2. BLOCKING: the Connection field says Lucifer took the sentence "word for word"

Lucifer recasts the sentences as he takes them over. Lactantius has "sentiunt … defendenda enim religio est non occidendo sed moriendo" (DI-L3 35546–35550). Lucifer turns them on the emperor and adds to them: "sentis … defendis gladio, at nos defendendam nouimus religionem non occidendo, sed moriendo pro deo" (LU 16936–16940). The wording is close but not identical. "Word for word" says more than the source.

**Correction.** In **Connection to carry into the story**, replace "took over Lactantius's own sentence word for word (LU 16934–16942; DI-L3 35546–35550)" with "took over Lactantius's own sentences without naming him, recast to speak to the emperor: 'you feel … you defend it with the sword, but we know religion is defended not by killing but by dying for God' (LU 16934–16942; DI-L3 35546–35550)".

Add to **Do not say**: "Lucifer quoted him word for word" → "Lucifer took over his sentences, recast to address the emperor, without naming him."

### m1. MINOR: the essence says "their own poets and philosophers"

### m2. MINOR: "while they were being tortured" says more than the line

Also told says the two men published "while they were being tortured". AN 4497 has "at the very same time in which a righteous people were impiously assailed". **Correction:** replace "while they were being tortured" with "while the Christians were under attack".

### m3. MINOR: Beat 2 drops a qualifier from the edict

Eusebius says household servants lost their freedom "if they persisted in the profession of Christianity". He also says it was "those who held places of honor" who were degraded (EU 43489–43491). **Correction:** in Beat 2, replace "stripped Christians of rank and made Christian servants unfree" with "stripped Christians who held rank of their honors, and took freedom from Christian servants who would not give up the faith".

Add to **Do not say**: "made all Christian servants slaves" → "took freedom from Christian servants who would not give up the faith".

### m4. MINOR: What came 2 rests on the census, not an old source

A held old source gives it: Jerome, *Lives* XCV, "When he would not condemn the Nicene faith as represented by Athanasius, sent again to Palestine" (JE 41266–41271). **Correction:** cite JE 41266–41271 in place of CEN. If the clause is kept, the gloss of the quarrel is "the faith of the council of Nicaea, which Athanasius stood for".

### m5. MINOR: the gloss "Arian" has no source line

### m6. MINOR: the manuscript fact in Beat 6 is an editor's, not marked

Both are modern works that clarify an old text, which the kit allows. "Lucius" is their expansion of "L.". **Correction:** in **Who tells it**, under Monceaux and Schanz, add: "Monceaux (MO 28682) and Gibbon in Coxe (AN-ed 9924) report the one manuscript and its name 'L. Cecilius': modern, clarifying an old text (Beat 6)."
