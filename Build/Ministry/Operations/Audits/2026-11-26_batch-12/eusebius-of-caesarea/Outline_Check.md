# Outline check: Eusebius of Caesarea (`eusebius-of-caesarea`)

## My account of the heart, written before reading `Outline.md`

From the census entry (fields other than `longDescription`), `Card.existing.json`, the source sheet and what he is known for. (The batch's `Hearts_Confidence.md` lines for this story were seen in passing while finding the length count; this account is drawn from the sources above.)

- **Essence.** A bishop of Caesarea, on the coast of Roman Palestine, who set out, as he says, as "the first" on an untrodden path to write the church's story from Christ to his own day, gathering the letters, lists and books of earlier writers into one *Church History*. He lived through the Great Persecution and wrote what he saw, came to know Constantine and wrote his *Life*, and signed the creed of Nicaea after asking what its added word meant. He is on the tree because later historians began where he stopped, and because his pages keep earlier writers' words that we would otherwise lack.
- **Also told.** What the History is and how he made it (his own list of what it covers; passages gathered "like flowers"; the library at Jerusalem). Pamphilus, his friend, whose name he took, with whom he wrote the Defense of Origen, and whose death he saw. The persecution of 303: churches torn down, Scriptures burned, leaders jailed and pressed to sacrifice, and his rule to leave out those who fell. Constantine: seen young beside the old emperor; the vision told him under oath (in that voice, a clause). Nicaea: his church's creed read, the added word, questions asked, assent for peace, told beside Socrates's and Athanasius's accounts. Tyre, 335: sitting among the judges of Athanasius; Potamo's charge and the Egyptian bishops' charge, told as charges, with no finding. The *Life of Constantine* and its rule to speak only of the emperor's religion. His death after Constantine, Acacius following.
- **What came of him.** (1) Socrates, Theodoret and Sozomen began their histories where his ended. (2) Rufinus put the History into Latin and continued it; Jerome translated and continued his *Chronicle* and drew on the History. (3) His pages keep earlier writers' words (Polycrates's letter as the case). (4) Judged both ways as believer and historian (Athanasius, Jerome hostile; Socrates defending). Defended: Origen's good name, with Pamphilus; his own reading of the Nicene creed.
- **Shape.** Teacher or thinker: the question is how to tell the church's story at all, with no one before him; the impact is in later readers and continuers.
