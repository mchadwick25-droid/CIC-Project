## Faults in X
- "He built his History from the letters, lists and books of the Christians before him. Later historians of the church began their books where his History ended. He lived through the emperors' persecution of the church and wrote the emperor Constantine's Life." | Craft, linking ideas: a run of short sentences that each tell one act, with no cause, contrast or time joining them.
- "At Nicaea he signed the creed of a council of bishops, its statement of faith, after asking what its words meant. At Tyre a bishop who had suffered in the persecution charged that Eusebius had got out of prison by giving way." | Narrated story: "never the same event twice." Both events are told here and then told again in full later.
- "His friend in Caesarea was Pamphilus, a priest there." | Narrated story, "tell it once, in order": a stray sentence dropped in before the plan of the History, with nothing happening in it. Pamphilus only acts two paragraphs later.
- "They accused him over a cup used at the Lord's Supper, the church's meal of bread and wine, that had been broken." | Explain it or leave it out: the charge is named, but it never says what Athanasius was accused of doing with the cup, so the listener cannot tell what the charge was.
- "Socrates began his own history of the church with what Eusebius had left out, and he defended Eusebius against the charge of favoring Arius, since he had signed at Nicaea. Jerome, writing in Latin, called his History fine, and called him the most open champion of Arius's ungodly teaching." | Narrated story, ending: "on a later writer only when that is the story's point." The story's question is Potamo's charge, so closing on two later writers' verdicts moves away from the subject.
## Faults in Y
- "The bishop of Caesarea made him a priest, but they quarreled, and Basil went back to Pontus." | Explain it or leave it out: a quarrel (a break) is named with no word on what it was about or why it happened.
- "His accusers were filling the governor's ears" | Explain it or leave it out: an act of conflict with nothing said about who the accusers were or what they charged.
- "In the sixth century Benedict of Nursia closed his own Rule by pointing his monks to Basil's as an example of good living." | Narrated story, ending: it closes on a later writer instead of on Basil himself, even though the story's own question, the Spirit, resolved in the sentence before.
