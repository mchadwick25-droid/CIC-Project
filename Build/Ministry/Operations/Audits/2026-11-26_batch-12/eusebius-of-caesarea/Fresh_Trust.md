## Findings

### 1. S32, Socrates on Tyre: SOFTENED OR CHANGED (BLOCKING)

**Brief:** "Socrates says the council removed Athanasius as bishop when its inquiry in Egypt sided with his accuser."

**Source**, Socrates I.31–32, SOC 5185–5211:

> Athanasius seeing that those very individuals were to be sent to whom he had taken exception … exclaimed that 'their procedure was both treacherous and fraudulent … in order that an ex parte collection of the facts in evidence might be made.' … Those, therefore, who were sent to Mareotis, having made an ex parte investigation, held that what the accuser said was true. … the Synod in the first place condemned him in his absence; and when the result of the enquiry which had been instituted at Mareotis was presented, they voted to depose him.

Socrates says twice that the inquiry was one-sided ("ex parte"). It was made by men Athanasius had objected to as his adversaries. The brief says only "its inquiry … sided with his accuser", which drops that force. The listener hears that the council's own inquiry found against Athanasius. Socrates says a one-sided inquiry, made by his opponents, held that the accuser spoke the truth. The outline's own source note for this beat quotes "having made an ex parte investigation", so the outline already holds the word.

**Exact change:** "Socrates says the council removed Athanasius as bishop when a one-sided inquiry in Egypt sided with his accuser."

### 2. For the researcher: the hand charge (raised, not blocking in this read)

Paragraph 6 tells the charge that "a priest's hand [was] cut off". The only answer the story gives is that Epiphanius "calls them slanders". Socrates, the witness the story credits in S32, shows that charge failed at Tyre itself. In Socrates I.29–30 (SOC around 5085–5150), Arsenius is brought in alive with both hands: "let my accusers show the place whence the third was cut off". Then "Athanasius cleared himself from this charge". At I.32 the sentence of deposition is "wholly silent respecting the disgraceful defeat of the charge of murder".

Without that, a listener may take the deposition in S32 as resting on the hand charge. That is a misleading implication the outline does not guard against. The outline does not hold the failure of the hand charge, so this reader does not write it into the story. It goes back to the researcher under Kit Part 3: content the outline lacks goes to the researcher and the outline check.

There are two options:
- (a) Add one clause, with Socrates credited. For example: "Socrates says the priest was found alive with both hands, and the council removed Athanasius as bishop when a one-sided inquiry in Egypt sided with his other accuser."
- (b) Leave it to the card.

A Do not say line for the outline: "the council removed Athanasius" told so that it seems to rest on the hand charge → the hand charge failed when Arsenius was shown alive, in Socrates (SOC I.29–30); the removal followed the one-sided Mareotis inquiry (SOC 5185–5211).
