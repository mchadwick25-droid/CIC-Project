## Findings

### 1. S32, Socrates on Tyre: SOFTENED OR CHANGED (BLOCKING). Applied. +1 word

The source is Socrates I.31–32 (SOC 5185–5211): "having made an ex parte investigation, held that what the accuser said was true". The brief drops the one-sidedness, so the inquiry sounds fair. That softens the source, and the bar blocks it. The outline's source note for B6 quotes "ex parte", so the outline holds the word. "One-sided" is its plain sense. The change rests on the outline, and it is applied.

- Old: "Socrates says the council removed Athanasius as bishop when its inquiry in Egypt sided with his accuser."
- New: "Socrates says the council removed Athanasius as bishop when a one-sided inquiry in Egypt sided with his accuser."

The outline left room for this error: B6's own beat sentence reads "its inquiry". Do not say line for the outline: "its inquiry in Egypt sided with his accuser" → "a one-sided inquiry", Socrates's "ex parte" (SOC 5185–5199).

### 2. The hand charge: misleading implication (BLOCKING). Needs the outline

The brief tells two charges, the broken cup and a priest's hand cut off. Its only answer to them is that Epiphanius "calls them slanders", and the story itself says he is on Athanasius's side. Then it says the council removed Athanasius after an inquiry that "sided with his accuser". On one hearing, a listener takes the removal as resting on both charges, the hand among them. Socrates, the witness credited in that same sentence, shows that the hand charge failed at Tyre. Arsenius was brought in alive with both hands, and "Athanasius cleared himself from this charge" (SOC I.29–30, around 5085–5150). Socrates also says the sentence of deposition is "wholly silent respecting the disgraceful defeat of the charge of murder" (I.32).

The bar blocks misleading implications, so as the brief stands, it misleads. Applying finding 1 shades the inquiry, but it does not cure this. The outline does not hold Arsenius or the failure of the charge, so no wording can go into the story yet. This finding goes to the researcher and the outline check.

The reader's options:
- (a) Add one clause, with Socrates credited. For example: "Socrates says the priest was found alive with both hands, and the council removed Athanasius as bishop when a one-sided inquiry in Egypt sided with his other accuser." That is +10 words over the sentence in finding 1, which would make the brief about 908 split on whitespace. The outline check must confirm "his other accuser" (the Mareotis accuser on the cup charge).
- (b) Leave it to the card. On this read's judgment, (b) does not cure the implication in the story, because the story still tells the hand charge as standing. It holds only if the hand charge also leaves the story.

Do not say line for the outline: "the council removed Athanasius" told so that it seems to rest on the hand charge → the hand charge failed when Arsenius was shown alive, in Socrates (SOC I.29–30); the removal followed the one-sided Mareotis inquiry (SOC 5185–5211).

### 3. F1, Potamo scene, Eusebius's answer (MINOR, failing sentence). Applied. +2 words

The outline's B6 sentence is a paraphrase of EPI 44608–44613. The new wording keeps every clause. Eusebius is the judge in the scene, so "accused the judge" names him.

- Old: "If Athanasius's side came here to speak against him so, he said, their accusers must be telling the truth, for if they played the tyrant here, they did much more at home."
- New: "If Athanasius's side came here and accused the judge like this, he said, then the charges against them must be true, for if they acted like tyrants here, they did much more at home."
