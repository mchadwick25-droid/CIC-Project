## Sentence by sentence

1. "He believed he was the first to try, and he built his History from the letters, lists and books of the Christians before him." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** HE I.1.4, "since I am the first to enter upon the subject" (npnf201_eusebius-church-history-life-of-constantine.xml 6934–6937). HE I.1.1, "the successions of the holy apostles" (6878–6885). HE I.1.5, "plucked like flowers from a meadow the appropriate passages from ancient writers" (6962–6965). HE V.24, Polycrates's letter given whole (31310–31325). "He believed" keeps the claim in his voice (Do not say, D1).

2. "Later historians of the church began their books where his History ended." **HELD.** Socrates I.1, "we begin with the narration of the particulars which he has left out" (npnf202_socrates-sozomen-ecclesiastical-histories.xml 1861–1869). Theodoret I.1, "I shall begin my history from the period at which his terminates" (npnf203 5219–5224). Now at word 135, inside the first minute.

3. "At Tyre a bishop who had suffered in the persecution charged that Eusebius had got out of prison by giving way." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** Epiphanius, Panarion 68.8: Potamo "τὸν ὀφϑαλμὸν … ἐν τῷ διωγμῷ … ἐτυφλωϑὴ", and "Πῶς ἀνεχώρησας ἀπὸ τῆς φυλακῆς, εἰ μὴ ὅτι ὑπέσχου … τὸ ἀϑέμιτον πρᾶξαι, ἢ ἔπραξας;" (epiphanius_panarion-haer-48-69…oehler1860.txt 44582–44604). It is told as his charge, and the narrator does not say Eusebius was jailed.

4. "Eusebius's plan, in his own list, was to trace the lines of bishops in the great churches and the men who spoke or wrote for the faith." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** HE I.1.1, "those who have governed and presided over the Church in the most prominent parishes, and those who in each generation have proclaimed the divine word either orally or in writing" (npnf201 6878–6885).

5. "He would also tell of those who led others into error, and those who stood firm under torture down to his own day." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** HE I.1.2, those who "have run into the greatest errors, and … like fierce wolves unmercifully devastated the flock of Christ" (npnf201 6887–6891). HE I.1.3, those who "contended for it in the face of blood and of tortures, as well as the confessions which have been made in our own days" (6896–6900).

6. "Eusebius saw it, and wrote of the leaders of the churches as shepherds: [Quote 2]" **HELD.** HE VIII.2.1, "τούς τε τῶν ἐκκλησιῶν ποιμένας" after "ἐπείδομεν ὀφϑαλμοῖς" (eusebius_werke-bd2-2…schwartz-mommsen1908.txt 13614–13620; McGiffert, npnf201 43431–43437). The quote has not changed from v1, and it matches Outline Quote 2 word for word.

7. "Eusebius wrote to his church that there the emperor urged one added word, the Greek homoousios, which says the Son is of the same substance as the Father." **HELD.** Letter to Caesarea: the emperor "advised all present to agree to it … with the insertion of the single word, 'One in substance' (ὁμοούσιος)" (npnf201 2088–2091). The letter also has "one in substance with the Father" (2121–2124).

8. "Eusebius asked what it meant, and after questions and answers, he wrote, he signed for the sake of peace and so as not to fall from the right belief." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** The Greek has "οὐκ ἀνεξέταστον … Ἐπερωτήσεις τοιγαροῦν καὶ ἀποκρίσεις" (socrates_historia-ecclesiastica-tomus1…hussey1853.txt 7307–7310). The Latin has "pacis intuitu, et ne a recta intelligentia excidamus" (same file, 7395–7396), and the English "peace being the object … and steadfastness in the orthodox view" (npnf201 2131–2134). The letter says "assented". "Signed" comes from Socrates, "subscribed it with all the rest" (npnf202 2971–2975). The compression is fair, and "he wrote" keeps the reasons in his voice.

9. "A century later Socrates, a historian of the church, says he held back a short time before signing." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** Socrates I.8, "who had held aloof for a short time … at length acquiesced in it, and subscribed it" (npnf202 2971–2975). On "a century later": Socrates carries his history "down to the seventeenth consulate of the Emperor Theodosius the Younger" (npnf202 891). That year is 439, about a century and a decade after Nicaea, so the round figure is fair.

10. "When he saw Eusebius seated as judge and Athanasius standing, he wept, then cried out at Eusebius." **HELD.** "ἑωρακὼς τὸν Εὐσέβιον καϑεζόμενον καὶ δικάζοντα, καὶ Ἀθανάσιον ἑστῶτα … δακρύσας … ἀπετείνατο φωνῇ μεγάλῃ Εὐσεβίῳ λέγων" (EPI 44584–44590). Petavius has "magna voce adversus Eusebium invectus est" (EPI 44668–44669).

11. "If Athanasius's side came here to speak against him so, he said, their accusers must be telling the truth, for if they played the tyrant here, they did much more at home." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** "Εἰ ἐνταῦϑα ἤλϑετε καὶ πρὸς ἡμᾶς τὰ τοιαῦτα ἀντιλέγετε, ἄρα οὖν ἀληϑεύουσιν οἱ κατήγοροι ὑμῶν. Εἰ γὰρ ὧδε τυραννεῖτε, πολλῷ μᾶλλον ἐν τῇ ὑμῶν πατρίδι" (EPI 44608–44613). Who the plural "you" is: "ἐκλήϑησαν ἐπίσκοποι τῆς Αἰγύπτου … ὑπὸ Ἀθανάσιον … Ἐν οἷς ἦν … Ποτάμων" (EPI 44483–44489). The narrator's "Athanasius's side" is a fair name for them.

12. "Socrates says the council removed Athanasius as bishop when its inquiry in Egypt sided with his accuser." **HELD.** Socrates I.31, "those who were sent to Mareotis, having made an ex parte investigation, held that what the accuser said was true" (npnf202 5185–5199). I.32 continues, "when the result of the enquiry which had been instituted at Mareotis was presented, they voted to depose him" (5208–5211). Mareotis is in Egypt: "Alexandria near Lake Mareotis" (Sozomen I.17, npnf202 27741). The sentence gives no vote for Eusebius, as the Do not say list requires.

13. "In its pages envy, like a dark cloud, stirs up quarrels in Egypt's churches, and the emperor calls the bishops to Tyre against it, while Athanasius and Potamo go unnamed." **HELD.** Life IV.41 has "that spirit of envy … like a dark cloud … by again raising contentions to disturb the tranquillity of the Egyptian churches". It goes on: the emperor "convened a synod … against this malignant spirit … at the capital city of Phœnicia", and the chapter title and IV.42 name Tyre (npnf201 67210–67240). I searched IV.41–47 (67200–67420) for "Athanas" and "Potam" and found neither.

14. "Jerome, writing in Latin, called his History fine, and called him the most open champion of Arius's ungodly teaching." **HELD.** Letter 84, "Eusebius has composed a fine history of the Church; yet … the latter is the most open champion of the Arian impiety" (npnf206_jerome-principal-works.xml 18977–18983). "Arius's ungodly teaching" is the plain sense of "the Arian impiety".
