## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing

- **S1, BLOCKING.** "His plan, in his own list, was to trace the lines of bishops..." It follows straight on "His friend in Caesarea was Pamphilus, a priest there." Heard once, "His plan" belongs to Pamphilus, so the History's plan is given to the wrong man. This loses the thread of the story's central work. Change: "Eusebius's plan, in his own words, was to trace the lines of bishops in the great churches, the men who spoke or wrote for the faith, those who led others into error, and those who stood firm under torture down to his own day."
- **S2, BLOCKING.** "If they came here to speak against him so, he said, their accusers must be telling the truth, for if they played the tyrant here, they did much more at home." Three "they"s and a "their" with no name; the listener cannot tell who accuses whom, at the climax of the story. Change: "If Athanasius's side came here to accuse him like this, he said, the charges against them must be true, for if they played the tyrant here, they did much more at home."
- **MINOR.** "Those before him, he wrote, had left only brief fragments, voices calling from far off like torches from a watchtower." Voices like torches is a mixed image heard once; the listener loses whether these are sounds or lights. Change: "Those before him, he wrote, had left only brief fragments, like torches seen from far off, or voices calling from a watchtower." (only if the outline's rendering allows; otherwise leave).
- **MINOR.** "The first to try, he believed, he built his History from the letters, lists and books of the Christians before him." The doubled "he believed, he built" trips the ear, and the first claim repeats the opening quote. Change: "He built his History from the letters, lists and books of the Christians before him."
- **MINOR.** "Eusebius asked what it meant, and after questions and answers he signed, he wrote, for the sake of peace and so as not to fall from the right belief." The inserted "he wrote" sounds like a second act (he signed and he wrote). Change: "Eusebius asked what it meant, and after questions and answers, he wrote, he signed for the sake of peace and so as not to fall from the right belief."
- **MINOR.** "Socrates began his own history of the church with what Eusebius had left out..." "What Eusebius had left out" is unclear heard once: what he omitted, or where he stopped? Change to the plain sense the outline gives (e.g. "began his own history of the church where Eusebius had stopped").

The 44-word list sentence (craft check, s12) is understood as a list once S1's "His" is fixed; it is not counted.

## 4. Bare statements (Part 1, Explain it)

- **B1, BLOCKING.** "They said a cup used at the Lord's Supper had been broken, and a priest's hand cut off." The charge does not say who was charged with doing these, nor why a broken cup was grave. A listener with no church background hears a list of damages, not an accusation against Athanasius. Change, as the outline holds it: "They said that men acting for him had broken a holy cup used at the Lord's Supper, and cut off a priest's hand." If the outline does not hold the agent, the trust reader rules on the wording.
- **B2, BLOCKING.** "The council removed Athanasius." Removed from what, and on what ground, are not said. Change: "The council removed Athanasius as bishop of Alexandria", with the ground the old sources give, if the outline holds one; if they give only the act, tell it as they give it.

**Lines that sound machine-made: 6** (MINOR each; for the reviser):
1. Paragraph 2, "He lived through the emperors' persecution of the church and wrote the emperor Constantine's Life. At Nicaea he signed... At Tyre a bishop... charged... Later historians began their books where his History ended." This reads as an abstract of the heart turned into prose, item by item, each later told in full. The listener hears the table of contents before the story.
2. The witness formula, four times the same shape: name, a participle clause dating him, then "says/calls": "Socrates, writing a century later, says...", "Athanasius, then a deacon of Alexandria who was there, says...", "Epiphanius, telling it about forty years later and on Athanasius's side, calls...", "Jerome, writing in Latin, called...". A human narrator varies these. Change at least one or two, e.g. "A century later the historian Socrates tells it differently: Eusebius held back a short time before he signed."
3. The gloss-by-apposition template, stacked: "the creed of a council of bishops, its statement of faith"; "to sacrifice, to make an offering to the Roman gods"; "a confessor, a Christian who had suffered..."; "the Greek homoousios, saying...". Each is clear; together they sound like a glossary pattern. Change the first: "he signed the council's statement of faith, its creed, after asking what its words meant", or work one gloss into the action.
4. "Eusebius saw it, and he calls the leaders of the churches shepherds:" Scaffolding before a quote, with a slip into present tense. Change: "Eusebius saw it, and wrote of the church's leaders as shepherds:"
5. "Later historians began their books where his History ended." A narrator's verdict with no witness; it sounds like a modern consensus line (the craft check flags it, s10, and by Part 1 it should rest on a named old witness). Change: "Socrates and other later historians of the church began their books where his ended."
6. The close: "Jerome, writing in Latin, called his History fine, and called him the open champion of Arius's teaching." Praise-then-blame balanced in one sentence is the tidy two-sided wrap a model reaches for. It is a later writer's verdict, not the subject. Part 1 allows ending on a later writer only when that is the story's point; here the point is that later historians continued his book, which the Socrates sentence carries. Suggest ending on the Socrates sentence, or on "He died after the emperor, in his sons' reign," with Jerome's judgment moved to the card. MINOR, the reviser's call.
