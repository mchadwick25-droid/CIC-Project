## The three BLOCKING sentences

**1. ¶7: "When he saw Eusebius seated as judge and Athanasius standing, he wept, then spoke."** SOFTENED OR CHANGED.
- Source, EPI 44584–44590: "καταπονηϑεὶς τῇ λύπῃ, καὶ δακρύσας … ἀπετείνατο φωνῇ μεγάλῃ Εὐσεβίῳ λέγων". Petavius's Latin, EPI 44671–44672: "dolore confectus, et illacrymans … magna voce adversus Eusebium invectus est". So he was worn down with grief, wept, and *cried out at Eusebius with a loud voice*.
- The brief has "then spoke". That drops the loud cry aimed at Eusebius, which is the force of the scene in Epiphanius.
- Change: "When he saw Eusebius seated as judge and Athanasius standing, he wept, then cried out at him." (or "then cried out to him in a loud voice").

**2. ¶9: "In its pages Tyre is a cloud of envy over the churches of Egypt, and Athanasius and Potamo go unnamed."** SOFTENED OR CHANGED.
- Source, VC IV.41, EUS 67217–67227: "that spirit of envy which is the enemy of all good, like a dark cloud intercepting the sun's brightest rays, endeavored to mar the joy of this festivity, by again raising contentions to disturb the tranquillity of the Egyptian churches. - In the Life, envy is the cloud and the quarrels in Egypt are its work. Tyre is the emperor's answer to it, his "host of God". The brief makes Tyre itself the cloud, which turns Eusebius's picture of the council upside down.
- The second half holds: neither Athanasius nor Potamo is named in VC IV.41–42 (no "Athanasius" or "Potam-" in the VC text of EUS outside the editors' notes).
- Change: "In its pages envy, like a dark cloud, stirs up quarrels in the churches of Egypt, and the emperor calls the bishops to Tyre against it; Athanasius and Potamo go unnamed."
- Outline: B7 has "In it Tyre is a cloud of envy over the churches of Egypt", so the outline left room for it. Do not say line: "Tyre is a cloud of envy" → "envy, like a dark cloud, stirs up quarrels in Egypt's churches, and the emperor calls the council against it" (VC IV.41, EUS 67217–67227).

## SUMMARY HOLDS (11)

- ¶1 s1, "opened his History of the Church with an admission": HE I.1.4, EUS 6934–6935, "for I confess that it is beyond my power…". This is the first chapter, after the plan; "admission" is the sense of "confess" here.
- ¶1 s3, "brief fragments, voices calling from far off like torches from a watchtower": EUS 6952–6960, "except in brief fragments … From afar they raise their voices like torches, and they cry out, as from some lofty and conspicuous watch-tower".
- ¶2 s1, the plan "from Christ to his own day: its bishops, its writers, its quarrels and its persecutions": HE I.1.1–3, EUS 6878–6901 ("from the days of our Saviour to our own … those who have governed … proclaimed the divine word … run into the greatest errors … contended for it in the face of blood and of tortures"). "On the coast of Roman Palestine" is a place gloss of "Cæsarea in Palestine" (SOC 2972).
- ¶2 s2, "The first to try, he believed, he built his History from the letters, lists and books of the Christians before him": "the first", EUS 6935–6936, credited. Letters: EUS 35434–35443 (the Jerusalem library) and 31310–31316 (Polycrates). Lists: HE V.6, EUS 27965ff., Irenaeus's "Catalogue of the Bishops of Rome" given whole. Books: EUS 6962–6965, "passages from ancient writers".
- ¶2 s5, "At Tyre a bishop who had suffered in the persecution charged that he had got out of prison by giving way": EPI 44598–44604, "Πῶς ἀνεχώρησας ἀπὸ τῆς φυλακῆς, εἰ μὴ ὅτι ὑπέσχου … τὸ ἀϑέμιτον πρᾶξαι, ἢ ἔπραξας;". It is told as a charge. Epiphanius is credited in ¶6, before the scene. Not a finding.
- ¶3 s2, his plan "in his own list": HE I.1.1–3, EUS 6878–6901, as above. "Led others into error" fairly gathers "run into the greatest errors, and … like fierce wolves unmercifully devastated the flock" (6887–6891). The plan's item on the Jewish nation is left out, which shortens his list but changes no part of it.
- ¶4 s1, the orders of 303, "church leaders were jailed and forced by every means to sacrifice": EUS 43482–43491 (churches leveled, Scriptures burned); 43578–43580, 43637 ("rulers of the churches … first thrown into prison … and afterwards by every artifice be compelled to sacrifice"). That it was done as ordered: HE VIII.6.9, EUS 44095–44096, "the prisons everywhere … were filled with bishops". 303 is the nineteenth year of Diocletian (GRK 13717–13724), Widely Accepted.
- ¶4 s5, his rule: HE VIII.2.3, EUS 43448–43453, "we shall not mention those who were shaken by the persecution … only those events which may be usefull first to ourselves and afterwards to posterity".
- ¶5 s1, "By the council at Nicaea in 325 he was bishop, and the church was divided over whether God's Son had always been, or had a beginning, as Arius taught": bishop before 325, EUS 2058–2060 ("in the presbytery, and in the Episcopate itself"). The quarrel: THD 6031–6034 and 6045–6046 (Arius: bishops of the East "condemned because they say that God had an existence prior to that of His Son"; "we say that the Son has a beginning").
- ¶5 s5, "Athanasius, then a deacon of Alexandria who was there, says he had refused the day before": ATHG 74624–74625, "πρὸ μιᾶς ἀρνούμενος, ὅμως ὕστερον ὑπογράψας" (ATH 26780–26781, "who had denied the day before, but afterwards subscribed"). "Refused" is the sense of ἀρνούμενος when the act is signing. The deacon at the council: SOC 2793–2796.
- ¶6 s3, "Epiphanius, telling it about forty years later and on Athanasius's side, calls them slanders and says the emperor ordered Eusebius to judge, with others": EPI 44476–44480, "ov μὴν ἤδει ὅτι συκοφάνται ἦσαν … Ἐκέλευσε δὲ δικάζειν Εὐσέβιον τὸν Καισαρείας, καὶ ἄλλους τινάς". He calls the accusers slanderers, so the charges are slanders. Date: EPI line 9 (374–377). On Athanasius's side: "Athanasio papae", EPI 44527–44528.

## The heart

The heart is right for him, from what the sources show. He is a teacher and thinker whose one great work is the History, built from the church's own papers and continued by later historians (I1). The story's question, a teller of others' trials whose own conduct was charged, stands on his own rule (E10), Potamo (E22) and the bishops of Egypt (E24). It is told as charges, as the old sources give them.
