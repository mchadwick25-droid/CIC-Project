## 1. The test (person kit)

- **Who he was:** filled. A bishop of Caesarea in Roman Palestine who wrote the first history of the church.
- **What happened:** filled. He wrote the History, saw the persecution, signed at Nicaea after asking what the word meant, was charged at Tyre by a confessor, and wrote Constantine's Life.
- **What it was about:** filled. Telling the church's past from its own letters and lists. At Nicaea, whether the Son always was or had a beginning. At Tyre, whether he got out of prison by giving in.
- **Why the connection matters:** filled, thinly. "Later historians began their books where his History ended" and the last paragraph (Socrates starting where he stopped) carry it. A listener gets that he started a chain of history-writing. See the craft-check note on s10: "later historians" can be heard as modern scholars.

## 2. The opening

After sentence 3 ("...like torches from a watchtower"), I know **who** (Eusebius of Caesarea, writer of a History of the Church) and why he matters (first to try). Missing:
- **Where:** "Caesarea" is only a name. It is placed in sentence 4 ("on the coast of Roman Palestine"), so I am not lost. Not blocking.
- **When:** no century or era until "In 303", in paragraph 4, about 290 words in. Until then I could not tell if this was the year 300 or 1300. "The emperors' persecution" and "Constantine" mean nothing to a visitor without church background. MINOR. Change, if the outline holds the date: in sentence 4, "Eusebius, bishop of Caesarea on the coast of Roman Palestine around the year 300, set out..."
- **Why there:** not given in the opening, and not needed at once. The persecution paragraph answers it.

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing (all MINOR)

1. "The first to try, he believed, he built his History from the letters, lists and books of the Christians before him." The fronted phrase parses as "he believed the first to try" on first hearing; I lost who believed what for a moment. Change: "He believed he was the first to try, and he built his History from the letters, lists and books of the Christians before him."
2. "At Tyre a bishop who had suffered in the persecution charged that he had got out of prison by giving way." Two losses: "he" could be the accusing bishop, and "giving way" is too soft for me to hear as "giving in to the persecutors." Change: "...charged that Eusebius had got out of prison by giving in."
3. "There, Eusebius wrote to his church, the emperor urged one added word, the Greek homoousios, saying the Son is of the same substance as the Father." The inserted "Eusebius wrote to his church" broke the sentence; "saying" could be the emperor or the word. Change: "Eusebius wrote to his church that the emperor urged one added word there, the Greek homoousios. It said the Son is of the same substance as the Father."
4. "Socrates, writing a century later, says he held back a short time before signing." I heard the Greek philosopher Socrates, who lived long before. The meaning lost is who this is. Change: "Socrates, a church historian writing a century later, says..."
5. "If they came here to speak against him so, he said, their accusers must be telling the truth, for if they played the tyrant here, they did much more at home." I lost who "they" were and whose accusers. I could not tell that Eusebius turned the charge back on Athanasius's side. The thread (he rose angry and broke up the court) holds, so not blocking. Change: "If Athanasius's party came here to accuse him like this, he said, then the charges against them must be true: if they bullied people here, they did much worse at home."
6. "In his History he set himself a rule: he would not record those who had been shaken by the persecution, only what would help his readers and those after them." "Shaken" can mean frightened. I did not hear it as "those who gave in." That matters, because the Tyre charge is about exactly that. Change: "...he would not record those who had given way in the persecution, only..." (only if the outline holds that sense).

The 44-word plan sentence (s12) is long but a clear parallel list; I understood it. Not counted.

## 4. Bare statements

1. **BLOCKING.** "The council removed Athanasius." A punishment with no reason and no plain sense. Removed from what? For what? Just before, Epiphanius calls the charges slanders, so the listener cannot tell if Athanasius was found guilty of the cup and the hand, or of something else. Change: tell the reason as the old sources give it, and say "removed him as bishop" (from the outline). If the outline holds no reason, tell the act as the sources give it, "The council removed Athanasius as bishop of Alexandria", with no reason added. The trust read confirms which.

## 5. Citations read aloud not called for (MINOR)

1. "His plan, in his own list, was to trace..." "In his own list" is a citation; the narrator can say it. Change: "He planned to trace..." (see the s12 split below).
2. "Eusebius saw it, and he calls the leaders of the churches shepherds:" This frames the quote as a report about the record. Change: "Eusebius saw it:" and let the quote carry "shepherds" (it is plain from context).

The credits for Eusebius's letter, Socrates, Athanasius, Epiphanius and the bishops of Egypt are called for: the old sources differ, a judgment is credited, or an enemy's charge is given.

## 6. Tells

None counted. No dash, no stock word, no question asked only to answer it, no moral close.

Flagged for the reviewer, judged not a tell: "...and Athanasius and Potamo go unnamed." Half the sentence says what is missing. Here, though, the gap is itself the story: his own book is silent on the charge against him. I heard it as a point, not filler.

## 7. Words, runs, tone

Unexplained words (MINOR):
- **Nicaea**: never placed. I did not know it was a town, or where. Change: "At Nicaea, a town in what is now Turkey," (or the outline's plain word).
- **Tyre**: never placed. Change: "Tyre, a city on the coast north of Caesarea," (or the outline's plain word).
- **the Lord's Supper**: a church word I do not know. Change: "a cup used for the bread and wine of worship".
- **martyr** in Potamo's speech: half-known. Context ("lost an eye", "nothing cut away") carries it. Note only.
- **Socrates**: see 3.4.
- **played the tyrant**: an idiom; see 3.5.

Names: Eusebius, Pamphilus, Constantine, Arius, Athanasius, Socrates, Epiphanius, Potamo, Jerome: nine. I kept them apart, but Epiphanius and Jerome were hard to place on one hearing.

No run of short one-act sentences (Potamo's short sentences are his own speech and work). Nothing talks down.

## 8. Pull, drift, finish

- **Opening:** pulls me in. A man saying he is first and alone on a path, with torches far off, is a human start.
- **First drift:** paragraph 3, "His friend in Caesarea was Pamphilus, a priest there." After the essence I expected the story to move. Instead it stepped back to a friend who does nothing yet, then a 44-word list of plans. Why, for the reviser: two setup sentences in a row with no event. The persecution quote brought me back at once.
- **Second, smaller dip:** the Tyre set-up paragraph (charges against a new name, then a witness credited before the scene).
- **Would I listen to the end:** yes. Potamo's question in prison terms is the strongest moment, and I wanted to hear how Eusebius answered.

## Craft-check lines (`tools/check_brief_craft.py`)

- **s10, modern-scholar word:** "Later historians began their books where his History ended." By Part 1 this is a fault of hearing, not of rigor: "later historians" sounds like modern scholars, though the last paragraph shows they are old church historians. MINOR. Change: "Church historians of the next century began their books where his History ended." (The trust read confirms the outline holds this.)
- **s12, 44 words:** a clear list, understood on one hearing; over the target by 14. MINOR. Change: "He planned to trace the lines of bishops in the great churches, and the men who spoke or wrote for the faith. He would also tell of those who led others into error, and those who stood firm under torture down to his own day."
