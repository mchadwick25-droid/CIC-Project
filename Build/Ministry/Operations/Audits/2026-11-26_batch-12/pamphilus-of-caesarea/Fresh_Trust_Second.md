"Eusebius of Caesarea wrote of how his friend Pamphilus gave what he could to poor book lovers:" **HELD.** Eusebius's Life, in Jerome's Latin: "Si quos videbat ad victum necessariis indigere, praebebat large quae poterat" (JERL 31257-31258); "my dearest friend Pamphilus" (EUS 46694-46695). The outline's Opening 1 holds this wording.

The new Do not say line is followed: no word on whether they met. Note, not a finding: told from 310, the death falls in the century before; the sentence is the outline's own (X11, Do not say).

"Eusebius ... says he shared his possessions with the needy and was devoted above all to the Scriptures." **HELD.** "for sharing his possessions with the needy ... most sincere devotion to the Divine Scriptures" (EUS 47072-47080). Note, not a finding: the outline's Also told lists the sharing, though Beat 1 and Cut first 5 send it to the card.

12. "...tested Pamphilus in public speaking and philosophy, then tried to force him to sacrifice." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** "τῆς ἐν ῥητοριχοῖς λόγοις φιλοσόφοις τε μαϑήμασιν ἀπόπειραν ... εἶϑ᾽ ὕστερον ϑύειν χαταναγχάσας" (GRK 24095-24098); "public speaking" is a fair plain word for rhetorical speeches.

13. "He refused and ignored his threats." **HELD.** "he refused and in nowise regarded his threats" (EUS 46697-46700; GRK 24098-24099).

16. "Photius of Constantinople, a scholar five centuries later, says Pamphilus wrote most of it in prison." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** "It is in six books, five of which were written by Pamphilus when in prison in the company of Eusebius" (PHOT 11437-11439). Five of six is "most"; credited as the outline requires.

17. "It answered charges that Origen taught that the soul lived before the body, and that he made Christ either a mere man or one whose deeds only seemed to happen." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** "quod ante corpus eam factam dicat exsistere" (APL 20291-20293); "purum hominem ... dicere Christum" (APL 17661-17664); "quae istis omnibus adversatur ... δοκήσει, id est, putative tantum ... gesta esse omnia quae a Salvatore gesta sunt" (APL 17665-17670). "Either ... or" keeps the Defense's point that the charges contradict each other.

18. "The Defense set out Origen's own words with replies between." (moved earlier) **HELD.** Headings "ORIGENES." and "PAMPHILUS." (APL 14264, 14271, 14273, 14334).

19. "It was written to confessors condemned to forced labor in the mines of Palestine." **HELD.** "Praefatio ad Confessores ad metalla Palaestinae damnatos" (APL 14112-14115). "Copper" is gone, as the Do not say line asks.

20. "Readers of pagan books, and even of heretics' books, whose teaching the church condemns, are usually pardoned, it says." **HELD.** "ne ea quidem, quam impertire solet ... his, qui Graecorum saecularium libros, vel nonnunquam etiam haereticorum ... decurrunt" (APL 14124-14139). The gloss on heretic is the outline's (X7).

21. "But Origen's accusers think that they alone have the skill to test what they read, keeping the good like a money-changer who tests each coin and throws out the false." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** "sibi solis scilicet concedi debere peritiam probandi sermonis putant, ut si quid bene ab aliquo dictum est, retinere sciant" (APL 14139-14142). The coin image is the outline's plain-sense gloss (X8), set up before Quote 2.

27. "Each gave the same answer, and he sentenced them to death, as he had others before them." **HELD.** "receiving from every one of them only this one answer ... he inflicted on them punishment similar to the others" (EUS 47195-47198); the man before them "passed against him sentence of death" (EUS 47186). No sword, per Do not say.

29. "Eusebius took Pamphilus's name as a surname." **HELD.** "His surname Pamphilus arose from his friendship for Pamphilus the martyr" (JER 41076-41077). Undated, as the Do not say line asks.

30. "Eusebius sent readers to the library's catalog ..., and Jerome used the library in his day." **HELD.** "a catalogue of the library ... Whoever desires may learn readily from this which of Origen's works have reached us" (EUS 36890-36894); "preserved until the present day in the library at Cæsarea which Pamphilus so diligently gathered" (JER 39359-39361).

32. "...Jerome complained that readers ignored bishops who had since condemned Origen as a heretic, because they thought a martyr had praised him." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** "they give no heed to the authority of the bishops who condemn Origen, since they think that a martyr has praised him. Of what use are the letters of the bishop Theophilus or of the pope Anastasius, who follow out the heretic in every part of the world" (JER 51453-51458). "Since" holds: Jerome names the bishops of his own day, writing about ninety years after the martyr. "Thought" is kept.
