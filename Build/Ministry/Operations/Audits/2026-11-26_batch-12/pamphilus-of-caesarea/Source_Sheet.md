## 1. Setting

S1. **Caesarea, presbyter.** "In his time we became acquainted with Pamphilus, that most eloquent man, of truly philosophical life, who was esteemed worthy of the office of presbyter in that parish." (EUS 43009-43010, 43036-43038, HE VII.32.25; Greek GRK 12921-12923, "πρεσβείου τῆς αὐτόθι παροικίας ἠξιωμένον Πάμφιλον ἔγνωμεν"). "Pamphilus, a presbyter, who was the great glory of the parish of Cæsarea, and among the men of our time most admirable." (EUS 44790-44792, HE VIII.13.6; GRK 15464-15466). CONTEMPORARY. Documented. Plain words: "Caesarea, a port on the coast of Roman Palestine" (CEN `placeLabel` "Caesarea Maritima"; Eusebius "Cæsarea in Palestine", EUS 42996). The "great glory" line stands in CEN `legacy`; not quoted.

S2. **A school of his own.** Eusebius wrote in his separate work "all the particulars of his life, and of the school which he established, and the trials which he endured in many confessions during the persecution, and the crown of martyrdom" (EUS 43043-43045; Greek GRK 12925, "ἧς συνεστήσατο διατριβῆς"). CONTEMPORARY. Documented; nothing is held of what the school taught.

S3. **The time.** The persecution in Palestine began "in the nineteenth year of the reign of Diocletian, in the month Xanthicus" with letters "commanding that the churches be leveled to the ground and the Scriptures be destroyed by fire" (EUS 45892-45911, MP intro). Later "imperial edicts were first issued to [Urbanus], commanding by a general decree that all the people should sacrifice at once in the different cities, and offer libations to the idols" (EUS 46170-46173, MP 4.8). The persecution's reason: Eusebius gives none here. The year of the start, 303, is Widely Accepted (EUS-ed 45897-45900). Pamphilus's arrest in the fifth year (EUS 46623-46624, MP 7.1 "when the persecution had continued to the fifth year"; 46694-46709), his death after two whole years in prison (EUS 47106-47107). Year of death: 309 (EUS-ed 43031; JERL-ed 58607-58608, "an. 309, ... Februarii"; JER 40958 editor's "Died 309") or 310 (EUS-ed 47122-47131, which calls 309 "a mistake"); CEN `dates` "307-310". Plain words: "about 310"; Widely Accepted only as "about 310" (both reckonings fall there); the exact year Contested among editors (M1).

S4. **The day.** "On the next day, which was the nineteenth of the month Peritius ... according to the Roman reckoning the fourteenth before the Kalends of March, they were brought ... before the judge, with Pamphilus and his associates" (EUS 47121-47134, MP 11.7). Documented: a day in February. The editor's gloss "Feb. 19" (47122) is his.

S5. **Rome's officials.** Urbanus, governor (EUS 46165-46171, 46700-46709); Firmilian, a later governor, who condemned him (EUS 47147, 47191-47198; EUS-ed 43030-43031, "the successor of Urbanus"). Plain words: "the Roman governor", "a new governor". Documented.

S6. **Tradition.** CEN `toldWithin` = `alexandria-catechetical`; spoken name (`informalName`) "Alexandrian Christianity". His city's own entry: `palestinian-church-pre-constantinian`, spoken name "Caesarea and Its Library". The slate rules: told within Alexandria in the census, but the story tells Caesarea as the record gives it (no census change). CEN `relationsSummary`: "He is said to have studied at Alexandria." That rests on Photius only (D4).

S7. **Origin and family (one witness, damaged).** MP-long: "ὡρμᾶτο μὲν οὖν ἐκ τῆς Βηρυτίων πόλεως, ἔνθα τὴν πρώτην ἡλικίαν τοῖς αὐτόθι τέθραπτο παιδευτηρίοις" ("He came from the city of Berytus, where he was brought up in his first years in the schools there", GRK 24841-24842); "ἐπεὶ δὲ τὰ τῆς φρονήσεως εἰς τελείους ἄνδρας αὐτῶι προήιει, μετέβαινεν ἀπὸ τῶνδε ἐπὶ τὴν τῶν ἱερῶν λόγων ἐπιστήμην" ("when his understanding came to full manhood, he moved from these to the knowledge of the sacred words", GRK 24842-24844); of noble birth and distinguished in his home city's public offices: "τοῦ μὲν Παμφίλου ἐξ εὐπατριδῶν κατάγοντος τὸ κατὰ σάρκα γένος ἐπισήμως τε ταῖς κατὰ τὴν πατρίδα πολιτείαις διαπρέψαντος" (GRK 24682-24683). Berytus is today's Beirut. CONTEMPORARY if the longer form is Eusebius's (EUS-ed 3041-3042, "That Eusebius himself was the author of the original cannot be doubted": an editor's judgment, M2). One damaged print, no other held. Documented in the longer form only; for the card.

## 2. Events, in time order

E1. **Gave away what he had.** MP 11.2: "a man who through his entire life was celebrated for every virtue, for renouncing and despising the world, for sharing his possessions with the needy, for contempt of earthly hopes, and for philosophic deportment and exercise. He especially excelled all in our time in most sincere devotion to the Divine Scriptures and indefatigable industry in whatever he undertook, and in his helpfulness to his relatives and associates." (EUS 47072-47080). MP-long, fuller: "ἀποδόμενός γέ τοι τὰ εἰς αὐτὸν ἐκ προγόνων ἥκοντα γυμνοῖς, πηροῖς καὶ πένησιν τὰ πάντα διένειμεν, αὐτὸς δὲ ἐν ἀκτήμονι διῆγε βίωι" ("he sold what came to him from his forebears and gave it all out to the naked, the maimed and the poor, and himself lived a life without possessions", GRK 24838-24840). CONTEMPORARY. Sharing with the needy: Documented (shorter form, English). Selling the inheritance: Documented in the longer form only, damaged print.

E2. **The library.** Eusebius, HE VI.32.3: "After showing how great the diligence of Pamphilus was in divine things, we give in that [the Life] a catalogue of the library which he collected of the works of Origen and of other ecclesiastical writers. Whoever desires may learn readily from this which of Origen's works have reached us." (EUS 36890-36894; Greek GRK 5152-5156, "τῆς συναχθείσης αὐτῷ τῶν τε Ὠριγένους καὶ τῶν ἄλλων ἐκκλησιαστικῶν συγγραφέων βιβλιοθήκης τοὺς πίνακας"). Jerome, letter to Marcella, quoted by Rufinus (RUF, JER 49608-49618): "The blessed Martyr Pamphilus ... wished to rival Demetrius Phalereus and Pisistratus, in his zeal to establish a library of sacred books: he sought out all through the world representative works of great minds ... but most of all he acquired at great expense all the books written by Origen, and gave them to the church at Cæsarea." Jerome, DVI 3: the Hebrew Matthew "has been preserved until the present day in the library at Cæsarea which Pamphilus so diligently gathered" (JER 39359-39361; Latin JERL 48159-48161, "in Caesariensi bibliotheca, quam Pamphilus martyr studiosissime confecit"). CONTEMPORARY (Eusebius) and LATER (Jerome). Documented: he gathered it, for the church, at great cost (Jerome), with a catalogue (Eusebius). Demetrius Phalereus and Pisistratus (founders of famous pagan libraries) need a gloss; not for the story.

E3. **Copying Origen's books by hand.** Jerome, DVI 75: "Pamphilus the presbyter, patron of Eusebius bishop of Cæsarea, was so inflamed with love of sacred literature, that he transcribed the greater part of the works of Origen with his own hand and these are still preserved in the library at Cæsarea." (JER 40958-40962). Latin: "Pamphilus presbyter, Eusebii Caesariensis episcopi necessarius, tanto Bibliothecae divinae amore flagravit, ut maximam partem Origenis voluminum sua manu descripserit, quae usque hodie in Caesariensi bibliotheca habentur." (JERL 58486-58490, printed "Cesariensis", "Bibliothece divine"). "Bibliotheca divina" is Scripture (JERL-ed 58553-58554). Photius: "is said to have copied most of Origen's commentaries on Scripture with his own hand" (PHOT 11510-11512). LATER. Documented as Jerome's report; "the greater part", never "all".
- **Quote candidate J1**, DVI 75 continued: "I have twenty-five volumes of Commentaries of Origen, written in his hand, On the twelve prophets which I hug and guard with such joy, that I deem myself to have the wealth of Croesus. And if it is such joy to have one epistle of a martyr how much more to have so many thousand lines which seem to me to be traced in his blood." (JER 40962-40968). Latin: "Sed et in duodecim Prophetas viginti quinque Ἐξηγήσεων Origenis volumina, manu ejus exarata reperi, quae tanto amplector et servo gaudio, ut Croesi opes habere me credam. Si enim laetitia est, unam epistolam habere martyris, quanto magis tot millia versuum, quae mihi videtur sui sanguinis signasse vestigiis!" (JERL 58490-58497; printed "vinginti", "Cresi", "letitia", "que"). A variant: "per quae mihi videtur sui sanguinis signasse vestigia" (JERL-ed 58587-58588). Sophronius's Greek beside it ends "τὰ ἴχνη τοῦ οἰκείου καταλέλοιπε πάθους" ("he has left the traces of his own suffering", JERL 58506-58507). "Manu ejus": in his hand; the sentence's subject is Pamphilus. Latin readable with letter faults; the 1892 English confirms. LATER (about 80 years). Documented as Jerome's words.

E4. **What Eusebius wrote of him as a giver of books (the lost Life).** Jerome, Apol. I.9, quoting Book III of Eusebius's *Life of Pamphilus*: "What lover of books was there who did not find a friend in Pamphilus? If he knew of any of them being in want of the necessaries of life, he helped them to the full extent of his power. He would not only lend them copies of the Holy Scriptures to read, but would give them most readily, and that not only to men, but to women also if he saw that they were given to reading. He therefore kept a store of manuscripts, so that he might be able to give them to those who wished for them whenever occasion demanded. He himself however, wrote nothing whatever of his own, except private letters which he sent to his friends, so humble was his estimate of himself. But the treatises of the old writers he studied with the greatest diligence, and was constantly occupied in meditation upon them." (JER 51429-51442). Latin: "Quis studiosorum amicus non fuit Pamphili? Si quos videbat ad victum necessariis indigere, praebebat large quae poterat. Scripturas quoque sanctas non ad legendum tantum, sed et ad habendum, tribuebat promptissime. Nec solum viris, sed et feminis, quas vidisset lectioni deditas. Unde et multos codices praeparabat, ut cum necessitas poposcisset, volentibus largiretur. Et ipse quidem proprii operis nihil omnino scripsit, exceptis epistolis, quas ad amicos forte mittebat; in tantum se humilitate dejecerat." (JERL 31256-31266; printed "prebebat", "qus", "Seripturas", "prepara-bat", "poposcissel"). CONTEMPORARY (Eusebius), held only in Jerome's Latin, which Jerome quotes as evidence against Pamphilus's authorship (D1). The Life is lost (EUS-ed 43032-43033). The Latin "et ad habendum" (to keep) is sharper than the English "lend". Documented as Eusebius's words in Jerome's quotation.
- **Quote candidate J2**: the sentences "Scripturas quoque ... volentibus largiretur" (JERL 31259-31264).

E5. **Arrest, testing and torture.** MP 7.4-6: "Yet others, after severe tortures, he cast into prison. Among these was my dearest friend Pamphilus, who was by reason of every virtue the most illustrious of the martyrs in our time. Urbanus first tested him in rhetorical philosophy and learning; and afterwards endeavored to compel him to sacrifice. But as he saw that he refused and in nowise regarded his threats, being exceedingly angry, he ordered him to be tormented with severest tortures. And when the brutal man, after he had almost satiated himself with these tortures by continuous and prolonged scrapings in his sides, was yet covered with shame before all, he put him also with the confessors in prison." (EUS 46691-46709). Greek: "ἐν οἷς καὶ ὁ πάντων ἐμοὶ γοῦν ποθεινότατος ἑταίρων Πάμφιλος ἦν" (GRK 24044-24045); "τούτου τῆς ἐν ῥητορικοῖς λόγοις φιλοσόφοις τε μαθήμασιν ἀπόπειραν πρότερον ὁ Οὐρβανὸς λαβών, εἶθ᾽ ὕστερον θύειν καταναγκάσας ... ταῖς κατὰ τῶν πλευρῶν αὐτοῦ ... ξυστήρων ... τοῖς ἐν τῷ δεσμωτηρίῳ καὶ αὐτὸν ὁμολογηταῖς καταλέγει" (GRK 24096-24106). Reason: the edict to sacrifice (S3) and his refusal; the testing in learning has no reason given. CONTEMPORARY. Documented. "Scrapings in his sides": tell as given; no tool is named.

E6. **The confessors and the mines.** Just before: the governor "condemned all of them to the copper mines in Phæno in Palestine"; others "were condemned by him to labor in the same copper mines, command being first given that their ankles be disabled with hot irons"; "Others who were in mature life he made eunuchs, and condemned them to the same mines." (EUS 46666-46692, MP 7.2-4). A confessor: one who had confessed the faith under trial and lived (EUS 46674-46675, Silvanus "a presbyter and confessor"). Documented.

E7. **The Defense, written in prison, with Eusebius.** Eusebius, HE VI.33.4: "But whatever it has seemed necessary to record about him can be found in the Apology in his behalf written by us and Pamphilus, the holy martyr of our day. We prepared this carefully and did the work jointly on account of faultfinders." (EUS 36973-36976). Greek: "ταῦτα καὶ ἐκ τῆς ὑπὲρ αὐτοῦ πεπονημένης ἡμῖν τε καὶ τῷ καθ᾽ ἡμᾶς ἱερῷ μάρτυρι Παμφίλῳ ἀπολογίας πάρεστιν ἀναλέξασθαι, ἣν τῶν φιλαιτίων ἕνεκα συμπονήσαντες ἀλλήλοις διὰ σπουδῆς πεποιήμεθα" (GRK 5259-5262; these lines readable in the scan). Photius: "It is in six books, five of which were written by Pamphilus when in prison in the company of Eusebius. The sixth is the work of Eusebius alone, after the martyr, having been deprived of life by the sword, was removed to God for whom his soul longed." (PHOT 11437-11442); "composed ... by Pamphilus when imprisoned together with Eusebius" (PHOT 11501-11502). EUS-ed: "During the two years of Pamphilus' imprisonment Eusebius spent a great deal of time with him ... There is, however, no other evidence that he was thus imprisoned, and in the face of Eusebius' own silence it is safer perhaps to assume (with most historians) that he simply visited Pamphilus in his prison." (EUS-ed 1480-1489). Documented: Pamphilus in prison; the two worked on it together (Eusebius's own word). Where Eusebius was: Contested (D2). That it was written in prison rests on Photius (late) and the editors; Eusebius says only that they wrote it together. Inferential-Thin from Eusebius alone; Documented in Photius. Tell: "with Eusebius, from prison" only as the slate's job line, credited where it matters (see Do not say).

E8. **To whom the Defense was written.** Rufinus's heading: "Praefatio ad Confessores ad metalla Palaestinae damnatos" (APL 14112-14115; APM 41938-41939). Photius: "addressed to those who were condemned to the mines for the sake of Christ, the chief of whom was Patermythius, who shortly after the death of Pamphilus ended his life at the stake with others" (PHOT 11502-11506). Documented. Plain words: "Christians who had held to the faith and been sent to forced labor in the mines of Palestine." Nothing held joins these readers to the men of E6 by name; do not join them.

E9. **What the Defense says, and how it is made.**
- Opening (APL 14118-14168; APM 41976-42046): the brothers have come to think of Origen as some others do, who out of ignorance cannot see the depth of his meaning, or out of malice not only accuse his words but take up hostile enmity against those who read them, granting his readers none of the pardon given to readers of pagan or even heretical books.
- **Quote candidate J3** (APL 14153-14158): "ab his vero, qui Origenis libros legunt, istud penitus exclusum putant esse mandatum, quo probabiles jubentur effici trapezitae, scientes quod bonum est retinere, ab omni autem se specie mala abstinere: sed tantummodo si legere libros illius visus fuerit, statim ab his haereticorum perfundetur infamia." APM 42029-42035 reads the same, with "si quis legere" for APL's "si legere", and OCR faults ("trapezitze", "autein", "h:ere-ticorum"). "Ab omni specie mala abstinere" echoes 1 Thessalonians 5:21-22 (APL note 14183). Both prints rough; they agree; no English held. Documented as the Defense's words in Rufinus's Latin; whose words: D1.
- Its first argument (APL 14208-14215; APM 42047-42056): Origen speaks "cum magno timore dei, et cum omni humilitate", asks pardon for what came to him in deep searching of the Scriptures, and often adds "se non haec quasi definitiva pronuntiare sententia, nec statuto dogmate terminare, sed inquirere pro viribus" ("that he does not pronounce these things as a final verdict, nor settle them as fixed doctrine, but searches as far as his strength allows"). "Cum omni humilitate et veritate non erubescit fateri haec sibi non liquere" ("with all humility and truth he is not ashamed to admit that these things are not clear to him", APL 14221-14222; APM agrees). This passage is the quote used in the existing card; narrate only.
- How it is made: it sets out Origen's own words under his name, with the defender's words between, headed "ORIGENES." and "PAMPHILUS." (APL 14264, 14271, 14273; 15243-15252: "Primum ergo ex ejus libris ponemus exemplum ... ex eo libro, quem in epistolam Pauli Apostoli ad Titum scripsit"). Rufinus, defending it: "He sets out quotations from the works of Origen himself, and exhibits his opinion upon each question not in the words of the apologist but in those of the accused himself" (RUF, JER 50214-50216). Documented.
- Photius's summary of the whole six books: fifteen counts against Origen, "which he declares to be mere slanders, proving it by quotations from his writings in his fourth book, and refuting them by the evidence of others on his behalf in the fifth" (PHOT 11400-11404); the counts listed (PHOT 11405-11428): that prayer should not be offered to the Son, that souls pass from body to body, that there is no eternal punishment nor resurrection of the flesh, and others. LATE. These are the accusers' charges in their own content, as Photius reports them. Card.
- Rufinus's charge (from the other side): Jerome says Rufinus changed "a few statements about the Son of God and the holy Spirit" (JER 51386-51388). J3 is not on those subjects.

E9a. **The charges, as the Defense itself names them.** Defense Book I, in Rufinus's Latin. The soul: "quae ei de anima objiciunt, id est, quod ante corpus eam factam dicat exsistere" ("what they charge him with about the soul, that is, that he says it was made and existed before the body", APL 20291-20293; printed "eam. fa-ctam"). The third charge: "quod dicunt eum, secundum Artemam vel Paulum Samosatenum, purum hominem, id est, non etiam Deum dicere Christum Filium Dei" ("that they say he, like Artemas or Paul of Samosata, calls Christ the Son of God a mere man, that is, not God as well", APL 17661-17664; printed "Saznosa-tenum", "purim"). The next, "quae istis omnibus adversatur (caeca enim est malitia)": "quod dicunt eum dicere, δοκήσει, id est, putative tantum, et per allegoriam, non etiam secundum ea, quae per historiam, gesta esse omnia quae a Salvatore gesta sunt" ("which opposes all these, for malice is blind: that they say he says all the Saviour's deeds were done in seeming, that is, only as supposed, and by allegory, not as history tells them", APL 17666-17670; printed "etiara"). The fourth, answered: "Responsio ad quartam criminationem. Adversum eos, qui dicunt eum per allegorias adimere omnia quae de Salvatore scripta sunt corporaliter facta" ("against those who say that by allegories he takes away everything written of the Saviour as done in the body", APL 18386-18390). CONTEMPORARY, the Defense's report of its accusers. Documented as the Defense reports the charges; whose words, D1. The accusers' own writings are not held.

E10. **The death.** MP 11.1-2: "It is time to describe the great and celebrated spectacle of Pamphilus, a man thrice dear to me, and of those who finished their course with him. They were twelve in all; being counted worthy of apostolic grace and number. Of these the leader and the only one honored with the position of presbyter at Cæsarea, was Pamphilus" (EUS 47067-47074). "After these persons had continued in prison for two entire years, the occasion of their martyrdom was a second arrival of Egyptian brethren who suffered with them." (EUS 47106-47109). Firmilian tortured the Egyptians (EUS 47131-47189); then "he proceeded against Pamphilus and his companions. And having learned that already under former tortures they had manifested an unchangeable zeal for the faith, he asked them if they would now obey. And receiving from every one of them only this one answer, as their last word of confession in martyrdom, he inflicted on them punishment similar to the others." (EUS 47191-47198). The Egyptian before him was sentenced "to death" (EUS 47186). MP-long: "παραπλησίως κεφαλικὴν τιμωρίαν κατ᾽ αὐτῶν ἐξενεγκάμενος ἐπάγει" ("he passed a capital sentence on them likewise", GRK 25243-25244). Photius: "deprived of life by the sword" (PHOT 11440-11441). Reason: they would not obey (sacrifice), as given. CONTEMPORARY. Put to death: Documented. By the sword: Photius (late) and the longer form's "capital" only; the shorter form says "similar".
- **The young man of his household.** "a young man, one of the household servants of Pamphilus, who had been educated in the noble life and instruction of such a man, learning the sentence passed upon his master, cried out from the midst of the crowd asking that their bodies might be buried" (EUS 47200-47204); asked only whether he was a Christian, scraped to the bone when he refused to sacrifice, and burned by slow fire, crying out once for the help of Jesus (EUS 47206-47245). He died "before the death of his earthly master" (EUS 47224). His name, Porphyry (EUS 47229-47233). MP-long: "Πορφύριος ἦν ὁ μακάριος, θρέμμα γνήσιον Παμφίλου οὐδ᾽ ὅλων ὀκτωκαίδεκα ἐτῶν, καλλιγραφικῆς ἐπιστήμης ἔμ-πειρος" ("a true foster-son of Pamphilus, not quite eighteen, skilled in the art of fine writing", GRK 25247-25248, 25310); "τὸ μὲν δοκεῖν τοῦ Παμφίλου γεγονὼς οἰκέτης, διαθέσει γε μὴν ἀδελφοῦ καὶ μᾶλλον γνησίου παιδὸς" ("in name Pamphilus's servant, but in affection a brother, or rather a true son", GRK 24696-24697). Documented (the shorter form); the fine writing and foster-son: longer form only, damaged.
- **The messenger.** Seleucus, a former soldier, reported Porphyry's death to Pamphilus and was put to death at once (EUS 47247-47254).
- **Eusebius's image.** "On this day, as was fitting, the chief gate was opened, and a ready way of entrance into the kingdom of heaven was given to the martyr Pamphilus and to the others with him." (EUS 47274-47278). Eusebius's own image; credit it.
- **The bodies (a wonder).** Kept four days and nights for the beasts, and "strange to say, through the providential care of God, nothing approached them" (EUS 47317-47323). A wonder in Eusebius's voice; leave out.

E11. **Eusebius's name and his Life.** Jerome, DVI 81: "His surname Pamphilus arose from his friendship for Pamphilus the martyr." (JER 41076-41077). The Letter 84 translator's endnote (modern, not Jerome): "Eusebius calls himself Eusebius Pamphili, that is, 'the friend of Pamphilus.'" (JER6 19417-19419). Eusebius wrote the Life in three books (EUS 47082-47085, MP 11.3; JER 41072-41073); lost (EUS-ed 43032-43033). EUS-ed 43011-43013: he took the name "after his death" (editor). LATER (Jerome). Documented as Jerome's report. The Eusebius story carries it; here a clause.

## 3. Explanations (Explain it), each side the texts give

X1. **Presbyter.** An elder of the church, a priest under the bishop. EUS 43037-43038 ("the office of presbyter in that parish"); MP 11.2 "the only one honored with the position of presbyter" (EUS 47073).
X2. **The library.** A collection of books gathered for the church at Caesarea: Origen's works and other church writers (E2, Eusebius), sought "all through the world" and bought "at great expense" (E2, Jerome). Why it mattered to the people in it: Eusebius sent readers to its catalogue to learn which of Origen's works survived (E2); Jerome found there books he could find nowhere else (I2).
X3. **The persecution and the sacrifice.** The emperors' orders, first to tear down churches and burn the Scriptures, then that "all the people should sacrifice ... and offer libations to the idols" (S3). To sacrifice: to offer to the Roman gods. Refusing it was what the governors punished (E5, E10). No motive for the emperors is given in these passages; tell as given.
X4. **The testing.** Urbanus first tested him "in rhetorical philosophy and learning" (E5): Eusebius gives no reason; tell as given.
X5. **Confessors; the mines.** Christians who had held to the faith under trial and lived; some were sent to forced labor in the copper mines of Phaeno in Palestine, lamed with hot irons (E6).
X6. **The Defense (Apology).** A book answering the charges made against Origen, a teacher of the century before (CEN `origen`). It set out Origen's own words, under his name, with the defender's comments between (E9). Why it mattered: its readers were being treated as heretics for reading Origen (J3). From the other side: the accusers' fifteen charges, as Photius lists them (E9), and Photius's own verdict that it "rather supports the accusation" (PHOT 11378-11382), card only; Jerome's charges against it (D1), card.
X7. **Heretic.** One who holds a teaching the church condemns. The Defense's own use (J3, APL 14158).
X8. **Money-changers.** In J3: a command to Christians to be "approved money-changers" ("probabiles trapezitae"), keeping what is good and refusing every kind of evil (APL 14155-14157), as one who tests coins keeps the good and throws out the false. The gloss on the coins is the plain sense of the word, not in the text; work it into the setup as "like a money-changer who tests coins".
X9. **The torture.** "Continuous and prolonged scrapings in his sides" (E5): tell as given.
X10. **Martyr.** One put to death for the faith; Eusebius's word for him and the eleven (EUS 46697, 47068-47070).
X11. **Origen.** One clause only: the Bible scholar who taught at Alexandria and then at Caesarea in the century before (CEN `origen`; Origen outline essence). Pamphilus never met him.

## 4. Impact, heaviest first (what later witnesses carried on and used)

I1. **The library, used and rebuilt after him.** Eusebius sent readers to its catalogue for Origen's works (E2). Jerome used it: the Hebrew Matthew (E2), the twenty-five volumes in Pamphilus's hand (E3), and Eusebius's six volumes on Origen, "in the library of Cæsarea I found the six volumes of Eusebius' Apology for Origen" (JER 54979-54980; Latin JERL 36197). Jerome, letter to Marcella: "This library was afterwards partly destroyed; but Acatius and later on Euzoius, Bishops of that church, endeavoured to reestablish it in parchment volumes. The last of these recovered a great many works, and left us an inventory of them" (JER 49618-49621). Isidore of Seville, about three centuries later, in a chapter on those who founded libraries among Christians: "quoque Pamphilus martyr, cuius vitam Eusebius Caesariensis conscripsit, Pisistratum in sacrae bibliothecae studio primus adaequare contendit. Hic enim in bibliotheca sua prope triginta voluminum milia habuit." ("Pamphilus the martyr too, whose life Eusebius of Caesarea wrote, strove first to equal Pisistratus in zeal for a sacred library. For he had in his library nearly thirty thousand volumes.", ISID 13131-13136; printed "bibHothecae", "voKiminum miHa"). LATE; the thirty thousand is Isidore's alone (the editor's note at JER6 42603-42604 repeats it). Documented as each witness says. EUS-ed 43017-43020, "Pamphilus is chiefly celebrated for the library ... to which Eusebius owes a large part of the materials of his history", is the editor's claim (M3): not told.

I2. **The Defense in the Latin quarrel over Origen (defended: Origen's faith and good name).** Rufinus put Book I into Latin in 397 under Pamphilus's name (JER-ed 45085, 45111-45112; RUF preface JER 45144-45147). Jerome, about 401: "The consciences of a great many persons have been wounded by the book which you have published under the name of the martyr; they give no heed to the authority of the bishops who condemn Origen, since they think that a martyr has praised him." (JER 51453-51456). Jerome again about 415: Rufinus "even published the first book of Eusebius' 'Apology for Origen' under the name of Pamphilus the martyr" (JER6 42598-42605, *Against the Pelagians*, prologue). Book I is all that survives (EUS-ed 3661-3664; JERL-ed 58602-58604). Rufinus on what the book is worth: it "sets out quotations from the works of Origen himself" (JER 50214-50215). Documented. The martyr's name gave the book its weight, by Jerome's own complaint.

I3. **Socrates kept him as a witness for Origen (defended).** "That holy man Pamphilus, and Eusebius who was surnamed after him, are trustworthy witnesses on this subject: both these witnesses in their joint life of Origen, and admirable defense of him in answer to such as were prejudiced against him, prove that he was not the first who made this declaration, but that in doing so he was the mere expositor of the mystical tradition of the church." (SOC 10542-10547, III.7). "Pamphilus Martyr mentions this person [Gregory] in the books which he wrote in defence of Origen" (SOC 14029-14030, IV.27). LATER (about 130 years). Documented. Card or one clause.

I4. **Eusebius took his name** (E11). One clause.

I5. **Remembered among the martyrs.** The Maronite Chronicle (Syriac, about 664): "Ibi autem erat presbyter nomine Pamphilus: is est qui edocuit Eusebium, qui propter eum cognominatus est Pamphilus" ("There was a presbyter named Pamphilus: he it is who taught Eusebius, who was surnamed Pamphilus after him", CHR 3727-3729); "Martyrium passus est hoc tempore etiam Pamphilus" (CHR 3747). Theophanes (9th century) lists among the martyrs of the persecution "Pamphilus presbyter Caesareae" (THEO 107185, Latin, damaged). LATE. Card.

I6. **Copies of Scripture from Caesarea (editors only).** A colophon in a Vatican manuscript, in Lightfoot's English: "Pamphilus and Eusebius corrected [this copy]" (EUS-ed 3824-3830); Taylor: copies of the fifth column of Origen's Hexapla "issued from Cæsarea under the direction of Pamphilus the martyr and Eusebius" (EUS-ed 3833-3838); a Coislin manuscript collated "Caesareae in bibliotheca sancti Pamphili cum exemplari manu ipsius scripto" (editor's Latin, `ignatius-deacon_vita-tarasii-in-germanus-tarasius-pg98-grc-lat_migne1860.txt` 60571-60574). The manuscripts are not held. Rests on editors (M4): not told; card only, marked.

I6a. **Jerome on the copies Palestine read.** Jerome, Apology against Rufinus II.27 (numbered 26 in this print, JERL 35034), quoting his own preface to Chronicles: "Medis inter has provincie Palestinos ecodiceslegunt, quos ab Origene elaboratos Eusebius et Pamphilius vulga. verunt: totusque orbis hac inter se trifaria varietate compugnat" (JERL 35093-35100, a damaged print; read "Mediae inter has provinciae Palaestinos codices legunt ... Pamphilus vulgaverunt"). Editors' quotations of the preface confirm the reading: "Mediae inter has provinciae Palaestinos Codices legunt; quos ab Origene elaboratos Eusebius et Pamphilus vulgaverunt" (APL 9979-9983), and the same with "Palaestinae" and "totusque orbis ... compugnat" (`wulfila_gothic-bible-v1-got_streitberg1908.txt` 1700-1705). Plain sense: between Egypt, which used Hesychius's Septuagint, and Constantinople to Antioch, which used Lucian's, the churches of Palestine read the copies that Origen prepared and Eusebius and Pamphilus published. LATER (about 401). Documented as Jerome's report. The colophons stay editors only (I6).

I7. **Photius's other mentions (card).** Library cod. 13, on Eusebius: "He was an ardent admirer of the virtuous saint and martyr Pamphilus, from whom he took his surname" (PHOT 2113-2115). Mystagogia, in Migne's Latin: "Pierium quoque, Pamphilum et Theognostum viros sanctos sanctarumque institutionum doctores, quos, licet propositiones eorum non cunctas omnino admittamus ... ingenti honore atque commendatione prosequimur, praecipue Pamphilum et Pierium, utpote martyrii quoque certaminibus inclytos" ("Pierius too, Pamphilus and Theognostus, holy men and teachers of holy learning, whom, though we do not accept all their propositions, we honour greatly, Pamphilus and Pierius above all, as famed also for the contests of martyrdom", `photius_opera-t2-contra-manichaeos-mystagogia-epistolae-pg102-grc-lat_migne1860.txt` 27836-27846; printed "cunclas", "prose-guimur", "precipue"). LATE (about 550 years). Documented as Photius's words. Card.

## 6. Where sources disagree or come late

D1. **Who wrote the Defense** (the slate sends this to the card). In time order:
- Eusebius, the co-author: "written by us and Pamphilus ... did the work jointly" (E7). The lost Life, as Jerome quotes it: he "wrote nothing whatever of his own, except private letters" (E4).
- Jerome, DVI 75 (392/3): "He wrote an Apology for Origen before Eusebius had written his" (JER 40968-40969; Latin JERL 58497-58498, "Scripsit, antequam Eusebius Caesariensis scriberet, Apologeticum pro Origene").
- Rufinus (397): "the book which he is said to have written in his defence in Greek" (JER 45146-45147; Latin "librum eius, quem pro Origene in Graeco scripsisse traditur", APL 13961-13962).
- Jerome, Letter 84 (400): "shew me any other work of Pamphilus; you will nowhere find any, this is his only one. How then can I know that it is by Pamphilus?" (JER6 19399-19401); it "contains nearly the first thousand lines of Eusebius's sixth book in defence of Origen" (JER6 19408-19410); "the book belongs not to Pamphilus but to Didymus or somebody else" (JER6 19423-19425); and if it is his, "only by Pamphilus not yet a martyr ... that he might efface his error by a martyr's death, and wash away his one fault by shedding his blood" (JER6 19426-19431).
- Rufinus's answer (about 400): Jerome says the martyr's works "are either not to be considered genuine or if genuine, to be treated with contempt"; "Now even the Martyrs fail to gain any respect from him" (JER 50158-50167); "we no longer spare even the martyrs" (JER 50209-50210).
- Jerome, Apol. (about 401-402): Eusebius, "the standard bearer of the Arian faction, wrote a large and elaborate work in six books in defence of Origen ... The first of these six books you have translated and assigned it to the martyr" (JER 51379-51384); Rufinus changed "a few statements about the Son of God and the holy Spirit" (JER 51386-51388); "You thought that men would believe the martyr, though they would have turned in abhorrence from the chief of the Arians" (JER 51400-51402); he had listed it under Pamphilus's name "ten years ago" (JER 53975-53981); he found the six volumes at Caesarea (JER 54979-54980).
- Socrates: their "joint" defense, and "the books which he wrote" (I3).
- Photius: five books by Pamphilus in prison with Eusebius, the sixth by Eusebius alone (E7).
- Modern (M5): McGiffert, "it is certain that Pamphilus was a joint author of it" and "Jerome's unfairness and dishonesty in this matter have been pointed out by Lightfoot" (EUS-ed 3671-3677); Fremantle, "Eusebius was the chief writer", and Pamphilus "actually wrote the sixth book" (JER-ed 45124-45132); Lightfoot reconciles the Life's sentence with joint work (JER-ed 51418-51423); the Letter 84 translator, "Jerome is again misled" (JER6 19410-19412).
- Confidence: that both worked on it, Documented (Eusebius's own word); how the work was shared, Contested; Jerome's denial, Documented as his charge.

D2. **Was Eusebius in prison with him?** Photius says so (E7); Eusebius never says it; the editor prefers a visitor (E7). The charge that Eusebius came out of prison unhurt is the Eusebius story's (Potamo, in its live text; barred). Contested. The story says "with Eusebius", never "in the same cell".

D3. **309 or 310** (S3). Editors differ. Story: "about 310" or "after two whole years in prison".

D4. **Study at Alexandria.** Photius: "Pierius was the teacher of Pamphilus, the head of the catechetical school at Alexandria" (PHOT 11506-11507). EUS-ed: "He pursued his studies in Alexandria (according to Photius, under Pierius, more probably under Achillas...)" (EUS-ed 43013-43015), and Photius's report on Pierius probably "false and rests upon a combination of the accounts of Eusebius and Jerome" (EUS-ed 43070-43071). LATE; Contested. The ANF translator's "According to the common account" (Berytus and Pierius) is an editor's (M6). Card; not told.

D5. **A different book under his name.** Gennadius (about 480) lists among Rufinus's translations "the work of Pamphilus Martyr Against the mathematicians" (GEN, JER 42476). Fremantle: Rufinus made his translation of the Defense for Macarius, who was arguing with the "Mathematici", astrologers (JER-ed 45087-45112). Card: a late confusion or a lost work; nothing held decides it.

D6. **The manner of death.** The shorter form: "punishment similar to the others"; the longer form: a capital sentence; Photius: the sword (E10). Silent, not contrary. Story: "put to death".

D7. **The Acts fragments** ascribed to him in ANF (`anf06_gregory-thaumaturgus-dionysius-julius-africanus-methodius-arnobius.xml`; corpus-map row, provisional); others give them to Euthalius (CEN `sourcing`). Not used.

D8. **Photius's verdict.** The Defense "is not a refutation of the charges against Origen for the most part, but rather supports the accusation" (PHOT 11378-11382). A hostile reader, about 550 years later. Card, credited.
