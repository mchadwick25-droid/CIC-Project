## The heart

It is the right heart. The old sources put four things at the centre of his life: the library he gathered (Eusebius in EUS 36890-36894, and Jerome), his copying of Origen by hand (Jerome, JERL 58486-58490), the stand and death (MP 7, MP 11), and the Defense written with Eusebius (EUS 36973-36976). The essence holds all four. It also carries the line to later readers through the books.

It comes on time. Who, where, the library, the copying, Origen and the death "about 310" are all told by word 148. The Defense and the later readers follow by word 168, within about the first minute.

## Sentence by sentence

**Paragraph 1**
1. "Eusebius of Caesarea wrote of how his friend Pamphilus gave what he could to poor book lovers" — SUMMARY HOLDS. "Quis studiosorum amicus non fuit Pamphili? Si quos videbat ad victum necessariis indigere, praebebat large quae poterat" (JERL 31256-31259); "my dearest friend" (EUS 46694).
2. Quote 1 — HELD. It was checked against the Latin, JERL 31259-31264: "tribuebat promptissime" (handed out most readily), "non ad legendum tantum, sed et ad habendum" (to keep and not only to read), "nec solum viris, sed et feminis, quas vidisset lectioni deditas" (to men, and to women too, when he saw they were devoted to reading), and "multos codices praeparabat ... volentibus largiretur" (kept many books prepared, to give freely to anyone who wished). Every clause is there and nothing is added.

**Paragraph 2**
3. Presbyter at Caesarea, a port in Roman Palestine — HELD. "esteemed worthy of the office of presbyter in that parish" (EUS 43036-43038); "Cæsarea in Palestine" (EUS 42996).
4. Built a library; Jerome says he copied most of Origen's works by hand — SUMMARY HOLDS. "the library which he collected" (EUS 36891); "gave them to the church at Cæsarea" (JER 49613-49614); "maximam partem Origenis voluminum sua manu descripserit" (JERL 58488-58489). "Built" fairly compresses "collected ... and gave to the church".
5. Origen taught at Alexandria and then at Caesarea, and had died in the century before — HELD. He moved "from Alexandria to Cæsarea" (EUS 36332, HE VI.26); "his customary duties in Cæsarea" (EUS 36568); "At this time Origen died", under Gallus (EUS 39162-39163).
6. Tortured, two years in prison, put to death about 310 — HELD. "scrapings in his sides" (EUS 46706-46707); "two entire years" (EUS 47106-47107); "about 310" follows from the fifth year (EUS 46623-46624) plus two years, as the outline's date rule allows.
7. "Eusebius says the two friends wrote a Defense of Origen together, and the books Pamphilus kept were read for generations after him." — SUMMARY HOLDS. "written by us and Pamphilus ... did the work jointly" (EUS 36974-36976). The second clause rests on later witnesses: Jerome found the books "still preserved" about 80-90 years on (JER 40960-40962), and later bishops restored the library (JER 49615-49618). Heard once, the clause reads as the narrator's, so it is not left on Eusebius.

**Paragraph 3**
8. Eusebius holds nearly all of his life; he shared his possessions with the needy and was devoted above all to the Scriptures — SUMMARY HOLDS. "for sharing his possessions with the needy ... He especially excelled all in our time in most sincere devotion to the Divine Scriptures" (EUS 47075-47079). "Above all" keeps "especially". The single-witness remark matches the record: Eusebius is its only first-hand witness.
9. Jerome says he searched the world for books, buying all of Origen's at great cost — HELD. "he sought out all through the world ... most of all he acquired at great expense all the books written by Origen" (JER 49611-49614).

**Paragraph 4**
10. The orders: tear down churches, burn the Scriptures, then make everyone sacrifice — HELD. "commanding that the churches be leveled to the ground and the Scriptures be destroyed by fire" (EUS 45907-45909); "that all the people should sacrifice at once ... to the idols" (EUS 46170-46172).
11. In the fifth year he was tested in public speaking and philosophy, then forced toward sacrifice — HELD. "Urbanus first tested him in rhetorical philosophy and learning; and afterwards endeavored to compel him to sacrifice" (EUS 46699-46700). MP 7 is set in the fifth year (EUS 46623-46624).
12. "He refused and ignored his threats." — HELD. "he refused and in nowise regarded his threats" (EUS 46701).
13. In anger, his sides were scraped in long torture and he was put in prison with the confessors — HELD. "being exceedingly angry ... continuous and prolonged scrapings in his sides ... he put him also with the confessors in prison" (EUS 46702-46709). The gloss on confessors is X5.

**Paragraph 5**
14. Eusebius says they wrote it with care, because of those who looked for faults — HELD. "We prepared this carefully and did the work jointly on account of faultfinders" (EUS 36975-36976).
15. Photius, five centuries later, says Pamphilus wrote most of it in prison — SUMMARY HOLDS. "six books, five of which were written by Pamphilus when in prison in the company of Eusebius" (PHOT 11437-11439). Five of six is fairly "most". The claim is credited.
16. The charges: the soul before the body, and Christ either a mere man or one whose deeds only seemed to happen — SUMMARY HOLDS. "quod ante corpus eam factam dicat exsistere" (APL 20291-20293); "purum hominem" (APL 17661-17664); "δοκήσει, id est, putative tantum ... gesta esse omnia quae a Salvatore gesta sunt" (APL 17666-17670). The Defense itself calls the last charge the opposite of the others ("quae istis omnibus adversatur"), and "either ... or" keeps that.
17. It set out Origen's own words with replies between — HELD. The text is laid out under the headings "ORIGENES." and "PAMPHILUS." (APL 14264-14271).
18. Written to confessors condemned to the mines of Palestine — HELD. "Praefatio ad Confessores ad metalla Palaestinae damnatos" (APL 14112-14115).
19. Readers of pagan and even heretics' books are usually pardoned — HELD. "venia ... quam impertire solet ... his, qui Graecorum saecularium libros, vel nonnunquam etiam haereticorum ... decurrunt" (APL 14126-14153).
20. The accusers think they alone have the skill to test what they read, like a money-changer — SUMMARY HOLDS. "sibi solis scilicet concedi debere peritiam probandi sermonis putant, ut si quid bene ... dictum est, retinere sciant" (APL 14153-14155). The coin-testing is the plain sense of "trapezitae", which the outline glosses in the setup (X8).
21. Quote 2 — HELD. It was checked against the Latin, APL 14155-14158: "ab his vero, qui Origenis libros legunt, istud penitus exclusum putant esse mandatum, quo probabiles jubentur effici trapezitae, scientes quod bonum est retinere, ab omni autem se specie mala abstinere: sed tantum modo si legere libros illius visus fuerit, statim ab his haereticorum perfundetur infamia." Every clause is rendered and nothing is added.

**Paragraph 6**
22. After two whole years, they came before a new governor — HELD. "continued in prison for two entire years" (EUS 47106-47107); Firmilianus, "the successor of Urbanus" (EUS 46757), "he proceeded against Pamphilus and his companions" (EUS 47192).
23. He knew they had held firm, and asked only whether they would now obey — HELD. "having learned that already under former tortures they had manifested an unchangeable zeal ... he asked them if they would now obey" (EUS 47192-47195). "Only" stands in the Greek: "τοσοῦτον μόνον εἰ κἂν νῦν πειθαρχοῖεν, ἀνεπυνθάνετο" (GRK 25241-25242).
24. Each gave the same answer; he sentenced them to death, as he had others before them — HELD. "receiving from every one of them only this one answer ... he inflicted on them punishment similar to the others" (EUS 47195-47198); "παραπλησίως κεφαλικὴν τιμωρίαν" (GRK 25243).
25. He died with eleven others, the only presbyter among them — HELD. "They were twelve in all ... the only one honored with the position of presbyter at Cæsarea, was Pamphilus" (EUS 47070-47074).
26. Eusebius took his name as a surname — HELD. "His surname Pamphilus arose from his friendship for Pamphilus the martyr" (JER 41076-41077).

**Paragraph 7**
27. Eusebius sent readers to the catalog; Jerome used the library — HELD. "a catalogue of the library ... Whoever desires may learn readily from this which of Origen's works have reached us" (EUS 36891-36894); "the library at Cæsarea which Pamphilus so diligently gathered" (JER 39359-39361).
28. Rufinus put the first book, the only one that survives, into Latin under Pamphilus's name — SUMMARY HOLDS. "the book which you have published under the name of the martyr" (JER 51453-51454); "the first book of Eusebius' 'Apology for Origen' under the name of Pamphilus the martyr" (JER6 42598-42605); Photius read all six books (PHOT 11437). That only Book I survives is the state of the text, not a reconstruction: the Library holds Book I alone (APL, APM). It is told as an event in the record, as Part 1 allows.
29. About ninety years on, Jerome complained that readers ignored the bishops who had since condemned Origen as a heretic, because they thought a martyr had praised him — SUMMARY HOLDS. "they give no heed to the authority of the bishops who condemn Origen, since they think that a martyr has praised him. Of what use are the letters of the bishop Theophilus or of the pope Anastasius, who follow out the heretic" (JER 51454-51458). "Thought" is kept.

**Paragraph 8**
30. Twenty-five volumes in Pamphilus's hand, guarded like a king's riches — HELD. "in duodecim Prophetas viginti quinque Ἐξηγήσεων Origenis volumina, manu ejus exarata reperi, quae tanto amplector et servo gaudio, ut Croesi opes habere me credam" (JERL 58490-58494). "A king's riches" is the plain sense of Croesus's wealth.
