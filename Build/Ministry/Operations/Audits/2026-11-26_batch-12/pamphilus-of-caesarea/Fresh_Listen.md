## 1. The test (person)

- **Who he was:** filled. A presbyter (glossed as an elder and priest) at Caesarea, a port in Roman Palestine, who built a church library and copied Origen's books by hand.
- **What happened:** filled. The persecution reaches Palestine; he refuses to sacrifice, is tortured, spends two years in prison, writes a Defense of Origen there, and is put to death with eleven others about 310.
- **What it was about:** filled. Origen was accused of false teaching (the soul living before the body; Christ a mere man, or his deeds only seeming); readers of Origen were shamed as heretics; Pamphilus answered with Origen's own words.
- **Why the connection matters:** filled. His library and copies kept Origen's books for Eusebius, Jerome and Rufinus, and his name as a martyr made readers keep trusting Origen after bishops condemned him.

## 2. The opening

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing (all MINOR)

**Finding 1.** "Readers of pagan books, and even of heretics' books, whose teaching the church condemns, are usually pardoned, it says."
Meaning lost: the speaker ("it", the Defense) comes only at the very end, after two nested clauses, so on one hearing I first took this as the narrator's own claim about the church. This is also where my attention first drifted (see 8).
Exact change: "The Defense says that readers of pagan books, and even of heretics' books, whose teaching the church condemns, are usually pardoned."

**Finding 2.** "They think the command to become approved money-changers is wholly shut out for those who read Origen's books."
Meaning lost: I do not know there is any "command" about money-changers, or whose it is, so "the command to become approved money-changers is wholly shut out" does not land; I recover the point only from the last quote sentence. The narrator's sentence before it gives the image but not that it is a command.
Exact change to the narrator's sentence before the quote: "But Origen's accusers think that they alone have the skill to test what they read, keeping the good like a money-changer who tests each coin and throws out the false." becomes "But Origen's accusers think that they alone may follow the command to test what they read like a money-changer, who tests each coin, keeps the good and throws out the false." (Uses only the word "command" the quote already holds; the check confirms against the outline's rendering.)

**Finding 3.** "In the persecution's fifth year, the governor tested Pamphilus in public speaking and philosophy, then tried to force him to sacrifice."
Meaning lost: I could not tell why a governor would examine a prisoner in public speaking, and briefly took it as a school exam. Understood once the sacrifice follows.
Exact change: "In the persecution's fifth year, the governor tested Pamphilus's learning in public speaking and philosophy, then tried to force him to sacrifice." (Apply only if the outline's beat holds that the test was of his learning; otherwise note.)
