## Blocking findings

**1. ASSUMPTION ADDED.** Paragraph 2: "He never met Origen, the Bible scholar who had taught at Alexandria and then at Caesarea in the century before."
- Origen's teaching at Alexandria and then Caesarea holds: "Origen removed from Alexandria to Cæsarea, ... leaving the charge of the catechetical school in that city to Heraclas" (EUS 36332-36334, HE VI.26). So does his death in the century before: "Gallus succeeded him. At this time Origen died, being sixty-nine years of age" (EUS 39162-39163, HE VII.1). Eusebius first knows Pamphilus "in his time", meaning Agapius's (EUS 43009-43010, HE VII.32.25).
- No old source says the two never met. I searched EUS (HE VI-VII, MP), JER (DVI 54, 75, 81, the Apology), PHOT 117-118 and the Defense's preface. The claim is an inference from dates. It reaches the outline through CEN `origen` dates (Source_Sheet L1), and no line in `cic/texts/` gives it. Pamphilus's birth year is not given anywhere.
- **Change:** cut "He never met Origen," and say only what the sources give: "Origen, the Bible scholar who had taught at Alexandria and then at Caesarea, had died in the century before." (HE VII.1 with the Widely Accepted reign of Gallus.) The outline's Connection line ("The writer must make plain that Pamphilus never met Origen") and its Do not say item "He met Origen ... → never" rest on the same inference. The verdict should weigh that.

**2. ASSUMPTION ADDED (a detail moved onto the wrong event).** Paragraph 5: "It is written to confessors condemned to forced labor in the copper mines of Palestine."
- The Defense's heading is "Praefatio ad Confessores ad metalla Palaestinae damnatos" (APL 14110-14112). That means the mines of Palestine, with no metal named. "Copper" comes from Eusebius's account of other confessors sent "to the copper mines in Phæno" (EUS 46666-46692, MP 7.2-4, Source_Sheet E6). The outline's Do not say keeps the two groups apart ("Do not join them by name").
- **Change:** "It is written to confessors condemned to forced labor in the mines of Palestine."

**3. SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** Paragraph 5: "Origen's accusers, it says, read pagan books and even those of heretics, whose teaching the church condemns, keeping the good like a money-changer who tests each coin and throws out the false."
- The source says the accusers refuse Origen's readers even the pardon "quam impertire solet ... his, qui Graecorum saecularium libros, vel nonnunquam etiam haereticorum, percunctandi atque agnoscendi studio decurrunt". It goes on: "sibi solis scilicet concedi debere peritiam probandi sermonis putant, ut si quid bene ab aliquo dictum est, retinere sciant, ab omni autem specie mala abstinere se noverint" (APL 14124-14152). In plain words: pardon is usually given to those who read pagan books, and sometimes heretics' books too, to inquire and learn. The accusers, though, think that the skill of testing what is said belongs to them alone.
- The brief turns that into a plain fact that the accusers read such books. The Defense's point is that the accusers claim the tester's skill for themselves alone, and refuse to Origen's readers the pardon that readers of pagan and heretical books usually get. The coin-testing gloss is fair as the plain sense of "trapezitae" (Source_Sheet X8).
- **Change:** "Readers of pagan books, and even of heretics' books, whose teaching the church condemns, are usually pardoned, it says. But Origen's accusers think that they alone have the skill to test what they read, keeping the good like a money-changer who tests each coin and throws out the false." (That is two sentences. They can stay one sentence if the writer prefers, with the same content.)

## Summary holds (not blocking)

- P2 "presbyter, an elder and priest of the church, at Caesarea, a port on the coast of Roman Palestine": "the office of presbyter in that parish" (EUS 43036-43038); "Cæsarea in Palestine" (EUS 42996). "Port on the coast" is a place gloss.
- P2 "tortured, held two years in prison and put to death about 310": "continuous and prolonged scrapings in his sides ... put him also with the confessors in prison" (EUS 46705-46709); "in prison for two entire years" (EUS 47106-47107). The year comes from the nineteenth year of Diocletian (EUS 45892-45894), the fifth year and then two years. "About 310" is as rough as the sources allow.
- P2 "Eusebius says the two friends wrote a Defense of Origen together, and the books Pamphilus kept were read for generations after him": "συμπονήσαντες ἀλλήλοις" (GRK 5261); "these are still preserved in the library at Cæsarea" (JER 40960-40962, about 80 years on).
- P3 "shared his possessions with the needy, was devoted above all to the Scriptures": "τῆς οὐσίας εἰς ἐνδεεῖς κοινωνίᾳ ... μάλιστα δὲ παρὰ τοὺς καθ᾽ ἡμᾶς πάντας διέπρεπεν τῇ περὶ τὰ θεῖα λόγια γνησιωτάτῃ σπουδῇ" (GRK 24801-24804; EUS 47075-47078). "Above all" keeps μάλιστα. It drops "beyond all of our time", which is a fair compression.
- P5 Photius "says Pamphilus wrote most of it in prison": "six books, five of which were written by Pamphilus when in prison in the company of Eusebius" (PHOT 11437-11439). It is credited.
- P5 the charges: "quod ante corpus eam factam dicat exsistere" (APL 20291-20293); "purum hominem ... dicere Christum" and "δοκήσει, id est, putative tantum ... gesta esse omnia quae a Salvatore gesta sunt" (APL 17661-17670). "Either ... or" keeps the Defense's "quae istis omnibus adversatur".
- P6 "asked only whether they would now obey": "he asked them if they would now obey" (EUS 47194-47195). The "only" marks that he made no new test, which the passage shows. This is fair.
- P7 the complaint: "they give no heed to the authority of the bishops who condemn Origen, since they think that a martyr has praised him", and the bishops "follow out the heretic" (JER 51453-51458). "Some ninety years": the complaint dates from about 401.
- P8 "like a king's riches": "ut Croesi opes habere me credam" (JERL 58493-58494). Croesus was a king, so this holds.
