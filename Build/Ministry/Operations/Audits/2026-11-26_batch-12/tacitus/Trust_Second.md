## Sentence by sentence

1. "When daylight failed in Nero's gardens, people were set alight to light the dark." MIR 2560–2562: "aliique, ubi defecisset dies, in usum nocturni luminis urerentur. Hortos suos ei spectaculo Nero obtulerat". SUMMARY HOLDS (the gardens are given for the whole show).
2. "Some, covered in the skins of animals, were torn apart by dogs, and many were nailed to crosses." MIR 2560: "ferarum tergis contecti laniatu canum interirent, multi crucibus adfixi aut flamma usti". SUMMARY HOLDS. "multi" covers the crucified or burned together; the burning is in sentence 1, so nothing is lost.
3. "The Roman who tells it, the historian Tacitus, thought them guilty and despised them." MIR 2563: "adversus sontes et novissima exempla meritos"; MIR 2544–2547: "exitiabilis superstitio ... originem eius mali ... atrocia aut pudenda". SUMMARY HOLDS.
4. "Writing a generation or more after the great fire of Rome in 64, Tacitus gave the fullest account from outside the church of how Nero punished the Christians." Fire of 64: Widely Accepted (Kit names it; MIR 2518). "A generation or more": Widely Accepted (Source_Sheet S3; Tertullian puts the *Histories* after the war of 70, TERT-E 2570–2572). Fullest: the only other held outside line is Suetonius's one clause, MIR 2570 "adflicti suppliciis Christiani". HELD.
5. "In it he named Christus, from whom their name came, put to death under Pontius Pilate." MIR 2541–2543: "Auctor nominis eius Christus ... per procuratorem Pontium Pilatum supplicio adfectus erat". HELD.
6. "His account runs to a few sentences, about 140 words of Latin, and the details of these deaths rest on him alone." Counted MIR 2537–2564 from "Ergo abolendo rumori" to the end: 139 words. Sulpicius's details are Tacitus's own words (SUL-L 4790–4793); no other held old source gives them. HELD.
7. "Later, one Christian, Tertullian, answered him by name on another matter, and another, Sulpicius Severus, retold his scene in nearly his own words and called the dead innocent." TERT-L 17833: "Hanc Cornelius Tacitus suspicionem ... inseruit"; SUL-L 4789–4790: "actaeque in innoxios crudelissimae quaestiones". HELD.
8. "In the Annals, his year-by-year history of Rome, Tacitus tells how Rome turned after the fire to prayers and offerings to the gods." MIR 2527–2528: "Mox petita dis piacula ... ex quibus supplicatum". SUMMARY HOLDS (the gloss of *Annals* clarifies a title, Outline Words to explain).
9. "But nothing, not human effort, not the emperor's gifts, not the offerings, ended the shameful talk that the fire had been set on an order." MIR 2534–2536: "Sed non ope humana, non largitionibus principis aut deum placamentis decedebat infamia, quin iussum incendium crederetur." HELD.
10. "So, to kill the rumor, Nero put forward others as the guilty: people the common crowd hated for their shameful acts and called Christians." MIR 2536–2540: "Ergo abolendo rumori Nero subdidit reos ... quos per flagitia invisos vulgus Christianos appellabat." SUMMARY HOLDS. Note, not a finding: the Latin does not say who hated them ("invisos" stands alone; TAC: "a class hated for their abominations, called Christians by the populace"); the crowd is the nearest reading, and the outline holds the wording.
11. "He punished them, Tacitus writes, with the most refined cruelty." MIR 2537: "quaesitissimis poenis adfecit". HELD.
12. "Then Tacitus stops to explain the name, naming no source for it: 'Christus, from whom that name comes, had been put to death in the reign of Tiberius by the governor Pontius Pilate.'" MIR 2541–2543 as in 5, "Tiberio imperitante". Rendering checked against the Latin: faithful; "the governor" for "per procuratorem", with no "one of our". No source is named in the chapter. HELD.
13. "Their religion was a deadly superstition, he says with contempt, and though it was checked for a moment, it broke out again, in Judaea, where the evil began, and in Rome itself." MIR 2543–2547: "repressaque in praesens exitiabilis superstitio rursum [er]umpebat, non modo per Iudaeam, originem eius mali, sed per urbem etiam". HELD.
14. "First those who confessed were seized." MIR 2547: "Igitur primum correpti qui fatebantur". HELD.

17. "He put on chariot races there too, mixing with the crowd dressed as a charioteer, or standing in a chariot himself." TERT-L 17833, 17914–17915: "Hanc Cornelius Tacitus suspicionem eiusmodi dei inseruit ... praesumptum opinor hos quoque ut Iudaicae religionis propinquos eidem simulacro initiari." TERT-L 17915–17918: "At enim idem Cornelius Tacitus, sane ille mendaciorum loquacissimus, in eadem historia refert Gneum Pompeium, cum Hierusalem cepisset ... templum adisset ... nullum illic reperisse simulacrum." Do not say line for the outline: Not that Sulpicius told the fire or the blame in Tacitus's words. Only the rumour of the order and the deaths are nearly his (R1).
