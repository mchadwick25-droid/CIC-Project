## Non-HELD sentences

**1. "When daylight failed in the emperor Nero's gardens in Rome, Christians were set alight to light the dark."** SUMMARY HOLDS. MIR 2560–2562: "aliique, ubi defecisset dies, in usum nocturni luminis urerentur. Hortos suos ei spectaculo Nero obtulerat". The dead are those "quos ... vulgus Christianos appellabat" (MIR 2537–2538); the city is Rome (MIR 2544 "per urbem etiam"). Tacitus places the burning in the gardens only by setting the two side by side; fair compression. Outline holds it word for word (Outline opening (a)).

**2. "Their crime, he says, was less setting the fire than hatred of the human race."** SUMMARY HOLDS. MIR 2549–2551: "haud perinde in crimine incendii quam odio humani generis convicti sunt" ("convicted not so much on the charge of the fire as of hatred of the human race"). "Crimen" is the charge; "their crime ... was" leans a little toward guilt of arson, but the force is kept. Optional, closer: "They were convicted, he says, less of setting the fire than of hatred of the human race." (Outline beat 4.)

**3. "Mockery was added to their deaths: Nero offered his own gardens for the show."** SOFTENED OR CHANGED. BLOCKING. Source, MIR 2551, 2560–2562: "Et pereuntibus addita ludibria, ut ferarum tergis contecti laniatu canum interirent, multi crucibus adfixi aut flamma usti ... Hortos suos ei spectaculo Nero obtulerat". In Tacitus the mockery is the manner of the deaths (the "ut" clause: skins and dogs, crosses, burning); the gardens are a separate sentence. The brief's colon makes the offering of the gardens what the mockery was. The outline (beat 5) holds "Mockery was added to their deaths: the deaths the opening showed"; the brief dropped the back-reference. Exact change: "Mockery was added to their deaths, the deaths told at the start, and Nero offered his own gardens for the show."

**4. "He also put on chariot races, mixing with the crowd dressed as a charioteer, or standing in a chariot himself."** SUMMARY HOLDS. MIR 2562–2563: "et circense ludicrum edebat, habitu aurigae permixtus plebi vel curriculo insistens." "Circense ludicrum" (the circus show) as "chariot races" is the outline's gloss (Outline, words: circus). Dropping v1's "there" is right: Tacitus names no place for the races (Outline, Do not say).

**5. "That account of the Jews, Tertullian says, started the tale that the Christians, close kin of the Jews, worshiped a donkey's head."** SOFTENED OR CHANGED. BLOCKING. Source, TERT-L 17832–17834 and the page after (Oehler p. 176): "somniastis caput asininum esse deum nostrum. Hanc Cornelius Tacitus suspicionem ... inseruit ... Atque ita inde praesumptum opinor hos quoque ut Iudaicae religionis propinquos eidem simulacro initiari." TERT-E 2580–2582: "as Christianity is nearly allied to Judaism". Tertullian makes the Christians near to the Jewish *religion*; "close kin of the Jews" makes it kinship with the people. "Donkey's head" for "caput asininum" holds. The outline holds "close to the Jews" (beat 6). Exact change: "the Christians, close to the Jews in religion," (or the outline's "close to the Jews").

**6. "If the Jews worshiped an image, Tertullian says, the temple was the very place it would be shown."** SUMMARY HOLDS. TERT-L (Oehler p. 176): "Et utique, si id colebatur quod aliqua effigie repraesentabatur, nusquam magis quam in sacrario suo exhiberetur". TERT-E 2585–2587: "if worship was rendered to any visible object, the very place for its exhibition would be the shrine". "Temple" for "sacrarium" (shrine) is fair in context, since the sentence before has Pompey enter the "templum". Outline holds it (beat 6).
