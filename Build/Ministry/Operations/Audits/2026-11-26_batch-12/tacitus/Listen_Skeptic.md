## 1. The test

- **Who this was:** a Roman historian, writing "a generation or more after" the fire, in "the Annals, his year-by-year history of Rome", who "thought them guilty and despised them." Filled, but thin: the shape asks also *why they wrote*, and the listener hears nothing of why Tacitus wrote the Annals. Fillable as "a Roman historian who despised the Christians"; not lost.
- **What happened:** the fire, the rumor, Nero blaming the Christians, the deaths in the gardens. Filled.
- **What it was about:** shifting blame for the fire; Christus named as executed under Pilate. Filled.
- **Why the connection matters:** "the fullest account from outside the church", Christus under Pilate, and later Christian readers. Filled, through Sulpicius Severus. Tertullian is weaker: "answered him by name on another matter" — the listener hears that Tertullian's quarrel was with a different book, and is left asking why he is in a story about this passage.

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing (MINOR)

1. "But nothing, not human effort, not the emperor's gifts, not the offerings, ended the shameful talk that the fire had been set on an order." — "set on an order" is opaque on one hearing, above all for a second-language listener: whose order? The thread is rescued only because Nero answers the rumor in the next sentence. Change: say plainly whom the talk pointed at, if the outline holds it (the trust readers confirm); else "the shameful talk that someone had ordered the fire."
2. "First those who confessed were seized." — confessed to what: to the fire, or to being Christians? Meaning lost. If the record leaves it open, say so inside the sentence ("those who confessed, though he does not say to what, were seized first"), as the Outside witness shape asks ("what the old sources leave open").
3. "That account of the Jews, Tertullian says, started the tale that the Christians, close to the Jews, worshiped an ass's head." — "close to the Jews" does not land: close how? Change, if the outline holds it: "...the tale that the Christians, taken for a kind of Jews, worshiped an ass's head."
4. "Tertullian called Tacitus a liar, and a wordy one, and caught him out: the same Tacitus tells that the Roman general who took Jerusalem went into the temple and found no image there." — the listener has to work out the catch (no image, so no ass's head). On one hearing the point slips. Change, if the outline holds it: add "so by Tacitus's own telling there was no ass's head to worship."

### 6b. Lines that sound machine-made (MINOR, for the reviser)

1. Paragraph 2 as a whole, especially "Later, one Christian, Tertullian, answered him by name on another matter, and another, Sulpicius Severus, retold his scene in nearly his own words and called the dead innocent." — a balanced "one... another" preview that reads like an abstract, a report about the record. It also tells Sulpicius's retelling, "nearly his own words" and "innocent", which paragraph 7 then tells again almost word for word. The listener hears the same fact twice. Change: cut the preview to "Later Christians answered him and retold his scene," and let paragraph 7 carry the detail.
2. "naming no source for it" (para 4), "none of them named" (para 5), "though he names no source" (para 7) — three absence flags in four paragraphs. Each is defensible for an outside witness, but together they sound like a caveat checklist. Keep the one the story needs most (the source of the name Christus); drop the others or fold them in.
3. "Mockery was added to their deaths, and these were the deaths in the gardens, which Nero gave for the show." — a stitched callback to the opening; "gave for the show" is stiff. Change: "Mockery was added to their deaths: these were the deaths in the gardens, which Nero offered as a show."
4. "Tacitus closed his own account with a sentence that still called them guilty:" — the narrator frames the last quote as a verdict against Sulpicius. It steers. Change: "Tacitus closed his own account this way:"
5. "But nothing, not human effort, not the emperor's gifts, not the offerings, ended..." — a tricolon with the cadence of machine prose. If it follows Tacitus's own list, keep it, but fix "set on an order" (item 3.1).
