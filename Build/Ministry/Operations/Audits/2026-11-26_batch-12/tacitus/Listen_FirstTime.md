## 1. The kind kit's test

- **Who this was:** filled. A Roman historian who wrote a generation or more after the fire and despised the Christians.
- **What happened:** filled. Rome burned, a rumor blamed the fire on an order, Nero blamed the Christians and killed many of them in his gardens, and Tacitus wrote it down.
- **What it was about:** filled, mostly. It was about shifting blame for the fire, and the charge of "hatred of the human race".
- **Why the connection matters:** **NOT FILLED. BLOCKING.** The story's key point is that he "named Christus ... put to death under Pontius Pilate". A listener with no church background does not know that Christus is Christ, the founder the Christians followed. To them it is one more strange name, so they cannot tell why this outside line matters. "From whom their name came" helps only a listener who already makes the link between Christus and Christians.
  - Exact change (s 5): "In it he named Christus, from whom their name came, put to death under Pontius Pilate." becomes "In it he named Christus, Latin for Christ, the founder from whom the Christians took their name, put to death under Pontius Pilate." The trust reader should confirm that the outline holds this gloss. If it does not, it goes back to the outline. Do not add it straight into the story.

## 2. The opening (s 1 to 3)

- **Who:** **missing. BLOCKING.** We do not hear who the burning people were. "Thought them guilty" leaves "guilty of what, and who are they?" until s 4. Nero is named but never called the emperor anywhere in the story; "the emperor's gifts" in s 9 only hints at it. Holding back the victims' name breaks Part 1's "never as a held-back reveal".
- **Where:** partial. "Nero's gardens" is given, but Rome is not named until s 4.
- **When:** missing until s 4 ("the great fire of Rome in 64").
- **Why there:** missing until s 9 to 10 (the rumor, then the blame).
- Exact change (s 1): "When daylight failed in Nero's gardens, people were set alight to light the dark." becomes "When daylight failed in the emperor Nero's gardens in Rome, Christians were set alight to light the dark."

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing (MINOR)

1. s 9: "...ended the shameful talk that the fire had been set on an order." The order is no one's. The listener has to guess that the talk blamed Nero, and the next paragraph's blame-shifting depends on that guess. This is close to losing the thread. If the source allows it, say whose order the talk meant; if it does not, keep the line as the source gives it.
2. s 13: "Their religion was a deadly superstition ... though it was checked for a moment, it broke out again..." A second-language listener hears "checked" as "examined", not "stopped". Suggest "held back for a moment".
3. s 14: "First those who confessed were seized." Confessed to what: to the fire, or to being Christians? The meaning is lost. If the source leaves this open, keep it open and do not fill it.
4. s 15: "Then, on their information, a huge number, none of them named, were convicted, less, he says, of setting the fire than of hatred of the human race." Four breaks in a row, and the "less ... than" is lost. The listener misses that the main charge was hatred, not arson.
5. s 16: "Mockery was added to their deaths, and these were the deaths in the gardens, which Nero gave for the show." "Gave" what for the show? The listener does not get that he opened his gardens for it.
6. s 21: "...the same Tacitus tells that the Roman general who took Jerusalem went into the temple and found no image there." The catch is lost. The listener does not see that no image means no ass's head. It is a side beat, so the thread holds.

The final paragraph steps back from Sulpicius (about 400) to Tacitus's own closing sentence. It is framed clearly ("Tacitus closed his own account") and ends on the subject's own words, so it is understood. It is not counted.

## 4. Bare statements

## 5. Citations read aloud

## 6. Tells

None that Part 1 names. There is no dash, no stock word, no moral close and no question answered by itself. The "not X but Y" in the last quote is Tacitus's own wording. Note for the reviser: three clauses say what is missing ("naming no source for it", "none of them named", "though he names no source"). For an outside witness, saying what the words cannot tell us is part of the shape. Three in a row starts to sound like a pattern.

## 8. Attention

- Attention first drifts at s 7: "Later, one Christian, Tertullian, answered him by name on another matter, and another, Sulpicius Severus, retold his scene..." Why: after the word count and "rest on him alone", two new names and a teaser ("another matter") arrive with no content yet. That stretch summarizes instead of telling. Attention comes back at s 8 with the fire.
