# Outline check: Tacitus

## My account of the heart (written before reading Outline.md)

From the census entry (`tacitus`, `suetonius`), the source sheet, and what Tacitus is known for.

- **The essence.** Tacitus, a Roman historian writing a generation or more after the fire of Rome in 64, gives in a few sentences of his year-by-year history (about 140 words of Latin) the fullest account from outside the church of Christians punished at Rome: to kill the rumour that the fire had been ordered, Nero put the blame on people the crowd called Christians and had them killed as a show. He names Christus, executed under Tiberius by Pontius Pilate, and despised the Christians while he recorded pity for them.
- **Also told.** The chapter in its order: the rites that failed to stop the rumour (C1–C2); Nero's blame on a class "hated for their shameful acts" (C3); Christus under Pilate, and the "deadly superstition" from Judaea that broke out again in Rome (C4); the arrests of those who confessed, then a huge number on their information, convicted less of the fire than of "hatred of the human race" (C5); the deaths made a show in Nero's gardens and at the chariot games (C6); the pity that arose, because they seemed to die for one man's cruelty, though he still calls them guilty (C7). What it cannot tell: what the shameful acts or the confessions were, and where he learned of Christus (D6). One witness: everything on the Christians rests on him.
- **What came of him.** (1) Sulpicius Severus, about 400, carried the scene into a Christian history in nearly Tacitus's own words, called the dead innocent, and made it the start of persecution (R1; what was defended: their innocence). (2) Later readers used the chapter to show how many Christians there were and that they died: Ruinart against Dodwell's "few martyrs", Hooker, Whiston (R2a–c); the 1876 debate and Bradlaugh's doubt to the card (ruling). (3) Tertullian, about 200, answered him by name over the ass's-head charge in the *Histories*, not this chapter (R3; what was defended: the Christians' worship against his charge).

## Findings

1. **BLOCKING. The essence says "Later Christians answered him by name"; only one did** (Outline line 6). Tertullian is the only held writer who names Tacitus (R3; N5: "none held beyond Tertullian (named) and Sulpicius (unnamed)"). Sulpicius names no source (R1). The plural says more than the record holds. Replace "Later Christians answered him by name, and one retold his scene in nearly his own words and called the dead innocent." with: **"Later, one Christian, Tertullian, answered him by name on another matter, and another, Sulpicius Severus, retold his scene in nearly his own words, without naming him, and called the dead innocent."** Keep the confidence parenthesis as it is.

2. **MINOR. "What came of it" item 1 says "in his own words"** (line 50). The sheet's caution (R1) is "nearly the same words", never "word for word". Replace "A Christian history carried his scene on, in his own words, and called the dead innocent" with **"A Christian history carried his scene on, in nearly his own words, and called the dead innocent"**.

3. **MINOR. The ass's-head tale is Tertullian's report of a book that is not held** (beat 6, first item, line 45). The *Histories* are not held (N1). What Tacitus wrote there rests on Tertullian alone (TERT-E 2569–2582). In beat 6, replace "Tacitus had written that the Jews" with **"Tacitus, Tertullian says, had written that the Jews"**. Add to Do not say: **"- Not the ass's-head tale or Pompey's empty temple as told straight from Tacitus. The Histories are not held; tell both as Tertullian reports them (R3)."**

4. **MINOR. The opening groups the deaths in a way the Latin does not** (line 35). "Others, covered in animal skins, were torn apart by dogs, or nailed to crosses" puts the skins and the crosses on the same people. MIR 2560–2561 gives the skins and dogs as one death, and "multi crucibus adfixi aut flamma usti" as a second group ("many were nailed to crosses or burned"). The census longDescription has the same slip. Replace "Others, covered in animal skins, were torn apart by dogs, or nailed to crosses (C6)." with **"Some, covered in animal skins, were torn apart by dogs; many were nailed to crosses (C6)."** Add to Do not say: **"- Not that the people in animal skins were also nailed to crosses. The skins and dogs are one death; the crosses and the burning are others (C6)."**

5. **MINOR. "Carthage" has no cited line** (beat 6, line 45; sheet, Witness kinds). It holds: Jerome, as quoted in TERT-E 620–622, calls Tertullian "a native of the province of Africa and city of Carthage" (old). Add "(Carthage: Jerome in TERT-E 620–622)" after "About 200, in Carthage".

6. **MINOR. The ending turns back from 400 to 64.** Q3 is an event in the chapter (C7), told after the readers. The kit allows closing on the subject's own words, so this stands. But the writer must frame it as the words that close his chapter, not as a later turn. Add to Ending (a): **"Frame Q3 as the sentence with which Tacitus ends his chapter ('Tacitus closed his account ...'), so it is heard as his words, not as a later event."**

7. **MINOR. The Library sweep misses four later readers** (sheet §8). They are card material only, under ruling 3, and no story line changes:
   - `acta-sanctorum_junii-tomus-5-lat_palme1867.txt` 153250–153285 (the Bollandists quote s6, s8 and s10 for Peter and Paul, and add Juvenal);
   - `mcgready_posthumous-works_smith1837.txt` 1263–1268;
   - `roberts-robert_the-trial_1908.txt` 4896–4922;
   - the note in `npnf111_chrysostom-homilies-acts-romans.xml` 19776.

   Add them to "Found" and "Set aside" as late readers for the card.
