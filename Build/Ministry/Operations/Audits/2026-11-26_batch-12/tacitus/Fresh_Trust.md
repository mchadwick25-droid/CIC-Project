**Heart.** Right for the subject: the sources show an outside, hostile witness whose few sentences are the fullest account of the deaths, naming Christus under Pilate, then answered by Tertullian and retold by Sulpicius. It is told by word 147 (the end of paragraph 2), inside the first minute.

**Quotes.** All three were checked against the Latin, not the English. It holds, and it rightly leaves out the English's "one of our". Q2: SUL-L 4787–4788, "[igi]tur uertit inuidiam in Christianos, actaeque in inno[x]ios crude-lissimae quaestiones". It holds. ## Sentences marked SUMMARY HOLDS

**1.** "When daylight failed in the emperor Nero's gardens in Rome, Christians were set alight to light the dark."
MIR 2561–2562: "aliique, ubi defecisset dies, in usum nocturni luminis urerentur. Hortos suos ei spectaculo Nero obtulerat". The night burnings were part of the show Nero gave his gardens for. Placing them in the gardens is a fair compression. No change.

**3.** "The Roman who tells it, the historian Tacitus, thought them guilty and despised them."
MIR 2563, "adversus sontes" (guilty); MIR 2538–2543, "per flagitia invisos", "exitiabilis superstitio", "originem eius mali". "Guilty" and the contempt are both his own words. "Historian": TERT-L 17834–17836, Tacitus's "historiarum suarum". No change.

**4.** "Writing a generation or more after the great fire of Rome in 64, Tacitus gave the fullest account from outside the church of how Nero punished the Christians."
The year 64 is Widely Accepted, and the kit names it as a date told plainly. "A generation or more" is kept rough, as the date rule asks: TERT-L 17835 shows his *Histories* began with the Jewish war, after 70, and the Library gives him no fixed year. "Fullest": the one other old outside witness held, Suetonius (MIR 2570, "adflicti suppliciis Christiani, genus hominum superstitionis novae ac maleficae"), gives one clause. No change.

**6.** "His account runs to a few sentences, about 140 words of Latin, and the details of these deaths rest on him alone."
Recounted for this read on MIR 2537–2564, from "Ergo abolendo rumori" to the end: 142 words. The only other held telling of the deaths is Sulpicius's (SUL-L 4789–4793), which is nearly his Latin and written about 335 years later. No change.

**10.** "So, to kill the rumor, Nero put forward others as the guilty: people the common crowd hated for their shameful acts and called Christians."
MIR 2537–2539: "Ergo abolendo rumori Nero subdidit reos et quaesitissimis poenis adfecit, quos per flagitia invisos vulgus Christianos appellabat." In the Latin, "vulgus" is the subject of "appellabat" (called) only. "Invisos" (hated) names no one who hated them; TAC 68 s6 has it the same way: "a class hated for their abominations, called Christians by the populace". The meaning and force hold, but the hatred has been tied to the crowd. MINOR. Exact change: "people the common crowd hated for their shameful acts and called Christians" → "people hated for their shameful acts, whom the common crowd called Christians".

**19.** "About 200, in Carthage, the Christian Tertullian answered Tacitus by name in his Apology, his defense of the Christians."
TERT-E 620–623 (Jerome): "a native of the province of Africa and city of Carthage ... flourished under Severus and Antoninus Caracalla". TERT-E 963–966: the dates proposed for the *Apology* run from 198 to 217. "About 200" is no finer than the old source allows. TERT-L 17832–17833 has "Hanc Cornelius Tacitus" (by name). No change.

**21.** "That account of the Jews, Tertullian says, started the tale that the Christians, close kin of the Jews, worshiped a donkey's head."
TERT-L 17832–17833: "caput asininum esse deum nostrum. Hanc Cornelius Tacitus suspicionem eiusmodi dei inseruit". At the passage "Atque ita inde praesumptum opinor hos quoque ut Iudaicae religionis propinquos eidem simulacro initiari", TERT-E 2580–2582 reads: "as Christianity is nearly allied to Judaism". Tertullian makes the Christians close to the Jewish *religion*. "Close kin of the Jews" on its own could be heard as kin by birth. MINOR. Exact change: "close kin of the Jews" → "close kin of the Jews in religion".
