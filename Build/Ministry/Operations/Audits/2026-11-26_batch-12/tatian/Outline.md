# Outline: Tatian

Kind: person (`kinds/person.md`). Census id `tatian`, told within `syriac-edessa-nisibis` ("Syriac Christianity"). File letters (AG, AG-G, IR, HI, HI-G, EU, EP, JE, JC, TH, TH-M, DI, AD, AP, CL, SE, CEN) and item numbers (S, E, I, D) are those of `Source_Sheet.md` in this folder. "-ed" marks a modern editor's note.

- **The heart** (set first, from the census entry and the slate's job, then checked against the Library):
  - **The essence:** Tatian, born in the land of the Assyrians and trained in Greek learning, became a Christian, heard Justin teach, and wrote an *Address to the Greeks* that scorned their learning as younger and worse than the old "barbarian" writings of Moses. After Justin's death, his opponent Irenaeus says, he left the church; and he wove the four Gospels into one book, the *Diatessaron*, which churches of the Syriac-speaking East read in worship; in the 400s one bishop, Theodoret, gathered the copies he found in his own churches and brought in the four Gospels instead. Own words (AG 7457-7464, 6971-6978, 7026-7028), Documented; Justin's hearer and the break (IR 33664-33666), Documented as Irenaeus's account; the *Diatessaron* (EU 26424-26433), Documented; read in Syriac churches (AD 65; DI-ed 1763-1768), Widely Accepted; Theodoret (TH 29735-29741), Documented.
  - **Also told:** the question he was answering, where truth is found, and his answer in the barbarian writings (E1, E2); what the *Address* says, in its own claims (E5); whom he taught (E6); the three charges Irenaeus brings, each in its content, and the Encratites (E7, E8); Epiphanius's East (E9); what the *Diatessaron* was and its name (E10).
  - **What came of him:** the *Diatessaron* read in the Syriac-speaking churches and commented on (I1); its removal in the 400s (I2); the *Address* kept and read for its proof that Moses was older (I4); remembered as a teacher the church judged a heretic (I5). *Defended: the one God against the gods, and the old barbarian writings against Greek learning (E2, E5c, E5g).*

- **Setting:**
  - Time: "the middle of the second century": Justin's hearer (IR 33664), the break after Justin's death (IR 33665-33666), in the twelfth year of Marcus Aurelius by the *Chronicle* (JC 42910-42912, 43059-43060), about 172 (Widely Accepted, S4); Jerome: he flourished in the reign of 'Marcus Antoninus Verus and Lucius Aurelius Commodus' (JE 40098-40099), Jerome's names for the emperors; not for the story. No birth or death date. Spoken: "in the middle of the second century"; the break "about 172, in the reign of Marcus Aurelius".
  - Place: born "in the land of the Assyrians" (AG 7460); at "the city of the Romans" (AG 7252); a teacher "at Rome" (EU 29214-29215); later, by Epiphanius only, Mesopotamia (EP 15997-16001). Spoken: "the land of the Assyrians, in the East", "Rome", "the East, in Mesopotamia, between the rivers". Not Edessa for Tatian himself (S5).
  - Tradition: census "Syriac Christianity"; spoken "the Syriac-speaking churches of the East", where his book was read (I1).
  - The danger at Rome: philosophers who sought the deaths of Justin and Tatian (AG 6550-6556); his own readiness to die rather than deny God (AG 5831-5834).

- **Words to explain:**
  - *Barbarian:* to a Greek, anyone not Greek: Babylonians, Persians, Egyptians, Phoenicians (AG 5686-5693). Say it before Quote 1: "the Greeks called every other people barbarians, and Tatian claimed the word for his own faith." Never the modern sense, savage.
  - *The mysteries:* secret rites of a god's cult, open only to those initiated (AG 6963; AG-G 10274 "μυστηρίων μεταλαβών"). Say "initiated into the mysteries, the secret rites of the gods".
  - *Rhetoric, sophist:* the art of public speaking, taught for pay (AG 5726-5728; EU 24054-24055; TH 29630). Say "a teacher of public speaking".
  - *Aeons:* invisible heavenly powers, one of whom, Hippolytus says Tatian taught, made the world below (HI 15349-15352). Say "invisible heavenly powers".
  - *Adam not saved:* that the first man was not saved, because, Hippolytus says, he was the beginning of disobedience (HI 13011-13012; HI-G 17427-17428).
  - *Encratites:* "the self-controlled" (IR 33656-33657): they refused marriage, and some of them animal food (IR 33656-33661), and wine, offering water in its place (TH 29639-29642). Say "the Encratites, the self-controlled".
  - *Heretic:* one the church judged to have left its faith for a teaching of his own; Irenaeus's own picture is "his own peculiar type of doctrine" (IR 33667-33668). Use only inside the *Chronicle*'s line ("known as a heretic"), with its plain sense.
  - *Diatessaron:* Greek for "through four" (EU 26433-26434); the Arabic copy calls it "The Fourfold, a compilation from the four Gospels" (DI 10422-10424). Say "the Diatessaron, 'through four': one gospel story made from the four".
  - *Address:* a speech written as a book, here to the Greeks (EU 26486-26487, λόγος ὁ πρὸς Ἕλληνας). Say "his Address to the Greeks, a book written as a speech".

- **Shape:** Teacher or thinker. The question: where truth is found, in Greek learning or in older writings (AG 6970-6971, 7026-7028). What he taught: the barbarian philosophy, free to rich and poor (AG 7076-7081), and Moses older than Homer (AG 7026-7034). Who learned it: Rhodo at Rome (EU 29214-29215); later readers of the *Address* (I4); the churches that read his Gospel book (I1). The record decides the order: his own turn first, the break and the Gospel book after, as the old sources tell them.

- **Openings:**
  1. (Recommended.) Tatian says he had been initiated into the mysteries, the secret rites of the gods, and that at Rome he found Jupiter honored with human blood, and Artemis near the city honored the same way (AG 6962-6968). So he went off by himself and asked how he might find the truth (AG 6969-6971). Then the essence. Leads straight into his search and the writings he found.
  2. The book's own last page: "These things, O Greeks, I Tatian … have composed for you", born in the land of the Assyrians, trained first in their learning (AG 7457-7464). Paraphrase only: its closing sentence (AG 7464-7471) is the old live story's quote and is not used.

- **Story beats** (after the opening and the essence, in time order):
  1. **Learning, and the turn.** He was born, he says, in the land of the Assyrians, and trained first in Greek learning (AG 7460-7463); he followed rhetoric and visited many lands (AG 7250-7252), and Eusebius says he won no small fame as a teacher of it (EU 24054-24056). After the search of the opening, he came upon old writings. Gloss *barbarian*; then Quote 1. In the same book Moses is "the founder of all barbarian wisdom" (AG 7029-7030), so these were the writings of Moses and the prophets, by his own linking (Documented as his). He says they set people free from many rulers and countless tyrants (AG 6993-6994; may be paraphrased). Explain it: *the mysteries*, above; the writings, his own reasons in Quote 1. Documented, his own telling.
  2. **Justin's hearer, and Crescens.** Irenaeus says he was a hearer of Justin's (IR 33664). Tatian calls him "the most admirable Justin" (AG 6526; AG-G 7956). In his own book he says that Crescens, a philosopher in the great city, Rome, who claimed to despise death, so feared it that he worked to bring death on Justin, "and indeed on me", because Justin, preaching the truth, showed the philosophers to be gluttons and cheats (AG 6550-6556; AG-G 8105-8111). Told once, here. Explain it: the quarrel, Tatian's side (the reason he gives); Crescens's own side is not held. Documented as Tatian's charge.
  3. **The *Address to the Greeks*.** What the book says, in its claims, not as a list of titles: every art the Greeks boast of came from barbarians, astronomy from Babylon, geometry from Egypt, letters from Phoenicia (AG 5686-5693); among Christians the poor are taught free, with old women and boys (AG 7076-7081), and women study philosophy (AG 7131-7133); he will pay the emperor's taxes and serve a master, but will die before he denies God (AG 5822-5834; narrated, AG-G 2903-2909); and Moses is older than Homer, proved from Greek, Chaldean, Phoenician and Egyptian writers, not from Christian ones (AG 7026-7034, 7389-7391, AG 7282-7450). He expects to hear, "Tatian ... has embraced the doctrines of Barbarians" (AG 7269-7271; may be paraphrased). No date or place of writing. Explain it: *Address*, above. Documented.
  4. **A teacher at Rome.** Rhodo, from Asia (spoken "from Asia, around Ephesus": "Ephesus in Asia", EU 28197-28198), writing in Tatian's own century, says he was taught at Rome by Tatian (EU 28922, 29214-29215; JE 40228-40229). Tatian wrote a book of *Problems*, promising to explain the dark passages of Scripture, and Rhodo promised answers of his own (EU 29217-29223). Do not place it before or after the break. Documented, through Eusebius.
  5. **The break.** After Justin's death, by Irenaeus, his opponent, writing not long after, who calls Tatian's teaching on Adam "lately" invented (IR 33662-33663). Quote 2 (Irenaeus's judgment, in his own voice). Then his three charges, each in its content: invisible aeons, "like the followers of Valentinus"; marriage "nothing else than corruption and fornication", like Marcion; and a teaching of his own, that Adam was not saved (IR 33668-33675), because, Hippolytus later says, Adam began disobedience (HI 13011-13012). Irenaeus puts him among the Encratites, the self-controlled, who refused marriage, and some of them animal food, a group he says sprang from earlier teachers, Marcion among them (IR 33655-33661). Eusebius's *Chronicle*, in the twelfth year of Marcus Aurelius, about 172: "Tatian is known as a heretic, from whom come the Encratites" (JC 43059-43060; narrated with "heretic" glossed). Theodoret, much later, says they offered water in place of wine (TH 29639-29642). Explain it: the break, Irenaeus's reason (pride in being a teacher) and his charges; *aeons*, *Adam*, *Encratites*, above. Tatian's own side of the break is not held. Documented as Irenaeus's account; founder or member, Contested (D4).
  6. **The East.** Epiphanius, writing some two centuries later, says that after Justin's death Tatian crossed from Rome to the East and set up his school first in Mesopotamia, and that his teaching took hold from Antioch to Cilicia and Pisidia (EP 15997-16007). That he alone names the place is Holl's judgment (EP-ed 16018): card only, not told. Credited once, in a clause; no year. Documented as Epiphanius's report: one witness, about 200 years later, and the only one for Mesopotamia (EP-ed 16018).
  7. **The *Diatessaron*.** Eusebius, about 150 years after the break, says Tatian put together a combination and collection of the Gospels, "I know not how", and called it the *Diatessaron*, and that some still had it (EU 26424-26433, 26479; Greek EP-ed 16075-16077). Gloss the name. It was one book compiled from the four Gospels, in Eusebius's words a combination of them (EU 26425) and in the Arabic copy's "a compilation from the four Gospels" (DI 10423-10424); the Arabic copy opens, as John does, "In the beginning was the Word" (DI 2195-2196). Not when, where or in what language (D6). Explain it: what the book was, from Eusebius's words and the Arabic text; Theodoret's "short book" (TH 29734) gives its use. The NPNF editor's "continuous narrative" (AP-ed 12594) is a modern description: not told. Documented that he made it.

- **What came of it** (heaviest first, told after beat 7):
  1. **The Syriac-speaking churches read it.** Edessa's later founding story has crowds come daily to hear the Old and New Testament read, "of the Diatessaron" (AD 65; one clause, as in the sibling). Later writers of the Church of the East say Ephrem commented on it (DI-ed 1763-1768; SE 15906-15907; Widely Accepted). Ephrem's role: a deacon of Nisibis, ordained there by its bishop (SE 15829-15831, late); spoken "Ephrem, a deacon of Nisibis". *Defended, in its way: the Gospel told as one story in worship.*
  2. **A bishop put it away.** Theodoret, bishop of Cyrrhus, in the 400s (Cyrrhus placed as "under Antioch": he served under "the chief bishop of the East" and "his successors in the see of Antioch", JE 33157-33161; its eight hundred parishes, JE 33165-33166, card; never tied to the Syriac-speaking churches): Tatian had cut out the genealogies and the other passages that show the Lord born of David's line; yet not only his own followers used it, but those who held to the apostles' teaching, not seeing the harm, and using it simply as a short book. He found more than two hundred copies held in honor in his churches, gathered them all, put them away, and brought in the Gospels of the four evangelists (TH 29727-29741; TH-M 14973-14997). Narrated: the wording of the two hundred books is the old live story's quote. Tell his reason and the users' side together. Documented as his act; his charge is his (D7).
  3. **His *Address* was kept and read.** Clement of Alexandria said it treated the age of Moses with accuracy (CL 29726-29727); Eusebius calls it his book "most in use among many" (EU 26484-26486). *Defended: Moses and the prophets older than all the Greeks (EU 26503-26505).* One clause.
  4. **Remembered as a heresy-founder:** Irenaeus, Hippolytus, Eusebius, Jerome (E7, E8). Carried by beat 5; no further line.

- **Quotes, 2 or 3 at most** (each rendered by me; none is used elsewhere on the tree; none is shared in this batch; none is the old live story's):
  1. **Address to the Greeks 29**, rendered from Otto's Greek (AG-G 10388-10394), a damaged OCR print, every clause confirmed by Otto's Latin on the same page (AG-G 10458-10465) and all but one clause by Ryland's English, printed 1867 and 1885 (AG 6971-6978), which drops "the clear account of how all things were made".
     "While I was thinking hard about what mattered, I came upon some barbarian writings. They were older than the teachings of the Greeks, and more divine than their error. I was won over by their plain words, and by the artlessness of those who spoke them. I was won over by their clear account of how all things were made, and by their foreknowledge of what was to come. I was won over by their excellent commands, and by the rule of one over all things."
     Job: the one idea, in his own voice: why he left Greek learning for the old writings. Beat 1.
  2. **Irenaeus, Against Heresies I.28.1**, rendered from the Greek as Holl and Wendland quote it from Eusebius (EP-ed 15968-15969, 16015-16016; HI-G 17441-17442), two prints of the same text; the clause both omit, "puffed up as if he were better than the rest", from the English printed before 1931 (IR 33666-33667, ANF 1885; McGiffert 1890, EU 26371-26372).
     "As long as he was with him, he let nothing of this kind show. But after his martyrdom he left the church. Lifted up by the conceit of being a teacher, and puffed up as if he were better than the rest, he set up a school with a character all its own."
     Job: the break, and the blame, in his opponent's own voice. Beat 5. The narrator names Justin in the sentence before.
  Every other short span in the beats ("and indeed on me", "the most admirable Justin", "I know not how", "lately", "of the Diatessaron", "nothing else than corruption and fornication", "like the followers of Valentinus") is a sense for the narrator's words, not a further quote.

- **Connection to carry into the story:** Tatian passed one book to the Syriac-speaking churches: the four Gospels woven into a single story, which they read in worship. What the listener must understand, in order:
  1. What he made: one gospel from four, called "through four", that runs as one story from "In the beginning was the Word" (EU 26424-26433; DI 2195-2196, 10422-10424).
  2. Who took it up: churches of the East read it aloud, as Edessa's later founding story pictures (AD 65), and later writers of the Church of the East say Ephrem commented on it (DI-ed 1763-1768).
  3. Why it was taken away: Theodoret judged that it cut out Jesus's descent from David, found it honored even by faithful believers who used it as a short book, and put the four Gospels in its place (TH 29727-29741).
  4. The break with the church is told as Irenaeus gives it (beat 5); the census has no break line (slate decision 3), so no link is drawn to the Encratites as a tree entry.
  Carry it through what each did with the book, never a "this mattered because" sentence.

- **Ending:**
  1. (Recommended.) Centuries later a priest translated it from Syriac into Arabic, and that copy still opens "In the beginning was the Word" and ends: here ends the Gospel which Tatian compiled and named Diatessaron, the Fourfold (DI 2195-2196, 10422-10429; narrated, not a third quote). Ends on his book, with his name in it.
  2. Theodoret's more than two hundred books gathered and put away, and the four Gospels brought in (TH 29735-29741).
