# Outline check: Tatian (`tatian`), batch 12

## My own account of the heart (written before reading `Outline.md`)

From the census entry (teaser, `dates`, `toldWithin`), the source sheet and what Tatian is known for:

- **Essence:** Tatian, born "in the land of the Assyrians" and once a teacher of Greek rhetoric, became a Christian at Rome, heard Justin, and wrote the *Address to the Greeks*, which turns on Greek learning and argues that Moses is older and truer than all of it. After Justin's death he broke with the church, as Irenaeus tells it, and he made the *Diatessaron*, one gospel woven from the four, which the Syriac-speaking churches read in worship.
- **Also told:** what the *Address* says (every Greek art borrowed from barbarians; the philosophers mocked; Moses older than Homer; one God, tribute to the ruler but no denial); his turn from the rites of Rome to the "barbarian writings"; his teaching at Rome (Rhodo); the break in Irenaeus's voice, with his three charges and his blame; the Encratites, founder or joiner, credited each way; Epiphanius's journey east, credited; what the *Diatessaron* was.
- **What came of him, heaviest first:** (1) the *Diatessaron* read in the Syriac churches, with Ephrem's commentary, by late witnesses (defended in its way: the four Gospels as one story); (2) Theodoret's removal of more than two hundred copies in the 400s, with his charge; (3) the *Address* kept and used as the defence of Moses' antiquity and of the one God (Clement, the writer against Artemon, Eusebius, Jerome) (defended); (4) remembered as a heresy-founder; (5) the *Diatessaron* surviving in Arabic and Latin.
- Shape: Teacher or thinker. Contested and not told: his race, the date and place of the *Address*, whether Crescens caused Justin's death, the language, place and timing of the *Diatessaron*.

## Comparison with the outline's heart

The outline's heart agrees with mine in substance: the turn from Greek learning to the barbarian writings, the *Address*, Justin's hearer, the break in Irenaeus's voice, the *Diatessaron*, and the same ranking of what came of him, with the defended things marked. No major contribution is missing (the Arabic and Latin survival, I3, rightly sits in the ending and the card). One wording in the essence overreaches the sources (B1). Shape (Teacher or thinker) fits. The opening and essence come in the first minute; beats 1-7 run once, in order, with no event in two beats and the ending not told early.

## Verdict

**FIX.** Two BLOCKING findings, nine MINOR.

## BLOCKING

**B1. The essence implies the Syriac churches' reading ended with Theodoret, and that his churches were Syriac-speaking.** Theodoret says only that he found the copies "in our churches" (ἐν ταῖς παρ᾽ ἡμῖν ἐκκλησίαις, TH 29738; TH-M 14985), and no held source calls his diocese Syriac-speaking. "Read in worship until, in the 400s, a bishop gathered its copies" makes one bishop's act end the whole tradition's use. Replace, in **The essence**:
"which the Syriac-speaking churches read in worship until, in the 400s, a bishop gathered its copies and brought in the four Gospels instead."
with:
"which churches of the Syriac-speaking East read in worship; in the 400s one bishop, Theodoret, gathered the copies he found in his own churches and brought in the four Gospels instead."

**B2. The Cut first list cuts the approved heart.** Mark's ruling applies every Cut first item, and the approved 650 is the independent count with all of them out (`../Length_Count.md`: 630, rounded up). But items 5, 10 and 12 remove three parts of the heart Mark approved: whom he taught (Rhodo, beat 4), Epiphanius's East (beat 6), and the *Address* kept and read (What came of him 3). Item 11 also removes the arts the Greeks owe to barbarians, the one claim that shows the book "scorned their learning", as the essence says. Replace the whole **Cut first** list, through its "Never cut" line, with:

- **Cut first** (in order; Mark approved 650, and every item applies; each cut is a whole item):
  1. The Problems book and Rhodo's promised answers, in beat 4, keeping the pupil at Rome (about 20). Card.
  2. The women who study philosophy, in beat 3 (about 10). Card.
  3. Antioch, Cilicia and Pisidia, in beat 6, keeping Mesopotamia (about 12). Card.
  4. Theodoret's water in place of wine, in beat 5 (about 12). Card.
  5. Clement on the *Address*, in What came of him 3, keeping Eusebius's "most in use" in one clause (about 13). Card.
  6. The taxes and the master, in beat 3 (about 25). Card.
  7. The writings that free people from many rulers, in beat 1 (about 15). Leave out.
  8. The *Doctrine of Addai* clause in What came of him 1, keeping Ephrem (about 15). Leave out; the sibling tells it.
  9. The expected reply "embraced the doctrines of Barbarians", in beat 3 (about 15). Leave out.
  10. Eusebius on his fame in rhetoric, in beat 1, keeping Tatian's own "I have followed rhetoric" (about 12). Card.
  11. The free teaching of rich and poor, old women and boys, in beat 3, keeping the arts from barbarians and Moses older than Homer (about 20). Card.
  12. "Heretic" and its gloss in the *Chronicle* line, beat 5: the *Chronicle* sets the year, about 172, and names him as the source of the Encratites (about 15). Card; *Heretic* comes off Words to explain and Do not say.
  13. The Arabic ending, closing instead on Ending 2 (about 10 net). Card.
  Never cut: the two quotes, the gloss of barbarian, the Crescens charge from Tatian's own book, Irenaeus's three charges in their content with the Encratites' plain sense, Eusebius on the *Diatessaron* with its name, Theodoret's act with his reason and the users' side; and, as parts of the approved heart, Rhodo the pupil at Rome (beat 4), Epiphanius's East in Mesopotamia (beat 6), the arts the Greeks owe to barbarians (beat 3), and Eusebius's "most in use" (What came of him 3).

## MINOR

**M1. Quote 2 puts Justin's name where the Greek has a pronoun** (ἐκείνῳ, ἐκείνου, EP-ed 15968-15969; HI-G 17442). Replace "As long as he was with Justin, he let nothing of this kind show. But after Justin's martyrdom he left the church." with "As long as he was with him, he let nothing of this kind show. But after his martyrdom he left the church." Add to the Job line: "The narrator names Justin in the sentence before." My rendering otherwise agrees with the outline's. Quote 1 agrees with mine clause for clause. The added "I was won over" repeats συνέβη πεισθῆναι across the split sentences and adds nothing.

**M2. Setting, Time.** Jerome names the emperors "Marcus Antoninus Verus and Lucius Aurelius Commodus" (JE 40098-40099). Replace "Jerome: he flourished under Marcus Aurelius and Lucius Verus (JE 40098-40099)" with "Jerome: he flourished in the reign of 'Marcus Antoninus Verus and Lucius Aurelius Commodus' (JE 40098-40099), Jerome's names for the emperors; not for the story".

**M3. Beat 6 confidence.** Replace "Widely Accepted as Epiphanius's report." with "Documented as Epiphanius's report: one witness, about 200 years later, and the only one for Mesopotamia (EP-ed 16018)."

**M4. "Later Syriac writers."** The Chronicle of Seert is an Arabic chronicle, not a Syriac one (SE, Scher's French of the Arabic). In What came of it 1, Connection 2 and Who tells it, replace "later Syriac writers" with "later writers of the Church of the East".

**M5. What came of it 3.** Clement says the age of Moses "has been discussed with accuracy by Tatian" (CL 29726-29727), not "praised". Replace "Clement of Alexandria praised its account of Moses's age" with "Clement of Alexandria said it treated the age of Moses with accuracy". This now goes to the card.

**M6. Ending 1.** "The book outlived its removal" implies that the Arabic line came down from copies that escaped Theodoret. No source says so. Delete that sentence. This goes to the card with item 13.

**M7. Beat 3 citation.** The Chaldean, Phoenician and Egyptian witnesses are in chapters 36-41. Add "AG 7282-7450" after "(AG 7026-7034, 7389-7391".

**M8. Do not say: add these.**
- "Theodoret removed it from the Syriac churches" / "the churches stopped reading it in the 400s." Say: "Theodoret gathered the copies he found in his own churches" (TH 29738).
- "Two hundred copies." Say "more than two hundred" (πλείους ἢ διακοσίας, TH 29735).
- "Justin converted him." No source says it. He says the old writings won him (AG 6971-6978), and Irenaeus calls him Justin's hearer.
- "Rhodo was his pupil before the break" / "after the break." Neither is told (E6).
- Any year for Justin's death. Say "after Justin's death".

**M9. Library sweep.** Spot-check of the corpus-map buckets and `cic/texts/` for "Tatian", "Tatien", "Diatessaron" and "Victor Capuanus":
- Victor of Capua's preface is held: `liberatus-victor-tunnunensis-primasius-junillus-pl68-lat_migne1866.txt` 23122-23218, *Praefatio in Evangelicas Harmonias Ammonii*. It says Tatian, Justin's disciple, made one Gospel out of four (unum ex quatuor) and called it *Diapente*. OCR is rough. Move it from **Not held** to **Used**, and in I3 cite it in place of Hogg's report (card only).
- Missing from the sweep: Michael the Syrian, *Chronique* (`michael-the-syrian_chronique-tome1-livres-i-vii-fra_chabot1899.txt` 14279-14356). This late Syriac witness names the aeons, marriage as corruption, the *Diatessaron* ("mélangé"), the Encratites and the paraphrase of the Apostle. Also missing: Jerome's own works (`npnf206_jerome-principal-works.xml` 9617, 34021, "the chief of the Encratites", "the leader of the Encratites"), a further Jerome witness for D4. Add both under Set aside or Used (card).
- Neither changes a story claim. The other "not held" items (Ephrem's commentary, Rabbula's canons, any Syriac *Diatessaron*) found nothing under their name forms.
