**Witness kinds.**
- **Own words:** the *Address to the Greeks* (AG, AG-G). The *Diatessaron* is his work but holds no words of his own, and survives only in later translations.
- **Contemporary:** Irenaeus of Lyon, his opponent, who calls Tatian's teaching on Adam "lately" invented (IR 33662-33663). Rhodo, who was taught by him at Rome, through Eusebius (EU 29214-29223). The anonymous writer against Artemon, early 200s, through Eusebius (EU 32129-32139).
- **Later, about one generation:** Hippolytus of Rome (early 200s, census `hippolytus-of-rome`), who repeats Irenaeus; Clement of Alexandria.
- **Later, about 150 years:** Eusebius of Caesarea (early 300s); Aphrahat and Ephrem in the 300s.
- **Later, about 200 years:** Epiphanius of Salamis; Jerome; Filastrius.
- **Later, about 280 years:** Theodoret of Cyrrhus (book written 453, by Hogg, DI-ed 1789-1790).
- **Late:** the *Doctrine of Addai* (dated "third to fourth century" by Hogg, DI-ed 1924; "c. 4th-5th c." by the file header, AD line 4); Isho'dad of Merv (about 852), Bar Salibi (1100s), 'Abdisho (d. 1318), all as Hogg quotes them; the Arabic *Diatessaron* (translator Ibn at-Tayyib, from a Syriac copy of about 900, DI-ed 1715-1722); the Chronicle of Seert.

## 1. Setting

S1. **His own name and birth.** AG 7457-7464, own words, Documented: "These things, O Greeks, I Tatian, a disciple of the barbarian philosophy, have composed for you. I was born in the land of the Assyrians, having been first instructed in your doctrines, and afterwards in those which I now undertake to proclaim." Greek AG-G 14221-14233: "Ταῦτα ὑμῖν, ὠὧ ἄνδρες Ἕλληνες, ... κατὰ βαρβάρους φιλοσοφῶν Τατιανὸς συνέταξα, γεννηθεὶς μὲν ἐν τῇ τῶν ᾿46- [Ἀσ]συρίων γῇ, παιδευϑεὶς δὲ πρῶτον μὲν Ξτὰ ὑμέτερα". Spoken: "born, he says, in the land of the Assyrians". Coxe, editor, glosses the land as "from Euphrates and the Tigris" (AG-ed 5545-5546): clarifies the word only. Plain: "in the East, beyond the Euphrates".
- Where the sources differ: Epiphanius (EP 15995) and Theodoret (TH 29630, "Τατιανὸς δὲ ὁ Σύρος") call him a Syrian; the Arabic copy's preface calls him "Titianus the Greek" (DI 2111), and so does the Chronicle of Seert (SE 15907). McGiffert, after Harnack, holds him of Greek parentage, against Zahn's Syrian (EU-ed 26191-26196): modern claims. **Contested. Use only his own words.**

S2. **Greek learning and rhetoric.** AG 7249-7253, own words, Documented: "I have visited many lands; I have followed rhetoric, like yourselves; I have fallen in with many arts and inventions; and finally, when sojourning in the city of the Romans, I inspected the multiplicity of statues brought thither by you". AG 5715-5716: "we have renounced your wisdom, though I was once a great proficient in it". Eusebius IV.16.7, later about 150 years (EU 24054-24057): "a man who early in life lectured upon the sciences of the Greeks and won no little fame in them"; Greek in EP-ed 15953-15954: "Τατιανός, ἀνὴρ τὸν πρῶτον αὐτοῦ βίον σοφιστεύσας ἐν τοῖς Ἑλλήνων μαϑήμασιν χαὶ δόξαν οὐ σμικρὰν ἐν αὐτοῖς ἀπενηνεγμένος". Jerome (JE 40088-40089): "while teaching oratory, won not a little glory in the rhetorical art". Theodoret (TH 29630): "σοφιστὴς ἐγεγόνει τὸ πρῶτον" (he was first a sophist). Documented. Plain: "a teacher of speaking and rhetoric".

S3. **Rome.** He was at "the city of the Romans" (AG 7252); he calls it "the great city" (AG 6550, 6966-6967; Otto's note, AG-G 10416-10419, "haec enim est μεγάλη πόλις"). Documented. Rhodo was taught by him "at Rome" (EU 29214-29215). No held source says where he first heard Justin (sibling's Do not say).

S4. **Time.** No old source gives his birth. He was Justin's hearer and broke after Justin's death (IR 33664-33666). The *Chronicle*, in Jerome's Latin, under the twelfth year of Marcus Antoninus (reign heading JC 42910-42912; entry 43059-43060): "XII Tatianus hereticus agnoscitur, a quo encratitae." (In the twelfth year, Tatian is known as a heretic, from whom come the Encratites.) Later about 150 years, Documented as the *Chronicle*'s entry; that year as 172 is Widely Accepted (McGiffert, EU-ed 26218-26222; census `dates`). Jerome: "He flourished in the reign of Marcus Antoninus Verus and Lucius Aurelius Commodus" (JE 40098-40099). His death: no held source (McGiffert, EU-ed 26234-26235). Spoken: "in the middle of the second century"; the break "in the twelfth year of the emperor Marcus Aurelius, about 172, by Eusebius's Chronicle".

S5. **Tradition.** Census `toldWithin` `syriac-edessa-nisibis`, spoken "Syriac Christianity"; its entry: Syriac-speaking churches of Edessa and Nisibis, 200-410, continued as `persian-church-of-the-east-early` (CEN). No held source places Tatian himself at Edessa; Edessa comes in only where the *Doctrine of Addai* has its people hear the *Diatessaron* read (I1). Spoken: "the Syriac-speaking churches of the East".

## 2. Events, in order

E1. **The rites at Rome, and the search.** AG 6962-6971, own words, Documented: "having seen these things, and moreover also having been admitted to the mysteries, and having everywhere examined the religious rites performed by the effeminate and the pathic, and having found among the Romans their Latiarian Jupiter delighting in human gore and the blood of slaughtered men, and Artemis not far from the great city sanctioning acts of the same kind, and one demon here and another there instigating to the perpetration of evil,—retiring by myself, I sought how I might be able to discover the truth." Greek AG-G 10274-10277, 10383-10388: "Ταῦτα οὖν ἰδών, ἔτι δὲ καὶ μυστηρίων μεταλαβών ... εὐρὼν δὲ παρὰ μὲν Ῥωμαίοις τὸν κατ᾽ αὐτοὺς Δατιάριον [Λατιάριον] Δία λύϑροις ἀνθρώπων ... καὶ ἐμαυτὸν γενόμενος ἐξήτουν ὅτῳ τρόπω τἀληθὲς ἐξευ- peiv [ρεῖν] δύνωμαι". 
- Explain it. *The mysteries*: secret rites of a god's cult, into which a person was initiated (μυστηρίων μεταλαβών, "having shared in the mysteries"; Otto takes them as the Eleusinian rites, AG-G 10351-10352: an editor's guess, not for the story). *Latiarian Jupiter*: the Jupiter of the Latins, worshipped with blood, Otto's scholion places his seat on the Alban hill (AG-G 10410-10414). Plain: "the god Jupiter, honored near Rome, he says, with human blood". Tatian's side only; the worshippers' side is not held.

E2. **The barbarian writings.** AG 6971-6978, own words, Documented: "And, while I was giving my most earnest attention to the matter, I happened to meet with certain barbaric writings, too old to be compared with the opinions of the Greeks, and too divine to be compared with their errors; and I was led to put faith in these by the unpretending cast of the language, the inartificial character of the writers, the foreknowledge displayed of future events, the excellent quality of the precepts, and the declaration of the government of the universe as centred in one Being." Greek AG-G 10388-10394: "Περινοοῦντι δέ μοε [μοι] τὰ σπουδαῖα συνέβη γρα- φαῖρ [γραφαῖς] τισιν ἐντυχεῖν βαρβαρικαῖς, πρεσβυτέραις μὲν ὗς [ὡς] πρὸς τὰ Ἑλλήνων δόγματα, ϑειοτέραις δὲ αἷς [ὡς] πρὸς τὴν ἐκείνων πλάνην" sul nos [καί μοι] πεισϑῆναι ταύταις συνέβη διά τὸ [τε] τῶν λέξεων τὸ ἄτυφον παὶ [καὶ] τῶν εἰπόντων τὸ ἀνεπιτήδευτον καὶ τῆς τοῦ παντὸς ποιής- σεως τὸ νὐκατάληπτον [εὐκατάληπτον] καὶ τῶν μελλόντων τὸ πποογνωστικὸν [προγνωστικὸν] καὶ τῶν παραγγελμάτων τὸ ἐξαίσιον καὶ τὸν [τῶν] ὅλων τὸ μοναρχικόν." Ryland drops the clause "τῆς τοῦ παντὸς ποιήσεως τὸ εὐκατάληπτον" (the clear account of the making of all things); Otto's Latin on the same page has it (AG-G 10458-10465): "propter dictionis humilitatem et scriptorum simplicitatem et universi creationis explicationem captu facilem et futurorum praecognitionem et praeceptorum excellentiam et singularem universorum principatum" (OCR corrected in my reading).
- What follows: "these put an end to the slavery that is in the world, and rescue us from a multiplicity of rulers and ten thousand tyrants" (AG 6993-6994; AG-G 10396-10397).
- What the writings were: in the same book Moses is "the founder of all barbarian wisdom" (AG 7029-7030) and is to be believed because he is older (AG 7389-7392). So the writings of Moses and the prophets, by Tatian's own linking (Documented as his). Coxe and Ryland call them the Holy Scriptures (AG-ed 6979; 5641-5642): editors; agree.
- Explain it. *Barbarian*: to a Greek, anyone not Greek (AG 5684-5693, where Babylonians, Persians, Egyptians and Phoenicians are the barbarians). Tatian takes the word for himself: "a disciple of the barbarian philosophy" (AG 7457-7458).

E3. **Justin's hearer.** Irenaeus, contemporary, hostile (IR 33664-33665): "He was a hearer of Justin's, and as long as he continued with him he expressed no such views". Hippolytus (HI 13005-13006; HI-G 17423-17424 "καὶ αὐτὸς γενόμενος μαϑητὴς Ἰουστίνου τοῦ μάρτυρος"): a disciple of Justin the Martyr. Tatian's own praise (AG 6526, 6530): "And the most admirable Justin has rightly denounced them as robbers"; Greek AG-G 7956-7957 "καὶ ὅ [ὁ] ϑαυμασιώτατος Ἰουστῖνος ὀρθῶς ἐξεφιρόνησεν [ἐξεφώνησεν] ἐοικέναι τοὺς προειρημένους λῃσταῖς". Documented. Coxe hears in it "The language of an affectionate pupil" (AG-ed 6526-6529): editor's view.

E4. **Crescens.** AG 6550-6556, own words: "Crescens, who made his nest in the great city, surpassed all men in unnatural love (παιδεραστία), and was strongly addicted to the love of money. Yet this man, who professed to despise death, was so afraid of death, that he endeavoured to inflict on Justin, and indeed on me, the punishment of death, as being an evil, because by proclaiming the truth he convicted the philosophers of being gluttons and cheats." Greek AG-G 8105-8111: "Κρίσκης οὖν, ὁ ἐννεοττεύσας τῇ μεγάλῃ πόλει ... ὡς καὶ Ἰουστῖνον, καθάπερ καὶ ἐμέ, ὡς κακῷ τῷ ϑανάτῳ περιβαλεῖν πραγματεύσασθαι, διότε [διότι] κηρύττων τὴν ἀλήϑειαν λίχνους καὶ ἀπατεῶνας τοὺς φιλοσόφους συνήλεγχεν". Documented as Tatian's charge; Crescens's side is not held.
- Where they differ: Eusebius quotes it without "and indeed on me" and adds, "And such was the cause of Justin's martyrdom" (EU 24073-24078). McGiffert: "As the text stands in Tatian, the passage is far from proving that Justin's death was caused by the machinations of Crescens, for Tatian puts himself on a level with Justin" (EU-ed 23895-23897). **Contested whether Crescens caused Justin's death. Tell only Tatian's words.**

E5. **The *Address to the Greeks*: what the book says.** Own words, Documented. No date or place of writing (McGiffert follows Harnack to 152-153, EU-ed 26492-26494: Dominant Modern Reconstruction; leave out).
- a. Every Greek art came from barbarians: "For which of your institutions has not been derived from the Barbarians? ... To the Babylonians you owe astronomy; to the Persians, magic; to the Egyptians, geometry; to the Phœnicians, instruction by alphabetic writing" (AG 5686-5693).
- b. The philosophers mocked: "What noble thing have you produced by your pursuit of philosophy? Who of your most eminent men has been free from vain boasting?" (AG 5738-5740).
- c. Taxes yes, denial no: "Does the sovereign order the payment of tribute, I am ready to render it. Does my master command me to act as a bondsman and to serve, I acknowledge the serfdom. Man is to be honoured as a fellow-man; God alone is to be feared,—He who is not visible to human eyes, nor comes within the compass of human art. Only when I am commanded to deny Him, will I not obey, but will rather die than show myself false and ungrateful." (AG 5822-5834). Greek AG-G 2903-2909: "Προστάττει φόρους τελεῖν ὁ βασιλεύς; Ἕτοιμος παφέχειν [παρέχειν]. Δουλεύειν ὁ δεσπότης καὶ ὑπηρετεῖν; Τὴν δουλείαν γινώσκων [γινώσκω]. ... φοβητέον δὲ μόνον τὸν ϑεόν ... Τοῦτον μόνον ἀρνεῖσθαι κελευόμενος οὐ πεισθήσομαι, τεθνήξομαι δὲ μᾶλλον, ἵνα μὴ ψεύστης καὶ ἀχάριστος ἀποδειχθῶ."
- d. One law for all: "there ought to be one common polity for all; but now there are as many different codes as there are states" (AG 6948-6950).
- e. Taught free, to all: "Not only do the rich among us pursue our philosophy, but the poor enjoy instruction gratuitously ... Thus we admit all who desire to hear, even old women and striplings" (AG 7076-7081); "we do not test them by their looks" (AG 7099-7101).
- f. The women: "you may not treat the women with scorn who among us pursue philosophy" (AG 7131-7133); "the maidens at their distaffs sing of divine things more nobly than that damsel of yours" (AG 7140-7141, 7157-7158), Sappho being the damsel.
- g. Moses older than Homer, proved from the Greeks' own side: "it seems proper for me to demonstrate that our philosophy is older than the systems of the Greeks. Moses and Homer shall be our limits" (AG 7026-7028); "I will not bring forward witnesses from among ourselves, but rather have recourse to Greeks" (AG 7033-7034); then Chaldean, Phoenician and Egyptian writers (chapters 36-41, AG 7282-7450); "it is evident that Moses was older than the ancient heroes, wars, and demons" (AG 7389-7391).
- h. The reply he expects: "Tatian, aspiring to be above the Greeks, above the infinite number of philosophic inquirers, has struck out a new path, and embraced the doctrines of Barbarians." (AG 7269-7271).
- i. The turn (AG 7258-7260) and the closing stand (AG 7464-7471): quoted in the old live story (card `quotationOriginals`); not used as quotes here.
- McGiffert: "he treats Greek philosophy with the greatest contempt, and finds nothing good in it" (EU-ed 26490-26491): editor's view; the text bears it out (a, b, h).

E6. **A teacher at Rome: Rhodo.** Eusebius V.13.8, quoting Rhodo, contemporary (EU 29214-29223): "the same writer acknowledges that he had been instructed at Rome by Tatian. And he says that a book of Problems had been prepared by Tatian, in which he promised to explain the obscure and hidden parts of the divine Scriptures. Rhodo himself promises to give in a work of his own solutions of Tatian's problems." Jerome (JE 40228-40229): Rhodo, "a native of Asia, instructed in the Scriptures at Rome by Tatian". Documented. Whether before or after the break: no old source says; "acknowledges" is Eusebius's word (McGiffert, EU-ed 26203-26209). **Do not place it before or after.**

E7. **The break, by his opponent.** Irenaeus I.28.1, contemporary, hostile (IR 33655-33675): "Springing from Saturninus and Marcion, those who are called Encratites (self-controlled) preached against marriage, thus setting aside the original creation of God ... Some of those reckoned among them have also introduced abstinence from animal food ... They deny, too, the salvation of him who was first created. It is but lately, however, that this opinion has been invented among them. A certain man named Tatian first introduced the blasphemy. He was a hearer of Justin's, and as long as he continued with him he expressed no such views; but after his martyrdom he separated from the Church, and, excited and puffed up by the thought of being a teacher, as if he were superior to others, he composed his own peculiar type of doctrine. He invented a system of certain invisible Æons, like the followers of Valentinus; while, like Marcion and Saturninus, he declared that marriage was nothing else than corruption and fornication. But his denial of Adam's salvation was an opinion due entirely to himself." McGiffert's English of the same (EU 26366-26387).
- Greek of Irenaeus, as Holl and Wendland print it from Eusebius: EP-ed 15968-15969 "ἐφόσον μὲν συνῆν ἐχείνῳ οὐδὲν ἐξέφηνε τοιοῦτον᾽ μετὰ δὲ τὴν ἐχείνου μαρτυρίαν ἀποστὰς τῆς ἐχκχλησίας"; EP-ed 16015-16016 "οἰήματι διδασχάλου ἐπαρϑεὶς ... ἴδιον χαραχτῆρα διδασχαλείου συνεστήσατο"; HI-G 17441-17445 "Ἰουστίνου ἀκροατὴς γεγονώς .... μετὰ δὲ τὴν ἐχείνου μαρτυρίαν ἀποστὰς τῆς ἐκκλησίας .... ἴδιον χαραχτῆρα διδασχαλείου συνεστήσατο, αἰῶνάς τινας ἀοράτους ὁμοίως τοῖς ἀπὸ Οὐαλεντίνου μυϑολογήσας γάμον τε φϑορὰν xal πορ- velav [πορνείαν] παραπλησίως Maoziovi [Μαρκίωνι] καὶ Σατορνίνῳ ἀναγορεύσας, τῇ δὲ τοῦ ᾿Αϑὰμ σωτηρίᾳ παρ᾽ ἑαυτοῦ τὴν ἀντιλογίαν ποιησάμενος." Both elide the clause "puffed up as if superior to others", which is held only in the English (IR 33666-33667; EU 26371-26372). Holl: διδασκαλεῖον means "school", not "doctrine" (EP-ed 16016-16017): clarifies a word.
- Kind and confidence: the break after Justin's martyrdom is Documented (Irenaeus, repeated by every later witness below). The motive ("puffed up") is Irenaeus's blame: keep in his voice.
- Explain it, each side. *The break*: Irenaeus's reason is pride in being a teacher (above); his charges are three: invisible aeons, marriage as corruption, Adam not saved. *Aeons*: invisible heavenly powers; Hippolytus adds that Tatian said "by some one of these the world below has been created" (HI 15349-15352; HI-G 20302-20304). *Marriage as corruption*: "marriage was nothing else than corruption and fornication" (IR 33670-33671). *Adam not saved*: Hippolytus gives Tatian's reason: "Adam is not saved on account of his having been the author of disobedience" (HI 13011-13012; HI-G 17427-17428 "τὸν δὲ Ἀδὰμ φάσκει μὴ σῴζεσθαι διὰ τὸ ἀρχηγὸν παρακοῆς γεγονέναι"). Irenaeus answers it in book III: "All therefore speak falsely who disallow his (Adam's) salvation ... False, therefore, is that man who first started this idea, or rather, this ignorance and blindness—Tatian." (IR 43907-43913). Tatian's own side of the break is not held. His own words on marriage survive only as Clement quotes a lost book of his, *On Perfection according to the Saviour*, against him, in a Latin passage of ANF 2 (CL 36517-36522; title EU-ed 26251-26255): hostile quoter; technical; leave out.
- Later repeaters: Hippolytus (HI 13005-13013); Pseudo-Tertullian, "After Justin's death he began to cherish different opinions from his" (TE 55817-55821); Filastrius (FI 4124-4132, who puts him wrongly "post decii ... persecutionem"); Epiphanius (EP 15937-15945); Theodoret: "Μετὰ δὲ τὴν τοῦ διδασχάλου τελείωσιν, ἐπόθησε γενέσθαι προστάτης αἱρέσεως" (after the teacher's death he longed to become the head of a sect, TH 29632-29633; TH Latin 29672-29674).

E8. **The Encratites.** Irenaeus's name, "Encratites (self-controlled)" (IR 33656-33657), for a group "springing from Saturninus and Marcion" (IR 33656), so Irenaeus does not make Tatian its founder; he says Tatian "first introduced" the denial of Adam's salvation among them (IR 33662-33664). Eusebius: "It is said that Tatian was the author of this false doctrine" (EU 26185-26186), and "their original founder, Tatian" (EU 26424-26425); the *Chronicle* "a quo encratitae" (JC 43059-43060); Jerome: "he founded a new heresy which is called that of the Encratites" (JE 40092-40093); Epiphanius: from him "by succession" the Encratites (EP 16007-16009). McGiffert notes that Irenaeus's own passage implies "that they were not founded by Tatian, but that he found them already in existence" (EU-ed 26310-26313): an editor reading the old text; the old text itself shows it. **Contested: founder (Eusebius, Jerome, Epiphanius) or a man who joined and led (Irenaeus).**
- Explain it, from each side. Irenaeus: they preached against marriage and abstained from animal food (IR 33656-33661). Theodoret (later, about 280 years): "Ὑδροπαραστάται δὲ ὀνομάζονται, ὡς ὕδωρ ἀντὶ οἴνου προσφέροντες· Ἐγκρατῖται δὲ, ὡς μήτε οἶνον πίνοντες, μήτε τῶν ἐμψύχων μεταλαμβάνοντες" (they are called Water-offerers, since they offer water instead of wine; Encratites, since they neither drink wine nor eat what has life, TH 29639-29642; Latin 29682-29685). Epiphanius: he "uses only water" in his mysteries, the church's rites (EP 16066-16072). Jerome's fragments: he "condemns and rejects not only marriage, but also meats" (FR 7575-7576), and wine (FR 7580-7587). The Encratites' own words are not held. Word: *Encratite*, from the Greek for self-control. Plain: "the self-controlled".

E9. **The East.** Epiphanius, *Panarion* 46.1, later about 200 years (EP 15995-16009; my rendering): "He was Syrian by race, as the knowledge that has reached us has it. He set up his school first in Mesopotamia, about the twelfth year of Antoninus, called the Pious, Caesar. For after holy Justin's death he crossed from Rome to the parts of the East and stayed there"; there he brought in aeons after Valentinus's myths, and his preaching took strongest hold "from Antioch by Daphne and in the parts of Cilicia, and still more in Pisidia" (Greek: "τὸ δὲ αὐτοῦ διδασκαλεῖον προεστήσατο ἀπ ἀρχῆς μὲν ἐν τῇ Μέσῃ τῶν ποταμῶν, ὡς περὶ τὸ δωδέκατον ἔτος Ἀντωνίνου τοῦ εὐσεβοῦς Καίσαρος ἐπικληϑέντος. ἀπὸ Ῥώμης γὰρ μετὰ τὴν τοῦ ἁγίου Ἰουστίνου τελείωσιν διελϑὼν ἐπὶ τὰ τῆς ἀνατολῆς μέρη χαὶ ἐκεῖσε διατρίθων"). Holl: "in Mesopotamia" is "nur durch Epiph. überliefert" (EP-ed 16018); Zahn read "Antoninus the Pious" as an error for Marcus, to fit the *Chronicle* (EP-ed 16018-16022). McGiffert: Epiphanius "falls into grave errors (especially in his chronology). The only trustworthy information that can be gathered from him is that Tatian, after becoming a Christian, returned to Mesopotamia and taught for a while there" (EU-ed 26210-26213). **One late witness; Widely Accepted by the editors. Tell only as Epiphanius says, credited, with no year.** Epiphanius's picture of Tatian as a blind man left by his guide (EP 15945-15993) is his own image; leave out. Ryland's "Tatian latterly established himself at Antioch" (AG-ed 5653) rests on no held old source besides Epiphanius's "from Antioch by Daphne"; not for the story.

E10. **The *Diatessaron*.** Eusebius IV.29.6, later about 150 years (EU 26424-26425, 26432-26433, 26479): "But their original founder, Tatian, formed a certain combination and collection of the Gospels, I know not how, to which he gave the title Diatessaron, and which is still in the hands of some." Greek in EP-ed 16075-16077: "ὁ Τατιανὸς συνάφειάν τινα καὶ συναγωγὴν οὐκ οἶδ᾽ ὅπως τῶν εὐαγγελίων συνϑεὶς τὸ διὰ τεσσάρων τοῦτο προσωνόμασεν, ὃ καὶ παρά τισιν εἰς ἔτι νῦν φέρεται". Documented that Tatian made it.
- The name: τὸ διὰ τεσσάρων, "through four" (EU 26433-26434); the Arabic copy: "Diatessaron, i.e., The Fourfold, a compilation from the four Gospels" (DI 10422-10424); its preface: "a word meaning 'fourfold'" (DI 2110). Plain: "through four", one gospel from four.
- What it was: one continuous story drawn from all four Gospels (DI-ed 1968-1969, Hogg, from the Arabic text: "In its present form the Harmony draws from all the four canonical gospels, and from very little else"); its first words in the Arabic: "In the beginning was the Word" (DI 2195-2196); Bar Salibi, late, as Hogg quotes him: "Its commencement was, 'In the beginning was the Word'" (DI-ed 1770-1772).
- "I know not how": McGiffert reads it as meaning Eusebius had not seen it, Lightfoot as disapproval (EU-ed 26426-26432): modern, card only.
- Eusebius adds: "they say that he ventured to paraphrase certain words of the apostle, in order to improve their style" (EU 26479-26482): hearsay; leave out.
- Epiphanius: "It is said that the Diatessaron gospel was made by him, which some call According to the Hebrews" (EP 16009, 16054-16055; my rendering). McGiffert: an incorrect identification by hearsay (EU-ed 26436-26438). Leave out.
- **Language (Greek or Syriac), place, and whether made before or after the break: Contested** (Zahn, Harnack, most scholars "before ... and with no heretical intent", EU-ed 26460-26466; Hogg, "an increasing majority" for Syriac, DI-ed 1995-1997, and "probably" in Mesopotamia, DI-ed 1961-1963). No old source says. Do not tell.

## 3. What came of him (heaviest first)

I1. **The *Diatessaron* read in the Syriac-speaking churches.** *Defended, in its way: the four Gospels as one story read in worship.*
- Edessa's founding story, the *Doctrine of Addai*, late (AD 65): "But a large multitude of people assembled day by day and came to the prayer of the service, and to the reading of the Old and New Testament, of the Diatessaron". Phillips: one manuscript read "Ditornon", the St Petersburg manuscript "Diatessaron" (AD-ed 219). Late legend in setting; Documented that the story names it. Already told in `syriac-edessa-nisibis` ("Edessa's later founding story pictures its people hearing the Diatessaron read"): here one clause at most.
- Ephrem: "Tatian, disciple of Justin, the philosopher and martyr, selected from the four gospels, and combined and composed a gospel, and called it Diatessaron, i.e., the Combined,…and upon this gospel Mar Ephraem commented" (Isho'dad of Merv, about 852, as Hogg quotes him, DI-ed 1763-1768); the Chronicle of Seert lists among Ephrem's works "un Commentaire de l'Évangile appelé Diatessaron, lequel est formé du mélange des quatre Évangiles" (SE 15906-15907). Hogg: an Armenian version of the commentary survives in two manuscripts dated 1195 (DI-ed 1799-1806); the NPNF editor: Ephrem treated the Gospels "not in their separate form, but in the continuous narrative known as the 'Diatessaron'" (AP-ed 12592-12595). Late witnesses plus editors: Widely Accepted. Ephrem's commentary is not held.
- Aphrahat, 300s: Christ is the Word "as is written in the beginning of the Gospel of our Saviour:—In the beginning was the Word" (AP 24764-24766); the NPNF editor: "he seems sometimes to follow the Diatessaron" (AP-ed 13666-13668). Editor's inference: Inferential-Thin; card only.
- Hogg's summary: "at a quite early stage the Diatessaron was very widely if not universally read in the Syriac churches, and commented on by scholars as the gospel" (DI-ed 1928-1931): modern judgment, clarifies the old witnesses; never "the only gospel".

I2. **Its removal in the 400s.** Force told plainly.
- Theodoret, bishop of Cyrrhus, *Compendium* I.20, own words (TH 29727-29741; TH-M 14973-14987, same Greek): "Οὗτος καὶ τὸ διὰ τεσσάρων καλούμενον συντέθεικεν εὐαγγέλιον, τάς τε γενεαλογίας περικόψας, καὶ τὰ ἄλλα ὅσα ἐκ σπέρματος Δαβὶδ κατὰ σάρκα γεγεννημένον τὸν Κύριον δείκνυσιν. Ἐχρήσαντο δὲ τούτῳ, οὐ μόνοι οἱ τῆς ἐκείνου συμμορίας, ἀλλὰ καὶ οἱ τοῖς ἀποστολικοῖς ἑπόμενοι δόγμασι, τὴν τῆς συνθήκης κακουργίαν οὐκ ἐγνωκότες, ἀλλ᾽ ἁπλούστερον ὡς συντόμῳ τῷ βιβλίῳ χρησάμενοι. Εὗρον δὲ κἀγὼ πλείους ἢ διακοσίας βίβλους τοιαύτας ἐν ταῖς παρ᾽ ἡμῖν ἐκκλησίαις τετιμημένας, καὶ πάσας συναγαγὼν ἀπεθέμην, καὶ τὰ τῶν τεττάρων [τεσσάρων, TH-M] εὐαγγελιστῶν ἀντεισήγαγον εὐαγγέλια." Maran's Latin (TH-M 14994-14997; TH 29718-29722): "Nactus sum etiam ipse libros huiusmodi supra ducentos in honore habitos in ecclesiis nostris, quos omnes in unum congestos seposui, et pro his quatuor evangelistarum evangelia introduxi." (He also made the gospel called Diatessaron, cutting away the genealogies and the other passages that show the Lord born of David's seed in the flesh. Not only his own followers used it, but also those who follow the apostles' teaching, not knowing the mischief in its making, but using it in all simplicity as a short book. I myself found more than two hundred such books held in honor in our churches; I gathered them all and put them away, and brought in the Gospels of the four evangelists in their place.) Documented as his act; the charge of cutting is his. The 200 books' wording was the old live story's quote: narrate here.
- Explain it, each side. Theodoret's reason: the book cut out Jesus's descent from David (his charge). The other side: the users held it "in honor" and used it "as a short book" (his own words); 'Abdisho, late and friendly: "of his own he did not add a single saying" (DI-ed 1904-1908). Hogg: the cutting of the genealogies agrees with the Ephrem source but the wider charge "conflicts with all three texts" now held (DI-ed 1844-1851): modern; card.
- Rabbula, bishop of Edessa (d. 435), as Hogg quotes Overbeck: "Let the presbyters and deacons give heed that in all the churches there be provided and read a copy of the Distinct Gospel" (DI-ed 1914-1919); Gwilliam gives the same canon from Overbeck, "a Gospel d Mepharreshe" (GW 12927-12929). Overbeck's Syriac is not held. The canon names no harmony; that it aimed at the *Diatessaron* is Hogg's and Gwilliam's reading (DI-ed 1919-1921; GW 12937-12938): modern. Card only.

I3. **It survived in translation.** Arabic, from Syriac, by "Abu'l Faraj 'Abdulla ibn-at-Tayyib", from a copy by a pupil of Honain (DI 10422-10435); its last line: "Here endeth the Gospel which Tatianus compiled and named Diatessaron, i.e., The Fourfold" (DI 10422-10423). A Latin arrangement in the Codex Fuldensis, which Bishop Victor of Capua (d. 554) took to be Tatian's: his *Praefatio in Evangelicas Harmonias Ammonii* says Tatian, Justin's disciple, made one Gospel out of four ("unum ex quatuor") and called it *Diapente* (`liberatus-victor-tunnunensis-primasius-junillus-pl68-lat_migne1866.txt` 23122-23218, OCR rough; Widely Accepted). Card only. "No copy of a Syriac Diatessaron has yet been shown to have survived" (DI-ed 1731-1733): Hogg in 1896; not for the story as present fact.

I4. **The *Address* kept and used.** *Defended: the one God against the gods; the antiquity of Moses against the Greeks.*
- Clement of Alexandria, Stromata I.21: the age of Moses "has been discussed with accuracy by Tatian in his book To the Greeks" (CL 29726-29727). That line is the quote in the census edge `greek-apologists-second-century--alexandria-catechetical`: narrate only.
- The anonymous writer against Artemon (early 200s), through Eusebius V.28.4: "writings of certain brethren older than the times of Victor, which they wrote in behalf of the truth against the heathen, and against the heresies ... I refer to Justin and Miltiades and Tatian and Clement and many others, in all of whose works Christ is spoken of as God." (EU 32129-32139). Contemporary. Documented.
- Eusebius: "He has left a great many writings. Of these the one most in use among many persons is his celebrated Address to the Greeks, which also appears to be the best and most useful of all his works. In it he deals with the most ancient times, and shows that Moses and the Hebrew prophets were older than all the celebrated men among the Greeks." (EU 26484-26505).
- Jerome: "a most successful book Against the nations, is extant, and this is considered the most significant of all his works" (JE 40096-40098).
- McGiffert: the chapters on Moses are why the *Oratio* was kept "while his other works disappeared" (EU-ed 26513-26515): modern judgment; card.

I5. **Remembered as a heresy-founder.** Irenaeus (E7), Hippolytus, Pseudo-Tertullian, Filastrius, Epiphanius, Theodoret (E7, E8); Jerome's chapter title "Tatian the heresiarch" (JE 40084); Origen, *On Prayer* 24, on Tatian's reading of "Let there be light" as a prayer, "since God was in darkness" (OR 4201-4205): technical; leave out.

## 5. Where the sources disagree or come late

- D1. Assyrian, Syrian or Greek (S1): Contested; his own words only.
- D2. Date and place of the *Address* (E5): Contested; none told.
- D3. One stay at Rome or two; heretic at Rome or in the East (EU-ed 26224-26234, McGiffert reporting Harnack against Zahn): Contested; tell what the old sources give in order, Justin's hearer, Rome, the break after Justin's death, then Epiphanius's East.
- D4. Founder of the Encratites (E8): Contested; credit each.
- D5. Crescens and Justin's death (E4): Contested; Tatian's words only.
- D6. *Diatessaron*: language, place, before or after the break (E10): Contested; not told.
- D7. What it left out (I2): Theodoret's charge; Hogg says the surviving texts partly bear it out and partly not: card.
- D8. Editors' sympathies: Coxe's "half Father and half heretic", falling away from "infirmity of mind and body" (AG-ed 5529-5533); Ryland's "unfortunately fell under the influence of the Gnostic heresy" and his claim that the *Diatessaron* excluded the passages on Christ's humanity (AG-ed 5649-5667), which outruns even Theodoret: modern views, not for the story.
