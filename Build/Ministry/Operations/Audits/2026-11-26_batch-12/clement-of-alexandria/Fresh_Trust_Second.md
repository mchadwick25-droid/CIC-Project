## Sentence by sentence

1. "The last he found was the first of all in ability." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** Strom. I.1, ANF2 27489-27491: "When I came upon the last (he was the first in power)"; McGiffert's English, EUS 28756-28757: "in ability truly he was first". The outline asks for this plain sense in the quote's setup (Quotes, 1). "Of all" only makes plain that he was first of the teachers just named.
2. "Alexandria in Egypt became Clement's own city, late in the second century and into the third." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** EUS V.11.1, 28691-28694: "being trained with him in the divine Scriptures at Alexandria, became well known"; EUS VI.6, 33040-33042: "had charge at that time of the catechetical instruction in Alexandria". The time is unchanged from v1 and rests on Eusebius's dating to Severus (S6, Setting). The tie to the hidden teacher is gone, as the outline's Do not say asks.
3. "Eusebius of Caesarea, writing about a century later, says Origen was among his pupils." **HELD.** EUS VI.6, 33042-33080: "so that Origen also … was one of his pupils." "While still a boy" is rightly left out (the manuscripts differ, EUS 33044-33046 note).
4. "Two centuries after Clement, Jerome, charged with defiling the church by quoting pagan writers, pointed to Clement's books." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** JERL 35102-35104 (Letter 70.2): "cur in opusculis nostris saecularium litterarum interdum ponamus exempla et candorem ecclesiae ethnicorum sordibus polluamus"; JERL 35364-35368 (70.4): Clement and his books named. See M1 on "about".
5. "In Eusebius's account, which holds most of what is known of his life, Clement was trained in the Scriptures at Alexandria under his teacher and then took over from him the teaching of the faith to learners there." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** EUS V.11.1, 28691-28694 ("being trained with him [Pantaenus]"); V.11.2, 28697-28699: "he speaks of Pantænus by name as his teacher"; VI.6, 33040-33042: "having succeeded Pantænus, had charge … of the catechetical instruction". "Under his teacher" is fair, since Eusebius reports Clement naming Pantaenus as his teacher; it sits inside "In Eusebius's account", so the link to the hidden teacher stays Eusebius's. The one-witness note is now inside a sentence in which Eusebius tells something, as the outline asks. See E2 on "known".
6. "Clement also wrote against the followers of Valentinus, a teacher he opposed, who had taught under the emperor Hadrian, by Clement's reckoning." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** Strom. II.3, ANF2 32104-32111 (his answer to "the followers of Valentinus"); Strom. VII.17, ANF2 52955-52960: "in the times of Adrian the king, that those who invented the heresies arose; and they extended to the age of Antoninus the elder", with Valentinus named in that list (52962). "Taught under Hadrian" compresses "arose under Hadrian"; the clause is v1's and the outline's (T19).
7. "They left faith, Clement wrote, to ordinary believers like himself, "us, the simple," and claimed knowledge for themselves as men saved by their very nature." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** ANF2 32106-32108: "the followers of Valentinus assign faith to us, the simple, but will have it that knowledge springs up in their own selves (who are saved by nature)". "Ordinary believers like himself" glosses Clement's own "us"; it is credited to him by "Clement wrote".
8. "His answer to the believers who feared philosophy took the word Paul uses in his letter to the Galatians for the guide who leads a child, and gave it to philosophy." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** Strom. I.5, ANF2 27890-27893: "this was a schoolmaster to bring 'the Hellenic mind' … 'to Christ'" (footnote Gal. iii. 24); the sense "who leads the children", Instructor I.7, ANF2 19935-19946. That Book I answers fearful believers is Clement's own frame, Strom. I.1, ANF2 27652-27661: "some, who in their ignorance are frightened at every noise … But I shall show, throughout the whole of these Stromata … that philosophy is in a sense a work of Divine Providence."
9. "He set philosophy beside the law that God gave the Hebrews through Moses." **HELD.** ANF2 27892-27893: "as the law, the Hebrews"; Strom. I.26, ANF2 31207-31208: "the law was divinely given by Moses". Said in the setup, as the outline asks.
10. "By philosophy he meant whatever each Greek school had said well." **HELD.** Strom. I.7, ANF2 28146-28150: "I do not mean the Stoic, or the Platonic, or the Epicurean, or the Aristotelian, but whatever has been well said by each of those sects … this eclectic whole I call philosophy."
11. "But he would never call divine the human reasoning that men had mangled and falsified." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** ANF2 28153-28155: "But such conclusions of human reasonings, as men have cut away and falsified, I would never call divine." "Mangled" for "cut away" is the outline's own word (Beat 3).
12. "Every day, Clement wrote, he and his fellow believers saw martyrs burned, impaled and beheaded before their own eyes." **HELD.** Strom. II.20, ANF2 34507-34509: "But we have exhibited before our eyes every day abundant sources of martyrs that are burnt, impaled, beheaded." It is not joined to Eusebius's Severus sentence beyond standing next to it, as in the outline's Beat 5.
13. "Some asked, he wrote, why Christians were persecuted and put to death if God cared for them." **HELD.** Strom. IV.11, ANF2 38903-38904: "But, say they, if God cares for you, why are you persecuted and put to death?"
14. "He answered that Christ had foretold it, and that Christians suffered unjustly, for doing right." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** ANF2 38905-38907: "He foretold prophetically what would happen—that we should be persecuted for His name's sake"; 38913-38914: "we being unjustly punished for righteousness' sake." The second part answers the further charge that they were "malefactors" (38912), which the outline's Do not say keeps out; Clement's word "unjustly" stands.
15. "Christ's word to flee when persecuted, he taught, was meant to keep believers from bringing harm on themselves or on the persecutor, and anyone who gave himself up to the judge shared the guilt of his own death." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** Strom. IV.10, ANF2 38875-38883: "nor does He enjoin them by flight to avoid death, as if in dread of it, but wishes us neither to be the authors nor abettors of any evil to any one, either to ourselves or the persecutor and murderer … he also who presents himself before the judgment-seat becomes guilty of his death." "Harm" compresses "any evil"; the outline holds the wording (Beat 5).
16. "Later, writing to Origen, Alexander counted two blessed men among those who had gone on ahead, one of them "the holy Clement," his master and helper." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** EUS VI.14.9, 34330-34355: "those blessed fathers who have trodden the way before us … Pantænus, the truly blessed man and master, and the holy Clement, my master and benefactor, and if there is any other like them"; Greek (Source Sheet E16): "κύριόν μου γενόμενον καὶ ὠφελήσαντά με". Two are named; "any other like them" is left unsaid, which changes nothing the story claims. "Alexander's own teacher" for Pantaenus is gone, as the Do not say asks.
17. "Jerome's critic had asked him why he stained the whiteness of the church with the filth of the pagans by quoting their books." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** JERL 35102-35104: "Quod autem quaeris in calce epistulae, cur in opusculis nostris saecularium litterarum interdum ponamus exempla et candorem ecclesiae ethnicorum sordibus polluamus". "Quoting their books" compresses "set down examples from secular literature".
