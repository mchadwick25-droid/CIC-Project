## BLOCKING

**B1. "That hidden teacher's city, Alexandria in Egypt, became Clement's own, late in the second century and into the third."** (Both trust reads: ASSUMPTION ADDED. Both listeners: the hidden teacher is left loose, and the skeptic could not tell whether he is the teacher of paragraph 3.)
- Evidence: Clement names only Egypt: "having tracked him out concealed in Egypt, I found rest" (ANF2 27489-27492). That the hidden teacher was at Alexandria rests on Eusebius's judgment that he was Pantaenus: "It seems to me that he alludes to the same person also in the first book of his Stromata" (EUS 28697-28704). Cut first 2 cut that credit, so the identification now stands in the narrator's voice, against the outline's Do not say ("Pantaenus, his teacher hidden in Egypt" as fact).
- Change: "Alexandria in Egypt became Clement's own city, late in the second century and into the third."
- **Needs the outline.** Opening 1 itself holds the wording that led here: "Leads straight into the essence: the city where that teacher hid became his." The researcher rewrites it, for example: "Leads into the essence: Alexandria in Egypt became his city."
- Do not say (add): "that hidden teacher's city" or "the city where that teacher hid" → "Alexandria became his own city", with no tie to the hidden teacher unless Eusebius's judgment is told and credited (E4).

**B2. "Eusebius says the boy Origen was among his pupils, …"** (Trust: SOFTENED OR CHANGED. The second trust read marked it SUMMARY HOLDS, from McGiffert's English.)
- Judged BLOCKING by the bar: the old witnesses differ. The critical Greek held reads "ὡς καὶ τὸν ᾿Ωριγένην τῶν φοιτητῶν γενέσθαι αὐτοῦ", without "while a boy" (EUSG 1840-1841); its apparatus shows the manuscripts split on παῖδα ὄντα (EUSG, apparatus after 1843). McGiffert notes that two important manuscripts and Rufinus's translation leave the words out (EUS 33044-33050). Part 1 bars a claim told as fact where the old sources differ, and the slate asks that the pupil be told "only as Eusebius says it". "Among his pupils" holds in every reading.
- Change: "Eusebius says Origen was among his pupils, …" (taken together with B3, below).
- **Needs the outline.** The approved essence ("Eusebius says the boy Origen was among his pupils"), Beat 1 ("then still a boy"), What came of it 1 ("Origen as a boy"), the Connection ("his pupil as a boy") and the Do not say line on Origen all hold "boy". The essence changes by one word; the researcher makes the change through the outline.
- Do not say (add): "the boy Origen" or "Origen as a boy" → "Origen was among his pupils" (the manuscripts of Eusebius differ on "while a boy", E7).

**B3. "Most of what is told of his life comes from Eusebius of Caesarea, who wrote about a century later."** (Both listeners: a stock how-we-know sentence, a tell Part 1 names. The skeptic heard it as misleading by ear, since much of what follows is Clement's own words. Both trust reads marked its content SUMMARY HOLDS.)
- Part 1: when the story rests on one witness, "say so once, early, inside a sentence about that witness, never as a stock how-we-know sentence". It also comes after Eusebius has already been named, unplaced, in s6.
- Change: cut the sentence. Place Eusebius where he is first named, and fold the one-witness note into the sentence where he next tells something. Paragraph 2's last sentence (with B2) becomes two: "Eusebius of Caesarea, writing about a century later, says Origen was among his pupils. Two centuries after Clement, Jerome, charged with defiling the church by quoting pagan writers, pointed to Clement's books." Paragraph 3 then reads: "In Eusebius's account, which holds most of what is known of his life, Clement was trained in the Scriptures at Alexandria under his teacher and then took over from him the teaching of the faith to learners there."
- Do not say (add; Cut first 2 removed the sentence Beat 1 named for this note, which left room): a standalone sentence on where his life is told → the one-witness note inside a sentence in which Eusebius tells something (Beat 1, Who tells it).

**B4. "Both sides of the quarrel Clement stepped into speak only in his telling."** (Trust: ASSUMPTION ADDED. Both listeners: a sentence that only says what is missing, a named tell, and a sentence that fails on one hearing. The second trust read marked it SUMMARY HOLDS.)
- Judged BLOCKING by the bar on both counts. For the fearful believers it holds (ANF2 47241-47247, 28313-28320). For Valentinus's followers it does not: Irenaeus reports the same claim to be saved "because they are spiritual by nature" in his own account (ANF1 30755-30763). It is also a sentence that only says what is missing.
- Change: cut the sentence. Sentences 2 and 4 of the paragraph already credit Clement ("he wrote", "Clement wrote").
- **Needs the outline.** Also told ("Their own words survive only in his account", for both sides) and Beat 2 ("Say once, inside a sentence, that the fearful believers' side survives only in his telling") hold this. The researcher corrects Also told to the fearful believers only, and Beat 2 to "credit each view to Clement where it is told".
- Do not say (add): "both sides speak only in his telling" → credit each view to Clement where it is told; Valentinus's followers' claims are also reported by Irenaeus (ANF1 30755-30763).

**B5. "On the other side stood the followers of Valentinus, who had taught under the emperor Hadrian, by Clement's reckoning."** (Trust: ASSUMPTION ADDED, the framing; the Hadrian clause holds. The skeptic: a bare statement, Valentinus is never said to be anyone, and "on the other side stood" is a stock turn. The second trust read marked it SUMMARY HOLDS.)
- Judged BLOCKING as a misleading implication. Clement answers the fearful believers (Strom. VI.10, ANF2 47241) and the Valentinians (Strom. II.3, ANF2 32104-32111) in separate books, and no line held sets them as two sides of one quarrel. The skeptic's bare statement is met by the same change: the next sentence tells what the followers claimed, and the outline gives Valentinus as a teacher Clement opposed (Beat 2, T19; Do not say, "teachers he opposed").
- Change: "Clement also wrote against the followers of Valentinus, a teacher he opposed, who had taught under the emperor Hadrian, by Clement's reckoning."
- Knock-on, taken with B7: paragraph 5 no longer follows a two-sided quarrel, so its first sentence names whom the answer was for (see B7).
- **Needs the outline.** Also told ("on one side … on the other") and Beat 2 ("On the other side stood the followers of Valentinus") hold the framing.
- Do not say (add): "on the other side stood" or "both sides of the quarrel" → the fearful believers and Valentinus's followers told as two groups Clement answered, each in its own place (T1, T17).

**B6. "By philosophy he meant no single school, but whatever each Greek school had said well, and what men had cut away and falsified he would never call divine."** (Both listeners: a "not X but Y" turn, a named tell; both also lost the inverted second half on one hearing.)

- Change: "By philosophy he meant whatever each Greek school had said well. But he would never call divine the human reasoning that men had mangled and falsified." ("Mangled" for Wilson's "cut away"; the fresh second trust read checks it.)
- **Needs the outline.** Beat 3 and the Words to explain hold "no one school but whatever each had said well" and "what men had cut away and falsified he would never call divine".
- Do not say (add): "no single school, but whatever…" → "whatever each Greek school had said well" (T5).

**B7. "For his own answer he borrowed a word from Paul's letter to the Galatians, the guide who leads a child, and gave it to philosophy."** (Second trust read: a live-text echo. **B8. "Martyrs, Clement wrote, were burned, impaled and beheaded before their eyes every day."** (Trust: SOFTENED OR CHANGED. Both listeners: fails on one hearing, "their" seems to point to the martyrs. The second trust read marked it HELD.)
- Judged BLOCKING by the bar: the source is first person, "we have exhibited before our eyes every day abundant sources of martyrs that are burnt, impaled, beheaded" (ANF2 34507-34509). "Their eyes" drops his witness and has no owner a listener can find, so it both softens the record and misleads.
- Change: "Every day, Clement wrote, he and his fellow believers saw martyrs burned, impaled and beheaded before their own eyes." The sentence before it stays as Eusebius's account of Severus, so the two are not joined (Do not say, "Clement saw the persecution of Severus every day").
- **Needs the outline.** Beat 5 holds "before their eyes every day".
- Do not say (add): "before their eyes" → Clement's first person, "he and his fellow believers saw … before their own eyes" (E11).

**B9. "Their enemies said that Christians were criminals, justly punished, and Clement answered that they suffered unjustly, for doing right."** (Both trust reads: SOFTENED OR CHANGED.)
- Source (Strom. IV.11): "But, say they, if God cares for you, why are you persecuted and put to death? … He foretold prophetically what would happen … although we are punished not alone, but along with many. But those, it is said, being malefactors, are righteously punished. Accordingly, they unwillingly bear testimony to our righteousness, we being unjustly punished for righteousness' sake" (ANF2 38903-38914). The "malefactors" are the many others punished beside the Christians. The objectors' charge is the question, and Clement does not call them "enemies".
- Change: "Some asked, he wrote, why Christians were persecuted and put to death if God cared for them. He answered that Christ had foretold it, and that Christians suffered unjustly, for doing right."
- **Needs the outline.** Beat 5 ("Their enemies said Christians were criminals, justly punished"), the Do not say line "'Persecutors said …' → 'their enemies said'", the Length line and the Never cut list ("the persecutors' charge") all hold the misreading.
- Do not say (add, replacing the "Persecutors said" line): "their enemies said Christians were criminals" → "some asked why, if God cared for them, Christians were persecuted and put to death" (E13).

**B10. "Later, writing to Origen, Alexander counted his own teacher and "the holy Clement," his master and helper, among the blessed men who had gone on ahead."** (Both trust reads: ASSUMPTION ADDED or SOFTENED. - Change: "Later, writing to Origen, Alexander counted two blessed men among those who had gone on ahead, one of them "the holy Clement," his master and helper. Through them, he wrote, he had come to know Origen." (Trust's wording: it keeps "through them" true to "through whom", which names both men; Pantaenus stays unnamed, as Cut first 2 marks.)
- **Needs the outline.** Beat 7 holds "counted his own teacher and 'the holy Clement'".
- Do not say (add): "Alexander's own teacher" for Pantaenus → "two blessed men … one of them the holy Clement", or Pantaenus as "the truly blessed man and master" (E16).
