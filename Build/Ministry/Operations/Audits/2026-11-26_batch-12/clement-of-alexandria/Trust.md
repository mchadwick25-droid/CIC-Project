## Heart

The heart is right for the subject. The sources show a teacher and presbyter at Alexandria (EUS 33040-33043; EUS 33614) who answered Christians who feared Greek philosophy with the claim that it led the Greeks to Christ (ANF2 47241-47247; ANF2 27881-27895). Eusebius names Origen as his pupil (EUS 33043-33080), and Jerome later defended himself by Clement's example (JERL 35102-35105, 35364-35368). The whole essence is told by the end of the second paragraph, at word 133, inside the first minute. Nothing BLOCKING here.

## BLOCKING sentences

**1. Paragraph 2, sentence 1: ASSUMPTION ADDED.**
"That hidden teacher's city, Alexandria in Egypt, became Clement's own, late in the second century and into the third."
- Clement names only Egypt, not a city: "having tracked him out concealed in Egypt, I found rest" (ANF2 27489-27492). That the hidden teacher taught at Alexandria depends on Eusebius's guess that he was Pantaenus: "It seems to me that he alludes to the same person also in the first book of his Stromata" (EUS 28697-28704). The story has cut that guess and its credit, so "that hidden teacher's city" now tells a judgment as fact. The Do not say list already bars "Pantaenus, his teacher hidden in Egypt" as fact.
- The time holds. Eusebius puts Clement "at this time" under Commodus (EUS 28461-28469, 28650-28656) and dates his Miscellanies to Severus's reign (EUS 33078-33087).
- **Change:** drop the link to the hidden teacher. For example: "Clement's own city became Alexandria in Egypt, late in the second century and into the third." Another fix is to keep the link and credit it: "Eusebius thought that teacher worked in Alexandria, in Egypt, ..." The outline does not hold that wording, though: its Cut first item 2 cut it.

**2. Paragraph 2, sentence 4: SOFTENED OR CHANGED.**
"Eusebius says the boy Origen was among his pupils, ..."
- McGiffert's English reads: "so that Origen also, while still a boy, was one of his pupils" (EUS 33043-33044, 33079-33080).
- The critical Greek held in the Library leaves out "while still a boy": "ὡς καὶ τὸν ᾿Ωριγένην τῶν φοιτητῶν γενέσθαι αὐτοῦ" (EUSG 1840-1841). McGiffert's own note says the manuscripts differ on the words παῖδα ὄντα and that Rufinus's old translation leaves them out (EUS 33046 onward).
- The old witnesses differ on "boy". The story tells one reading as what Eusebius says.
- **Change:** "Eusebius says Origen was among his pupils, ..." This holds in every reading. The second clause of the sentence, about Jerome, is HELD (JERL 35102-35105).

**3. Paragraph 4, sentence 1: ASSUMPTION ADDED.**
"Both sides of the quarrel Clement stepped into speak only in his telling."
- For the fearful believers this holds: T1 and T2 survive only in Clement (ANF2 47241-47247, 28313-28320).
- For the followers of Valentinus it is false. Irenaeus gives the same Valentinian claim in his own report: "Animal men … are established by their works, and by a mere faith, while they have not perfect knowledge. We of the Church, they say, are these persons … they shall be entirely and undoubtedly saved … because they are spiritual by nature" (ANF1 30755-30763).
- "The quarrel Clement stepped into" also makes the two groups two sides of one existing quarrel. No line given shows that. Clement answers each one, in separate books (Strom. VI.10, ANF2 47241; Strom. II.3, ANF2 32104).
- **Change:** "The believers who feared philosophy speak only in his telling." Or cut the sentence and credit Clement where each view is told, as sentences 2 and 4 of the paragraph already do.

**4. Paragraph 4, sentence 3: ASSUMPTION ADDED** (the framing; the date clause holds).
"On the other side stood the followers of Valentinus, who had taught under the emperor Hadrian, by Clement's reckoning."
- "On the other side stood" sets Valentinus's followers against the fearful believers within one quarrel. Clement's charge against the Valentinians is about faith and knowledge: "the followers of Valentinus assign faith to us, the simple" (ANF2 32104-32111). He does not oppose them to the believers who feared philosophy.
- The Hadrian clause is SUMMARY HOLDS: "in the times of Adrian the king, … those who invented the heresies arose; and they extended to the age of Antoninus the elder … Likewise they allege that Valentinus …" (ANF2 52955-52966).
- **Change:** "Clement also wrote against the followers of Valentinus, who had taught under the emperor Hadrian, by Clement's reckoning."

**5. Paragraph 6, sentence 2: SOFTENED OR CHANGED.**
"Martyrs, Clement wrote, were burned, impaled and beheaded before their eyes every day."
- Source: "But we have exhibited before our eyes every day abundant sources of martyrs that are burnt, impaled, beheaded" (ANF2 34507-34509).
- Clement writes as a witness ("our eyes"). The brief's "their eyes" has no clear owner, and it drops his first-person witness. That softens the record's force.
- **Change:** "Every day, Clement wrote, he and his fellow believers saw martyrs burned, impaled and beheaded before their own eyes." Or keep his words: "before our eyes every day."

**6. Paragraph 6, sentence 3: SOFTENED OR CHANGED.**
"Their enemies said that Christians were criminals, justly punished, and Clement answered that they suffered unjustly, for doing right."
- Source (Strom. IV.11): "But, say they, if God cares for you, why are you persecuted and put to death? … we are punished not alone, but along with many. But those, it is said, being malefactors, are righteously punished. Accordingly, they unwillingly bear testimony to our righteousness, we being unjustly punished for righteousness' sake" (ANF2 38903-38914).
- In the source, "those" who are called malefactors are the many others punished alongside the Christians. The opponents' recorded charge against the Christians is the question "if God cares for you, why are you persecuted?" Nowhere in the passage do they say Christians are criminals. The brief changes the enemy's charge. The outline's beat 5 and its "persecutors' charge" carry the same misreading.
- **Change:** "Their enemies asked, Clement wrote, why Christians were persecuted and put to death if God cared for them. He answered that Christ had foretold it, and that Christians were punished unjustly, for doing right." Both parts are held: "He foretold prophetically what would happen" and "we being unjustly punished for righteousness' sake" (ANF2 38903-38914). For Review, a Do not say line: "Their enemies said Christians were criminals" → "their enemies asked why, if God cared for them, they were persecuted" (E13).

**7. Paragraph 7, sentence 2: ASSUMPTION ADDED.**
"Later, writing to Origen, Alexander counted his own teacher and "the holy Clement," his master and helper, among the blessed men who had gone on ahead."
- Source: "those blessed fathers who have trodden the way before us … Pantænus, the truly blessed man and master, and the holy Clement, my master and benefactor" (EUS 34338-34356). The Greek is "Πάνταινον. τὸν μακάριον ὦ ιληϑῶς καὶ κύριον" (EUSG 3044-3052).
- Alexander says "my" only of Clement. Pantaenus is "the truly blessed man and master" with no possessive. Eusebius himself calls the two only Alexander's "familiar acquaintances" (EUS 34328-34331). "His own teacher" adds a relationship the source does not give.
- The rest holds: "gone on ahead" for "trodden the way before us", and "helper" for "benefactor" / ὠφελήσαντά.
- **Change:** "Later, writing to Origen, Alexander counted two blessed men among those who had gone on ahead, one of them "the holy Clement," his master and helper." Or name Pantaenus with Alexander's own words: "Pantaenus, "the truly blessed man and master," and "the holy Clement," his own master and helper." For Review, a Do not say line: "Alexander's own teacher" for Pantaenus → "the truly blessed man and master" (E16).
