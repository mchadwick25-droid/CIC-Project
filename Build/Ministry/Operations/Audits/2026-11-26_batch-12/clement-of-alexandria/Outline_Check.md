# Outline check: Clement of Alexandria (`clement-of-alexandria`)

## My account of the heart, written before reading `Outline.md`

From the census entry (knownFor: "Presbyter and teacher at Alexandria, who held that Greek philosophy prepared the way for Christ and taught Origen"), the slate's job and `Source_Sheet.md`:

- **The essence:** Clement, a presbyter of the church at Alexandria who taught the faith to learners there around the year 200, answered Christians who feared Greek philosophy: he held that God gave philosophy to the Greeks as a guide to bring them to Christ, as the Law brought the Hebrews (Strom. I.5). Eusebius says Origen, as a boy, was among his pupils.
- **Also told:** his search for teachers across the Greek world, ending with a teacher hidden in Egypt with whom he "found rest" (Strom. I.1; Eusebius judges this Pantaenus); what he meant by philosophy (no one school, "whatever has been well said", Strom. I.7) and faith and knowledge held together (Strom. V.1, VII.10); his books, chiefly the Miscellanies, with the Exhortation and the Instructor; his argument against the Valentinians, whose words he kept; the Severan persecution, with his rule against handing oneself to the judge; and his last notices in Alexander's letters (carrying the prison letter to Antioch; "my master and benefactor").
- **What came of him, heaviest first:** his pupils, Origen (Eusebius) and Alexander; Jerome's defence of Christian use of pagan learning by his example, naming him the most learned of men and Origen's Miscellanies as imitation (**defended:** Greek learning in Christian use); Eusebius copying his Exhortation; Theodoret counting him among those who fought the heresies (**defended:** faith with knowledge against the Valentinians); Photius's mixed judgment (card).
