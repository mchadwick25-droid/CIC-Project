## r10

Y is somewhat better.

Reasons:
1. Y follows one question all the way through: is Greek philosophy a danger or a gift? It moves from "feared Greek philosophy as children fear masks" to the Galatians guide to the martyrs. X spreads across conversion, monks, politics, the poor and doctrine.
2. X tells things twice. "he died before a council... declared it in its creed" comes back as "In 381, after his death, a council at Constantinople declared". The lodging for the poor also appears in both paragraph 2 and paragraph 7.
3. Both credit their witnesses well ("Origen, Eusebius says"; "out of envy, Gregory of Nazianzus says"). X carries more conflict, but it costs the listener more.

X faults:
- The first sentence is nested and runs long.
- "They quarreled": the quarrel is never explained.
- "His accusers were filling the governor's ears": what they charged is not given.
- "held them equal" has an unclear "them".
- The two prayer forms ("with the Son, together with... through the Son, in...") are hard to follow heard once.
- "made more bishops": the reason is only implied.
- Too many names: Macrina, Gregory of Nyssa and Valens each appear once.
- It ends on a later writer, Benedict, which is not the story's point.

Y faults:
- "His answer took the word Paul uses" repeats "His answer" from paragraph 2, and the word itself is never said.
- "under his teacher" and "the teaching of the faith to learners" are vague.
- The Jerome sentence jumps two centuries ahead, and then the story goes back.
- "the last, who was the first in power" is cryptic heard once.
- "through whom he had come to know Origen" is clumsy.

Grades: X C+, Y B.

## r11

X is clearly better.

Reasons:
1. X has a real story question that runs to an end. The medicine-chest image opens it, and it closes on the same image: "one thing more after the eighty heresies in his chest of cures: the church." Y has no plain essence and no stakes. It drifts from philosophy to Valentinus to persecution.
2. X credits witnesses where they differ: "The historian Socrates says... Another historian, Sozomen, says instead..."
3. Y tells its main point twice and jumps in time. "His answer was that it might have been God's gift" comes back later as "His answer took the word Paul uses." The Jerome line, set "two centuries after Clement," comes before Eusebius on his training.

X faults:

- One sentence runs 37 words and tangles: "The monks of Jerome's monastery had complained for a year... he explains, and he acted from the fear of God..."
- Too many names for one hearing: Adam, Jerome, Socrates, Sozomen and others.
- Augustine and Nicaea come early, then again at the end.
- "A book written to answer the council's argument" is vague.
- "He preached against Origen in the church at Jerusalem" has no time link.

Y faults:

- "the word Paul uses... for the guide who leads a child": the word itself is never said.
- Valentinus is only "a teacher he opposed," and his teaching is barely explained.

- It ends on Alexander's praise, not on Clement.

Grades: X B+, Y C.

## r12

X is clearly better.

Reasons:
1. X's opening scene carries its own stakes: Eutropius "himself fled into the cathedral" after ending the right to shelter there. Y opens on a quote, but no tension follows from it.

- One sentence is nested too deeply for one hearing: "They decked the altar, where the bread of Communion lies, which Christians call Christ's body, with gold cups and silk."

Y faults:

- "But he would never call divine the human reasoning that men had mangled and falsified" is hard to follow.

- No question carries through the story.
