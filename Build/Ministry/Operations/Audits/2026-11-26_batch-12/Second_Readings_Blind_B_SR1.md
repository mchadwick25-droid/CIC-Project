# Second Readings, Blind (B)

## SR1-10
Source: Latin. Read: "manu enim expansa et complanata ad spatae modum ictum solitae animadversionis imitatus, quod volebat intellegi, ad instar liquidi sermonis expressit; intellexi sententiam passionis futuram." (The scan runs "volebatintellegi" together; read as two words.)

For he spread his hand out flat, like a sword, and mimed the blow of the usual execution. In this way he made plain what he wanted me to understand, as clearly as if he had spoken. I understood that the sentence would be martyrdom.

## SR1-11
Source: Latin. Read: "placuit summatim pauca conscribere, non quo aliquem uel gentilium lateat tanti uiri uita, sed ut ad posteros quoque nostros incomparabile et grande documentum in immortalem memoriam porrigatur". (The "lo" between "documentum" and "in" is the edition's line number 10, misread by the scan; it is not part of the text.)

It seemed right to write down a few things in brief. This is not because the life of so great a man is hidden from anyone, even from the pagans. It is so that a great example, without equal, may reach those who come after us as well, and live in a memory that does not die.

## SR1-12
Source: Latin. Read: "dolebo quod non comes fuerim? sed illius uictoria triumphanda est. de uictoria triumphabo? sed doleo quod comes non sim. uerum uobis tamen et simpliciter confitendum est, quod et uos scitis, in hac me fuisse sententia. multum ac nimis multum de gloria eius exulto, plus tamen doleo quod remansi." (The "5" after "plus" is the edition's line number.)

Shall I grieve that I was not his companion? But his victory must be celebrated as a triumph. Shall I celebrate his victory? But I grieve that I am not his companion. Still, I must confess it to you plainly, as you already know: this was my own intention. I rejoice greatly, and more than greatly, in his glory. Yet I grieve more that I was left behind.

## SR1-13
Source: Latin. Read: "Auctor nominis eius Christus Tiberio imperitante per procuratorem Pontium Pilatum supplicio adfectus erat". (The scan reads "Cliristus" for "Christus"; "procurato-/rem" is split across lines.)

Christus, from whom the name came, had been executed in the reign of Tiberius by the procurator Pontius Pilate.

## SR1-14
Source: English (translation). Read: "He therefore turned the accusation against the Christians, and the most cruel tortures were accordingly inflicted upon the innocent." (A page-break tag sits just before this passage; it does not touch the words.)

So he turned the charge against the Christians. As a result, the cruelest tortures were inflicted on innocent people.

## SR1-15
Source: English (translation). Read: "Hence, even for criminals who deserved extreme and exemplary punishment, there arose a feeling of compassion; for it was not, as it seemed, for the public good, but to glut one man's cruelty, that they were being destroyed."

Because of this, people began to feel pity, even for criminals who deserved the harshest punishment, made an example to others. For it seemed they were being destroyed not for the public good, but to feed one man's cruelty.

## SR1-16
Source: English (translation). Read: "although the moon may be greater than the stars, the light of the other stars is not destroyed by the moonlight."

The moon may be greater than the stars. Even so, its light does not wipe out the light of the other stars.

## SR1-17
Source: Greek. Read: "τῷ μὲν γὰρ ἀγάλματι ἀπολέσθαι οὐκ ἔστιν ὑπὸ τῷ αὐτῷ τεχνίτῃ ὄντι, κἂν πάλιν εἰς ὕλην λυθῇ, ἀποκατασταθῆναι δὲ ἔστι· τοῖς δὲ αἴσχεσι καὶ λωβήμασιν ἀπολέσθαι μὲν ἔστιν (τήκονται γάρ), ἀποκατασταθῆναι δὲ οὐκ ἔστι". (The scan prints χ for κ in several words, "οὖκ" for "οὐκ", "eis" in Latin letters for "εἰς", and a stray apostrophe inside "ἀποκατασταθῆναι"; none of these leaves the reading in doubt.)

For the statue cannot be lost while it remains in the hands of the same craftsman. Even if it is melted down again into raw material, it can be restored. But the ugly marks and damage can be lost, because they melt away. They cannot be restored.

## SR1-18
Source: Greek. Read: "οἱ εὐτελεῖς καὶ ἀφ’ ἑαυτῶν μὴ δυνάμενοι φαίνεσθαι ἐκ τοῦ ψέγειν τοὺς ἑαυτῶν κρείττονας δείκνυσθαι βούλονται." (The scan reads "dp’" for "ἀφ’", and the "25" before it is the edition's line number.)

Petty people, who cannot win notice on their own merits, try to make a show of themselves by finding fault with those better than they are.

Note on extent: this sentence has 16 words, but the request gives 20, and line 13704 goes on with the next sentence. If the passage is meant to run on, the next words, "τοῦτο πέπονθε πρῶτος Μεθόδιος", mean: "This is what happened first with Methodius". The sentence goes on to name him as bishop of the city in Lycia called Olympus.
