## The heart

Right heart for the subject. The sources show a bishop chosen by his people's demand (ANF 27818–27837), who led Carthage through persecution (EP 2972–2976), plague (MORT 301–302) and division (EP 6505–6511; SENT 105–109). They show his teaching of the one mother church (UNIT 363–365) and of each bishop judged by Christ alone (SENT 142–145). They show his refusal of Stephen's rule (EP 15426–15433), his death by the sword in 258 (KNOPF 4908–4911), and both sides of Africa claiming him (PETS 10206–10211; AUG 11416). The Founder or leader shape fits: what he built and how it ran come before the end, and the trial does not swallow the life.

Told in time. The essence ends at word 152 ("Later the Donatists and Augustine both claimed him."), inside about 150 words.

## Findings

No BLOCKING finding. The Do not say list is honoured throughout: "withdrew", not "fled"; no year for the plague, the book or the council; "put to the sword", not "beheaded"; "He said:" before Quote 3; "the question was whether"; Stephen "as Cyprian copies it"; the eyes covered by his own hand, the cloth tied by the two Julians; Rome's letter told in both halves, with no verdict.

**MINOR 1 (sentence 15, apply word for word or note).** Pontius's conditional is dropped, so "would have escaped that too" can be heard as "he could have escaped his death". Source: "this suffering he assuredly would have evaded as usual, if he had evaded it before" (ANF 27906–27908).
- Change: "and would have escaped that too" → "and would have run from that too, had fear made him run before".

**MINOR 2 (sentence 55, apply word for word or note).** "A table for God's altar" makes the table something that belongs to an altar. The Latin's table *is* the altar: "mensa Deo constructa est ... in qua sacrificium Deo ... offeratur" (PL38 99282–99286).
- Change: "a table for God's altar now stood" → "a table built for God, an altar, now stood".

Notes, no change: sentence 43, "last letter", holds by content, not by an old label. Sentence 51, "From the 310s", rests on the census's Widely Accepted date.
