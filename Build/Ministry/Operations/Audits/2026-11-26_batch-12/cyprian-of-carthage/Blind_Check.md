# Verdict

## 1. Which is better
X is slightly better. Both are strong and close to the standard. X keeps its story's question (the fight with "poisons") running clean to the death at sea, and handles the witnesses that disagree better than Y. Y is richer, but it drops its central quarrel without resolving it and ends on a later writer.

## 2. Faults

### X
1. "The monks of Jerome's monastery had complained for a year that no one celebrated the sacraments, the church's holy rites, for them, he explains, and he acted from the fear of God, to provide what the church needed." It runs to about 40 words, and "for them, he explains" is hard to follow on one hearing. Breaks: "Aim to keep sentences under about 30 words". Moderate.
2. "He copied one opponent's own letter whole, so that no one could say he answered from report alone." The "so that" gives a motive. It needs a line from the sources behind it, or it is a reason added. Breaks: "Every claim, date, and 'so' or 'because' stands on a line from one of them." Moderate, unless the outline supports it.
3. "Later writers, from Augustine to a council at Nicaea four centuries after Epiphanius, took his book as their list." This overstates what the council did. Later the text says Nicaea used the list as an argument about images, and did not take it as its own list. Breaks: "a date or detail moved onto the wrong event" (the four faults). Minor.
4. Paragraph 2 packs in the meaning of heresy, his life, the eighty heresies, his later influence and the Origen fight. That is a heavy load for one hearing. Breaks: "Let time, place and tradition come in a clause at a time." Minor.
5. "Then Theophilus, bishop of Alexandria, wrote…" Several years pass between the curtain episode and Theophilus's letter, and "Then" hides the gap. Breaks: "Link ideas with cause, contrast and time". Minor.
6. "A book written to answer the council's argument replied with his own letter…" The book is never said to be anything more specific. Breaks: "Explain it or leave it out." Minor.
7. Around ten names (Adam, Socrates, Sozomen, John Chrysostom and others). Most of them earn their place. Breaks: "Past about six names, ask whether each is needed." Minor.

### Y
1. Y never carries the Stephen quarrel through to an outcome. It stops at the council speech and cuts to the arrest, and the threatened break in communion is missing. Breaks: "Keep the story's own question alive… carried through to where it resolves" and "never soften the record by leaving out its force or conflict." Moderate.
2. The text closes on three "he said"s from Augustine's sermon. Breaks: "End on the subject itself… on a later writer only when that is the story's point." Moderate.
3. "He would not accept the ruling of Stephen… and in 258 he was put to the sword." Putting these side by side implies that the dispute caused the execution. Breaks: "Every… 'so' or 'because' stands on a line" (an implied cause). Minor.
4. "So he withdrew for a while, thinking less of his own safety than of his fellow Christians' peace…" Cyprian's praise of his own motive starts a new sentence, so it slips out of his voice and into the narrator's. Breaks: "a judgment of praise or blame… stays in its witness's voice". Minor.
5. "as that rival rose, Cyprian wrote a book on the church's unity." The dating of this book is probably a modern reconstruction. Breaks: "A date… that only a modern work makes… stays out". Minor, unless the outline supports it.
6. "a presbyter and a subdeacon, both named Julian" names two people who appear nowhere else. Breaks: "Name a person only when they act… give anyone else by role." Minor.
7. "Communion meant belonging to the church and sharing its bread and cup." It is dropped in on its own, between "Cyprian spoke first" and his words. Breaks: "work any puzzling word… into the sentence as part of what happens." Minor.
8. "None of us sets himself up as a bishop of bishops" is aimed at Stephen, but the text never says so. Breaks: "Explain it or leave it out." Minor.
9. "With councils of African bishops he held that there is one baptism because the church is one, and none outside it, so…" At about 32 words, it is unclear what "none outside it" refers to. Breaks: the sentence-length aim and "Plain modern words". Minor.
10. "ordered Christians before the magistrates to sacrifice" is a garbled phrase. "an enemy of the Roman gods whose blood would make the law hold" is murky. "his gardens" is never explained. Breaks: "Plain modern words". Minor.

## 3. Grades
- **X: A−.** Clean, faithful, and well sourced where its witnesses disagree. It loses marks for one long, tangled sentence and a "so that" that may lack support.
- **Y: B+.** Vivid and fair to Stephen. It loses marks because its central quarrel never resolves, it closes through a later writer, and it carries several clumsy phrases.


Key: X = Epiphanius of Salamis (reference), Y = Cyprian of Carthage.
