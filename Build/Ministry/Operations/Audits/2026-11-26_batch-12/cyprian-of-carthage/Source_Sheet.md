## 1. Setting

S1. **Carthage, Roman Africa.** He signs the council "Cyprianus a Cartagine dixit" (SENT 1514). The council met with bishops "ex prouincia Africa Numidia Mauritania" (SENT 107–108). Census `placeLabel` Carthage, modern country Tunisia (CEN). OWN. Documented. Plain words: "Carthage, the great city of Roman Africa, in today's Tunisia".

S2. **Time.** Bishop before the persecution of 250 (he writes of withdrawing "orto statim turbationis impetu primo", at the first outbreak, EP 2971–2972). Death 14 September 258 (E12). Year of becoming bishop: no ancient source gives it. ANF-ed gives 248 (ANF 27522–27523); Eusebius's editor "247 or 248" (EUS 39310); Benson reckons from Ep. 59's "in episcopatu quadriennio iam probatus", four years already a bishop (EP 9662–9663), by his own dating of that letter. Dominant Modern Reconstruction. Census `dates` "248-258" places it on the map only. Plain words: "in the middle of the third century", or "before 250".

S3. **Tradition.** Census `toldWithin` `latin-pastoral-congregational-christianity`; its `informalName`, the spoken name, is "The Latin Pastoral Congregations" (CEN). Its `relationsSummary`: "The churches led by Cyprian at Carthage and Augustine at Hippo. From the 310s a rival Donatist church stood in the same towns." Plain words: "the Latin-speaking congregations of Roman Africa".

S4. **Emperors.** Decius followed Philip and "commenced a persecution of the churches, in which Fabianus suffered martyrdom at Rome" (EUS 37383, 37413–37416). LATER. Documented. Year 250: EUS-ed gives Philip's death June 249 (EUS 37384–37385); 250 for the persecution is Widely Accepted and the tree already tells it so (CEN, `councils-of-rome-and-carthage-251`). Valerian and Gallienus: named in the *Acts* (KNOPF 4823–4826) and Ep. 80 (EP 17288–17289). Documented.

## 2. Events

**E1. Before his baptism: a teacher of rhetoric.** Jerome, Latin: "Cyprianus Afer, primum gloriose rhetoricam docuit; exinde suadente presbytero Caecilio, a quo et cognomentum sortitus est, Christianus factus, omnem substantiam suam pauperibus erogavit, ac post non multum temporis electus in presbyterum, etiam episcopus Carthaginiensis constitutus est" (JERL 57553–57558). English: "Cyprian of Africa, at first was famous as a teacher of rhetoric, and afterwards on the persuasion of the presbyter Caecilius, from whom he received his surname, he became a Christian, and gave all his substance to the poor. Not long after he was inducted into the presbytery, and was also made bishop of Carthage" (NPNF3 40815–40820). Jerome's *Chronicle*: "Cyprianus primum rhetor, deinde presbyter, ad extremum Carthaginiensis episcopus martyrio coronatur" (CHRON 44192–44194). Lactantius: "he had sought great glory to himself from the profession of the art of oratory" (L7 4474). LATER (about 50 and 135 years). Widely Accepted (two later witnesses agree). Pontius passes over it: "He may have had pursuits previously, and liberal arts may have imbued his mind while engaged therein; but these things I pass over" (ANF 27705–27708). ANF-ed: "highly educated for the profession of a rhetorician, is all that can be said with any degree of certainty" (ANF 27514–27516). Explain: rhetoric was the art of public speaking, taught to young men for the law courts and public life; plain words "a teacher of public speaking".

**E2. Conversion.** Pontius: a presbyter "by name Cæcilius … who had converted him from his worldly errors to the acknowledgment of the true divinity" (ANF 27802–27805). He gave away the price of whole estates: "by dispensing the purchase-money of entire estates" (ANF 27730–27732). CONTEMP, praise. Documented as Pontius's account. In his own words, *To Donatus*: "Ego cum in tenebris atque in nocte caeca iacerem … difficile prorsus ac durum pro illis tunc moribus opinabar … ut quis renasci denuo posset" (DON 194–199): while he lay in darkness and blind night, he thought it hard and harsh, given his habits then, that anyone could be born again. OWN. Documented. Year of baptism: "conversion a.d. 246" (ANF 27513–27514); Benson "We may perhaps assume" 246 (BEN 2628). Dominant Modern Reconstruction: no year in the story.

**E3. Chosen bishop while still a new Christian.** Pontius: "by the judgment of God and the favour of the people, he was chosen to the office of the priesthood and the degree of the episcopate while still a neophyte, and, as it was considered, a novice" (ANF 27818–27821; Latin H3 40554 "adhuc neophytus et ut putabatur nouellus"). "When the entire people by God's inspiration leapt forward in his love and honour, he humbly withdrew, giving place to men of older standing … A crowded fraternity was besieging the doors of the house, and throughout all the avenues of access an anxious love was circulating" (ANF 27826–27837). "Some resisted him, even that he might overcome them … reckoning them afterwards, to the astonishment of many, among his closest and, most intimate friends!" (ANF 27844–27848). CONTEMP, praise. Documented as Pontius's account. ANF-ed's "older presbyters, who saw themselves superseded" (ANF 27521–27523) is an editor's gloss on "some resisted": leave out. Explain: a neophyte is a newly baptized Christian; Paul's letter (1 Tim 3:6) bars a neophyte from the office of bishop, and Jerome's own text in H3 quotes "non neophytus" (H3 9345); the story need not cite it. Plain words: "still a new Christian".

**E4. Under Decius: hunted, and he withdraws.** His own account to the clergy at Rome: "cum me clamore uiolento frequenter populus flagitasset, non tam meam salutem quam quietem fratrum publicam cogitans interim secessi, ne per inuerecundam praesentiam nostram seditio quae coeperat plus prouocaretur. absens tamen corpore nec spiritu nec actu nec monitis meis defui" (EP Ep. 20.1, 2972–2976). ANF: "the people with violent clamour repeatedly demanded me, I, taking into consideration not so much my own safety as the public peace of the brethren, withdrew for a while, lest, by my over-bold presence, the tumult which had begun might be still further provoked. Nevertheless, although absent in body, I was not wanting either in spirit, or in act, or in my advice" (ANF 30180–30185). "these thirteen letters sent forth at various times declare to you" (ANF 30188–30189; EP 2983–2984 "epistulae … numero tredecim"). Of the same years, to Cornelius: "in tempestate proscriptus, adplicito et adiuncto episcopatus sui nomine, totiens ad leonem petitus, in circo in amphitheatro" (EP Ep. 59.6, 9664–9666): in the storm proscribed, with the name of his office added, so often demanded for the lion in the circus and the amphitheatre. To Florentius Puppianus, the public notice: "cum publice legeretur: SI QVIS TENET POSSIDET DE BONIS CAECILI CVPRIANI EPISCOPI CHRISTIANORVM" (EP Ep. 66.4, 12232–12234); ANF "If any one holds or possesses any of the property of Cæcilius Cyprian, bishop of the Christians" (ANF 37811–37813). Pontius: "with repeated calls he was frequently demanded for the lions" (ANF 27879–27880). OWN and CONTEMP. Documented. Explain: to be proscribed was to have one's name posted publicly and one's property made forfeit; the notice called on anyone holding his goods to declare them. Plain words: "he went into hiding". Note: the `latin-pastoral` live story tells his withdrawal to keep the peace and his rule by letter; here it is the setting of the charge, told in new words.

**E5. The charge it brought.** The clergy at Rome, whose bishop Fabianus had been killed (EUS 37414–37415), wrote to the clergy of Carthage, not to Cyprian. They had learned that he "has for a certain reason withdrawn; 'in doing which he acted quite rightly, because he is a person of eminence, and because a conflict is impending'" (ANF 28797–28801; Latin EP 1055–1056, 1077: "Didicimus secessisse benedictum Papatem Cyprianum … certa ex causa, quod utique recta fecerit, propterea cum sit persona insignis"). Then the good shepherd who lays down his life and "the hireling, whose own the sheep are not, seeth the wolf coming, and leaveth the sheep, and fleeth" (ANF 28817–28820; EP 1088–1092), and "We are unwilling, therefore, beloved brethren, that you should be found hirelings, but we desire you to be good shepherds" (ANF 28838–28840; EP 1099–1100). CONTEMP. Documented that they wrote both. **Whether it was a reproach is the editors' reading:** ANF-ed calls the passage "very obscure … intended to bear upon Cyprian's retirement" and "It seems, in a slight degree, to reflect on Cyprian's withdrawal" (ANF 28827–28833). Benson reads the "quite rightly" as the Carthage clergy's own justification, which the ANF English prints in quotation marks, and says such a sarcasm "might perhaps have seemed intelligible" only had Rome first heard the news from its own envoy, "But it was Carthage which had communicated both fact and justification" (BEN 6704–6713). The editors differ. Contested. His answer, Ep. 20.1: "Quoniam conperi, fratres carissimi, minus simpliciter et minus fideliter uobis renuntiari quae hic a nobis et gesta sunt et geruntur, necessarium duxi has ad uos litteras facere" (EP 2967–2969): having learned that what was done here was reported to you less than plainly and faithfully, I thought it necessary to write. ANF's "somewhat garbled and untruthful" (ANF 30175–30176) is stronger than the Latin. OWN. Documented. Pontius defends him at length, which shows the question stayed alive: "Do you wish to be assured that the cause of his withdrawal was not fear? to allege nothing else, he did suffer subsequently, and this suffering he assuredly would have evaded as usual, if he had evaded it before" (ANF 27905–27908). CONTEMP, defence. Documented. Explain (both sides): Rome's clergy had lost their bishop to the persecution and spoke of shepherds who stay; Cyprian said he left to keep the peace and kept leading by letter; Pontius said the later death proved it was not fear. Puppianus's charges against him (Ep. 66, ANF 37724–37733) are unnamed "things so wicked": leave out.

**E6. The lapsed (a clause).** "ultro ad forum currere, ad mortem sponte properare" (LAPS 349): they ran of their own accord to the forum. He would not settle their return alone. The `councils-of-rome-and-carthage-251` story tells all of it, with its own quotes. Documented. Plain words: "those who had given way".

**E7. Novatian (a clause) and the mother church.** "Venerunt ad nos … missi a Nouatiano … cum … Nouatianum episcopum factum conperissemus, inlicitae et contra ecclesiam catholicam factae ordinationis prauitate commoti a communicatione eos nostra statim cohibendos esse censuimus" (EP Ep. 44.1, 6186–6192). OWN. Documented. To Roman confessors who had sided with Novatian: they had consented "alium episcopum fieri … ecclesiam alteram institui, Christi membra discerpi" and he begs "ad matrem reuertamini unde prodistis" (EP Ep. 46.1, 6503–6511): another bishop made, a second church set up, Christ's limbs torn apart; come back to the mother you came from. OWN. Documented. The `novatian` story tells Novatian's side; here one clause.

**E8. On the Unity of the Catholic Church.** Occasion, in his own words: to the Roman confessors on their return to the church, "sed et catholicae ecclesiae unitatem quantum potuit expressit nostra mediocritas: quem libellum magis ac magis nunc uobis placere confido … siquidem quod nos uerbis conscripsimus uos factis inpletis, quando ad ecclesiam caritatis ac pacis unitate remeatis" (EP Ep. 54.4, 7397–7421): my small ability set out the unity of the catholic church; I trust the booklet pleases you more now, since what we wrote in words you fulfil in deeds, coming back to the church. OWN. Documented that the book was sent at the Novatian split. Year 251: ANF-ed heading "Written a.d. 251" (ANF 42105); Dominant Modern Reconstruction: say "in those months". The images: "quomodo solis multi radii sed lumen unum, et rami arboris multi sed robur unum tenaci radice fundatum … ab arbore frange ramum, fractus germinare non poterit: a fonte praecide riuum, praecisus arescit" (UNIT 341–347). "episcopatus unus est, cuius a singulis in solidum pars tenetur" (UNIT 339–340): the office of bishop is one, and each bishop holds his share of it in full. "una mater fecunditatis successibus copiosa: illius fetu nascimur, illius lacte nutrimur, spiritu eius animamur" (UNIT 352–354; ANF 42310–42312 "she is one mother … from her womb we are born, by her milk we are nourished, by her spirit we are animated"). Chapter 6: "alienus est, profanus est, hostis est. habere non potest Deum patrem qui ecclesiam non habet matrem" (UNIT 363–365; ANF 42320–42322 "He is a stranger; he is profane; he is an enemy. He can no longer have God for his Father, who has not the Church for his mother"; "no longer" is not in the Latin). OWN, clean TEI, confirmed by the 1868 English. Documented. **Quote 1.** Explain: by "the church" he means the one body of Christians in communion with their lawful bishops; one who leaves it for a rival bishop is, to him, outside. The other side, from the same years: Novatian's party held their own bishop and church (E7; `novatian` story).

**E9. The plague.** Pontius: "Afterwards there broke out a dreadful plague … All were shuddering, fleeing, shunning the contagion, impiously exposing their own friends" (ANF 27939–27944; H3 40783 "lues dira"). He gathered the people and taught "that there was nothing wonderful in our cherishing our own people only … but that he might become perfect who would do something more than the publican or the heathen … loved even his enemies" (ANF 27957–27966); "Many who, by the straitness of poverty, were unable to manifest the kindness of wealth, manifested more than wealth, making up by their own labour" (ANF 27984–27987); "good was done … to all men, not to those only who are of the household of faith" (ANF 27990–27992). CONTEMP, praise. Documented as Pontius's account. (The `latin-pastoral` live story tells this Pontius passage; here, one clause at most.) Date: Jerome's *Chronicle* sets the plague under Gallus, citing "Cypriani de mortalitate … liber" (CHRON 43963–43967); ANF-ed "a.d. 252" (ANF 46658–46663). Widely Accepted for the early 250s; say "a few years later". *On the Mortality*, his own address: why he wrote it: "At enim quosdam mouet quod aequaliter cum gentilibus nostros morbi istius ualitudo corripiat" (MORT 301–302): some are troubled that the disease seizes our people just as it does the pagans. The disease: "solutus in fluxum uenter … in faucium uulnera conceptus medullitus ignis … adsiduo uomitu … oculi ui sanguinis inardescunt … pedes uel aliquae membrorum partes … amputantur" (MORT 498–502). Chapter 16: "pestis ista et lues quae horribilis et feralis uidetur explorat iustitiam singulorum et mentes humani generis examinat, an infirmis seruiant sani, an propinqui cognatos pie diligant, an misereantur seruorum languentium domini, an deprecantes aegros non deserant medici" (MORT 584–588; ANF 47052–47057 "searches out the righteousness of each one … whether they who are in health tend the sick; whether relations affectionately love their kindred; whether masters pity their languishing servants; whether physicians do not forsake the beseeching patients"). OWN, clean TEI, confirmed by the English. Documented. **Quote 2.** Also: "exercitia sunt nobis ista, non funera" (MORT 595–596; ANF 47066 "These are trainings for us, not deaths"); "non amitti sed praemitti" (MORT 700; ANF 47145 "not lost, but sent before"), with the warning not to put on black for them, lest pagans rightly blame Christians (MORT 702–705). Card. **Note:** the care for pagans is in Pontius only; *On the Mortality* asks about the healthy and the sick, kin, masters and doctors, and names no pagans (D5).

**E10. The quarrel with Stephen of Rome over baptism.**
- *His side.* To Quintus: some colleagues say there is one baptism; "quod unum scilicet in ecclesia catholica est, quia ecclesia una est et esse baptisma praeter ecclesiam non potest … nos autem dicimus eos qui inde ueniunt non rebaptizari apud nos sed baptizari. neque enim accipiunt illic aliquid ubi nihil est" (EP Ep. 71.1, 14138–14139, 14144–14145, 14169). ANF: "there cannot be any baptism out of the Church … But we say that those who come thence are not re-baptized among us, but are baptized. For indeed they do not receive anything there, where there is nothing" (ANF 38173–38174, 38183–38185). OWN. Documented. To Stephen, with a council of 71 bishops: "unum baptisma esse quod sit in ecclesia catholica constitutum ac per hoc non rebaptizari sed baptizari a nobis quicumque ab adultera et profana aqua uenientes" (EP Ep. 72.1, 14488–14492; ANF 38443–38450). And: "qua in re nec nos uim cuiquam facimus aut legem damus, quando habeat in ecclesiae administratione uoluntatis suae arbitrium liberum unusquisque praepositus, rationem actus sui Domino redditurus" (EP Ep. 72.3, 14439–14442): we force no one and lay down no law, since each bishop has free choice in running his church and will answer to the Lord. OWN. Documented. The question began with Novatian's followers: a layman asked whether those "qui a Nouatiano ueniunt post profanum eius lauacrum" must be baptized in the catholic church (EP Ep. 69.1, 13133–13136). He cites an older African council under Agrippinus (EP Ep. 73.3, 14546–14549; Ep. 71.4, 14273–14276). Explain: baptism, the washing that makes a Christian, was given only once; the question was whether a washing given by a group outside the church counted.
- *Stephen's side, as Cyprian quotes it.* "si qui ergo a quacumque haeresi uenient ad uos, nihil innouetur nisi quod traditum est, ut manus illis inponatur in paenitentiam, cum ipsi haeretici proprie alterutrum ad se uenientes non baptizent, sed communicent tantum" (EP Ep. 74.1, 15430–15433; ANF 39094–39096). Cyprian calls the letter "uel superba uel ad rem non pertinentia uel sibi ipsi contraria" (EP 15428) and Stephen's view his "errorem" (EP 15426). Stephen's own letter is lost; only this extract is held. OWN, hostile. Documented as quoted. Eusebius: "Stephen considering it unnecessary to add any innovation contrary to the tradition which had been held from the beginning, was very indignant at this" (EUS 39312–39314). Vincent, from Stephen's side (about 434): Agrippinus "was the first who held it—that Baptism ought to be repeated"; Stephen's rule "Let there be no innovation—nothing but what has been handed down" (VIN 12513–12515, 12527–12528). LATER. Explain: Stephen held that the custom handed down was to receive such people with the laying on of hands for repentance, not a new washing, and that the heretics themselves did not wash again those who came over to them. The census edge `latin-pastoral-congregational-christianity--roman-church-third-century` paraphrases this exchange (CEN); its quotes are the ANF English, not used as quotes here.
- *Stephen's response, by a witness on Cyprian's side.* Firmilian, bishop of Caesarea in Cappadocia, to Cyprian: Stephen received the African envoy bishops so "patienter satis et leniter" that he would not admit them even to talk, and told the brothers "ne quis eos in domum suam reciperet, ut uenientibus non solum pax et communio sed et tectum et hospitium negaretur" (EP Ep. 75.25, 16669–16673; ANF 40048–40053 "not only peace and communion, but also a shelter and entertainment, were denied to them"). Stephen "de episcopatus sui loco gloriatur et se successionem Petri tenere contendit" (EP 16447–16448). Stephen called Cyprian "pseudochristum et pseudoapostolum et dolosum operarium" (EP 16709–16710; ANF 40088–40089 "a false Christ and a false apostle, and a deceitful worker"). And to Stephen: "dum enim putas omnes a te abstineri posse, solum te ab omnibus abstinuisti" (EP 16636–16637): while you think all can be cut off by you, you have cut yourself off from all. CONTEMP, hostile to Stephen. Documented as Firmilian's report. Credit him.
- *The council of 1 September.* Title: "SENTENTIAE EPISCOPORVM NVMERO LXXXVII DE HAERETICIS BAPTIZANDIS" (SENT 105); met "kalendis Septembribus … ex prouincia Africa Numidia Mauritania cum presbyteris et diaconibus, praesentibus etiam plebis maxima parte" (SENT 107–109). His opening: "superest ut de hac ipsa re singuli quid sentiamus proferamus neminem iudicantes aut a iure communicationis aliquem si diuersum senserit amouentes. neque enim quisquam nostrum episcopum se episcoporum constituit aut tyrannico terrore ad obsequendi necessitatem collegas suos adigit, quando habeat omnis episcopus pro licentia libertatis et potestatis suae arbitrium proprium tamque iudicari ab alio non possit, quam nec ipse possit alterum iudicare. sed expectemus uniuersi iudicium Domini nostri Iesu Christi" (SENT 121, 138–145). English, in Augustine: "It remains that we severally declare our opinion on this subject, judging no one, nor depriving any one of the right of communion if he differ from us. For no one of us sets himself up as a bishop of bishops, or, by tyrannical terror, forces his colleagues to a necessity of obeying" (AUG 11292–11293). OWN, clean TEI, confirmed by the 1887 English. Documented. **Quote 3.** Year: 256 by most (VIN-ed 12550–12551 "held under the presidency of Cyprian in 256"); Dominant Modern Reconstruction; the record gives only the day. Whether "bishop of bishops" aimed at Stephen: D7.
- *How it ended.* No settlement between the two is held. Pontius calls Stephen's successor "Xistus, the good and peace-making priest" (ANF 28137–28138). EUS-ed: Xystus "seems to have adopted a more conciliatory tone" (modern; card). Documented that no settlement is held.

**E11. Exile, 257.** The *Acts*: in the consulships of Valerian (fourth) and Gallienus (third), 30 August, the proconsul Paternus says the emperors ordered "eos, qui Romanam religionem non colunt, debere Romanas caeremonias recognoscere"; "Cyprianus episcopus dixit: Christianus sum et episcopus. nullos alios deos noui, nisi unum et uerum deum … hunc deprecamur diebus ac noctibus pro nobis et pro omnibus hominibus et pro incolumitate ipsorum imperatorum" (KNOPF 4823–4832; GEB 6480–6497). Asked for his presbyters: "Legibus uestris bene atque utiliter censuistis delatores non esse" (KNOPF 4839–4840). Sent to Curubis: "exul ad urbem Curubitanam proficisci? … Proficiscor" (KNOPF 4835–4836). CONTEMP. Documented. Explain: the Roman rites were the sacrifices to the gods of the state; he would not take part, and he would not name his clergy because, he said, their own laws forbade informers. The dream at Curubis is the `pontius-the-deacon` story's (Leave out).

**E12. 258: the last letter, the trial and the death.**
- *The new order.* Valerian wrote to the senate "ut episcopi et presbyteri et diacones in continenti animaduertantur"; Xistus of Rome was put to death "in cimiterio … VIII. id. Aug." with four deacons (EP Ep. 80.1, 17288–17289, 17322–17324). OWN. Documented.
- *The last letter.* "commentarios esse missos qui me Vticam perducerent et consilio carissimorum persuasum esset ut de hortis nostris interim secederem, iusta interueniente causa consensi, eo quod congruat episcopum in ea ciuitate in qua ecclesiae dominicae praeest illic Dominum confiteri et plebem uniuersam praepositi praesentis confessione clarificare" (EP Ep. 81, 17370–17375; ANF 41230–41238). "expectamus ergo hic in secessu abdito … aduentum proconsulis Cartaginem redeuntis" (EP 17383–17385). OWN. Documented. Pontius: friends "repeatedly urged his withdrawal … offered places to which he might retire. But he … did not consent" (ANF 28143–28149). CONTEMP. They differ; credit each (D3).
- *The trial.* Taken at his gardens on the Ides of September, consuls Tuscus and Bassus (KNOPF 4857–4858); kept overnight in an officer's house, "illuc uniuersus populus fratrum conuenit … custodiri puellas praecepit" (KNOPF 4877–4881). Next day before Galerius Maximus: "Tu es Thascius Cyprianus? … Ego sum. … Tu papam te sacrilegae mentis hominibus praebuisti? … Ego. … Iusserunt te sacratissimi imperatores caeremoniari. … Non facio. … Consule tibi! … Fac, quod tibi praeceptum est. in re tam iusta nulla est consultatio" (KNOPF 4890–4899; GEB 6593–6602). The sentence: "Diu sacrilega mente uixisti et plurimos nefariae tibi conspirationis homines adgregasti et inimicum te diis Romanis et religionibus sacris constituisti … sanguine tuo sancietur disciplina … Thascium Cyprianum gladio animaduerti placet. Cyprianus episcopus dixit: Deo gratias" (KNOPF 4902–4911; GEB 6604–6626). CONTEMP. Documented. Explain (the Roman side, in its own words): he had lived long with an impious mind, gathered many into a wicked conspiracy, set himself against the Roman gods and rites, would not be called back by the emperors; his blood would make the law hold. The `latin-pastoral` live story gives this sentence in paraphrase; here it is told briefly and in its own terms.
- *The death.* "turba fratrum dicebat: Et nos cum ipso decollemur" (KNOPF 4913–4915; GEB 6628–6629). At the field of Sextus he took off his cloak, knelt and prayed, gave his tunic to the deacons, stood in his linen; "iussit suis, ut eidem speculatori uiginti quinque aureos darent"; the brothers set cloths before him; "beatus Cyprianus manu sua oculos sibi texit. qui cum lacinias manuales ligare sibi non potuisset, Iulianus presbyter et Iulianus subdiaconus ei ligauerunt" (KNOPF 4917–4927; GEB 6629–6643). The body was laid nearby because of the pagans' curiosity, then carried by night "cum cereis et scolacibus … in uia Mappaliensi … cum uoto et triumpho magno" (KNOPF 4929, 4950–4952; GEB 6643–6647). Date: "Passus est … die octaua decima kalendarum Octobrium" (KNOPF 4955–4956), 14 September; the year from the consuls; Widely Accepted 258. CONTEMP. Documented. Pontius: people "had climbed up into the branches of the trees"; "having with his own hands bound his eyes, he tried to hasten the slowness of the executioner … who with difficulty clasped the blade in his failing right hand with trembling fingers, until … strengthened … with power granted from above" (ANF 28257–28266; H3 41353–41355). CONTEMP, praise. Documented as Pontius's. Pontius: "the first who in Africa imbued his priestly crown with blood of martyrdom … from the time at which the episcopal order is enumerated at Carthage, not one is ever recorded … to have come to suffering" (ANF 28278–28285; H3 41369–41373 "in Africa primus … numquam aliquis … ad passionem uenisse memoratur"). Documented as Pontius's claim.

## 3. What came of him (heaviest first)

I1. **The Donatists claimed him, and Augustine answered.** "uos certe nobis obicere soletis Cypriani litteras, Cypriani sententiam, Cypriani concilium; cur auctoritatem Cypriani pro uestro schismate adsumitis et eius exemplum pro ecclesiae pace respuitis?" (PETS 10206–10211; AUG 11300 "You are wont, indeed, to bring up against us the letters of Cyprian, his opinion, his Council"). "Cease, then, to bring forward against us the authority of Cyprian in favor of repeating baptism, but cling with us to the example of Cyprian for the preservation of unity" (AUG 11416). Augustine quotes the "bishop of bishops" opening and says: "He allows me, therefore, without losing the right of communion, not only to continue inquiring into the truth, but even to hold opinions differing from his own … What could be more kind? what more humble?" (AUG 11626). "though he held a different view concerning baptism, he yet did not forsake the unity of the Church, and was in the Lord's vine a branch firmly rooted, bearing fruit, which the heavenly Husbandman purged with the knife of suffering, that it should bear more fruit" (AUG 11949). LATER, about 140 years. Documented. Census edge `latin-pastoral-congregational-christianity--donatism--2` agrees (CEN). Explain the Donatists: a rival church in Africa from the 310s that baptized again those who came over (CEN `donatism` `relationsSummary`). Defended (by him): the one church, and each bishop's freedom to judge in his own church, under Christ's judgment.

I2. **The West settled the question against his rule.** Council of Arles (314), canon 8: "De Afris quod propria lege sua utuntur ut rebaptizent, placuit, ut si ad ecclesiam aliquis de haeresi venerit, interrogent eum symbolum; et si perviderint eum in patre et filio et spiritu sancto esse baptizatum, manus ei tantum imponatur ut accipiat spiritum sanctum. Quod si interrogatus non responderit hanc trinitatem, baptizetur" (LAUCH 2708–2714; header "Concilium Arelatense primum", 2660). LATER, 56 years. Documented. Vincent, about 434: "The authors of this same doctrine are judged Catholics, the followers heretics; the teachers are absolved, the disciples condemned" (VIN 12563–12565), naming Cyprian among the saints and the Donatists among the condemned (VIN 12567–12572). LATER. Documented as Vincent's judgment. The tree has `council-of-arles-314`.

I3. **His feast at Carthage, and the table at the place he died.** Augustine, a sermon on his feast day: "Natale Cypriani martyris Africa tota percelebre" (PL38 99224–99225); "Quis enim hodie, non dicam in hac nostra civitate, sed plane per Africam totam transmarinasque regiones, non Christianus solum, sed Paganus, aut Judaeus, aut etiam haereticus poterit inveniri, qui non nobiscum dicat Natalem martyris Cypriani?" (PL38 99254–99258); "Quando natus sit, ignoramus; et quia hodie passus est, Natalem ejus hodie celebramus" (PL38 99258–99260). "in eodem loco mensa Deo constructa est; et tamen mensa dicitur Cypriani, non quia ibi est unquam Cyprianus epulatus, sed quia ibi est immolatus" (PL38 99282–99284; GAUME 101464–101466 confirms): in that same place a table was built to God, and yet it is called Cyprian's table, not because Cyprian ever feasted there, but because there he was offered up. "ut illa modo cingatur ab obsequentibus, ibi Cyprianus cingebatur a persequentibus" (PL38 99289–99290): as the table is now ringed by worshippers, there Cyprian was ringed by persecutors. LATER, about 140 years; a damaged OCR confirmed by a second print. Documented. Also: his mother spent a night "in a place quite close to our ship, where there was an oratory … in memory of the blessed Cyprian" (CONF 8103–8104); Morin's sermon, the people asking him to stay till "diem natalis beati Cypriani" (MORIN 17204–17212). In 533: "Cyprian, a holy man, is reverenced above all others by the Carthaginians … a very noteworthy temple in his honour before the city on the sea-shore … a festival which they call the 'Cypriana'"; the Vandals took it "in the reign of Honoric" (PROC 2206–2216). LATER. Documented. Explain: the "birthday" of a martyr was the day of death, kept as a feast (PL38 99258–99260).

I4. **Read and praised.** Lactantius: "Cyprianus, therefore, was above all others distinguished and renowned" (L7 4472–4474), and adds that a learned man mocked him as "Coprianus" (L7 4474). Jerome: his works "are more conspicuous than the sun" (NPNF3 40820–40821; JERL 57559–57560 "sole clariora"). Pontius: "he will probably never cease to speak even to the end of the world" (ANF 27670–27671). ANF-ed calls the *Unity* "a key to the entire writings of Cyprian" (ANF 42107–42108, modern). Card.

I5. **Tertullian (link).** Jerome's report that he read Tertullian daily and asked for "the master" belongs to `tertullian-s-voice`, whose outline uses it as a quote; barred here.

Who keeps his feast today: no held source and no census tradition entry says. Leave out.

## 5. Where sources disagree or come late

D1. **The Roman clergy's letter.** It both says he acted rightly and speaks of hirelings who flee (E5). Whether it reproached him is the editors' reading (ANF-ed "very obscure", "in a slight degree"; Benson reads it as reporting Carthage's own justification, and doubts a sarcasm). Contested. The letter's own "quite rightly" stands beside the hireling lines. Tell what the letter says, both parts, and his answer.

D2. **Chapter 4 of the *Unity*, two forms.** Hartel's text lacks "primatus Petro datur" and "cathedra Petri" and prints them in the notes as added in some manuscripts (UNIT 279–286, 315–320). ANF-ed: "falsifying interpolations, long since exposed and detected" (ANF 42113–42115). Koch reports Chapman's view that Cyprian wrote both forms, and doubts it (KOCH 8065–8100). Contested. Card; the quote is from chapter 6.

D3. **His last hiding.** His own letter: he left his gardens for a while, so as not to be taken to Utica, and to confess in his own city (EP 17370–17375). Pontius: friends offered places to withdraw, and he did not consent (ANF 28143–28149). Credit each.

D4. **How his eyes were bound.** The *Acts*: he covered his eyes with his own hand and two Julians tied the cloths when he could not (KNOPF 4925–4927). Pontius: "with his own hands bound his eyes" (ANF 28260–28261). Credit the *Acts* for the scene; Pontius's strengthening "from above" stays in his voice.

D5. **Who urged care for the pagan sick.** Pontius only (ANF 27961–27966, 27990–27992). *On the Mortality* names no pagans (MORT 584–588). Credit Pontius.

D6. **Who first held that heretics must be baptized.** Eusebius: "First of all, Cyprian" (EUS 39310–39311). Cyprian: an African council under Agrippinus, long before (EP 14546–14549). Vincent: Agrippinus "the first who held it" (VIN 12513–12515). Augustine names Agrippinus too (AUG 11417). Card; the story says only that Cyprian cited an older African council, if room.

D7. **"Bishop of bishops": aimed at Stephen or not.** Benson reads it against one who would make himself a bishop of bishops (BEN 21452–21461, modern); Augustine reads it as kindness and humility (AUG 11626). Contested. The story tells the words at the council, in the months of the quarrel, and does not say they named Stephen.

D8. **The manner and day of death in a later writer.** Theodoret says he "found a death in the fire" (NPNF3 36796–36798); his editor gives "Aug. 13, 258" (NPNF3 36798). Against the *Acts*. Leave out.

D9. **"Two years after baptism."** No ancient source; editors' 246 and 248 (E2, S2). Say "while still a new Christian".
