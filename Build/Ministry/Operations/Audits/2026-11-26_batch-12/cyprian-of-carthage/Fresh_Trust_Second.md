Dropping "for a set reason" loses nothing of the letter's force. The sentence reports what the letter says and gives no verdict on the contested reading (Source_Sheet D1).
7. "Pontius later wrote that fear was not the cause: he did suffer afterwards, and would have escaped that too." **SUMMARY HOLDS, MINOR.** ANF 27905–27908 "Do you wish to be assured that the cause of his withdrawal was not fear? … he did suffer subsequently, and this suffering he assuredly would have evaded as usual, if he had evaded it before"; Latin H3 40729 "quam passionem utique ex more uitaret, si et ante uitasset" (`cyprian_opera-spuria-vita-pontius-lat_hartel-csel3-pars3.txt`). The outline holds this wording word for word. Pontius's point is a condition: if he had fled from fear before, he would have fled this too. "Too" carries the condition only by implication. Optional exact change, for clarity on one hearing: "Pontius later wrote that fear was not the cause: he did suffer afterwards, and had he fled from fear before, he would have escaped that too." Not blocking: the meaning is there and the outline holds the line.
