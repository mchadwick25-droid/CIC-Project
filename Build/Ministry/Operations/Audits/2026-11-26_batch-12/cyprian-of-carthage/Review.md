## Verdict: REVISE

## BLOCKING findings

**B1. The charge (Trust; Trust, second; both listeners in part). ASSUMPTION ADDED. Needs the outline; heart wording (report).**
- "His going brought a charge against him: a shepherd had left his flock."
- Trust: no old source gives a charge in these terms. Rome's letter says he withdrew "certa ex causa, quod utique recta fecerit" (EP 1055–1077) and urges Carthage's own clergy "Nolumus ergo … mercennarios inueniri" (EP 1099–1100); whether the hireling lines aimed at Cyprian is Contested (D1). The one old witness to a charge gives it as fear: "Do you wish to be assured that the cause of his withdrawal was not fear?" (Pontius, ANF 27905–27906). Second trust: the sentence sets Rome's hireling passage up as the accusation, against the outline's own Do not say ("rebuked him").
- Listeners: both heard the hireling passage as the charge and could not tell whether Rome defended or blamed him (Listen_FirstTime 3a; Listen_Skeptic 3a).
- Change: cut the sentence. Tell the charge as its witness gives it, at the end of the Rome paragraph (the outline's Beat 2 order): "Pontius later wrote that fear was not the cause: he did suffer afterwards, and would have escaped that too." (ANF 27905–27908.) This line is Cut first 14, sent to the card, so the researcher restores it at the outline first. The heart's "Also told" holds "faced the charge that a shepherd had fled", the same error: heart wording (report), for Mark.
- Do not say (add to the outline): "a charge that a shepherd had fled / left his flock", or any wording of the charge that makes Rome's hireling passage the accusation → the charge as Pontius gives it, fear, with his answer.

**B2. "For a set reason" (both listeners). Fails on one hearing, loses the story's question. Needs the outline.**
- "The clergy at Rome wrote to Carthage's clergy that he had withdrawn for a set reason, and had done rightly."
- First-time: "'For a set reason' says nothing to me." Skeptic: "names no reason", and the shepherd image then seemed to cut against the defense.
- Judged: blocking in part. The letter's two halves are the record (D1), and telling both with no verdict is right; what loses the listener is the charge sentence before it (B1) and a reason named without its content. With B1 fixed and these words cut, the paragraph says plainly what the letter does.
- Change: "...that he had withdrawn, and had done rightly." Beat 2 holds "for a set reason", so the outline changes with it. (If the reason is wanted, the source's own, "since he was a man of standing, with a struggle at hand", is Cut first 19, on the card: about +6 words.)
- Do not say: "for a set reason" with no reason given.

**B3. The plague's date (Trust; Trust, second disagrees). ASSUMPTION ADDED. Needs the outline.**
- "A few years later a dreadful plague swept the city."
- Trust: Pontius gives only "Afterwards there broke out a dreadful plague … over the whole city" (ANF 27939–27946); Jerome's *Chronicle* sets it in the same Olympiad entry as Novatian (CHRON 43961–43970). Second trust marked it SUMMARY HOLDS because the outline rates it Widely Accepted.
- Judged by the bar: it blocks. The second read rests on the outline, not on a source line; the gap of "a few years" after Novatian's rise stands on no old witness, and the outline's own Do not say bars "the plague of 252" as editorial, so a relative date built on that reckoning is not Widely Accepted. No third reader has shown it holds.
- Change: "Afterwards a dreadful plague swept the city." (Skeptic hears "swept" as a stock verb; not a Part 1 word, so MINOR: "broke out over the whole city" is Pontius's own, +3.)
- Do not say: "a few years later" for the plague → "afterwards" (Beat 4's date line, and the replacement list in the outline's date Do not say, change with it).

**B4. "He began:" (Trust; Trust, second held the quote's words only). SOFTENED. Needs the outline.**
- "He began: 'It remains for each of us to say what we think on this matter. …'"
- Trust: he opened on the letters of Jubaianus, "Cyprianus dixit: audistis, collegae dilectissimi, quid mihi Iubaianus … scripserit" (SENT 111–113); the quoted words come after (SENT 121), and "It remains" shows it. Checked against the Petschenig text: the quote follows the reading of Jubaianus's letters.
- Change: "He said:" Beat 5 prescribes "He began:", so it changes at the outline.
- Do not say: "He began:" before Quote 3 → "He said:".

**B5. A question asked only to answer it (both listeners). Tell. Needs the outline.**
- "But when someone washed by a group outside the church came over to it, did that washing count?"
- Both: the next sentences answer it at once (Listen_FirstTime 6; Listen_Skeptic 6b).
- Change, in the outline's own words from Words to explain: "The question was whether a washing given by a group outside the church counted when the one washed came over to it." Beat 5 holds the question form.
- Do not say: "did that washing count?" as a question the story then answers → "the question was whether…".

**B6. Held-back name (Skeptic blocking; first-time MINOR). Tell. Needs the outline.**
- "…soon after this man's baptism… besieged the doors of Cyprian's house…"
- Skeptic: "this man" holds the name back a sentence when nothing needs it. First-time: "I nearly missed that this was him."
- Judged: blocking. Part 1 names the held-back reveal, and the moment allows the name in sentence 1. The outline's Openings prescribe "this man" and "Name him in the second sentence".
- Change: "...soon after Cyprian's baptism the whole people of Carthage wanted him as their bishop. A crowd, he says, besieged the doors of his house, while he stepped back to give place to older men."
- Do not say: "this man" for Cyprian in the opening → his name in the first sentence.

**B7. The last letter and the gardens (both listeners). Loses the thread of the ending. Needs the outline.**
- "In 258, at his gardens, he learned he was to be taken from the city. In his last letter he says he left the gardens for a while… He was taken at his gardens…"
- First-time: taken where, and why; did he hide again; did he come back? Skeptic: "I lost track of where he was", and could not tell whether this answered the old charge or repeated it.
- The source holds what is missing: officers were sent "qui me Vticam perducerent" (EP 17370–17371), and he hid to wait for the governor's return to Carthage: "expectamus ergo hic in secessu abdito constituti aduentum proconsulis Cartaginem redeuntis" (EP 17381–17383). No old source says he came back to the gardens; the *Acts* say only that he was there when taken (KNOPF 4855–4860). "When he came back" (Skeptic's option) would be ASSUMPTION ADDED.
- Change: "In 258, at his gardens, he learned that officers were coming to take him to Utica, another city. In his last letter he says he left the gardens for a while, because a bishop should confess his faith in the city where he leads the church, before all his people. He was waiting in hiding, he wrote, for the governor's return to Carthage. In the end he was taken at his gardens, and next morning…" Utica is Cut first 26 (card) and the waiting line is not in the outline: the researcher adds both, with lines.
- Do not say: "taken from the city" with no place named; "left the gardens" followed by "taken at his gardens" with nothing between; and "he came back / returned to his gardens".

**B8. Laying on of hands (first-time blocking; Skeptic "half explained", MINOR). Explain it.**
- "Those who came over were to have hands laid on them for repentance, without a new washing…"
- First-time: "I do not know what laying on hands does, or why it could stand in for a washing."
- Judged: blocking. It is Stephen's whole rule, and the quarrel is to be told from both sides; the outline's gloss says what the act did, "received with hands laid on them", and the brief dropped it.
- Change: "Those who came over were to be received with hands laid on them for repentance, without a new washing…" From the outline's Words to explain; no outline change.
- Do not say: "hands laid on them" without "received".

## Disagreements judged not blocking

- **"Cyprian counted as heretics all who stood outside the one church"** (first-time blocking; Skeptic not). Not blocking: the sentence gives the word's meaning in his own terms, as the outline's Words to explain holds it, and the next sentence gives why it mattered for baptism.
- **Novatian set up with no reason** (both listeners, conditional on the sources). Not blocking: the outline's sources give the act and Cyprian's verdict on it, an "inlicitae … ordinationis" against the church (EP 6186–6192), which the brief tells through "lawful bishops". Part 1 says then to tell it as given. Novatian's own side is the sibling's, one clause here by the slate.
- **The Donatists, "a rival church", with no reason for the split** (Skeptic). Not blocking: the brief says what they were and what they did with his words, which is all they do in this story; the split itself is the `donatism` story's, and the outline holds no reason.

- **M7. "Whose blood would make the law hold"** (both: half caught). The sentence's own terms, which the outline keeps; noted, no change.
- **M8. When and where in the first three sentences** (both). Given in sentence 5; not lost. Noted.
- **M9. Unexplained words** (both): presbyter, subdeacon, pagans, martyr, altar, offered up, the cloth. Noted. A grown listener follows them in context.
- **M10. Summary lines in the essence** (Skeptic): "persecution, plague and division", "Later the Donatists and Augustine both claimed him". Heart wording (report); noted, no change.
