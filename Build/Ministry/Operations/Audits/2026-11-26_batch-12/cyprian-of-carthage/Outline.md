# Outline: Cyprian of Carthage

Kind: person (`kinds/person.md`). Items S, E, I, D are in `Source_Sheet.md`, which gives each passage verbatim with file and line; short names are listed at its head (EP, UNIT, MORT, SENT, KNOPF, GEB, ANF, AUG, PL38 and the rest). CEN = the census.

## The heart

- **The essence:** Cyprian, a teacher of public speaking made bishop of Carthage while still a new Christian, led the church there and the councils of Africa's bishops through persecution, plague and division (E1, E3, E10; SENT 107–108; Documented; "teacher of rhetoric" Widely Accepted, from Jerome and Lactantius). He taught that the church is one, a mother outside whom no one has God as Father, and that each bishop answers to Christ alone (E8, E10; UNIT 363–365; SENT 138–145; EP 14439–14442; Documented). He would not accept the ruling of Stephen, bishop of Rome, on baptism, and he was put to the sword in 258 (E10, E12; Documented). Later the Donatists and Augustine both claimed him (I1; Documented).
- **Also told:** what he built and how it ran. A bishop chosen by his people's demand who, hunted, led his church by letter from hiding, and faced the charge that a shepherd had fled (E3–E5). A book on the one church, written as a rival bishop rose at Rome (E7, E8). A sermon in the plague that turned the dying city into a test of who served whom (E9). Councils where each bishop said his mind, and a quarrel with Rome over whether a washing outside the church counted as baptism, told from both sides (E10). Then the end he chose: to confess and die before his own people (E11, E12).
- **What came of him:** below, ranked. Defended: the one church and its one baptism; each bishop's freedom to judge in his own church under Christ's judgment; his duty to confess in his own city.

## Setting

- **Time:** the middle of the third century. He was bishop before the persecution of 250 (S2; EP 2971–2972) and died on 14 September 258 (E12; KNOPF 4955–4956; the year from the consuls, Widely Accepted). The year he became bishop rests on editors' reckoning (S2; Dominant Modern Reconstruction): give none. Plain words: "in the middle of the third century".
- **Place:** Carthage, the great city of Roman Africa, today in Tunisia (S1; SENT 1514; CEN `modernCountries`). His last exile: Curubis, a town of the province (KNOPF 4835–4836). Plain words: "Carthage, in Roman Africa".
- **Tradition:** the tree's spoken name is "the Latin Pastoral Congregations" (S3; CEN `informalName`). Plain words: "the Latin-speaking congregations of Roman Africa".
- **Rulers:** the emperor Decius, from 250 (S4; EUS 37383, 37413–37416); the emperors Valerian and Gallienus in 257–258 (KNOPF 4823–4826; EP 17288–17289). Name Decius once; the others by role.

## Words to explain

- **rhetoric:** "public speaking", the art Lactantius calls oratory (E1; L7 4474). Say "a teacher of public speaking".
- **neophyte:** "a new Christian, newly baptized" (E3; ANF 27820). Never the word bare.
- **persecution under Decius:** the emperor's persecution, in which Christians were called before the magistrates to sacrifice to the gods; one who gave way 'obeyed his edict' (LAPS 345–351, 1050–1051; EUS 37413–37416; the `councils-of-rome-and-carthage-251` story tells the order). One clause.
- **proscribed:** his name and title posted in public, and his goods made forfeit: the notice asked anyone who held them to come forward (E4; EP 12232–12234; ANF 37811–37813).
- **hireling:** the hired man in the gospel who runs from the wolf and leaves the sheep, against the good shepherd who lays down his life (E5; ANF 28817–28820).
- **the lapsed:** "those who had given way", who sacrificed (E6).
- **rival bishop / schism:** Novatian, made a second bishop of Rome against the one already chosen; Cyprian calls it "another church set up" (E7; EP 6505–6506). Avoid the word schism.
- **communion:** belonging to the church and sharing its bread and cup; to cut someone off from communion is to bar him from both (E10; SENT 138–139). Gloss where Quote 3 is heard.
- **the Church as mother:** his image: Christians are born from her womb and fed with her milk (E8; UNIT 352–354). Give the image in the sentence before Quote 1.
- **baptism, and baptism outside the church:** baptism is the washing that makes a Christian, given once; the quarrel was whether a washing given by a group outside the church, a heretic's, counted (E10). In his terms, heretics are those who stand outside the one church (EP 13138; 14138–14139).
- **laying on of hands for repentance:** Stephen's rule: those who came over were received with hands laid on them for repentance, without a new washing (E10; EP 15430–15433).
- **council:** a meeting of bishops (E10; SENT 107–109).
- **bishop of bishops:** one bishop who sets himself over the rest and forces them to obey (Quote 3).
- **proconsul:** "the governor of the province" (E11, E12). Say "the governor".
- **the Roman rites:** the sacrifices to Rome's gods (E11; KNOPF 4826–4827).
- **birthday (Natalis):** a martyr's feast kept on the day of his death (I3; PL38 99258–99260).
- **Donatists:** a rival church in Africa from the 310s that baptized again those who came over to it (I1; CEN `donatism`).
- **Catholic:** "the main church" if used (I2).

## Shape

**Founder or leader.** What he built: a church held together around its bishop, and councils of Africa's bishops in which each spoke his own mind (E4, E10). How it ran: by letter from hiding, by the rule of one church and one baptism, by sermons in the plague (E4, E8, E9). Who it served: his people at Carthage, whom he would not leave to die elsewhere (E12). What outlasted him: his letters and council, claimed by both sides of the African split, and his feast at the place he died (I1, I3). The trap is titles in place of acts: each office comes with what he did in it. The record decides the order; the trial does not swallow the life.

## Openings

1. **Chosen: the crowd at his doors** (E3). His deacon, Pontius, says that soon after this man's baptism the whole people of Carthage wanted him as their bishop. A crowd besieged the doors of his house, while he stepped back to give place to older men (ANF 27826–27837). He was still a new Christian (ANF 27818–27821), and before that, Jerome says, a teacher of public speaking (NPNF3 40815–40816; JERL 57553–57554). Name him in the second sentence: "the doors of Cyprian's house". Then the essence. Pontius writes as an admirer: say so in the sentence that names him.
2. Not chosen: the public notice of 250, "If anyone holds or possesses any of the goods of Caecilius Cyprian, bishop of the Christians" (E4; EP 12232–12234). It comes after his choosing as bishop, so the story would have to go back.

## Story beats

(The opening and the essence fill the first minute, about 130 words.)

1. **Hunted under Decius.** In 250 the emperor Decius ordered sacrifice to the gods (S4, E6; LAPS 1050–1051; Widely Accepted for the year). At Carthage the crowd demanded Cyprian again and again for the lion in the circus (EP 9664–9666; ANF 27879–27880; Documented). A public notice named him "bishop of the Christians" and called on anyone holding his goods to come forward (EP 12232–12234; Documented). Why he withdrew, in his words: the people kept demanding him with violent shouting, and he left for a while, thinking less of his own safety than of the brothers' peace, so that his presence would not stir the riot further (EP 2972–2975; Documented). Absent in body, he led by letter, thirteen of them (EP 2975–2976, 2983–2984). Explain it: proscription, glossed above. Confidence: Documented, his own letters.
2. **The charge.** The clergy at Rome, whose own bishop had been killed in the same persecution (EUS 37413–37416), wrote to Carthage's clergy. They wrote that they had learned, from a subdeacon who came from Carthage, that he had withdrawn for a set reason, and had done rightly, being a man of standing with a struggle at hand (EP 1055–1056, 1077; ANF 28797–28801). In the same letter they spoke of the good shepherd who lays down his life and the hired man who runs from the wolf, and asked Carthage's clergy not to be hired men (EP 1088–1092, 1099–1100; ANF 28817–28820, 28838–28840). Explain it: hireling, glossed above. Cyprian wrote to Rome that his acts had been reported to them less than plainly and faithfully, and gave his account (EP 2967–2970). Pontius later asked whether the cause could have been fear, when he suffered afterwards (ANF 27905–27908). Confidence: Documented that each wrote it; Contested whether Rome meant a reproach (D1): tell both parts of the letter and the answer, and no verdict.
3. **The lapsed, the rival bishop and *On the Unity*.** Many gave way and sacrificed, and he would not settle their return alone (E6; one clause; the sibling tells it). At Rome a second bishop, Novatian, was set up against the first; Cyprian refused his envoys (EP 6186–6192), and to Roman confessors who had joined him he wrote: come back to the mother you came from (EP 6510–6511; told in narration). For them he sent his book on the unity of the church, saying they had now done in deeds what he wrote in words (EP 7397–7421; Documented). What the book says: the church is one as a tree's many branches are one trunk; break off a branch and it cannot bud (UNIT 342–347). The office of bishop is one, and each bishop holds his share of it in full (UNIT 339–340). She is one mother: from her womb Christians are born, by her milk they are fed (UNIT 352–354). Then Quote 1. Explain it: the church here is the one body in communion with its lawful bishops; the other side, Novatian's, held its own bishop and church (E7). Confidence: Documented; give no year for the book (ANF-ed's 251 is an editor's).
4. **The plague.** Afterwards a dreadful plague swept the city, and people fled from the sick, even their own (ANF 27939–27944; Pontius). Some Christians were troubled that it struck them as hard as it struck the pagans (MORT 301–302; Documented). He answered in a sermon, *On the Mortality*, which names the disease: the bowels loosened, a fire in the throat, eyes red with blood (MORT 498–501). Then Quote 2. Pontius, his deacon, says he urged his people to help everyone, not only their own (ANF 27961–27966, 27990–27992; credited to Pontius, D5). Date: "a few years later" (E9; Widely Accepted for the early 250s). Explain it: the title means "on the deaths"; plain words "a sermon in the plague".
5. **The quarrel with Stephen of Rome.** The question (E10): baptism, the washing that makes a Christian, is given once; but when someone washed by a group outside the church came over, did that washing count? It came up first with those coming from Novatian's church (EP 13133–13136). Cyprian's answer, with councils of African bishops: there is one baptism because the church is one, and none outside it; so those who come are not baptized again but baptized, for they received nothing where there was nothing (EP 14138–14139, 14144–14145, 14169, 14488–14492; told in narration). Stephen's answer, as Cyprian copies it: let nothing new be done beyond what was handed down; lay hands on them for repentance, since the heretics themselves do not wash again those who come over to them (EP 15430–15433). Cyprian called the letter proud and Stephen's view an error (EP 15426–15428). A bishop in Cappadocia who took Cyprian's side (Firmilian, Ep. 75) wrote that Stephen would not even speak with the African bishops sent to him, and called Cyprian a false Christ (EP 16669–16673, 16709–16710; credited). Then at a council of eighty-seven bishops from across Roman Africa, with presbyters, deacons and many of the people present, Cyprian opened with Quote 3 (SENT 105–109). Each bishop then gave his view in turn (SENT 105, the record's title, "the opinions of eighty-seven bishops"; one clause). Explain it: communion, glossed at the quote. No record of a settlement between the two is held, and Stephen was succeeded by Xystus, whom Pontius calls 'the good and peace-making priest' (EUS 39406–39407; ANF 28137–28138): card. (E10; say only that no settlement is recorded, if room). Confidence: Documented; Stephen's side is known only as Cyprian quotes it: say "as Cyprian copies it". No year for the council.
6. **Exile, and the last letter.** In 257 (the year from the consuls, Valerian for the fourth time and Gallienus for the third, KNOPF 4823; Widely Accepted) the governor told him the emperors had ordered all who did not keep the Roman religion to take part in the Roman rites (KNOPF 4825–4827). His answer: "I am a Christian and a bishop", who knows no gods but the one true God (KNOPF 4828–4830; short phrase in narration). He was sent to Curubis (KNOPF 4835–4836). In 258 a new order from the emperor Valerian (by role: "the emperor") had bishops put to death at once, and the bishop of Rome was killed in a cemetery (EP 17288–17289, 17322–17324). Back at his gardens, he heard officers were coming to take him to Utica, another city. In his last letter he says he left his gardens for a while, because a bishop should confess his faith in the city where he leads the church, before all his people (EP 17370–17375; Documented). Pontius says only that he refused friends' offers of places to hide (ANF 28143–28149; credit each, D3).
7. **Trial and death, 14 September 258.** Taken at his gardens, he was held one night in an officer's house, and the whole people kept watch in the street outside; he had the young women guarded (KNOPF 4877–4881). Next morning the governor asked: "Are you Thascius Cyprian?" "I am." The emperors had ordered him to perform the rites. "I will not." (KNOPF 4890–4896.) The sentence, the Roman side in its own terms: he had lived long with an impious mind, gathered men into a wicked conspiracy and made himself an enemy of the Roman gods, so his blood would make the law hold; he was to be put to the sword (KNOPF 4902–4910). He said, "Thanks be to God" (KNOPF 4911; GEB 6626). The crowd cried that they would die with him (KNOPF 4913–4915). In the field of Sextus he knelt to pray, gave his tunic to the deacons, had twenty-five gold pieces given to the executioner, and covered his eyes with his own hand; a presbyter and a subdeacon, both named Julian, tied the cloth when he could not (KNOPF 4917–4927). People climbed into the trees to see (Pontius, ANF 28257–28258). By night the brothers carried his body with candles and torches to a burial ground on the Mappalian road (KNOPF 4950–4952). Confidence: Documented, the Christian account of his trial (KNOPF, GEB).

## What came of him (heaviest first)

1. **Both sides of Africa's split claimed him.** From the 310s a rival church, the Donatists, held that baptism must be given again to all who came over, and quoted his letters and council for it (I1; PETS 10206–10209; AUG 11300; CEN `donatism`). Augustine of Hippo, a later bishop in the same Africa, answered them (about 140 years after his death, roughly): stop citing Cyprian's authority for repeating baptism, and hold with us to his example of keeping unity (AUG 11416). He quoted "bishop of bishops" again and again, and said Cyprian, though he held a different view on baptism, never left the church's unity (AUG 11626, 11949). Documented as each one's words. Defended by Cyprian: the one church; each bishop's freedom.
2. **The West ruled against his rule.** In 314 the bishops at Arles ruled that one who came from heretics and had been baptized in the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit should only have hands laid on him (I2; LAUCH 2709–2714; Documented). One clause, no quote (the `council-of-arles-314` story quotes it). Vincent of Lérins's "teachers absolved, disciples condemned" goes to the card.
3. **His feast, and the table where he died.** Augustine preached on his "birthday", the day of his death, kept, he said, through all Africa (I3; PL38 99224–99225, 99254–99260). At the very place he was put to death, Augustine says, a table for God's altar now stood, called Cyprian's table, not because Cyprian ever feasted there, but because there he was offered up; it is ringed now by worshippers, where he was ringed by persecutors (PL38 99282–99290; GAUME 101464–101466; told in narration). Documented as Augustine's.

Who keeps him today: no held source or census tradition entry says; leave out.

## Connection to carry into the story

What passed from him: a rule and a voice. The rule, one church and one baptism, with none outside it (UNIT 363–365; EP 14138–14139), passed to a later rival church in Africa, the Donatists, who used his letters and his council to defend baptizing again (PETS 10206–10209; AUG 11300). The voice, his council's "none of us sets himself up as a bishop of bishops" (SENT 140–141), passed to Augustine, who turned it against them: Cyprian let his colleagues differ and still kept unity, so the Donatists should copy his example of unity, not his view on baptism (AUG 11416, 11626, 11949). The listener needs to hear that both sides were right about what he said: he did hold that baptism outside the church was nothing, and he did refuse to cut off those who disagreed. The writer shows this through what each side did with his words, after Beat 5 has told both halves, never with a "this matters because" line.

- **Augustine of Hippo** (old; about 140 years later): the Donatists' use, his answer, the feast and the table; credited each time.
- **The bishops at Arles, 314** (old; 56 years later): canon 8.
- **Modern, each for what it does:** ANF-ed (Coxe, Wallis) and EUS-ed (McGiffert): clarify texts and give dates; their 248, 251 and 252 and their reading of Rome's letter are their own claims, kept out. Benson (1897) and Koch (1910): claims of their own on the Roman letter, "bishop of bishops" and chapter 4 of the *Unity*: card, Modern works list only.
- The story does not rest on one witness.

## Do not say

- "two years after his baptism" → "while still a new Christian" (D9).
- "Rome accused him of cowardice" or "rebuked him" → the letter said he had done rightly, and in the same letter spoke of the hired man who runs; tell both (D1).
- "he fled" in the narrator's voice → "he withdrew", "he went into hiding", as he says.
- "He was baptized in 246", "bishop in 248", "in 251 he wrote", "the plague of 252", "the council of 256" → no year; "a few years later", "in those months", "at a council" (S2, E8, E9, E10).
- "rebaptism" as his word → he said they were "not baptized again, but baptized" (EP 14144–14145).
- "Pope Stephen" → "Stephen, bishop of Rome".
- "Stephen excommunicated Cyprian" → only Firmilian's report: Stephen would not speak with the African bishops and called Cyprian a false Christ (EP 16669–16673, 16709–16710).
- "The bishop of bishops line attacked Stephen" → say only that he said it at the council, in the months of the quarrel (D7).
- "In the plague he told Christians to nurse pagans" as his sermon's words → that is Pontius's account; the sermon asks about the healthy and the sick, kin, masters and doctors (D5).
- "He bound his own eyes" → the *Acts*: he covered his eyes with his own hand, and two Julians tied the cloth (D4). The executioner's hand "strengthened from above" stays Pontius's, if told.
- "He chose to stay in his gardens" or "by imperial order" → no reason given for his staying at the gardens (*ex sacro praecepto* is unclear; card).
- "the first bishop of Africa to die a martyr" as fact → Pontius's claim, credited, if told (E12).
- "Arles condemned Cyprian" → Arles ruled on the African practice; it names no one (LAUCH 2709).
- "Augustine said Cyprian was a heretic" → Augustine said he held a different view on baptism and kept unity (AUG 11949).
- "a bishop laid hands on them" → "hands were laid on them for repentance".
- "He was exiled for refusing to name his presbyters" → in the *Acts* he is sent to Curubis after he will not take part in the rites, and the question about his presbyters comes afterwards (KNOPF 4825–4840).
- "beheaded" in the sentence → the sentence says "put to the sword" (*gladio*); the crowd's own word was beheaded.
- No invented feelings: no fear, doubt or calm the record does not give.

## Leave out

- His family, birth year, wealth before baptism and the name Caecilius taken from his teacher: the record is late or editorial (E1, E2; card).
- *To Donatus*, his own account of thinking rebirth impossible: strong, but room (card).
- The certificates, the confessors' pardons and the councils of 251: the sibling tells them.
- Puppianus's unnamed charges (Ep. 66): too thin to tell.
- The dream at Curubis and Pontius's reading of it: `pontius-the-deacon`.
- Chapter 4 of the *Unity* and its two forms; Rome's primacy: card (D2).
- Who first held rebaptism (Agrippinus; Eusebius's "first of all, Cyprian"): card (D6).
- Theodoret's fire and his editor's August date: wrong against the *Acts* (D8).
- Tertullian, "the master": the sibling tells it, and its quotes are barred.
- Lactantius's praise and the "Coprianus" jibe; Jerome's "more conspicuous than the sun"; Procopius on the Cypriana and the Vandals; Facundus: card.
- Xistus's milder tone after Stephen: an editor's reading (card).
