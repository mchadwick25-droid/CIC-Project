## 1. The test (person kit)

- **Who:** filled. A teacher of public speaking, made bishop of Carthage while new to the faith, killed in 258.
- **What happened:** filled. He hid under Decius, wrote on unity as Novatian rose, preached in the plague, quarrelled with Stephen over baptism, and died by the sword.
- **What it was about:** filled. The church is one; baptism outside it does not count; no bishop rules the others.
- **Why the connection matters:** partly filled. I learn that the Donatists quoted him for baptizing again and that Augustine turned him against them. I never learn what the Donatists were, or what made them "a rival church". See finding 4b.

## 2. The opening

After sentences 1 to 3, I know who (Cyprian, a new Christian made bishop) and where (Carthage). I do not know when (no century until paragraph 2), or that this is the Latin church of Roman Africa. I am not lost. MINOR.

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing

a. **BLOCKING (loses the thread of the charge).** "The clergy at Rome wrote to Carthage's clergy that he had withdrawn for a set reason, and had done rightly. In the same letter they spoke of the gospel's good shepherd ... They asked Carthage's clergy not to be hired men." On one hearing I could not tell whether Rome was defending Cyprian or rebuking him. "For a set reason" names no reason. The flight charge is the story's question, so this matters. **Change:** give the reason the letter gives, as the outline holds it, or cut "for a set reason". If the outline holds who the hired-man warning was aimed at, say so in the last sentence. Otherwise the trust read rules on the sentence.

b. **BLOCKING (loses the thread of the ending).** "In his last letter he says he left the gardens for a while, because a bishop should confess his faith in the city ..." then "He was taken at his gardens." He left the gardens, and then he was taken at the gardens. I lost track of where he was. I also could not tell whether this second withdrawal answered the old charge or repeated it. **Change:** if the outline holds his return, open the next paragraph with "When he came back, he was taken at his gardens". If it does not, cut "for a while" or whatever the outline does not hold, and let the trust read confirm the order.

c. MINOR. "He taught that the church is one, a mother outside whom no one has God as Father ..." The phrase "outside whom" is hard to catch spoken. **Change:** "He taught that the church is one, and a mother: no one outside her has God as Father, and each bishop answers to Christ alone."

d. MINOR. "Stephen, bishop of Rome, answered otherwise, as Cyprian copies his letter: let nothing new be done ..." The words "as Cyprian copies his letter" stop me mid-sentence. **Change:** see 5b.

e. MINOR. "The sentence, in Rome's own terms, named him an enemy of the Roman gods whose blood would make the law hold ..." The phrase "whose blood would make the law hold" is hard to follow aloud. It is understood on a second pass, not on the first.

## 4. Bare statements

a. **BLOCKING.** "At Rome a second bishop, Novatian, was set up against the one already chosen ..." Part 1 requires a break to say why it happened. This sentence gives no reason. **Change:** add the reason in one clause, as the outline holds it. If the old sources give only the act, the trust read confirms that and the sentence stands.

b. **BLOCKING.** "From the 310s a rival church in Africa, the Donatists, baptized again ..." A rival to whom, and why? The break is the census's direct line, so it needs its reason. **Change:** add one clause, from the outline, saying what split the Donatists from the other African church. If the outline does not hold it, the trust read confirms that the sources give only the act.

Not counted: "Cyprian counted as heretics all who stood outside the one church" (flagged by the craft check). In context the sentence defines the word: heretics are those outside the one church.

## 5. Citations read aloud

a. MINOR. "Jerome says he had once been a teacher of public speaking." Jerome is never said to be anyone. Part 1 calls for a credit only where the witness wrote long after, and the sentence does not say he did. **Change** (narrator's voice): "He had once been a teacher of public speaking." If the credit is kept because Jerome wrote long after, say so: "Jerome, writing long after, says he had once been a teacher of public speaking."

b. MINOR. "as Cyprian copies his letter". The credit may be called for, since Stephen's words come through his opponent, but it is placed badly. **Change:** "Stephen, bishop of Rome, answered otherwise. His letter, as Cyprian copied it, said: let nothing new be done beyond what has been handed down."

Called for and not counted: Pontius "as an admirer" (praise), "Cyprian wrote later" and "In his last letter he says" (his own account of his motive), and Augustine's "he said".

## 6. Tells Part 1 names

a. **BLOCKING. Held-back reveal.** "... soon after this man's baptism the whole people of Carthage wanted him as their bishop." "This man" holds the name back by one sentence, and nothing in the moment needs it held. **Change:** "His deacon, Pontius, who writes as an admirer, says that soon after Cyprian's baptism the whole people of Carthage wanted him as their bishop. A crowd, he says, besieged the doors of his house, while he stepped back to give place to older men."

b. **BLOCKING. A question asked only to answer it.** "But when someone washed by a group outside the church came over to it, did that washing count?" The next sentences answer it at once. **Change:** "But when someone washed by a group outside the church came over to it, the churches did not agree on whether that washing counted."

No stock words, dashes, "not X but Y" turns, moral close or sentences that only say what is missing.

## 6b. Lines that sound machine-made (not blocking; for the reviser)

- "Communion meant belonging to the church and sharing its bread and cup." This is a glossary line dropped in as its own sentence. It reads as an inserted definition, not as the narrator telling it. Work it into the next sentence, for example: "... we cut no one off from communion, from the church's bread and cup, ..." That would mean changing a quote, though, so the gloss may instead go into the lead-in clause: "Cyprian spoke first, and spoke of communion, the sharing of the church's bread and cup."
- "... through persecution, plague and division." A tidy summary triad of the generated kind.
- "Later the Donatists and Augustine both claimed him." A teaser line that names a group I cannot yet place.
- "A few years later a dreadful plague swept the city." "Swept" is the stock verb.
- "Worshippers now ringed the place where persecutors had ringed him." A polished mirror line for a closing cadence. If it is Augustine's image, keep it inside his "he said". If it is the narrator's, it is the shaped kind of close a skeptic hears as written by a machine.
