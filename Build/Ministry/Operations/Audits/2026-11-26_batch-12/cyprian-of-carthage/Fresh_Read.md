### 1. Sentence 15, Pontius's "if" clause (both trust reads, MINOR; listener, sentence fails on one hearing, MINOR)

Source (ANF 27905–27908): "he did suffer subsequently, and this suffering he assuredly would have evaded as usual, if he had evaded it before"; Latin H3 40729: "quam passionem utique ex more uitaret, si et ante uitasset". Pontius's point is a condition on Cyprian's own conduct: had he gone from fear the first time, he would have gone this time too. The story drops the condition, so "would have escaped that too" can be heard as "he could have escaped his death", and the listener needed a second hearing.

The three wordings offered:

- Fresh trust: "and would have escaped that too" → "and would have run from that too, had fear made him run before". Cost +7 (6 words → 13).
- Second trust: "and would have escaped that too" → "and had he fled from fear before, he would have escaped that too". Cost +7 (6 → 13).
- Listener (not a trust reader's, so not eligible): "Pontius later answered that fear was not the cause, since Cyprian did suffer afterwards, when a frightened man would have escaped that too." Cost +4 (19 → 23). It also swaps Pontius's condition on Cyprian himself for a general "frightened man", losing the "as usual, if he had evaded it before".

**Chosen: the fresh trust read's wording.**

- Old: "Pontius later wrote that fear was not the cause: he did suffer afterwards, and would have escaped that too."
- New: "Pontius later wrote that fear was not the cause: he did suffer afterwards, and would have run from that too, had fear made him run before."
- Cost: +7 words (19 → 26).

Why this one: it keeps the condition the source line states (*si et ante uitasset*), and it names fear as the cause in that condition, which is the charge as Pontius gives it (ANF 27905, "the cause of his withdrawal was not fear"). The second trust read's wording puts "had he fled" in the narrator's voice. The outline's Do not say bars "he fled" in the narrator's voice ("he withdrew", "he went into hiding"), and a listener hears "he fled" even inside a condition. "Run" stays in Pontius's answer, not in the charge, so it does not make Rome's hireling passage the accusation (Do not say), and it adds no feeling the record does not give: the fear is Pontius's own hypothesis.

Ruled against the outline: Beat 2 holds the old wording word for word, but Beat 2 also calls for "the charge as Pontius gives it, fear, with his answer", and the answer is the condition. The new wording meets the outline's source line (ANF 27905–27908) and its Do not say list. Beat 2 would take the same wording.

Words: the story stands at 999 against "about 965", so this change is applied only with a cut of at least 7 words in the same pass. No reader gives a cut, and a cut worded here would be this read's own, so the offset is **noted**, for the caller or Mark to rule. Until it is ruled, the change is not applied.
