## The BLOCKING finding

**"His going brought a charge against him: a shepherd had left his flock."** ASSUMPTION ADDED.

- No source line states a charge in these terms. Three sources come near it, and none says this:
  - Rome's letter does say he withdrew "certa ex causa, quod utique recta fecerit" (`cyprian_epistulae-lat_hartel1871-csel3-tei.txt` 1055–1077; ANF 28797–28801, "in doing which he acted quite rightly"). It speaks of the hireling only as the gospel's warning, and urges Carthage's clergy "mercennarios [non] inueniri" (EP 1088–1100).
  - Cyprian says only that his acts were reported at Rome "minus simpliciter et minus fideliter" (EP 2967–2968).
  - Pontius answers a suspicion of fear: "Do you wish to be assured that the cause of his withdrawal was not fear?" (`anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian.xml` 27904–27906).
- The sentence gives the charge a content (a shepherd who left his flock) that no witness gives. It sits right before Rome's letter, so the listener hears the hireling passage as the charge. The outline rates that reading Contested (D1), and its Do not say list bars "Rome accused him" or "rebuked him".
- **Exact change.** Tell it as Pontius gives it: "Pontius later answered those who said he had gone out of fear: he did suffer afterwards, and would have escaped that too." This draws on the outline's Beat 2 line (ANF 27905–27908), which the outline sent to the card as Cut first 14. If the reviewer will not bring that line back, cut the sentence and let Rome's letter follow straight on.
- **Do not say** (add to the outline): "a charge that a shepherd had left his flock", or any wording of the charge that sets Rome's hireling passage up as the accusation.

## The heart

The heart is the right one for the subject. His own letters and books and the *Acts* show the same things: a bishop who held one church and one baptism, and let each bishop judge under Christ; who led his church through the hiding, the break with Novatian and the plague; and who chose to confess and die in his own city. The essence names each of these. It closes, with the claim of both African sides, by about word 150, inside the first minute.
