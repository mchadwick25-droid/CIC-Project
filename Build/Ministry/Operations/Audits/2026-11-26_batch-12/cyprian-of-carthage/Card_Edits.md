# Card edits: Cyprian of Carthage

Card: `cyprian-of-carthage.card.json`, built from `Card.existing.json`. Keys are in the layout's order. No `briefLength`, no `secondReading` of a quotation of six words or more except where the old entry had one (the three long quotations carry `same` from their entries' old readings; release resets them from the final check's blind renderings). Connections to other entries are left out. Line numbers are lines of the files under `cic/texts/`.

## The standard (Part 1, date rule, modern works)

- **knownFor.** Old: "Bishop of Carthage from about 248 until he was put to the sword in 258." New: "Bishop of Carthage until he was put to the sword in 258." Evidence: 248 rests on editors' reckoning (Source_Sheet S2; Dominant Modern Reconstruction); brief says "while still a new Christian".
- **datesNote.** Old words: the Edinburgh editor's 248, 246, born about 200 and 251; Coxe's 251; 252 from Eusebius's Chronicle as the editor cites it; the council "in 256 by most sources" and "258 by the Edinburgh heading"; Stephen died 257 and Xystus's accession by an editor's reckoning; Theodoret's editor's 13 August 258. All are modern reckonings (Part 1, Rigor; "Modern works on the cards", Decision-Log 2026-11-26). New: what the old sources fix (consuls, days) and what they leave open. Kept from the old note: 30 August 257 and exile to Curubis (KNOPF 4823-4836), the day and year of the trial (KNOPF 4857-4858, 4955-4956), the Decius persecution as 250 (Widely Accepted, EUS 37413-37416).
- **placeNote.** Removed "which E. W. Benson describes as a small walled coast town about fifty miles from Carthage" (modern); removed "256" from the council (no year in the record); removed "of the province" after Utica (the letter says only Utica). Kept the rest (SENT 107-108; KNOPF 4835-4836, 4950-4952; EP 17370-17372).
- **keyWorks[1] (On the Unity).** Old: "written at the time of Novatian's break, in 251 by Coxe's dating." New: "written at the time of Novatian's break, as his own letter to the Roman confessors says." (EP 7397-7421). **keyWorks[2] (On the Mortality).** Old: "of about 252." Removed (modern reckoning).
- **uncertain[1].** Removed "The Edinburgh editor says the older presbyters objected;" (modern gloss; Source_Sheet E3). **uncertain[2].** Removed the Edinburgh editor's claim on the name. **uncertain[3].** Removed the Edinburgh editor's sentence on rhetoric.
- **uncertain[4].** Old: Rome's letter "of the good shepherd and the hired man..."; "Whether this was a reproach is the editors' reading. The Edinburgh edition's translator calls... Coxe says... Benson calls it a sarcasm." New: the letter said he withdrew for a certain reason and acted rightly, and in the same letter spoke of the hired man; "Whether the hired man passage was meant as a reproach is not settled." Evidence: EP 1055-1056, 1077, 1088-1100; brief paragraph 4. The modern opinions are out.
- **uncertain[7]** (Unity ch. 4). Old words named Benson, Coxe, Koch and Chapman. New: Hartel prints the text without the Peter words and gives them in his notes; "disputed among modern readers" (UNIT 279-286, 315-320; Source_Sheet D2).
- **uncertain[8].** Removed "and the editors think it was aimed at Cyprian".
- **uncertain[9]** (Stephen). Added Eusebius's "Xystus succeeded him after two years" (EUS 39406-39407). Replaced "Eusebius's editor says Xystus seems to have taken a more conciliatory tone" with "Pontius calls Xystus, Stephen's successor at Rome, the good and peace-making priest" (ANF 28137-28138).
- **uncertain[10]** (bishop of bishops). Old named Benson and the Edinburgh editor and gave 256 and 258. New: Augustine's reading; "not settled"; "the day, 1 September, and no year" (SENT 107; AUG 11626).
- **uncertain[12]** (how he died). Old: Theodoret "which his editor corrects ..., though that editor gives 13 August 258". New: Theodoret, long after, says a death in the fire; the account gives 14 September 258 (KNOPF 4955-4956; Source_Sheet D8).
- **sources.about.** Moved out, word for word, to the entry's `modernWorks` (below): "Cyprian: his Life, his Times, his Work" (E. W. Benson, 1897) and "Cyprian und der römische Primat" (Hugo Koch, 1910). No other modern work stands in `sources`.
