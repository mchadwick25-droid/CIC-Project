## Blocking findings

**1. Para 3, "Tertullian alone says he broke with the church."** SOFTENED OR CHANGED. Tertullian does tell it: "de ecclesia authenticae regulae abrupit" (KR 9653–9654). But he is not the only witness. Epiphanius says that before Cyprus Valentinus "was thought to have a share of piety and right faith", and that in Cyprus "τῆς πίστεως ἐξέστη" (he left the faith) (HOLL 29801–29803). Epiphanius is late, about 375, and the outline rightly keeps his report out. But "alone" is a flat claim that the held record does not support. **Change:** "Of the writers near his time, Tertullian alone says he broke with the church."

**2. Para 7, "Hippolytus says the school then split over what kind of body Jesus had."** ASSUMPTION ADDED ("then"). Hippolytus gives no order in time. He names Ptolemy as one of the Italian branch: "They from Italy, of whom is Heracleon and Ptolemæus" (HIP 9645–9646; WEN 12545). So he does not put the split after Ptolemy's change, which is what "then", after the Ptolemy sentence, implies. **Change:** "Hippolytus says the school split over what kind of body Jesus had."

**3. Para 9, "By Tertullian's day, he says, Valentinus himself was now nowhere."** SOFTENED OR CHANGED. The Latin is "ita nusquam iam Valentinus" (KR 9663). It follows straight after the students' changes: Ptolemy "intrauit", then "deduxit et Heracleon inde tramites quosdam et Secundus et magus Marcus" (KR 9657–9662). "Ita" ("so", "thus") makes his disappearance a result of his students taking his teaching their own ways. "By Tertullian's day" makes it only a matter of time. Placed after the opponents' books, the sentence can also be heard as if they had made him vanish. The cause Tertullian gives is lost. **Change:** "So many paths had branched off from his, Tertullian says, that Valentinus himself was now nowhere."

**4. The heart: right heart, told late.** The heart fits the subject and the slate's job. It is the Rome teacher whose school taught paired powers, told from his own words as Clement kept them, with his opponents credited. The sources hold it (IR 31519–31531; CL 34289–34303; KR 9633–9666; HIP 9642–9663). But paragraphs 1 and 2 run 133 words, so the essence only starts at word 134. "Founded a school" comes at about word 148, the pairs at about 168, the break at about 192, and the last clause (the two branches and the answer) ends at about word 215. That is roughly 65 words, or 25 seconds, past the first minute. **Change:** move "For eighteen centuries his school was known almost only through their books, until writings from these circles were found in Egypt in 1945." from paragraph 2 to the end of paragraph 8, after "…a long refutation of their teaching." That keeps the one-witness clause early, inside the Clement sentence, and brings the essence's core (Rome, the school, the pairs and the fall) to about word 158. If the reviewer still judges it late, the only other room is the opening paragraph itself, which the outline says never to cut.

## Non-blocking notes (SUMMARY HOLDS, recorded for the second reader)

- Para 6, "a higher race come down from above". CL 39097–39098 has "this different race has come hither to us from above". "Higher" is backed by "from above" and by the "germ of superior excellence" the Valentinians claim (CL 32108–32109). "A different race come down from above" would be closer to the source.
- Para 6, "they had come into death only to use it up and end it". The sermon has "ye would have death distributed to you, that ye may spend and lavish it, and that death may die in you" (CL 39091–39093). Clement's reading is "come hither… for the abolition of death" (CL 39097–39098). The outline holds "only", and it adds a slight exclusiveness.
- Para 4, "writing at Carthage early in the next century". Jerome says "from the city of Carthage… flourished chiefly in the reign of the emperor Severus and Antoninus Caracalla" (JER 40494–40499). That he wrote at Carthage is a fair compression, Widely Accepted.
- Para 4, "for which the church at Rome put Valentinus out more than once". The source is "in the church of Rome under the episcopate of the blessed Eleutherus… they were more than once expelled" (TE 21335–21339). It is said of Marcion and Valentinus together. The brief tells no time for the expulsion, so Tertullian's clash with Irenaeus over dates (Cut first 4) is not told as fact.
- Para 2, the 1945 sentence. Its only support is the census tradition entry as the Source Sheet quotes it (I3). I did not open the census. The Kit lets a modern event be told as an event, and the outline rates it Widely Accepted.
- Para 5, "a lesser maker who made it". Irenaeus I.11.1 says the excluded mother "brought forth another son, namely, the Demiurge… supreme ruler of all those things which are subject to him" (IR 31538–31541), and Clement says Valentinus made death "the work of the Creator of the world" (CL 39099–39100). "Lesser" is a fair compression: he is born outside the Fullness, of a mother "deprived of her spiritual substance".
