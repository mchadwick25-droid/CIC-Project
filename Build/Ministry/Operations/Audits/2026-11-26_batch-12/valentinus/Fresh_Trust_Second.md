## Sentence by sentence

1. "In a letter, Valentinus, who taught at Rome in the middle of the second century, compared the heart to an inn for travelers." **HELD.** Clement says he wrote "in a letter to certain people… the heart seems to be treated somewhat like a caravanserai" (`anf02_…clement-alexandria.xml` 34289, 34296–34297). For Rome: "Valentinus came to Rome in the time of Hyginus, flourished under Pius" (`anf01_…irenaeus.xml` 39968–39969). For the time: "in the reign of Antoninus for the most part" (`anf03_tertullian.xml` 21333–21334).
2. "It has holes and ruts and is often full of dung, and the men who stay there live filthily and take no care of a place that belongs to others." **HELD.** Clement: "has holes and ruts made in it, and is often filled with dung; men living filthily in it, and taking no care for the place as belonging to others" (CL 34297–34299).
3. "At Rome he founded a school, a circle of students around a teacher." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** Irenaeus has "his own school" (διδασκαλεῖον, IR 31519–31520; HOLL 32861–32863). No source says in so many words that the school was founded at Rome. But Irenaeus places him at Rome (IR 39968–39969), Tertullian says he "infected the brethren" there (TE 21337–21338), and Justin names Valentinians at Rome in his lifetime. That is a fair compression. The wording is the same as v1's "taught at Rome… and founded a school".
4. "Of the writers near his time, Tertullian alone says he broke with the church." **HELD** (a claim that no one else says it, checked by search). Tertullian: "de ecclesia authenticae regulae abrupit" (`tertullian_opera-pars3-lat_kroymann-csel47.txt` 9652–9653). I searched the other writers near his time and found no break: Irenaeus III.4.3 gives only his years at Rome (IR 39964–39970); Hippolytus's charge is "a Pythagorean and Platonist, not a Christian", which is not a break; pseudo-Tertullian, *Against All Heresies* IV, gives only the teaching (TE 55573ff); Clement tells none. Epiphanius (about 375) is not near his time. "Valentinus the apostate" (TE 46183) is again Tertullian.
5. "In the same letter he went on: 'So it is with the heart, while no one gives it any thought. It is unclean, a home for many demons. But when the Father, who alone is good, visits it, it is made holy, and it shines with light.'" **HELD.** The quote follows the inn image directly in the same letter: "So fares it with the heart as long as there is no thought taken for it, being unclean, and the abode of many demons. But when the only good Father visits it, it is sanctified, and gleams with light" (CL 34299–34302). The rendering, which was checked against the ANF English because Clement's Greek is not held, keeps every clause and adds nothing.
6. "Those words survive because Clement of Alexandria quoted them to argue against him, and almost everything known of Valentinus comes from men who wrote against him." **HELD.** Clement quotes the letter to argue that it makes the soul "a saved nature, as he would have it" (CL 34307–34309). Every fragment and every account of the teaching comes through Clement, Hippolytus, Irenaeus and Tertullian (Source_Sheet I3). "Almost" still fits beside the 1945 finds, which the brief now gives before this sentence.
7. "In another book he gives a second reason, a restless curiosity that spread to other believers, for which Valentinus was put out of the church at Rome more than once." **HELD.** Tertullian: "believers… in the church of Rome… until on account of their ever restless curiosity, with which they even infected the brethren, they were more than once expelled" (TE 21335–21339). OE 1776 has "semel et iterum eiecti". The passive voice leaves out who did the expelling, and so does the source.
8. "Hippolytus says the school split over what kind of body Jesus had, into an Italian branch, Ptolemy's, and an Eastern one." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** Hippolytus: "the doctrine of these has become divided: and one doctrine… is termed Oriental, and the other Italian. They from Italy, of whom is Heracleon and Ptolemæus, say that the body of Jesus was (an) animal (one)" (`anf05_hippolytus…xml` 9641–9646). The brief's "Ptolemy's" leaves out Heracleon, but the meaning holds. Removing v1's "then" was right, because Hippolytus gives no time for the split.
9. "So far had his students moved from him, Tertullian says, that Valentinus himself was now nowhere." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** Tertullian lists the students' departures (Ptolemy, Heracleon, Secundus, Marcus, Theotimus) and then concludes: "ita nusquam iam Valentinus" (KR 9655–9663). *Ita* ("thus, so") makes the conclusion follow from the students' changes, so "So far had his students moved" is a fair reading. "Now nowhere" follows the Latin *nusquam iam*, not the ANF's mistaken "as yet nowhere" (TE 43124).
