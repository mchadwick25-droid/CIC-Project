## Setting

**S1. When and where: Irenaeus.** IR 39968–39970 (*Against Heresies* III.4.3): "For Valentinus came to Rome in the time of Hyginus, flourished under Pius, and remained until Anicetus." Greek, as Eusebius kept it, in Holl's note (HOLL 29827–29828): "Οὐαλεντῖνος μὲν γὰρ ἦλϑεν eis Ῥώμην ἐπὶ Ὑγίνου, ἤχμασε δὲ ἐπὶ Πίου καὶ παρέμεινεν ἕως Ἀνιχήτου". Eusebius repeats it (EUS 22154–22155). LATER (Irenaeus writes about 180, a generation on; Widely Accepted). The report: Documented. Hyginus, Pius and Anicetus are bishops of Rome; Irenaeus counts Hyginus "the ninth bishop" (IR 39971). Years for those bishops come only from McGiffert (EUS 22162–22164, "Hyginus held office in all probability from 137–141, and Anicetus from 154 or 155 to 166 or 167"): EDITOR, a claim of his own, not for the story.

**S2. Under which emperors.** Clement (CL 52956–52959, *Stromata* VII.17): "It was later, in the times of Adrian the king, that those who invented the heresies arose; and they extended to the age of Antoninus the elder". Tertullian (TE 21332–21335, *Prescription* 30) of Marcion and Valentinus: "those men lived not so long ago,—in the reign of Antoninus for the most part". LATER (about 200). Documented. Plain spoken form: "at Rome, in the middle of the second century, in the reign of the emperor Antoninus" (that the reign falls in the middle of the century: Widely Accepted).

**S3. Tradition and setting word.** The census tradition entry: "Valentinian & other Gnostic Christianities", spoken "the Gnostic Currents". Irenaeus calls Valentinus the one "who adapted the principles of the heresy called 'Gnostic' to the peculiar character of his own school" (IR 31519–31520; Greek HOLL 32861–32863, "τὰς ἀρχὰς εἰς ἴδιον χαραχτῆρα διδασχαλείου μεϑαρμόσας"). The word *gnosis* means knowledge; Clement says the followers of Valentinus "will have it that knowledge springs up in their own selves" (CL 32107–32108). Irenaeus calls his own work a "refutation of knowledge falsely so called" (IR 50552–50554, *Against Heresies* V preface, "this the fifth book of the entire work"). Plain word: "those who claimed a special knowledge". "School" here renders διδασκαλεῖον, a teaching house: a circle of students around a teacher.

**S4. Where he came from.** Epiphanius (HOLL 28939–28947, *Panarion* 31.2): "τὴν μὲν οὖν αὐτοῦ πατρίδα ἢ πόϑεν οὗτος γεγέννηται, οἱ πολλοὶ ἀγνοοῦσιν… ἐν ἀμφιλέχτῳ μέν… ἔφασαν γὰρ αὐτόν τινες γεγενῆσϑαι Φρεβωνίτην, τῆς Αἰγύπτου Παραλιώτην, ἐν ᾿Αλεξανδρείᾳ δὲ πεπαιδεῦσϑαι τὴν τῶν Ἑλλήνων παιδείαν" (most do not know his homeland; he gives the report as doubtful: some said he was born on the coast of Egypt and schooled in Greek learning at Alexandria). LATE (about 375, two centuries on), hearsay he himself doubts. Inferential-Thin. Not for the story; card only.

**S5. His teacher, by his followers' claim.** CL 52962–52967 (*Stromata* VII.17): "Likewise they allege that Valentinus was a hearer of Theudas. And he was the pupil of Paul." The claim is the followers', Clement reports it. Contested. Card only.

## Events

**E1. His followers named in his own time.** Justin (IR 19254–19256, *Dialogue* 35): "Some are called Marcians, and some Valentinians, and some Basilidians, and some Saturnilians, and others by other names; each called after the originator of the individual opinion". Greek JU 1637–1638: "οἱ δὲ Οὐαλεντινιανοί… ἀπὸ τοῦ ἀρχηγέτου τῆς γνώμης ἕκαστος ὀνομαζόμενος". CONTEMP (Justin, at Rome in Valentinus's years: Widely Accepted). Documented. Tertullian later lists Justin among those "contemporary with the heresiarchs themselves" who wrote against them (TE 43161–43163).

**E2. The bishopric he hoped for, and the break (Tertullian alone).** KR 9633–9634, 9652–9654 (*Against the Valentinians* 4): "sperauerat episcopatum Yalentinus, quia et ingenio poterat et eloquio, sed aliura ex martyrii praerogatiua loci potitum indignatus de ecclesia authenticae regulae abrupit, ut solent animi pro prioratu exciti praesumptione ultionis accendi." (OCR "Yalentinus", "aliura" for Valentinus, alium; apparatus KR 9647: "sperauerat R, separauerat P".) ANF TE 43109–43115: "Valentinus had expected to become a bishop, because he was an able man both in genius and eloquence. Being indignant, however, that another obtained the dignity by reason of a claim which confessorship had given him, he broke with the church of the true faith. Just like those (restless) spirits which, when roused by ambition, are usually inflamed with the desire of revenge, he applied himself with all his might to exterminate the truth". LATER (Tertullian at Carthage, early third century; the ANF editor dates the book "not earlier than a.d. 207", TE 42916: EDITOR). One hostile witness. Documented as Tertullian's account; the event Contested (no second witness).
- Tertullian names **no city, no year and no rival**. "At Rome" and "bishop of Rome" are not in the text.
- *martyrii praerogativa*: the other man's prior claim came from having suffered for the faith. ANF renders "confessorship" (note TE 43112: "Martyrii").
- *ecclesia authenticae regulae*: "the church of the true rule". Tertullian's "rule" is the rule of faith; he gives its content in *Prescription* 13 (TE 20640–20645): "the belief that there is one only God, and that He is none other than the Creator of the world". The ANF's "true faith" loosens it.
- The motive clause ("ut solent animi…": as minds roused for first place are usually set alight by the hope of revenge) is Tertullian's own judgment.
- Tertullian's order: after the break he turned to fight the truth, and "finding the seed of a certain old opinion, he marked out a path for his serpent" (KR 9654–9655: "cuiusdam ueteris opinionis semen nactus colubro suo uiam delineauit"; TE 43115–43117). So by Tertullian, the system came after the break.

**E3. Expelled more than once (Tertullian, a second account).** TE 21335–21339 (*Prescription* 30): Marcion and Valentinus "at first were believers in the doctrine of the Catholic Church, in the church of Rome under the episcopate of the blessed Eleutherus, until on account of their ever restless curiosity, with which they even infected the brethren, they were more than once expelled." OE 1776 confirms "semel et iterum eiecti". LATER, hostile. Documented as his account. The reason Tertullian gives here is different from E2: restless curiosity that spread to other believers. His Eleutherus is a later bishop than Irenaeus's Hyginus to Anicetus (see D1). The Marcion story tells this passage; here one clause.

**E4. What he taught: powers in pairs (Irenaeus, on Valentinus himself).** IR 31519–31541 (*Against Heresies* I.11.1): "He maintained that there is a certain Dyad (twofold being), who is inexpressible by any name, of whom one part should be called Arrhetus (unspeakable), and the other Sige (silence). But of this Dyad a second was produced, one part of whom he names Pater, and the other Aletheia. From this Tetrad, again, arose Logos and Zoe, Anthropos and Ecclesia. These constitute the primary Ogdoad. He next states that from Logos and Zoe ten powers were produced… But from Anthropos and Ecclesia proceeded twelve, one of which separating from the rest, and falling from its original condition, produced the rest of the universe." Then Christ, brought forth by "the mother who had been excluded" from the Fullness, "not without a kind of shadow"; the mother, left with the shadow, "brought forth another son, namely, the Demiurge" (IR 31532–31541). Greek HOLL 32862–32879 ("δυάδα (τὶνὰ) ἀνονόμαστον, ἧς τὸ μέν τι χαλεῖσϑαι ΓΑρρητον, τὸ δὲ Σιγήν… ὧν μίαν ἀποστᾶσαν χαὶ ὑστερήσασαν τὴν λοιπὴν πραγματείαν πεποιῆσϑαι… τὸν Δημιουργόν"). LATER (about 180), hostile. Documented as Irenaeus's account.
- **Explain it, from his opponent's side:** first an unnamed pair, Unspeakable and Silence; from them Father and Truth; then Word and Life, Man and Church; from these, more powers. One of the last broke away and fell, and its fall produced everything outside; from that line came a lesser maker of this world. Irenaeus gives no reason for the fall here; tell it as he gives it.
- **Why it mattered to the church:** Irenaeus sets against it the faith the church received, "in one God, the Father Almighty, Maker of heaven, and earth, and the sea, and all things that are in them" (IR 31390–31392, I.10.1). Tertullian's rule: one God, "the Creator of the world" (TE 20643–20644). Clement: "there being but one First Cause" (CL 32737).

**E5. Inside God or outside: Valentinus against his pupil (Tertullian).** KR 9655–9660: "eam postmodum Ptolomaeus intrauit, nominibus et numeris Aeonum distinctis in personales substantias, sed extra deum determinatas, quas Valentinus in ipsa summa diuinitatis ut sensus et affectus <et> motus incluserat." TE 43117–43121: "Ptolemæus afterwards entered on the same path, by distinguishing the names and the numbers of the Ænons into personal substances, which, however, he kept apart from God. Valentinus had included these in the very essence of the Deity, as senses and affections of motion." LATER, hostile. Documented as Tertullian's account. Plain sense: for Valentinus the powers were thoughts, feelings and movements within God; Ptolemy made them separate beings outside God. Irenaeus's long account in Book I is of Ptolemy's school (IR 29871–29873: "the disciples of Ptolemæus, whose school may be described as a bud from that of Valentinus").

**E6. Why pairs: the school's account (Hippolytus).** WEN 11938–11942 (*Refutation* VI.29): "φιλέρημος γὰρ ovx ἣν. ἀγάπη γάρ, φησίν, ἢν ὅλος, N de ἀγάπη οὐχ ἔστιν ἀγάπη, ἐὰν μὴ 7 To ἀγαπώμενον. προέβαλεν οὖν xci ἐγέννησεν αὐτὸς ὁ Πατήρ, ὥσπερ ἦν μόνος, Nov» καὶ Ar ϑειαν, τουτέστι δυάδα". ANF HIP 9170–9174: "(the Father) was not fond of solitariness. For, says he, He was all love, but love is not love except there may be some object of affection. The Father Himself, then, as He was solitary, projected and produced Nous and Aletheia, that is, a duad". LATER (Hippolytus at Rome, early third century), hostile. Hippolytus opens the passage on "Valentinus, therefore, and Heracleon, and Ptolemæus, and the entire school" (HIP 9129–9131; WEN 11881–11883), and says they disagree whether the Father had Silence as a partner (WEN 11887–11931). The "he" who says it is not named. Documented as the school's teaching; that the words are Valentinus's own: Contested. Hippolytus's charge (HIP 9125–9129; WEN 11878–11881): "from this (system), not from the Gospels, Valentinus… may (therefore) justly be reckoned a Pythagorean and Platonist, not a Christian."

**E7. His own words: the heart as an inn.** CL 34289–34303 (*Stromata* II.20): "Valentinus too, in a letter to certain people, writes in these very words respecting the appendages: 'There is one good, by whose presence is the manifestation, which is by the Son, and by Him alone can the heart become pure, by the expulsion of every evil spirit from the heart: for the multitude of spirits dwelling in it do not suffer it to be pure; but each of them performs his own deeds, insulting it oft with unseemly lusts. And the heart seems to be treated somewhat like a caravanserai. For the latter has holes and ruts made in it, and is often filled with dung; men living filthily in it, and taking no care for the place as belonging to others. So fares it with the heart as long as there is no thought taken for it, being unclean, and the abode of many demons. But when the only good Father visits it, it is sanctified, and gleams with light. And he who possesses such a heart is so blessed, that "he shall see God."'" OWN, kept by Clement, an opponent. Documented. "presence" is Grabe's emendation for "boldness of speech" (CL 34290–34291): EDITOR, clarifies the text. Greek not held.
- **Explain it, his side:** a letter to "certain people" about the "appendages", the spirits that crowd the soul. Only the one good Father, through the Son, drives them out and makes the heart pure.
- **Clement's side:** he quotes it to argue that it makes the soul "a saved nature, as he would have it" (CL 34307–34309).

**E8. His own words: a sermon on death.** CL 39090–39096 (*Stromata* IV.13): "Valentinian, in a homily, writes in these words: 'Ye are originally immortal, and children of eternal life, and ye would have death distributed to you, that ye may spend and lavish it, and that death may die in you and by you; for when we dissolve the world, and are not yourselves dissolved, ye have dominion over creation and all corruption.'" OWN. The ANF prints "Valentinian"; its own index files the passage under Valentinus ("errors of Valentinus, concerning, 425", CL 58379; the page is 425, CL 39055), and Clement goes on "For he also, similarly with Basilides…" (CL 39095–39096). Documented as Clement's quotation of Valentinus; the attribution rests on the ANF text and index (Greek not held).
- **Explain it, his side:** a homily is a talk to hearers. He tells them they were deathless from the start, and came into death in order to use it up, so that death would die in them.
- **Clement's side:** "he also, similarly with Basilides, supposes a class saved by nature, and that this different race has come hither to us from above for the abolition of death, and that the origin of death is the work of the Creator of the world" (CL 39095–39100). His answer: "If for the purpose of doing away with death the peculiar race has come, it is not Christ who has abolished death" (CL 39123–39125).

**E9. Saved by nature (Clement's charge).** CL 40955–40960 (*Stromata* V.1): "the precepts both of the Old and of the New Testament are, then, superfluous, if one is saved by nature, as Valentinus would have it… and nature would have been able, one time or other, to have shone forth, apart from the Saviour's appearance." CL 32106–32109 (II.3): "the followers of Valentinus assign faith to us, the simple, but will have it that knowledge springs up in their own selves (who are saved by nature)". LATER (about 200), hostile. Documented as Clement's reading.
- **Explain it:** by Clement's reading, some people belong by birth to a higher race and are saved because of what they are. Clement objects that then the commandments would be useless, and salvation would come without the Savior.

**E10. His own words: a psalm (Hippolytus).** WEN 12707–12719 (*Refutation* VI.37): "καὶ δεδήλωκεν αὐτὴν óc ἐλαχίστων Οὐαλεντῖνος ἔν ψαλμῷ… λέγων οὕτως" (al)8£ooc πάντα xosuausva πνεύματι βλέπω, πάντα δ᾽ ὀχούμενα πνεύματι νοῶ" σάρχα μὲν ἐκ ψυχῆς χρεμαμένην, ψυχὴν δὲ ἀέρος ἐξεχομένην, ἀέρα δὲ ἐξ αἴϑρης xgsuausvov, &x δὲ βυϑοῦ καρποὺς φερομένους, ἐκ untoas δὲ βρέφος φερόμενον" (OCR as printed, line breaks and accent marks above the lines dropped; "(al)8£ooc", "xosuausva", "xgsuausvov", "untoas" are OCR for (αἰ)θέρος, κρεμάμενα, κρεμάμενον, μήτρας). ANF HIP 9787–9802: "Valentinus has elucidated this (arrangement) very succinctly, in a psalm commencing from below… 'I behold all things suspended in air by spirit, and I perceive all things wafted by spirit; the flesh (I see) suspended from soul, but the soul shining out from air, and air depending from Æther, and fruits produced from Bythus, and the fœtus borne from the womb.'" OWN, kept by Hippolytus. Documented that Hippolytus quotes it as his; **the text is damaged**: the first word is θέρος in the manuscript, emended (WEN 12738 apparatus "αἰϑέρος Gà: ϑέρος P"); the verb for the soul is a conjecture (WEN 12739–12740: "ἐξεχομένην; Bunsen… ἐξειχουμένην P, ἐξοχουμένην Miller"), and the ANF's "shining out" follows another reading. The two held prints differ. Card only, as his own words; not quoted in the story.

**E11. His own words: the angels' fear.** CL 32722–32735 (*Stromata* II.8): "And as terror fell on the angels at this creature, because he uttered things greater than proceeded from his formation, by reason of the being in him who had invisibly communicated a germ of the supernal essence… for Adam formed into the name of man inspired the dread attaching to the pre-existent man… and they were terror-stricken, and speedily marred the work." OWN. Documented. Hard to follow heard once; card only.

**E12. Other fragments.** CL 46642–46648 (*Stromata* VI.6, *On the Intercourse of Friends*); CL 39102–39108 (IV.13, the image and the living face); CL 36157–36161 (III.7, to Agathopus, on Jesus's eating, in Latin). OWN. Documented. Each needs long explaining; Leave out.

**E13. A vision (Hippolytus).** WEN 13128–13132: "καὶ γὰρ Οὐαλεντῖνος φάσχει ξαυτὸν ἑωρακέναι παῖδα νήπιον ἀρτιγέν»ῆτον… ὁ δὲ ἀπεχρίνατο λέγων, ἑαυτὸν εἶναι τὸν λόγον". HIP 10054–10059: "Valentinus likewise alleges that he had seen an infant child lately born; and questioning (this child), he proceeded to inquire who it might be. And (the child) replied, saying that he himself is the Logos, and then subjoined a sort of tragic legend". LATER, hostile, one witness; a wonder, told only as Hippolytus's report. Card only.

**E14. How he read Scripture (Tertullian).** TE 21806–21817 (*Prescription* 38): "Valentinus, however, abstained from such excision, because he did not invent Scriptures to square with his own subject-matter, but adapted his matter to the Scriptures; and yet he took away more, and added more, by removing the proper meaning of every particular word". LATER, hostile. Documented as his view. The Tertullian story already quotes his Marcion-and-Valentinus line; Leave out.

**E15. The end of the record.** Irenaeus: "remained until Anicetus" (S1). Epiphanius (HOLL 29802–29807, *Panarion* 31.7): "ἀλλὰ zei ἐν Ῥώμῃ ἀνελϑὼν κεχήρυχεν. εἰς Κύπρον δὲ ἐληλυϑώς… τῆς πίστεως ἐξέστη" (he preached at Rome too; coming to Cyprus, he left the faith). LATE (about 375). Holl's note (HOLL 29829–29833) says the Cyprus report "steht im Widerspruch mit Irenaeus", whose παρέμεινεν implies he died at Rome: EDITOR, clarifies. No held source gives his death. Leave out; card notes it.

## What came of it, heaviest first

**I1. His students carried the school on, and changed it.** Tertullian (KR 9655–9666; TE 43117–43128): Ptolemy made the powers separate beings; "deduxit et Heracleon inde tramites quosdam et Secundus et magus Marcus"; then "ita nusquam iam Valentinus, et tamen Valentiniani, qui per Valentinum. solus ad hodiernum Antiochiae Axionicus memoriam Valentini integra custodia regularum eius consolatur" (KR 9663–9666). The ANF's "as yet nowhere" (TE 43124) misreads *nusquam iam*, "now nowhere"; render from the Latin. Hippolytus (HIP 9642–9663, *Refutation* VI.35): the school split over the body of Jesus into an "Italian" teaching, "of whom is Heracleon and Ptolemæus", and an "Oriental" one, "of whom is Axionicus and Bardesianes". Origen answers "Heracleon, the friend, as it is said, of Valentinus" on John's Gospel (OR 23165–23169). Eusebius: Bardaisan "was at first a follower of Valentinus, but afterward, having rejected his teaching… did not entirely wash off the filth of the old heresy" (EUS 26652–26658); Ambrose, "who held the heresy of Valentinus, was convinced by Origen's presentation of the truth" (EUS 34778–34815; Greek EUSG 3316–3319, "᾿Αμβρόσιος τὰ τῆς Οὐαλεντίνου φρονῶν αἱρέσεος"). LATER, hostile, several independent witnesses. Documented.

**I2. The answer, and what it defended.** Justin named the Valentinians in Valentinus's lifetime (E1). Polycarp, at Rome under Anicetus, "caused many to turn away from the aforesaid heretics to the Church of God" (IR 39869–39873; the heretics are Valentinus and Marcion, IR 39871). Irenaeus wrote five books (IR 50552–50554) against Ptolemy's school, "a bud" of Valentinus's (IR 29871–29873), and set against it one God, Maker of heaven and earth (IR 31390–31392). Tertullian wrote *Against the Valentinians* (TE 42892ff; KR 9473ff). Hippolytus, *Refutation* VI (charge E6). Epiphanius, *Panarion* 31, copying Irenaeus's Greek. **Defended:** one God who made the world (Irenaeus I.10.1; Tertullian *Prescription* 13), and salvation by Christ, not by nature (Clement V.1, IV.13). Documented.

**I3. His words survive only in his opponents' books.** Every fragment (E7–E12) comes to us inside Clement or Hippolytus, who quote it to answer it; the teaching (E4–E6) is described only by opponents. The census tradition entry: "For eighteen centuries, these groups were known almost entirely through such opponents", until in 1945 a jar near Nag Hammadi "turned out to hold their own writings" (census `valentinian-and-other-gnostic-christianities`, `longDescription` and `why`). Those books are not held. The finding is a modern event in the record (Kit Part 1, Rigor): told as an event. Widely Accepted.

**I4. The Gospel of Truth.** Irenaeus (IR 41174–41178, III.11.9): "those who are from Valentinus… entitle their comparatively recent writing 'the Gospel of Truth,' though it agrees in nothing with the Gospels of the Apostles". Who wrote it: no held old source says. The census voices say "possibly the author" (a modern view): Contested. Card only.

**Who keeps it today.** Census tradition `legacy`: "No community survives, and none claims descent." Card only.

## Where sources disagree or come late

**D1. When he was at Rome.** Irenaeus: Hyginus to Anicetus. Tertullian: a believer at Rome "under the episcopate of the blessed Eleutherus" before he was expelled, a later bishop. No held old source settles it. Irenaeus wrote nearer the time. The story says "in the middle of the second century" (S2) and credits Irenaeus with the bishops if it names them.

**D2. Where the bishopric was, and who won it.** Tertullian names no see and no rival (E2). Do not say "bishop of Rome" or name the other man.

**D3. Why he broke.** Tertullian alone gives reasons, and two of them: thwarted ambition and a wish for revenge (*Against the Valentinians* 4), and restless curiosity that spread to others, with more than one expulsion (*Prescription* 30). Irenaeus tells no break. Tell each in Tertullian's voice.

**D4. What is his and what is his school's.** Irenaeus I.11.1 is the account of Valentinus himself; Book I's long account is Ptolemy's school (E5). Tertullian sets the teacher against the pupil (E5). Hippolytus mixes Valentinus with Heracleon and Ptolemy (E6). Contested; say plainly that the teacher and his students cannot always be told apart.

**D5. Where he came from and where he ended.** Only Epiphanius, two centuries on and on hearsay (S4, E15). Inferential-Thin. Irenaeus's "remained until Anicetus" is the only early word.

**D6. Whose words.** The sermon is printed "Valentinian" in the ANF (E8); the psalm's text is damaged (E10). Each is noted where used.
