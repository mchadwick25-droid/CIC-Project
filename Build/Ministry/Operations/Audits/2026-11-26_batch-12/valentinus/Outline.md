# Outline: Valentinus (`valentinus`)

Line references are to `Source_Sheet.md` rows (S, E, I, D), each of which gives the file and its lines. Files are under `cic/texts/`. The job on the slate: the teacher at Rome whose school taught a heaven of divine powers in pairs, told from his few surviving words, kept by Clement, with his opponents credited openly.

- **The essence:** Valentinus taught at Rome in the middle of the second century and founded a school. By his opponents' accounts it taught a hidden Father, from whom heavenly powers came forth in pairs, and a world that came from the fall of one of them. Tertullian alone says he broke with the church; his students carried the school on in an Italian branch and an Eastern one, and the church's writers who answered it set out against it the faith in one God who made the world. (S1, S2, E2, E4, I1, I2)
- **Also told:**
  - What he taught, in his own words: the heart is unclean while many spirits crowd it, and only the one good Father, through the Son, makes it pure (E7). His sermon: his hearers were deathless from the start and came into death to use it up (E8). Documented, OWN, kept by Clement.
  - What his school taught, by his opponents: the pairs, Unspeakable and Silence, Father and Truth, Word and Life, Man and Church, and the fall from which the world and its lesser maker came (E4, Irenaeus). Tertullian says Valentinus kept these powers inside God, as his thoughts and feelings, and his pupil Ptolemy made them beings outside God (E5). Documented as their accounts; what is his and what is his students' is Contested (D4).
  - The quarrel, from both sides: Clement says the sermon makes some people saved by their nature, so that the commandments are useless and salvation comes without the Savior (E8, E9). The break, in Tertullian's voice only (E2, E3, D3).
  - What came of it: his students, the two branches, and the books written against him (I1, I2), and his words kept only inside those books (I3).
  - Checked against the Library: all held. "A heaven of divine powers in pairs" is held as Irenaeus's account of Valentinus himself (E4) and, as the school's, Hippolytus (E6). "Broke with the church" is Tertullian's alone (E2); Tertullian names no city for the bishopric (D2). "Kept by Clement" is held for the letter and the sermon (E7, E8); the psalm is kept by Hippolytus (E10).
- **Length:** about 3:30 (about 525 words), at most 3:45 (560). Must carry: three quotes, 124 words (40, 49, 35); glosses about 45 words (school, the paired powers, the fall and the lesser maker, saved by nature, the suffering for the faith that won the other man the place, "the true rule"); credited witnesses about 55 words (Clement twice, Irenaeus, Tertullian three times, Hippolytus, the one-witness-for-almost-everything sentence). About 225 words before the beats are told. Six beats, the impact and the ending need about 300 more. The record is thin in fact (no birth, no death, a handful of fragments) but full in argument, so the story runs past three minutes and stops well short of four and a half.
- **Setting:**
  - Time: the middle of the second century, in the reign of the emperor Antoninus (S2; Clement, Tertullian). Irenaeus: he came to Rome under the bishop Hyginus, flourished under Pius and stayed until Anicetus (S1). No year is told: the bishops' years are only the editor's (S1). Spoken: "in the middle of the second century".
  - Place: Rome (S1). Where he was born: not told (S4).
  - Tradition: "Valentinian and other Gnostic Christianities", spoken "the Gnostic Currents" (census). The word "Gnostic" is glossed where heard (below) or left out.
- **Words to explain:**
  - school: "a circle of students around a teacher" (S3)
  - the powers: "heavenly powers" or "divine powers"; avoid "aeons" (E4, E5)
  - the Fullness, Demiurge: do not use; say "the world below" and "a lesser maker of this world" (E4)
  - homily: say "a sermon", "a talk to his hearers" (E8)
  - saved by nature: "saved by what they were born as, not by Christ's coming" (E9; Clement's words "apart from the Saviour's appearance")
  - martyrii praerogativa: "the claim his suffering for the faith gave him" (E2)
  - the true rule: Tertullian's rule of faith, "the faith that there is one God, the maker of the world" (E2; TE 20643–20644), worked into the sentence after Quote 2
  - Gnostic, if used: "those who claimed a special knowledge" (S3)
- **Shape:** Teacher or thinker. The record decides the order: his own words first, then his years at Rome, the break and the teaching by his opponents, then his students and his answerers. Trap avoided: one idea, the heart and the Father, shown through his own letter.
- **Sibling:** none in the batch. On the tree: `irenaeus-of-lyon` (live) tells Irenaeus's answer, his portrait-into-a-dog image and the rule of faith; here Irenaeus is credited for the dates and the pairs, and no quote of his is used. `tertullian-s-voice` (live) tells Tertullian's springs-and-timber line naming Valentinus; barred, not used. The bishopric line stays with this story only (batch header). `clement-of-alexandria` (live) tells Clement's school; here Clement is the keeper of the fragments and their critic. `bardaisan-of-edessa` (live) tells Eusebius's "never wholly washed off" line; here Bardaisan is a clause in the Eastern branch, no quote. `valentinian-and-other-gnostic-christianities` (live) tells the whole current and the 1945 finding; here the finding is one clause.
- **Openings:**
  1. His own words (E7, Quote 1): in a letter to some friends, Valentinus compared the heart to an inn for travellers. It has holes and ruts in it and is often full of dung, and the men who stay there live filthily and take no care of a place that belongs to others (CL 34296–34299). Then Quote 1. The name comes in the first sentence. Leads straight to the essence, and to the one-witness sentence: the words survive because Clement of Alexandria quoted them to argue against him.
  2. Tertullian's bishopric (E2, Quote 2). Use only if Opening 1 is not used; Beat 2 then does not repeat it.
- **The one-witness sentence (early, once):** almost everything known of him comes from men who wrote against him, chiefly Clement, Irenaeus, Tertullian and Hippolytus, and his own words survive only inside their books (I3). Said inside the sentence about Clement, after Opening 1. Never as a stock how-we-know line.
- **Story beats:**
  1. **At Rome.** Irenaeus says Valentinus came to Rome under one bishop and stayed until the time of another, in the middle of the second century (S1, S2). In those same years, Justin Martyr, at Rome, already wrote of "Valentinians", named after their teacher (E1; paraphrase). He founded a school, a circle of students around a teacher (S3). Confidence: Documented (Irenaeus, Justin); the century Widely Accepted.
  2. **The break, in Tertullian's voice.** Tertullian, writing at Carthage early in the next century, alone tells why he left: Quote 2 (E2). Gloss after it: by "the true rule" Tertullian meant the faith in one God, the maker of the world. Tertullian puts it down to ambition, and a wish for revenge such as men who want first place feel (E2, his judgment, credited). In another book he gives a second reason: a restless curiosity that spread to other believers, for which the church at Rome put Valentinus out more than once (E3). Tertullian places that under a later bishop than Irenaeus does: say only that the two do not agree on the time (D1). Reason: as Tertullian gives it, both reasons, both credited. Tertullian names no city for the bishopric and no rival (D2). Confidence: Documented as Tertullian's account; the event Contested (one witness).
  3. **The powers in pairs.** As Irenaeus tells it, a pair beyond any name, Unspeakable and Silence, gave rise to Father and Truth, then to Word and Life, and Man and Church, and to more powers after them. One of the last broke away and fell, and from its fall came the world below and a lesser maker who made it (E4). No reason for the fall is given in this passage; tell it as given. Tertullian says Valentinus held these powers inside God, as his thoughts, feelings and movements (E5). Hippolytus, another opponent, says the school taught that the Father was alone and was all love, and brought forth the first pair because love needs something to love (E6; paraphrase, the school's, not named as Valentinus's own). Why it mattered: against it the church's writers set one God who made heaven and earth (E4; Irenaeus I.10.1, Tertullian's rule). Hippolytus's charge in his own content: he drew this from Pythagoras and Plato, not from the Gospels (E6). Confidence: Documented as their accounts; whose words, Contested (D4).
  4. **Saved by what they were.** In a sermon, he told his hearers they were deathless from the start: Quote 3 (E8). Clement answers that this makes some people saved by their nature, a higher race come down from above. Then the commandments would be useless, he says, and salvation would come without the Savior, and it would not be Christ who abolished death (E8, E9). Plain sense, inside the setup: they came into death only to use it up and end it. Confidence: Documented (OWN; Clement's reading credited).
  5. **His students.** Tertullian says his pupil Ptolemy made the powers into separate beings outside God, and that others, Heracleon among them, struck off paths of their own (E5, I1). Hippolytus says the school split into an Italian branch and an Eastern one, and puts Bardaisan of Edessa in the Eastern one (I1). Confidence: Documented.
  6. **The answer.** Irenaeus wrote five books against the school of Ptolemy, which he calls a bud of Valentinus's (IR 29871–29873, 50552–50554), Tertullian wrote a book against the Valentinians, and Hippolytus a long refutation (I2). For eighteen centuries his school was known almost only through those books, until its own writings were found in Egypt in 1945 (I3; census tradition entry, told as an event). Confidence: Documented; the 1945 finding Widely Accepted.
- **What came of them** (ranked, heaviest first; about a quarter of the story, inside Beats 5–6 and the ending):
  1. **His school outlived him and changed in his students' hands** (I1): Ptolemy, Heracleon, the Italian and Eastern branches, Axionicus at Antioch; later Bardaisan, first a follower (Eusebius) and Ambrose, won from the school by Origen (Eusebius). Several independent later witnesses.
  2. **The answer, and what it defended** (I2): Justin, Polycarp, Irenaeus, Tertullian, Hippolytus, Epiphanius. **Defended:** one God, the maker of the world; salvation by Christ, not by nature.
  3. **His own words survive only in his opponents' books** (I3), until his school's own writings were found in 1945.
  - Who keeps him today: the census tradition entry says no community survives and none claims descent. Card only.
- **Quotes** (3; none is used in another story on the tree; none is a reuse):
  1. **The heart as an inn** (E7). Rendered from the ANF English, CL 34299–34302 (Clement's Greek is not held; English printed before 1931): "So it is with the heart, while no one gives it any thought. It is unclean, a home for many demons. But when the Father, who alone is good, visits it, it is made holy, and it shines with light." Job: the opening. His own voice on the one idea the story shows: the heart made pure only by the good Father. The inn image before it is told in the narrator's words (CL 34296–34299).
  2. **The bishopric** (E2). Rendered from Kroymann's Latin, KR 9633–9634 and 9652–9653 (a clean held print; the ANF English, TE 43109–43113, confirms): "Valentinus had hoped to be made a bishop, because he had the talent and the gift of speech for it. But another man won the place, by the claim his suffering for the faith gave him. Angry at this, he broke away from the church of the true rule." Job: the break, in the one witness's own words, which the narrator cannot give without taking a side. "Suffering for the faith" renders *martyrii*, where the ANF has "confessorship"; "the true rule" renders *authenticae regulae*, where the ANF has "the true faith".
  3. **The sermon on death** (E8). Rendered from the ANF English, CL 39091–39093 (Clement's Greek is not held; English printed before 1931): "You are immortal from the beginning, and children of eternal life. You wanted death shared out to you, to spend it and use it up, so that death might die in you and through you." Job: his own teaching in the quarrel Clement answers; the narrator could not give its force. The ANF prints the speaker as "Valentinian"; its index and Clement's next sentence give Valentinus (E8). The quote stops where the clause on dissolving the world begins, whose "we"/"yourselves" is confused in the print.

- **Connection to carry into the story:** what passed from him was a school, and it went two ways. His students took his teaching on and changed it: Ptolemy made his powers beings outside God, and the school divided into an Italian and an Eastern branch (I1; E5). His opponents, answering it, set out what the church held against it: one God who made heaven and earth (Irenaeus I.10.1; Tertullian's rule, E2) and salvation through Christ, not by birth (Clement, E9). The listener needs to understand that most of what we know of him comes through that second line, the men who answered him, which is why his own few words, kept by Clement, matter. Carry it through what they did (wrote, quoted, answered), never as a "this matters because" sentence.
- **Ending:** on the subject, in Tertullian's account (I1; KR 9663–9666): by Tertullian's day, he says, Valentinus himself was now nowhere, yet there were still Valentinians, named after him, and only one man, Axionicus at Antioch, kept his rules whole. Narrator's words, Tertullian credited. Alternative: the opening image again, the heart that shines with light when the Father visits it (E7), placed after the 1945 finding.

- **Do not say:**
  - "nearly became bishop of Rome" or "a bishop at Rome": say "had hoped to be made a bishop"; Tertullian names no city (D2).
  - "Valentinus came from Alexandria" or "from Egypt": leave his birthplace out (S4).
  - "around 136", "from 137 to 167", or any year: say "in the middle of the second century" (S1, S2).
  - "the church threw him out because of his teaching": say Tertullian gives two reasons, ambition (Against the Valentinians) and restless curiosity that spread to others (Prescription), and that Tertullian alone tells it (D3).
  - "the confessor" or "a martyr was chosen": say "another man won the place, by the claim his suffering for the faith gave him" (E2).
  - "Valentinus taught thirty aeons": the thirty, in fifteen pairs, are Epiphanius's and the school's; Irenaeus's account of the man himself gives the first pairs and "more powers after them" (E4).
  - "Valentinus said, 'love is not love unless there is something to love'": Hippolytus gives it as the school's teaching; the speaker is not named (E6).
  - "a divine spark trapped in human beings": not in the held old texts on Valentinus himself; say what Valentinus's sermon says (Quote 3) and what Clement charges (saved by nature).
  - "he wrote the Gospel of Truth": no held old source says who wrote it (I4).
  - "he died at Rome" or "he died in Cyprus": no held source gives his death (E15).
  - "the Valentinians were a rival church": nothing held about Valentinus himself says so; tell only the break, the school and the students.
  - "Gnostic" without its plain sense the first time: "those who claimed a special knowledge" (S3).
  - "Valentinus was a Platonist": that is the charge of Tertullian and Hippolytus; credit it to Hippolytus as his charge (E6).
  - "Valentinus himself was as yet nowhere": the ANF misreads; say "now nowhere" (I1, KR 9663).
  - A detail for color: no age, no family, no voice or face, no feeling at the break beyond the anger Tertullian names.
