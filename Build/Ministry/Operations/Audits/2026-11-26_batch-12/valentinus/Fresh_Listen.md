## 1. The test (person kit)

- **Who:** filled. A teacher at Rome in the middle of the second century who founded a school.
- **What happened:** filled. He taught, founded a school and broke with the church, which gave two reasons for the break. His students carried the school on, then split, and church writers wrote against it.
- **What it was about:** filled. Powers in pairs, the world coming from a fall, a lesser maker, and people who are immortal by birth (Clement's objection).
- **Why the connection matters:** filled, but weakly. The listener has to work out for themselves that "a lesser maker who made it" clashes with "one God, the maker of the world". Both are in the story, so this is not a finding.

## 2. The opening

The first three sentences give who (Valentinus, a teacher), where (Rome) and when (the middle of the second century). Two things are missing:
- why he was at Rome;
- that he taught as a Christian. A listener with no church background learns this only at "broke with the church".

Neither leaves the listener lost, so neither is a finding.

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing (MINOR)

1. "...while the church's writers who answered it set against it the faith in one God who made the world." Heard once, the inverted "set against it the faith" is lost. Meaning lost: their answer was to insist on one God who made the world. Possible change: "...while the church's writers who answered it held to the faith in one God who made the world."
2. "...Unspeakable and Silence, gave rise to Father and Truth, then to Word and Life, and Man and Church..." You cannot hear capital letters, so the names blur into ordinary words, and the pairs are hard to hear as pairs. "Church" as a heavenly power gets confused with the church in the rest of the story. Meaning lost: these are the names of pairs of powers. This is noted only. The list is the content.
3. "Irenaeus wrote five books against what he called knowledge falsely so called..." The phrase "called... so called" tangles on one hearing. See bare statement B.
4. "...and only one man, Axionicus at Antioch, kept his rules whole." Meaning lost: which rules? Valentinus has not been given any rules. See section 7.

## 4. Bare statements (BLOCKING)

A. "Hippolytus says the school split over what kind of body Jesus had, into an Italian branch, Ptolemy's, and an Eastern one."
- The quarrel is named, but the story never says what each side held or why it mattered.
- Exact change: give the two views as the outline holds them. If the outline does not hold them, cut the quarrel: "Hippolytus says the school split into an Italian branch, Ptolemy's, and an Eastern one."

B. "...against what he called knowledge falsely so called..."
- This is a label. The listener is never told that the school claimed a special knowledge, so the jab means nothing.
- Exact change: explain the claim the label answers, only if the outline holds it. Otherwise cut it: "Irenaeus wrote five books against the school, aimed above all at Ptolemy's followers, which he calls a bud of Valentinus's."
