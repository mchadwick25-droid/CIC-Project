## BLOCKING

**S21.** "Clement answers that this makes some people saved by nature, by what they were born as, a higher race come down from above."
- SOFTENED OR CHANGED. Clement, CL 39095–39097 (*Stromata* IV.13), right after the sermon: "he also, similarly with Basilides, supposes a class saved by nature, and that this different race has come hither to us from above for the abolition of death". Later he says "the peculiar race" (CL 39123). Clement says "different" and "peculiar", not "higher". "Higher" adds a rank the line does not give. ("A germ of superior excellence", CL 32108, describes the knowledge the Valentinians claim, not this race, and it is in another book.)
- **Exact change:** "a higher race come down from above" → "a different race come down from above".

## SUMMARY HOLDS (no change)

- **S3.** "At Rome he founded a school, a circle of students around a teacher." Irenaeus, IR 31519–31520: Valentinus "adapted the principles of the heresy called 'Gnostic' to the peculiar character of his own school"; IR 39969–39970: he "came to Rome in the time of Hyginus, flourished under Pius, and remained until Anicetus". Justin, who wrote at Rome, names "Valentinians" after "the originator" (IR 19254–19256). No line names the city where the school began. But a school that took his name while he was at Rome, under three bishops, is a fair compression.
- **S4.** "By his opponents' accounts, it taught that heavenly powers came forth in pairs from a first pair beyond any name, and that the world came from the fall of one of them." IR 31521–31531: "a certain Dyad… inexpressible by any name… one of which separating from the rest, and falling from its original condition, produced the rest of the universe". Hippolytus gives the pairs as the school's (HIP 9170–9174). Fair.
- **S6.** "His students carried the school on in two branches, while the church's writers who answered it set against it the faith in one God who made the world." HIP 9642–9645: "the doctrine of these has become divided… Oriental, and the other Italian". IR 31390–31391: "in one God, the Father Almighty, Maker of heaven, and earth"; TE 20642–20644: "one only God… the Creator of the world". Fair.
- **S7.** "For eighteen centuries his school was known almost only through their books, until writings from these circles were found in Egypt in 1945." This rests on the census tradition entry (Source_Sheet I3), and every held witness to him is an opponent (E7–E12). The Kit, Part 1, Rigor, says a lost text found "may be told as an event". "Writings from these circles", not "his school's own writings", keeps to the census wording. Holds.
- **S9.** "Those words survive because Clement of Alexandria quoted them to argue against him, and almost everything known of Valentinus comes from men who wrote against him." CL 34303–34309: Clement goes straight on to argue, "or it is a saved nature, as he would have it". No other held copy of the letter exists, and the teaching is described only by Irenaeus, Tertullian and Hippolytus. Fair.
- **S13.** "By the true rule Tertullian meant that faith in one God, the maker of the world." KR 9652: "de ecclesia authenticae regulae". Tertullian's own "rule of faith", TE 20640–20644: "the belief that there is one only God, and that He is none other than the Creator of the world". Fair: it is the writer's own gloss on the writer's own term.
- **S14.** "He puts the break down to ambition, and to the wish for revenge that men who want first place feel." KR 9652–9653: "ut solent animi pro prioratu exciti praesumptione ultionis accendi" (as minds roused for first place are usually set alight by the hope of revenge). The judgment is credited to Tertullian. Holds.
- **S17.** "One of the last broke away and fell, and from its fall came the world below and a lesser maker who made it." IR 31529–31541: one of the twelve, the last group produced, "separating from the rest, and falling… produced the rest of the universe"; then "the mother who had been excluded… brought forth another son, namely, the Demiurge… the supreme ruler of all those things which are subject to him". "Lesser" holds, since he comes from the excluded mother outside the Fullness. "Who made it" holds by his name (Demiurge, craftsman), and Clement says Valentinus makes death "the work of the Creator of the world" (CL 39097–39098). A fair compression.
- **S19.** "In a sermon Clement also keeps, Valentinus told his hearers they had come into death only to use it up and end it." CL 39091–39093: "ye would have death distributed to you, that ye may spend and lavish it, and that death may die in you and by you". "Only" sharpens the purpose clause but does not change it. Holds. The attribution to Valentinus, where the ANF prints "Valentinian", rests on the ANF index ("errors of Valentinus, concerning, 425", CL 58379; the passage is on p. 425, CL 39055) and on Clement's "For he also, similarly with Basilides" (CL 39095).
- **S22.** "Then the commandments would be useless, he says, and salvation would come without the Savior." CL 40955–40960 (*Stromata* V.1): "the precepts both of the Old and of the New Testament are, then, superfluous, if one is saved by nature, as Valentinus would have it… nature would have been able… to have shone forth, apart from the Saviour's appearance." Fair.
- **S24.** "Hippolytus says the school split over what kind of body Jesus had, into an Italian branch, Ptolemy's, and an Eastern one." HIP 9641–9655; WEN 12542–12554: a "great question" over the Logos "born of Mary", the Italians (Heracleon and Ptolemy) saying the body of Jesus was "animal" (ψυχικόν), and the Orientals saying it was "spiritual". No time for the split is given, and none is told. Holds.
- **S27.** "So far had his students moved from him, Tertullian says, that Valentinus himself was now nowhere." KR 9655–9663: the students' paths (Ptolemy, Heracleon, Secundus, Marcus, Theotimus), then "ita nusquam iam Valentinus"; the chapter opens "abscesserunt enim a conditore" (KR 9632). "Now nowhere" follows the Latin, not the ANF's "as yet nowhere". Holds.
