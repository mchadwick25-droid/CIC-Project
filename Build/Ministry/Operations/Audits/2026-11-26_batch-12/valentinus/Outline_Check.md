# Outline check: Valentinus

## My account of the heart (written before reading Outline.md)

From the census entry (`valentinus`: "A teacher at Rome who broke with the church and founded a school that taught a heaven of divine powers in pairs"), the slate job, the source sheet and what he is known for.

- **Essence.** Valentinus was a Christian teacher at Rome in the middle of the second century (Irenaeus: from Hyginus to Anicetus) whose school taught, by its opponents' account, that divine powers came forth in pairs from a nameless first source, that one of them fell, and that this world and its lesser maker came out of that fall. By Tertullian's hostile account he broke with the church; the church's answer to his school drew out its own teaching of one God who made the world.
- **Also told.** His few surviving words, all kept by an opponent (Clement): the heart as an inn crowded by spirits until the one good Father visits it; the sermon telling hearers they were deathless from the start, "that death may die in you". The teacher and his pupils are hard to tell apart (Tertullian: he kept the powers inside God, Ptolemy made them separate beings). Clement's charge: a class "saved by nature". Tertullian's account of the hoped-for bishopric and the break, credited to Tertullian alone.
- **What came of him.** (1) His students carried the school on and changed it: Ptolemy, Heracleon, Italian and Eastern branches, Bardaisan for a time, Ambrose won back by Origen. (2) The answer: Justin, Polycarp, Irenaeus, Tertullian, Hippolytus; **defended**: one God, Maker of heaven and earth, and salvation by Christ, not by nature. (3) Known almost only through his opponents' books until the 1945 finding.
