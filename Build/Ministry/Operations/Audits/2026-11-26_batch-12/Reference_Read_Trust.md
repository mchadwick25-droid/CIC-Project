## Basil of Caesarea: SUMMARY HOLDS

- "...defending himself in a letter against men who, he wrote, were spreading lies about him": Letter 223, "the calumnies directed against me" (`npnf208` 39061–39130).
- "...the learning of this world": a gloss of "the wisdom made foolish by God" (`npnf208` 39131–39135).
- "That waking set the course of his life in Caesarea, an inland city in Asia Minor, in the fourth century." "Inland": Or. 43.34 (`npnf207` 41972–41976). "Asia Minor" and the century are plain glosses.
- "Basil gave up teaching rhetoric... for a life of prayer and shared work": Rufinus HE 11.9, and Life of Macrina 136.
- "He wrote rules... and he built a great lodging... a little way outside the city": Or. 43.34 and 43.63, "the new city".
- "In Pontus, near the Black Sea, Basil founded a community and led it": Or. 43.29.
- "Love does not seek its own good, Basil answers... but a man alone has no one to serve": Longer Rule 7 (Clarke 13209–13215, 13452–13464).
- "The emperor's highest official ordered him to accept the emperor's faith...": Gregory calls him "the prefect" (`npnf207` 42305 ff.). "Highest official" is a plain gloss of the office.
- "...once blocked his road with armed men": Gregory says "a robber band" (Or. 43.58).
- "Lepers driven from their towns were taken in, and Basil greeted them as brothers and tended them himself": "saluted them as brethren ... taking the lead in approaching to tend them" (`npnf207` 42755–42776).
- "Basil closed prayers by giving glory to the Father in two forms": the "full doxology ... in both forms" (On the Holy Spirit 1.3, `npnf208` 8793–8799).
- "The first set the Spirit beside the Father and the Son.": this is the argument of the book, told in the narrator's voice.
- "Some attacked these as new, contradictory words, holding that the Spirit could not be ranked...": 1.3 is joined to 10.24 (`npnf208` 10556–10558). The book itself answers both points.

The rest is HELD, including the three quotes. "Whom, then, will you wash?..." was checked against the Greek, "Τίνα οὖν ἀπονίψεις; τίνα θεραπεύσεις; τίνος ἔσχατος ἔσῃ" (PG 31, 72542–72549). Also HELD: Letter 94 ("besieging your impartial ears ... what harm the government suffers from me", `npnf208` 30011–30023), and the baptism argument ("baptize all nations in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Ghost", On the Holy Spirit 10).

## Epiphanius of Salamis: SUMMARY HOLDS

- "A heresy, to Epiphanius, was a wrong belief held by people led astray ever since Adam, the first man." His words are "κακοδόξου γνώμης ἀνθρωπείας ὑπὸ πεπλανημένων ἀνδρῶν ἐν κόσμῳ ἐσπαρμένης, ἐξότε ἄνθρωπος ἐπὶ γῆς πέπλασται" (HOLL1 13420–13423). "Adam" glosses the first man formed, and "held by" stands for "sown by".
- "Later writers, from Augustine to a council at Nicaea four centuries after Epiphanius, took his book as their list." Augustine: "ordinem sum secutus" (AUG 2411–2426). Nicaea II: "a treatise of eighty chapters ... he passes by none" and "about four hundred years" (MEN 26273–26281, 26327–26331).
- "In old age he carried the same fight to Jerusalem and Constantinople, against the followers of Origen, a Christian teacher who had died long before." Jerome: the "silly old man" (J6 40982 ff.). Letter 91: "my last days". Theotimus: "one who ended his life piously long ago" (SS).
- "He wrote the Panarion about 375.": his own regnal date (HOLL1 13424–13426). The AD conversion is plain.
- "He knew some heresies from books...": "ἐκ φιλομαθίας" (from study, HOLL1 13428).
- "Origen, he writes, taught that souls were angels...": Letter 51 (J6 10813–10818, 10899–10903).
- "Epiphanius held to the plain sense.": the narrator's sum of J6 10914–10922.
- "His letter said he had put a few monks who followed Origen out of the church in Egypt." Letter 90: "cut down the Origenist serpents" and "a few men have been expelled from the Church" (J6 19716–19719, 19747).
- "The historian Socrates says Theophilus wanted revenge on those monks, the Long Brothers, and used Epiphanius...": Socrates 6.10 names "Dioscorus and his brethren". The Long Brothers name is Sozomen's (SS 43604–43605).
- "A book written to answer the council's argument replied with his own letter about the torn curtain.": the Caroline Books, in Mendham's note (MEN 26282–26316). Their purpose is stated in Mendham's title, and their own text answers the council.

The rest is HELD. Checked in particular:
- The Proem quote against the Greek (HOLL1 12387–12393).
- "painted": Jerome's Latin "Velum ... tinctum atque depictum", given in the J6 footnote at 11102.
- "for a year": "For a year before I had heard many of them complain..." (Letter 51).
- "against church rules": John's own message in Socrates 6.14, "You do many things contrary to the canons ... you have made an ordination in the churches under my jurisdiction" (SS 18212–18215). So it is not resting on Zenos's note alone.
- Sozomen's "no danger in following the example of Epiphanius, who was the object of popular praise" (SS 43502–43520).
- "was there from the beginning": "οὖσα μὲν ἀπ᾽ αἰῶνος" (HOLL1 12397).

## John Chrysostom: SUMMARY HOLDS

- "He preached plainly about the poor, and his blunt words to the powerful led to his exile.": Socrates VI.5 (SS 17317–17321, 17368–17374), and the statue sermon leading to the second council (SS 18427–18490).
- "His sermons were read for centuries, and the followers who left the church over his fall came back only when his body came home." "Read": Socrates (SS 17303–17308), Cassian, the Augsburg Confession and Calvin. "Came back ... when his body came home": Socrates VII.45 (SS 21459–21471).
  - A note, not a finding: "only" is a fair compression. Socrates VII.25 (SS 20190–20200) shows an earlier step by Atticus, who had John's name read in the prayers, "by which means he trusted that many would be induced to return". It tells of no return before 438.
- "John was born at Antioch... and as a priest there for twelve years he preached on what the rich owed the poor.": Socrates VI.3, Dialogue ch. V, and Homily 50 (preached at Antioch, by Homily 7's own words).
- "They decked the altar... with gold cups and silk.": "a gold and jewelled cup", "silken garments", "cloths bespangled with gold" (N110 29905–29965).
- "Public games were held at a silver statue... just across the street from the cathedral.": "one-half the breadth of the street separated them" (SS 18434–18436).

The rest is HELD. Checked in particular:
- The Eutropius quote against the Greek, "πάντων αὐτὸν ἐν ἐρημίᾳ παριδόντων, μόνη καθάπερ μήτηρ φιλόστοργος, ὑπὸ τὰ παραπετάσματα αὑτῆς ἔκρυψε..." (PG52 822–827).
- "honor Christ in the poor first": "First fill Him, being an hungered, and then abundantly deck out His table also" (N110).
- "Gothic general": "Gaïnas the Goth" (SS 17388).
- The Oak and its minutes: "forty-five in all ... unanimously" (PHOT 4029–4032).
- The consular dates for 398, 399, 404, 407 and 438.
