## Findings

### B1. BLOCKING, goes to Mark: the heart's "set a stricter rule than the bishops" no longer stands as worded

The approved "Also told" says the turn "set a stricter rule than the bishops" (E6, E7, E11). In the draft, with Cut first 1 to 14 out, only Augustine's line in Beat 3 now carries a stricter rule: he "began to condemn second marriage as fornication" (AUG 2868–2872). That is a later and hostile report, not Tertullian's own word. **"Than the bishops" is carried nowhere.** Only On Modesty set his rule against the bishops ("not the Church which consists of a number of bishops", ANF4 9543–9545; CSEL20 15312–15314), and On Modesty is cut 9. The bishop of Rome in Cut first 3 is about the prophets' peace, not a rule. So this part of the heart cannot stand without a cut item. The researcher cannot change it. Mark rules.

Options for Mark:
- **(a) Recommended. Reword the heart to what the story carries. No change in length.** Replace "and set a stricter rule than the bishops, and he parted from those who refused it (E6, E7, E11)" with: "and kept a stricter rule than the church's (by Augustine's report, on second marriage), and he parted from those who refused it (E6, E8)." The heart is then carried by Beat 3, with the witness credited.
- **(b) Carry the rule in his own words.** Take Cut first 6 back into the beat ("Their rule was stricter than the church's: one marriage only, harder fasts, and no flight from persecution", E7, about 17 words). Reword the heart to "a stricter rule than the church's (E6, E7)". The draft then runs about 685, so at least 10 more words must come out somewhere other than Never cut.
- **(c) Keep the wording.** Take back On Modesty's last sentence, with the edict it needs to make sense (about 50 words of the 76). The draft then runs about 718, which needs about 45 words cut elsewhere. This is not likely to fit without touching what Never cut protects.

### B2. BLOCKING: four beats do not meet the kit; split Beat 3 with no added words

Kit Part 2 asks for "5 to 7 events … fewer for a short kind". At about 650 words, the top of the person range (450 to 675), this story is not a short kind, and no kind kit calls a person short. So four beats do not meet the kit. Beat 3 holds two events in his own order: he came to believe the new prophets, and **later** ("postea", KRO 11745) his side's defense of the Paraclete parted it from the psychics. Splitting them is not filler. It adds no words, and the beats still run once, in order.

Replace Beat 3 (the whole item) with:

> 3. **The turn.** He came to believe that the Paraclete, the Holy Spirit, "the guide into all truth" in his phrase, was speaking anew through prophets in Phrygia (E6; KRO 11732–11733, 11770–11771; Documented). Their rule was stricter than the church's: one marriage only, harder fasts, and no flight from persecution (E7; ANF4 5731–5733, 9760–9765, 11455–11457; Documented; one clause). Why he turned, two later writers differ, and the story credits each (E8, D1; Contested): Jerome says the envy and insults of the clergy at Rome drove him to it (JER 40506–40509; JERL 55627–55629); Augustine says he went over to the Phrygians, whom he had once refuted, and began to condemn second marriage as fornication (AUG 2868–2872). No years: Monceaux's are his own.
> 4. **The parting.** Later, he wrote, his side's acceptance and defense of the Paraclete parted it from the rest, whom he called the "psychics", the merely natural (E6; ANF3 51074–51076; KRO 11745–11746, "postea"; Documented as his words). From whom he parted is read differently (D4): tell only his words. No year.
