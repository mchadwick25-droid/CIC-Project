# Outline: Tertullian

Kind: person (`kinds/person.md`). Items S, E, I, D are in `Source_Sheet.md`, which gives each passage verbatim with file and line. Short names: ANF3, ANF4 (Ante-Nicene Fathers English); OEH1, OEH2, KRO, CSEL20 (his Latin); JER, JERL (Jerome's Lives, English and Latin); JERW (Jerome's letters and treatises, English); EUS (Eusebius, English); AUG (Augustine, Latin); VIN (Vincent, English); MON (Monceaux, modern); CEN (the census).

## The heart

- **The essence:** Tertullian of Carthage, around the year 200, was the foremost early writer of the faith in Latin (S1, S2, D5; Jerome ranks him "chief of the Latin writers after Victor and Apollonius", JER 40495; Vincent gives him "the first place … among the Latins", VIN 13555–13557; Documented as their judgment). Eusebius calls him well versed in Roman law (S5; Documented as Eusebius's word; Contested that he practised). He pleaded the Christians' case before Rome's judges, claimed the Scriptures for the churches the apostles founded, and wrote of God as one substance and three persons in Latin words that stand in his text (E1, E4, E10; Documented). Then he joined the strict New Prophecy (E6; Documented). Cyprian read him every day, but Jerome and Augustine counted him outside the church (I1, I4; Documented as their reports).
- **Also told:** what each book says, with its weight. The Apology: the Christians are condemned for a name by judges who will not learn what it means (E1, E2). The Prescription: arguments from Scripture should not be heard until it is settled whose the Scriptures are, and they belong to the churches the apostles founded (E4, E5). Against Praxeas: God is one substance, and the Son is another person, distinct but not divided (E9, E10). The turn: he came to hold that the Paraclete spoke through new prophets and set a stricter rule than the bishops, and he parted from those who refused it (E6, E7, E11).
- **What came of him:** below, ranked. Defended: the Christians' innocence before Rome's courts; the apostles' churches' title to the Scriptures; one God in three against Praxeas; later, the New Prophecy's rule.

## Setting

- **Time:** around the year 200; Jerome says he flourished under the emperors Severus and Caracalla (S2; JER 40498–40499; JERL 55616–55617; Documented). The Apology is dated about 197 by held editors (E1; EUS-ed 10296–10303; Widely Accepted), but Coxe gives older views of 198 and 204 (ANF3-ed 1443–1446): say "around the year 200". Give no year for any other book; Monceaux's dates are his own (D6). Plain words: "around the year 200".
- **Place:** Carthage, the great city of Roman Africa, today in Tunisia (S1; JER 40496; CEN `modernCountries`). Plain words: "Carthage, in Roman Africa".
- **Tradition:** the Christians who first made the faith's case in Latin; the tree's spoken name is "The Case Made in Latin" (S3; CEN `latin-apologists`). Plain words: "the first Christians to argue for the faith in Latin".
- **Language:** Latin. The New Prophecy came from Phrygia, in inland Asia Minor, today in Turkey (CEN `montanism-the-new-prophecy`).

## Words to explain

- **Apology:** "a defense of the Christians", addressed to the governors who sat as judges (E1; OEH1 11033–11034 "antistites … praesidentibus ad iudicandum"). Say the title once with that sense.
- **governors / "rulers of the Roman Empire":** "the governors who judged cases for Rome" (E1). Not "the emperor".
- **Prescription:** "a plea from the law courts that throws a case out before it is argued" (S6; ANF3-ed 20037–20038, "a demurrer, or formal objection"). Never the word bare.
- **heretics:** in his own terms, those who teach a faith of their own choosing (E4; ANF3 21730–21734). Say "teachers he called heretics".
- **Paraclete:** his own gloss: "the guide into all truth" (E6; KRO 11770–11771); the Holy Spirit Jesus promised. Say "the Paraclete, the Holy Spirit" the first time.
- **New Prophecy:** a movement from Phrygia whose prophets said the Spirit was speaking through them now (E6; CEN `montanism-the-new-prophecy`). Augustine's "Cataphrygians" and the name "Montanists" are its opponents' words (E8).
- **psychics (*psychici*):** his scornful name for church members who refused the new prophets: "the merely natural", or "the unspiritual" (E6; ANF3 51074–51076; KRO 11745–11746).
- **substance (*substantia*):** what God is, one and undivided (E10; KRO 11815–11818 "unius autem substantiae … quia unus deus"). Its modern sense, "material", would mislead: give the plain sense where the word is first heard.
- **person (*persona*):** how the Son is "another" than the Father: a distinction, not a division (E10; KRO 12559–12560). Its modern sense, a separate human being, would mislead: give the plain sense beside Quote 3.
- **Trinity (*trinitas*):** a grown listener knows it; say once that the Latin word stands in his text (E10; KRO 11802).
- **edict:** a public ruling, here a bishop's (E11; ANF4 7178–7189).
- **second marriage:** marrying again after a husband or wife had died (E7, E8).
- **Catholic** (Augustine): "the main church" (I5).

## Shape

**Teacher or thinker.** The question he was answering: in a language the faith had hardly been argued in, who holds the right? Before Rome's judges, the right of the accused to be known before they are condemned; against rival teachers, the right of the apostles' churches to the Scriptures; against Praxeas, the right way to say God is one and yet Father, Son and Spirit; and at the end, whether the Spirit's prophets or the bishops hold the right to forgive. He argues each like a case at law (S6, E5). Who learned it: Cyprian, Novatian, and the Latin readers after them (I1–I3). The trap is a list of books: show each through one moment or one line of his own (Quotes 1–3), and keep the turn as a turn, not a footnote.

## Openings

1. **Chosen: the crowd's cry, and his plea** (E2, E1). If the Tiber flooded the walls or the Nile did not rise over the fields, if no rain fell, if the earth shook, if famine or plague came, the crowd had one cry, he wrote: "Christians to the lion!" (ANF3 3995–4002; OEH1 27573–27576). Around the year 200, Tertullian of Carthage put this before the governors who judged for Rome, in a defense of the Christians, his Apology. If they would not hear the case openly, he asked that the truth reach them in writing, and he said of the truth: Quote 1 (ANF3 1457–1465; OEH1 11256–11261). Then the essence.
2. Not chosen: Jerome, nearly two centuries on, hearing from an old man of Italy that Cyprian never let a day pass without reading Tertullian (I1). It reaches to the end of the story, and its chain of witnesses needs room the opening does not have.

## Story beats

(The opening and the essence fill the first minute, about 145 words.)

1. **The Apology.** His case: the governors hated the very name of Christian without knowing what it was, and hatred of a thing unknown is unjust (E1; ANF3 1472–1476; Documented). Those who came to know it, he said, laid down their hatred and became its followers (ANF3 1488–1490). He had once laughed at the faith himself, he told them, since no one is born a Christian (S7; ANF3 2710–2712; OEH1 18939–18940; Documented). Why the crowd cried out: they blamed the Christians for every disaster (E2; ANF3 3995–3997). Why the governors' courts were closed to a defense: recent harsh private trials, as he says (E1; ANF3 1455–1459). Confidence: Documented, his own book.
2. **The Prescription.** Teachers he called heretics, among them Marcion and Valentinus, argued from Scripture (E4, E5; ANF3 20755–20756, 21744–21746). He answered with a plea from the law courts that throws a case out before it is argued: first settle whose the Scriptures are (S6, E4; ANF3 20759–20768, 20860–20866). The faith passed from Christ to the apostles, and from them to the churches they founded, which anyone could visit: Corinth, Philippi, Ephesus, Rome (E4; ANF3 20938–20941, 21657–21676). A heretic, he said, teaches a faith of his own choosing, and so has no right to the Scriptures (ANF3 21730–21734, 21740–21742). Then Quote 2. Explain it: the heretics' side is known here only from his book; give it in its own content, that they argued from the Scriptures (ANF3 20755–20756). Confidence: Documented as his argument; no year.
3. **The turn.** He came to believe that the Paraclete, the Holy Spirit, "the guide into all truth" in his phrase, was speaking anew through prophets in Phrygia (E6; KRO 11732–11733, 11770–11771; Documented). Their rule was stricter than the church's: one marriage only, harder fasts, and no flight from persecution (E7; ANF4 5731–5733, 9760–9765, 11455–11457; Documented; one clause). Later, he wrote, his side's acceptance and defense of the Paraclete parted it from the rest, whom he called the "psychics", the merely natural (E6; ANF3 51074–51076; KRO 11745–11746; Documented as his words). From whom he parted is read differently (D4): tell only his words. Why he turned, two later writers differ, and the story credits each (E8, D1; Contested): Jerome says the envy and insults of the clergy at Rome drove him to it (JER 40506–40509; JERL 55627–55629); Augustine says he went over to the Phrygians, whom he had once refuted, and began to condemn second marriage as fornication (AUG 2868–2872). No years: Monceaux's are his own.
4. **Against Praxeas.** Praxeas had brought to Rome from Asia, by Tertullian's account, the teaching that the Father himself came down into the Virgin, was born and suffered, and was himself Jesus Christ (E9; ANF3 51010–51016, 51038–51041; KRO 11692–11693, 11727–11729). Tertullian also says Praxeas pressed the bishop of Rome to withdraw his peace from the new prophets (ANF3 51046–51053; KRO 11732–11736): so, he says, Praxeas drove out prophecy and crucified the Father (ANF3 51054–51057; told in narration, not quoted). Why Tertullian wrote: to answer that teaching (E9). His answer: the one God has a Son, his Word (ANF3 51101–51102; KRO 11772–11773). God said "Let there be light", and the Word appeared; God spoke, and through the Word the rest was made; so there is one who commands and another who makes (E10; ANF3 51876–51894; KRO 12546–12558). Then Quote 3, with the plain sense of substance and person beside it. The Latin words *trinitas*, *persona* and *substantia* stand in this book (E10; KRO 11802, 12559, 12561; Documented). He wrote it after his turn: he says the Paraclete had taught him better (ANF3 51098–51099; KRO 11770; Documented).
5. **On Modesty.** A bishop, whom he mocks with the pagan high priest's title, issued an edict: to those who had done penance, he forgave adultery and fornication (E11; ANF4 7178–7189; CSEL20 12495–12499; Documented). Tertullian answered that the Paraclete, speaking through the new prophets, had said the church could forgive a sin but would not, lest people sin again (ANF4 9468–9473; CSEL20 15213–15214). Only the church of the Spirit, through a spiritual man, could forgive such sins, he wrote, not the bishops counted as a body (ANF4 9543–9545; CSEL20 15312–15314; told in narration). Explain it: the edict is the other side, given in its own words (E11). Do not name the bishop (E11). Confidence: Documented; "probably his last surviving book" is Monceaux's (MON 17467–17469), so say nothing of its place.

## What came of him (heaviest first)

1. **Cyprian read him as his master.** Jerome met an old man in Italy, Paul of Concordia, who as a youth had met Cyprian's aged secretary at Rome. The secretary said Cyprian, bishop of Carthage a generation later, never let a day pass without reading Tertullian, and would say, "Give me the master" (I1; JERL 55618–55626; JER 40500–40505; Documented as Jerome's report, hearsay through two people; the English wrongly makes Paul the secretary, D2). Jerome also says Cyprian took him for his master "as his writings prove", yet did not follow him into the New Prophecy (JERW 18969–18972; Documented as Jerome's). One clause each, by the slate.
2. **Novatian kept his book on God.** Jerome calls Novatian's On the Trinity "a sort of epitome of the work of Tertullian", a kind of summary of it (I2; JER 40895–40897; Documented as Jerome's judgment). Modern doubts go to the card.
3. **Read, and judged.** Augustine says many of his short works, "most eloquently written", were still read (I3; AUG 2827–2828). But Augustine lists his followers among the heresies and says he became a heretic by the turn (I4, E8; AUG 2827, 2868–2873), and Jerome, answering a writer who cited him, said he would say no more of Tertullian than that he was not a man of the church (I4; JERL 15618–15620; JERW 33677–33681). Documented as each one's verdict. Vincent's praise and blame: card.
4. **His followers.** A small group at Carthage bore his name, the Tertullianists, dwindling down to Augustine's day; then the very few left joined the main church and handed over their church building, still well known (I5; AUG 2829–2835; Documented as Augustine's report).

Who keeps him today: no held source or census tradition entry says; leave out.

## Connection to carry into the story

What passed from him was a Latin way of arguing the faith, made in the courtroom's terms and kept by Latin readers after him. The listener must understand three things, through what people did:
- **Why it was new:** Jerome names only two Latin writers of the faith before him, Victor and Apollonius, and ranks him first after them (D5; JER 40495; JERL 55610–55613); he made Latin carry the case: a plea that throws out a case, a title to an estate (S6, E5), and words for God, person and substance (E10). - **Who carried it:** Cyprian read him daily and called him the master, and Novatian's book on God was, Jerome says, a summary of his (I1, I2). The writer must say that Cyprian did not follow him into the New Prophecy (JERW 18969–18972), so the listener does not think his whole line passed on.
- **Why it split:** the same readers who kept his words would not keep him. Augustine names him a heretic, and Jerome says he was not a man of the church, both because of the turn (I4, E8). His own followers lasted at Carthage only until Augustine's day (I5).
This comes through the acts above, never a sentence saying why he matters.

## Do not say

- "A lawyer", "an advocate" or "trained as an advocate" as fact. Say Eusebius calls him well versed in Roman law (S5, D3).
- "The first Christian to write in Latin", or any "first". Say "the foremost early writer of the faith in Latin" (D5).
- "In 197" or any year but "around the year 200"; no year for the Prescription, the turn, Against Praxeas or On Modesty (S2, D6).
- "His last book" (E11).
- "The Christians to the lions": the Latin is singular, "the lion" (E2).
- "The blood of the martyrs is the seed of the church" (E3).
- "He invented the words Trinity, person and substance", "he coined them", or "the first to call God a Trinity". Say the Latin words stand in his text (E10). The Greek word for a triad is older (E10).
- "The Latin church took its words for God from him" as the narrator's claim: no held old source says it (E10).
- "The Council of Nicaea used his words": no held source says it.
- "Praxeas taught that the Father died on the cross" as fact: credit Tertullian, "by his account" (E9). Do not call Praxeas a bishop, and do not name the bishop of Rome or the bishop of the edict (E9, E11).
- "He wrote Against Praxeas as a loyal son of the church": he wrote it after the turn (E9).
- "He left the church at Carthage" or "he was expelled", "excommunicated", "condemned by a council": no held source records it (D4, I4).
- "Never made a saint": no held source says it (I4).
- "He founded his own church" as fact: credit Augustine, who says he split from the New Prophecy too and spread gatherings of his own (E8).
- Why he turned, as the narrator's fact: credit Jerome and Augustine, each, side by side (D1).
- "Montanists" or "Cataphrygians" in the narrator's voice for his side: say "the New Prophecy" (E8).
- "Psychics" bare: give its plain sense (E6).
- "Persona means a mask" or "a character in a play" (Leave out).
- "Paul of Concordia was Cyprian's secretary": Paul had met the secretary (D2).
- "Novatian copied" or "summarized his book" as fact: Jerome's judgment, credited (I2).
- "Priest" or "presbyter": his office rests on Jerome alone; leave it out (E8).

- That the crowd's cry was heard in Rome only: he lists the Tiber and the Nile; say "the crowd" (E2).

## Cut first

In order, to bring about 745 words to about 650:

1. Beat 1's sentence that he had once laughed at the faith himself (about 20 words). **Card.**
2. Quote 3's last sentence, "Yet the meaning forces me …" (about 17 words); keep the narrator's line before it that there is one who commands and another who makes. **Card** (with the full rendering).
3. Beat 4's clause on Praxeas and the bishop of Rome's peace with the new prophets (about 30 words). **Card.**
4. What came 2, Novatian, to half a clause inside What came 1, or out (about 20 words). **Card.**
5. Beat 2's list of churches: keep Ephesus and Rome, drop Corinth and Philippi (about 5 words). **Card.**
6. If still long: Beat 3's clause on the stricter rules (about 15 words). **Leave out** (the `montanism-the-new-prophecy` story tells it).

Never cut: the crowd's cry and Quote 1 (the opening), the gloss of the Prescription, the plain sense of substance and person, the two witnesses on the turn, and Augustine's and Jerome's verdicts.

**For the card, not the story:** "the blood of Christians is seed" and "kill us, torture us, condemn us, grind us to dust" (E3); "Haec et nos risimus aliquando … Fiunt, non nascuntur Christiani" (S7); Prescription 37's opening questions and the Marcion, Valentinus and Apelles lines (E5); Against Praxeas 2, "three … not in substance, but in form … yet of one substance" (E10); "he drove away prophecy … crucified the Father" (E9; also on the census edge); the Paraclete's saying in On Modesty and "not the Church which consists of a number of bishops" (E11); Jerome's father the centurion and "celebrated in the speech of all the churches" (S4); Jerome's Letters 58 and 70 (I3); Vincent's "every sentence a victory" and "more eloquent by far than faithful", with Hilary's line (I3, I4); Eusebius on the Apology in Greek (I6); Benson, Harnack, Fausset, McGiffert, Monceaux and Coxe for the Modern works list, each with what it covers.
