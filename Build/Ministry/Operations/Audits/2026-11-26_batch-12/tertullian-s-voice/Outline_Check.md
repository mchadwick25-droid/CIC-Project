# Outline check: Tertullian (`tertullian-s-voice`)

## My account of the heart, written before reading `Outline.md`

From the census entry (`why`, `knownFor`), the slate's job and the source sheet.

- **Essence.** A convert at Carthage, around 200, who became the foremost early writer of the faith in Latin (Jerome and Vincent rank him first among the Latins; not "the first" in order). He defended the Christians before Rome's governors, claimed the Scriptures for the churches the apostles founded, and gave the Latin church words for God, one substance in three persons. Later he took up the strict New Prophecy and was judged outside the church; his followers outlasted him at Carthage, and Cyprian read him daily as "the master". He is on the tree as the voice that opened Latin Christian argument (census home `latin-apologists`).
- **Also told.** The Apology: the charge is only the name; the governors condemn what they do not know; the crowd blames the Christians for every disaster; the more they are cut down, the more they grow (Apology 1, 40, 50). The Prescription: a law-court plea that throws the heretics' case out before it is argued, because the Scriptures belong to the apostles' churches (chs. 15, 19, 21, 36, 37). Against Praxeas: against a teacher who, by his hostile account, said the Father himself was born and suffered; one substance, three, distinct not divided, with *trinitas*, *persona*, *substantia* in his text (chs. 1, 2, 12). The turn to the New Prophecy, in his own words (Against Praxeas 1) and its stricter rules; why, two later witnesses differ (Jerome: envy and insults of the Roman clergy; Augustine: condemning second marriage, then a split from them too). On Modesty: the church of the Spirit against a church that is a number of bishops.
- **What came of him, heaviest first.** Cyprian took him as his master (Jerome, Lives 53 and Letter 84); his Latin words for God (in his text; that he coined them is not held in an old source); Novatian's On the Trinity called a sort of epitome of his work (Jerome); read and praised (Augustine, Vincent, Jerome) and judged for his turn (Jerome, Augustine, Vincent via Hilary); the Tertullianists dwindling at Carthage until they handed over their basilica (Augustine). **Defended:** the Christians' innocence; the apostles' churches' title to the Scriptures; one God in three; later the New Prophecy.
- **Shape.** Teacher or thinker.

## Verdict: FIX

The outline's heart matches mine in substance and leaves out no major contribution. The beats tell the essence in the first minute and follow the story once, in order, with the ending (Jerome's "is said to have lived to a decrepit old age") held back. Quotes 1 to 3 are verbatim against their prints and my own renderings agree with the outline's in sense. The length holds: the independent count (`../Length_Count.md`, 638 after Cut first) and my recount (quotes about 84 words after cut 2, 13 glosses, the beats' facts) both sit under 650. ## Findings

### BLOCKING 1. The essence moves Against Praxeas before the turn

Outline line 7: "... and wrote of God as one substance and three persons in Latin words that stand in his text (E1, E4, E10; Documented). Then he joined the strict New Prophecy." Against Praxeas was written after the turn, in his own words: "more especially since we have been better instructed by the Paraclete" (ANF3 51098–51099; KRO 11768–11770 "nunc magis ... instructiores per paracletum"), and "we ... subsequently withdrew from the carnally-minded on our acknowledgment ... of the Paraclete" (ANF3 51074–51076; KRO 11745–11746). Beat 4 and Do not say (line 173) say so too. Heard in the first minute, "Then" tells the listener the opposite: a detail moved onto the wrong event. Only the order changes. The heart's substance stays as approved.

Replace, in line 7, from "He pleaded the Christians' case" to "(E6; Documented)." with:

> He pleaded the Christians' case before Rome's judges and claimed the Scriptures for the churches the apostles founded (E1, E4; Documented). Then he joined the strict New Prophecy (E6; Documented), and after that turn wrote of God as one substance and three persons, in Latin words that stand in his text (E9, E10; ANF3 51098–51099; Documented).

### BLOCKING 2. "Jerome ranks him" rests on Richardson's English, not Jerome's Latin

Essence (line 7), Do not say (line 163) and sheet D5 credit Jerome with ranking him "chief of the Latin writers" (JER 40495). Jerome's Latin says "nunc demum primus post Victorem et Apollonium Latinorum ponitur" (JERL 55610–55611): "now at last he is placed first of the Latins after Victor and Apollonius". In a catalogue of writers, that is his place in Jerome's list, after two Latins Jerome put earlier. It is not a judgment of rank. Only Vincent ranks him: "as Origen holds by far the first place among the Greeks, so does Tertullian among the Latins" (VIN 13555–13557; 434, about two centuries later). So "foremost" stands, credited to Vincent alone.

- Line 7, replace "(S1, S2, D5; Jerome ranks him "chief of the Latin writers after Victor and Apollonius", JER 40495; Vincent gives him "the first place … among the Latins", VIN 13555–13557; Documented as their judgment)" with: "(S1, S2, D5; Vincent of Lérins, about two centuries later, gives him "the first place … among the Latins", VIN 13555–13557; Documented as Vincent's judgment. Jerome's Latin, "primus post Victorem et Apollonium Latinorum ponitur", JERL 55610–55611, places him in his list after two earlier Latin writers: no ranking, card)".
- Line 163, replace "credit it the first time it is heard: Jerome ranks him so, and so does Vincent (JER 40495; VIN 13555–13557)." with "credit it the first time it is heard, to Vincent of Lérins, writing about two centuries later (VIN 13555–13557). Never say Jerome ranks him first: Jerome's Latin places him in his list after Victor and Apollonius (JERL 55610–55611); Richardson's "regarded as chief" (JER 40495) is the translator's."
- Names: add "Vincent of Lérins: link, witness (the essence). Say "Vincent of Lérins" once." Who tells it, line 155: replace "card only (I3, I4)" with "beside the first place among the Latins (the essence); his praise and blame otherwise card (I3, I4)".
- Sheet D5: correct to match (Jerome = place in his list; Vincent = rank).

### BLOCKING 3. The Tertullianists are named without why they existed

What came 4 (line 85) names "his followers ... the Tertullianists" but never says why there was a group of his own, apart from the New Prophecy. Augustine gives the reason, in the passage the outline already cites but stops short of: "et postmodum etiam ab ipsis divisus, sua conventicula propagavit" (AUG 2872–2873): "and afterwards, divided from them too, he spread gatherings of his own." Without it a listener takes the Tertullianists for the New Prophecy itself. This is a thing named without the explanation Part 1 requires.

- What came 4, line 85, replace "A small group at Carthage bore his name, the Tertullianists, dwindling down to Augustine's day;" with "Augustine says he later broke from the New Prophecy too and gathered followers of his own (E8; AUG 2872–2873; Documented as Augustine's report). At Carthage they bore his name, the Tertullianists, and dwindled down to Augustine's day;".
- Do not say: add "The Tertullianists were the New Prophecy" or "his New Prophecy followers": Augustine says he broke from the New Prophecy too, and the Tertullianists were his own gatherings (AUG 2872–2873).

### BLOCKING 4. Library sweep misses Optatus, an African witness who counts him a champion of the Catholic church

Optatus of Milevis, Against Parmenian I.9, Latin `optatus_libri-vii-lat_ziwsa1893-csel26-tei.txt` 492–496: "Marcion Praxeas Sabellius Ualentinus et ceteri usque ad Cataphrygas temporibus suis a Uictorino Petauionensi et Zephyrino Urbico et a Tertulliano Carthaginensi et ab aliis adsertoribus ecclesiae catholicae superati sunt". English: `optatus_against-the-donatists.txt` 139 ("all of whom were confuted in their time by Victorinus of Pettau, by Zephyrinus of Rome, by Tertullian of Carthage, and by other champions of the Catholic Church"). The apparatus (lines 518–523) says some manuscripts place "usque ad Cataphrygas" after "Tertulliano Carthaginensi". Optatus was an African bishop of the later fourth century. Neither the sheet nor the outline holds him. He is the one held later witness who counts Tertullian among the church's defenders against Marcion, Praxeas and Valentinus, the very fights this story tells. Left out, What came 3 carries only the "outside the church" side of the verdict (Jerome, Augustine), and the record's own counterweight drops out: "never carry one side's view forward".

- Sheet: add as I-item (LATER, African, later fourth century; Documented as Optatus's word), with the apparatus note. Add it to Library sweep "Used".
- What came 3, after the Jerome sentence (line 84), add: "Yet Optatus, a bishop in Africa a century and a half later, still named Tertullian of Carthage among the champions of the Catholic church who had overcome Marcion, Praxeas and Valentinus in their time (Optatus I.9; CSEL 26, `optatus_libri-vii-lat_ziwsa1893-csel26-tei.txt` 492–496; English `optatus_against-the-donatists.txt` 139; Documented as Optatus's word)." Names: "Optatus: witness, by name or as 'an African bishop'". Who tells it: add him.
- Length: findings 2 to 4 add about 45 words over 638. To hold 650, add to Cut first, in order: (7) Beat 1's sentence that those who came to know it laid down their hatred (ANF3 1488–1490, about 15 words), **Card**; (8) What came 3's Augustine line that his short works, "most eloquently written", are still read (about 15 words), **Card**; and apply MINOR 3 below (about 15 words saved). If it still runs over, the researcher says so, and it goes to Mark under the approved length's rule. It does not drop Optatus.

### MINOR 1. Quote 3: "three who hold together"

"in tribus cohaerentibus" (KRO 12561; OEH2 43867–43868) has no word for "who". The outline faults Holmes for supplying "(Persons)", then itself makes the three personal. Replace "Everywhere I hold one substance in three who hold together." with "Everywhere I hold one substance in three that hold together." My rendering otherwise matches the outline's. Note on Cut first item 2: once "Yet the meaning forces me …" is cut, "Everywhere I hold" stands without its concession. That is acceptable, because both prints make it his own position (Kroymann's "(etsi) … teneo", Oehler's "teneam", each followed by "tamen").

### MINOR 2. Cyprian's secretary was not certainly "aged", and the meeting was not said to be in Italy

"beati Cypriani, jam grandis aetatis, notarium" (JERL 55620–55621): the phrase "already of great age" can go with Cyprian or with the secretary. And Jerome says only that he saw Paul "of Concordia, a town of Italy" (JERL 55618–55619), not where. In What came 1 (line 82), replace "Jerome met an old man in Italy, Paul of Concordia, who as a youth had met Cyprian's aged secretary at Rome." with "Jerome met an old man from Concordia, a town of Italy, who as a youth had met Cyprian's secretary at Rome." Do not say: add "Cyprian's aged secretary", or "Jerome met him in Italy".

### MINOR 3. The slate gives Cyprian a clause here

Slate: "Jerome's 'Give me the master' is a clause here." What came 1 plans two sentences of chain. Add to What came 1: "In the story, one clause: Cyprian, Jerome heard from an old man who had it from Cyprian's secretary, never let a day pass without reading him, and would say, 'Give me the master'; Jerome adds that Cyprian did not follow him to the New Prophecy (JERW 18969–18972)." This keeps the hearsay credit and the Connection's required point, and saves about 15 words.

### MINOR 4. "Say so once" rests on McGiffert, and the kit doesn't require it
