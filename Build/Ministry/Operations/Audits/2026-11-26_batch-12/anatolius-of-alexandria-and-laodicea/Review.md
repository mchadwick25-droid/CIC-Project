- Now: "Later bishop of Laodicea in Syria, he wrote a rule for finding Easter, and three and four centuries on, Irish churchmen and the defenders of Rome's Easter both claimed him."
- Where the trust readers disagree: the second read marks it SUMMARY HOLDS. By the bar, it blocks. The outline's Do not say already bars "As bishop he wrote…", and a sentence that sets a date or place the source does not give is an assumption added. Until it is fixed or a third reader clears it, a finding from either trust read stays BLOCKING.
- The appositive reads "once bishop, he wrote". No old source dates or places the book (EUS 42769–42773). The outline's Cut first 17 already gives the right wording; the draft left it.
- Change: "He later became bishop of Laodicea in Syria. He also wrote a rule for finding Easter, and three and four centuries on, Irish churchmen and the defenders of Rome's Easter both claimed him." (+3)
- Do not say, to add: "'Later bishop of Laodicea, he wrote…' or any wording that sets the Easter book in his years as bishop → 'He later became bishop of Laodicea in Syria. He also wrote…' (E6; Cut first 17)."

**B2. Paul of Samosata's teaching told as the narrator's fact.** (Trust, SOFTENED OR CHANGED; the first-time listener lost the subject in the same sentence, MINOR, merged here.) **Needs the outline:** the outline's Words to explain and Cut first 22 hold the uncredited wording.

**B3. Bede's closing line: widened, softened, and its meaning lost.** (Trust, SOFTENED OR CHANGED; second trust, SOFTENED OR CHANGED, BLOCKING; skeptic, BLOCKING; first-time listener, MINOR.) **Needs the outline:** Ending 2 holds "the Irish" and "skilled reader".
- Now: "Of the Irish before Whitby, Bede wrote that any skilled reader could tell whether they followed, as they believed, the holy and praiseworthy Father Anatolius."
- Source: "the northern province of the Scots, and all the nation of the Picts … believing that they therein followed the writings of the holy and praiseworthy Father Anatolius; the truth of which every skilful person can discern" (BEDE 5752–5758; "peritus quisque", BEDE-lat 18702–18709). Latin: "aestimans se … sancti ac laude digni patris Anatolii scripta secutam. Quod an uerum sit, peritus quisque facillime cognoscit" (BEDE-lat 18705–18709). Both trust reads agree on three faults: Bede limits the claim to the northern Irish and the Picts, and sets the southern Irish apart, so "the Irish" widens it; the second read adds that "facillime" ("very easily"), which carries the edge of Bede's doubt, is dropped, which softens its force; and "scripta" ("the writings") is dropped, with "skilled reader" standing in for "peritus quisque" (any skilled person).
- Where the listeners disagree: the bar blocks a genuine failure to understand on one hearing. Both listeners, separately, could not tell whether Bede thought the Irish followed Anatolius or not. This is the last line, and it carries the one doubt the outline's connection names ("Bede doubted the Irish claim"). Lost there, the ending fails, so it blocks. The jump back ("before Whitby") is not the fault by itself: it is Bede's judgment, written in 731, which the outline chose as the ending. Once Bede's doubt is said plainly first, it is heard as his comment, not as an event told out of order.
- Change: "Bede doubted the Irish claim. The northern Irish and Picts before Whitby, he wrote, believed they followed the writings of the holy and praiseworthy Father Anatolius, and anyone skilled could very easily tell whether that was true." (+12). This merges both trust readers' changes with the listeners' finding. "Doubted" is the outline's own word (Connection; What came 3), and the Do not say bar on "Bede proved the Irish wrong" still holds. The second read's "in Aidan's time" is left out: "before Whitby" places it, and Aidan would be one more name heard once that does not act in the story. Its "skilled in the reckoning" becomes "skilled", which is closer to "peritus quisque" and costs no gloss.
- Do not say, to add: "'the Irish' alone for Bede's doubt, or his doubt without 'very easily' or 'the writings' → 'the northern Irish and Picts … believed they followed the writings of … Anatolius, and anyone skilled could very easily tell whether that was true' (I1c, BEDE-lat 18705–18709); Bede's line never without 'Bede doubted the Irish claim' before it."
