# Outline check: Anatolius of Alexandria and Laodicea

## My own account of the heart (written before reading Outline.md)

From the census teaser, the source sheet and what he is known for.

- **Essence:** A learned Alexandrian, ranked by Eusebius first among the ablest men of his day, who during the siege of the city's palace quarter talked the council into letting the "useless" leave and, under that cover, got almost all the besieged out, his church's people first; he later became bishop of Laodicea in Syria and wrote an Easter book that fixed the Passover's first month after the spring equinox on a nineteen-year cycle.
- **Also told:** the siege as Eusebius reports it (report, "they say"): the famine, the deacon outside winning safety for deserters, the council's anger at a call for peace, his speech, the vote, men in women's dress leaving by night. Then the road to Laodicea (ordained by Theotecnus at Caesarea as successor, called to the synod on Paul of Samosata, detained at Laodicea). Then the Easter book in his own words: the cycle of nineteen years, 22 March, the error of placing the first month before the equinox, the rule as old as the Jews before Christ.
- **What came of him:** heaviest, his name in the Irish-Roman Easter quarrel (Columbanus to Gregory, Columbanus to the Gallic bishops, Bede on Aidan, both sides at Whitby in 664), much of it through a Latin book under his name whose authorship is contested; then his fame for learning (Jerome, Syriac writers); then later reckoners using his extract (Elias, Bede's own letter). Defended: Easter's first month after the equinox.
- Shape: Founder or leader, as the slate proposed, though the record is about acts (the siege) and a book; the risk is that "bishop" stands in for acts.

**B2. Connection claims the two sides quoted "two different things".** The Connection and What came 2 say Colman drew on the Latin book and Wilfrid on "his Greek words that Eusebius kept", so "the two sides were quoting two different things under one man's name". The Latin book also sets out a cycle of nineteen years (LATCAN 14840–14843, 15187–15192, 15308–15309). Bede's Wilfrid names no book (BEDE 8134–8150). The split is a reading of the texts that no old source makes, and it is wrong as stated. Correction:
- In **Connection**, replace "The listener must understand that the two sides were quoting two different things under one man's name, and that Bede doubted the Irish claim (I1c)." with: "The listener must understand that both sides called him holy and claimed his authority: Colman for keeping Easter from the moon's fourteenth day to its twentieth, Wilfrid for the nineteen-year cycle and for counting his days the Egyptian way, which he said the Irish did not keep. Bede doubted the Irish claim (I1c)."
- In **What came 2**, replace "The listener must hear why one man served both sides: Colman's range is in the Latin book under his name; Wilfrid's nineteen-year cycle is in his Greek words that Eusebius kept (I1d, D5)." with: "The listener must hear that both sides claimed the same man, each crediting him with a different rule (I1d, D5)."
- Add to **Do not say**: "'Colman quoted the Latin book and Wilfrid the Greek' or 'they quoted two different books' → each claimed Anatolius; no old source says which text either used (BEDE 8117–8150; LATCAN 14840–14843)."

**B3. The essence implies he wrote the Easter book as bishop of Laodicea.** "Kept as bishop of Laodicea in Syria on his way to a synod, he wrote a rule for finding Easter…" No old source says where or when he wrote it (sheet E6, last line). This moves a detail onto the wrong event. Correction:
- In **The essence**, replace "Kept as bishop of Laodicea in Syria on his way to a synod, he wrote a rule for finding Easter on a cycle of nineteen years" with "He was later kept as bishop of Laodicea in Syria, on his way to a synod. He wrote a rule for finding Easter on a cycle of nineteen years".
- In beat 5, begin: "Eusebius kept part of his Easter book; when or where he wrote it, no old source says, so the story does not place it."
- Add to **Do not say**: "'As bishop he wrote…' or 'at Laodicea he wrote…' → he wrote an Easter book; no time or place for it (E6)."

## MINOR

1. **The deacon's office rests on an old source, not on McGiffert.** Dionysius's letter, as Eusebius quotes it, names "the deacons Faustus, Eusebius" (EUS 40140–40141, 40420), and Eusebius ties that man to this one (EUS 42662–42664). In **Names** and beat 2, cite these lines for "a deacon of the church", marked old.
2. **"Columbanus, an Irish abbot in Gaul"** rests only on Barmby (COL-ed 4174–4187, modern). The tree has a Columbanus page, so he needs only his role here. Replace with "Columbanus, an abbot who kept his homeland's Easter" (COL-lat 20791–20792, "traditioni patriae meae", OCR "potrim mec"), or cite an old line for "Irish" and "in Gaul".
3. **Columbanus's letter to the Gallic bishops (Cut first 2)**: "over the Easter tables Gaul used" is not in the line. Replace with "over Victorius, who, he wrote, had written lately and in doubt" (COL-lat 20796–20797, "juxta Victorium nuper dubie scribentem"). If kept, a one-clause gloss is needed: "Victorius, the author of an Easter table" (cite a held line, or leave him out).
4. **Quote 3 wording.** "That is why we say this" and "and no chance one" are unclear heard once. My rendering: "So we say that those who put the first month in it, and count the Passover's fourteenth day by it, make no small mistake, and no careless one." Replace the outline's rendering with that. Change the plain sense alongside to: "'it' is the last twelfth of the sun's yearly path, the weeks just before the spring equinox" (EUS 42810–42815).
5. **Beat 4, "After the war he left for Syria".** Caesarea is in Palestine. Eusebius says both men "came after the aforesaid war from Alexandria" to Laodicea (EUS 42764–42767). Replace with "After the war he left Alexandria".
6. **Beat 4 / What came 2: "The king ruled for Rome".** Bede has the king rule for Peter, "who is proved to have the keys" (BEDE 8204–8218). Replace with "The king ruled for Peter, who held the keys of heaven, and so for Rome's Easter."
7. **Whitby "in northern England"**: cite BEDE 7951–7952 ("the province of the Northumbrians") and say "in Northumbria, in the north of England".
8. **Quote 1 split.** The opening takes its first three sentences, and beat 3 the rest after the peace proposal. That is allowed (the hook may reach ahead), but Cut first 5 ("first four sentences") does not match the split. Change Cut first 5 to: "Quote 1 in beat 3 reduced to its fourth sentence; the rest in paraphrase, keeping 'only men and youths'."
9. **Library sweep: Fragments of the Books on Arithmetic** are held in English (ANF6 15411–15470, from Fabricius; attribution modern). The sheet lists the arithmetic work only as "not held" in Greek or Latin. Add them under Library sweep as held, card only, marked modern for the attribution.
10. **Do not say additions:** "'a revolt against Rome' or any cause for the siege → none; Eusebius gives none (E1)"; "'Columbanus quoted Anatolius's own words' → words from a Latin Easter book under his name (I1a, D4)".
11. **What came 3 / Ending 2.** Bede's doubt is about the Irish practice of Aidan's time, before Whitby. Tell it as Bede's judgment, written in 731, never as an event after Whitby. Keep it in one place only, as Ending 2 or What came 3, not both.
