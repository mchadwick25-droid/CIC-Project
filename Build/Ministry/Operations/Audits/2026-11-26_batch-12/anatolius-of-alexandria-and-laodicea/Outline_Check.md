# Outline check: Anatolius of Alexandria and Laodicea

## My own account of the heart (written before reading Outline.md)

From the census teaser, the source sheet and what he is known for.

- **Essence:** A learned Alexandrian, ranked by Eusebius first among the ablest men of his day, who during the siege of the city's palace quarter talked the council into letting the "useless" leave and, under that cover, got almost all the besieged out, his church's people first; he later became bishop of Laodicea in Syria and wrote an Easter book that fixed the Passover's first month after the spring equinox on a nineteen-year cycle.
- **Also told:** the siege as Eusebius reports it (report, "they say"): the famine, the deacon outside winning safety for deserters, the council's anger at a call for peace, his speech, the vote, men in women's dress leaving by night. Then the road to Laodicea (ordained by Theotecnus at Caesarea as successor, called to the synod on Paul of Samosata, detained at Laodicea). Then the Easter book in his own words: the cycle of nineteen years, 22 March, the error of placing the first month before the equinox, the rule as old as the Jews before Christ.
- **What came of him:** heaviest, his name in the Irish-Roman Easter quarrel (Columbanus to Gregory, Columbanus to the Gallic bishops, Bede on Aidan, both sides at Whitby in 664), much of it through a Latin book under his name whose authorship is contested; then his fame for learning (Jerome, Syriac writers); then later reckoners using his extract (Elias, Bede's own letter). Defended: Easter's first month after the equinox.
- Shape: Founder or leader, as the slate proposed, though the record is about acts (the siege) and a book; the risk is that "bishop" stands in for acts.
