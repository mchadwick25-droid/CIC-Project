## Blocking

**1. ASSUMPTION ADDED.** "Later bishop of Laodicea in Syria, he wrote a rule for finding Easter, and three and four centuries on, Irish churchmen and the defenders of Rome's Easter both claimed him."
- The appositive "Later bishop …, he wrote" reads as "once bishop, he wrote". No old source gives a time or place for the Easter book: EUS 42769–42773 says only "in such as have come down to us … he states particularly his opinions on the passover". The outline's Do not say bars "As bishop he wrote…".
- Change: "He later became bishop of Laodicea in Syria. He also wrote a rule for finding Easter, and three and four centuries on, Irish churchmen and the defenders of Rome's Easter both claimed him."

**2. SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** "Then bishops meeting at Antioch to judge its own bishop, Paul of Samosata, who taught that Christ was by nature an ordinary man, called him there."
- Source: "As he held, contrary to the teaching of the Church, low and degraded views of Christ, namely, that in his nature he was a common man" (EUS 41838–41840, VII.27.2). The calling: "the synod which was held to consider Paul's case called him to Antioch" (EUS 42933–42936).
- The account of Paul's teaching is his condemners' account, given by a hostile witness. The brief tells it as the narrator's fact. Part 1 names the witness for an enemy's charge.
- Change: "… Paul of Samosata, who, Eusebius says, taught that Christ was by nature an ordinary man, called him there."

**3. SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** "Of the Irish before Whitby, Bede wrote that any skilled reader could tell whether they followed, as they believed, the holy and praiseworthy Father Anatolius."
- Source: "the northern province of the Scots, and all the nation of the Picts, celebrating Easter then after that manner, and believing that they therein followed the writings of the holy and praiseworthy Father Anatolius; the truth of which every skilful person can discern. But the Scots which dwelt in the South of Ireland had long since …" (BEDE 5752–5758; Latin "peritus quisque", BEDE-lat 18702–18709).
- Bede limits the claim to the northern Irish and the Picts, and in the next sentence sets the southern Irish apart. "The Irish" widens it to all of them. "Reader" also narrows "skilful person", but that is minor.
- Change: "Of the northern Irish and the Picts before Whitby, Bede wrote that any skilled person could tell whether they followed, as they believed, the holy and praiseworthy Father Anatolius."

## Summary holds (with the line)

- "Anatolius, a learned Christian, stood before the city council." The source: "he convened the senate of Alexandria" (EUS 42734; βουλὴν τῶν Ἀλεξανδρέων συναγαγών, EUS-gr 12483). "Stood before" is a fair picture of it.
- "Anatolius used the council's vote to slip out almost all the besieged, his church's people first." The source: "he saved almost all the besieged … first, those belonging to the church … under cover of these … secretly" (EUS 42753–42760; μικροῦ δεῖν τοὺς πάντας … διεσώσατο, EUS-gr 12499).
- "Born in Alexandria, he lived in the later 200s." The source: "an Alexandrian by birth" (EUS 42684; γένος … Ἀλεξανδρεύς, EUS-gr 12392). Jerome: "flourished under the emperors Probus and Carus" (JER 40933–40934).
- "The palace quarter held out against a Roman army, while the rest of the city sided with Rome." The source: "the siege of the Pyrucheium" (EUS 42697) and "the other part of the city was allied with the Roman army, and therefore was not under siege" (EUS 42721–42723). "Palace quarter" is McGiffert's gloss, used only to clarify.
- "He argued instead that the city should feed only men and youths." The source: "we ought to provide only for men and youth" (EUS 42741; τρέφειν δέον μόνους ἄνδρας καὶ νεανίας, EUS-gr 12491).
- "Under cover of that decree … men among them secretly dressed in women's clothes." The source: "under cover of these, a multitude of others, secretly clothed in women's garments" (EUS 42758–42759; μυρίους ἄλλους λεληθότως γυναικείαν στολὴν ἀμπισχομένους, EUS-gr 12502, 12570). "Men among them" is not stated. It does follow, though: the decree already freed women "not needful for the army" (EUS 42747), so only those it did not free needed the disguise. Kept as a fair compression.
- "After the war, the bishop of Caesarea in Palestine made him a second bishop there, to take over at his death." The source: "first ordained him as bishop, designing to make him his successor in his own parish after his death" (EUS 42925–42928). For the war: "came after the aforesaid war from Alexandria" (EUS 42765–42767).
- "On the way, at Laodicea … the believers kept him as bishop." The source: "as he passed through the city of Laodicea, Eusebius being dead, he was detained by the brethren there" (EUS 42937–42940), and "was appointed his successor" (EUS 42683).
- "Easter's date was tied to the Jewish Passover full moon, the fourteenth day of the first month of spring." The source: "the day of the passover was appointed on the fourteenth of the month … as may be seen in full moons" and "after the vernal equinox, in the middle of the first month" (EUS 42866–42878).
- "That month, his rule ran, must not be counted from a new moon before the spring equinox, in March, when day and night are equal." The source: "those who place the first month in it [the segment before the equinox] … commit no slight or common blunder" (EUS 42812–42817). "In March" rests on his own count: "the eleventh before the Kalends of April", with the sun "already passing through the fourth day" past the equinox (EUS 42800–42809).
- "He set his count on a nineteen-year table of the spring new moons, which then begins again." The source: "the new moon of the first month, which is the beginning of every cycle of nineteen years" and "the head of the cycle" (EUS 42789–42811).
- "About 600, Columbanus, an abbot who kept his Irish homeland's Easter, wrote to Pope Gregory the Great to defend it." The source: the letter to Gregory, which cites "our masters and the Irish ancients" against Victorius (COL 4423–4425). In the Gallic letter he writes "plus credo traditioni patriae meae" (COL-lat 20796). "Abbot": Migne heads the letters "ABBATIS" (COL-lat 20276, 21392), and the Gallic letter itself speaks of "abbatibus" (COL-lat 20518). "About 600" rests on Gregory's pontificate, which is Widely Accepted. The Benedictines' 598–9 (COL 4179–4180, modern) is not told.
- "Citing Anatolius, he drew on a Latin Easter book under his name." The source: "how Anatolius … disputes with strong disapprobation about this age of the moon" (COL 4321–4345). The words he quotes are in the Latin canon under Anatolius's name (ANF6 LATCAN 15014–15024).
- "A moon past its twentieth day, he argued, rises after midnight, so darkness rules most of the night, but the feast of the resurrection is a feast of light." The source: "Easter on the 21st or 22nd day of the moon … a time of darkness … if the rising of the moon be delayed till the end of two watches, which indicates midnight, light does not overcome darkness, but darkness light … the solemnity of the Lord's Resurrection is light" (COL 4318–4355).
- "Colman, for the Irish side, said Anatolius, a holy man, had written that Easter could fall any day from the moon's fourteenth to its twentieth." The source: "Did Anatolius, a holy man … act contrary to the law and the Gospel, when he wrote, that Easter was to be celebrated from the fourteenth to the twentieth?" (BEDE 8117–8121; BEDE-lat 21561–21571). This is a rhetorical question turned into a statement, as the outline allows. Colman's side is given at BEDE 7963–7967 ("Bishop Colman with his Scottish clerks").

## Held (line only)

- "In a besieged quarter of Alexandria, the bread had run out, and hunger was harder to bear than the enemy outside." The source: "bread had failed the besieged, so that it was more difficult to withstand the famine than the enemy outside" (EUS 42718–42720).
- Quote 1: "I advise sending out of the gates those we have too many of, who are of no use to us at all: old women, infants and old men." Checked against the Greek: "εἰ τοὺς περιττοὺς καὶ ἡμῖν αὐτοῖς οὐδαμῇ χρησίμους, γραΐδας καὶ νήπια καὶ πρεσβύτας, ἐκδοῦναι πυλῶν ἔξω … συμβουλεύσαιμι" (EUS-gr 12486–12489). The rendering is faithful.
- Eusebius's ranking and the school. The source: "he stood first among the ablest men of our time … at the head in rhetorical science. It is reported that … he was requested by the citizens … to establish there a school of Aristotelian philosophy" (EUS 42685–42690). The ranking is credited to Eusebius, and the school is told as a report.
- "Of the siege, Eusebius tells one of his deeds as others told it to him …" The source: "They relate of him many other eminent deeds … I will give the following only as an example. They say that …" (EUS 42696–42718; μνημονεύουσιν, φασίν, EUS-gr 12400, 12402).
- The message to the deacon. The source: "Anatolius sent for Eusebius … among those who were not besieged … and he informed him of those who were perishing" (EUS 42723–42729). The deacon is identified by Eusebius himself through Dionysius's words (EUS 42662–42664, 40140–40141).
- The promise of safety. The source: "a renowned name which had reached even the Roman general … to grant safety to deserters from the enemy" (EUS 42727–42731).
- "Anatolius called the council and urged peace with Rome, but they grew angry." The source: "at first proposed that all should come to a reconciliation with the Romans … they were angered by this advice" (EUS 42734–42736; ἀγριαίνοντας, EUS-gr 12485).
- The first vote. The source: "rising first he gave his vote that the entire multitude, whether of men or women, who were not needful for the army, should depart … they would perish with famine … all the others in the senate agreed" (EUS 42745–42753).
- The night exit. The source: "they went out of the gates by night and escaped to the Roman camp. There Eusebius, like a father and physician, received all of them … and restored them by every kind of prudence and care" (EUS 42760–42762; πατρὸς καὶ ἰατροῦ δίκην, EUS-gr 12574).
- "Eusebius kept part of his Greek book on Easter." The source: "It seems important to give here the following extracts" (EUS 42773–42774; Greek extract EUS-gr 12595ff).
- Quote 3: "make no small mistake, and no careless one". The source: "οὐ μικρῶς οὐδ᾽ ὡς ἔτυχεν ἁμαρτάνειν φαμέν" (EUS-gr 12616). ὡς ἔτυχεν means "at random" or "haphazardly", so "careless" is within its range.
- "The rule was not his own, he said, since the Jews kept it before Christ." The source: "this is not an opinion of our own; but it was known to the Jews of old, even before Christ, and was carefully observed by them" (EUS 42819–42821).
- "In 664 the quarrel came before a church meeting at Whitby, in Abbess Hild's monastery in northern England, as Bede, writing in 731, tells it." The source: "a synod should be held in the monastery of Streaneshalch … where the Abbess Hilda … then presided" in "the province of the Northumbrians" (BEDE 7951–7962). The year comes from "the year of our Lord's incarnation 664" (BEDE 8236–8237), and Bede's date of writing from "In the year of our Lord's incarnation 731" (BEDE 15126).
- Quote 2: "It is agreed that Anatolius was a most holy man, most learned and most worthy of praise." Checked against the Latin: "Constat … Anatolium uirum sanctissimum, doctissimum, ac laude esse dignissimum" (BEDE-lat 21581–21582). The rendering is faithful.
- "Anatolius, he said, had set out a cycle of nineteen years, which the whole church of Christ kept." The source: "circulum X et VIIII annorum posuit, quem … a tota Christi ecclesia custoditum" (BEDE-lat 21584–21587).
