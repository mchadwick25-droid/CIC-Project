## Sentence by sentence

1. "Anatolius used the council's vote to slip out almost all the besieged." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** EUS 42752–42753: "As all the others in the senate agreed to this, he saved almost all the besieged." Cutting "his church's people first" removes no claim; it is told in paragraph 6.
2. "He later became bishop of Laodicea in Syria." **HELD.** JER 40933: "bishop of Laodicea in Syria"; EUS 42939–42940: "he was detained by the brethren there."
3. "He also wrote a rule for finding Easter, and three and four centuries on, Irish churchmen and the defenders of Rome's Easter both claimed him." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** EUS 42770–42772 (his work "on the passover"); COL 4320–4363 (Columbanus cites him); BEDE 8117–8121, 8134–8141 (Colman and Wilfrid). Only "He also" is new.
4. "Of the siege, Eusebius tells one of his deeds, as others told it to him." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** EUS 42696–42716: "They relate of him many other eminent deeds during the siege … but I will give the following only as an example"; EUS 42718 "They say". Greek μνημονεύουσιν, φασίν (EUS-gr 12400, 12402).
5. "Anatolius sent word to a deacon of the church in the free part of the city." **HELD.** EUS 42722–42727: "As the other part of the city was allied with the Roman army, and therefore was not under siege, Anatolius sent for Eusebius … and he informed him." Cutting "As the quarter starved" removes no claim.
6. "Under cover of that decree he saved almost all the besieged: the church's people first, and with them many more, men among them secretly dressed in women's clothes." **SOFTENED OR CHANGED. BLOCKING.**
   - Source: EUS 42754–42758: "not only the classes included in the decree, but, under cover of these, a multitude of others, secretly clothed in women's garments". Greek EUS-gr 12501–12502: οὐ μόνον τῶν κατὰ τὴν ψῆφον δεδογμένων, τῇ δὲ τούτων προφάσει καὶ μυρίους ἄλλους ("not only those the vote allowed, but under that pretext countless others").
   - Brief: "and with them many more". v1 had "many more beyond it". With "beyond it" gone, the listener can take the "many more" as people the decree allowed. Eusebius's point is that they went out past the decree. The outline's Do not say also asks for "many others beyond it" (Outline.md, Do not say: "He saved the old and the weak").
   - Exact change: "the church's people first, and with them many more" → "the church's people first, and with them many more beyond it".
   - "men among them" rests on the disguise, since the vote already let out men who were not needed for the army (EUS 42747–42749, εἴτε ἀνδρῶν εἴτε γυναικῶν, EUS-gr 12494). The revision did not change this wording, and the outline holds it.
7. "Then bishops meeting at Antioch called him there, to judge its own bishop, Paul of Samosata, who, Eusebius says, taught that Christ was by nature an ordinary man." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** EUS 42931–42939: "the synod which was held to consider Paul's case … called him to Antioch"; EUS 41838–41840: "low and degraded views of Christ, namely, that in his nature he was a common man". With the moved clause, "to judge" now reads as the reason he was called. That fits a bishop summoned to a synod hearing the case, and the outline holds the wording word for word (Cut first 22). The view is credited to Eusebius.
8. "About 600, Columbanus, an Irish abbot, wrote to Pope Gregory the Great to defend his homeland's Easter against an Easter past the moon's twentieth day." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** COL 4320–4322: "What, then, dost thou say concerning Easter on the 21st or 22nd day of the moon, which … is proved by many calculators not to be Easter"; COL 4423–4425: "by our masters and the Irish ancients … Victorius was not received". "About 600" follows from Gregory's papacy and is not a single-year claim. "Abbot" stands as it did in v1.
9. "He drew on a Latin Easter book under Anatolius's name." **HELD.** The sentence Columbanus quotes (COL 4349–4355, "if the rising of the moon be delayed till the end of two watches, which indicates midnight …") is in the Latin *Paschal Canon* under his name, LATCAN 15014–15020. It is not in Eusebius's Greek extract.
10. "Such a moon, he argued, rises after midnight, so darkness rules most of the night, but the feast of the resurrection is a feast of light." **HELD.** COL 4391–4394 gives it in Columbanus's own words: "a moon in its 21st or 22nd day is out of the dominion of light, as having risen at that time after midnight; and, when darkness overcomes light, it is said to be impious to keep the solemnity of light"; COL 4353–4354: "the solemnity of the Lord's Resurrection is light".
11. "Bede doubted the Irish claim." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** BEDE 5747–5749: Aidan was zealous "though not altogether according to knowledge"; BEDE-lat 18709: "Quod an uerum sit, peritus quisque facillime cognoscit." The outline gives this lead-in.
12. "The northern Irish and Picts before Whitby, he wrote, believed they followed the writings of the holy and praiseworthy Father Anatolius, and anyone skilled could very easily tell whether that was true." **HELD.** BEDE-lat 18705–18709: "septentrionalis Scottorum prouincia, et omnis natio Pictorum illo adhuc tempore … aestimans se in hac obseruantia sancti ac laude digni patris Anatolii scripta secutam. Quod an uerum sit, peritus quisque facillime cognoscit." BEDE 5757–5758 sets the southern Irish ("the Scots which dwelt in the South of Ireland") against them. "Before Whitby" = "illo adhuc tempore", Aidan's time.
