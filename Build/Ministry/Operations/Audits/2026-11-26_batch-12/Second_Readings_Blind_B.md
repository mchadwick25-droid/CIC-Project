# Second Readings, Blind B (SR2)

## SR2-07
Source: Latin, lines 24576-24579. "uidi ego in Bithynia praesidem gaudio mirabiliter elatum tamquam barbarorum gentem aliquam subegisset, quod unus qui per biennium magna uirtute restiterat, postremo cedere uisus esset."

I saw it myself in Bithynia. A governor was wildly carried away with joy, as if he had conquered some barbarian nation. The reason was that one man, who had held out with great courage for two years, at last seemed to give way.

Note: "praesidem" is the governor of a province. "cedere uisus esset" says only that he seemed to yield, not that he certainly did. Scan clear.

## SR2-08
Source: Latin, line 22775. "dei templum euerteretur"

the temple of God was being torn down

Note: in context this sits inside a "cum ... contigisset ut" clause: it happened at that same time that God's temple was overthrown. "euerteretur" means overturned or demolished. Scan clear.

## SR2-09
Source: Latin, line 23206. "praesente me ac dolente"

while I was there and grieving over it

Note: an ablative absolute, literally "with me present and grieving." It could also be put "in my presence and to my sorrow." Scan clear.

## SR2-10
Source: Latin, lines 26240-26242. "defendenda enim religio est non occidendo sed moriendo, non saeuitia sed patientia, non scelere sed fide"

For religion must be defended, not by killing but by dying, not by cruelty but by patient endurance, not by crime but by faith.

Note: "patientia" carries the sense of enduring suffering, not only calm. "fide" could also be "faithfulness." Scan clear.

## SR2-11
Source: English (ANF translation), lines 6971-6978. Begins "while I was giving my most earnest attention to the matter" and ends "as centred in one Being."

While I was giving the matter my closest attention, I came across some foreign writings. They were too old to be compared with the opinions of the Greeks, and too divine to be compared with their errors. I came to trust them for several reasons: their plain way of speaking, the simple, unpolished style of their writers, the way they knew future events beforehand, the excellence of their moral teaching, and their statement that the whole universe is ruled by one Being.

Note: "barbaric" here means non-Greek, foreign, not savage (the translator's own footnote says the same). Scan clear.

## SR2-12
Source: English (ANF translation), lines 33664-33668. Begins "as long as he continued with him" and ends "his own peculiar type of doctrine."

As long as he stayed with him, he voiced no such views. But after Justin's martyrdom he broke away from the Church. Excited and swollen with pride at the idea of being a teacher, as if he stood above everyone else, he put together his own peculiar brand of doctrine.

Note: "he" is Tatian and "him" is Justin, from the sentence just before the passage. Scan clear.
