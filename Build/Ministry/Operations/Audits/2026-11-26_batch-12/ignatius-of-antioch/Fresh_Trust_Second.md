## Sentence by sentence

1. "Ignatius, bishop of the church at Antioch in Syria, was a prisoner bound for death in the arena at Rome when he wrote: …" **SUMMARY HOLDS.** Rom. 2, ANF 6961: "the bishop of Syria"; Smyrn. 11, ANF 8406–8407: "the Church which is at Antioch in Syria. Coming from that place bound with chains"; Eph. 1, ANF 4703–4704: "to fight with beasts at Rome". "Arena" is the plain sense of fighting beasts at Rome (outline beat 1 and gloss).
2. "On that road he wrote seven letters, six to churches and one to a fellow bishop, and they tell nearly all we know of him." **HELD.** EUS 3.36.5–10 names the seven, Polycarp's among them (EUS 19961–20035); Pol. 1, ANF 8549–8553, is to a bishop. "Nearly all we know" is the outline's own line (Outline, Witnesses, His seven letters) and matches the witness list: later writers add only the bishop list and the report of death.
3. "Polycarp of Smyrna gathered the letters he held and sent them on, and later bishops quoted them for centuries." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** *Phil.* 13, ANF 3613–3616: "all the rest [of his Epistles] which we have by us, we have sent to you". Later bishops: Irenaeus (ANF 53556–53560), Eusebius (EUS 3.36), Athanasius and Theodoret (Source Sheet I-list).
4. "He was taken in chains as a Christian, for the name of Christ." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** Eph. 1, ANF 4703: "I came bound from Syria for the common name and hope"; Eph. 3, ANF 4793: "I am bound for the name [of Christ]" (the bracket is the translators'). "As a Christian" is the outline's reading of "the common name" (beat 1); EUS 19935 gives "his testimony to Christ".
5. "It was the reign of the emperor Trajan, as the church historian Eusebius wrote two centuries later." **HELD.** CHRON 42193–42202: "Traiano aduersum Christianos persecutionem mouente … Ignatius quoque Antiochenae ecclesiae episcopus Romam perductus bestiis traditur". Eusebius tells Ignatius in EUS 3.36 within the Trajan chapters (EUS 19270, the reign "whose times we are now recording"). Credited to him, as the outline requires (Time: Contested).
6. "At Ephesus, he wrote, the elders, the council who led a church with its bishop, were fitted to him like strings to a harp, and he urged the whole church to sing with one voice." **HELD.** Eph. 4, ANF 4828–4836: "your justly renowned presbytery … is fitted as exactly to the bishop as the strings are to the harp … do ye, man by man, become a choir … ye may with one voice sing to the Father". The v1 "should be fitted" had turned his praise into a command; this restores it.
7. "They shunned the thanksgiving meal, he wrote, because they would not say it was Christ's flesh." **HELD.** Smyrn. 7, ANF 8220–8222: "They abstain from the Eucharist … because they confess not the Eucharist to be the flesh of our Saviour Jesus Christ".
8. "They thought his own sufferings only seemed real too, he wrote, and he charged that they cared nothing for the widow, the orphan or the hungry." **SOFTENED OR CHANGED. BLOCKING.** Source, Smyrn. 5, ANF 8131–8134: "nor the sufferings we have individually endured. For they think also the same thing regarding us." Two shifts. (a) Ignatius says "us", the sufferings "we have individually endured"; the sentence narrows it to "his own". (b) He says only "the same thing"; the sentence spells it out as "only seemed real". That reading leans on his own inference in Smyrn. 4 (ANF 8102–8103: "then am I also only in appearance bound"), which is his argument, not their view, and the ANF editor reads it differently (ANF 8134: "so they believe that we suffer in vain"). The outline holds neither addition (beat 4: "they thought the same of his own sufferings and those of others like him"). The second clause holds: Smyrn. 6, ANF 8184–8186. **Change to:** "They thought the same of his own sufferings and those of others like him, he wrote, and he charged that they cared nothing for the widow, the orphan or the hungry."
9. "His third message went to Rome." **HELD.** Rom. 1–4, ANF 6917–7042.
10. "He was afraid, he wrote, that the love of the church there would try to spare him." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** Rom. 1, ANF 6917–6921: "For I am afraid of your love, lest it should do me an injury. For it is easy for you to accomplish what you please; but it is difficult for me to attain to God, if ye spare me." It says nothing of how they might spare him, which keeps the editor's guess (prayer, ANF-ed) out.
11. "If they spared him, it would be hard for him to reach God; if they kept silent about him, he would become God's." **HELD.** Rom. 1, ANF 6920–6921 (above); Rom. 2, ANF 6956–6957: "For if ye are silent concerning me, I shall become God's".
12. "The bread was an offering: by the teeth of the beasts, he wrote, he would be found a sacrifice to God." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** Rom. 4, ANF 7040–7046: "that I may be found the pure bread of Christ … Entreat Christ for me, that by these instruments [ANF note: i.e., by the teeth of the wild beasts] I may be found a sacrifice [to God]". The referent is the beasts of the line just before; the same verb, "be found", joins bread and sacrifice, and Rom. 2, ANF 6958–6959, has "that I be sacrificed to God while the altar is still prepared". Outline gloss "the bread line's sense" holds it.
13. "News had come that the church at Antioch, which he had left in chains, was at peace and whole again, and he asked them to send a messenger to Syria to rejoice with it." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** Philad. 10, ANF 7855–7860: "it is reported to me that the Church which is at Antioch in Syria possesses peace … to elect a deacon … that he may rejoice along with them"; Smyrn. 11, ANF 8406–8407: "Coming from that place bound with chains"; ANF 8418–8424: "they are [now] at peace, and are restored to their proper greatness, and that their proper constitution [Literally, 'body'] has been re-established among them".
14. "Later Polycarp wrote to the church in the city of Philippi, which had seen Ignatius pass, and counted him among those whose endurance they had witnessed." **HELD.** *Phil.* 9, ANF 3484–3487: "such as ye have seen [set] before your eyes, not only in the case of the blessed Ignatius".

16. "Eusebius also wrote that, by report, Ignatius was sent from Syria to Rome and became food for wild beasts, for his testimony to Christ." **HELD.** EUS 3.36.3, 19935–19936: "Report says that he was sent from Syria to Rome, and became food for wild beasts on account of his testimony to Christ."

## The two wordings the review flagged as its own

- **"the church historian Eusebius."** Holds. It names him by the work the story draws on, his *Church History* (EUS title, line 47; the work at EUS 865), and tells a first-time listener who he is. It adds no claim and no modern judgment. It also answers the outline's rule that the Trajan reign be credited to him.
- **"at peace and whole again" (Smyrn. 11).** Holds, as a fair compression. Smyrn. 11 says they are at peace, "restored to their proper greatness", and their "proper constitution", literally their "body", is "re-established" (ANF 8418–8424). "Whole again" carries the restoring and the body made one again more closely than "constitution" does. It says nothing of what the trouble was, so it keeps the outline's bar against saying a persecution had ended (the ANF chapter heading at Philad. 10 is the editors').
