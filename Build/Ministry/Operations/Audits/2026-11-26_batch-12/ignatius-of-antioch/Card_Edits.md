## Fields changed or new

**`knownFor`** (changed). Old: "Bishop of Antioch whose seven letters, written under guard on the way to his death at Rome, warn the churches to keep to their bishops and to Christ who truly suffered." New: "Bishop of the church at Antioch in Syria. As a prisoner bound for death in the arena at Rome, he wrote seven letters: keep together around your bishop, hold that Christ truly suffered, and, to Rome, do not save me." Why: "on the way to his death at Rome" tells the death at Rome in the narrator's voice (Outline, Do not say: "That he was killed by beasts at Rome, told in the narrator's voice"). The new words are the brief's sentences 1 and 3 ("a prisoner bound for death in the arena at Rome"; "keep together around your bishop, hold that Christ truly suffered, and, to Rome, do not save me").

**`shortDescription`** (new). "Ignatius was bishop of the church at Antioch in Syria, taken under guard to Rome to face the wild beasts. On the road he wrote seven letters, urging the churches to keep together around their bishops and to hold that Christ truly suffered, and asking the church at Rome to let him die." Evidence: brief paragraph 1 and 2; Outline, essence; Eph. 1, ANF 4703-4705 ("to fight with beasts at Rome"); Rom. 4, ANF 7036-7039 ("I shall willingly die for God, unless ye hinder me ... Suffer me to become food for the wild beasts"). Opens on his name, says what he was then; no negative or contrast.

**`datesNote`** (changed). Old: "Died under the emperor Trajan, or later". New: "His letters give no year. Eusebius, about two centuries later, sets his death in the reign of the emperor Trajan, when Trajan stirred up persecution against the Christians. Why: "or later" rests only on Harnack's modern dating (EUS-ed 19813-19816). Evidence: CHRON 42193-42202 ("Traiano aduersum Christianos persecutionem mouente ... Ignatius ...
