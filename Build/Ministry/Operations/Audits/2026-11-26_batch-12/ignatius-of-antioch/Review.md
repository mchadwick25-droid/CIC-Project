## BLOCKING

**B1. A change to the approved heart.** "On that road he wrote seven letters to the churches that came out to meet him, and they tell nearly all we know of him." (Trust, second: SOFTENED OR CHANGED. Trust marked it SUMMARY HOLDS. A finding from either read blocks until it is fixed.)
- Evidence: Rome did not come out to meet him. It was where he was going (Eph. 1, ANF 4704–4705; Rom. 4, ANF 7036–7037). One letter went to a man, Polycarp (Pol. 1, ANF 8549).
- Change: "On that road he wrote seven letters, six to churches and one to a fellow bishop, and they tell nearly all we know of him."
- The essence in `Outline.md` holds the old wording, so the approved heart changes here.
- Do not say (add): "seven letters to the churches that came out to meet him" → "seven letters, six to churches and one to a fellow bishop" (Rome had not met him, and one letter went to Polycarp). Change the essence to match.

**B2. A change to the approved heart.** "Polycarp of Smyrna gathered the letters and sent them on, and later bishops quoted them for centuries." (Both trust reads: SOFTENED OR CHANGED.)
- Evidence: the sentence comes right after "seven letters", so a listener hears that Polycarp gathered all seven. Polycarp says "The Epistles of Ignatius written by him to us, and all the rest [of his Epistles] which we have by us, we have sent to you" (*Phil.* 13, ANF 3608–3614). The second half of the sentence holds.
- Change: "Polycarp of Smyrna gathered the letters he held and sent them on, and later bishops quoted them for centuries."
- The essence holds "gathered the letters and sent them on", so the approved heart changes here.
- Do not say (extend the "all seven" line): "gathered the letters" with no limit, wherever it follows "seven letters" → "gathered the letters he held". Change the essence to match.

**B3.** "The elders, the council who led a church with its bishop, should be fitted to him like strings to a harp, so that the whole church sings with one voice." (Trust: SOFTENED OR CHANGED. The second trust read marked it SUMMARY HOLDS.)
- Evidence: Eph. 4, ANF 4828–4836: "your justly renowned presbytery ... is fitted as exactly to the bishop as the strings are to the harp ... do ye, man by man, become a choir, that ... ye may with one voice sing". The harp is his praise of the church at Ephesus. The one voice is what he urges. The sentence turns praise into a rule.
- Change: "At Ephesus, he wrote, the elders, the council who led a church with its bishop, were fitted to him like strings to a harp, and he urged the whole church to sing with one voice."
- Do not say (add; beat 3 left room): the harp as a rule for every church → his praise of the elders at Ephesus, with the one voice as what he urged (Eph. 4).

**B4.** "About seventy years later, Irenaeus of Lyon quoted the wheat line, without naming him, as the words of a fellow believer condemned to the beasts." (Trust, second: ASSUMPTION ADDED, a date. Trust marked it SUMMARY HOLDS.)
- Evidence: Irenaeus gives no interval (*Haer.* 5.28.4, ANF 53556–53558). "Seventy years" is worked out from a year for Ignatius, and the outline rates that year Contested (Setting; SS S2, D3). The narrator states it in his own voice.
- Change, taken together with MINOR 6: "Later still, Irenaeus, writing at Lyon in Gaul, quoted the wheat line, without naming him, as the words of a fellow believer condemned to the beasts."
- Do not say (add): "about seventy years later" → "later" (the interval rests on a Contested date for Ignatius). Also strike it from beat 7, Connection to carry and Who tells it.

**B5. The opening holds back who is speaking.** (Skeptic: BLOCKING. The first-time listener heard the same gap and called it MINOR, because sentence 4 fills it.)
- The first three sentences are the quote. The name only comes after it, as a held-back reveal. "And the beasts on his road were his guards" then repeats the quote's own gloss.
- Change: replace paragraph 1 with: Ignatius, bishop of the church at Antioch in Syria, was a prisoner bound for death in the arena at Rome when he wrote: "From Syria all the way to Rome I fight with beasts, by land and by sea, by night and by day. I am bound to ten leopards, I mean a band of soldiers. Even when they are treated well, they grow all the worse."
- Do not say (add; Openings 1 left room): Q1 before he is named → name him, his see and his road first, then Q1. Do not follow the quote with "the beasts on his road were his guards". Change Openings 1 to match.

**B6. Bare statement.** "News had come that the church at Antioch was at peace, and he asked them to send a messenger to Syria to rejoice with it." (Skeptic: BLOCKING. The first-time listener: a MINOR failing sentence, "at peace after what?")

- Change: "News had come that the church at Antioch, which he had left in chains, was at peace and whole again, and he asked them to send a messenger to Syria to rejoice with it." This keeps the outline's "Also told" (his care for Antioch) and its Do not say. The fresh trust read checks "whole again" against Smyrn. 11.
- Do not say (add; beat 6 left room): "at peace" alone → "at peace and whole again" (Smyrn. 11). Never that the persecution had ended.
