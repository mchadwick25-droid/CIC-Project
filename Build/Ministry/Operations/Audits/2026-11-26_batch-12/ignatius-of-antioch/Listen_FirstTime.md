## 1. The kind kit's test (person)

- **Who this was:** filled. "Ignatius, bishop of the church at Antioch in Syria," a prisoner taken under guard to die in the arena at Rome.
- **What happened:** filled. He was taken in chains to Rome, churches met him on the road, he wrote seven letters, and Eusebius's report says he was killed by wild beasts.
- **What it was about:** filled. The three messages: keep together around your bishop, Christ truly suffered, and do not save me.
- **Why the connection matters / why he is on the tree:** filled, though thin. "Polycarp of Smyrna gathered the letters and sent them on, and later bishops quoted them for centuries," and then Polycarp's letter to Philippi and Irenaeus quoting the wheat line. I can say that his letters were kept and passed on. I cannot say what they changed beyond being quoted, but the bar's "could be stronger" does not count, so this is not a finding.

## 2. The opening

The first three sentences are the quote itself. After them I do not know who is speaking, when it is, or why he is on the road. The place is half there: "From Syria all the way to Rome." Sentence 4 fills in who and why at once, so I am not lost. **When** never comes as a time I can place. "In the reign of the emperor Trajan" arrives in paragraph 3, and to me Trajan is only a name. No century is given anywhere. MINOR: if the outline's Setting holds a spoken date, work it into that clause, e.g. "in the reign of the emperor Trajan, early in the 100s". If it does not, leave it as it is.

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing (all MINOR)

1. "Even when they are treated well, they grow all the worse." Treated well by whom? I miss that the Christians are being kind to the guards.
2. "His third message went to Rome, and it came from fear of that church's love." "Fear of love" is a puzzle on one hearing. The next sentence, "if they kept silent, he would become God's," does not tell me what speaking up would be. I do not get that the Roman Christians might try to stop his death. The wheat line rescues the main point (he wants to die), so the thread holds. Make it plain only as far as the outline's line on the Romans letter allows.
3. "They thought the same of his own sufferings." The same what? I cannot tell whether it means they thought his sufferings were only seeming, or that they did not care.
4. "News had come that the church at Antioch was at peace." At peace after what? Nothing earlier said Antioch was in trouble, so I catch only half of it.
5. "Eusebius wrote that report said Ignatius was sent from Syria to Rome…" On first hearing I parse it as "wrote that report," and I stumble before the meaning comes. Suggested: "Two centuries on, Eusebius wrote that, by report, Ignatius was sent from Syria to Rome and became food for wild beasts, for his testimony to Christ." Or use the outline's own wording for "report."

## 4. Bare statements

## 5. Citations read aloud

The craft check flags s8: "He was taken in chains for the name of Christ, in the reign of the emperor Trajan, Eusebius says." Part 1 allows a witness to be named where the source wrote long after, and the last paragraph shows that Eusebius did ("Two centuries on"). So the credit is called for, and I do not count it here. As heard, though, he is an unexplained name at this point (see 7).

## 6. Tells

None. There is no "not X but Y" turn, no dash, no stock word and no moral close. The questions are Ignatius's own, inside the quote. "They tell nearly all we know of him" sits inside a sentence about the letters, which Part 1 allows. Grade 7.2 does not sound flat to me. The images (leopards, harp strings, wheat) carry the voice.

## 7. Unexplained words, runs, talking down (all MINOR)

- **"Eusebius"** (s8): first heard with no hint of who he is or when he wrote. That only comes in the last paragraph. Suggested: "…in the reign of the emperor Trajan, says Eusebius, a church historian writing two centuries later." If this is used, trim the last paragraph's "Two centuries on" so the point is not made twice.
- **"Irenaeus of Lyon"**: a name with no role. Something like "Irenaeus, a bishop in Gaul" would do, if the outline gives his role.
- **"Neapolis"**: a place with no gloss. It is harmless as a destination.
- **"confess it as Christ's flesh"**: to a second-language ear, "confess" means owning up to a wrong. "Would not say it was Christ's flesh" would be plainer, if the outline allows.
- There are no runs of short one-act sentences, and nothing talks down.

## 8. Pull and attention

- **Pull:** strong. The leopards that turn out to be soldiers make me want to know who this man is.
- **First drift:** "The churches of Ephesus, Magnesia and Tralles sent their bishops to him there, and he wrote to those three churches and to Rome." It is three unfamiliar city names in a row, right after "Asia" and "Smyrna", with no act to hold onto. For the reviser: let the names land a clause at a time, or tie them to the letters as they are told.
- **Second dip:** the Troas paragraph. After the wheat line, the climax, it turns to messengers and sailing plans before the end comes.
- **To the end:** yes. The three messages hold me, and the ending returns to the beasts of the opening.
