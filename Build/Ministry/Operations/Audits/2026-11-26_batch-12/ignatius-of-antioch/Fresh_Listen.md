## 1. The test

- **Who:** Ignatius, bishop of the church at Antioch in Syria, a prisoner taken to Rome to die. Filled.
- **What happened:** he was taken in chains as a Christian, churches greeted him on the road, he wrote seven letters from Smyrna and Troas, and he died by the beasts at Rome (Eusebius, by report). Filled.
- **What it was about:** his three messages. Keep together around your bishop, Christ truly suffered, and Rome must not save him. Filled.
- **Why the connection matters:** Polycarp gathered the letters and sent them to Philippi, Irenaeus quoted the wheat line, and later bishops quoted the letters. Filled.

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing (MINOR)

1. "Even when they are treated well, they grow all the worse." For a second-language listener, "grow all the worse" is an idiom, and the meaning is lost: the soldiers get crueler the better they are treated. It is a quote, so its words stay. **Noted, no change.**
2. "If they spared him, it would be hard for him to reach God; if they kept silent about him, he would become God's." "Kept silent about him" is not clear on one hearing: it means they did not speak up to save him. The thread survives, because "do not save me" was already heard in paragraph 2. **Exact change:** "if they kept silent about him" → "if they said nothing on his behalf".
3. "They thought his own sufferings only seemed real too, he wrote, and he charged that they cared nothing for the widow, the orphan or the hungry." Christ was named just before, so "his own" could mean Christ or Ignatius. The meaning lost is that they doubted Ignatius's own suffering. **Exact change:** "They thought his own sufferings only seemed real too" → "They thought his own sufferings as a prisoner only seemed real too".
