# Source sheet: Ignatius of Antioch

Researcher's sheet for batch 12 (2026-11-27). Job on the slate: the bishop taken under guard to Rome, and the seven letters he wrote on the road: keep to your bishop, Christ truly suffered, and do not save me from the beasts. Part B. Shape: Martyr. This sheet is the evidence behind `Outline.md`. Every passage below was read in its own file for this batch; the batch-6 sheet was used only to find places.

## Files, short names and conventions

All in `cic/texts/` unless marked.

- **ANF** = `anf01_apostolic-fathers-justin-irenaeus.xml`. Ante-Nicene Fathers vol. 1 (American edition, 1885; Roberts and Donaldson's translation, revised by A. Cleveland Coxe). English printed before 1931. Holds the seven letters, each chapter printed twice: the **shorter** Greek form first, then the **longer** form. Also the Syriac version of three letters (from 8903), the eight spurious letters (from 9509), the late *Martyrdom of Ignatius* (from 11423), Polycarp's *Letter to the Philippians* and Irenaeus, *Against Heresies*. - **NPNF3** = `npnf203_theodoret-jerome-gennadius-rufinus.xml`. Jerome, *Lives of Illustrious Men* 16; Theodoret, *Eranistes* and letters.

John Chrysostom, *Eulogy on Saint Ignatius*, preached at Antioch (English, 1889). **CHR-lat** = `john-chrysostom_homily-martyr-lucian-grc-lat_migne-pg50.txt`, the same homily in Zini's Latin (PG 50; the Greek column is not in the scan).

- **CEN** = `cic-website/data/world-census.json` (not in `cic/texts/`): entries `ignatius-of-antioch` (not its `longDescription`), `polycarp-of-smyrna`, `post-apostolic-house-church`.

Quotes give the printed words with XML tags removed. `[n]` marks where a translator's footnote stood; footnotes that matter are given separately. Words in [square brackets] are the translators' own supplements, as printed.

**Language.** Ignatius wrote in Greek. No edition of the Greek of his letters is held. The Greek is held only for (a) the wheat sentence, as HOR prints it from Eusebius, in a damaged scan that EUS and the ANF Syriac note confirm, and (b) a few chapters in MIRBT (Romans greeting, 3 and 4.3; Smyrnaeans 8; Magnesians 6; Polycarp 5; MIRBT 2635–2685), where only Smyrnaeans 8 is partly legible (MIRBT 2668–2669). Eusebius's Greek of Book 3 is not held (the Schwartz volume held is Books 6–10). So every other rendering is made from the ANF English of the shorter form, checked against the longer form and, where they quote, against EUS and NPNF3.

## 1. Setting

**S1. Bishop of the church at Antioch in Syria.** Own words. Documented (for the letters' writer).
- Rom. 2, ANF 6961–6963: "that God has deemed me, the bishop of Syria, worthy to be sent for[n] from the east unto the west."
- Eph. 21, ANF 5584–5586: "Pray ye for the Church which is in Syria, whence I am led bound to Rome, being the last of the faithful who are there"
- Smyrn. 11, ANF 8408–8410: "Your prayer has reached to the Church which is at Antioch in Syria. Coming from that place bound with chains"
- Later: EUS 3.22, 17006–17009: "At this time Ignatius[n] was known as the second bishop of Antioch, Evodius having been the first." EUS 3.36, 19740–19742: "Ignatius, who was chosen bishop of Antioch, second in succession to Peter, and whose fame is still celebrated by a great many." Jerome, NPNF3 39787–39788: "third bishop of the church of Antioch after Peter the apostle" (the same count, with Peter as first). Theodoret, NPNF3 20251–20253: "who received episcopal grace by the hand of the great Peter". Later, 200–330 years. The order of bishops: Widely Accepted as Eusebius's report; ordination by Peter: late, Contested (Theodoret's own editor notes Paul in the *Apostolic Constitutions*, NPNF3-ed 20254). Plain spoken words: "Antioch, in Syria"; "a great city of the Roman East" is not in the record: do not add it.

**S2. When.** The letters give no year. Contested.
- CHRON 42193–42202, 42243: "Traiano aduersum Christianos persecutionem mouente Simon filius Cleopae, qui in Hierosolymis episcopatum tenebat, crucifigitur, cui succedit Iustus: Ignatius quoque Antiochenae ecclesiae episcopus Romam perductus bestiis traditur" (OCR "Cbristianos", "ecdesiae" corrected; the margin year is unreadable, "2 0 x" and "XI"). Plainly: when Trajan stirred up persecution against the Christians, Simeon was crucified at Jerusalem, and Ignatius too, bishop of the church of Antioch, was brought to Rome and handed over to the beasts.
- Jerome, NPNF3 39788–39789, 39829–39830: "condemned to the wild beasts during the persecution of Trajan"; "He was put to death the eleventh year of Trajan".
- EUS-ed 19792–19797: the *Chronicle* gives "the tenth year of Trajan, i.e. 107 a.d."; EUS-ed 19813–19816: Zahn and Lightfoot leave the date "uncertain, claiming simply that he died under Trajan"; Harnack "puts his death into the reign of Hadrian". The census entry's dates read "died under Trajan, or later" (CEN). Modern claims; for the card only.
- Spoken: "in the reign of the emperor Trajan, as Eusebius places it two centuries later". No year in the story.

**S3. The tradition.** CEN `post-apostolic-house-church`: spoken name "The Scattered Households" (informalName); the churches of Antioch, Asia Minor and Rome in the century after the apostles. Tell as "the churches of the generation after the apostles" only if needed; the house-church story carries the tradition.

**S4. The name Theophorus.** Own words: every letter opens "Ignatius, who is also called Theophorus" (e.g. Rom. greeting, ANF 6863–6864). Documented (as his self-naming). Meaning, from late witnesses only: the *Martyrdom* 2, ANF 11494–11495: "Trajan answered, “And who is Theophorus?” Ignatius replied, “He who has Christ within his breast.”" (late legend); HOR 15545–15546: "Ἐπωνομάσθη δὲ Θεοφόρος, ὡς τὸν θεὸν ἔνοικον φέρων ἐν τῇ ψυχῇ" (OCR "Θ εο- φόρος", "ε ενοικον", "Ψυχή"; plainly: he was named Theophorus as one who carried God dwelling in his soul). The Greek word means "God-bearer". The gloss rests on late witnesses: card, not story.

## 2. Events

**E1. Arrested and sent under guard; the reason given.** Own words, Documented; later witnesses agree.
- Eph. 1, ANF 4703–4705: "on hearing that I came bound from Syria for the common name and hope, trusting through your prayers to be permitted to fight with beasts at Rome, that so by martyrdom I may indeed become the disciple of Him"
- Eph. 3, ANF 4793–4795: "For though I am bound for the name [of Christ], I am not yet perfect in Jesus Christ. For now I begin to be a disciple"
- Irenaeus, ANF 53556–53558: "As a certain man of ours said, when he was condemned to the wild beasts because of his testimony with respect to God". Later, about 70 years.
- EUS 3.36.3, 19935–19936: "Report says that he was sent from Syria to Rome, and became food for wild beasts on account of his testimony to Christ." Later, about 200 years.
- No Roman record, charge or judge's name is held. The late *Martyrdom* 2 (ANF 11466–11483) has him tried by Trajan in person at Antioch: late legend; leave out.

**E2. The guard and the road.** Own words, Documented.
- Rom. 5, ANF 7083–7090: "From Syria even unto Rome I fight with beasts,[n] both by land and sea, both by night and day, being bound to ten leopards, I mean a band of soldiers, who, even when they receive benefits,[n] show themselves all the worse." ANF-ed 7084–7086 on "fight with beasts": "Comp. 1 Cor. xv. 32, where the word is also used figuratively." ANF-ed 7089–7090: "Probably the soldiers received gifts from the Christians, to treat Ignatius with kindness" (an editor's guess: do not tell). Same passage quoted by EUS 19994–20002 and Jerome NPNF3 39808–39812 ("ten leopards, that is to say soldiers who guard me").
- EUS 3.36.4, 19952–19959: "And as he made the journey through Asia under the strictest military surveillance, he fortified the parishes in the various cities where he stopped by oral homilies and exhortations, and warned them above all to be especially on their guard against the heresies that were then beginning to prevail, and exhorted them to hold fast to the tradition of the apostles. Moreover, he thought it necessary to attest that tradition in writing, and to give it a fixed form for the sake of greater security." Later, about 200 years; Eusebius's own reading of the letters (EUS-ed 19779–19782 says his account adds nothing beyond the letters except the bishop list and the death at Rome).

**E3. The churches came out to meet him; Smyrna and Polycarp.** Own words, Documented.
- Rom. 9, ANF 7280–7287: "My spirit salutes you, and the love of the Churches that have received me in the name of Jesus Christ, and not as a mere passer-by. For even those Churches which were not[n] near to me in the way, I mean according to the flesh,[n] have gone before me,[n] city by city, [to meet me.]"
- Eph. 1, ANF 4707–4711: "I received, therefore,[n] your whole multitude in the name of God, through Onesimus, a man of inexpressible love,[n] and your bishop in the flesh"
- Magn. 2, ANF 5674–5677: "I have had the privilege of seeing you, through Damas your most worthy bishop, and through your worthy presbyters Bassus and Apollonius, and through my fellow-servant the deacon Sotio"
- Trall. 1, ANF 6279–6282: "as Polybius your bishop has shown me, who has come to Smyrna by the will of God and Jesus Christ, and so sympathized in the joy which I, who am bound in Christ Jesus, possess, that I beheld your whole multitude in him."
- Eph. 21, ANF 5581–5583: "whom, for the honour of God, ye have sent to Smyrna; whence also I write to you, giving thanks unto the Lord, and loving Polycarp even as I do you."
- Magn. 15, ANF 6220–6223: "The Ephesians from Smyrna (whence I also write to you), who are here for the glory of God, as ye also are, who have in all things refreshed me, salute you, along with Polycarp, the bishop of the Smyrnæans."
- Pol. 1, ANF 8549–8553: "I loudly glorify [His name] that I have been thought worthy [to behold] thy blameless face" (he met Polycarp in person).
- Rom. 10, ANF 7309–7310: "Now I write these things to you from Smyrna by the Ephesians". The letter's own date, ANF 7316–7318: "on the day before the ninth of the Kalends of September" (ANF-ed: "that is, on the twenty-third day of August" is a gloss in the text). Leave out.
- Which letters from where: EUS 3.36.5–10, 19961–20035 (Ephesians, Magnesians, Trallians, Romans from Smyrna; Philadelphians, Smyrnaeans, Polycarp from Troas). Jerome NPNF3 39790–39795 the same. Documented from the letters themselves (Rom. 10; Philad. 11, ANF 7894–7895; Smyrn. 12, ANF 8466–8467).

**E4. Message one: keep together around the bishop, with the elders and deacons.** Own words, Documented.
- Eph. 4, ANF 4828–4842: "Wherefore it is fitting that ye should run together in accordance with the will of your bishop, which thing also ye do. For your justly renowned presbytery, worthy of God, is fitted as exactly to the bishop as the strings are to the harp. Therefore in your concord and harmonious love, Jesus Christ is sung. And do ye, man by man, become a choir, that being harmonious in love, and taking up the song of God in unison, ye may with one voice sing to the Father through Jesus Christ"
- Eph. 5, ANF 4873–4874: "Let no man deceive himself: if any one be not within the altar, he is deprived of the bread of God."
- Magn. 6, ANF 5851–5856: "I exhort you to study to do all things with a divine harmony,[n] while your bishop presides in the place of God, and your presbyters in the place of the assembly of the apostles, along with your deacons, who are most dear to me"
- Magn. 7, ANF 5895–5898: "As therefore the Lord did nothing without the Father, being united to Him, neither by Himself nor by the apostles, so neither do ye anything without the bishop and presbyters."
- Trall. 3, ANF 6347–6352: "let all reverence the deacons as an appointment[n] of Jesus Christ, and the bishop as Jesus Christ, who is the Son of the Father, and the presbyters as the sanhedrim of God, and assembly of the apostles. Apart from these, there is no Church."
- Philad. 4, ANF 7497–7502: "Take ye heed, then, to have but one Eucharist. For there is one flesh of our Lord Jesus Christ, and one cup to [show forth[n]] the unity of His blood; one altar; as there is one bishop, along with the presbytery and deacons"
- Philad. 7, ANF 7717–7731: "For, when I was among you, I cried, I spoke with a loud voice: Give heed to the bishop, and to the presbytery and deacons. Now, some suspected me of having spoken thus, as knowing beforehand the division caused by some among you. … But He is my witness, for whose sake I am in bonds, that I got no intelligence from any man.[n] But the Spirit proclaimed these words: Do nothing without the bishop; keep your bodies[n] as the temples of God; love unity; avoid divisions" (a claim of the Spirit's voice: keep in his voice).
- Smyrn. 8, ANF 8274–8288: "See that ye all follow the bishop, even as Jesus Christ does the Father, and the presbytery as ye would the apostles; and reverence the deacons, as being the institution[n] of God. Let no man do anything connected with the Church without the bishop. Let that be deemed a proper[n] Eucharist, which is [administered] either by the bishop, or by one to whom he has entrusted it. Wherever the bishop shall appear, there let the multitude [of the people] also be; even as, wherever Jesus Christ is, there is the Catholic Church. It is not lawful without the bishop either to baptize or to celebrate a love-feast". Greek, damaged, MIRBT 2668–2669: "ὅπου ἂν φανῇ ὁ ἐπίσκοπος, ἐκεῖ τὸ πλῆθος ἔστω, ὥσπερ ὅπου ἂν ᾖ Χριστὸς Ἰησοῦς, ἐκεῖ ἡ καθολικὴ ἐκκλησία" (read through OCR "o7:ou av cpavfj 6 en i g-kotzo q. kxel x6 ttX'^^os £axw, WGTCp ÖTtOu av fj Xp:axcg 'Ir^aoO;, £X£: 19 xa{)-oX:xyj £xxXrja:a"). καθολικὴ means "whole, universal"; the modern sense of "Catholic" misleads. The claim that this is the phrase's first use is Coxe's (card), modern: stays out.
- **Explain it.** *Bishop, elders, deacons*: in each city one bishop led, with a council of elders (the ANF "presbytery") and deacons who served (Magn. 6; Trall. 3). *Eucharist*: the thanksgiving meal of one bread and one cup that the church shared (Philad. 4; Eph. 20, ANF 5547–5550: "breaking one and the same bread, which is the medicine of immortality, and the antidote to prevent us from dying"). Why it mattered to him: "where there is division and wrath, God doth not dwell" (Philad. 8, ANF 7756–7757); division was "the beginning of evils" (Smyrn. 7, ANF 8239–8240). The opposite side: some at Philadelphia "suspected" his loud call was aimed at a split he already knew of (Philad. 7, above); no text by those he opposed is held.

**E5. Message two: Christ truly suffered.** Own words, Documented.
- Trall. 9, ANF 6617–6620: "Jesus Christ, who was descended from David, and was also of Mary; who was truly born, and did eat and drink. He was truly persecuted under Pontius Pilate; He was truly crucified, and [truly] died, in the sight of beings in heaven, and on earth, and under the earth. He was also truly raised from the dead"
- Trall. 10, ANF 6671–6676: "But if, as some that are without God, that is, the unbelieving, say, that He only seemed to suffer (they themselves only seeming to exist), then why am I in bonds? Why do I long to be exposed to[n] the wild beasts? Do I therefore die in vain?" ANF-ed 6675: "Literally, “to fight with.”" ANF-ed 6677–6678: "Some read this and the following clause affirmatively, instead of interrogatively." Longer form, ANF 6683–6688, the same argument.
- Smyrn. 2, ANF 8005–8009: "And He suffered truly, even as also He truly raised up Himself, not, as certain unbelievers maintain, that He only seemed to suffer, as they themselves only seem to be [Christians]."
- Smyrn. 4, ANF 8102–8107: "But if these things were done by our Lord only in appearance, then am I also only in appearance bound. And why have I also surrendered myself to death, to fire, to the sword, to the wild beasts? But, [in fact,] he who is near to the sword is near to God; he that is among the wild beasts is in company with God; provided only he be so in the name of Jesus Christ."
- Smyrn. 5, ANF 8130–8137: "These persons neither have the prophets persuaded, nor the law of Moses, nor the Gospel even to this day, nor the sufferings we have individually endured. For they think also the same thing regarding us."
- Smyrn. 6, ANF 8184–8186: "They have no regard for love; no care for the widow, or the orphan, or the oppressed; of the bond, or of the free; of the hungry, or of the thirsty."
- Smyrn. 7, ANF 8220–8225: "They abstain from the Eucharist and from prayer,[n] because they confess not the Eucharist to be the flesh of our Saviour Jesus Christ, which suffered for our sins, and which the Father, of His goodness, raised up again."
- Smyrn. 5, ANF 8143–8147: "I have not, however, thought good to write the names of such persons, inasmuch as they are unbelievers."
- **Explain it.** The teaching he opposed: that Christ only seemed to have a body and only seemed to suffer (Trall. 10; Smyrn. 2). Why it mattered to him: if the suffering was only seeming, his own chains were only seeming and his death would be "in vain" (Trall. 10; Smyrn. 4). Their side as he gives it: they kept away from the eucharist because they did not confess it to be Christ's flesh (Smyrn. 7), and they thought the same of the martyrs' sufferings (Smyrn. 5). Their charge against him, if any, is not held; he names none of them. No label is needed: the ANF headings ("heretics", Smyrn. 4–7) are the editors'. Ignatius calls it "heresy" in Trall. 6, ANF 6464. Tell it by what they said.

**E6. Message three: the letter to Rome, do not save me.** Own words, Documented.
- Rom. 1, ANF 6917–6922: "For I am afraid of your love,[n] lest it should do me an injury. For it is easy for you to accomplish what you please; but it is difficult for me to attain to God, if ye spare me." ANF-ed 6918–6920: "He probably refers here, and in what follows, to the influence which their earnest prayers in his behalf might have with God." (editor's guess: do not tell what Rome could do).
- Rom. 2, ANF 6956–6960: "For if ye are silent concerning me, I shall become God’s; but if you show your love to my flesh, I shall again have to run my race. Pray, then, do not seek to confer any greater favour upon me than that I be sacrificed to God while the altar is still prepared"
- Rom. 4, ANF 7036–7042: "I write to the Churches, and impress on them all, that I shall willingly die for God, unless ye hinder me. I beseech of you not to show an unseasonable good-will towards me. Suffer me to become food for the wild beasts, through whose instrumentality it will be granted me to attain to God. I am the wheat of God, and let me be ground by the teeth of the wild beasts, that I may be found the pure bread of Christ."
  - Longer form, ANF 7059–7061: "I am the wheat of God, and am ground by the teeth of the wild beasts, that I may be found the pure bread of God."
  - Syriac version, ANF 9389–9391: "I am the wheat of God, and by the teeth of the beasts I shall be ground,[n] that I may be found the pure bread of God." ANF-ed 9390: "Literally, “I am ground.”"
  - Irenaeus, ANF 53558–53560: "“I am the wheat of Christ, and am ground by the teeth of the wild beasts, that I may be found the pure bread of God.”" ANF-ed 53561–53566: the Latin is followed; "the Greek of Ignatius would give “the wheat of God,” and omits “of God” towards the end, as quoted by Eusebius."
  - EUS 20069–20071 (Irenaeus as Eusebius gives him): "I am God’s wheat, and by the teeth of wild beasts am I ground, that I may be found pure bread."
  - Greek, HOR 15547–15552, damaged scan: "ΒΊτος εἰμὶ τοῦ Θ εοὗ, και δὶ ὀδόντων ϑηρίων ἀλήϑομα!, ἵνα καϑαρὸς ἄρτος εὐρεϑῶ, (Εὐσέβ. Εκκλ. Ἱστορ. Βιβλ. }. 30)." Read: Σῖτός εἰμι τοῦ Θεοῦ, καὶ δι᾽ ὀδόντων θηρίων ἀλήθομαι, ἵνα καθαρὸς ἄρτος εὑρεθῶ. ἀλήθομαι is a statement, "I am ground". Four witnesses (Greek through Eusebius, the longer form, the Syriac, Irenaeus) read the statement; only the ANF English of the shorter form reads a plea.
- Rom. 5, ANF 7093–7097: "May I enjoy the wild beasts that are prepared for me; and I pray they may be found eager to rush upon me, which also I will entice to devour me speedily, and not deal with me as with some, whom, out of fear, they have not touched. But if they be unwilling to assail me, I will compel them to do so."
- Rom. 4, ANF 7047–7050: "I do not, as Peter and Paul, issue commandments unto you. They were apostles; I am but a condemned man". Peter and Paul as the earlier apostles known at Rome.
- **Explain it.** *Fight with beasts / food for wild beasts*: he was condemned to be killed by wild animals in the arena at Rome (Eph. 1; Rom. 4; Irenaeus). Why he begged Rome not to act: to be spared was to be kept from God ("difficult for me to attain to God, if ye spare me", Rom. 1); death for Christ was how he would become a disciple (Rom. 4–5: "Now I begin to be a disciple", ANF 7102). The record does not say what Rome could have done.

**E7. Troas: Antioch at peace; messengers; the sudden sailing.** Own words, Documented.
- Philad. 10, ANF 7856–7861: "it is reported to me that the Church which is at Antioch in Syria possesses peace, it will become you, as a Church of God, to elect a deacon to act as the ambassador of God [for you] to [the brethren there], that he may rejoice along with them when they are met together". ANF chapter heading 7853–7854 ("on the close of the persecution") is the editors', not the letter's: do not tell what the trouble was.
- Smyrn. 11, ANF 8418–8424: "your Church should elect some worthy delegate; … so that he, journeying into Syria, may congratulate them that they are [now] at peace, and are restored to[n] their proper greatness, and that their proper constitution[n] has been re-established among them."
- Pol. 7, ANF 8806–8819: "Seeing that the Church which is at Antioch in Syria is, as report has informed me, at peace, through your prayers, I also am the more encouraged … It is fitting, O Polycarp, most blessed in God, to assemble a very solemn[n] council, and to elect one whom you greatly love … who may be designated the messenger of God;[n] and to bestow on him this honour that he may go into Syria". 8819–8821: "A Christian has not power over himself, but must always be ready for[n] the service of God."
- Pol. 8, ANF 8854–8858: "Inasmuch as I have not been able to write to all the Churches, because I must suddenly sail from Troas to Neapolis, as the will[n] [of the emperor] enjoins". ANF-ed 8856–8857: "Some suppose the reference to be to the soldiers, or perhaps to God Himself." The bracket is the translators'. The last we hear in his own words.
- Pol. 3, ANF 8649–8651: "Stand firm, as does an anvil which is beaten. It is the part of a noble[n] athlete to be wounded, and yet to conquer." (his counsel to Polycarp; usable on the card).

**E8. The end.** No witness to the death itself is held from the second century.
- Polycarp, *Phil.* 9, ANF 3484–3492: "I exhort you all, therefore, to yield obedience to the word of righteousness, and to exercise all patience, such as ye have seen [set] before your eyes, not only in the case of the blessed Ignatius, and Zosimus, and Rufus, but also in others among yourselves, and in Paul himself, and the rest of the apostles. [This do] in the assurance that all these have not run[n] in vain, but in faith and righteousness, and that they are [now] in their due place in the presence of the Lord, with whom also they suffered." Contemporary. Philippi saw him pass ("ye have seen … before your eyes"). Ch. 9 speaks of him among the dead.
- Polycarp, *Phil.* 13, ANF 3619–3630: "Any[n] more certain information you may have obtained respecting both Ignatius himself, and those that were[n] with him, have the goodness to make known to us." ANF-ed 3622–3623: "The Latin version reads “are,” which has been corrected". So ch. 13 asks for surer news. The two chapters sit in tension; do not resolve it. EUS-ed 20087–20090: the genuineness of ch. 13 "has been questioned by some scholars" (modern; card); census `polycarp-of-smyrna` sourcing says the same.
- EUS 3.36.3, 19935–19936 (above): "Report says that he was sent from Syria to Rome, and became food for wild beasts". Later, about 200 years. Contested in detail (Eusebius's word is "report"); that he died at Rome: Widely Accepted. EUS-ed 19943–19950: "That he suffered martyrdom somewhere is too well attested to be doubted"; John Malalas alone names Antioch (late; leave out).
- CHRON 42199–42202, 42243: "Romam perductus bestiis traditur" (above).
- Chrysostom, CHR 10316–10329: "not outside the walls, in a dungeon, nor even in a court of justice, nor in some corner, did he receive the sentence which condemned him, but in the midst of the theatre, while the whole city was seated above him, he underwent this form of martyrdom, wild beasts being let loose upon him … From the words which he uttered when about to die, for when he heard that this manner of punishment awaited him, “may I have joy,” said he, “of these wild beasts.”" Later, about 280 years; he moves a line written on the road (Rom. 5) to the moment of death. Do not use.
- Jerome, NPNF3 39826–39829: "When he had been condemned to the wild beasts and with zeal for martyrdom heard the lions roaring, he said “I am the grain of Christ. I am ground by the teeth of the wild beasts that I may be found the bread of the world.”" Later; again moves the Rom. 4 line onto the arena. Do not use.

## 3. Impact, heaviest first

**I1. The letters were gathered and passed on as a set (what he left).** Contemporary, then later.
- Polycarp, *Phil.* 13, ANF 3613–3618: "The Epistles of Ignatius written by him[n] to us, and all the rest [of his Epistles] which we have by us, we have sent to you, as you requested. They are subjoined to this Epistle, and by them ye may be greatly profited; for they treat of faith and patience, and all things that tend to edification in our Lord." ANF-ed 3613–3615: the two letters to Smyrna and to Polycarp. EUS 20096–20101 gives the same in McGiffert's English: "For they comprise faith and patience, and every kind of edification that pertaineth to our Lord."
- Polycarp, *Phil.* 13, ANF 3608–3613: "Both you and Ignatius wrote to me, that if any one went [from this] into Syria, he should carry your letter[n] with him; which request I will attend to if I find a fitting opportunity". Polycarp promises to do what Ignatius (Pol. 7–8) and the Philippians asked; no held source says he did it.
- EUS 3.36.5–15 names all seven and quotes Romans, Smyrnaeans, Irenaeus and Polycarp (19961–20101). Jerome NPNF3 39790–39824 the same.
- Later growth of the name: fifteen letters carry it (ANF-ed 4490–4496); eight are spurious and a longer form padded the seven (EUS-ed 19824–19829). Shows the name carried weight; card.

**I2. The wheat line carried on.** Later, 70 to 1,700 years.
- Irenaeus uses it as the pattern of those saved through suffering, ANF 53550–53560: "the wheat, that is, those who bring forth fruit to God in faith, being gathered into the barn. And for this cause tribulation is necessary for those who are saved, that having been after a manner broken up, and rendered fine, and sprinkled over by the patience of the Word of God, and set on fire [for purification], they may be fitted for the royal banquet. As a certain man of ours said".
- EUS 20067–20071 quotes Irenaeus quoting it. HOR 15547–15552 prints it at his feast, citing Eusebius. Jerome (above) repeats it.

**I3. His words on Christ brought as witness in later disputes (what he defended: Christ truly in the flesh).**
- Athanasius, NPNF4 59204–59210: "Ignatius, for instance, who was appointed Bishop in Antioch after the Apostles, and became a martyr of Christ, writes concerning the Lord thus: ‘There is one physician, fleshly and spiritual, originate and unoriginate[n], God in man, true life in death, both from Mary and from God’". Source in the letters: Eph. 7, ANF 4973–4978. NPNF4 59228–59231: "we are persuaded that the blessed Ignatius was right in writing that Christ was originate on account of the flesh (for He became flesh), yet unoriginate".
- Theodoret, NPNF3 20264–20290: "Hear them now bringing forward the apostolic teaching. Testimony of Saint Ignatius, bishop of Antioch, and martyr. From the letter to the Smyrnæans (I.):— “Having a full conviction with respect to our Lord as being truly descended from David …”". And NPNF3 36775–36780: "this is the doctrine of the great saints of the East and of the West; of the far-famed Ignatius, who received his archpriesthood by the right hand of the great Peter, and for the sake of his confession of Christ was devoured by savage beasts".
- Eusebius quotes Smyrn. 3 on the risen Christ's flesh (EUS 20038–20065), as does Jerome (NPNF3 39798–39803).

**I4. Kept at Antioch and on the calendars (remembered as martyr).**
- EUS 19742: "whose fame is still celebrated by a great many."
- Chrysostom, CHR 10343–10357: "When, therefore, he made an end of life there … this also happened through the dispensation of God, that he restored him again to us, and distributed the martyr to the cities. For that city received his blood as it dropped, but ye were honoured with his remains, ye enjoyed his episcopate, they enjoyed his martyrdom. … Ye sent forth a Bishop, and received a martyr; ye sent him forth with prayers, and ye received him with crowns". Latin, CHR-lat 26004–26006: "Emisistis enim episcopum, et martyrem recepistis: emisistis cum precibus, et cum coronis suscepistis". Also CHR 10255–10258: "For the cities which were on the road running together from all sides, encouraged the athlete, and sped him on his way with many supplies".
- Jerome, NPNF3 39830–39831: "the remains of his body lie in Antioch outside the Daphnitic gate in the cemetery."
- HOR 15536–15538: "μνήμη τοῦ ἁγίου Ἱερομάρτυρος Ἰγνατίου, τοῦ Θεοφόρου" on 20 December; HOR 15540–15546 (damaged): disciple of John, successor of the apostles, second bishop of Antioch after Euodius, brought to Rome under Trajan and given to lions; his bones gathered and carried to Antioch. BREV 1596: "1 .  Ignatius,  Bishop  [of  Antioch,]  Martyr.      Double." (February).

**I5. His order of church life.** What he defended (one bishop, elders, deacons, one eucharist; E4). Later use in the held old texts: Eusebius's summary that he urged the churches "to hold fast to the tradition of the apostles" (EUS 19957). Chrysostom praises him as Paul's pattern of a bishop (CHR 10065–10071). The judgment that he was the "earliest advocate of the hierarchical principle" is Schaff's, quoted by McGiffert (EUS-ed 19888–19904): modern; card's Modern works only.

**I6. Late legends.** Socrates 6.8, SOC 17775–17779: "Ignatius third bishop of Antioch in Syria from the apostle Peter, who also had held intercourse with the apostles themselves, saw a vision of angels hymning in alternate chants the Holy Trinity. Accordingly he introduced the mode of singing he had observed in the vision into the Antiochian church". A wonder, 5th century: leave out. The *Martyrdom of Ignatius* (above): leave out.

## 4. Links to other people

- **Polycarp of Smyrna** (`polycarp-of-smyrna`, live). Met at Smyrna (Eph. 21; Magn. 15; Pol. 1); wrote to him from Troas (Pol. 1–8) asking him to send to Antioch; Polycarp gathered and sent on the letters (Phil. 13). Kind: met, wrote to.
- **Irenaeus of Lyon** (`irenaeus-of-lyon`). Quoted the wheat line (ANF 53556–53560). Kind: quoted.
- **Eusebius of Caesarea** (`eusebius-of-caesarea`, in this batch). Wrote the first account of him and kept his letters' list (EUS 3.36). Kind: wrote of. Any Eusebius quote here must not be shared with that story.
- **Athanasius of Alexandria**, **John Chrysostom**, **Jerome**, **Theodoret of Cyrrhus** (all on the tree): quoted or praised him (I3, I4). Kind: quoted; preached the feast of (Chrysostom).
- **post-apostolic-house-church**: told within; its live story narrates his road (bishop and unity; greetings at Smyrna; Polycarp asked to send to Antioch).

## 5. Where sources disagree or come late

- D1. Plea or statement in the wheat line: ANF shorter English "let me be ground"; every other held form "am ground" (E6).
- D2. Which letters are his. All old witnesses treat the seven as his (Polycarp, Irenaeus, Eusebius). Moderns: the seven shorter letters accepted by most (EUS-ed 19838–19847), still "stoutly denied by some" (19843); the census tradition entry calls the dispute unresolved (CEN). Widely Accepted for the story's purposes; the dispute goes on the card.
- D3. The year: Chronicle (illegible margin; McGiffert reads tenth year of Trajan), Jerome the eleventh year, the late *Martyrdom* the ninth (ANF 11466–11470, with the editors' note that the numeral is uncertain). Moderns differ further. Contested: no year in the story.
- D4. Who ordained him: Peter (Theodoret, late); Paul (*Apostolic Constitutions*, per NPNF3-ed 20254). Leave out.
- D5. Polycarp ch. 9 (among the dead) and ch. 13 (asking for news). Do not resolve.
- D6. The death scene and his "last words": Chrysostom and Jerome move lines from Romans 4–5 to the arena. Leave out.
- D7. Disciple of John: CHRON 42133–42135, 42164–42166 ("Iohannem apostolum usque ad Traiani tempora Hireneus episcopus permansisse scribit, post quem auditores eius insignes fuerunt Papias Hierapolitanus episcopus et Polycarpus Zmyrnaeus et Ignatius Antiochenus"), HOR, the *Martyrdom*. Late; leave out.
