## BLOCKING

**1. SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** "On that road he wrote seven letters to the churches that came out to meet him, and they tell nearly all we know of him."
- Source: Rome did not come out to meet him. It was where he was going: "trusting ... to be permitted to fight with beasts at Rome" (Eph. 1, ANF 4704–4705), and "I write to the Churches ... unless ye hinder me" (Rom. 4, ANF 7036–7037). One of the seven went to a man, not a church: "Having obtained good proof that thy mind is fixed in God" (Pol. 1, ANF 8549). The churches that met him were those of Ephesus, Magnesia and Tralles (Eph. 1, ANF 4707–4711; Magn. 2, ANF 5674–5677; Trall. 1, ANF 6279–6282).
- The outline's essence holds this wording, but the source does not. The outline needs a Do not say line too.
- Change: "On that road he wrote seven letters, six to churches and one to a fellow bishop, and they tell nearly all we know of him."
- Do not say (add): "seven letters to the churches that came out to meet him" → Rome had not met him, and one letter went to Polycarp.

**2. SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** "Polycarp of Smyrna gathered the letters and sent them on, and later bishops quoted them for centuries."
- Source: "The Epistles of Ignatius written by him to us, and all the rest [of his Epistles] which we have by us, we have sent to you, as you requested" (Polycarp, *Phil.* 13, ANF 3608–3614). Coming straight after "seven letters", "the letters" tells the listener all seven, which the outline's Do not say bars ("That Polycarp collected 'all seven'" → "the letters of Ignatius that he held"). Paragraph 8 gets it right; this sentence does not.
- Change: "Polycarp of Smyrna gathered the letters he held and sent them on, and later bishops quoted them for centuries."

**3. ASSUMPTION ADDED (a date).** "About seventy years later, Irenaeus of Lyon quoted the wheat line, without naming him, as the words of a fellow believer condemned to the beasts."
- Source: Irenaeus gives no interval: "As a certain man of ours said, when he was condemned to the wild beasts because of his testimony with respect to God" (*Haer.* 5.28.4, ANF 53556–53558). "Seventy years" is a reckoning between Irenaeus's date and a year for Ignatius. The outline rates that year Contested and has the brief credit even the reign to Eusebius (Setting; SS S2, D3). Here the narrator states the interval in his own voice, with no credit. Under the later dating it would be wrong.
- Change: "Later still, Irenaeus of Lyon quoted the wheat line, without naming him, as the words of a fellow believer condemned to the beasts."
- Do not say (add): "about seventy years later" → "later" (the interval rests on a Contested date for Ignatius).

## SUMMARY HOLDS (not blocking)

- "Three messages run through them: keep together around your bishop, hold that Christ truly suffered, and, to Rome, do not save me." Eph. 4, ANF 4828–4830; Trall. 9–10, ANF 6617–6620, 6671–6673; Rom. 1, ANF 6921–6922 ("difficult for me to attain to God, if ye spare me").
- "He was taken in chains for the name of Christ, in the reign of the emperor Trajan, Eusebius says." "bound for the name [of Christ]" (Eph. 3, ANF 4793; the bracket is the translators', and Eph. 1 has "for the common name and hope", ANF 4703–4704). The reign: "Traiano aduersum Christianos persecutionem mouente ... Ignatius quoque Antiochenae ecclesiae episcopus Romam perductus bestiis traditur" (CHRON 42193–42202).
- "The elders, the council who led a church with its bishop, should be fitted to him like strings to a harp, so that the whole church sings with one voice." "fitted as exactly to the bishop as the strings are to the harp ... ye may with one voice sing to the Father" (Eph. 4, ANF 4828–4836). Gloss: "presbyters in the place of the assembly of the apostles" (Magn. 6, ANF 5854–5855).
- "Nothing should be done without the bishop: one thanksgiving meal of one bread and one cup, at one altar, as there is one bishop." "neither do ye anything without the bishop and presbyters" (Magn. 7, ANF 5895–5897); "one Eucharist ... one cup ... one altar; as there is one bishop" (Philad. 4, ANF 7497–7501). "One bread" is in Eph. 20 ("breaking one and the same bread", ANF 5548), not Philad. 4.
- "They thought the same of his own sufferings, and he charged that they cared nothing for the widow, the orphan or the hungry." "nor the sufferings we have individually endured. For they think also the same thing regarding us" (Smyrn. 5, ANF 8133–8135); "no care for the widow, or the orphan ... of the hungry" (Smyrn. 6, ANF 8184–8186).
- "The bread was an offering, as the letter says next: he would be found a sacrifice to God." "that by these instruments I may be found a sacrifice [to God]" (Rom. 4, ANF 7046–7047); also "that I be sacrificed to God while the altar is still prepared" (Rom. 2, ANF 6958–6959). The meaning holds. "Next" is loose: four sentences come between the wheat line and this one. Optional, not blocking: "as the same passage says a few lines on".
- "At their request he sent on the letters of Ignatius he held, because they spoke of faith and patience, and asked for surer news of him and those with him." "as you requested ... by them ye may be greatly profited; for they treat of faith and patience ... Any more certain information you may have obtained respecting both Ignatius himself, and those that were with him, have the goodness to make known" (*Phil.* 13, ANF 3613–3630).

## HELD (one line each)

- Q1: "From Syria even unto Rome I fight with beasts, both by land and sea, both by night and day, being bound to ten leopards, I mean a band of soldiers, who, even when they receive benefits, show themselves all the worse" (Rom. 5, ANF 7083–7089).
- "Ignatius, bishop ... his guards": "the Church which is at Antioch in Syria. Coming from that place bound with chains" (Smyrn. 11, ANF 8408–8409); "fight with beasts at Rome" (Eph. 1, ANF 4704–4705); "I mean a band of soldiers" (Rom. 5).
- "Across Asia ... even those off his route": "even those Churches which were not near to me in the way ... have gone before me, city by city" (Rom. 9, ANF 7280–7287); "the journey through Asia" (EUS 19952).
- "At Smyrna he met its bishop, Polycarp": "Polycarp, the bishop of the Smyrnæans" (Magn. 15, ANF 6221–6222); "thought worthy [to behold] thy blameless face" (Pol. 1, ANF 8549–8550).
- "The churches of Ephesus, Magnesia and Tralles sent their bishops ... and to Rome": Onesimus (Eph. 1, ANF 4708–4710); Damas (Magn. 2, ANF 5674); Polybius "who has come to Smyrna" (Trall. 1, ANF 6279–6280); "from Smyrna" (Eph. 21, ANF 5581; Magn. 15, ANF 6221; Trall. 12, ANF 6786; Rom. 10, ANF 7309).
- "His first message was unity ... deacons": "Give heed to the bishop, and to the presbytery and deacons" (Philad. 7, ANF 7718–7719).
- "He warned that God does not live where there is division and anger": "where there is division and wrath, God doth not dwell" (Philad. 8, ANF 7756–7757).
- "His second message ... a body and to suffer": "not confessing that He was [truly] possessed of a body" (Smyrn. 5, ANF 8137–8138); "He only seemed to suffer" (Smyrn. 2, ANF 8006).
- Q2: "But if, as some that are without God, that is, the unbelieving, say, that He only seemed to suffer (they themselves only seeming to exist), then why am I in bonds? Why do I long to be exposed to [lit. 'to fight with'] the wild beasts? Do I therefore die in vain?" (Trall. 10, ANF 6671–6676). The Greek is not held; this checks against the ANF English.
- "They shunned the thanksgiving meal ... Christ's flesh": "They abstain from the Eucharist ... because they confess not the Eucharist to be the flesh of our Saviour" (Smyrn. 7, ANF 8220–8222).
- "His third message ... fear of that church's love": "For I am afraid of your love" (Rom. 1, ANF 6917).
- "If they spared him ... become God's": "difficult for me to attain to God, if ye spare me" (Rom. 1, ANF 6921–6922); "if ye are silent concerning me, I shall become God's" (Rom. 2, ANF 6956).
- Q3, checked against the Greek: "Σῖτός εἰμι τοῦ Θεοῦ, καὶ δι᾽ ὀδόντων θηρίων ἀλήθομαι, ἵνα καθαρὸς ἄρτος εὑρεθῶ" (HOR 15547–15552, damaged scan). The rendering is exact, and ἀλήθομαι is a statement, "I am ground". EUS 20069–20071 agrees.
- "At Troas ... and to Polycarp": "The love of the brethren at Troas salutes you; whence also I write" (Philad. 11, ANF 7894–7895; Smyrn. 12, ANF 8466–8467); "sail from Troas" (Pol. 8, ANF 8855).
- "News had come ... rejoice with it": "it is reported to me that the Church which is at Antioch in Syria possesses peace ... that he may rejoice along with them" (Philad. 10, ANF 7856–7860); Smyrn. 11, ANF 8418–8421; Pol. 7, ANF 8806–8817.
- "He could not write to every church ... Neapolis": "I have not been able to write to all the Churches, because I must suddenly sail from Troas to Neapolis" (Pol. 8, ANF 8854–8856).
- "Later Polycarp wrote ... endurance they had witnessed": "accompanied ... those who were bound in chains" (*Phil.* 1, ANF 3244); "such as ye have seen [set] before your eyes, not only in the case of the blessed Ignatius" (*Phil.* 9, ANF 3484–3486).
- "Two centuries on, Eusebius ... testimony to Christ": "Report says that he was sent from Syria to Rome, and became food for wild beasts on account of his testimony to Christ" (EUS 19935–19936). "Two centuries on" holds under any dating of Ignatius.
