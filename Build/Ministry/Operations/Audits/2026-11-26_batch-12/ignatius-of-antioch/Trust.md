## BLOCKING

**1. "Polycarp of Smyrna gathered the letters and sent them on, and later bishops quoted them for centuries."** SOFTENED OR CHANGED (overstated).
- It follows "he wrote seven letters", so a listener hears that Polycarp gathered all seven. Polycarp says only: "The Epistles of Ignatius written by him to us, and all the rest [of his Epistles] which we have by us, we have sent to you, as you requested" (ANF 3609–3613, *Phil.* 13). The outline's own Do not say bars "all seven". The second half holds: Irenaeus, Eusebius, Athanasius and Theodoret, all bishops, quote him (ANF 53556–53560; EUS 19961–20101; NPNF4 59204–59210; NPNF3 20264–20272).
- Change: "Polycarp of Smyrna gathered the letters he held and sent them on, and later bishops quoted them for centuries."

**2. "The elders, the council who led a church with its bishop, should be fitted to him like strings to a harp, so that the whole church sings with one voice."** SOFTENED OR CHANGED (praise turned into a rule).
- Source, Eph. 4 (ANF 4828–4836): "your justly renowned presbytery, worthy of God, is fitted as exactly to the bishop as the strings are to the harp. Therefore in your concord and harmonious love, Jesus Christ is sung. And do ye, man by man, become a choir, that ... ye may with one voice sing to the Father". The harp is what he praises in the Ephesian church; the one voice is what he urges. The gloss holds (Magn. 6, ANF 5854–5856, "presbyters in the place of the assembly of the apostles"; Trall. 3, ANF 6349, "the sanhedrim of God").
- Change: "At Ephesus, he wrote, the elders, the council who led a church with its bishop, were fitted to him like strings to a harp, and he urged the whole church to sing with one voice."

## SUMMARY HOLDS (not blocking)

- **"On that road he wrote seven letters to the churches that came out to meet him, and they tell nearly all we know of him."** Six of the seven go to churches or a bishop he met or whose envoys came to him (Eph. 1, ANF 4707–4711; Magn. 2, ANF 5674–5677; Trall. 1, ANF 6279–6282; Philad. 7, ANF 7717–7731; Smyrn. 12; Pol. 1); Rome did not come out, but the next sentence sets Rome apart. "Nearly all we know": the old witnesses add only the meeting and the letters sent on (Polycarp, ANF 3484–3488, 3609–3630), the wheat line (Irenaeus, ANF 53556–53560), the reign and the death as report (EUS 19935–19936; CHRON 42193–42202). The Library holds nothing else early.
- **"Three messages run through them: keep together around your bishop, hold that Christ truly suffered, and, to Rome, do not save me."** Eph. 4; Trall. 9–10 ("He was truly crucified", ANF 6617–6618); Rom. 1 ("it is difficult for me to attain to God, if ye spare me", ANF 6921–6922).
- **"His first message was unity: each church should act as one with its bishop, its elders and its deacons."** Magn. 6, ANF 5852–5856; Philad. 4, ANF 7500–7501: "one bishop, along with the presbytery and deacons".
- **"Nothing should be done without the bishop: one thanksgiving meal of one bread and one cup, at one altar, as there is one bishop."** Philad. 7, ANF 7727: "Do nothing without the bishop"; Philad. 4, ANF 7497–7501: "one Eucharist. For there is one flesh ... and one cup ... one altar; as there is one bishop". "One bread" is Eph. 20, ANF 5547: "breaking one and the same bread". A fair joining of two letters.
- **"The bread was an offering, as the letter says next: he would be found a sacrifice to God."** Rom. 4, ANF 7046–7047: "Entreat Christ for me, that by these instruments I may be found a sacrifice [to God]", with the note "i.e., by the teeth of the wild beasts"; also Rom. 2, ANF 6958: "that I be sacrificed to God". The sense holds. MINOR: four sentences come between in Rom. 4, so "next" is loose; "as the same passage says" is exact.
- **"Later Polycarp wrote to the church at Philippi, which had seen Ignatius pass, and counted him among those whose endurance they had witnessed."** *Phil.* 9, ANF 3484–3486: "all patience, such as ye have seen [set] before your eyes, not only in the case of the blessed Ignatius".
- **"About seventy years later, Irenaeus of Lyon quoted the wheat line, without naming him, as the words of a fellow believer condemned to the beasts."** ANF 53556–53560: "As a certain man of ours said, when he was condemned to the wild beasts because of his testimony with respect to God: 'I am the wheat of Christ, and am ground ...'". The gap rests on old placings, not an editor's year: Eusebius sets the death under Trajan (CHRON 42193–42202), and Irenaeus writes while Eleutherus is bishop of Rome (*Haer.* 3.3.3). It passes the date rule.

## The heart

Right for its subject. The record is his letters from the road, and the brief is built on them: the bishop under guard, the three messages, the plea to Rome, and the letters passed on by Polycarp. The death stays in Eusebius's voice as report. The essence is fully told by the end of the second paragraph, at about word 139, inside the first minute.
