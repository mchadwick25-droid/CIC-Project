# Outline check: Ignatius of Antioch

## My account of the heart (written before reading Outline.md)

From the census entry (teaser, knownFor), the slate job and Source_Sheet.md:

- **The essence.** Ignatius, bishop of the church at Antioch in Syria, was taken under military guard across Asia to Rome to be killed by wild beasts. On the road, met by delegates of the churches, he wrote seven letters, which the churches kept and Polycarp gathered: they tell the churches to hold together around one bishop with the elders and deacons, and one eucharist; that Christ truly had a body and truly suffered, against those who said he only seemed to; and they beg the Romans not to save him from the beasts.
- **Also told.** The road and the guard ("ten leopards"); the churches coming to meet him, Smyrna and Polycarp; the reason each message mattered to him (division drives God out; if Christ only seemed to suffer, his own chains and death are "in vain"; to be spared is to be kept from God); Troas, news of peace at Antioch, the request that the churches send messengers, the sudden sailing; the end told only as the record gives it (Polycarp counts him among the dead yet asks for surer news; Eusebius two centuries later: "report says" he died by the beasts at Rome).
- **What came of him.** Heaviest first: the letters gathered and passed on as a set (Polycarp, then Irenaeus and Eusebius); the wheat line carried on (Irenaeus, Eusebius, the feast); his words on Christ's true flesh brought as witness in later disputes (Athanasius, Theodoret) [defended: Christ truly in the flesh]; his order of one bishop, elders, deacons and one eucharist [defended], used later only in summary in the held old texts; remembered as martyr at Antioch and on the calendars.
- **Shape.** Martyr; the trap is the death swallowing the letters, so the three messages carry the weight.

## Comparison with the outline's heart

The outline's essence and Also told match mine in substance: the road under guard, the seven letters to the churches that met him, the three messages with the reason each mattered to him, Troas, Polycarp gathering the letters, the later quoting. No major contribution is left out. The beats put the heart in the first minute (Opening 1, then the essence) and, apart from the duplicates in B2 and M2 below, follow the road once, in order. The ending is not told early.

## Quotes: my rendering first, then the comparison

- **Q1 (Rom. 5, ANF 7083–7090).** Mine: "From Syria to Rome I fight with beasts, on land and sea, by night and day, chained to ten leopards, that is, a band of soldiers, who only grow worse when they are treated well." The outline's rendering is faithful to the ANF English. See M1 on "kindness".
- **Q2 (Trall. 10, ANF 6671–6676).** Mine: "If, as some godless people, that is, unbelievers, say, he only seemed to suffer (they themselves only seem to exist), why am I in chains? Why do I long to fight the wild beasts? Do I die for nothing, then?" The outline's rendering matches, every clause kept. "Fight" stands on the literal note at 6675. The note that some read the last clauses as statements (6677–6678) is recorded. It holds.
- **Q3 (Rom. 4; HOR 15547–15552).** Mine, from the Greek as HOR prints it: "I am God's wheat, and I am ground by the teeth of wild beasts, so that I may be found pure bread." It matches the outline's. "Am ground" (ἀλήθομαι) stands on the Greek, on McGiffert's English of Eusebius (EUS 20069–20071) and on the Syriac note "Literally, 'I am ground'" (ANF 9390). It holds. See M3 on the plain sense beside it.

## Findings

### BLOCKING

**B1. The Connection field and What came of it 1 claim that Polycarp did the Syria errand. The source says only that he will do it.** At *Phil.* 13 (ANF 3608–3613) Polycarp writes: "which request I will attend to if I find a fitting opportunity". That is a promise. And the request he answers is the Philippians' and Ignatius's together: that whoever went into Syria should carry the Philippians' letter. The Connection also says the passing of the letters "is how Irenaeus, seventy years on, could quote him without naming him and be understood". No held source says how Irenaeus came by the line or why he left out the name. That is an inferred "so". (The same overstatement stands in Source_Sheet.md I1, line 138: "Polycarp did what Ignatius asked (Pol. 7–8)". Correct it there too.)
- What came of it 1, replace "and did what Ignatius asked about Syria (*Phil.* 13, ANF 3608–3613)" with: "and promised to send a letter on to Syria when he found the chance, as Ignatius and the Philippians had both asked (*Phil.* 13, ANF 3608–3613)".
- Connection, replace "Polycarp also did the errand Ignatius gave him, sending letters on to Syria (ANF 3608–3613; Pol. 7–8)." with: "Polycarp also promised to send a letter on to Syria at the first fitting chance, as Ignatius and the Philippians had both asked (ANF 3608–3613; Pol. 7–8)."
- Connection, replace "that is how Irenaeus, seventy years on, could quote him without naming him and be understood." with: "and about seventy years later Irenaeus quoted the wheat line as the words of "a certain man of ours" (ANF 53556–53560)."
- Do not say, add: "That Polycarp sent a messenger or letter to Syria → he promised to, "if I find a fitting opportunity" (*Phil.* 13, ANF 3608–3613)." Also add: "Why Irenaeus left out his name, or how he knew the line → the record does not say."

Beat 1 also gives Eusebius's reason, "his testimony to Christ" (EUS 19935–19936). Ending 1 then tells the same Eusebius sentence again.
- In beat 1, delete "A squad of ten soldiers guarded him, and they grew worse when treated kindly (Rom. 5)."
- In beat 1, delete "; Eusebius, "his testimony to Christ" (EUS 19935–19936)". Eusebius's wording stays in Ending 1 only.

### MINOR

**M1. Q1 and the Do not say line use "kindness".** The text has "receive benefits" (ANF 7088). "Kindness" echoes the editor's guess at 7089–7090 ("to treat Ignatius with kindness"). In Q1, replace "Even when they receive kindness, they grow all the worse." with "Even when they are treated well, they grow all the worse." In Do not say, replace "→ "even when they receive kindness" only" with "→ "even when they are treated well" only".

**M2. Opening 2 and beat 2 tell the same thing.** Both give Rom. 9, the churches meeting him city by city. Under Opening 2, replace "Can follow Opening 1 as the bridge to Smyrna." with "Use one or the other: if this opens, beat 2 does not retell Rom. 9."

**M3. Q3's plain sense rests on a bracketed supplement.** "[to God]" at ANF 7047 is the translators' addition. The plain sense, an offering to God, stands unbracketed at Rom. 2, ANF 6958–6959 ("that I be sacrificed to God while the altar is still prepared"). In Words to explain ("the bread line's sense") and in beat 5, add "Rom. 2, ANF 6958–6959" beside "ANF 7046–7047".

**M4. Confidence for the reign is given two ways.** Beat 1 rates it "Widely Accepted (the reign, as Eusebius's)". Setting and SS S2 rate it Contested. In beat 1, replace "Widely Accepted (the reign, as Eusebius's)" with "Contested (the reign; told crediting Eusebius, SS S2)".

**M5. "Teachers" has no cited support in beat 4.** Trall. 9–10 and Smyrn. 2 say "some that are without God" and "certain unbelievers". The word "teach" is Ignatius's own at Pol. 3, ANF 8646 ("those who seem worthy of credit, but teach strange doctrines"). In beat 4 and in Words to explain, add "Pol. 3, ANF 8646" beside the cite.

**M6. "They thought the same of the martyrs' sufferings" (beat 4) says a little more than Smyrn. 5.** The text has "the sufferings we have individually endured … they think also the same thing regarding us" (ANF 8134–8137). **M7. "His own words end there" (beat 6) claims more than the record shows.** The letters do not fix which Troas letter came last. Replace it with: "It is the last stage of the road his letters tell."

**M8. The elders gloss adds "senior men".** In Words to explain, replace "the council of senior men who led a church with its bishop (Magn. 6, ANF 5854–5856)" with "the council of elders who led a church with its bishop (Magn. 6, ANF 5854–5856; Trall. 3, ANF 6347–6352)". In Do not say, add: ""priests" for the elders → "elders"."

**M9. Do not say should cover the death told as fact.** Add: "That he was killed by beasts at Rome, told in the narrator's voice → "two centuries later Eusebius wrote that report said he became food for wild beasts" (EUS 19935–19936)."
