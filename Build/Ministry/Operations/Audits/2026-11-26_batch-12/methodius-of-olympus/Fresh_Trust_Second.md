## Each changed sentence

1. "They feast under a wide, shady tree, and she asks each guest to praise the virgin life." **HELD.** A6 29950-29952: "a lofty tree, under which we reposed, from its being exceedingly wide-spreading and shady."

2. "The dialogue, as a later reader summed it up centuries on, asks what the body is to the soul." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** A6 35864-35867 (Photius, cod. 234): "Read the Book on the Resurrection by St. Methodius ... that the body is not the fetter of the soul, as Origen thought". Photius is in the ninth century, so "centuries on" holds. The credit is placed where the outline puts it (Beat 3), and it covers the Photius-kept account of Origen that follows.

3. "At the resurrection, Origen held, the same flesh does not return, only each person's shape, stamped on a different, spiritual body." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** A6 36450-36453: "the same flesh will not be restored to the soul, but that the form of each ... shall arise stamped upon another spiritual body". "Different" for "another" keeps the meaning, which is not the same body.

4. "Methodius could not bear, he says, those who turn bones and flesh into symbols, so that the dead rise without flesh." **SUMMARY HOLDS.** Checked against the Greek, EPI 35267-35270: "οὐ γὰρ δὴ ἀνέξομαι φληναφώντων τινῶν ... ἵνα αὐτοῖς δὴ σαρκὸς μὴ εἶναι προχωρήσῃ ἡ ἀνάστασις, ὀστᾶ νοητὰ καὶ σάρκας νοητὰς ὑποτιθεμένων ... ἀλληγορίαις". Clark, A6 35355-35359, agrees. The ἵνα clause gives the purpose, a resurrection that for them is not of flesh. "So that" keeps it. No change needed. If the reviser wants to be exact, "αὐτοῖς" would allow "so that, for them, the dead rise without flesh", but that is optional and not a finding.

5. "If the body were a chain to hold back evil, he answered, it had failed, since Cain committed murder in this body." **HELD.** A6 35880-35890: "It remains, then, that it was a fetter upon evil. But surely we see that, at the beginning, Cain, clad in this body, committed murder ... The body is not, then, a fetter upon evil".

6. "In his telling, a craftsman finds that an envious man has spoiled a beautiful statue he made, so he melts it down and casts it again." **HELD.** EPI 35647: "ἄγαλμα καλὸν ἀπὸ χρυσοῦ"; A6 35423-35428: "a noble image, wrought by himself of gold or other material ... too envious to endure the image being beautiful, spoiled it"; A6 35431-35432: "he will be impelled to melt it down".

7. "Eusebius of Caesarea, in a book he wrote in Origen's defense, asked how Methodius dared to write against Origen now." **HELD.** NP3 51485-51487: "in the sixth book of his Apology for Origen ... How could Methodius dare to write now against Origen". Cutting "after what he had said of Origen's teachings" removes words the source leaves unexplained ("this thing and that"). Nothing is lost.

8. "Jerome, who reports the question, says Methodius was crowned with martyrdom at Chalcis in Greece, at the end of the last persecution, though others, he adds, placed it under earlier emperors, Decius and Valerian." **HELD.** NP3 41102-41105: "At the end of the recent persecution or, as others affirm, in the reign of Decius and Valerianus, he was crowned with martyrdom at Chalcis in Greece." "Earlier" stands on Eusebius. In EUS 37382-37384 (VI.39.1), Decius follows Philip. In EUS 40442-40446 (VII.11.26), a confessor of that time "was preserved until the persecution in our day". So the old sources order Decius and Valerian before the last persecution. This is not an editor's date.

9. "More than a century after Methodius, the church historian Socrates, who defended Origen, put him first among four men who attacked Origen." **HELD.** N2 891: Socrates's history runs "down to the seventeenth consulate of the Emperor Theodosius the Younger". Methodius was writing at the time of Eusebius's Defense (NP3 51485-51487), and Jerome puts his death at the end of the recent persecution (NP3 41102-41105). The gap is more than a century on either of Jerome's dates. Socrates's attack is at N2 18135-18144.
