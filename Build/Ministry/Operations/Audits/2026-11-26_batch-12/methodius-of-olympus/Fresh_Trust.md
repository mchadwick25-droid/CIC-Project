3. "One guest says praising virginity does not abolish marriage, which is still God's work." A6 30269-30290: heading "Marriage Not Abolished by the Commendation of Virginity"; "the Word did not altogether abolish the generation of children"; "the Creator still fashioning man … God, like a painter, is at this very time working at the world". Holds: her case is that begetting children is still God's ordinance and work.
4. "Virtue, speaking last, says it is no chastity to keep the body pure but not the tongue, the eyes, the hands or the mind." A6 34138-34144: "all the members are to be preserved intact … it would be ridiculous to preserve the organs of generation pure, but not the tongue; or … neither the eyesight, the ears, nor the hands; or lastly … not the mind". 5. "The dialogue, as a later reader summed it up centuries on, asks what the body is to the soul." The later summary is credited; the question is a fair frame for what it lists.
6. "Methodius could not bear, he says, those who turn bones and flesh into symbols, so that the dead rise without flesh." A6 35355-35359: "I cannot endure the trifling of some who shamelessly do violence to Scripture, in order that their opinion, that the resurrection is without flesh, may find support; supposing rational bones and flesh … by allegorizing." Holds; "those who" keeps his unnamed "some".
7. "So, he wrote, God, seeing his finest work spoiled by envy, dissolves the body in death and remakes it in the resurrection." A6 35447-35457: "seeing man, His fairest work, corrupted by envious treachery … the melting down of the statue … corresponds to the death and dissolution of the body … the remoulding of the material … to the resurrection after death".
