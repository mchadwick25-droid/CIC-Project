## Worded by me: checked

- **B3, the folded credit (Beat 3, line 42; Who tells it, line 66):** holds. Photius at A6 35864-35870 ("Read the Book on the Resurrection by St. Methodius ... of which that which follows is a selection, that the body is not the fetter of the soul, as Origen thought") gives the subject as what the body is to the soul. "A later reader ... centuries on" is right for Photius (A6 35862-35863, *Bibliotheca* cod. 234). Old witness, much later, credited in a sentence that says something. The next sentence still tells Origen's teaching as "Methodius wrote", so the account stays Methodius's. Documented as Methodius's account, through Photius.
- **B5, Theodoret to the card (What came of it 2, line 49; Shape, line 30; Connection, line 59):** holds. The story no longer tells Theodoret. The Connection now names only Epiphanius and Socrates, and Epiphanius's copying (I1) carries it. A few other fields still speak of Theodoret as told: see MINOR 1-3.
- **MINOR 4, "earlier emperors" (Beat 7, line 46):** holds. EUS 37382-37384: "After a reign of seven years Philip was succeeded by Decius", under the heading "The Persecution under Decius" (37380-37381). EUS 39898-39901 heads Book VII, chapter X, "Valerian and the Persecution under him". EUS 40438-40446: Faustus, a confessor of that time, "was preserved until the persecution in our day, when being very old and full of days, he closed his life by martyrdom" (verbatim; an endnote marker stands after "in our day", and the outline rightly ends before "being beheaded"). So by Eusebius's own account, Decius and Valerian come before the persecution of his day. Jerome wrote after Eusebius, so his "recent persecution" cannot come before it. "Earlier" says no more than this. Widely Accepted is a cautious rating, and it is allowed for a story line. The witness kind is right: old (Eusebius) in McGiffert's English of 1890. The editor's footnote ("Diocletian, a.d. 303") is rightly not used, so no emperor and no year is named.

## The heart change (MINOR 2, reported to Mark)

"On a different, spiritual body" (Also told, line 8; Beat 3, line 42; Do not say, line 84): holds at its source. A6 36450-36452 ("Then he says, refuting Origen"): "the form of each ... shall arise stamped upon another spiritual body". "Another" here means a body other than the flesh now worn. "A different, spiritual body" says that and no more. Documented as Methodius's account, through Photius. Only the wording changes; the heart's substance does not. It is to go to Mark as the fix pass says. If he does not take it, the three lines go back to "another".
