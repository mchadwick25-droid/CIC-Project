## Heart

Right heart. The record holds his books in his own words and little of his life: a see, a quarrel and Jerome's word on his martyrdom. The heart (bishop in Lycia; the Banquet; On the Resurrection against Origen; Epiphanius's copying; Jerome's martyrdom) is what the sources show. The essence is complete at the end of paragraph 2, word 146, inside the first minute.

## SUMMARY HOLDS (non-HELD, none blocking)

1. **P1 s3** "They feast under a tall, shady tree, and she asks each guest to praise the virgin life." A6 29950-29952 "under which we reposed, from its being exceedingly wide-spreading and shady"; 29969-29974 "we have now had enough of food and feasting ... That each of you shall pronounce a discourse in praise of virginity." A fair compression.
2. **P3 s2** "One guest says praising virginity does not abolish marriage, which is still God's work." A6 30279-30281 "the Word did not altogether abolish the generation of children"; 30285-30287 "the sentence and ordinance of God respecting the begetting of children is confessedly being fulfilled to this day, the Creator still fashioning man". The force is intact. (The wording "Marriage Not Abolished" is Clark's chapter heading, 30269, but the speech carries the sense.)
3. **P3 s4** "Virtue, speaking last, says it is no chastity to keep the body pure but not the tongue, the eyes, the hands or the mind." A6 34140-34144 "it would be ridiculous to preserve the organs of generation pure, but not the tongue; or ... the eyesight, the ears, nor the hands; or ... not the mind". "No chastity" stands for "ridiculous" in a speech on chastity, and the ears are dropped. The meaning holds.
4. **P4 s1** "The dialogue asks what the body is to the soul." A6 35864-35870, Photius's statement of the book's subject: the body "the fetter of the soul" or "carried about with it".
5. **P4 s5** "Methodius could not bear ... those who turn bones and flesh into symbols to make the resurrection fleshless." A6 35355-35359 "I cannot endure the trifling of some who ... that the resurrection is without flesh, may find support; supposing rational bones and flesh ... by allegorizing." Fair.
6. **P5 s1** "If the body were a chain on evil, he answered, it had failed, since Cain committed murder in this body." A6 35884-35890 "It remains, then, that it was a fetter upon evil. But surely we see that ... Cain, clad in this body, committed murder ... The body is not, then, a fetter upon evil". "It had failed" puts the reductio in plain words. The logic holds.
7. **P5 s3** "a craftsman finds that an envious man has spoiled his finest statue, so he melts it down and casts it again." A6 35424-35428 "an eminent craftsman were to cast over again a noble image ... mutilated by some infamous man, who, too envious ... spoiled it". "Finest" for "noble" (and "beautifully proportioned") is a fair compression, and it matches R9's "His fairest work".
8. **P7 s2** "A bishop of the next century kept Methodius's words on martyrdom among earlier bishops' testimonies, in arguing that God was made man." NP3 20517-20526 "Testimony of the Holy Methodius, bishop and martyr, from his discourse on the martyrs"; NP3 20249-20250 "his mind about the divine incarnation". Fair compression.
9. **P8 s1** "More than a century later, the church historian Socrates, who defended Origen, put him first among four men who attacked Origen." N2 18133-18145 "I deem it not unseasonable to make a few observations respecting him ... first Methodius ... This quaternion of revilers has traduced Origen". On the date: Socrates dates his history himself (N2 891, "down to the seventeenth consulate of the Emperor Theodosius the Younger"), and on either of Jerome's dates that is over a century on.
10. **P8 s4** "Socrates alone tells of this change of heart." This is a claim of absence. Source_Sheet D2 rates it "Contested (Socrates alone; the Xenon is not held)", and the outline holds it. My search of `cic/texts/` for Xenon/Ξένων together with Methodius brings up Socrates (N2, SOCG) and later or editorial files only. The PG17 and Lommatzsch Pamphilus files are editors' prefaces, as the sheet's sweep notes. I found no other old witness. Not blocking. The reviewer may weigh whether the sentence only says what is missing (a listener matter, not a trust one).
11. **P9 s2** "One guest begins a hymn, and the others answer her, each keeping herself for Christ, the Bridegroom." A6 34174-34179 "Thekla should begin ... the rest ... responded to her: 'I keep myself pure for Thee, O Bridegroom'"; 34216-34217 "for Thou, O Christ, art all things to me". Fair. Note for the reviewer: this is close to the refrain, which the Do not say list bars from close paraphrase.
