## 1. The kind kit's test

- **Who he was:** filled. A bishop of Olympus, a city in Lycia in the south of Asia Minor, who wrote books. Not filled: when he lived. The story never says his century. I could only guess from "Later in the fourth century" that he lived before then. (MINOR; see 2.)
- **What he did:** filled. He wrote the Banquet, where ten young women praise the unmarried life given to God, and On the Resurrection, where he argued against Origen that the same body rises. Jerome says he died a martyr.
- **What it was about:** filled. Origen said the body was a chain put on the soul after the first sin, and that the same flesh does not rise. Methodius said a person is soul and body together, and God remakes the same body, like a craftsman recasting a spoiled statue. The statue made it clear.
- **What it changed / why he is on the tree:** mostly filled. A later bishop, Epiphanius, built his case against Origen on this book, and a church historian, Socrates, called Methodius the first of Origen's attackers. Partly unfilled: the "bishop of the next century" line (see 4); I could not say what Methodius gave that bishop or why it mattered.

## 2. The opening

After the first three sentences I know: a book called the Banquet by someone named Methodius, three women climbing to a garden, a woman called Virtue. Missing: who Methodius was, where he lived, and when. Sentence 4 gives who and where at once ("bishop of Olympus, a city in Lycia"), so I am not lost. **When** never comes. The opening is a scene inside the book, and "a book by" makes that clear, so I did not mistake it for a real event.
