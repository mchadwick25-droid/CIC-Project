# Operations Decision Log

Decisions on operations and protection for Church in Conversation, the Church Family Tree and the Church Traditions Archive: the license, copyright, names, rights, launch readiness and accounts. Entries are dated, numbered and append-only. Each quotes the project lead's own words. Open items live in `CiC_Operations_Open_Items_Tracking.md`. The license policy itself was first approved on 2026-11-06 (`CiC_System_Hub_Decision_Log.md`, "License policy for Faithways Studio's published work").

Nothing here is legal advice. Items that need a qualified attorney are named as such and are on hold by decision 4.

## 2026-11-12 - 7. Work that earns money, and schools that charge tuition, help cover costs

**Project lead.**
- "if they are generating revinue then we should recieve compensation"
- "if a school is charging tuition and wants to use this for a class then there should be compensation"
- "a, and institutional curriculum"
- "using it as a resource stays free, building a curriculum around it, like look for 5 traditions that is documented is not"
- "i dont want the students to pay i want the instituion to cover costs i have"
- "i want to make it gracious and accessable, but i don't want schools that charge tuition to use my material not to help cover costs weather its a fee or suggested contribution"

**Decision (license page, section 4).**
- Work that earns more than it costs pays a modest fee: a paid course, a book or guide sold for profit, a paid app, a channel with ads.
- Work given away, sold at cost, or done as open scholarship stays free. A small ministry can have the fee waived.
- Using the sites as a resource is always free: sending students to them, showing them in class, listing them in a syllabus.
- A school that charges tuition and builds our work into its teaching is asked to help carry the cost. That covers copying into course materials and assignments built around the sites. It can agree a modest fee or give a suggested contribution. The school pays, never its students. A school that cannot pay is invited to write.

**Limit, stated to the project lead.** Copying into paid course materials is a use CC BY-NC does not grant, so permission can be required. An assignment that only sends students to the free site copies nothing, so the page asks rather than requires. Whether that ask can be made binding (terms of use, or an institutional login) is in the attorney brief (open item 1).

**The study.** Published prices were gathered on 2026-11-12 for homeschool curricula and co-op licences, Christian-school site licences (RightNow Media, Britannica, Oxford), course-pack permissions (CCC, CLA) and seminary resources. Families pay $40–55 for a single add-on. Christian schools average about 144 students. Course materials come to a few dollars per student per course. Most religious-studies products publish no prices.

**Decision (license page, section 4).**
- Most use is free: reading, study, churches, sermons, families teaching their own children, using the sites in any class, and quoting a short passage with credit without asking, even in work that is sold.
- When work earns money from ours, a small fee is asked. Schools and homeschool co-ops pay by term: $75 for fewer than 150 students, $150 for 150 to 500, $300 for more. One college or seminary course pays by class size per term: $75 for up to 25 students, $150 for 26 to 75, $250 for more. A curriculum across a college or seminary program pays $250, $750 or $1,500 a year by size. One person whose work earns money pays about $50 a year. Companies, and work earning more than about $100,000 a year, are by agreement.
- The school or ministry pays, never its students. Anyone who cannot pay can ask, and cost should never stop a small church.
- Fees and contributions help cover development and operational costs, listed as: hosting, the narration voices, research, review by scholars, translations that open the work to people around the world, and the people who do the work.
- The page shows one typical figure ($75 a term). The full amounts are on the request form (Tally `kb97Br`), which gained a "homeschool co-op" choice. The form was published 2026-11-12, with Mark's approval.

**Said to the project lead.** Fees at this level cannot carry a salary on their own, so a salary belongs to the wider funding plan (open item 16).

## 2026-11-13 - 15. The account register is kept privately, with two named backups

**Project lead.** On where it lives: "i think b for now and i will update later if needed". He named two backups in order, the first and then a second. Their names are recorded in the register, not here.

**Decision.** The account register is a private claude.ai document, outside this repository. This repository is public, and a list of the accounts and their login emails would show an attacker what to target. The register lists each service, what it runs, the login email, whose name it is in, how it is paid, whether two-step login is on, and whether the first backup can get in. It also lists the four domains and gives the backups a short plan if the project lead is unavailable. It holds no passwords, keys or recovery codes. A password manager with emergency access is the recommended later step.
