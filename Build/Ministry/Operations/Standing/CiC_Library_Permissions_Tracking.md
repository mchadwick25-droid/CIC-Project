## Interim rule (Mark, 2026-10-27)

Until a permission below is granted, the Library runs on public-domain texts and on legal referencing. Legal referencing means an in-copyright work is cited bibliographically (author, title, edition, page or locus) and is not copied into `cic/texts/`, quoted beyond what the law allows, or stored in a table. Source records for such works are marked unvendored, and the wants register lists them as purchasable. No request below has been sent. Sending is Mark's call, and a request goes out only when Mark has edited and approved its text.

## Item 2: what CCEL's own terms say

Read on 2026-10-27 from CCEL's copyright page. Most editions "are based on books that are public domain in the United States. However, they may have copyrighted introductions, cover art, and other special contents." A few books are under another publisher's copyright and used by permission, noted on the book page. The books "may be used for personal, educational, or non-profit purposes. Contact us for permission to republish CCEL works or to use them commercially." CCEL also notes that copyright law differs by country.

What this means for us, and why a request fits:
- The vendored ThML files carry CCEL's editors' prefaces and introductions, which CCEL may hold copyright in, and CCEL's scripture markup, which the passage index reads.
- A public conversation app that serves extracts is closer to "republish" than to personal use. Whether it is non-profit use depends on the organization's legal status, which is still open in the funding strategy.
- The request should ask: whether serving extracts and index results to the public is covered; whether the markup may be used; whether the editors' introductions in each volume are excluded; and what attribution CCEL wants.
- Proposed interim practice, not yet a ruling: quotes from CCEL volumes come from the public-domain text of the work, not from an editor's introduction, and each record cites the underlying work.
